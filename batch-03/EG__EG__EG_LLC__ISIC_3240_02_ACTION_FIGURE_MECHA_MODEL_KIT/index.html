Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; unified e-services portal at https://www.incometax.gov.eg and digital tax portal under the 'E-Tax' program (Misr Digital Finance), including the e-invoicing platform at https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must (1) complete commercial registration at the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Commercial Registry, (2) register with the National Population Register and obtain the electronic file from the ETA by submitting the registration application (Form 1 for income tax and Form 9 for VAT at the relevant ETA branch or via the unified portal), (3) provide the certificate of incorporation, commercial registry extract, lease contract of the registered office, board resolutions, statutory auditor appointment, authorizations, and the passport/copy of the non-resident shareholders' IDs, (4) receive the Tax Card (TIN) confirming CIT and VAT registration, and (5) activate the password and digital certificate for the ETA e-portal. For foreign entities not yet resident, a simplified 'tax identification for non-residents' can be obtained for withholding and filing purposes; the LLC itself is registered as a resident in Egypt if incorporated under Egyptian law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Egyptian LLCs are automatically resident subject-to-tax entities under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 (Tax Law), taxed on worldwide income and always treated as separate from shareholders; no pass-through eligibility. Territorial_Scope: Exemptions apply only to statutory categories (e.g., Suez Canal Economic Zone income under enabling legal frameworks); the standard regime is worldwide taxation for Egyptian incorporated entities.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or CFC-blending election exists under Egyptian law; Egypt has not adopted OECD hybrid mismatch legislation. Available statutory elections include: (a) the option for VAT-registered persons below the EGP 500,000 threshold to elect voluntary VAT registration to recover input VAT; (b) small-business taxpayers (turnover thresholds under Law No. 150 of 2021) may elect presumptive taxation on gross turnover instead of full CIT accounting; (c) election of the foreign tax credit method vs. expense deduction for foreign taxes; and (d) election under Article 52 of the Tax Law to treat a non-Egyptian branch's remittances as taxable dividends where applicable. For export-related activity, an election to settle VAT refunds as cash vs. carry-forward offset is available upon meeting substantiation requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 per annual taxable turnover (mandatory VAT registration); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. Any person making taxable supplies of goods or services in Egypt above EGP 500,000 annually must register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including action figure mecha model kits) are zero-rated under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 when the goods are physically delivered outside Egypt and are evidenced by a valid export customs declaration. Services rendered to non-residents (including overseas design, licensing, and offshore logistics) may be zero-rated if the direct benefit is received outside Egypt and all supporting contractual and payment documents are contemporaneous. The zero-rating applies only to supplies exported through the registered Egyptian entity; consignment sales, direct-injection outside Egypt, and re-imported goods require express ETA approval.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing outbound movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-currency credit advice showing the export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Export Contract / Purchase Order from the foreign customer
  • VAT registration certificate photocopy and E-invoice export entry

Mandatory e-invoicing for all VAT-registered suppliers in B2B transactions through the ETA's 'E-Invoice' system, with real-time fiscal clearance via REST/JSON APIs or the approved cloud interface; every invoice receives a unique UUID, cryptographic hash, and QR code before issuance (clearance model). B2C e-receipts (E-Receipt system) are also mandatory for retailers on a phased basis. All files must include the company tax ID, item classifications, unit prices, VAT amount, and compliant Arabic/English layout. As of 2025, virtually all taxpayer segments, including toy and plastic-kit manufacturers, are integrated; failure to clear invoices digitally results in legal invalidity of the invoice and penalties up to EGP 50,000 per transaction plus loss of input VAT deduction rights.

The ETA applies strict audit scrutiny to zero-rating refund claims using risk analytics and physical verification. Where export evidence is incomplete or the foreign currency proceeds are not repatriated within the applicable Central Bank of Egypt deadline (180 days from shipment for manufactured goods export proceeds), the ETA will claw back the input VAT claimed and assess the 14% VAT plus late-payment interest on the full export value. Fraudulent claims (fictitious exports, overstated quantities, forged IRC/BL) render the liability retroactive for up to 5 years, with fines of not less than 100% and up to 500% of the evaded tax, imprisonment up to 5 years for the legal representative, and administrative debarment from VAT registration. Good-faith documentary defects may be cured within 30 days but still attract penalties of up to EGP 100,000 under the e-invoicing regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP is formally convertible on the current account since the CBE's exchange-rate liberalisation of March 6, 2024, which unified the official and interbank rates under a managed floating regime. Current-account settlements, including merchandise export receipts, service fees and profit remittances, are not subject to prior CBE licensing but must be settled through a CBE-licensed bank and are subject to documentary discipline. Capital-account flows are registrable: foreign direct equity injection into an Egyptian LLC requires prior GAFI registration, an inward transfer declaration from the receiving bank, and matching share subscription documentation. Dividend and profit remittances are cleared after the bank verifies audited financial statements, a valid tax card, certified evidence of corporate income tax and withholding tax payments, and the CBE investor-support documentation routinely required under Form No. 13/3 procedures. Practical hurdles include bank-level USD liquidity prioritisation during periods of scarcity, the mandatory reconciliation of all offshore e-commerce receipts against customs export declarations, and the CBE's temporary administrative measures, reactivated during balance-of-payments stress, that direct banks to prioritise the conversion or queuing of export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current flexible-rate framework there is no fixed statutory percentage surrender quota applicable to export receipts. The mandatory regime consists of: (i) full repatriation of merchandise export proceeds through a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank and crediting to an Export Proceeds/Foreign Currency Account within the contractual payment term, normally 30 to 180 days for ISIC 3240 toy and action-figure goods, with any longer tenor requiring prior bank endorsement; (ii) conversion to EGP at the prevailing interbank market rate, with no official-rate discount or differentiated subsidy; (iii) retention of foreign-currency balances being permissible only for documented future trade payments and operating needs, not for offshore deposit accumulation; and (iv) the CBE retaining administrative authority to mandate or prioritise conversion of export proceeds during extraordinary FX liquidity conditions, as exercised through bank-directed circulars in December 2022 and February 2023. Each credit received from offshore e-commerce gateways, including PayPal payouts, must be matched with the corresponding customs export declaration and platform settlement statement; otherwise the bank may classify the credit as a non-trade remittance and apply enhanced due diligence. No special surrender exemption applies for ordinary toy manufacturing exports unless the LLC is licensed within the Suez Canal Economic Zone or another regulated free-zone scheme with a distinct CBE/GAFI protocol.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), also known as Al-Bank al-Markazi al-Misri, acting as the unified regulator of the banking system and the sole national authority for foreign exchange administration under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and its executive regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low-Medium. Egypt does not license foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex or MultiPass to operate as settlement institutions; such providers hold no CBE banking licence and cannot act as the statutory channel for export proceeds repatriation, capital injection, or dividend remittance. PayPal and similar EMI receipts are acceptable only as a supplementary e-commerce collection rail for consumer sales, with proceeds periodically swept into a licensed Egyptian bank account under a documented foreign-currency manifest and matched to underlying export declarations. If an EMI or PayPal is used as the primary receiving rail, the Egyptian bank will classify the credits as unexplained third-party funds, may suspend clearance, and will require enhanced audit documentation. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant equity-inflow protocol: (1) the LLC files the share-capital increase with the Commercial Register and GAFI and notifies the receiving bank in writing before transmission; (2) the offshore investor wires USD via SWIFT MT103 with cover via MT202, stating the purpose code of equity injection into an Egyptian LLC engaged in ISIC 3240 toy and action-figure manufacturing, and referencing the beneficiary's capital escrow account number; (3) the receiving bank credits the funds to a blocked escrow sub-account and issues the Inward Transfer Declaration evidencing the entry of foreign currency, which is the indispensable document for any future dividend or liquidation repatriation; (4) funds are released to the operating account only after the GAFI certificate and Commercial Register amendment are presented, with conversion to EGP applied at the unified interbank market rate; (5) subsequent usage of the injected capital for import purchases is governed by Egyptian customs and import-finance rules, requiring the entity's valid Import/Export Card and compliance with the prevailing documentary requirements, including letters of credit or documentary collections where the CBE directs banks to apply them.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wires for open-account purchases of polymer resin, steel moulds, packaging and tooling, supported by validated import declarations, commercial invoices and tax-clearance evidence; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) used as the primary rail for high-value mould and resin purchases, protecting against shipment risk and foreign-currency repricing; (iii) standby letters of credit (SWIFT MT760) issued as performance security in GCC and EU distribution contracts; (iv) domestic EGP payments executed through the Egyptian RTGS and CNAPS clearing systems for local suppliers of packaging, logistics and utilities; and (v) PayPal and other e-commerce sweeps routed daily into the USD operating account and reconciled against VAT invoices and the platform settlement statement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-Medium. Egypt does not license foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex or MultiPass to operate as settlement institutions; such providers hold no CBE banking licence and cannot act as the statutory channel for export proceeds repatriation, capital injection, or dividend remittance. PayPal and similar EMI receipts are acceptable only as a supplementary e-commerce collection rail for consumer sales, with proceeds periodically swept into a licensed Egyptian bank account under a documented foreign-currency manifest and matched to underlying export declarations. If an EMI or PayPal is used as the primary receiving rail, the Egyptian bank will classify the credits as unexplained third-party funds, may suspend clearance, and will require enhanced audit documentation. These providers also lack importer-card interface, cannot issue CBE-recognised inward transfer declarations, and create VAT and customs reconciliation mismatches for the treasury function.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions requiring a Local File; EGP 15,000,000 cross-border controlled transactions requiring a Master File; CbC Report required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding EGP 50 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute for ordinary LLCs, but the general anti-abuse rule under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 Art. 111, treaty beneficial-ownership tests, and ETA practice require genuine commercial substance: registered premises in Egypt, payroll and technical staff, physical operation of injection-molding assets, and documented decision-making. A paper shell in Egypt claiming treaty benefits or free-zone concessions without local production, employees, or bank transactions will be disregarded and the profit reallocated to the business realistically present in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: imprisonment of at least 6 months and up to 5 years plus a fine of EGP 10,000-50,000 (or the evaded tax, whichever the court determines) under Law No. 91/2005 Arts. 133, 138 and 139; civil enforcement includes a default assessment for the true tax, a monthly 2% delay surcharge, attachment and forced auction of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Companies Law No. 159/1981: directors and the authorized general manager who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax; the ETA may freeze personal bank accounts, impose a personal travel ban, and the responsible officer personally faces imprisonment.
Withholding Agent Default (Dividend / Interest / Royalty WHT)

Statutory sanction: The LLC is personally liable as withholding agent for the full unpaid tax amount, plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge, and a penalty up to 100% of the evaded WHT; the ETA may seize bank balances, attach receivables from distributors, and cancel the entity's tax card for repeated violations.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or knowingly permit non-withholding are jointly and severally liable with the corporate fund; personal recovery extends to interest and surcharges; a travel ban and criminal sentence may be imposed where the false application of a DTA is intentional.
Transfer Pricing / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: An ETA reassessment under Law No. 91/2005 Art. 107 reallocates income to the arm's length result; the additional tax bears a 2% monthly surcharge and an administrative penalty not exceeding 100% of the reassessed tax; where incentives were misused, the ETA withdraws Investment Law deductions and claw back previously recognized incentives.

Director personal liability: In sham structures the courts join directors and UBOs as debtors to the assessed tax; personal travel bans and asset freezes apply to unpaid assessments; an insolvency court may set aside transfers to related parties and make directors personally accountable for the shortfall.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Substance Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80/2002 (as amended): fines up to EGP 2,000,000 and imprisonment for natural persons; the Money Laundering Combating Unit may freeze corporate and personal assets, cancel the commercial registration and tax card, and place the entity on a cross-agency blacklist preventing use of payment rails such as SWIFT and PayPal.

Director personal liability: Nominee structures collapse: the actual UBO is treated as the true shareholder and operator; personal liability includes confiscation of laundered funds, personal imprisonment in aggravated laundering cases, and a travel ban while the criminal investigation is open; the ETA concurrently enforces the unpaid tax against the responsible person's assets.
