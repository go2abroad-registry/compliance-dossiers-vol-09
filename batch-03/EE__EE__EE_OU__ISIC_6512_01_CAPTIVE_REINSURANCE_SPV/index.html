Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an OÜ (formation possible with €0 under the Commercial Code); if share capital is stated, it must be fully paid before filing and credited to an escrow/settlement account of the future company at a licensed Estonian credit institution, with the bank confirmation attached. For the captive reinsurance license, Finantsinspektsioon requires initial capital equal to the Minimum Capital Requirement (MCR), which for a captive reinsurer may not fall below the €1,000,000 absolute floor (Solvency II Article 129(4), implemented by KTS § 38).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance: the registered office must hold a valid building use certificate and be inspected for fire safety by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet); occupational clearance: workplace risk assessment and compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus), registration of the working environment with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) where employees are engaged, and insurance of employees' occupational accidents; no structural load certification beyond the building's use certificate is required for office use.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an OÜ (formation possible with €0 under the Commercial Code); if share capital is stated, it must be fully paid before filing and credited to an escrow/settlement account of the future company at a licensed Estonian credit institution, with the bank confirmation attached. For the captive reinsurance license, Finantsinspektsioon requires initial capital equal to the Minimum Capital Requirement (MCR), which for a captive reinsurer may not fall below the €1,000,000 absolute floor (Solvency II Article 129(4), implemented by KTS § 38).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); digital portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned OÜ must first be entered in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri) to obtain a registry code, which serves as the general tax identification number. VAT registration is then applied for separately via the MTA e-MTA portal using the registry code. Non-resident ownership is permitted; no minimum share capital is required. An authorised representative with an Estonian address for service may be required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a check-the-box regime; an OÜ is per se a corporate taxpayer subject to the Estonian CIT Act's deferred distribution taxation. No comprehensive statutory entity classification election exists for a resident private limited company. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are implemented, limiting deductible-payment structures for captive reinsurance SPVs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover per calendar year for resident suppliers; non-resident suppliers supplying goods or services in Estonia are subject to registration or reverse-charge mechanisms regardless of turnover where no deemed-resident taxable supplier exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported to non-EU destinations and to intra-Community supplies, subject to proof of dispatch or transportation. Services directly related to exported goods and certain cross-border B2B services may be zero-rated. A captive reinsurance SPV's supplies of reinsurance to an EU or non-EU cedant are generally VAT-exempt (factoring in the financial-service exemption) rather than zero-rated, and input VAT recovery is blocked unless specific cost-attribution rules apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (as electronically accepted by Estonian customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing payment from the foreign buyer
  • Underlying insurance or reinsurance contract and policy certificate where applicable
  • Underwriting reserve ledger extract prepared under IFRS or Estonian GAAP

VAT-registered persons must file VAT returns and line-item appendices electronically via e-MTA by the 20th day of the following month. There is no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for standard B2B invoicing; however, structured e-invoices (e-arve) are mandatory for suppliers to the Estonian public sector, and MTA applies automated transaction-risk scoring to VAT period returns and credit notes.

Unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT clawback with statutory late-payment interest of 0.06% per day under § 118 of the Estonian Tax Procedure Act, plus a penalty levy up to 100% of the evaded tax under § 154. Fraudulent VAT and customs claims constitute a criminal offence under § 397 of the Penal Code carrying up to five years' imprisonment, and the MTA's assessment window is ordinarily three years, extended to five years for transfer-pricing or deliberate concealment cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full member of the Eurozone and the EU single market. The euro is fully and freely convertible in both current and capital account transactions. There are no foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on cross-border transfers, no approval requirements for profit remittances, and no limitations on inward or outward foreign investment. Capital account convertibility is unrestricted under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital, Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act in conjunction with EU sanctions regulations. A captive reinsurance SPV can freely receive premiums, allocate reserves, and pay dividends or claims in euros or any other currency without central bank consent, subject only to standard bank verification and sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. As a Eurozone member and a net importer of services, Estonia does not impose any surrender, retention, or mandated conversion requirements for export receipts or foreign currency earnings. Reinsurance premium and claims flows are classified as financial services (ISIC 6512) and no foreign exchange surrender obligations exist. All foreign currency receipts may be retained in external currency accounts or converted at market rates without restriction.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the national monetary authority; in euro area, foreign exchange and monetary policy competence is delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB). Financial services supervision, including AML/CFT compliance of credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions, is vested in Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating in Estonia (e.g., Revolut Business, Monese, Paysera) can provide fast onboarding and multi-currency IBANs for operational expense accounts, low-volume commission payments, and staff payroll. However, EMIs are not credit institutions under the Credit Institutions Act and may not legally accept deposits or hold reinsurance technical reserves; most EMIs' terms expressly prohibit insurance/reinsurance underwriting activity, premium collection, and strategic reserve parking. Therefore, an EMI is not viable as the primary treasury or clearing rail for a captive reinsurance SPV. It may be used only as a secondary ancillary account for treasury visibility, provided the captive's main underwriting reserve and claims settlement accounts are held with a full-service Tier-1 commercial bank or a licensed branch of a foreign credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection for the captive SPV must follow a structured wire protocol: (i) Initial share capital contribution (if required) is credited to a dedicated Capital Escrow Account (reserved within the operational bank) with payment reference 'SUBSCRIBED SHARE CAPITAL – OÜ [name]'. SWIFT MT103 should include the Estonian e-notary code or the bank's internal capital subscription number and the ISIC code '6512'. (ii) Reinsurance premiums from group captives or from the parent's insurance subsidiary must be remitted using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 8931 ('Insurance services') per Standard Settlement Instructions (SSI) agreed with the bank; in the payment narrative, include the reinsurance treaty reference, policy period, and risk coding. (iii) Loan funding from the parent must use purpose code 7431 ('Intra-group financing') and be supported by a formal intercompany loan agreement, transfer pricing documentation, and an arm's length interest rate set at EURIBOR + margin to avoid reclassification as equity. Verification documents (invoices, treaties, LOIs) must be pre-deposited with the bank's compliance team to prevent rejection or 5-7 day hold under sanctions screening.Outbound trade disbursements from the captive SPV consist primarily of (i) claims settlement to the parent insurance company or third-party insureds, (ii) premium ceding to a fronting insurer or external reinsurer, and (iii) investment asset purchases. The preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for all EUR-denominated cash flows using the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) Instant Credit Transfer for values under €100,000, using purpose code 8921 ('Claims under insurance/reinsurance') or 8931 ('Reinsurance premium ceding'). For high-value claims over €100,000 or non-EUR currency settlements, immediate cross-border SWIFT MT103 with pace/cover (MT202) must be used; all wire instructions must pre-notify the bank's trade operations team. Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) and Standby LCs are permissible for collateralization of reinsurance obligations when the captive acts as beneficiary or issuer, but are not standard for captive reinsurance claims. Escrow-based clearing is acceptable when settlement requires release of funds against proof of loss verification. A daily treasury reconciliation process, including internal FX netting if multiple currencies are held, is required to ensure optimised liquidity utilisation.Outright foreign exchange forwards (FXA) with maturity ≤18 months, well hedged against known premium/claim settlement timelines, executed with a bank counterparty under ISDA 2002 or CSA.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) for exotic currencies (e.g., emergency claims settlements in non-convertible currencies or onshore RMB) only if underlying exposure is demonstrable and the bank counterparty offers a credible price.Cross-currency swaps (CCSS) to convert long-term reinsurance technical reserves into neutral base currency (EUR) or to hedge the currency basis of exotic asset portfolios; only within investment policy limits approved by the management board.Zero-cost collars and FX options (European style) using EUR/USD and EUR/GBP under an approved treasury hedging policy, limited to hedging of net exposure not exceeding 100% of booked risk.
