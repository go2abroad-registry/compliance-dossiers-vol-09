Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an Egyptian LLC. The memorandum fixes the share capital, which is divided into equal shares, and each share must be paid in cash or in kind before Commercial Registry issuance. GAFI practice requires evidence of capital availability in a provisional bank account opened in the Company’s name; the funds are released after incorporation is completed. No formal escrow account is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Civil Defense Department (Ministry of Interior) after inspection of extrusion, potting, packaging, and cleanroom areas; municipal structural load and building safety clearance; occupational health and safety registration with the Ministry of Manpower under Law No. 12 of 2003; and cleanroom validation per ISO 14644 appropriate for manufacture of sterile dialysis filters.

A foreign entity shall submit the unified tax registration application to the ETA, attaching notarized and legalized certificate of incorporation, articles of association, proof of local registered address, board resolution on local representative, power of attorney, passport copies of authorized signatories, and local bank account details; the ETA verifies the file and issues the Tax Identification Number (TIN) and separate VAT registration number where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity election exists under Egyptian law; an LLC is mandatorily taxed as a corporate entity at entity level, while certain investment incentive elections and special economic zone tax regimes may be separately approved by the General Authority for Investment (GAFI).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is triggered when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; producers/exporters may also be required to register irrespective of turnover if export activities generate recoverable input VAT claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of exported goods are zero-rated where the taxpayer proves the goods physically left Egypt under customs supervision, holds valid export declarations and transport documents, receives the sale price in hard currency through an Egyptian bank, and submits the export-related evidence with the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form No. 62),
  • Commercial Invoice endorsed by customs,
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill,
  • Packing List and certificate of origin where required,
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Receipt confirming hard-currency repatriation
  • Bank/Letter of Credit payment evidence (Swift MT700/MT103)

VAT-registered enterprises must integrate certified billing software with the ETA e-invoicing system; invoices and e-receipts are transmitted in real time to the ETA in XML or JSON formats, the ETA validates and clears each document, returns a unique invoice UUID, digital signature, and machine-readable QR code; rejected or tampered invoices are deemed non-compliant and attract administrative penalties.

The ETA and customs authorities may retrospectively audit export zero-rating files; failure to substantiate physical export or to repatriate proceeds within the statutory deadline invalidates the zero rate, resulting in assessment of VAT at 14%, late-payment interest, and administrative fines; fraudulent claims for zero-rating or export refunds may trigger criminal penalties including imprisonment and fines up to the amount of tax evaded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) is convertible for genuine current-account transactions (trade invoices, freight, insurance and post-audit profit remittances), while the capital account remains controlled: inward FDI is freely registrable, but outbound capital items such as equity divestment proceeds, shareholder loan repayments and liquidation distributions require CBE documentary vetting and may be queued during interbank liquidity stress. Profit remittance to the non-resident shareholder is permitted without CBE pre-approval once the handling bank verifies (i) audited financial statements, (ii) full settlement of corporate income tax and the 5% dividend withholding regime, and (iii) a valid notarised distribution resolution; practical hurdles include a 3-to-5 business-day FX execution window, bank-level source-of-funds statements and possible deferral under CBE priority settlement rules. Since the March 2024 unified float, interbank pricing reflects market supply and demand, but full capital mobility cannot be assumed outside normal trade cycles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation deadline: export proceeds from dialysis hemofilter sales must be collected through an authorised Egyptian bank within 180 calendar days from the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date, or within one month of contractual maturity for open-account and deferred-payment terms; regulated medical consumables are frequently policed on a 90-day bank-internal target. Mandatory conversion ratio: under the post-March 2024 flexible exchange-rate framework, the CBE no longer enforces a blanket 100% EGP surrender requirement; up to 100% of export receipts may be retained in a monitored foreign-currency account when the exporter submits a CBE-compliant FX retention budget linked to documented import expenditures (polyethersulfone polymer, polycarbonate housings, sterilisation and freight costs). Where no retention plan is approved, the authorised bank will convert 100% at the daily interbank rate; non-repatriation triggers CBE export-default registry sanctions, loss of trade-finance limits and Article 207 Law No. 194/2020 penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting through its Foreign Exchange Operations, Banking Control and Anti-Money Laundering supervisory sectors under Law No. 194 of 2020 on the Central Bank and the Banking System, together with the Ministry of Investment (GAFI) for FDI registration and the Egyptian Drug Authority for medical-device trade veracity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW / NOT VIABLE as the LLC's primary treasury or clearing account. Egypt's payment-systems regulation (Law No. 18 of 2019) and CBE licensing rules do not recognise offshore fintech e-money institutions for EGP-denominated balances, local payroll, supplier settlements, tax payments, export-proceeds collection or import trade settlement; EGP is non-deliverable outside Egypt and all inward export dollars must be routed to a CBE-licensed bank for customs and export-incentive matching. An overseas FCA- or EMI-licensed wallet may be used by the foreign parent only for incidental offshore vendor payments and must never book the Egyptian subsidiary's operational revenue or capital contributions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the Egyptian bank's USD/EUR nostro account, populating Field 70 with the remittance reason 'INWARD CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - EGYPT LLC - GAFI FILE NO.' and Field 72 with the LLC's TIN and bank account IBAN. The Egyptian bank screens the remittance against CBE sanctions lists, matches it to the inward remittance declaration submitted by the company, and credits the capital-escrow account; the credit is then converted domestically to EGP at the interbank rate or retained in USD according to the approved retention budget. The LLC must pre-file the GAFI investment update, a board resolution accepting the subscription, a share-issuance schedule and source-of-funds supporting documents; amounts above USD 500,000 trigger enhanced due diligence and a potential one-to-two business-day compliance hold. The receiving bank issues a certificate of inward remittance, which is later used for dividend matching and capital-repatriation evidence.Input-material procurement (polyethersulfone granules, PU potting resin, polycarbonate case shells and sterile packaging) is cleared through: (i) confirmed irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued by the Egyptian bank under UCP 600, advised via SWIFT MT700/MT710 and settled through MT103/MT202 after compliant negotiation; (ii) documentary collections D/P or D/A under URC 522 for trusted EU and US suppliers; or (iii) advance-payment wire transfers via SWIFT MT103 for low-value repeat purchases under USD 25,000. The company must maintain an import tax card, Egyptian Drug Authority import licence for regulated medical-grade input codes, customs declarations through NAFIZA, and import payments are settled in USD/EUR through the bank's correspondent rails (Deutsche Bank, JPMorgan, HSBC, Commerzbank) without touching the offshore EGP market; domestic EGP payroll, tax and liquidity disbursements run onshore through CBE RTGS from the operational account.Outright USD/EGP forward contracts (tenors 90-180 days) booked by the Egyptian LLC with a CBE-licensed treasury bank, permitted only when backed by a firm underlying import invoice, export LC or audited cash-flow exposure; extensions and margin top-ups follow CBE derivative margin rules.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP principal-exchange swaps) with the main clearing bank to bridge the capital-escrow period and operational-account EGP needs, subject to ISDA/CSA netting, CBE reporting and a documented non-speculative treasury mandate.Offshore EGP non-deliverable forward (NDF) overlay permissible solely at the level of the foreign parent company to hedge translation and equity exposure; the Egyptian LLC itself must not enter an NDF, and any offshore settlement must occur in USD outside CBE territory with no onshore EGP refunding leg.

10% withholding tax on dividend distributions paid by an Egyptian LLC to shareholders; the tax applies to both resident and non-resident payees and is withheld from the gross dividend at distribution

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory contemporaneous Local File must be prepared and filed on request where the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds EGP 10 million in the tax year; a group Master File is required where the group-wide related-party transaction volume or group revenue exceeds ETA-specified thresholds in the same annual guidance; all taxpayers must complete the TP disclosure annex irrespective of the threshold

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not yet maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations statute comparable to Gulf Cooperation Council jurisdictions, but the ETA applies OECD substance-over-form and the Investment Law operational requirements: an LLC deriving tax treaty benefits, free-zone exemptions, or investment incentives must have real premises, employees, and actual manufacturing activity in Egypt; a letter-box company or conduit arrangement without economic substance is treated as a sham for tax purposes and is denied treaty relief, incentives, and related-party deductions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Omission

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Law 91/2005 carries imprisonment for 3 to 5 years plus a fine ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 5,000,000; the ETA may additionally reassess the hidden tax and impose the 2% monthly delay surcharge and 100% cap

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, the fines, and civil restitution; Egyptian courts may issue travel bans and asset freezes and, upon conviction, impose the same imprisonment penalty on directors
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA issues a transfer pricing adjustment, disallows excessive related-party deductions, and imposes the unpaid tax with the 2% monthly delay surcharge, plus a documentation penalty of up to EGP 50,000 under the ETA administrative penalty framework; severe factual falsification is treated as tax evasion with fines and imprisonment

Director personal liability: Executives and in-house accountants who engineered the mispricing may be held personally liable for the entire adjusted tax difference, disgorgement of diverted profits, and criminal culpability; directors may be banned from leaving Egypt pending settlement of the TP assessment
Shell Company / Abusive Conduit

Statutory sanction: The ETA disregards the entity for tax purposes, denies treaty benefits and investment incentives, taxes the income at the full 22.5% in the name of the true owner, and imposes a 2% monthly delay penalty and criminal fines under the anti-abuse articles of Law 91/2005; the company's free-zone license may be revoked by the General Authority for Investment and the customs duty benefits recovered

Director personal liability: Nominee shareholders and directors who enabled the conduit arrangement risk veil-piercing under Egyptian company law, joint and several liability for tax debts, civil damages for commercial fraud, travel bans, and imprisonment for participation in an unlawful structure
AML, Foreign Exchange, and Custom Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002 and Egyptian anti-money laundering amendments, the offense is punishable by imprisonment of 1 to 7 years and fines from EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000; customs violations draw additional penalties equal to several times the evaded duty and confiscation of goods

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be jailed for money laundering facilitation, fined personally, restrained from traveling, and ordered by court to repatriate the offshore funds; the LLC may be dissolved as a non-operational shell after repeated violations
