Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Business Register (Äriregister), operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus - RIK) under the supervision of the Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), Sections 135-178 (private limited company, Osaühing) and Sections 23-35 (registration); Business Register Act (Äriregistri seadus); General Part of the Civil Code Act, Sections 26-39 (legal persons).

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic registration application (kandeavaldus) submitted through the e-Äriregister portal with a qualified digital signature; paper-based filing must be notarized.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital: an Osaühing may be incorporated with nominal share capital from EUR 0.01 upward (2011 amendment abolished the mandatory EUR 2,500 formation threshold). If subscribed capital is below EUR 2,500, statutory distribution restrictions apply, including prohibition on dividends and asset distributions until capital reaches at least EUR 2,500. For aircraft-engine leasing transactions, a subscribed and paid capital of EUR 2,500 is recommended market practice; funds are paid to the company's own bank account and no escrow account is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and structural clearance: the registered office must be located in a building with a valid use-and-occupation permit and fire-safety compliance certified by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet); the lease provider must confirm evacuation plans and fire-extinguishing equipment. Occupational clearance: the OÜ must conduct a workplace risk assessment and appoint an occupational-health physician or specialist under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, including ergonomic assessment for office-based staff. No physical asset clearance is required because the leased engines are stationed at the lessee's premises outside Estonia.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital: an Osaühing may be incorporated with nominal share capital from EUR 0.01 upward (2011 amendment abolished the mandatory EUR 2,500 formation threshold). If subscribed capital is below EUR 2,500, statutory distribution restrictions apply, including prohibition on dividends and asset distributions until capital reaches at least EUR 2,500. For aircraft-engine leasing transactions, a subscribed and paid capital of EUR 2,500 is recommended market practice; funds are paid to the company's own bank account and no escrow account is required by law.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, by the founding shareholders of the Osaühing, that the incorporation under the Estonian Commercial Code is approved; that the subscription of the shares in accordance with the articles of association is ratified; that the registered office of the company is established at the address stated in the articles; that the management board comprising the nominated directors is appointed with power to represent the company; that the management board is authorized to file the registration application with the Business Register, to open bank accounts, and to execute all necessary filings; and that matters concerning the share capital, initial funding, and the commencement of the aircraft-engine leasing business are delegated to the management board. This resolution is effective immediately upon signing.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for a pure dry-leasing of turbine aircraft engines: Estonia imposes no industrial or operational license for asset-leasing activities under the Commercial Code. If the company later establishes an MRO, storage depot, or engine test cell facility, the relevant authorities would be the Estonian Transport Administration (Transpordiamet) and the Ministry of Climate.

Category: No operating license required. The lessor of an aircraft engine without crew is not an air operator and does not hold an Air Operator Certificate (AOC) under Regulation (EU) 2018/1139; operational certification responsibility remains with the lessee air carrier. No civil-aviation economic license applies to engine-only leasing in Estonia.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Eesti Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), Tallinn; electronic filing portal: e-MTA (https://e-mta.emta.ee); company registration portal: E-Business Centre (https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ): (1) incorporate via the Commercial Register in the e-Business Centre using an e-Residency digital ID or notary; registration is usually completed within 3-5 working days. (2) MTA automatically allocates the statutory national tax number, the 8-digit Commercial Register code (registrikood), which serves as the universal tax identification number for corporate income, payroll, and excise; no separate TIN certificate is issued. (3) If actual or expected taxable turnover reaches EUR 40,000 in a calendar year, or earlier on a voluntary basis, file the VAT registration application in e-MTA; MTA issues the EE-prefixed VAT identification number (EE plus 9 digits) and activates the VAT return obligation. (4) A non-resident entity without an Estonian establishment that performs a taxable supply in Estonia must register for VAT before the first supply (the domestic threshold is not available) and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative; after registration all returns and communications are digitally signed via e-MTA using e-Residency or Mobile-ID.

Fringe benefits, gifts, write-offs of non-business expenses and other deemed distributions are taxed immediately at entity level. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Estonian distribution-based corporate income tax regime: retained and reinvested profits are subject to 0% corporate income tax until distribution; regular profit distributions are taxed at 20/80 (20% of the gross distributed amount), qualifying recurring distributions may use the reduced 14/86 rate, and non-regular distributions bear the higher rate applied from 1 January 2025 (22% of gross). Fringe benefits, gifts, write-offs of non-business expenses and other deemed distributions are taxed immediately at entity level. The OÜ is not a pass-through entity; corporate income tax on distributions is withheld and declared to MTA on form TSD.

Available election forms

Estonian law does not provide a check-the-box election; entity-level taxation of the OÜ is statutory. Relevant mechanisms: (1) reduced 14/86 corporate income tax election for regular profit distributions under Income Tax Act Section 50(2), declared on form TSD; (2) voluntary VAT registration election before the EUR 40,000 turnover threshold; (3) Union/Non-Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for distance sales and certain cross-border B2C services; (4) application to the MTA Large Taxpayers' Office for an advance ruling (eelotsus) on place-of-supply and zero-rating of aircraft engine dry-leasing revenue; (5) hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are mandatory, but the taxpayer may elect the alignment of accounting-period treatment where the mismatch is not resolved by the primary rule; any such election must be supported by contemporaneous documentation in the annual tax file.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 net annual taxable turnover of domestic supplies (VAT Act Section 3(4)); voluntary registration is available below the threshold; non-resident taxable persons must register before the first taxable domestic transaction without any threshold; intra-EU distance-selling registration threshold is EUR 10,000 under the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: 0% VAT applies where goods are dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU, provided the supplier holds proof that the goods left EU territory within 90 days of dispatch (VAT Act Section 37). Offshore services: aircraft engine dry leasing is treated as a B2B equipment lease, not short-term hiring of a means of transport; therefore the place of supply is the customer's place of establishment (VAT Act Section 12, implementing Article 44 VAT Directive). If the lessee is established outside the EU, the service is outside Estonia and reportable at 0%; if the lessee is established in another EU Member State, the supply is reverse-charged to the lessee and shown in Estonia as a nil/zero-rated intra-Community supply. The lessor must hold a written lease agreement, proof of lessee establishment (VIES validation for EU lessees, official registration extract for non-EU lessees), an invoice referencing the applicable zero-rating or reverse-charge rule, and bank payment evidence; the engine's operational base must be outside Estonia for the lease period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD / ECS messaging) with MTA exit confirmation, required where the aircraft engine is physically exported from the EU
  • Bill of Lading and/or Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note, proving movement of the engine to a destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / matched SWIFT MT103 bank credit advice evidencing lease consideration repatriated to the Estonian OÜ within the contractual payment schedule
  • Executed dry-lease agreement (Aircraft Engine Master Lease) identifying the lessee, its registered seat, engine serial number, lease term, and operational jurisdiction
  • Commercial VAT invoice satisfying Article 226 VAT Directive content requirements, with express reference to the applicable zero-rating or reverse-charge provision of the Estonian VAT Act
  • Proof of lessee establishment: VIES validation report for EU lessees; certificate of incorporation or official trade register extract for non-EU lessees
  • Engine operator or registry movement log confirming that the engine remained stationed and operated outside Estonia during the zero-rated lease period
  • Escrow agent and banking records corroborating the payment rails (Swift_Wire / Escrow_Account) and the beneficial ownership of the paid consideration

Estonia has mandated electronic invoicing for B2G transactions since July 2019, using the national e-Arve framework and Peppol network; public-sector buyers must receive structured e-invoices. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary but market-standard in XML/UBL format. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime; however, VAT returns (form KMD) and the supporting sales list (KMD INF) must be filed electronically through e-MTA, including via public REST API endpoints, and all VAT invoices must satisfy the EU minimum content requirements and be retained for seven years.

MTA audits export and offshore-service zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs data, place-of-supply evidence, VAT returns, and bank flows. Defective documentation triggers retrospective recharacterisation of the supply as taxable at 22% for all periods still open; the taxation assessment period is three years from the end of the relevant tax year, extended to six years where returns are incomplete or conceal material facts (Taxation Act Section 95(4)). Reassessments carry statutory late-payment interest and, for negligent conduct, additional penalty interest. Deliberate forgery of export or offshore-service documentation is prosecuted as criminal tax fraud under Penal Code Section 388 (maksupettus), punishable by fines, confiscation of proceeds, and imprisonment of up to five years for aggravated offences; directors may be disqualified, VAT registration may be cancelled, claw-back assessments can be issued from the first affected period, and the MTA may require security or bank guarantees for future filing compliance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has no foreign-exchange control statute. As a euro-area member it maintains full current and capital account convertibility under TFEU Article 63, the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and the euro-area legal framework. There are no convertibility certificates, no compulsory channels through authorised dealers, no exchange-rate band, and no restriction on profit or capital repatriation. The only material hurdle to profit remittance is fiscal rather than FX: distributions are subject to the Estonian dividend/CIT regime (standard 20/80; reduced 14/86 for qualified regular distributions within the three-year averaging frame), while qualifying EU-resident corporate parents may access the Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, and shareholder-loan repayments are tax-free. An aircraft-engine dry-leasing OÜ also benefits from the Estonian zero-percent reinvested-profit regime (no CIT on undistributed retained earnings), leaving an effectively unconstrained convertibility position for USD lease receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement exists under Estonian or euro-area law. Export and lease proceeds may be retained in any currency in any account worldwide; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no statutory sales deadline, and no obligation to repatriate proceeds to Estonia. The only compliance duty is statistical: resident persons' balance-of-payments and capital-flow data are collected by Eesti Pank through banks on a monthly basis under Regulation (EU) No 184/2005; this is a data-reporting obligation and does not constitute exchange control authorisation.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), central bank and Eurosystem member; since 1 January 2025 the former Finantsinspektsioon (Financial Supervision Authority) is fully integrated into Eesti Pank as the unified prudeential supervisor. Monetary and foreign-exchange policy are set by the European Central Bank for the euro area, with Eesti Pank executing euro-area operations, operating TARGET2-Eesti settlement, publishing balance-of-payments statistics, and supervising credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions under national and EU law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to moderate. An EU/EEA-authorised e-money institution may legally provide SEPA/EUR payment accounts for operational expenses, and the estonian e-Residency programme facilitates remote onboarding; however, EMIs cannot grant credit, cannot hold a true demand deposit, cannot issue commercial letters of credit or demand guarantees, and, after the Estonian supervisory cleanup of the EMI sector (post-2019), no EMI offers a reliable USD correspondent/Fedwire channel for aircraft-scale lease receipts. EMIs are therefore unsuitable for capital escrow or USD lease proceeds; a fully licensed Estonian credit institution is the mandated principal treasury bank for this SPV.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA credit transfer) from the parent/shareholder to the OÜ operating account, with the Eesti Pank/ECB balance-of-payments statistical purpose code for direct investment equity capital (category Direct Investment - Equity Capital; selected in the bank payment interface as equity contribution / capital injection or share-capital increase under the Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik) as applicable). The supporting file comprises the certified shareholders resolution, an updated share register, the bank credit advice, and, for a formal share-capital increase, a notarially authenticated registration application filed with the Commercial Register within the statutory period. Funds are immediately available for treasury deployment; no conversion, surrender, or Eesti Pank approval is required because euro-area capital movements are fully liberalised.All significant outbound settlements run over SWIFT: MT103 for customer credit transfers (lease-rent onward payments, maintenance-reserve remittances, supplier payments) and MT202/MT202COV for large-value and cover payments over the correspondent network. EUR payments clear through SEPA and TARGET2-Eesti (RTGS, 10 p.m. cut-off); USD payments clear through the banks US correspondent via Fedwire/CHIPS; GBP through CHAPS. For engine purchase and finance-lease variations, documentary trade rails are available: commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707) and demand guarantees / standby letters of credit (MT760) issued in favour of sellers or MROs. In the core dry-leasing business, documentary credits remain the exception, with clean SWIFT transfers and the escrow rail serving as the standard clearing mechanism.Outright deliverable USD/EUR forward contracts, standard corporate FX product offered by Estonian and international banks and subject to EMIR clearing, margining, and trade-reporting rules.USD/EUR FX swaps for managing short-term liquidity mismatches between USD lease receipts and EUR operating expenses.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EUR periodic interest and principal exchange) to hedge long-term USD-denominated lease receivables against the EUR-functional OÜ.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in currencies without deliverable local markets (for example, certain African and Asian lease-rent jurisdictions), where permitted by counterparty-credit policy and ESMA/MiFIR reporting obligations.Interest-rate swaps (fixed-to-floating) as a treasury adjunct for floating-rate engine-finance debt, permissible for a non-financial counterparty under EMIR with appropriate reporting or clearing exemptions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to moderate. An EU/EEA-authorised e-money institution may legally provide SEPA/EUR payment accounts for operational expenses, and the estonian e-Residency programme facilitates remote onboarding; however, EMIs cannot grant credit, cannot hold a true demand deposit, cannot issue commercial letters of credit or demand guarantees, and, after the Estonian supervisory cleanup of the EMI sector (post-2019), no EMI offers a reliable USD correspondent/Fedwire channel for aircraft-scale lease receipts. EMIs are therefore unsuitable for capital escrow or USD lease proceeds; a fully licensed Estonian credit institution is the mandated principal treasury bank for this SPV.

0% on retained profits; 20% grossed-up corporate income tax on profit distributions (effective 25% on net cash dividend); reduced 14% rate for regular dividends to Estonian resident natural persons

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory local TP documentation (Local File); Master File/CbC Report required for multinational groups with consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 750 million

Economic substance scope: No dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in Estonia, but general anti-abuse rules under the Tax Procedure Act, commercial substance requirements for e-Residency OÜs, and judicial substance-over-form doctrines apply; an aircraft engine lessor OÜ must demonstrate genuine management, staff or outsourced functions, physical presence in Estonia, and substantive decision-making to maintain tax residence and treaty access

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment plus 0.06% daily interest; administrative fine up to EUR 32,000; criminal tax fraud conviction punishable by fine and/or imprisonment up to 5 years under Estonian Penal Code

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate income tax and penalties if found to have knowingly participated; civil recovery claims by the tax authority; travel bans and asset freezes under collection proceedings; disqualification from serving as director
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of tax residence to the shareholder's jurisdiction under international skeleton-company rules; denial of treaty benefits; VAT registration cancellation; corporate dissolution proceedings in the Estonian Commercial Register; fines up to EUR 32,000 for false filings

Director personal liability: Directors face strict liability for incorrect registry declarations; criminal liability for false statements may result in imprisonment up to 3 years; personal liability for taxes of the entity if the court pierces the corporate veil
AML / Economic Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative AML penalties up to EUR 400,000 or 10% of annual turnover under Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; additional sanctions under EU financial embargoes may include asset freezing and contract nullity

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and compliance officers are personally exposed to criminal prosecution; confiscation of illicit proceeds; travel bans; prohibition from holding management positions in credit institutions and regulated financial businesses
