Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registry Department of Tartu County Court (Tartu Maakohtu registriosakond), operating through the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister) portal administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK) under the Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), consolidated text RT I 1995, 26, 355, Chapter 5, Sections 135-173 (Private Limited Company, Osaühing), including Sections 137 (registration), 140 (share capital) and 144 (management board); State Fees Act (Riigilõivuseadus), Section 21; General Part of the Civil Code Act (legal persons).

Formation / constitutional filings

Registration application for entry of a private limited company (kandeavaldus osaühingu asutamiseks) submitted as an electronically signed filing through the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister); notarial authentication is waived when the mandatory standard articles of association are used.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The standard statutory minimum share capital of an Osaühing is EUR 2,500 under Section 140 of the Commercial Code; however, since the 1 February 2011 reform, incorporation is permitted with zero paid-up capital (EUR 0) using the registry's standard articles. No escrow or notarial deposit account is required: contributions, if any, are paid into the OÜ's own bank account before or at registration and the management board certifies the receipt in the registration application. Where share capital remains below EUR 2,500, profit distributions and shareholder credits are restricted until net assets reach the statutory threshold.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense certificate, structural load inspection or workplace activity clearance is required for an intangible publishing business with no production machinery and no public access. If employees are engaged, the Occupational Health and Safety Act requires a written workplace risk assessment and access to occupational health services. If a serviced office or co-working space is used, the operator must hold fire-safety compliance documentation under the Structural Fire Safety Act, and the OÜ should obtain a copy of the building's fire-safety certificate for its governance records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet; e-MTA online portal at https://emta.ee; eesti.ee business service portal)

E-government filing portal

An Osaühing (OÜ) is first registered in the Estonian Commercial Register through the e-business register and receives a registry code that also serves as its tax identification number. For VAT, the OÜ must register as a taxable person via the e-MTA portal before making taxable supplies. A non-resident entity with Estonian taxable supplies may apply for a non-resident VAT registration number through e-MTA; alternatively, it may use the EU One Stop Shop (OSS) for distance sales and electronically supplied services without obtaining a separate Estonian VAT ID.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An Osaühing (OÜ) is first registered in the Estonian Commercial Register through the e-business register and receives a registry code that also serves as its tax identification number. For VAT, the OÜ must register as a taxable person via the e-MTA portal before making taxable supplies. A non-resident entity with Estonian taxable supplies may apply for a non-resident VAT registration number through e-MTA; alternatively, it may use the EU One Stop Shop (OSS) for distance sales and electronically supplied services without obtaining a separate Estonian VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists for an OÜ; the entity is automatically treated as an entity-level taxpayer under the Estonian corporate income tax system, which defers taxation until profit distribution. Estonian tax law does not provide transparent-entity elections for an OÜ. Optional VAT group registration is available for Estonian-established taxable persons. Hybrid mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive apply automatically; no elective hybrid mismatch mechanism is available domestically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover for Estonian-established taxable persons; €10,000 EU-wide threshold for intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied services; non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Estonia generally have no registration threshold and must register or use OSS from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% export VAT rate if the title to the goods passes to a non-EU buyer and the goods are physically exported from Estonia to outside the EU, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration, transport documents, and receipt of export proceeds. Services qualify for 0% only when directly connected to exported goods or international transport; cross-border B2B digital webtoon syndication services are generally placed outside Estonia under place-of-supply rules, and B2C electronic services to overseas consumers are taxed in the consumer's destination country, with OSS or local registration requirements instead of an Estonian 0% rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ECS) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (CMR, air waybill, or sea waybill)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying webtoon syndication or redistribution contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment proof evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction-control regime is mandated in Estonia. Electronic invoicing is mandatory only for invoices issued to Estonian public-sector entities under the EU EN 16931 standard. VAT returns, OSS returns, and e-MTA filings use secure web portals; B2B and B2C e-invoicing is permitted but not generally mandatory.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may deny export zero-rating where documentary evidence is incomplete, late, or inconsistent. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating is clawed back at the standard 22% VAT rate, plus statutory late-payment interest of 0.06% per day calculated from the original return deadline. Deliberate falsification of export proof constitutes tax fraud, punishable by fines up to the amount of the unpaid tax and, in serious cases, criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code including imprisonment of up to three years. The tax assessment and correction period is generally three years, extended where fraud or gross negligence is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro (EUR), and there are no exchange controls on current account transactions. Capital account convertibility is fully liberalized under EU law and Estonian Foreign Exchange Act, subject only to general EU financial sanctions and statistical reporting obligations. Profit remittances may be freely transferred without any FX approval; the only practical hurdle is the distributed profits tax (20% or reduced 14% if regular distributions for certain periods) which is withheld by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA) at the time of distribution, and bank verification of corporate benefit and tax residence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements. All export receipts can be retained in any currency. However, for balance of payments statistics, resident companies must report cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction to Eesti Pank via electronic reporting systems (e.g., TSD / LEI reporting). In practice, for continuous webtoon subscription revenue, institutional reporting is filed monthly if aggregated flows exceed materiality thresholds, but no conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem and subordinate to the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and financial stability functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for auxiliary payment services using EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or Monese, but with strict legal constraints: (i) the EMI must hold an EEA passport into Estonia; (ii) the EMI cannot open a conventional IBAN based on an Estonian sort code if the parent account is a segregated omnibus account; (iii) capital injection and profit distribution must ultimately settle through a full license credit institution (A-kategooria) to satisfy MTA obligations and to obtain tax residency statements; (iv) the corporate account in an EMI is often flagged as high-risk for e-Residency companies under batch onboarding, requiring enhanced due diligence and documentary proof of substantive commercial activity (e.g., original webtoon licensing agreements, website traffic reports, VAT registration certificate).

Revolut Fit: High

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The wire reference must clearly state the purpose code 'S0101 – Equity capital issued to residents' (pursuant to ECB BPM 6 / Estonian BOPS reporting) and include a narrative 'Share capital contribution for EE_OU'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained profits; 20% on distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (no withholding tax on dividends; corporate income tax is levied on the distributing company at 20% or 14% as applicable)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 50,000 EUR (goods) / 20,000 EUR (services) – mandatory Local File and Master File required if the annual value of controlled transactions exceeds these thresholds. For financial transactions, the threshold is 10,000 EUR.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to offshore jurisdictions. However, general substance requirements apply: the company must have real economic activity (e.g., office, employees, decision-making) to benefit from treaty provisions and to avoid being classified as a shell company. The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may challenge the substance of a company if it lacks genuine business purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €1,000,000 for the company; additional tax assessment of up to 100% of the evaded tax. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to 5 years for responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties if they knowingly participated in the evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years are possible.
Shell Company (Lack of Economic Substance)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 20% corporate income tax on deemed distributions. Fines up to €32,000 for non-compliance with substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if the company is deemed a sham. They may face civil restitution, travel bans, and potential criminal charges for fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1,000,000 for the company; suspension of business license; criminal prosecution for money laundering with imprisonment up to 8 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML breaches, including fines and imprisonment. They may also face travel bans and asset freezes.
