Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum paid-up capital for a standard LLC is EGP 50,000, fully subscribed and deposited in a blocked capital escrow account at a licensed Egyptian bank before incorporation (Executive Regulations of Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, Decree No. 6 of 1982); an LLC registering as a micro or small enterprise under Law No. 152 of 2020 may adopt a reduced capital floor of EGP 1,000. The escrow account is released to the company only upon presentation of the Commercial Registration Certificate, and the deposit certificate must evidence each subscriber's pro-rata contribution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official national portals: https://www.eta.gov.eg (general e-services), https://eservices.eta.gov.eg and https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg (e-invoice/e-receipt clearance).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting a Tax Registration Form to ETA together with certified copy of the commercial registry extract, LLC Articles of Association and memorandum signed and notarized, proof of approved tax address, legalized parent-company certificate of incorporation if ultimate beneficial owner is foreign, power of attorney for the resident tax representative, and bank account details. ETA issues an Income Tax/VAT unified identifier; after initial registration the taxpayer must enroll on the ETA eVAT portal, obtain a certified digital signature, and if commercially active in B2B sales enroll separately on the e-invoicing clearance platform.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available; an Egyptian LLC is per se a juridical person subject to income tax on worldwide taxable results and VAT as a fully taxable person. For VAT, a person whose annual taxable turnover is between EGP 250,000 and EGP 500,000 may elect voluntary registration; mandatory registration applies above EGP 500,000. The company may elect to file consolidated VAT refunds for exported goods; no hybrid mismatch or group taxation elections are applicable to an LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies (exclusive of VAT) for mandatory registration; EGP 250,000 minimum annual turnover for voluntary VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export of tangible goods provided the goods are sold by a VAT-registered Egyptian LLC to a foreign buyer, physically delivered to a foreign territory, and leave Egypt via a customs export declaration. Services that are economically related to exported goods (e.g., handling, freight insurance to foreign customers) and certain offshore services performed entirely outside Egypt for a non-resident recipient may also qualify where legal title and benefit accrue to the foreign purchaser. The 0% rate is only applied where adequate export documentation is retained and export foreign-currency proceeds are repatriated to Egypt within the Central Bank of Egypt deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Customs Bayan) stamped by the relevant Egyptian customs authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Egypt
  • Inward Foreign Currency Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Egypt enforces mandatory real-time fiscal clearance for B2B e-invoices and B2C e-receipts through the ETA platform. The taxpayer must push invoice XML generated from ETA-compliant software to the API; ETA validates the invoice hash, unique code, seller/buyer VAT registration and totals, then returns an acceptance or reports the invoice in the issuer's Inbox; if accepted, the system digitally stamps an ETA QR code and unique identifier that must be printed on the final invoice. Non-clearance before issuance is treated as a non-compliant invoice. Phased implementation has made e-invoicing obligatory for nearly all commercial taxpayers, and penalties are imposed for late adoption, rejection outliers, and failure to report corrective credit/debit notes.

ETA and Egyptian Customs conduct post-export audits of zero-rated transactions. If export evidence is incomplete, incorrect, forged, or proceeds are not repatriated within the regulatory deadline, the zero rating is retrospectively disallowed and VAT plus statutory late-payment interest is assessed. Violations attract monetary penalties ranging from EGP 2,000 to EGP 10,000 per invoice under VAT procedural rules, penalty percentages equal to 50% to 100% of tax evaded for misdeclaration, and repeated fraudulent export claims can result in imprisonment under VAT anti-evasion provisions and denial of refunds for future periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is fully convertible for bona fide current account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, and profit remittances, provided underlying documents are presented. Capital account transactions are restricted and require CBE approval or registration; profit remittance is allowed through authorized banks after audited accounts, CBE repatriation clearance, and tax clearance certificates. Non-resident shareholders may convert EGP profits freely to foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system, subject to anti-money laundering and transfer pricing documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt through an authorized bank within 90 days from the date of shipment, with a mandatory reconversion of 100% of the foreign currency export receipt into Egyptian pounds at the prevailing market rate, unless a general or specific CBE exemption is obtained. For low-risk manufactured goods such as silicone fidget toys, exporters must present the final customs export declaration, invoice, packing list, and proof of shipment to the settlement bank to trigger the surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under the Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE Foreign Exchange Rules (2017, as amended)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI or fintech payment service providers cannot directly hold EGP deposits or execute domestic Egyptian clearing without a CBE-licensed payment institution or a partnership with an authorized local bank. CBE requires physical incorporation and licensing in Egypt; non-bank EMIs may only facilitate inbound/outbound transfers via a local agent bank, making full treasury autonomy via offshore EMI legally unpracticable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound FDI capital is wired via SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's account to the Egyptian LLC's EGP operational account through an authorized local bank. The payment must be described as 'Share Capital Increase' or 'FDI Contribution' and cryptographically referenced to the Egyptian Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. The receiving bank files the FDI notification with CBE, verifies the issuance of new equity, and registers the capital inflow under the CBE's electronic registration system. Supporting documents: board resolution, notarized amendment to the Articles of Association, shareholders' resolution, and the investment certificate issued by the General Authority for Investment (GAFI).Trade disbursements for raw materials and production inputs must settle via SWIFT MT103 (direct payment) or an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) issued through the local Egyptian bank. For safe-clearing, the bank will require the underlying sales contract, customs approvals, and usage confirmation consistent with the CBE's foreign exchange budget. Mature n-tier clearing may use a CBE RTGS transfer for same-day EGP leg, while foreign currency leg clears via correspondent bank MT103/202; exports to overseas buyers are typically collected via T/T advance payment or at-sight documentary collection to mitigate receivable risk.Plain vanilla FX forwards executed with CBE-authorized banks for up to 12-month tenors against firm and committed trade flowsNDFs (non-deliverable forwards) referencing the USD/EGP exchange rate for offshore entities when counterparties cannot physically deliver EGPCross-currency swaps for capital repatriation and profit remittance planning when both legs are allowed by CBECurrency options or option-like structures only on a case-by-case basis with a CBE-authorized local bank

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 aggregate related-party transaction value per year, or where related-party transactions account for more than 50% of gross revenue

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations framework to ordinary LLCs, but the Tax Law empowers the ETA to disregard artificial transactions and re-characterize income under anti-abuse provisions. A silicone fidget export affiliate must maintain real staff, local premises, procurement role and risk management in Egypt to support profit recognition and treaty/zone benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Income Suppression

Statutory sanction: Additional tax at 22.5% on the concealed amount, monthly 2% late-payment surcharge, criminal fine of EGP 20,000–500,000, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and surcharges; Egyptian tax courts may issue travel bans and asset freezes in parallel with criminal proceedings.
Shell Company and Sham Corporate Structure

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the UAE entity's profits in Egypt, denial of treaty relief on distributions, additional taxable base, and 100% penalty on under-declared profit under anti-abuse rules.

Director personal liability: The veil of incorporation may be pierced, making the foreign parent and board jointly liable for all Egyptian tax liabilities, VAT, WHT and contribution penalties.
AML and Cash Settlement Evasion

Statutory sanction: Violation of Egypt's anti-money-laundering regime (Law No. 80/2002) may lead to imprisonment of 3–7 years, fines of EGP 50,000–5 million, and confiscation of the shipped goods and associated bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be indicted personally; the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit may issue travel bans and revoke the company's Egyptian import/export registration.
Transfer Pricing Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Secondary TP adjustment by the ETA using TNMM or CUP, additional tax at 22.5%, monthly surcharge, and a separate penalty of up to 20% of the TP adjustment.

Director personal liability: The tax director and approving CFO are jointly and severally liable for the TP adjustment; their personal passports may be held by the Egyptian courts pending full restitution.
