Competent corporate registry

Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK / Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus) operating the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister / Äriregister), Estonia; notarial functions are optional because digital signing is accepted under the Digital Signatures Act.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), Chapter 2 — Sections 135–154 (general provisions on the private limited company OÜ), especially §135 (definition), §136 (share capital), §137–§142 (formation and registration), and §315–§323 (management board duties); supplemented by the Regulation of the Commercial Register and the e-Business Register Act (Äriregistri seadus).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Entry in the Commercial Register (Äriregistri registreerimisavaldus) containing the Foundation Deed / Resolution of Foundation (asutamisotsus) and the Articles of Association (põhikiri), submitted electronically via the e-Business Register portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 9 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for Osaühing has applied since 1 February 2011 (ÄS §136(6)); the shareholders may fix the share capital at any positive amount, commonly €2,500, but if it is set below €2,500 the company must observe the net-assets and dividend-distribution restrictions in §176(2¹) of the Commercial Code. The monetary contribution must be paid into a pre-registration blocked (escrow) bank account opened in the name of the OÜ, and the bank's confirmation of receipt must be attached to the registration application before the entry is made.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and structural clearance from the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) for the registered office, including a fire safety risk assessment, functioning fire alarm and evacuation plan, and a compliance statement on the occupancy load; occupational safety clearance is obtained through the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) with an internal control (OH&S) procedure, and, where trial staff handle human biological samples or visit trial sites, a workplace health-risk assessment and, if required, occupational health surveillance certificate from an appointed occupational health physician.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for Osaühing has applied since 1 February 2011 (ÄS §136(6)); the shareholders may fix the share capital at any positive amount, commonly €2,500, but if it is set below €2,500 the company must observe the net-assets and dividend-distribution restrictions in §176(2¹) of the Commercial Code. The monetary contribution must be paid into a pre-registration blocked (escrow) bank account opened in the name of the OÜ, and the bank's confirmation of receipt must be attached to the registration application before the entry is made.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet), portal: e-MTA (emta.ee/e-MTA)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Osaühing (OÜ) must first register in the Estonian Commercial Register via the e-Äriregister (e-Business Register) using an e-Residency digital ID. The Commercial Register automatically assigns the Estonian legal entity registry code, which serves as the tax identification number (TIN). The entity then completes tax registration with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board through e-MTA to activate VAT liability, employer registration, and excise/customs profiles. VAT identification (KMKR number) is applied for separately when taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold or when voluntary registration is required for cross-border transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; retained profits are not taxed until distribution (deferred corporate income tax system), while distributed profits and deemed distributions are subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the entity level; nonetheless, the OÜ retains full limited liability and is a separate taxpayer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; retained profits are not taxed until distribution (deferred corporate income tax system), while distributed profits and deemed distributions are subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the entity level; nonetheless, the OÜ retains full limited liability and is a separate taxpayer. Territorial scope applies only to distributions and certain deemed benefits under §50 of the Estonian Income Tax Act.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Estonian law. The principal statutory election is the timing and character of profit distribution, which determines the applicable corporate income tax withholding rate (regular 20/80 gross method, 14/86 qualifying regular dividends, and 25/75 irregular distributions). There is no hybrid mismatch election available; Estonia has implemented ATAD anti-hybrid rules, and treaty misalignment is corrected through mandatory disallowance rather than elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 in taxable supplies under a rolling 12-month period, calculated per calendar month; foreign suppliers with no fixed establishment in Estonia must register for VAT when required by the place-of-supply rules or when acquiring/supplying goods subject to domestic reverse-charge and distance sales thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to intra-Community exports (goods dispatched to another EU Member State for their VAT purposes), third-country exports outside the EU/EEA, and international transport services. Offshore services supplied to non-resident businesses outside the EU qualify for zero-rating (or are outside the scope of Estonian VAT) when the recipient is established outside Estonia and the benefit is received abroad. Clinical trial CRO services contractually rendered to a foreign sponsor outside the EU, including data management and project oversight performed in Estonia, may be zero-rated under the general B2B place-of-supply rule, provided the sponsor is identified as a non-taxable customer not established in Estonia or a taxable person in another jurisdiction and documentary evidence confirms the recipient's foreign status.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EAD) with electronic EXS movement status
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Consignment Note (CMR)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT proof of foreign-currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and executed clinical trial service contract
  • Sponsor's foreign VAT registration certificate or equivalent government-issued business registration proof
  • Certificate of origin / EUR.1 form when preferential origin is claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous control, or mandatory digital stamping regime applies in Estonia. VAT returns are submitted monthly (or quarterly for low-risk taxpayers) through e-MTA, and e-invoicing is mandatory for all invoices issued to Estonian public-sector entities using the PEPPOL/EN 16931 format. Private B2B cross-border electronic invoices are permitted but not pre-cleared by fiscal authorities; structured machine-readable VAT declaration data is submitted via XML/API interfaces to e-MTA.

Estonian Tax and Customs Board can retrospectively reassess zero-rated supplies and charge VAT at 22% plus statutory interest under the Tax Interest Act (currently 0.06% per day) for up to three additional years (five years in cases of intentional concealment). Misdeclaration penalties range from €200 to €6,400 for natural persons and can be increased for companies; deliberate VAT evasion or abuse of export zero-rating is subject to criminal liability and a fine of up to €1,600,000. Additionally, the authority can issue an anti-abuse VAT adjustment and claw back input VAT recoveries, applying aggravated interest where irregularity is linked to fraudulent cross-border clinical-trial invoicing chains.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, economically collected as 22/78 of the net amount distributed; retained and reinvested profits are generally not taxed until distribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% separate withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ under the post-2024 regime. The distributing company generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the net dividend or other taxable distribution. Transitional rules for dividends subject to the former 14% regime and 7% withholding tax must be checked for historical distributions and residual transitional cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD and relevant annexes are generally due by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the payment or taxable event occurred. The OÜ annual report is generally due within six months after the end of the financial year. Tax payments are generally due by the same statutory deadline as the relevant declaration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For the principal large-taxpayer documentation thresholds, Local File obligations generally apply where the Estonian entity or permanent establishment exceeds at least one relevant size threshold, commonly EUR 50 million annual turnover, EUR 43 million balance-sheet total, or an average of 250 employees, subject to the applicable Estonian transfer-pricing regulation. Master File obligations generally arise for an Estonian entity belonging to a multinational group whose consolidated group turnover meets or exceeds EUR 750 million, consistent with country-by-country reporting standards. Controlled transactions may still be reviewed or require arm's-length support below these thresholds, particularly for transactions with low-tax jurisdictions, material related-party financing, IP, management services, or restructuring.

Economic substance scope: Estonia applies transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, general anti-avoidance, permanent-establishment, controlled foreign company, interest limitation, and principal-purpose concepts. Estonia is not generally governed by a separate universal offshore Economic Substance Regulation comparable to certain zero-tax jurisdictions. A company must nevertheless maintain real commercial substance proportionate to its activities, including qualified personnel, decision-making, premises or operational capacity, risk control, accurate books, functional control over clinical-trial activities, and evidence supporting intercompany pricing. An e-residency card does not establish tax residence, management substance, or treaty entitlement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, statutory interest, recovery costs, administrative fines, and potential criminal liability. Serious tax evasion may lead to a criminal fine or imprisonment, with enhanced consequences for substantial damage, organized conduct, or repeated offences.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability where they personally participate, order, knowingly facilitate, or negligently permit unlawful conduct. Personal tax liability may arise for deliberate or grossly negligent failure to pay taxes where statutory conditions are met. Travel restrictions, asset seizure, and imprisonment are not automatic and require the applicable administrative or judicial process.
Undeclared Permanent Establishment and Payroll Exposure

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax or other source taxation, payroll tax assessments, social tax, interest, penalties, registration obligations, and potential reassessment of the foreign enterprise's taxable profit.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for knowingly maintaining inaccurate registrations, payroll records, or tax returns. Civil liability and criminal exposure depend on intent, materiality, and the applicable offence provisions.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length profit, corporate income tax on a deemed distribution or other taxable benefit, interest, penalties, documentation demands, and possible corresponding-adjustment disputes with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the company for loss caused by knowingly approving unsupported pricing or distributions. Criminal liability generally requires intentional participation in tax evasion, fraud, accounting falsification, or another offence.
Beneficial Ownership, Treaty Abuse, and Conduit Structures

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding-tax assessment, interest, penalties, anti-avoidance reassessment, and possible reporting or investigation consequences in Estonia and the source jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for knowingly providing false residence, ownership, or substance information. Asset-freezing or travel restrictions require a lawful investigation or court process; they do not arise merely from a denied treaty claim.
Illegal Profit Repatriation and Shareholder Loans

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax on deemed distribution or benefit, repayment claims, interest, accounting restatement, penalties, and possible insolvency or unlawful-distribution liability.

Director personal liability: Management-board members may be liable to the company, creditors, or shareholders for unlawful distributions and insolvency damage. Personal criminal liability can arise for intentional asset stripping, accounting offences, or fraudulent insolvency.
Clinical-Trial Regulatory Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory inspection, corrective orders, suspension or prohibition of activities, withdrawal or refusal of authorization, administrative fines, loss of sponsor or CRO contracts, and potential criminal liability for serious unlawful conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be personally liable for deliberate or negligent breaches of regulatory, patient-safety, data-protection, or accounting obligations. Imprisonment is reserved for applicable serious offences and requires the statutory criminal process.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, supervisory measures, transaction restrictions, reporting obligations, license or registration consequences, asset freezing, and criminal sanctions for money laundering or sanctions evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal administrative or criminal liability for knowing, intentional, or grossly negligent participation. Travel bans, detention, and imprisonment require competent-authority or judicial action under applicable law.
Accounting, Annual Report, and Record-Keeping Failure

Statutory sanction: Coercive fines, late-filing measures, tax assessment based on indirect evidence, administrative penalties, audit escalation, and possible criminal liability for intentional accounting falsification or document destruction.

Director personal liability: Management-board members have statutory duties to maintain accounting and submit reports. They may incur civil liability for company or creditor losses and criminal liability where falsification, concealment, or deliberate obstruction is proven.
Data Protection and Cross-Border Clinical Data Misuse

Statutory sanction: Supervisory orders, processing restrictions, corrective measures, GDPR administrative fines of up to EUR 20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher, and civil compensation claims.

Director personal liability: The company is ordinarily the primary GDPR fine recipient, but directors and responsible officers may face civil, employment, regulatory, or criminal consequences for personal misconduct, concealment, or obstruction. Imprisonment is not an automatic consequence of a GDPR infringement.
