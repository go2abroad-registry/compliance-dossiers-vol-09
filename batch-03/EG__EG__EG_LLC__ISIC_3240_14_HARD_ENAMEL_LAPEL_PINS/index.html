Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under Law No. 159 of 1981, the statutory minimum capital of an LLC is EGP 1,000, with the actual capital set by the founding members in the Memorandum of Association. The entire issued capital must be subscribed by the founders, and not less than 50% of the aggregate cash capital must be deposited into a temporary escrow bank account opened in the name of the company under incorporation. The bank deposit certificate accompanies the GAFI file, and the escrow balance converts to the corporate account immediately after the Certificate of Incorporation is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire-Safety Certificate from the Ministry of Interior confirming fire extinguishers, emergency exits, electrical grounding and egress pathways; structural load inspection certificate issued by a certified engineer validating the flooring and mezzanine against machine and storage loads; Ministry of Labor occupational health clearance with first-aid provisions and employee safety training records; all certificates renewed annually and presented to the Industrial Development Authority before the final industrial operating license is issued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-driven floating exchange-rate regime following the CBE's 6 March 2024 liberalization, under which the EGP/USD rate is determined by interbank supply and demand. Current-account transactions (export proceeds, import payments, service fees, royalties) are freely convertible for bona fide commercial purposes through CBE-licensed banks, subject to underlying document verification (commercial invoice, customs declaration Form 4, contract/PO). Capital-account transactions (foreign equity injection, FDI repatriation, shareholder loans, dividend remittances) are permitted but require strict documentary compliance: GAFI registration, audited financial statements, settlement of corporate tax liabilities, board/shareholder resolutions, and the CBE's economic-status codes. Profit remittance for an LLC is lawful but practically conditioned on obtaining a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority and a no-objection from the dealing bank. No dual exchange rate or generalized convertibility moratorium is currently in force; however, banks may sequence FX availability according to interbank liquidity, so treasury planning must maintain a 2–4-week buffer for EGP conversion of large USD amounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters must repatriate 100% of export proceeds through a CBE-licensed bank in Egypt within a statutory maximum of 4 months from the date of shipment for manufactured goods (hard enamel lapel pins under HS 7326/7117 or similar), extendable to a maximum of 8 months with the assigned bank's approval in cases of open-account receivables or verified buyer delays. As of the March 2024 exchange-rate unification, the earlier mandatory surrender/conversion ratios (50%–100% of export receipts converted to EGP) have been abolished; the LLC may retain 100% of export proceeds in USD or another convertible currency in its foreign-currency account, or convert any portion to EGP at the prevailing interbank rate at its own discretion. The dealing bank will match each incoming receipt against the customs export declaration and commercial invoice; failure to receive proceeds within the prescribed window must be explained in writing and may trigger CBE reporting, suspension of export financing facilities, or classification of the LLC as a non-performing exporter.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, the Foreign Exchange Regulations and related circulars issued by the CBE's Foreign Exchange and Bank Supervision Departments, in coordination with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMCLU).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The LLC's foreign shareholder must remit the capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 to the Capital Escrow Account (USD-denominated) or an EGP account opened at the chosen Egyptian bank. The remittance instruction must include: the LLC's exact registered name, the account IBAN, the bank's SWIFT/BIC, and the CBE-approved FDI purpose code (e.g., 'FDIEQ – Equity Investment / Subscription'). The bank will require the following to be presented or pre-uploaded: (i) the LLC's commercial registration and incorporation contract; (ii) a notarized shareholder resolution authorizing the injection; (iii) the GAFI investment notification/certificate under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; (iv) the CBE capital-inflow monitoring form; (v) the remitting shareholder's bank-reference letter and source-of-funds documentation; (vi) the LLC's current tax card. If the capital is injected in USD and local expenses require EGP, the conversion is executed at the interbank rate on the dealing bank's platform; no parallel market or cash deposit over EGP 250,000 (or equivalent) may be used without full evidenced-source paperwork. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% (Egyptian CIT Law 91/2005 as amended; general rate for limited liability companies)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividend distributions to non-residents (and resident shareholders) under Egypt Income Tax Law 91/2005, unless reduced by an applicable DTA (commonly 5%-15%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian Transfer Pricing Guidelines require a contemporaneous Local File where the annual aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds EGP 8,000,000; a Master File is additionally required for taxpayers belonging to MNE groups with consolidated group turnover above EGP 5,000,000,000 (or where the local file triggers a specific ETA notice). Transactions below EGP 8,000,000 still need an annual TP disclosure form and arm's-length benchmarking at the request of the ETA.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the ETA enforces substance-oriented anti-abuse rules: DTAs are applied only where the recipient has substantive business activity, real premises, personnel, and spontaneous beneficial-owner documentation. A pure shell holding or conduit outside Egypt is disregarded for treaty relief and may be ignored for corporate/TP purposes, causing the profits to be re-attributed to the Egyptian industrial permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through under-declared digital sales

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Law 91/2005 Art. 132: imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000, plus civil assessment of the full evaded tax, a 2% monthly delay fine and, in aggravated cases, an equalization surcharge of up to 100% of the tax defect.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and signatories who materially participated in the false filing are co-defendants; the ETA may obtain freezing orders on personal assets, travel bans, and police removal from office pending trial, and the liability tracker can continue against directors after liquidation.
Shell/conduit company and DTA abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty claim; reclassification of dividends to 10% domestic WHT (or, under substance-over-form theory, full 22.5% corporate tax on the profit distribution); addition of EGP 10,000-100,000 penalty for false statement in a taxpayer registration or return, and potential AML prosecution for asset hiding.

Director personal liability: Where corporate separateness is algorithmic sham, the court may pierce the veil and make the offshore beneficial owners and founding directors jointly liable for the underpaid Egyptian CIT and WHT, plus asset confiscation and denial of future business licenses; directors face a personal travel ban until the tax arrear is settled.
Manipulative transfer pricing and missing TP file

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer pricing adjustment: profits reallocated to the Egyptian LLC and taxed at 22.5%; plus 2% monthly delay surcharge, an administrative penalty of up to EGP 2 million for non-submission/ false TP documentation, and, in intentional cases, a fine equal to 100% of the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare/approve the TP file are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax if they knowingly relied on false intercompany agreements or failed to release the Egyptian company from non-arm's-length margins; civil penalization includes disqualification from serving as directors and seizure of director bank balances.
AML / financial crime arising from low-risk digital export collections

Statutory sanction: Violations of Egypt Law 80/2002 on Anti-Money Laundering: imprisonment from 2 to 7 years and fines from EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000; independent case under CIT where undeclared income is confiscated; bank accounts and merchant terminals are immediately suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and ultimate beneficial owners can be prosecuted individually; punishments include forfeiture of assets, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel bans, and director disqualification for up to 10 years; statutory auditors also face liability if they certified misleading financial statements.
Unlawful dividend remittance without WHT / treaty procedure

Statutory sanction: Company is treated as an unfaithful withholding agent: aggressive recovery of the full 10% WHT not withheld plus a fine of 200% of that amount, 2% monthly delay penalty, and denial of treaty benefits for future distributions; directors may be barred from foreign exchange release approvals.

Director personal liability: Corporate officers who order overseas remittances will be jointly and severally liable for the unremitted WHT; Egyptian authorities may issue administrative attachment against personal and real estate assets of directors and impose a ban on travel/discretionary foreign transfers until restitution.
