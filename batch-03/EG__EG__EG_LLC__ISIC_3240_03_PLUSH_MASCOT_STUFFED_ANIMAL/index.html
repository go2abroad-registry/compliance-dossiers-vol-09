Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on LLCs under Companies Law No. 159/1981; the founding capital is freely set by the shareholders and must be adequate for the corporate object. The founders must subscribe 100% of the share capital, pay at least the percentage required by the bylaws, and deposit those funds into the company's corporate bank account in Egypt; no escrow account is legally mandated for LLC formation.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense clearance from the Ministry of Interior: issuance of a Fire Safety Certificate after inspection of fire alarm and extinguishing systems, emergency exits, evacuation planning, and electrical load compliance. Structural load inspection by the municipality/IDA or certified engineering office verifies the building's capacity for industrial machinery. Occupational safety clearance from the Ministry of Manpower and Immigration: workplace risk assessment, personal protective equipment program, needle and ultrasonic machine guards, dust control, and first-aid provisions must be documented and approved prior to start of production.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official national portal: https://eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian Limited Liability Company must first complete commercial registry incorporation and obtain a commercial registration certificate, then file an income tax registration application with the competent ETA tax office within 30 days of commencement of business. A separate VAT registration application must be filed if annual taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold. The ETA issues a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) used for both income tax and VAT obligations; the LLC must also register in the electronic invoicing system and, if engaged in export transactions, obtain an importer/exporter card from the Egyptian General Organization for Export and Import Control.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Egyptian Limited Liability Company must first complete commercial registry incorporation and obtain a commercial registration certificate, then file an income tax registration application with the competent ETA tax office within 30 days of commencement of business. A separate VAT registration application must be filed if annual taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold. The ETA issues a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) used for both income tax and VAT obligations; the LLC must also register in the electronic invoicing system and, if engaged in export transactions, obtain an importer/exporter card from the Egyptian General Organization for Export and Import Control.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or OECD-style hybrid mismatch elections exist under Egyptian tax law. An Egyptian LLC is mandatorily treated as a separate taxable corporate entity on its worldwide income. Limited elections are available under Law No. 91 of 2005 and Law No. 67 of 2016, including the election to file consolidated tax returns for qualifying groups and the election to use the cash-basis method for small businesses meeting statutory thresholds; however, no pass-through classification is permitted for LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual turnover of taxable supplies; registration is mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the threshold, while persons with turnover above EGP 50,000 may apply for voluntary registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods physically leaving Egypt (including custom plush dolls) qualify for the 0% VAT rate provided the seller is a VAT-registered taxpayer, holds valid export documentation, retains proof of actual shipment, and receives the export proceeds through the Egyptian banking system. Offshore services rendered entirely outside Egypt to non-resident clients may also qualify for 0% if the benefit of the service is received outside Egypt and the exporter complies with documentary evidence requirements established by ETA.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Cargo X-Release proof)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (from a licensed Egyptian bank)
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • ETA-approved e-invoice with valid digital stamping
  • Certificate of origin (where required by the importing country)

All Egyptian VAT-registered taxpayers are subject to the mandatory e-invoicing regime administered by the Egyptian Tax Authority. ETA-certified cloud-based e-invoicing API integration is required; invoices must be transmitted in near-real-time using JSON/XML schemas, validated by the ETA system, stamped with a digital signature and QR code, then reported to the ETA. Non-compliance results in invoice rejection, blocking of clearance, and financial penalties.

Export zero-rating is subject to teeth-of-audit substantiation. If the ETA determines that the export did not actually occur, the documents were forged or mismatched, or the foreign currency proceeds were not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the VAT at 14% becomes immediately due, with late payment interest, a 100% penalty on the due tax in cases of fraudulent evasion, and possible criminal fines under Law No. 67 of 2016 and the Central Bank of Egypt foreign exchange regulations. Directors and shareholders in the exporting LLC may also be liable to customs and tax clawback administrative penalties plus blacklisting from the import/export registry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian current account is broadly convertible for merchandise trade, service payments, and profit remittances, with settlement through CBE-licensed banks at the interbank market rate. Capital account transactions remain restriction-heavy: inward equity and shareholder loans must be recorded with GAFI and effected through authorized dealers under the Investment Law No. 72/2017. Profit remittance is permitted for Egyptian LLCs with registered foreign ownership, subject to audited financials, tax clearance certificates, and CBE/Local Bank remittance validation. Offshore convertibility of the Egyptian pound remains incomplete; EGP is non-deliverable outside Egypt and is traded overnight in a limited offshore non-deliverable forward market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters must negotiate export proceeds in foreign currency through an authorized bank. Statutory repatriation applies to the full amount of the export invoice, with collection due within 180 days (approximately 6 months) from the bill of lading/customs export declaration date unless the CBE grants an extension for specific trade categories. Mandatory conversion to EGP is currently set at a minimum of 50% of the received foreign currency amount; the remaining balance may be retained in a foreign-currency operational account for bona fide working capital, import offset, or servicing of registered external debt, subject to CBE periodic circulars. Non-compliance attracts CBE penalties, bank de-risking, and delay in approval of subsequent FX transactions. Plush-toy OEM export receipts should be documented with customs approved manifests, packing lists, purchase orders, and settlement advice notes to substantiate the timing and amount of surrender.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – Financial Sector Supervision and Payment Systems Oversight; the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) supervises foreign direct investment registration and capital account filings.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low – Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMI/PSP opening is low under Egyptian Law No. 18 of 2019 regulating Payment Services Providers. An offshore EMI cannot lawfully provide multi-currency current accounts, FX conversion, or pound settlement without a CBE license and in-country presence as a PSP or a bonded partnership with a locally licensed bank. Foreign EMIs can serve only as auxiliary payout gateways if the treasury context remains bank-centrics; the governing KYC obligation belongs to the local bank and the CBE-regulated PSP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or shareholder-loan capital must be received by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the investing parent's bank into the Egyptian LLC's designated foreign-currency account held at a CBE-authorized dealer bank. The wire transfer narrative must clearly state 'Capital contribution under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 – LLC registration No. [EG commercial registration] – purpose code E-INV/EG 0123' (or the CBE-prescribed purpose code for direct investment issued at the time of submission). On receipt, the bank will request a currency declaration, the GAFI annual report, a certified board resolution, an account opening certificate, and a notarized power of attorney of the authorized signatory. The inflow will be credited to the capital account block and subsequently converted or re-invested only after CBE/GAFI reporting form is signed by the bank's senior FX officer. Inbound remittance should be made in USD or EUR, with EGP denominations not permitted from offshore sources.For plush-toy manufacturing, outbound payments for fabric, pellets, eye assemblies, embroidery threads, machinery spare parts, and logistics are cleared via SWIFT MT103 direct and semi-electronic channels used by CBE-authorized dealer banks. For larger consignment-based imports, a commercial letter of credit (at sight or deferred, possibly confirmed by an international bank) is the recommended rail. Every payment file must reference the import letter of credit, proforma commercial invoice, customs declaration, and warehousing report. Payments in EGP for local suppliers should be settled through the Egyptian Real-Time Gross Settlement System (RTGS, interbank facilities); foreign suppliers must not receive EGP outside Egypt. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard rate on taxable profits of resident juridical persons; non-resident branches pay 22.5% on Egyptian-source profits (Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended by Law No. 227 of 2022).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on cash or in-kind dividends distributed by unlisted Egyptian LLCs to resident and non-resident shareholders (Law No. 227 of 2022). Dividends from EGX-listed shares are exempt. The LLC, as paying entity, must withhold, remit, and file the withholding return by the statutory deadline; the 10% WHT is a final tax with no gross-up obligation on the LLC.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return is due within four (4) months after the fiscal year-end (Art. 88, Law No. 91 of 2005); a calendar-year LLC files by 30 April. Advance CIT installments fall due by the end of the 3rd, 6th, and 9th months of the current fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million in aggregate related-party transactions during the fiscal year triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are triggered for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EGP 10 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). The related-party disclosure form must be attached to the annual Model 19 regardless of quantum.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but substance is enforced indirectly: (a) tax residency depends on place of effective management (Art. 7, Law No. 91 of 2005); (b) the GAAR clause (Art. 38 as amended by Law No. 227 of 2022) permits recharacterization of arrangements without commercial rationale; (c) the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test applies where Egyptian treaty benefits are claimed. A plush-toy LLC with a physical manufacturing plant, local payroll, inventory, and board decisions taken in Egypt has sufficient substance. Conversely, a shell LLC whose only Egyptian contact is a mail-forwarding address and an appointed nominee will be disregarded and its profits re-attributed to the foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Records

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000 under Art. 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005, together with restitution of the evaded tax plus the 1.5% monthly late-payment surcharge and revocation of the tax card suspending customs and export privileges.

Director personal liability: Managers who sign a knowingly false Model 19 incur personal criminal liability and civil restitution; the ETA may obtain a travel ban and asset freeze against the managing director under the Tax Procedures Law No. 8 of 2022; disqualification from acting as a director of any Egyptian company follows conviction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under Art. 38 (Law No. 227 of 2022), denial of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, reassessment at 22.5% CIT plus 1.5% monthly surcharge, and monetary penalties for misuse of the corporate form; the license and commercial registration may be struck off.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent abuse of legal personality; directors must restore distributions made without a solvency basis under Companies Law No. 159 of 2020, and the Economic Court may order winding-up and impose travel bans where the tax debt exceeds the statutory collection threshold.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment by the ETA, additional CIT at 22.5% plus 1.5% monthly surcharge, separate statutory penalties for failure to submit transfer pricing documentation and the mandatory disclosure, and reversal of the burden of proof requiring the taxpayer to rebut the ETA's benchmarked margin.

Director personal liability: Where the transfer pricing scheme is found to be a deliberate fraudulent diversion of profits rather than a mere documentation error, the finance director and legal representative face personal criminal exposure under Art. 133, civil claims for the shortfall, exit bans, and seizure of personal assets.
AML Breach and Unreported Cash Movement

Statutory sanction: Fines for the company and compliance officer, imprisonment for responsible officers, extension of the tax audit window, CIT and VAT reassessments on the unreported turnover, and the 1.5% monthly delay surcharge; the Ministry of Finance may also suspend the company's VAT and import codes.

Director personal liability: The ultimate beneficial owner and executing directors are jointly liable; the ETA and the Anti-Money Laundering Unit may freeze bank accounts, order preventive travel bans, and pursue personal enrichment actions against managers who directed payments outside the banking system.
