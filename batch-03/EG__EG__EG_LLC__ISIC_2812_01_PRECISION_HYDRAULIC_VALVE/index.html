Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under which the Egyptian Pound (EGP) is accepted for current account transactions, including trade-related payments and profit remittances for foreign investors. Capital account transactions are subject to increased regulatory oversight, prior approvals, and documentary scrutiny, but foreign direct investment capital repatriation and profit remittance are generally permissible after the successful completion of the foreign-investment registration and compliance with the CBE's established formalities, including submission of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and original proof of offshore-funds injection. Profit remittance hurdles include withholding taxes, statutory documentation requirements, and the necessity of confirming that the underlying investment was registered with the CBE through the authorized banking channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters are required to repatriate 100% of export proceeds into Egypt within 4 months from the date of shipment, subject to a possible extension by the CBE under force majeure or exceptional circumstances. Once the hard-currency proceeds are received, the exporter may retain the foreign currency in an export proceeds account held with an Egyptian licensed bank; the historical statutory conversion ratio requiring 100% surrender into EGP has been replaced by a regime that allows exporters to hold, use, and fully dispose of the foreign currency for trade and debt settlement purposes, provided the repatriation deadline and the documentary submission requirements (bill of lading, commercial invoice, and bank credit advice) are met. Non-compliance can result in fines, closure of the exporter's bank facilities, and seizure of the unrepatriated proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and the Egyptian Exchange Control Administration acting under the CBE's delegated authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) for direct account opening on behalf of an Egyptian LLC. The CBE has reserved the authorization to operate payment and e-money services to Egyptian-licensed banks and licensed fintech companies; offshore EMIs may not hold Egyptian client funds or perform payment initiation without a local payment service provider (PSP) license. An offshore EMI may provide only settlement-channel consultancy or act as a software-only platform interoperable with a licensed Egyptian bank's API, but must not receive, hold, or route client deposit funds without the CBE's express authorization.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound wire transfers are to be executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a licensed Egyptian bank, using the CBE-approved purpose code for foreign-direct-investment equity or shareholder-loan proceeds. The sending bank must cite the underlying investment registration number issued by the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) in the remittance reference field, and the receiving Egyptian bank will require the upload of the joint-venture or investment registration certificate, the board-approved capital-call resolution, and the source-of-funds declaration. The Egyptian bank performs an inbound verification step and applies the exchange rate of the day if conversion into EGP is required for the operational escrow account.Outbound trade payments for imported raw materials or overseas services will be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for straightforward transfer-disbursements and via irrevocable documentary Confirmed or Unconfirmed Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) for high-value settlements or deferred-payment terms. Payments under LCs will require an L/C application form, a pro-forma invoice, a trade-contract certificate, and any necessary CBE trade-finance declarations; wire payment outflows are executed against the invoice, the customs declaration, and the quarterly corporate-tax filing, ensuring that the transaction is traceable and does not constitute an evasion of the repatriation rules.Plain vanilla FX forwards (deliverable) executed onshore with a CBE-licensed bankNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with no principal exchange, permitted only where the underlying commercial trade exposure is evidenced by invoicing and logistics documentsCross-currency interest-rate swaps (where both legs are executable with a licensed Egyptian bank for the purpose of liability management on foreign-currency trade loans)Currency over-the-counter options (European-style, permitted by the CBE for hedging commercial trade flows only, not for speculative treasury operations)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million of annual related-party transactions requires mandatory Local File preparation; Master File is generally mandatory when related-party cross-border transactions exceed EGP 30 million or net turnover exceeds EGP 60 million

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime as in UAE; however, Egypt-source profit attribution and treaty access require commercial substance, physical presence, full-risk supply chain participation, and board/management activities to be conducted in Egypt. Entities in the Suez Canal Economic Zone must maintain real employment and warehousing or manufacturing activity to retain incentive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for a term not less than 3 years and a fine equal to 3 to 5 times the tax evaded; the tax authority also imposes civil penalties equal to the evaded tax plus surcharges of 1.5% monthly pending payment

Director personal liability: Court order per Law 91 confirms that managers and directors who sign false returns are criminally liable; the corporate veil is lifted to recover the evaded tax from directors' personal assets and to issue asset freeze orders, travel bans, and confiscation orders
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty relief under general anti-abuse principles; reassessment of Egypt-source income at the full 22.5% corporate rate plus non-resident margin adjustments; penalties between 20% and 100% of the tax difference and cancellation of investment incentives

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve phantom share capital or artificial intercompany arrangements may be jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax under Article 70 of Law 91; judicial liquidation may be ordered, and directors may be subjected to personal liability orders and travel restrictions
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to EGP 50 million or three times the laundered amount, whichever is greater, under the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 and amendments; penalties include revocation of CIF/import-export licenses and confiscation of funds
