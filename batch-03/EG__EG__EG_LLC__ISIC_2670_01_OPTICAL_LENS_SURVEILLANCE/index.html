Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Law No. 159 of 1981, but the founding quota capital must be adequate for the stated manufacturing objective and fully subscribed with each cash contribution deposited in a temporary bank escrow account opened in the proposed company name, released only after issuance of the Commercial Registration Certificate; in-kind contributions must be supported by a certified auditor valuation and recorded in the notarized contract.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Authority (Ministry of Interior) fire safety clearance and operating fire certificate; municipality structural load inspection certificate for the production hall and annexes; Ministry of Manpower workplace occupational safety and health clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 including noise exposure assessment (8-hour TWA <= 85 dBA), machine guarding, eye protection for polishing lines, chemical hygiene for coating solvents, and first aid and emergency evacuation plans.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; unified taxpayer e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing/e-receipt fiscal clearance portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Procedure for an EG_LLC controlled by non-residents: after incorporation through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), the LLC performs one-time unified tax registration at the ETA portal. Steps: (1) file the notarized articles of association, electronic commercial-registry excerpt, investment licence and applicant identification; (2) appoint a local tax file administrator and grant a notarized power of attorney; (3) obtain the statutory unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) certificate and corporate income-tax registration simultaneously; (4) activate the ETA e-invoicing API credentials (client-ID, client secret, digital signature certificate) and e-receipt profile; (5) apply for VAT registration at initial registration, which is mandatory when aggregate taxable supplies reach EGP 500,000 within any 12-month period and is required on the first export transaction to benefit from zero-rating and input VAT refund. A local ETA-qualified tax representative is strongly recommended where non-resident directors do not travel to Egypt.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide a check-the-box election or a hybrid-mismatch pass-through regime; LLCs are taxable as separate juridical persons. Elections available to the optics-manufacturing LLC include: (i) voluntary early VAT and e-invoicing registration from the first export contract; (ii) consolidated corporate tax filing for affiliated Egyptian companies with at least 95% direct or indirect ownership under Law 38 of 2024; (iii) unilateral and bilateral advance pricing agreement (APA) options with the ETA; (iv) election of an approved foreign-currency translation method for export proceeds where consented to by the ETA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 aggregate taxable turnover of goods and services in any rolling 12-month period; registration is required at first export transaction even below the threshold to claim zero-rating and refunds

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies when (i) tangible goods, here precision optical lenses and camera modules, are physically exported from Egyptian customs territory, (ii) the supplier holds a valid customs export declaration, and (iii) export proceeds are received in convertible currency. International transport and auxiliary logistics services rendered in connection with the exported goods also qualify for 0%. Any domestic supply, installation or services consumed in Egypt remain taxable at 14%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (official C87/export manifest) stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority with the relevant HS code
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from Egyptian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank, matching the SWIFT credit advice with the exported invoice value
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and purchase order or letter of credit describing the optical goods
  • Certificate of origin and inspection conformity report where required by the destination market

All VAT-registered suppliers must issue e-invoices in JSON/XML through the ETA REST API and obtain real-time fiscal clearance before transmission to customers. The ETA returns a QR-coded fiscal identifier, rejects non-conforming invoices and maintains a transaction log cross-linked to customs declarations. Since 2023/2024, the e-receipt obligation has been phased in for all B2C sales. An uncleared invoice is treated as not issued: input VAT deduction is blocked for the buyer and per-invoice penalties apply.

The ETA validates zero-rating against the e-invoice register, customs CIB export records, shipping manifests and bank inward-transfer evidence. If the FOB value is not repatriated within 180 days or an ETA-approved extension, the transaction is retroactively re-characterized as taxable at 14%, with delay interest from the original due date. Fraudulent claims, sham declarations, circular FX transfers and overstated export values are punished by clawback of the zero-rated benefit for up to five prior years, fines between 100% and 200% of the evaded tax, suspension of the e-invoice clearance profile and TIN, bank-account attachment under Law 38 of 2024, and imprisonment of the responsible manager or signatories under the tax evasion provisions of the VAT law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP is accepted as current-account convertible under Egypt's IMF Article VIII obligations; capital-account transactions, including FDI equity injections and profit remittances, remain subject to CBE registration and licensed-bank intermediation. Egypt has unified the official exchange rate to close the parallel-market spread, but banks still apply internal FX liquidity controls. Dividend and profit remittances require: (i) CBE-registered direct investment in the capital records of the LLC, (ii) Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax clearance confirming payment of all corporate and dividend taxes, (iii) audited financial statements evidencing distributable reserves, and (iv) execution through an onshore licensed bank via SWIFT MT103. No standing blockage on profit transfers exists, but settlement windows, documentation reviews, and bank-level foreign-currency deferral mechanisms can delay outflows by several days.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE consolidated exchange-control instructions for non-oil exporters, the optical lens manufacturer must negotiate export collections through a licensed Egyptian bank and repatriate the full proceeds to its onshore foreign-exchange retention account within the earlier of the commercial credit period or a statutory maximum collection window currently capped at 4 months from the date of loading/negotiation (extendable to 270 days with bank-justified documentary support). The mandatory hard conversion ratio for export receipts into EGP is currently zero — exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency — provided that the bank receives, per shipment: the ACI/NAFEZA advance cargo notification, customs export declaration, bill of lading/airway bill, commercial invoice, and the FCReceipt/negotiation form. Failure to meet the repatriation timeline triggers automatic inclusion on the CBE exporter default list, suspension of import-financing facilities, and customs clearance restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Foreign Exchange Regulation Sector and Banking Supervision Sector; powers derived from Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 88 of 2003, as amended by Law No. 194 of 2020, and the Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for onshore settlement; MEDIUM-LOW for auxiliary offshore use. Legal constraints: CBE Law 194/2020 and Fintech Law 18/2019 require any entity providing payment, wallet, or remittance services in Egypt to hold a CBE fintech or payment-service license; therefore, offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, Payoneer, or Paysera cannot open EGP accounts, issue Egyptian IBANs, or participate in local clearing. They may be used only as off-cycle USD/EUR collection nodes outside Egypt, and funds so collected must be swept back to the Egyptian corporate account via licensed banks to satisfy CBE trade-file reporting and export-proceeds repatriation rules. Capital injections, export receipts, and supplier payments must never bypass the EGP banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity must be wired by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank account (third-party settlement and cash deposits are prohibited). Field 70 'Remittance Information' must state 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION — CAPITAL INCREASE — DIRECT INVESTMENT UNDER EGYPT LAW 72/2017' with the receiving bank mapping the transaction to the CBE balance-of-payments direct-investment code for equity capital. The funds are credited to a segregated Capital Escrow Account and verified against: (i) the LLC's notarized capital increase resolution, (ii) the GAFI-issued investment certificate, (iii) confirmation of the source-of-funds questionnaire, and (iv) the bank's cross-check with the CBE electronic investment registry. After verification, the bank issues a receipt-of-online-remittance certificate acceptable to the ETA and the Cairo notary public for updating the paid-in capital; funds may thereafter be converted to the CBE-approved parallel rate and transferred to the operating account only for documented project expenditures.Primary clearing rails for trade settlement: (1) SWIFT MT103 (TT/wire) for raw-material imports (optical glass blanks, chemical coatings, packaging) using CBE BoP trade-purpose code 5110; the Egyptian importer must hold a valid tax card, active commercial register, and the ACI/NAFEZA advance cargo registration number per shipment, and the bank marries the wire to the customs declaration before release.
