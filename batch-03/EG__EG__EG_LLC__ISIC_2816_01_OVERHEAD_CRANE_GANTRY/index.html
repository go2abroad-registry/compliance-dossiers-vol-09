Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; unified e-government tax portal 'Ingez' at https://tax.gov.eg; e-invoicing platform at https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) Notarize articles of association and obtain incorporation and commercial registration from the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI); (2) After commercial registration, file the unified tax registration application with the ETA via the Ingez portal or in-person service center; (3) Attach the formation contract, commercial register extract, passport or foreign corporate certificate of the shareholders, board resolution, and registered office lease; (4) ETA issues the unified Tax Card and the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) covering income tax, VAT, and stamp taxes; (5) Appoint a resident legal representative and a licensed tax accountant to act as tax domicile for filings and service of notices. Standard processing time is 5 to 10 business days after GAFI final approval.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) Notarize articles of association and obtain incorporation and commercial registration from the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI); (2) After commercial registration, file the unified tax registration application with the ETA via the Ingez portal or in-person service center; (3) Attach the formation contract, commercial register extract, passport or foreign corporate certificate of the shareholders, board resolution, and registered office lease; (4) ETA issues the unified Tax Card and the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) covering income tax, VAT, and stamp taxes; (5) Appoint a resident legal representative and a licensed tax accountant to act as tax domicile for filings and service of notices. Standard processing time is 5 to 10 business days after GAFI final approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Egyptian LLC is a non-transparent corporate taxpayer subject to corporate income tax at 22.5% under Law No. 91 of 2005; no pass-through or check-the-box election exists. Available elections: (a) consolidated CIT grouping for Egyptian companies with at least 90% common ownership (Article 60 of Law 91/2005, subject to ETA approval); (b) voluntary VAT registration at an annual turnover of EGP 250,000 below the mandatory EGP 500,000 threshold; (c) tax stabilization agreement under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 allowing fixation of tax rates and exemptions for up to 10 years; (d) actual-margin versus deemed-margin computation for any permanent establishment characterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: annual taxable supplies of goods and services equal to or exceeding EGP 500,000; voluntary registration available from EGP 250,000 annual turnover; threshold assessed on gross taxable turnover excluding exempt supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies under Article 19 of Law 67/2016 and Article 49 of the Executive Regulations to: (i) goods physically exported from Egypt under customs control by the VAT-registered exporter, including port container gantry and overhead cranes; (ii) transport, handling, and ancillary services directly connected to the export shipment; and (iii) services performed from Egypt for a non-resident wholly for use in the non-resident's business outside Egypt. The claim is available only to the registered exporter named as a consignor in the customs documentation; domestic supply, installation, and commissioning of machinery within Egypt remain taxable at 14%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document) duly released and stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch of the cranes and gantry equipment from an Egyptian port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank confirming receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Sales contract, final commercial invoice, and Central Bank of Egypt export registration form

Mandatory near-real-time fiscal reporting regime: all registered VAT payers must document B2B supplies through the ETA e-invoicing system (Ministerial Decree No. 113 of 2020) using certified billing software integrated to the ETA via REST API; XML or JSON invoice files are submitted to the central ETA platform, automatically validated against taxpayer master data and transactional business rules, and an acceptance certificate with a unique validation code and QR digital stamp is returned for printing and transmission to the buyer; invoices failing validation are rejected and the underlying sale is non-compliant. The e-receipt (RTC) system extends the same fiscal clearance mechanism to B2C sales. Non-integration or non-submission triggers blockage of the trader's e-invoicing portal access and substantial administrative penalties.

The ETA applies targeted audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims and cross-matches customs release data, bill of lading records, and bank remittance certificates. If the physical export is not completed or the proceeds are not received in and repatriated through the Egyptian banking system within the CBE-standard deadline of 180 days (extendable only by CBE approval), VAT at 14% is retroactively assessed on the invoice value with a delay surcharge of 2% per month or part thereof under the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 73 of 2021. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, including false export declarations, fictitious remittances, round-tripping of goods, or diversion of goods to the domestic market, trigger criminal tax evasion prosecution with fines of up to 100% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment, and cancellation of the taxpayer's VAT registration and e-invoicing license.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed floating exchange rate with partial capital account convertibility. Current account transactions, including merchandise trade and service payments, are generally convertible upon submission of supporting documentation. Capital account transactions, such as equity contributions and intercompany loans, require prior CBE approval and are subject to repatriation controls. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted but subject to CBE review, potential surrender obligations, and withholding tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters must surrender 100% of foreign currency export proceeds to an authorized bank within six months of collection. Surrender is mandatory for all export receipts, supported by commercial invoices, packing lists, and bills of lading. Delays beyond six months require CBE justification and may attract penalties. For overhead crane and gantry crane exports, proceeds must be converted and surrendered in full to maintain regulatory compliance and avoid forex allocation restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is low. Egyptian LLCs are prohibited from using unlicensed offshore digital payment platforms under CBE regulations. All cross-border transactions must route exclusively through licensed Egyptian commercial banks; offshore EMIs are not permitted absent specific CBE exemptions, which are rarely granted for manufacturing entities.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividend distributions by an Egyptian LLC are subject to withholding tax at the statutory rate of 10% on the gross amount distributed in excess of the paid-up capital and reserves as calculated under the Egyptian tax framework. For an LLC owned by a non-resident parent, the company itself, as the distributing payer, must withhold and remit 10% to the Egyptian Tax Authority.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate tax return must be filed within four months from the end of the fiscal year. For a December 31 fiscal year-end, the filing deadline is April 30 of the following year. Companies operating in a fiscal year other than the calendar year must file by the last day of the fourth month following their financial statement date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian TP documentation thresholds under the ETA's transfer pricing decree (Decree No. 82 of 2020 and Law 91/2005 amended by Law 58/2021): The Local File must be maintained if the value of controlled transactions with Egyptian related parties exceeds EGP 10 million per fiscal year. The Master File must be prepared if the worldwide gross revenue of the multinational group exceeds EGP 2 billion and the Egyptian entity's related-party transactions in Egypt exceed EGP 10 million. For the Egyptian LLC manufacturing overhead cranes, typical controlled transactions include (i) purchase of steel and electrical components from related-party suppliers; (ii) license of crane design software or patented hoisting mechanisms; (iii) management, engineering support, and shared IT services received from the parent; and (iv) sales of cranes to related trading or port companies. A benchmarking study is required for each controlled transaction, using Egyptian or comparable regional comparables where available, or OECD-compliant international search if no local comparables exist.

Economic substance scope: Egypt's economic substance doctrine is codified under tax law via anti-avoidance rules (Article 46 of Law 91/2005, amended by Law 58/2021). The ETA may disregard transactions or restructure arrangements that lack economic substance and whose sole or dominant purpose is obtaining a tax benefit. In specific free zones and the SCZone, the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply indirectly as a general anti-abuse test for preferential tax treatment; a licensed SCZone entity deriving income from port crane manufacturing must demonstrate actual employees, physical equipment, risks control, and substantive decision-making in Egypt. The SCZone Authority and ETA can apply substance-over-form doctrine to re-characterize agency, financing, or asset use arrangements between the Egyptian LLC and non-resident parent. Additionally, for Egyptian entities receiving offshore loans, the interest deduction is disallowed to the extent that debt exceeds a 4:1 debt-to-equity ratio, aligning with thin capitalization rules. The LLC must retain board minutes, technical staff presence, production floor evidence, and project execution records to evidence substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Corporate Income Tax Underreporting)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 3 to 5 years and a fine ranging from EGP 50,000 to EGP 1,000,000 (Law 91/2005, Articles 132 and 133). The tax authority may additionally impose after-tax penalties of up to 100% of the tax underpaid, plus monthly 2% late-payment interest. The court may order the publication of the conviction at the taxpayer's cost.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly participate in tax evasion scheme are jointly solidarily liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and fees. A conviction may also result in a travel ban, asset freeze, and disqualification from managing any company incorporated in Egypt for up to five years. If fraudulent trading is proven, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold all directors personally liable for third-party port operator damages.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance in SCZone

Statutory sanction: The SCZone Authority and ETA may withdraw the Golden License and tax incentives, and the company becomes subject to standard 22.5% corporate income tax on all profits retroactively. An anti-abuse penalty of up to 40% of the tax difference can be imposed, plus disqualification from future free-zone licensing in Egypt.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false reports declaring factory operations in the SCZone without actual operations are personally liable for the recovered incentive amounts. The Ministry of Investment and ETA may refer the matter to the Public Prosecutor; directors may face criminal fraud charges and a travel ban pending settlement or trial.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The ETA may make a transfer pricing adjustment to the taxable income, recompute the corporate tax, and impose additional tax plus interest of 2% per month on the deficiency. A penalty of up to 30% of the adjusted portion can be levied if the taxpayer has failed to submit timely transfer pricing documentation. The full amount must be paid within 60 days of final assessment; otherwise an enforcement order is issued against the company's movable assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the inflated intercompany agreements in the absence of any independent benchmarking study may be held personally liable to the company for the amount of tax loss if the tax authority collects the deficiency from the company. The ETA may also pursue directors for criminal misstatement if they certified false documents to banks or port authorities.
Money Laundering / AML (using port crane project payments)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 7 years and a fine from EGP 100,000 to EGP 500,000 for intentional money laundering of funds derived from a criminal activity. The entity is subject to winding-up and forfeiture of the laundered funds and the instruments or assets connected to the offense. The relevant supervisory authority may also permanently close the company's Egyptian bank accounts and impose non-refundable penalties of up to 20% of the transaction value on non-compliant institutions.

Director personal liability: Every director and senior officer is personally liable for failing to implement adequate anti-money-laundering compliance controls, independent of company liability. Courts have imposed travel bans and seizure of personal property where directors signed off on payment instructions on behalf of a shell company with no economic substance. Serious cases may result in imprisonment of the directors themselves.
Failure to Withhold Dividend WHT / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC as withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld 10% dividend tax, in addition to a 100% penalty on the withheld amount: i.e., a total of 20% of the distribution may be collected. Article 115 of Law 91/2005 requires the company to calculate the withholding correctly and remit within the statutory period; failure triggers an additional 2% monthly penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors and the company's tax representative are personally liable for the unpaid withholding amount if the company is dissolved or becomes insolvent before settlement. Directors who knowingly used a shell intermediary to evade dividend WHT may face criminal tax evasion charges with up to 5 years' imprisonment.
Failure to Maintain Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local / Master File)

Statutory sanction: A fixed penalty of EGP 100,000 for failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation plus an additional EGP 100,000 penalty for failure to submit the file within the specified period (total up to EGP 200,000). The tax authority may disregard the taxpayer's sales data and estimate taxable profit using a deemed markup derived from any available comparable. The ETA may impose a 30% penalty on any favorable TP adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the tax return and attested that TP documentation was prepared when no valid file actually exists risk a conviction for false attestation before the Egyptian tax prosecution. If the penalty is collected from the company and the company has insufficient assets, the directors who approved the missing documents may be sued in civil court for damages resulting from the penalty.
