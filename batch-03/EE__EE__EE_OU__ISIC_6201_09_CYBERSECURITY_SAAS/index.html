Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA) via e-MTA portal (e-maksuamet.ee); company registration through the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister).

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Osaühing (OÜ): (i) register the company in the e-Business Register using an e-Residency digital ID, digital signature, or notarized application; (ii) the resulting Estonian registry code serves as the statutory tax identification number; (iii) for VAT purposes, submit the VAT registration application via e-MTA when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 in a calendar year; (iv) a foreign entity acting in Estonia without a permanent establishment may also register directly for VAT and may be required to appoint a local tax representative where the law so prescribes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a newly incorporated Osaühing (OÜ): (i) register the company in the e-Business Register using an e-Residency digital ID, digital signature, or notarized application; (ii) the resulting Estonian registry code serves as the statutory tax identification number; (iii) for VAT purposes, submit the VAT registration application via e-MTA when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 in a calendar year; (iv) a foreign entity acting in Estonia without a permanent establishment may also register directly for VAT and may be required to appoint a local tax representative where the law so prescribes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonian law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch entity election. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) VAT group registration under the Estonian VAT Act; (ii) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and Import-One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) registration for intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2C electronic services; (iii) voluntary VAT registration below the applicable statutory threshold; (iv) retention versus distribution of profit as a taxpayer-controlled deferral of the 20% profit-distribution corporate tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 (domestic taxable turnover per calendar year); for non-resident suppliers of electronic services, including cybersecurity SaaS, to Estonian private consumers, no turnover threshold applies and registration is immediately required; the EU cross-border distance-sales threshold for OSS is EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union qualifies for the 0% VAT rate upon documentary proof of departure and the existence of a Customs Export Declaration. Cross-border B2B services, including cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration-testing SaaS, are outside the scope of Estonian VAT where the customer is a taxable person established in another country and the place of supply is determined under Chapter 2 of the Estonian VAT Act; proper customer VAT registration certificates and commercial evidence must be maintained. B2C digital services to non-EU customers are outside the scope where the effective place of use and residence of the customer is outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-Customs SAD/EXA with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / Air Waybill or customs exit confirmation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof of the foreign customer's payment
  • Commercial invoice and underlying supply or subscription agreement
  • For B2B services: valid foreign VAT or tax identification certificate and place-of-supply determination memorandum

Estonia does not operate a real-time transaction clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime for B2B/B2C invoicing. E-invoicing is mandatory for supplies to Estonian public-sector entities using the PEPPOL/EN 16931 standard. In the private sector, e-invoicing is voluntary but legally recognized. All tax-relevant invoices and records must be preserved for 7 years in their original or legally equivalent electronic form.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may retrospectively claw back the 0% rate and assess unpaid VAT at 20% on the taxable value where export evidence is missing, false, or inconsistent. Such reassessment additionally attracts statutory default interest of 0.06% per day or other late-payment interest prescribed by law. Deliberate or fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code, punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years for aggravated VAT fraud, with possible direct liability of the OÜ's management board.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on profit distributions and other taxable profit distributions, calculated using the 22/78 gross-up mechanism; undistributed accounting profits are generally not subject to corporate income tax. The rate applies from 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% withholding tax on dividends paid to resident or non-resident shareholders under the rules applying from 1 January 2025. The Estonian distributing company generally pays corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net distribution. Prior reduced-rate distribution rules and the associated 7% withholding mechanism may remain relevant to qualifying distributions made under transitional provisions; confirm the distribution's source and date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No single transaction-value threshold exempts related-party dealings from the arm's-length requirement. Detailed documentation obligations apply to specified categories of taxpayers, including qualifying large groups and entities meeting statutory size criteria; commonly relevant thresholds include at least 250 employees, annual turnover exceeding EUR 50 million, or balance-sheet total exceeding EUR 43 million. Master File obligations may apply to a group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Confirm the current Estonian documentation regulation and the taxpayer's classification before relying on thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not impose a general standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant to tax residence, permanent establishment, beneficial ownership, transfer pricing, anti-abuse rules, and treaty or EU-law relief. An Estonian OÜ should maintain commercially credible governance, personnel, decision-making, contracts, and records consistent with its actual functions, assets, and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Undeclared profit distribution or taxable benefit

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax and interest, possible penalties for reporting failures, and potential administrative or criminal tax proceedings where statutory elements and thresholds are met.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for company tax debts solely by holding office. Personal liability may arise under the Taxation Act for intentional or grossly negligent breach of duties causing unpaid tax, and criminal liability may apply to culpable individuals. Travel bans or imprisonment are not automatic consequences of an ordinary filing default.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment, additional tax, interest, and possible penalties for incorrect returns or failure to provide required documentation. Treaty mutual agreement procedures may be available for eligible double-taxation disputes.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal consequences only where statutory grounds are established, including culpable conduct causing tax loss or participation in an offence. A transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Tax fraud and false records

Statutory sanction: Tax recovery, interest, administrative penalties, and criminal sanctions under the Estonian Penal Code where the elements of a tax offence are satisfied. Criminal penalties depend on the offence, scale, intent, and whether the offender is an individual or legal person.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct, commit, or knowingly facilitate tax fraud may incur personal criminal liability. Corporate liability may also apply. Imprisonment is possible for qualifying criminal offences, but is not a standard sanction for a late return or an ordinary tax disagreement.
Improper treaty or EU withholding-tax relief claim

Statutory sanction: Denial or recovery of relief, assessment of withholding tax and interest, and potential penalties or criminal proceedings if the claim involved intentional misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Personal liability depends on the director's participation, knowledge, and applicable statutory duties; unsupported claims do not by themselves create automatic personal liability.
Shell or sham arrangements and abuse of legal form

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under tax law, denial of treaty or other tax benefits, tax assessment and interest, and possible administrative or criminal sanctions where the facts satisfy the relevant statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face liability for their own unlawful conduct. Limited liability is not automatically disregarded; veil-piercing or personal recovery requires a legal basis and the relevant evidentiary findings.
AML, sanctions, and payment-rail violations

Statutory sanction: Potential transaction blocking, account restrictions, regulatory or criminal investigation, fines, confiscation, and criminal penalties under the applicable Estonian and EU rules. Obligations depend on the company's activities and regulated status; an ordinary SaaS provider is not automatically a financial institution.

Director personal liability: Individuals may be personally liable for their own participation or culpable breach of applicable duties. Corporate status does not immunize directors from criminal liability, but travel restrictions or imprisonment require statutory grounds and competent authority action.
