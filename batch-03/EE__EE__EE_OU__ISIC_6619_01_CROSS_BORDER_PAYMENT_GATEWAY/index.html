Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital; an Osaühing (OÜ) may be formed with zero or EUR 1 share capital. If the company elects the contributed capital (aktsiakapital) regime, the minimum is EUR 25,000. Contributions in cash or kind are permitted; no escrow account is legally required, but share capital must be credited to a corporate bank account or received by the management board before registration. Separately, full-scope payment institution authorization requires initial own funds of at least EUR 125,000.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board) — e-MTA digital portal at emta.ee; commercial registration through e-Äriregister (e-Business Register); sectoral licensing authority for payment services: Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entrepreneurs incorporate the Osaühing (OÜ) online via e-Äriregister using an e-Residency digital ID; an 8-digit registry code is issued immediately and constitutes the statutory TIN. Employer/payer registration and monthly TSD declarations are filed through e-MTA. VAT registration is required in the KMD register when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 per calendar year (from 1 Jan 2025) or immediately for non-established suppliers; VAT number format is EE + 9 digits (e.g., EE100123456, comprising 1 + 8-digit registry code). Activity code EMTAK 6619 and the PSP/payment-institution licence from Finantsinspektsioon must be in place before merchant acquiring. No separate transfer-pricing or FX registration is required; all filings are electronic via e-MTA.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based deferral). Retained profits bear 0% corporate income tax; tax arises only upon profit distribution or taxable fringe benefits (ITA §50). General distribution rate: 20/80 of the net distribution (effective 25% of gross dividend); reduced rate 14/86 (approximately 16.3% effective) for regular recurring distributions to qualifying resident shareholders. Foreign shareholders are not separately taxed at source; the 20/80 distribution tax, or treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption for qualifying 10%-plus holdings, is discharged by the OÜ. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based deferral). Retained profits bear 0% corporate income tax; tax arises only upon profit distribution or taxable fringe benefits (ITA §50). General distribution rate: 20/80 of the net distribution (effective 25% of gross dividend); reduced rate 14/86 (approximately 16.3% effective) for regular recurring distributions to qualifying resident shareholders. Foreign shareholders are not separately taxed at source; the 20/80 distribution tax, or treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption for qualifying 10%-plus holdings, is discharged by the OÜ. Estonian permanent establishments of non-residents are taxed annually on Estonian-source profits under the same charge.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists; the OÜ is per se an entity-level CIT payer. Available elections and mandatory regimes: (i) classification as a regular distribution to access the 14/86 reduced rate, subject to a three-year distribution history and quantitative criteria; (ii) VAT group registration under VAT Act §25 for closely related taxable persons; (iii) EU OSS/IOSS elections for distance sales and distance services; (iv) mandatory interest-limitation and thin-capitalisation rules (ITA §§41-42) with no elective carve-out; (v) mandatory hybrid-mismatch disallowances under ATAD 2 (ITA §§50f-50i), leaving no elective mismatch shelter.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable supplies with right of deduction (effective 1 January 2025; EUR 16,000 in prior periods), excluding exempt financial services and capital goods; non-established suppliers remain registrable irrespective of threshold. For a PSP, merchant acquiring, payment processing and clearing fees are exempt without input credit under VAT Act §16 implementing Directive Article 135(1)(d); registration is typically triggered by ancillary taxable supplies (terminal sales, data analytics, platform fees) or by B2C TBE cross-border services under OSS rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT where goods are physically exported to a non-EU destination within 12 months (VAT Act §17(1)(1)) or dispatched to another EU Member State (§17(1)(2)) with the buyer's valid VAT ID; both require transport evidence and, for intra-EU supplies, a recapitulative statement. Services: 0% or outside the scope of Estonian VAT where the PSP supplies B2B services to a customer established outside Estonia (place-of-supply rules, VAT Act §§20-21), with reverse charge applying in the customer's EU Member State and full out-of-scope treatment for third-country recipients. Pure payment, acquiring and settlement fee income is exempt under Directive Article 135(1)(d) irrespective of destination; export zero-rating for a PSP is relevant mainly to terminal/hardware exports and separately charged IT or transaction-monitoring services provided to non-resident banks and merchants.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX1/SAD) and, where applicable, the export accompanying document issued through the MTA customs system
  • Transport document evidencing departure from the EU within 12 months — Bill of Lading (sea), CMR (road) or Air Waybill (air)
  • Commercial contract and invoice bearing the EE OÜ VAT ID, ISIC 6619 service description and an explicit 0% VAT / Art. 17 KMS zero-rating annotation
  • Inward payment evidence — SWIFT MT103/MT202 or bank credit advice (inward remittance certificate equivalent) together with the merchant settlement ledger

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or government API authorisation regime exists; MTA applies post-filing risk analysis of monthly VAT returns (KMD plus KMDP annex) due by the 20th day following the tax period. B2G e-invoicing has been mandatory since 1 July 2019; a phased B2B mandate requires issuance and receipt of structured e-invoices in the EN 16931-compliant e-arve format from 2026 via certified service providers, per amendments to the Accounting Act. Paper and PDF invoices remain valid during 2025; e-MTA publishes machine-to-machine APIs for direct return submission.

Estonia imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline as a euro-area State with unrestricted capital movements under Article 63 TFEU; the value 0 is deliberate. MTA may reassess misdescribed or falsely zero-rated transactions within a three-year window, extended to seven years for non-declaration or fraud (Taxation Act §96), with daily default interest of 0.06% (approximately 21.9% per annum) compounding from the original due date. False export zero-rating is re-invoiced at the 22% VAT rate with full retrospective clawback of input credits; wilful misdeclaration constitutes criminal tax fraud under Penal Code §389 et seq., punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to five years (seven years in aggravated or organised cases), and extends to the OÜ management board and the e-Residency verification agent, with mandatory removal from the VAT and OSS registers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full member of the euro area; the euro is fully convertible on both capital and current accounts under Articles 56-60 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive. There are no exchange control laws or administrative convertibility restrictions. Profit remittance is free but the Estonian dividend taxation regime (0% undistributed corporate income tax; 20% or 25% at distribution depending on regularity and beneficiary characteristics) must be accounted; no FX-required approvals apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio in Estonia. Export receipts in foreign currency may be held indefinitely in foreign currency accounts or converted at commercially agreed bank rates. Estonian residents remain subject solely to OECD/EU reporting obligations (e.g., TARC, Intrastat) and AML/CFT transaction monitoring under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem and lawfully subordinate to the European Central Bank for monetary policy; Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) exercises consolidated prudential and AML/CFT supervision over banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderately high viability for EEA/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions operating through EU single-licence passporting under PSD2, provided the EMI is licensed by a credible EEA regulator and not merely an offshore shell. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs are legally unavailable to conduct regulated payment services in Estonia and cannot provide a substitute for a domestic bank account. The PSP must maintain a genuine, auditable settlement account with a core Estonian/EU bank or a licensed credit institution to avoid AML concentration risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, applied as 22/78 of the net amount distributed; retained and reinvested profits are generally taxed at 0% until distribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ to a non-resident. The OÜ instead generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the net distribution. The recipient's country may impose its own tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For the enhanced Estonian transfer-pricing documentation regime, the principal size thresholds are annual turnover of at least EUR 50,000,000, balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43,000,000, and an average of at least 250 employees, generally assessed by the statutory two-of-three threshold test. Additional documentation obligations may apply to specified regulated, listed, cross-border, permanent-establishment, or group situations. The OECD Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is generally EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. There is no universal transaction-value threshold that makes every cross-border related-party payment automatically compliant without arm's-length support.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a standalone broad Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, general anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, EU anti-abuse rules, treaty principal-purpose analysis, controlled foreign company rules, and financial-sector licensing. A payment gateway should maintain Estonian decision-making capacity, qualified personnel, risk control, operational systems, contractual authority, settlement controls, AML functions, accounting records, and commercially justified remuneration. A registered address, e-residency, nominee director, or outsourced administrator alone is insufficient substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed payment services

Statutory sanction: Regulatory prohibition, supervisory orders, authorization refusal or withdrawal, compulsory cessation, administrative fines, and potential criminal liability for unlawful economic activity. Customer funds may be frozen or redirected through enforcement measures.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face personal criminal liability, disqualification, civil damages, restitution, and confiscation where they knowingly organize or permit unauthorized activity. Imprisonment is possible where the conduct satisfies the applicable criminal offense.
AML, counter-terrorist financing, and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Supervisory fines, remediation orders, business restrictions, authorization withdrawal, asset freezing, confiscation, and criminal penalties. Legal-person AML violations may attract substantial statutory fines, with the precise ceiling depending on the offense and applicable AML legislation.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may be personally prosecuted where they intentionally or negligently approve, direct, or conceal the violation. Civil liability, confiscation, management prohibition, travel restrictions in criminal proceedings, and imprisonment may apply.
Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, statutory interest, collection enforcement, administrative fines, and criminal penalties for intentional or large-scale tax evasion. Criminal consequences may include fines, confiscation, and imprisonment depending on amount and intent.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal liability for knowing participation and may face civil liability for tax damage, restitution, confiscation, management prohibition, and restrictions on international movement during criminal proceedings.
VAT fraud and carousel arrangements

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input VAT, interest, fines, repayment demands, joint-liability measures in qualifying cases, and criminal prosecution for fraud or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly arrange or approve fraudulent VAT chains may face personal restitution, confiscation, civil damages, management prohibition, and imprisonment.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length profit, interest, penalties, denial of deductions, possible withholding-tax reassessment, and increased audit exposure. Grossly inadequate or fabricated documentation may support an evasion case.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for losses caused by deliberate mispricing and criminal exposure where the arrangement is knowingly fraudulent. Professional advisers may also face liability if they intentionally participate.
Beneficial-ownership concealment and shell-company abuse

Statutory sanction: Registry correction, AML enforcement, account restrictions, fines, license consequences, confiscation, tax reassessment, and criminal prosecution for document fraud, money laundering, or related offenses.

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors may be personally liable for false declarations and concealment. Sanctions may include civil restitution, confiscation, management prohibition, travel restrictions during proceedings, and imprisonment.
Client-money, safeguarding, and settlement misuse

Statutory sanction: Supervisory remediation, capital and safeguarding orders, license suspension or withdrawal, repayment obligations, insolvency consequences, administrative fines, and criminal liability for embezzlement or fraud where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers may face personal fiduciary, civil, and criminal liability, including restitution, confiscation, management prohibition, and imprisonment for intentional misappropriation.
Corporate-law and accounting non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines, compulsory filing, registry enforcement, director liability, recovery of unlawful distributions, creditor claims, and potential insolvency or criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unlawful distributions, delayed insolvency filing, creditor prejudice, accounting failures, and losses caused by breach of duty. Civil restitution and management prohibition may apply.
Improper profit repatriation and withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Withholding-tax assessment, corporate income tax assessment, interest, penalties, denial of treaty benefits, and potential anti-avoidance or evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid tax and damages where they knowingly approve improper repatriation. Criminal liability, confiscation, management prohibition, and imprisonment may arise in intentional evasion cases.
