Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, via the unified electronic tax portal (https://www.eta.gov.eg and the Tax Authority's e-services platform)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must obtain a tax card and Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the ETA by submitting: (1) certified copies of the commercial registry extract, (2) the LLC's incorporation contract and memorandum of association, (3) proof of the legal representative's appointment and national ID/passport, (4) the articles of association and board resolutions, and (5) the lease or ownership documents of the Egyptian registered office. The application is submitted online or in person; the ETA issues a unified tax ID that integrates income tax, VAT, and stamp tax registration. Foreign entities without a local establishment that are subject to Egyptian-source income or VAT may be required to register through a representative responsible for tax obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must obtain a tax card and Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the ETA by submitting: (1) certified copies of the commercial registry extract, (2) the LLC's incorporation contract and memorandum of association, (3) proof of the legal representative's appointment and national ID/passport, (4) the articles of association and board resolutions, and (5) the lease or ownership documents of the Egyptian registered office. The application is submitted online or in person; the ETA issues a unified tax ID that integrates income tax, VAT, and stamp tax registration. Foreign entities without a local establishment that are subject to Egyptian-source income or VAT may be required to register through a representative responsible for tax obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Egyptian tax law. An LLC is a separate juridical person subject to corporate income tax at the entity level. Egypt has enacted controlled-foreign-company and hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 (as amended) that override contractual classifications. Hybrid mismatch notifications and elections under OECD BEPS Action 2 are recognized through the Egyptian transfer pricing guidelines, but no elective entity classification regime analogous to the US check-the-box is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT registration; EGP 1,000,000 for the simplified tax registration regime; voluntary registration is permitted for importers and suppliers below these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services that physically leave Egypt are zero-rated for VAT purposes. Zero-rating applies upon substantiation by the customs export declaration and the actual transfer of ownership to a non-resident. Services provided to non-residents through offshore or deemed-offshore arrangements, and goods supplied within free zones and economic zones, are also eligible for zero-rating. The taxpayer must prove that the sale was made to a customer outside Egypt and that the goods were exported within the statutory time frame. Inward processing and temporary export regimes may also be zero-rated if all regulatory conditions are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Cargo Declaration) accepted and stamped by Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean) or Air Waybill (AWB) showing exporter as shipper
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian bank proving receipt of export proceeds in freely convertible currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract or purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Importer's customs entry or proof of arrival at destination where available

Egypt mandates a real-time electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) and e-receipt regime for taxable persons above the prescribed filing thresholds, as determined by ETA decree. B2B invoices must be issued in XML or JSON format, digitally signed with the taxpayer's e-signature, submitted to the ETA through the integrated electronic invoicing system (applicable API endpoints), and validated by the ETA's clearance engine before the invoice is considered compliant. The ETA assigns a unique universal identification code (UUID) and QR code for each cleared invoice. E-receipts for B2C sales are being phased in. Non-compliance may result in blocked invoices, fines, and suspension of VAT registrations.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt rigorously audit export zero-rating claims through the 'General Export Evidence' system. If export documents are incomplete, inconsistent, or if foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days (or any approved extension), the zero-rated VAT becomes immediately assessable with interest. Fraudulent or repeated unjustified zero-rating claims trigger penalties of up to 100% of the tax due, fines of up to EGP 50,000 per violation, possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion, denial of VAT refunds, and clawback of any earlier refunds. Exporters must maintain full auditable records for the legal prescription period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP is effectively convertible on the current account for trade, services, and profit remittance through authorized banks without ex-ante CBE licensing, subject to documentary verification (customs declarations for goods, tax clearance certificates for dividends, audited financial statements for profit flows). The capital account is open for foreign direct investment: LLC equity contributions, capital increases, and liquidation-repatriation proceeds require no prior CBE permit, but must be routed through CBE-licensed banks and declared for ITRS/BOP reporting. The March 2024 liberalization cleared the 2022–2023 dividend-repatriation backlog and eliminated the dual-rate distortion; residual hurdles include bank-level 'economic purpose' review of large outflows, absence of a freely deliverable offshore EGP market, thin onshore derivatives, and strict matching of payments to underlying trade or investment documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory EGP surrender ratio exists as of 6 March 2024; the previous 50% export-proceeds conversion obligation was abolished and 100% of export receipts may be retained in the exporter's foreign-currency account. Substantive compliance still requires: (i) collection of proceeds within 90 days from the export customs declaration/Bill of Lading date, extendable to 180 days for installment or open-account contracts evidenced by the sale agreement; (ii) settlement exclusively through an Egyptian CBE-licensed bank and matching to the underlying export ED number and buyer-country ACI reference; (iii) prompt submission of the export customs declaration, commercial invoice, and shipping documents to the receiving bank; and (iv) documented CBE tolerance/approval for any non-standard collection pattern. Violations expose the exporter to CBE banking-facility suspensions, penalties, and loss of trade-related banking privileges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary, credit, and foreign-exchange regulator, exercising powers under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and its delegated Foreign Exchange Rules; all FX dealings, trade settlements, and cross-border payments must be executed through CBE-licensed banks. The 6 March 2024 reform package abolished the administered exchange-rate structure and established a market-determined, flexible EGP regime anchored in the IMF Extended Fund Facility; CBE intervention is limited to smoothing excessive volatility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the non-resident shareholder dispatches USD/EUR by SWIFT MT103 from its own account at a reputable bank to the LLC's USD FDI Capital Contribution Account at the Egyptian settlement bank. Beneficiary name and account must exactly match the Commercial Registry extract; MT103 field 50K must identify the ultimate contributing shareholder; pooled or third-party funding is not acceptable without a notarized assignment. The remittance narrative must state 'Contribution to share capital — [LLC legal name] — Egypt' and reference the notarized board resolution and updated shareholding schedule. The receiving bank completes CBE ITRS/BOP coding as foreign direct investment — equity injection, verifies the paid-in capital increase in the Commercial Registry, and releases funds either (i) for USD retention to fund future component imports, or (ii) for conversion into EGP at the prevailing interbank rate for working capital. No CBE prior approval is required for FDI inflows.Trade payments for imported bottling-line components use two primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103/TT for advance payments, freight, installation support, and open-account balances under standard trade-documentation thresholds, and (ii) irrevocable USD Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) for major component packages, issued by the Egyptian bank in favor of the overseas supplier, with a nominated confirming bank and transport documents routed through correspondent clearing. Post-2024, CBE rules allow import settlement via TT/collections with proper documentation, though banks still prefer LC/discrepancy-checks for capital-equipment purchases. All disbursements are formally disclosed on import settlement forms and ITRS reports; export proceeds are received via MT103 advances, sight-export LCs negotiated by the Egyptian bank, and open-account rails, with proceeds credited to the USD DDA. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of annual taxable income for ordinary Egyptian companies under the Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company are generally subject to 10% withholding tax. A 5% rate may apply to qualifying dividends relating to listed securities and satisfaction of statutory ownership, listing and holding conditions. Intercompany dividend exemptions or reductions require separate analysis under current Egyptian law and cannot be assumed solely from a foreign parent's ownership percentage.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within four months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year company with a 31 December year-end, the ordinary deadline is 30 April of the following year. A return is generally required even where the company has no taxable income or has incurred a tax loss.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation requirements generally apply where annual controlled transactions exceed EGP 8 million in a relevant transaction category or where the Egyptian Tax Authority otherwise requires documentation. The taxpayer should confirm the current executive regulations, portal guidance and category aggregation rules for the relevant filing year. Large multinational groups may also be subject to Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting requirements based on the applicable consolidated-group revenue threshold and Egyptian implementation rules.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime of general application. Nevertheless, Egyptian tax authorities may challenge arrangements lacking commercial substance under the Income Tax Law, transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership standards and substance-over-form analysis. The LLC should maintain real Egyptian personnel, premises, production assets, decision-making, accounting records, commercial contracts, inventory controls, banking activity and operational risk consistent with its manufacturing and export functions. A low-tax foreign affiliate should not receive residual profit without performing functions, using assets and assuming risks that justify the return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax-evasion exposure under the Egyptian Income Tax Law may include imprisonment and a fine generally linked to the evaded tax, in addition to assessment of the principal tax, additional tax, administrative penalties and possible settlement or seizure measures. Exact sanctions depend on the offense, amount, repetition and judicial findings.

Director personal liability: Managers, directors, authorized signatories and persons who knowingly participate may face criminal prosecution, fines and imprisonment. Courts may order civil restitution and asset recovery. Travel restrictions or precautionary measures may be imposed by competent authorities or courts where legally justified; limited liability does not protect personal criminal conduct.
Failure to register, file or pay tax

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, additional tax, interest-like late-payment additions, tax assessments based on authority estimates, collection enforcement and potential criminal liability may apply. The amount depends on the breached obligation and the applicable version of the Tax Procedures Law and Income Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Responsible managers and signatories may incur personal criminal liability for deliberate non-compliance or misappropriation of withheld taxes. Civil liability may arise for losses caused by unlawful conduct. Travel bans and detention are not automatic and require applicable legal process.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian Tax Authority may make a primary adjustment, impose tax on the increased Egyptian profit, charge additional tax and penalties, reject deductions, and require transfer-pricing documentation. Corresponding relief is not automatic and may require competent-authority procedures under an applicable treaty.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal exposure where they knowingly approve fabricated documentation, sham transactions or fraudulent tax evasion. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments generally impose liability on the company, not automatically on directors.
Shell company, conduit or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief may be denied, domestic withholding tax may be assessed, and additional tax and penalties may apply. Sham arrangements, fabricated residence certificates or deliberate concealment may also create criminal tax-evasion exposure.

Director personal liability: Persons knowingly designing or implementing fraudulent conduit arrangements may face criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment and civil restitution. Corporate limited liability does not shield deliberate personal fraud.
Improper withholding on outbound payments

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC may be assessed for the unwithheld amount, additional tax and penalties, and may lose the deduction for the underlying payment. Failure to remit tax collected from another person may create aggravated enforcement and criminal consequences.

Director personal liability: The responsible financial officer, manager or signatory may face personal criminal exposure for deliberate non-remittance or falsification. Civil recovery may be pursued against persons who misappropriate withheld amounts.
Customs, import valuation and indirect-tax misstatement

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, customs fines, confiscation or seizure, VAT and other tax assessments, interest or additional tax, import restrictions and potential criminal proceedings may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, customs agents and responsible employees may be prosecuted for deliberate smuggling, falsification or fraudulent declarations. Civil restitution and asset measures may follow; travel restrictions require competent legal authority.
Books, records and audit obstruction

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties, adverse assessment, disallowance of deductions, compulsory examination procedures and possible criminal charges for obstruction or document falsification may apply.

Director personal liability: The company and responsible officers may be separately liable where obstruction is intentional. Directors may face fines or imprisonment for falsification or deliberate obstruction, while ordinary record deficiencies generally remain corporate liabilities.
Anti-money-laundering and sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: AML investigations may result in account freezes, confiscation, administrative measures, substantial fines and criminal prosecution under Egyptian AML and related criminal laws. Sanctions violations may create separate criminal and regulatory exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally liable for knowing participation, consent, recklessness or failure to implement required controls. Criminal penalties can include fines and imprisonment; asset freezes and travel restrictions may be ordered under applicable legal procedures.
Profit repatriation and foreign-exchange control failure

Statutory sanction: The company may face tax reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, banking or foreign-exchange enforcement, repayment demands, penalties and potential invalidation of the distribution. Banks may suspend or report transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions may face civil liability to the company and creditors, and criminal liability where fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, money laundering or foreign-exchange violations are established.
Unlawful corporate distributions and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, damages, creditor claims, insolvency proceedings, asset recovery and possible criminal sanctions may apply under company and insolvency legislation.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors may be personally liable for civil restitution and creditor losses. Criminal liability and imprisonment may arise for fraudulent insolvency, asset concealment or intentional creditor prejudice; veil piercing is fact-dependent and not automatic.
Bribery and public-official corruption

Statutory sanction: Criminal bribery sanctions may include imprisonment, substantial fines, confiscation, contract consequences and exclusion from public procurement. False accounting and tax-evasion charges may be added.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, employees and intermediaries who authorize or participate may face direct criminal liability, imprisonment, fines, confiscation and civil restitution. The company may also face corporate, procurement and reputational consequences.
