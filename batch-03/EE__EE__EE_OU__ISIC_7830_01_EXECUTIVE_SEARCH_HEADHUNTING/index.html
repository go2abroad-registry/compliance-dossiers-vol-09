Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Commercial Code sets the ordinary statutory minimum share capital of an OÜ at EUR 2,500. An OÜ may nevertheless be formed with a lower nominal share capital, commonly EUR 1 or no paid-up amount, provided the articles so provide. Where registered capital remains below EUR 2,500, the shareholders are jointly and severally liable for the company's obligations to the extent of the deficit until the capital reaches the statutory threshold. No escrow account is required by the registrar; monetary contributions are paid into the company's bank or collection account, and non-monetary contributions must be supported by an auditor's valuation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia adopted the euro on 1 January 2011 and is bound by the EU Treaty's free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU); there are no residual exchange-control statutes, no surrender obligations, and no central-bank approval requirements for current or capital account transactions. The Estonian Foreign Exchange Act was repealed after euro adoption, leaving a fully liberalised regime in which EUR is freely convertible and foreign-currency balances can be held without restriction. Profit remittance and dividend distributions from an OÜ to non-resident shareholders require no FX authorisation or exchange-control notification; the only meaningful fiscal hurdle is the Estonian corporate income tax triggered at distribution (20% standard rate, or 14% under the regular annual distribution regime in Section 50 of the Income Tax Act), which is settled in EUR and creates no convertibility impediment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory surrender or repatriation timeline on export proceeds or cross-border service fees, including executive-search and headhunting commissions. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: resident companies may retain foreign currency in EUR, USD, GBP or other freely convertible balances at Eesti Pank-licensed credit institutions. For balance-of-payments statistics, resident companies must report direct-investment and selected non-resident transactions to Eesti Pank either through their bank or via Eesti Pank's e-statistics reporting portal, but this is a statistical reporting obligation only and contains no conversion or surrender mandate; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is the central bank and, within the Eurosystem, jointly with the European Central Bank exercises monetary policy and foreign-exchange authority. Prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions is conducted by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High-medium for operational payment accounts: EEA e-money institutions passporting into Estonia, including Wise, Revolut, Payhawk and Monese, are recognised under the Estonian E-Money Institutions Act and EU Directive 2009/110/EC, and can provide IBAN-denominated accounts that traditional banks may decline for newly incorporated e-Residency OÜs. Legal constraints: an EMI may not accept deposits, may not lend, and holds client funds under safeguarding rather than the statutory Deposit Guarantee Act; consequently, any third-party client-fee escrow or retainer float should be maintained at a supervised credit institution, not an EMI. A sensible treasury architecture uses a domestic bank as the anchor relationship and an EMI as a secondary low-friction collection/disbursement rail for cross-border retainer wires.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions and shareholder loans enter the OÜ's IBAN via SEPA Credit Transfer from EEA banks or via SWIFT MT103 from non-SEPA correspondent banks. The payment message must carry the OÜ's Estonian registry code as beneficiary identification and a reference line stating share capital contribution or loan agreement date to satisfy AML purpose classification. The onboarding bank typically verifies the shareholder resolution approving the capital increase, the payment origin, proof of beneficial ownership of the injected funds, and consistency with the UBO profile on file. For balance-of-payments statistical compliance, Eesti Pank accepts reporting filed by the bank for transfers above the reporting threshold, and direct-investment flows are reported through the bank's e-reporting channel. If the injecting shareholder is domiciled in a non-cooperative jurisdiction, banks require enhanced due diligence, a written source-of-funds confirmation, and often insist that the funds be routed through a qualifying EU or EEA intermediary bank before final credit to the Estonian IBAN.Outbound executive-search fee settlements are cleared by SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant within the EEA, and by SWIFT MT103 outside the EEA with USD, GBP or other foreign-currency payments routed through correspondent accounts at Swedbank, SEB or LHV. Commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700/MT707 are permissible and occasionally required for large placement milestones in documentary-credit jurisdictions, although rare for pure advisory services. Domestic operating disbursements, including payroll taxes and subcontract headhunter fees, are settled through the Estonian Tax and Customs Board's e-Maks e-service and standard SEPA mandates. Cross-border subcontractor payments require FATCA/CRS self-disclosures and a valid VAT number or reverse-charge declaration to avoid withholding complications. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on gross distributed profits (effectively 20/80 of the net cash dividend); 0% on retained and reinvested earnings

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The Estonian statutory rate on distributed profits is 20% (computed as 20/80 of the net distribution). The same rate applies to non-resident shareholders in the absence of an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. Under a DTA the rate can drop to 5%–15% for portfolio investors and to 0% for a controlling EU/EEA parent company satisfying the directive’s conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a dividend distribution made in month M, the TSD must be filed and the CIT paid by the 10th day of month M+1. Payment is deemed made on the day it reaches the bank account of the Tax and Customs Board.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual report must be filed no later than 30 June for the prior calendar year, unless the financial year is extended.

Domestic WHT on royalties and interest is 0%, but the MTA requires full descriptive invoices and substance evidence to resist recharacterisation as a deemed dividend. Late filing penalty of up to 5% of the declared tax, not less than a €200 fixed administrative fine; accruing interest at 0.06% per calendar day on every unpaid euro of CIT; failure to file for two consecutive periods triggers an escalated non-compliance fee of up to €5,000 and the e-MTA may initiate a compulsory collection process.
TSD – Correction and cancellation toggles Used to correct any previously reported distribution amount or rate. Amendments must be submitted before the MTA opening a tax audit. A correction that increases tax due will be treated as the original filing if made before the due date; otherwise late-payment interest begins from the original due date. Late filing penalty of up to 5% of the declared tax, not less than a €200 fixed administrative fine; accruing interest at 0.06% per calendar day on every unpaid euro of CIT; failure to file for two consecutive periods triggers an escalated non-compliance fee of up to €5,000 and the e-MTA may initiate a compulsory collection process.
Income Statement – Revenue from executive search services Gross retainer and placement fees received from clients; netting of internal group invoices is not allowed. For cross-border fee-splitting with a foreign partner, disclose the net revenue accruing to the Estonian OÜ and list the related party contract. Failure to publish annual accounts leads to striking off (e‐kinnistus) from the Commercial Register; a director may be ordered to file under penalty; if no one files, a monetary penalty of up to €5,000 per quarter is imposed.
Balance Sheet – Distributable reserve (jaotamata kasum) Cumulative retained earnings available for future cash dividends; the MTA uses this figure to verify that the 20% distribution tax has been reported. If non-resident directors decide not to distribute profits, no CIT is due; however, funding a personal overseas account from this reserve without formal distribution will be taxed as a deemed dividend plus sanctions. Failure to publish annual accounts leads to striking off (e‐kinnistus) from the Commercial Register; a director may be ordered to file under penalty; if no one files, a monetary penalty of up to €5,000 per quarter is imposed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: no codified advance threshold is published in the Estonian Income Tax Act, but the MTA follows OECD guidance and will request a formal TP Local File whenever cross-border related-party transactions in a single fiscal year exceed EUR 50,000 in aggregate. For an executive headhunting company, the practical de minimis to prepare a contemporaneous Local File is B2B revenue or cost of EUR 20,000 with related entities. Master File: mandatory where the worldwide consolidated group revenue is equal to or exceeds EUR 50,000,000; for a typical headhunting OÜ under this threshold, only the Local File is relevant.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations code like the Gulf states. Instead, the General Anti-Abuse Rule in the Taxation Act and the criteria of beneficial ownership under EU case law require a resident company to have genuine economic activity before treaty benefits are granted. For a shell headhunting OÜ with no employees, no in-country directors, no commercial premises, and no substantial client contractual activity, the MTA may deny a Tax Residence Certificate, impose the 20% distribution tax on all cash inflows, and refuse DTA relief. Substance criteria are benchmarked against the C-suite service revenue actually generated in Estonia: licensee, staff, client meetings, and case-management data must be present.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Taxation Act §110, a penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax may be added, plus late-payment interest at 0.06% per day from the original due date. For intentional evasion of more than the ‘large-scale’ statutory threshold, the Penal Code imposes a custodial sentence of up to five years and a criminal fine for the legal person.

Director personal liability: Pursuant to the piercing doctrine, a director who knowingly signs a false TSD or who causes the OÜ to distribute disguised dividends is personally liable for the company’s unpaid tax where the company is rendered insolvent. Civil restitution of the hidden profit, a travel ban, and disqualification as director for up to two years are standard measures; in flagrant cases, imprisonment applies.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Deficit

Statutory sanction: The MTA can disregard the corporate form, equate the external fee income with a notional profit distribution and levy 20% CIT on the full amount plus 0.06% daily interest. The Commercial Register may expel the company for failing to evidence a valid business address or for the repeated absence of annual accounts. EU-ATAD CFC rules may also attribute the OÜ’s retained cash to its foreign parent and tax it in the parent state.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell receiving passive pipes are treated as the true beneficial operators; after the MTA has revoked the tax-residency certificate, the directors are personally exposed to the company’s debts under the ‘trading while insolvent’ rule, a travel ban is issued until restitution, and custodial sentences for market manipulation or fraud are available in criminal proceedings.
AML and Unlicensed Executive Search Activity

Statutory sanction: Under the Employment Services Act §25, an unlicensed headhunter can receive a fine of up to €100,000 for a legal entity (or €32,000 per violation) and may be prohibited from continuing operations. For money laundering controls, the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act exposes the OÜ to fines of up to €200,000, confiscation of proceeds and a ban on acting as a payment intermediary.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally receive client monies into designated personal accounts lose the corporate shield. They may be prosecuted for unauthorized payment services, fined, imprisoned for up to eight years for organised money laundering, and forced to return each fee with interest. The personal bank account is subject to freezing and a travel-ban order is commonly attached to an AML criminal investigation.
Systematic Non-Filing of TSD and Annual Accounts

Statutory sanction: Administrative coercion: a fixed late-filing fine of €200 plus 5% of the tax sum; for each month of continuing non-payment, an additional 0.06% penalty interest. A final decision by the MTA can be enforced by withholding VAT refunds and by imposing a virtue levy on the personal bank account of the director. A prolonged strike of the register leads to forced dissolution of the OÜ.

Director personal liability: The director is jointly and severally liable for any penalty arising from wilful non-compliance once the MTA proves the failure was deliberate. In repeated cases, a director’s personal assets may be attached; criminal prosecutions for gross non-compliance can lead to disqualification and imprisonment of up to three years, and the court may issue a travel ban to prevent asset dissipation.
