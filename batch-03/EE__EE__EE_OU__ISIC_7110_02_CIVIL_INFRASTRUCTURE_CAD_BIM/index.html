Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate U.S.-style check-the-box rules or general hybrid mismatch elections. The Osaühing is taxed as a standard distribution-based corporate entity: undistributed profits are tax-exempt, and corporate income tax is imposed on distributions, fringe benefits, gifts, and non-business expenses. Available statutory elections include the reduced 14/86 regular-distribution rate for qualifying profit distributions and the calculation of deemed profit distribution tax at the standard 20/80 rate for disallowed expenses and benefits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 in taxable turnover per calendar year for Estonian-established persons; supplies include goods and services taxable in Estonia and intra-Community distance sales of goods; non-established foreign suppliers supplying taxable electronic, telecommunications, and broadcasting services may use the OSS framework and the EU EUR 10,000 distance-sales threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically departing the EU to a non-EU destination qualify for 0% VAT if the seller obtains and preserves proof of departure within the time limits. Intra-EU B2B supplies qualify for 0% VAT only when the customer's valid EU VAT identification number is stated and a recapitulative statement is filed. For offshore BIM/CAD drafting and engineering services supplied to a non-EU business customer, the place of supply is outside Estonia under the B2B 'recipient-location' rule; such services are therefore not taxable in Estonia and may be invoiced with a non-application/outside-scope statement, requiring evidence of the customer's business status, place of establishment, and actual receipt of the service.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX) or electronic EX declaration
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing removal from the EU
  • Contract, service agreement, or technical specification for the offshore BIM/CAD engagement
  • Invoice with clear indication of zero-rated supply or 'place of supply outside Estonia' and customer's foreign VAT/tax identification number
  • Service acceptance certificate or daily/monthly progress report from the foreign recipient
  • Bank payment evidence / SWIFT wire transfer confirmation; inward remittance certificate where requested by the bank or MTA for foreign-currency receipts

Estonia does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of invoices. Statutory e-invoicing is interoperable through the PEPPOL network and X-Road infrastructure, and VAT liability is declared through periodic e-MTA VAT returns. No API-based continuous transaction control or mandatory clearance by the tax authority is currently required for domestic or export invoices.

Estonian tax law requires a taxpayer claiming 0% VAT export treatment to substantiate the supply with contemporaneous documentary evidence. Inadequately documented exports are recharacterized as taxable domestic supplies at 22% VAT, with interest and a tax penalty assessed under the Taxation Act and VAT Act. Fraudulent or grossly negligent zero-rating claims, false invoicing, or sham export documentation expose the company to VAT reimbursement, interest, late-felony fines, and criminal liability for tax fraud. While Estonia does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline, failure to receive payment within 180 days materially weakens the commercial reality of the export and increases MTA audit scrutiny; the MTA may dispute the true place of supply and impose retrospective VAT clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has been a member of the eurozone since 1 January 2011; the euro is fully convertible. There are no capital controls, exchange restrictions, or approval requirements for current or capital account transactions, in line with EU Treaty freedoms (Articles 63–66 TFEU). Profit remittance by way of dividends is unencumbered from an FX control perspective; however, dividend distributions may trigger the Estonian corporate income tax at distribution (20% standard rate or 14% when applying the reduced rate conditions), which is a separate fiscal compliance matter, not a currency restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules. Export proceeds are not required to be repatriated within any specific timeline, and no mandatory conversion into euros applies. Exporters may lawfully keep receivables in any currency with any authorised credit institution or licensed e-money institution within the EEA, subject to normal invoicing and corporate tax documentation.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), in coordination with the European Central Bank (Eurosystem)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational treasury and international receivables; Low for primary statutory corporate banking where full credit institution infrastructure and deposit guarantees are required. Legal constraints: Non-Estonian e-money institutions must be passported under the EEA EMD2 regime and registered with the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) before providing payment services to Estonian entities. Funds held at EMIs are safeguarded through tiered accounts and are not covered by the Estonian Deposit Guarantee Scheme. EMIs may not issue a true 'bank account' or provide a capital escrow service acceptable for completion of incorporation in the Estonian Commercial Register; thus, for initial capital injection a domestic bank may be preferred.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed and retained profits; 20/80 (i.e., 20% of gross distribution, equivalent to 25% of net retained profit) on profit distributions, gifts, fringe benefits, non-business expenses and transfer-pricing adjustments deemed as distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20/80 (20% on gross distribution, 25% effective on the cash retained amount of the distributing company); preferential 14/86 (14% of gross distribution) applies to regular distributions of profits that have previously been taxed at the 20/80 level, paid to a resident individual or to an Estonian resident juridical person, subject to anti-stuffing (Look-through) rules and minimum-distribution conditions. The company must have paid the 20/80 tax on the underlying distribution in order to qualify for the reduced 14/86 rate on subsequent distributions; specific conditions under §50(4) of the Estonian Income Tax Act apply.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form TSD must be submitted by the 10th day of the calendar month following the month in which a taxable distribution, payment or withholding triggering event occurred. For a dormant OÜ making no distributions or withholdings in a given month, a nil declaration must nevertheless be filed monthly unless the company has been granted an exemption by the Tax and Customs Board; annual CIT return positions are not applicable because the Estonian system taxes only distributions, and therefore no separate annual corporate income tax return exists. The annual accounting period is the tax period for determining retained profits subject to future distributions, and annual financial statements must be submitted to the Commercial Register within six months of the financial year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Total amount of profit distributions declared in the reporting month, including cash dividends, share premium distributions, and deemed profit distributions (fringe benefits, non-business expenses, transfer pricing adjustments, gifts). Non-resident shareholders' dividends are reported in the same box with withholding tax calculated on the gross distribution; DTA-derived reduced rates must be pre-approved or documented before filing. Late filing penalty: up to EUR 1,200 or, for repeated violations, up to 10% of unpaid tax, and daily late-payment interest of 0.06% per day (approximately 21.9% annually) on all unpaid income tax. Failure to file nil returns or monthly TSD returns may trigger a coercive penalty of up to EUR 600 for a first-time violation and up to EUR 1,200 for repeat violation per the Estonian Tax Code (MKS). Deliberate non-declaration may amount to a tax misdemeanor with a fine of up to EUR 20,000; criminal-tax fraud can rise to a fine of EUR 400,000 to the company and imprisonment of up to five years for responsible individuals.
Box 2 Amount of income tax payable calculated at 20/80 of the gross distribution (25 effective on net) for ordinary distributions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (approximately USD 1,080,000) in aggregated cross-border related-party transactions for mandatory Estonian Local File (Estonian TP form). Master File is required where consolidated group revenues exceed EUR 50,000,000 and the group has Estonian resident related parties; the statutory de minimis for small groups: groups with fewer than 250 employees and either annual turnover ≤ EUR 50,000,000 or balance sheet total ≤ EUR 43,000,000 are exempt from the Local File preparation but must still comply with contemporaneous reasonable-documentation principles. Estonia's TP requirements are aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, including Chapter VI for intangibles and Chapter VII for low-value-adding intra-group services (5% cost-plus safe harbour for low-value-added services). A specific transfer-pricing disclosure in Form TSD Annex 8 is required for all transactions with related parties residing in a low-tax jurisdiction or in non-cooperative states and for all controlled transactions above EUR 50,000 per counterparty.

Economic substance scope: Estonia is not a ring-fenced zero-tax haven: OÜ entities are tax resident in Estonia and are subject to full transparency under the Estonian Commercial Code and Taxation Act. Legal substance requirements for an OÜ are minimal (no minimum capital, nominal share capital EUR 1 at formation) but the OÜ must demonstrate genuine commercial substance for treaty access, bank KYC, VAT registration and any claim of commercial autonomy. The Tax and Customs Board applies the following substantive-economics tests: actual business premises in Estonia, real directors with decision-making authority in Estonia (board formation), contracts, purchase orders, invoices, and accounts prepared under Estonian GAAP or IFRS, and actual performance of BIM/CAD services by employees or qualified contractors. For e-resident companies, the e-Residency programme does not confer tax-resident status on the foreign founder; tax residence of a company is based on place of incorporation and statutory seat (registry seat) in Estonia. The OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 5 (Substance) and European Union Council conclusions (Code of Conduct Group) – the Estonia regime is not harmful because the 0% rate applies to undistributed profits and the 20% distribution tax applies even on distributions to tax havens. Transparent entities under the Commercial Code (Usaldusühing, Täisühing) are transparent; an OÜ is a corporate tax payer only upon distributions, so anti-shell rules under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, RT I, 2017) require nominee-director disclosure; formal ESR-type economic substance regimes (like Cayman/CARICOM) do not apply in Estonia, but substance-over-form doctrine, sham-transaction doctrine and the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR § 83(5) of the Income Tax Act) invalidate artificial arrangements where the sole purpose is the avoidance of the 20/80 distribution tax, treating the transaction as a deemed distribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Deemed Distribution Avoidance)

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment with back interest of 0.06% per day (21.9% p.a.); penalty of up to 10% of the unpaid tax; tax misdemeanor fine up to EUR 20,000 on the company or its management; criminal tax fraud (§ 493 of the Penal Code) for amounts exceeding EUR 10,000: fine up to EUR 400,000 on the company, plus revocation of VAT registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and board members are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax debts if the failure to file or pay results from intentional conduct or gross negligence; courts can pierce the corporate veil where the company is under-capitalised or a personal alter-ego of the director; the Tax and Customs Board may issue travel bans for debt over EUR 300, and executives may be imprisoned up to five years for aggravated tax fraud (group or large-amount).
Shell Company / Sham Non-resident Conduct

Statutory sanction: Mandatory compulsory dissolution procedure: the Registry Maintenance Department initiates deletion of the entity from the Commercial Register under §60(2) of the Commercial Code if annual reports are not submitted for 2 consecutive years; director disqualification orders; fines up to EUR 1,200; all existing distribution tax is deemed due at the moment of deletion; a zero-profit OÜ found to be managed from abroad may be re-domiciled (deemed foreign company subject to Estonian CFC rules); under CFC rules in § 50(10)-(12) of the Income Tax Act, foreign undistributed profits of controlled foreign companies (OÜ ownership in low-tax jurisdictions) may be imputed to Estonian shareholders at the 20/80 rate; harmful shell-company exposure may trigger ATAD reverse hybrid mismatch denial.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for corporate tax arrears up to the full undistributed profit amount; directors may be prohibited from managing companies for a period of 1–5 years; creditor civil action for fraudulent conveyance; travel restrictions and substantial civil damages for subordination and abuse of legal personality; criminal liability for false declarations under § 297 of the Penal Code – up to 3 years imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) fines up to EUR 1.2 million or 10% of turnover for violation of the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (MTRPA); asset freezing orders under EU Regulations 269/2014 and 833/2014; administrative fines up to EUR 2,500,000 for credit institutions but for OÜs: fines up to EUR 320,000; criminal AML penalties: imprisonment up to 5 years; confiscation of proceeds of crime under the Criminal Assets Confiscation Act; forced deregistration and prohibition of professional activity of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners must report beneficial ownership to the e-Business register; failure to do so: fine up to EUR 200 and forced restrictions on distribution of profits; for involvement in money laundering (Penal Code § 394): up to 5 years imprisonment for individuals; where the OÜ is used as a money-laundering vehicle, directors face criminal restitution of the full laundered amount, banking restrictions, 5-year travel bans, and are stripped of professional licences; banks are required to undertake enhanced due diligence and can terminate accounts of any OÜ, causing liquidity collapse and collapse of contractual operations.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income upwards at the 20/80 rate plus 0.06% daily interest; 10% penalty on the under-declared amount; €1000–€20,000 fine for failure to file CbCR; public disclosure of tax non-compliance under DAC6 (EU tax intermediate reporting); unilateral upward adjustment by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board even without treaty mutual agreement procedure initiation; statute of limitation for transfer pricing is extended to five years from that statutory date for intentional under-declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing declarations or knowingly approve related-party invoice fabrication are personally liable for the company's tax arrears under § 8(3) of the Taxation Act; criminal liability under § 493-1 of the Penal Code (fraud) with maximum imprisonment of 5 years; civil damages to minority shareholders; travel ban and seizure of property; disqualification as a director; board member personal liability to the bankruptcy trustee for gross negligence in failing to maintain TP records.
