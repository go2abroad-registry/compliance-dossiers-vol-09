Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Investor Services and Incorporation One-Stop Shop; Commercial Registration Department of the Ministry of Trade and Industry for the Commercial Registry entry.

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 (as amended by Law No. 4 of 2018 and Law No. 149 of 2019), Title I, Limited Liability Company chapter (Articles 102 et seq.); Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 for one-stop-shop incorporation through GAFI; Unified Licensing Law No. 15 of 2004 for the interface with industrial and healthcare activity licensing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Contract of Incorporation (Articles of Association) with the mandatory Annexes, submitted through the GAFI electronic incorporation platform together with the official Establishment Application Form.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an Egyptian LLC under Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended. The full subscribed capital must be allocated among the founders, and at least 50% of cash contributions must be deposited in a temporary bank account (escrow-type certification) at a Central Bank of Egypt licensed bank before notarized signing; the deposit must be evidenced by an official bank certificate. In-kind contributions must be fully delivered and appraised before incorporation. The remaining cash balance must be paid within five years from the Commercial Registry date, failing which the management must restore the capital base in accordance with the executive regulations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Clearance from the Civil Defense Authority of the Ministry of Interior for project sites, medical gas manifold rooms, and workshops, with verification of compliance with NFPA 99 and the Egyptian Fire Code; structural load and routing approval from the licensed engineering consultant certifying compliance with building safety requirements for copper vacuum and oxygen manifold installations; and an Occupational Safety and Health clearance from the Ministry of Manpower under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003, including permit-to-work procedures for confined-space works and authorized gas testing.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to an Egyptian LLC under Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended. The full subscribed capital must be allocated among the founders, and at least 50% of cash contributions must be deposited in a temporary bank account (escrow-type certification) at a Central Bank of Egypt licensed bank before notarized signing; the deposit must be evidenced by an official bank certificate. In-kind contributions must be fully delivered and appraised before incorporation. The remaining cash balance must be paid within five years from the Commercial Registry date, failing which the management must restore the capital base in accordance with the executive regulations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — Ministry of Finance; principal national e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing/e-receipt integration portal: https://www.invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) one-stop shop and obtain the Commercial Registry certificate. Step 2: Apply to the ETA for the unified tax card (TIN) by submitting the tax card application form together with the notarized Arabic Memorandum of Association, Commercial Registry extract, board of managers resolution appointing a tax representative, certified accountant appointment, and proof of registered office. Step 3: File mandatory VAT registration with the competent ETA VAT office when taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 per annum; a foreign-owned LLC with no Egyptian branch must also appoint an Egyptian fiscal representative for VAT purposes (Article 20, VAT Law No. 67 of 2016). Step 4: Enroll in the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt program via the invoicing portal, obtain API client credentials (Client ID/Secret), certify digital signing keys, and configure the ERP/CAS interface to transmit invoices in JSON format to the ETA validator. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The Egyptian LLC is a resident corporate taxpayer liable to income tax on worldwide taxable profits under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as unified by Law No. 26 of 2020; no pass-through treatment is available for domestic LLCs; non-resident shareholders are taxed at source on dividends (withholding) and on deemed business income attributable to the LLC's Egyptian activities.

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election. The LLC may, however, exercise specific administrative elections: (i) cash-basis accounting election for small taxpayers; (ii) depreciation method election (straight-line vs. declining balance) for plant and equipment; (iii) investment-incentive election for projects qualifying under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; (iv) advance pricing agreement application for cross-border EPC-related intra-group charges; (v) participation-exemption claim for qualifying foreign dividends; and (vi) free-zone/economic-zone tax treatment election where the gas pipeline activity is licensed in the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE). Hybrid instruments remain subject to general anti-avoidance and thin-capitalization rules under Law 26 of 2020.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is triggered when the annual value of taxable supplies (exclusive of VAT) exceeds EGP 500,000 in any 12 consecutive months; registration must be completed within 30 days of crossing the threshold. Taxpayers below the threshold may apply for voluntary registration; non-resident suppliers maintaining an Egyptian taxable establishment or performing construction/EPC activities in Egypt are treated as carrying on taxable activity and are registrable regardless of the 500,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 35 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 for (i) goods physically exported from Egypt, (ii) services attributable to exported goods or performed offshore for a non-resident recipient, and (iii) supplies to licensed free-zone/SCZONE operators used outside the customs territory. For the medical gas pipeline EPC scope, only the export-validated component — e.g., pre-fabricated manifold skids, copper/vacuum terminal kits, and interference-supply materials that are customs-cleared for export and installed in an offshore/free-zone project by non-resident contract parties — is eligible for the 0% rate; onshore installation into Egyptian hospitals is standard-rated at 14%. The claimant must be registered for VAT, hold an export contract, evidence actual dispatch within the declared period, receive the price in convertible foreign currency within the repatriation window, and report the transaction using a zero-rated invoice code in the ETA e-invoicing system.

  • Customs Export Declaration — final machine-released copy from the Egyptian Customs Authority evidencing export of the exact goods and quantities referenced in the zero-rated invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — clean transport document naming the Egyptian LLC as shipper and the non-resident buyer as consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — foreign-currency settlement certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian bank crediting the LLC's account with the export proceeds

Mandatory. All registered B2B VAT taxpayers subject to the phased rollout must issue invoices in the ETA-prescribed JSON schema, submit them in real time to the ETA e-invoicing API endpoints using OAuth2-secured connectors, and receive a validation code before the invoice is fiscally recognized. B2C issuers must also use the e-receipt system with QR-code fiscal marking. Invoices failing clearance are deemed non-compliant and cannot support input VAT recovery; recurring violations attract fines of up to EGP 50,000 per invoice, temporary suspension of API credentials, and exclusion from public procurement. For a hospital EPC contractor, all supplier invoices, subcontractor invoices and final progress invoices to the hospital must be cleared through the ETA platform in the construction-services code.

Zero-rating claims are subject to documentary desk audit and joint ETA/Customs inspection. The ETA may reverse the 0% rate if the exporter fails the mandatory documentation test, exports are shadow-invoiced, proceeds are not repatriated within the applicable Central Bank of Egypt deadline, or the goods return without authorization. Consequences include: assessment of the full 14% VAT on the gross consideration, statutory delay surcharges running from the original filing due date, an evasion fine of EGP 5,000 up to EGP 50,000, an additional 100% evaded-tax clawback in fraud cases, suspension of e-invoicing credentials, joint and several liability of the LLC and its managers, and imprisonment of responsible officers for up to five years under Article 83 of Law No. 67 of 2016; falsified customs declarations further trigger penalties under the Unified Customs Law No. 207 of 2020.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is fully convertible for current-account transactions under the CBE market-flexible exchange-rate regime introduced in March 2024; trade and service flows settle freely at bank-quoted market rates. Capital-account flows remain regulated: inward direct investment must be registered at the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and with the receiving bank under the CBE foreign-investment framework; profit and dividend remittances require an ETA tax-clearance certificate under Law No. 91 of 2005, audited financial statements evidencing distributable profits, completed CBE capital-account registration, and bank verification of the underlying purpose code. Healthcare/EPC transactions with documented import and contract files face low practical convertibility risk; undocumented or purpose-mismatched remittances are rejected or deferred.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Article 209 of Law No. 194 of 2020 obliges exporters to repatriate export proceeds into a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank within four months of the export date. Following CBE Decision No. 87 of 2023 and the March 2024 FX liberalization, the mandatory conversion (surrender) ratio is zero percent: exporters may retain 100% of repatriated proceeds in foreign-currency accounts and may freely deploy them for imports or domestic EGP conversion at market rates. For the Hospital Medical Gas Pipeline LLC, all USD export/EPC service receipts must be credited to the company's CBE-regulated beneficiary account within the statutory window with matching export declarations and contract files; failure to repatriate within four months forfeits export incentives and triggers CBE penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the supreme regulatory authority for banking, foreign exchange and payment systems under Law No. 194 of 2020; FX policy, bank licensing and conduct supervision are executed by the CBE's Banking Supervision Sector and Foreign Exchange Operations Department in coordination with the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Unit (AMLFU) under Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended by Law No. 181 of 2022).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low — prohibited as a primary treasury rail. Egyptian law reserves deposit-taking and corporate payment-account services for CBE-licensed banks and licensed payment-service providers; offshore fintech EMIs and digital banks (e.g., Wise Business, Mercury, Revolut Business) lack a CBE banking licence, cannot be recognized by customs, the ETA or the healthcare-authority wire rails, and cannot satisfy export-proceeds repatriation or GAFI capital registration. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on distributions of profits to resident and non-resident shareholders (Article 57 of Law No. 91 of 2005). For non-residents, the 10% is final and applies on gross distributions. No additional WHT on repatriation of capital or liquidation surplus beyond the 10% dividend tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Egyptian Transfer Pricing Guidelines (Andres Beard Guidelines, Per Decree No. 616 of 2023 and Law 91/2005): mandatory Local File and Master File thresholds — Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EGP 10 million per transaction category; Master File required if consolidated group revenue exceeds EGP 200 million and related-party transactions exceed EGP 10 million. The threshold is set in the local currency (EGP). Transactions below these thresholds require a simplified contemporaneous information file.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have separate 'Economic Substance Regulations' similar to the UAE, but requires that any non-resident taxpayer claiming treaty benefits or operating through a PE must demonstrate actual economic substance, including premises, employees, equipment, and decision-making presence. Additionally, under the Egyptian Investment Law, tax incentives are contingent on substantive business activity in Egypt. For hospital medical gas pipeline EPC, substance includes having qualified engineers, project management cadres, and installation equipment located in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment not less than 3 years and a fine equal to the amount of tax evaded, plus an additional fine of 10% of the tax due for each year of non-payment. Civil liability includes payment of the full evaded tax, delinquency fees, and monthly delay interest of 2%.

Director personal liability: Directors and board members personally liable if they knowingly participated in evasion or failed to exercise due diligence. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, imposing joint liability on personal assets. Travel bans may be issued by the tax authorities for unpaid tax debts exceeding EGP 100,000. Imprisonment and personal bank account freezes are possible under the Tax Procedures Law and AML law.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of income as taxable in Egypt at full statutory rate, penalty of 40% of the tax due for misreporting, and revocation of any investment incentives under Law 72 of 2017. The ETA may reassess multiple years and impose fines up to EGP 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for taxes, and personal assets may be seized for tax debts if the corporate entity lacks assets. Travel bans and 5-year imprisonment apply for deliberate misuse of legal structuring to disguise true beneficial ownership.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 30% of the additional tax assessed on the adjusted amount, plus a late payment interest of 2% per month. In flagrant cases, a criminal fine of EGP 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years apply.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for transfer pricing penalties when they intentionally approved the intercompany arrangements without obtaining proper comparability analysis or documentation. Personal bank account seizure and travel bans are possible.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / UBO Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fine of EGP 200,000–500,000 and revocation of company registration. For evasion of AML reporting, criminal penalties include imprisonment of 6 months to 3 years, asset forfeiture, and delisting from government tenders.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers personally criminally liable for AML failures; personal assets are subject to confiscation and directors face travel bans and imprisonment. Evidence of knowingly structuring a shell arrangement to mask shareholders leads to judicial piercing of the corporate veil.
Non-Payment of Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax is deemed due from the Egyptian payer; failure to withhold exposes the payer to assessment of the full amount plus a 40% penalty and monthly interest at 2%. Repeated violations result in imprisonment up to 3 years and fines up to EGP 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign off on dividend distributions without ensuring WHT remittance are personally liable to the tax authority for the withheld amount, with personal assets attachable. Travel bans and criminal prosecution may follow.
