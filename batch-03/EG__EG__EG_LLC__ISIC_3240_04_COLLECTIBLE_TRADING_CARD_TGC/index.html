Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for an Egyptian LLC; the share capital is fixed by the founders in the Articles of Association and must be fully subscribed. Contributions are paid into the corporate bank account or valued in-kind in accordance with the Companies Law. No mandatory escrow account is required at incorporation, but evidence of the capital contribution may be required by GAFI or banks at account opening.

Funding & unlock path: The printing and foil-lamination facility must obtain a Civil Defense clearance certificate confirming fire-resistant construction, emergency egress, fire alarm/suppression systems, and safe storage of inks and solvents. A structural load inspection certificate is required from the local municipality for machine loads and occupancy. Occupational workplace clearance must be issued by the Ministry of Manpower after verification of ventilation, machine guarding, chemical safety data sheets, and personal protective equipment protocols.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for an Egyptian LLC; the share capital is fixed by the founders in the Articles of Association and must be fully subscribed. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) within the Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; integrated e-invoicing and e-receipts platform: https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg; corporate registration gateway: General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) – https://www.gafi.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A foreign parent incorporates the Egyptian LLC through GAFI's One-Stop Shop; the investment file receives a provisional tax file number simultaneously with commercial registration. Step 2: File the 'Tax Card' (Bayan) application with the competent ETA directorate using ETA Form 01/10, attaching notarized and Arabic-translated articles of association, GAFI investment certificate, commercial registration extract, board resolution/power of attorney, passport copies of shareholders/managers, proof of registered office (lease/utility), and the tax file opening declaration. Step 3: Open a corporate bank account with an Egyptian-licensed bank (or register a non-resident FX account for a foreign company without local establishment). Step 4: ETA reviews and issues the consolidated Tax Card containing a single TIN (unified for income tax, VAT, salary tax, and withholding tax). Step 5: If actual or expected annual taxable supplies reach EGP 500,000, complete VAT registration within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Egypt (including via reverse charge) must register with the ETA's non-resident registration desk. Total statutory timeframe: approximately 30 working days from complete documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is a taxable legal person under Law 91/2005; no pass-through treatment); Territorial_Scope: Worldwide for Egyptian-resident LLCs (resident = incorporated in Egypt or central management/control in Egypt); Tax_Exempt_Zone: Not applicable to a standard non-free-zone LLC unless it obtains a specific free-zone/SEZ license from GAFI.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through election for LLCs. Available statutory elections: (i) simplified SME regime under Law 91/2005 (turnover up to EGP 20 million: presumptive 0.4%–1.5% turnover tax) elected in the annual tax return; (ii) Article 53 deemed-profit election for non-resident contractors/consultants deriving service income from Egypt; (iii) tax-neutral restructuring election for qualifying mergers/demergers; (iv) free-zone/SCZone income-tax exemption only by express GAFI licence (not presumed). No hybrid mismatch election available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 (annual value of taxable supplies of goods and services; voluntary registration permitted at EGP 100,000)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for: (i) physical export of collectible trading cards and associated security holographic print products where the goods are actually shipped out of Egypt under a customs export declaration; and (ii) export of services (e.g., offshore printing/design royalties or overseas distribution rights) rendered to non-residents who are outside Egypt and not benefiting from the services inside Egypt. Qualifying conditions: seller must be a VAT-registered exporter; the contract/invoice must evidence export; goods must leave Egypt within the CBE allowed period; the export proceeds must be received in freely convertible foreign currency through Egyptian banks and repatriated within 180 days; services must be proven to be wholly performed or consumed outside Egypt. Taxable person must hold a valid export certificate and file VAT returns claiming zero rate; input VAT relating to export sales is credited/refundable with statutory priority.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Cargo Export Manifest) issued by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for airfreight exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / FX Credit Advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in Egypt

Mandatory for all VAT registrants: B2B e-invoices must be transmitted in near-real-time to the ETA's clearance engine via REST APIs (integration profile) or the web portal for small taxpayers; ETA validates the invoice and returns a unique UUID and 2D QR code (digital stamp) that must be printed/transmitted on the document; no e-invoice QR means the deduction is disallowed. Since 2024, the e-receipt platform covers B2C transactions (payment-card and POS flows). Non-compliance penalties apply per invoice and per day of delay; taxpayers must use the ETA 'my invoices' worklist, update product codes (custom harmonized codes), and archive XML-based e-invoices for 5 years.

ETA cross-matches e-invoices, customs export declarations (the ASYCUDA cargo system) and CBE bank FX records via the 'Tahweel' data exchange. If the 180-day repatriation date is missed, or the export declaration is missing/fraudulent, ETA will claw back the zero-rate benefit and issue an assessment equal to 14% VAT plus late-payment interest of 2% per month on the unpaid tax (minimum EGP 50/month), and a non-deduction penalty up to EGP 5 million under Law 67/2016 Art. 66; deliberate evasion can attract criminal fines of 1–3 times the tax amount and imprisonment (Art. 74 of Law 67/2016). Directors/founders are jointly and severally liable and the exporter's VAT refund file will be suspended.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a soft managed-float foreign-exchange regime post 3 November 2016: the EGP/USD rate is formed in the CBE-supervised interbank market without administrative parity; current-account convertibility is broadly available for goods, services, dividends, interest, and royalties provided that the receiving or remitting bank validates the underlying commercial documents and records the correct balance-of-payments code. Capital-account transfers are permitted without CBE prior approval for foreign investors holding a GAFI investment certificate, but inward equity injection and outward repatriation are processed only after bank KYC, Commercial Registry (DERAT) verification, Tax Card and audited financial statement scrutiny; dividends are subject to the applicable dividend withholding tax under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and may be reduced by double taxation treaties where available. Residual profit-remittance hurdles are bank compliance artifacts: mismatched invoice descriptions, missing board dividend resolutions, unverified UBO passports, and inconsistent BoP code capture; each is mitigable through a standard remittance dossier comprising the GAFI certificate, audited distributable reserves, customs export declarations, and a consolidated banking confirmation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation timeline: 6 calendar months from the date of shipment (bill-of-lading date) for merchandise exports, maintained by the CBE in its consolidated forex instructions applicable to exporters; 100 percent of gross export proceeds must be credited to an EG_LLC account at a CBE-licensed bank in Egypt before the deadline, including proceeds generated through card-acquiring platforms that are cleared into Egyptian bank channels. Mandatory surrender ratio: zero percent — the CBE abolished forced conversion after the 2016 flotation, so funds may be retained in USD/EUR accounts, applied to import payments, or converted to EGP through the interbank market at the prevailing rate. Non-repatriation within the six-month window triggers bank-led penalty notifications, possible inclusion in the CBE defaulters list, restrictions on future export customs declarations, and tax-audit scrutiny of offshore receivables; relief is granted only with documented carrier or tracking evidence, SWIFT cover records, and a written explanation accepted by the account bank before customs enforcement blocks further shipments.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the exclusive monetary and foreign-exchange regulator under Law No. 88 of 2003 (Banking and Monetary System Law), as amended by Law No. 18/2019, acting in coordination with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for investment inflows and with the Ministry of Finance for external borrowing; CBE supervises licensed banks, interbank FX clearing, AML/CFT execution under Law No. 80 of 2002, and balance-of-payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) operating as the primary Egyptian treasury rail; MEDIUM as a technical card-acquiring route for the stated Stripe/Authorize.Net rails when mounted on a licensed local PSP-aggregator architecture. Legal constraints: CBE e-money and payment service provider licensing under the amended Banking Law requires the payment issuer or acquiring entity operating in Egypt to be a CBE-licensed domestic corporate with registered capital and a physical presence; offshore EMIs holding UK/FCA or EU licenses are not recognised as eligible clearing partners for Egyptian export-proceeds repatriation and cannot open the required local account, hold foreign currency in-country, or issue EGP e-money. Therefore, EG_LLC should contract an Egyptian PSP (Paymob, Fawry, eFinance) or a card-network licensed local acquirer to settle Stripe/Authorize.Net transactions into the EG_LLC operational bank account; where cross-border retail volume is financially material, a foreign interposed sales entity becomes necessary to avoid violating the export-proceeds repatriation window, but the commodities sold and the end-use substance must correspond to the print-and-manufacture business scope.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing sequence: (1) EG_LLC shareholder meeting adopts the capital increase; (2) the foreign shareholder transmits a SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the EG_LLC account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank with the purpose code corresponding to Direct Investment in Egypt — Equity Capital (BPM6 A8) and a payment reference equal to the GAFI certificate and board resolution numbers; (3) the receiving bank conducts inflow verification — Commercial Registry extract, GAFI investment certificate, notarised memorandum, board resolution, UBO and sanctions screening — and credits the funds either to the capital contribution or deposit sub-account or to the operational USD account; (4) settlement completes through the CBE RTGS platform for the EGP leg or through a correspondent banking network (for example CIB or HSBC USD Nostro via J.P. Morgan or Citi) for the hard-currency leg; (5) after credit confirmation, the Commercial Registry and the Tax Authority are updated to certify the capital increase, and the bank files the BoP statement with the CBE. The remitter must never instruct the Egyptian bank to convert the equity proceeds to a fixed EGP rate; conversion, if requested, is executed at the interbank rate upon treasury desk instruction only, to avoid black-market records; all MT103 messages must use the exact legal name EG_LLC in field 59 to preserve the audit trail.Clearing rails ranked per value and counterparty: (a) SWIFT MT103/MT202 bank transfers for mid-value import payments — holographic films, cold-foil substrate, inks, varnish, packaging board, cutting dies, tools, and royalty or licence fees; these are cleared through the Egyptian bank's correspondent network (USD via CHIPS/Fedwire correspondents such as J.P. Morgan, HSBC or Citi; EUR via TARGET2 used by a European correspondent); (b) commercial documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 with ISBP 745 practice for high-value equipment and large print-supplier contracts, opened by the Egyptian account bank and confirmed by an international bank; (c) standby letters of credit or bank guarantees where a foreign supplier demands performance assurance; (d) local EGP supplier settlements through the CBE RTGS same-day clearing rail. Every outbound disbursement requires the underlying pro-forma or commercial invoice, customer and supplier KYC certification, customs declaration where goods are physically imported, and a purpose code matching the CBE balance-of-payments matrix; Therefore, EG_LLC should contract an Egyptian PSP (Paymob, Fawry, eFinance) or a card-network licensed local acquirer to settle Stripe/Authorize.Net transactions into the EG_LLC operational bank account; where cross-border retail volume is financially material, a foreign interposed sales entity becomes necessary to avoid violating the export-proceeds repatriation window, but the commodities sold and the end-use substance must correspond to the print-and-manufacture business scope.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 for controlled related-party transactions triggering a Local File; Master File and CbCR reporting are obligatory when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EGP 15,000,000,000, as set by the Egyptian TP Decree (Ministerial Decree No. 577 of 2020).

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a GCC-style 'Economic Substance Regulation'; however, the Egyptian tax authority applies the abuse-of-law doctrine, sham transaction doctrine, and the beneficial ownership requirement embedded in Law No. 91 of 2005 and anti-avoidance case law. A licensed printing/manufacturing LLC must maintain actual premises, staff, equipment, bank account, stock and management decision-making in Egypt to be recognized as a taxable resident company and to obtain treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of not less than 100% of the evaded tax and legal imprisonment; in aggravated cases, the fine reaches double the tax evaded; interest and late payment surcharges run to 30%.

Director personal liability: Directors/signatories who participate or conspire in filing false returns are personally subject to criminal conviction, joint and several civil liability for restitution of the evaded tax, and travel bans under criminal/administrative enforcement.
Transfer Pricing / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: ETA may re-determine the profit by arm's-length adjustment, disallow expense deductions, impose the 22.5% CIT on the adjusted profit, plus late payment surcharge and a penalty of up to 10% of the incorrectly priced transactions under TP enforcement practice.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute export/import pricing at artificial margins can be assessed as tax representatives personally, with veil-piercing where the LLC is found to be a façade; director travel bans are available in tax debt recovery.
Shell Company / Abusive Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: ETA may disregard the entity for income tax purposes, attribute its income to the Egyptian permanent establishment or beneficial owner, refuse VAT input credits, and impose evasion penalties plus court-ordered annulment in appropriate cases.

Director personal liability: Court-piercing of the corporate veil substitutes personal property liability of directors/shareholders for corporate tax debts, exposes them to denunciation of fake inventories, and may include imprisonment for fraud if third-party creditors were misled.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian AML Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended): financial penalties up to EGP 1,000,000 and imprisonment for 6 months to 7 years for CDD breaches; for proven money-laundering of proceeds, imprisonment not less than 3 years and a fine of EGP 100,000–1,000,000, plus confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and accountants are personally liable for failure to file suspicious transaction reports; travel bans, asset freezing orders and criminal imprisonment apply to those who knowingly facilitate or conceal offshore beneficial ownership.
