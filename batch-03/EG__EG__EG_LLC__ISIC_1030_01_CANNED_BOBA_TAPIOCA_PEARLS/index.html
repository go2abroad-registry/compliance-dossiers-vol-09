Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLCs must first obtain a Tax Card (TIN) from the Egyptian Tax Authority. Procedure: (i) execute statutory incorporation before the ETA-compatible Commercial Registry; (ii) file an ETA registration application (Form No. 49/2) enclosing the commercial register extract, incorporation deed, certificate of deposit of issued capital, board of directors resolution, and valid power of attorney for the tax agent; (iii) obtain a preliminary Tax Card, then undergo the physical tax inspection of the registered head office; (iv) receive the definitive Tax Card bearing the 9-digit Tax ID number; (v) if taxable turnover crosses the VAT threshold, complete a separate VAT registration application to obtain a VAT registration certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign-owned Egyptian LLCs must first obtain a Tax Card (TIN) from the Egyptian Tax Authority. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through elective regime; an LLC is treated as a wholly taxable juridical person. No entity classification election is permitted for tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch rules are limited to anti-avoidance provisions under the Egyptian Income Tax Law (Law No. 91 of 2005) and the new Unified Tax Law framework; no formal hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration: any person whose annual taxable goods/services turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 (VAT Law No. 67 of 2016); simplified registration applies when turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 and does not exceed EGP 1,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated for VAT (0%) under Article 15 of Law No. 67 of 2016, provided that the exporter is registered and the goods are physically purchased and exported under an Egyptian export declaration within the time frame stipulated in the Egyptian Foreign Exchange regulations. Services rendered in Egypt and actually used or consumed entirely outside Egypt (including offsite engineering, quality control, and logistics services for canned boba tapioca pearls) are also zero-rated if the purchaser is a non-resident and the services are not performed on real property in Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (A.C.D. – Automatic Customs Declaration form, stamped and released at the port of exit)
  • Bill of Lading (e.g., MBL or HBL for maritime export; Letter of Credit/FIATA air waybill for air export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Form ETC/dedicated bank statement evidencing receipt of hard-currency export proceeds within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract / purchase order and any LC/Islamic financing instruments.
  • ETA export invoice (electronically cleared via E-Invoicing system bearing the QR code)
  • Certificate of Egyptian origin (for customs preferences if applicable)

Egypt has mandated E-Invoicing (fiscal clearance) for VAT-registered persons under ETA resolution No. 461 of 2021. The taxpayer must integrate its billing system with the ETA via REST/XML APIs; each invoice and credit note must be transmitted to the ETA and receive a digitally stamped 'Soap/coded' QR validation before issuance. E-Receipts are also mandatory for consumer-facing B2C taxpayers from 2023 onward. No physical or paper invoice may be issued in place of an electronically cleared invoice once the taxpayer is integrated. ETA requires real-time (sale-by-sale) clearance and rejects non-compliant invoices; a time-stamped, digitally signed JSON/XML record is returned for each validated invoice.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt retain a full audit trail over zero-rated export invoices. If the exporter fails to furnish the mandatory customs and FX documents within 180 days, the zero rating is reversed and VAT at 14% is immediately payable, plus a late-payment addition of 2% per month (limited to 40%) and a penalty of up to 60% of the tax if fraud is established (Article 60 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016). In addition, the ETA may impose a supplementary inspection and impose double VAT on knowingly false zero-rating, criminalize the document forgery under Egyptian Penal Code, and refer the matter to the Economic Courts; beneficial owners may then be subject to criminal conviction and recovery of the full uncollected VAT plus fines as high as EGP 500,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) operates under a managed-float regime with a unified interbank exchange rate effective 6 March 2024. Current-account convertibility (trade in goods, services, dividends, and intra-group fees) is broadly open to licensed-bank transactions backed by commercial documentation and tax clearances. Capital-account transactions remain more restricted: inward FDI under Law 72 of 2017 must be registered with a CBE-licensed bank at the time of the inward transfer, and onward conversion to EGP is executed at the interbank fixing. Dividend and profit remittances by an LLC are legally unrestricted after audit and tax clearance, but the USD-liquidity queues observed in 2022-2024 have only gradually normalized; large remittances may still face intra-week FX availability sequencing by the receiving bank. The principal repatriation hurdles are bank-level documentary checks (audited financial statements, tax receipts, GAFI registration certificates) rather than exchange-control licenses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts for processed/canned food products must be repatriated through a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank within six (6) months from the bill-of-lading or air-waybill date. There is presently no mandatory conversion ratio (0% surrender): proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account or converted to EGP at the interbank rate at the exporter's discretion. The full invoiced value (including prepayments and retention amounts) must be credited to the exporter's Egyptian account; the exporter must file the CBE export declaration and retain the SWIFT credit advice, collection documents, and customs digital export forms for five (5) years. In a reserve-stress scenario, the CBE retains the legal power to re-impose up to 100% mandatory surrender conversion by circular, and banks may condition export working-capital facilities on proof of timely repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) (Arabic: Al-Bank al-Markazi al-Misri), the sole monetary, banking, and foreign-exchange regulatory authority, acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and CBE foreign-exchange circulars; the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the General Organization for Export and Import Control (GOEIC) are supplementary gatekeepers for capital and trade registration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Paysera) hold no CBE banking license, cannot open EGP-denominated accounts, and cannot receive Egyptian export proceeds under CBE repatriation rules; their use is legally limited to pre-funding the LLC's accounts from abroad or paying non-regulated overheads. A defensible structure uses EMIs solely as zero-balance collection vehicles for small retail e-commerce receivable pools, swept daily to a licensed Tier-1 Egyptian bank via SWIFT MT103. Any reliance on an EMI as settlement agent for letters of credit or capital injections would violate CBE exchange-control circulars and trigger correspondent-bank de-risking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the foreign shareholder wires equity via SWIFT MT103 to the LLC's CBE-registered capital account at the designated Tier-1 bank, citing the LLC's GAFI file number, Egyptian commercial registration number, and purpose code 'capital subscription / paid-up share capital under Law 72 of 2017'; (2) the receiving bank verifies the KYC file and issues an electronic credit advice and CBE inward-transfer report (capital registration certificate) within one to two business days; (3) conversion to EGP is executed exclusively at the daily interbank weighted-average rate upon written instruction; (4) the registered capital certificate becomes the chain-of-title for future dividend repatriation and must be updated with GAFI whenever capital is increased. Rule: payments from third parties or factoring vehicles are not accepted as capital; the remitting beneficial owner must match the GAFI shareholder schedule exactly.For import of tapioca starch, dextrose, acidulants, and food-grade PET or can packaging, the LLC may use: (a) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (at-sight or usance) issued in EGP, USD, or EUR via a licensed Egyptian bank, transmitted by SWIFT MT700/MT707, with settlement under MT103/MT202 against the seller's draft and transport documents; (b) direct telegraphic transfer (SWIFT MT103) for open-account suppliers, permitted after the 2022-2023 CBE import relaxation subject to GOEIC importer registration and a bank-approved proforma invoice; and (c) documentary collections (documents against payment or acceptance) as a middle-risk solution. All outward remittances require the importer's GOEIC registration code, corporate tax card, supplier proforma or contract, transport and customs documents, and the bank's CBE outward-report filing. Export collections flow in via MT103/MT202 against the LC or collection instruction and are traceable to the CBE export-proceeds monitoring module.Deliverable outright FX forwards (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP), tenors 1 to 12 months, available from Tier-1 banks for documentation-backed trade and capital flows; no speculative position permitted.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between the LLC and a licensed bank, matching USD or EUR term-loan liabilities against EGP operational income, under CBE derivatives authorization.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on EGP referencing an agreed interbank midpoint, executable offshore by the foreign parent for economic hedging; the Egyptian LLC may only book onshore NDFs with a bank-confirmed derivative capability and an underlying trade exposure.Fully-paid FX zero-cost collar structures, where offered, for trade-based hedging; naked FX options and leveraged swaps are outside the permitted set for non-financial corporates.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of taxable profits for ordinary Egyptian resident companies, subject to sector-specific regimes, incentives, and special-zone rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 10% on dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company, subject to statutory exceptions and treaty reduction. A 5% rate may apply to qualifying dividends from listed shares or securities where the statutory conditions are met. Dividend distributions must also be reviewed for distributable-profit, corporate-law, accounting, and foreign-exchange compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within four months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 30 April of the following year. The return must generally be accompanied by financial statements and tax reconciliation schedules. A different fiscal year may produce a different deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The final annual liability is generally settled by the annual return deadline. Any statutory advance-payment, installment, withholding, or payment-notice deadline must be followed separately from the annual filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is generally required where the aggregate value of a related-party transaction category exceeds EGP 8 million during the fiscal year, subject to the current executive rules and transaction-category analysis. A Master File may be required for an Egyptian constituent entity of a multinational group meeting the applicable group-revenue and Egyptian filing conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups meeting the prescribed consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly EGP 3 billion or its applicable equivalent under current Egyptian rules.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not generally operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Nevertheless, economic substance is relevant through transfer pricing, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse principles, permanent-establishment rules, general anti-avoidance concepts, substance-over-form analysis, investment-incentive conditions, and the ETA's power to challenge artificial or non-commercial arrangements. An Egyptian manufacturing LLC should maintain real production capacity, personnel, premises, inventory controls, quality systems, procurement functions, decision-making, commercial risk management, and books and records proportionate to its declared activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax, additional tax, administrative fines, collection measures, and potential criminal prosecution under Egyptian tax-procedure and tax-evasion rules. Criminal consequences may include imprisonment commonly ranging from six months to five years and a fine potentially linked to the tax evaded, subject to the applicable offense and current legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, authorized signatories, and responsible finance personnel may incur personal criminal liability where they participated in, ordered, approved, or knowingly facilitated the conduct. Corporate status does not immunize deliberate individual misconduct. Civil restitution, asset attachment, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may arise under a court order and applicable enforcement law.
VAT and Invoicing Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input credits, additional tax, administrative penalties, suspension or restriction of tax registration or clearance, and criminal sanctions for deliberate tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and signatories may face criminal prosecution and personal civil exposure where they knowingly approved false invoices or fraudulent refund claims. Travel restrictions or imprisonment require statutory and judicial grounds.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to arm's-length taxable income, secondary tax consequences where applicable, denial of deductions, additional tax, documentation penalties, interest or surcharges, and possible double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and, in cases involving intentional falsification or evasion, criminal liability. Personal exposure is strongest where the director approved sham agreements, fabricated benchmarking, or knowingly concealed related-party dealings.
Beneficial Ownership, Treaty Abuse, and Conduit Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, withholding at the domestic rate, recovery of underwithheld tax, additional tax, penalties, interest, and possible anti-evasion prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorized signatories may be personally exposed for knowingly submitting false residence or beneficial-ownership evidence. Courts may impose asset restrictions, travel bans, or imprisonment where supported by criminal findings.
Customs, Import Valuation, and Excise Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, confiscation, fines, seizure, licensing consequences, delayed release, and criminal proceedings for smuggling or intentional customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors, customs brokers, and responsible officers may face joint or personal liability where they participated in false declarations. Criminal cases may involve asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, denial or withdrawal of incentives, treaty-benefit denial, permanent-establishment exposure, reassessment, penalties, and possible fraud or money-laundering investigation.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and actual controllers may face personal civil and criminal exposure if the entity is used to conceal ownership, evade tax, or launder proceeds. Veil piercing is fact-dependent and generally requires proof of abuse, fraud, or unlawful personal conduct.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breaches

Statutory sanction: Account freezing, transaction blocking, forfeiture, regulatory penalties, license consequences, criminal prosecution, and imprisonment and fines under Egyptian anti-money-laundering and related criminal laws.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, beneficial owners, and finance personnel may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or reckless failure to perform legally required controls. Travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment may follow judicial or competent-authority action.
Illegal Profit Distribution and Foreign-Exchange Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Repayment or reversal of unlawful distributions, tax withholding reassessment, penalties, banking restrictions, regulatory action, and potential criminal liability for fraud or unauthorized transfer activity.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions may incur fiduciary, civil restitution, and potentially criminal liability. Personal asset attachment, travel restrictions, and imprisonment depend on the specific offense and judicial findings.
Books, Records, and Audit Obstruction

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, estimated assessment, loss of deduction or credit, adverse evidentiary inferences, additional tax, and criminal sanctions for obstruction or document destruction.

Director personal liability: Directors and designated representatives may be personally liable for intentional obstruction, destruction, or certification of false records. Civil and criminal enforcement may include asset restraint, travel restrictions, and imprisonment where authorized.
Unlicensed Manufacturing and Regulatory Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, production suspension, permit cancellation, fines, confiscation or destruction of unsafe goods, tax consequences, and criminal proceedings for serious violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and plant managers may face personal civil or criminal liability for knowingly operating without required approvals or releasing unsafe products. Imprisonment and travel restrictions depend on the governing regulatory offense and court action.
