Competent corporate registry

Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus – RIK), Estonian Commercial Register / e-Business Register (e-äriregister), within the Ministry of Justice area of administration.

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik) — Chapter 5 'Private Limited Company' (Osaühing), sections 135–178; general Commercial Register provisions, sections 23–26; General Part of the Civil Code Act; State Fees Act (Riigilõivuseadus); e-Business Register operating rules of the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Entry in the Commercial Register (Äriregistri avaldus), submitted through the standard e-Business Register online electronic questionnaire together with the articles of association (põhikiri) and the founding resolution.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 3 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No mandatory paid-up minimum capital is required at registration: a private limited company may be incorporated under the Commercial Code with subscribed but unpaid capital. Where a conventional share capital is adopted, the statutory benchmark capital is EUR 2,500; cash contributions are deposited to a restricted bank account of the company 'in formation' or an escrow-type notary account and may not be withdrawn prior to entry in the register. If capital below EUR 2,500 is used, the company must allocate at least 20% of annual net profit to its legal reserve until EUR 2,500 is accumulated.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA), e-MTA digital portal at https://www.emta.ee; company registry filings are processed by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RKIK) via the e-Business Register.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity can obtain an Estonian tax identity in two ways: (i) if it establishes a branch or permanent establishment in Estonia, it must first file for registration in the Commercial Register through the e-Business Register and will receive an Estonian registry code (registrikood), which serves as the primary taxpayer identification number; (ii) if the entity is not establishing a branch but needs an Estonian VAT number for supplies taxable in Estonia and not reverse-charged to the Estonian customer, it must apply directly to EMTA through e-MTA before commencing the taxable activity and will be issued a separate VAT payer code. The application must include the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution, proof of legal existence, and, where required, appointment of a fiscal representative. The registry code or VAT code is then used for VAT returns, customs filings and other tax declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Estonian Osaühing (OÜ) is a resident legal entity subject to Estonian corporate income tax as a separate tax subject. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment. Current tax on profits is deferred and triggered on profit distributions, deemed distributions, fringe benefits and disallowed expenses; the standard tax rate is 20% of the gross distribution (20/80 of the net amount distributed).

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election exists under Estonian law. Entity classification is determined by the Commercial Register and the Income Tax Act; a foreign company cannot elect flow-through treatment. Available statutory elections applicable to an OÜ include voluntary VAT registration below the 40,000 EUR turnover threshold, the reduced rate for qualifying regular profit distributions, and the general choice to retain profits without current corporate tax. Estonia has no private-sector VAT group registration, and hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD apply automatically without elective entity classification options.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year (exclusive of VAT) of taxable supplies made in Estonia. A non-established taxable person must register before making supplies taxable in Estonia unless the recipient is a taxable person liable for VAT under the reverse charge; an Estonian-established person whose taxable turnover exceeds the threshold must register with effect from the beginning of the following tax period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Estonia zero-rates exports of goods outside the EU, intra-EU supplies of goods to taxable persons holding a valid EU VAT registration number, and certain services directly connected with exports or international transport. For physical goods the supply must leave Estonia and the EU within the statutory period and be fully evidenced by customs and commercial records. For data-centre colocation, bare-metal hosting and related IT services, the treatment depends on the precise contractual characterisation: if the supply involves the grant of a right to use specific physical data-centre space in Estonia, it may be treated as a service connected with immovable property and remain subject to Estonian VAT; if it is a standard IT-hosting or service supply to a business customer established outside Estonia, the place of supply is the customer's country and Estonian VAT is outside the scope. The supplier must retain a complete substantiation file, including contract, customer status and VAT ID, IP and geolocation evidence, performance data, service delivery reports and payment records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document lodged with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board)
  • Original Bill of Lading, air waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident customer

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control regime applies to B2B transactions. Taxable persons must file VAT returns electronically through e-MTA; machine-to-machine e-MTA APIs are available for approved software senders. E-invoices are accepted, and for Estonian public-sector procurement, suppliers must transmit e-invoices in a PEPPOL/UBL-compatible format through the government e-invoice environment, consistent with Directive 2014/55/EU.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board scrutinises export and out-of-scope treatment by matching the VAT return, customs departure records, inventory data and foreign exchange movements. If the taxpayer cannot substantiate the export, VAT is reassessed at 22% with statutory default interest at 0.06% per day and an additional tax penalty of up to 20% of the assessed sum in cases of negligence. Fraudulent claims may trigger criminal tax evasion liability for natural persons with imprisonment up to five years, up to seven years for large-scale evasion, and personal or fiscal representative liability for the VAT clawed back. Retrospective reassessment is generally possible within statutory limitation periods, extended in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full member of the euro area; the euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender and freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange controls or restrictions exist on the repatriation of capital, dividends, interest, or profits to non-residents under the EU Treaty freedoms and Estonian law. Conversion between EUR and other currencies is unrestricted at commercial market rates. Estonia maintains full liberalization of capital movements; however, statistical reporting and beneficial ownership declarations to Eesti Pank and Finantsinspektsioon may be required for large cross-border flows, especially for aggregated credit institution records. Profit remittance is subject to standard withholding tax (generally 0% for dividends paid to residents and often 0% under parental directive or tax treaty; non-treaty may trigger 20% or less), and no currency conversion approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements for export proceeds in Estonia. Estonian law does not require repatriation of proceeds within a set timeline or conversion into EUR. However, all resident enterprises must include export transactions in their periodic value-added tax (VAT) and income tax declarations, and must maintain proper documentation (invoices, customs declarations, SWIFT records) for the MTA and statistical authorities for a minimum retention of seven (7) years. For data center colocation rental income (cross-border service export), the proceeds can be retained in any currency, but prudent treasury policy recommends converting to EUR to mitigate currency risk.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro area monetary policy and the European Systemic Risk Board for macroprudential oversight. External transactions are additionally supervised by the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) and the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA) for compliance with the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (MLTFPA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for non-EU/EEA offshore EMIs; High for EU-licensed (passporting) EMIs, such as those authorized by the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) or other EEA authorities under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Offshore EMIs outside the EEA (e.g., licensed in BVI, Cayman, or unregulated jurisdictions) are generally unacceptable for settlement treasury, may trigger severe AML/KYC scrutiny, and are not integrated with the Estonian payment rails (TARGET2-Estonia, SEPA). For operational treasury, only EMIs holding a valid e-money license in the EU/EEA and operating through a correspondent bank in Estonia or another euro area state are legally viable. The data center leases must ensure that all EMI payments are traceable and can settle in EUR via SEPA Instant or TARGET2.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for equity injection into an Estonian OÜ: Use SEPA (for EUR) or SWIFT MT103 (for non-EUR) to the bank account in Estonia. The remitter must use the correct purpose code defined in the Integrated Nomenclature of Currency Operations (INO, Eesti Pank classification); for direct investment in share capital, code 'D.1.1' (Equity capital in Estonia) or 'S.1.1' (Direct investment) should be referenced in the SWIFT 70-field as 'INCAP' or 'FROINV'. For shareholder loans, use purpose code 'S.1.2' (Loans from direct investors). The recipient bank will require the subscription resolution and a validation of the transfer amount against the registered share capital or supplementary payment. If using a foreign intermediary, the beneficiary IBAN must be the Estonian operating or escrow account. Ensure the wire is routed through a correspondent that is on the Estonian Bank's approved list (e.g., Deutsche Bank, BNP, or local branch), to avoid sanctions screening delays.Clearing rails for trade settlements related to data center colocation (payments to hardware suppliers, cloud providers, energy vendors, and cross-border maintenance contracts): (a) For European Economic Area and EU counterparties, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) for sub-100k EUR payments with valid IBAN and a reference to the invoice number and VAT line. (b) For non-EUR jurisdictions (e.g., USD, GBP, SEK), use SWIFT MT103 wire transfer through the Estonian bank's correspondent network, with purpose code 'S.1.8' (Trade credits) or 'A.2.1' (Services) under INO. (c) For larger or more complex transactions, issue an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit (LC) through the bank's trade finance desk, typically on SWIFT MT700/MT760, aligned with ICC UCP600 rules. All outbound disbursements must be backed by an invoice, a full service or lease agreement, and a beneficial ownership declaration for the counterparty. For data center infrastructure, an escrow clearing structure may be established using a commercial escrow account at the same bank to ensure performance and refund conditions.Outright foreign exchange forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/SEK, EUR/GBP) tradable OTC with the banking counterparty under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a standard FX master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for illiquid or restricted currencies (e.g., RUB, TRY) if the counterparty has access to an offshore NDF market; execution is permitted under Estonian law provided the underlying exposure is genuine and documented as a forward transaction under the EMIR clearing threshold.Currency swaps (FX swaps) between EUR and other major currencies for liquidity management, subject to standard prudential limits, EMIR trade reporting to the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority, and collateralization requirements if the counterparty is not a financial institution.Interest rate / cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) are permissible for aligning lease payments in foreign currencies with the funding currency; these must be entered into with an EU-licensed bank and require post-trade transparency under MiFID II.Exchange-traded derivatives (futures and options) on EUR crosses are available on regulated venues (e.g., Eurex) for treasury hedging, but due to the low-risk nature of the data center model they are treated as auxiliary.
