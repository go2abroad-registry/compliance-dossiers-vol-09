Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an LLC under Law No. 159/1981; the founding members freely fix the subscribed capital in the Memorandum of Association. The full agreed capital contributions must be deposited into a temporary corporate bank account opened with an Egyptian licensed bank, and the bank's deposit certificate must accompany the GAFI filing; the funds remain blocked until the incorporation is completed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must (1) incorporate through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain the certificate of incorporation; (2) register with the Egyptian Tax Authority's Taxpayer Registration Department to obtain the Tax Card (TIN) by filing the statutory forms, power of attorney, and the LLC's constitutional documents; (3) activate VAT registration and e-invoice enrollment through the ETA portal when taxable turnover reaches or exceeds the mandatory threshold; and (4) for non-resident vendors without a PE, registration is limited to withholding tax obligations unless an Egyptian commercial establishment is created. The LLC's place of effective management in Egypt automatically creates corporate residence and full CIT registration obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must (1) incorporate through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain the certificate of incorporation; (2) register with the Egyptian Tax Authority's Taxpayer Registration Department to obtain the Tax Card (TIN) by filing the statutory forms, power of attorney, and the LLC's constitutional documents; (3) activate VAT registration and e-invoice enrollment through the ETA portal when taxable turnover reaches or exceeds the mandatory threshold; and (4) for non-resident vendors without a PE, registration is limited to withholding tax obligations unless an Egyptian commercial establishment is created. The LLC's place of effective management in Egypt automatically creates corporate residence and full CIT registration obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through election regime. An Egyptian LLC is mandatorily classified as a juridical person and taxed at entity level on its worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended. No hybrid mismatch election is available domestically. Voluntary VAT registration is permitted below the mandatory threshold, and the arm's-length transfer-pricing regime allows taxpayers to elect certain valuation methods, such as the transactional net margin method subject to ETA approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods; EGP 150,000 for services. Voluntary registration is available at lower thresholds (EGP 100,000 for goods and EGP 50,000 for services) as recognized by ETA practice.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Egyptian VAT Law zero-rates the export of goods, including medical devices such as ECG monitors, when the goods are physically exported outside Egypt under a customs export declaration and a valid sales contract with a foreign buyer. The exporter must be VAT-registered, maintain transport documents evidencing departure, receive the export consideration in convertible foreign currency through Egyptian banks, and comply with e-invoice transmission for the export invoice. Services that are directly utilized abroad may also qualify for zero-rating, provided the recipient is outside Egypt and the economic use occurs offshore. The zero-rated supply enables recovery or refund of input VAT attributable to export activities.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment date
  • Commercial invoice and foreign sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • VAT e-invoice with ETA-approved QR code and matching shipment references

Egypt's E-Invoice system requires all registered B2B taxpayers to transmit structured XML/JSON invoice data in near real time through the ETA digital portal or an approved ERP API connection. The ETA clears each invoice electronically and assigns a unique invoice ID and QR code, which must be printed or embedded in the invoice. B2C receipts are separately subject to the E-Receipt system. Compliance with e-invoicing is mandatory in successive taxpayer waves; failure to obtain a clearance ID or use the prescribed digital stamping invalidates the invoice for VAT credit purposes and triggers penalties.

The ETA co-ordinates with customs authorities and Central Bank of Egypt records to verify zero-rated supplies. If an exporter fails to provide complete export documentation, does not receive export proceeds within the prescribed 180-day FX repatriation deadline, mis-states the destination market, or submits fraudulent customs/remittance evidence, the ETA will reverse the zero-rating, re-assess the supply at 14% VAT, and impose late payment interest plus penalties of up to 1% per month and additional administrative fines. Criminal liability, including imprisonment, may attach in cases of malicious evasion or falsification of export documentation, and refunds already paid may be summarily clawed back with a 20% administrative surcharge.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. Since the November 2016 FX liberalization, CBE-licensed banks are permitted to price the Egyptian pound freely, and current-account trade and service transactions are broadly convertible, subject to lodgment of underlying documentation through CBE forms (e.g., Form 4/A, Form 3) and bank screening. Capital-account transfers, including foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, portfolio investment, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, are subject to enhanced scrutiny, mandatory CBE registration or licensing, tax-clearance certificates, and proof of compliant entry of funds into Egypt. Profit remittances require audited financial statements, corporate resolutions, tax clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority, and evidence that the underlying funds were lawfully converted and credited through a licensed bank. In periods of system-wide FX tightness, CBE may prioritize essential imports, defer discretionary repatriations, or temporarily apply administrative queues, creating practical convertibility risks despite formal openness.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current CBE instructions and Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, private-sector export receipts are no longer subject to mandatory conversion into EGP. However, 100% of export proceeds must be received through an Egyptian licensed bank and deposited into the exporter's foreign-currency account within a maximum of 30 days from the date of shipment, unless a longer period is specifically pre-approved by CBE in writing. Public-sector and commodity strategic export proceeds remain potentially subject to directed surrender or priority-crediting arrangements by CBE decree. Exports must be registered electronically, and banks must obtain the export registration, invoice, bill of lading, and customs declaration before releasing foreign currency to the exporter's account. Failure to timely repatriate export proceeds can result in bank account restrictions, imposition of exchange-control penalties, and adverse credit-report treatment.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – the principal monetary, banking, and foreign exchange regulatory authority acting under Egyptian Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, together with the Egyptian Ministry of Finance and the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) in respect of investment and exchange-control matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for unlicensed offshore digital EMI platforms. Egypt does not permit passporting or branch operation by foreign electronic-money institutions unless they obtain a CBE license under the Payment Activities Law No. 18 of 2019 and the CBE's payment-system regulations. Offshore EMIs may provide software-based payment initiation or card-issuing services only through a CBE-licensed bank as settlement sponsor, but they cannot independently hold EGP clearing accounts, provide statutory escrow accounts, or directly execute FX conversions. Any such arrangement increases AML/CFT compliance burden and is rated medium-to-low viability as a primary treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital transfers must flow via SWIFT wire (MT103) from the non-resident shareholder's bank account to the Egyptian LLC's capital escrow account maintained with a tier-1 Egyptian bank. The purpose code should be stated as 'Foreign Equity Investment' or 'Capital Contribution to an Egyptian LLC' under CBE's investor registration coding. The LLC must submit the GAFI incorporation certificate, the board resolution approving the capital contribution, the executed share-subscription agreement, and the UBO structure declaration. For equity injections exceeding per-transaction CBE thresholds, the receiving bank must verify registration of the investment in the CBE's Foreign Investment Registry and may request prior approval from GAFI/CBE for issuance of shares to a non-resident. All foreign-currency capital funds may be retained in an onshore foreign-currency account or converted to EGP at the licensed bank's offered rate. For optional conversion, banks require an executed Form 4/A or its electronic equivalent, and the bank will issue an exchange receipt that is required for subsequent repatriation of profits or capital.Outbound trade payments for component imports and third-party services are cleared via: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for advance payments, open-account settlements, or commission payments; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600, transmitted through SWIFT MT700, for secured import financing; and (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) under SWIFT MT400-series. All outbound disbursements require submission of a completed CBE import declaration (Form 4/A), the underlying purchase contract or proforma invoice, export loan/advance-payment receipt, and a valid tax identification number. For EGP-denominated payments, settlement occurs through the CBE RTGS system (Egyptian Pound Real-Time Gross Settlement) with same-day finality. Foreign-currency settlement is executed through correspondent banks in USD, EUR, or GBP via the Egyptian bank's nostro accounts. For imports of ECG-monitor components, an import letter-of-credit over USD 50,000 or its equivalent may also require a commercial invoice certified by the Egyptian Chamber of Commerce or an authorized forwarder, a certificate of origin, and standard shipping documents.Onshore deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts with tenors generally between one and twelve months, executed between Egyptian LLCs and CBE-licensed banks based on a forward-leg EGP/FCY trade, subject to bank credit lines and underlying trade/balance-sheet exposure.FX swaps (classic back-to-back maturity exchanges of one currency against the other) used for rolling forward liquidity and managing timing mismatches on trade receivables and payables, permitted where the bank has a licensed derivatives license.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps for project-finance or borrowings, permitted for hedging re-pricing risk when entered into through a licensed bank under ISDA/CSA documentation and reported to CBE.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing EGP for use by the foreign parent or a non-resident treasury counterparty, provided that (i) the Egyptian subsidiary does not transact directly in the offshore NDF, (ii) the subsidiary's bank is informed in advance of any resulting synthetic payout that may be subject to CBE cross-border payment review, and (iii) the NDF is structured strictly as a hedge with no speculative element.Currency options (European-style) available from tier-1 licensed banks for hedging forecast receipts or export proceeds, where the notional principal is supported by outstanding commercial exposure and the premium is payable in FX or EGP in line with CBE circulars.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on Egyptian-source dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced under an applicable double-taxation agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within four months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the due date is 30 April; for a 30 June fiscal year-end, the due date is 31 October.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation. A Master File is also expected when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 500,000,000 or ETA requires it during a transfer-pricing audit.

Economic substance scope: Egypt is not a Caribbean-law Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction, but the Income Tax Law and ETA practice apply a robust substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance doctrine. The Egyptian LLC must maintain real premises, personnel, decision-making capacity, and core income-generating functions in Egypt. Artificial shell arrangements, back-to-back contracts, or conduit licensing can be recharacterized, treaty benefits denied under beneficial-ownership analysis, and tax advantages reversed with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Bookkeeping

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to five years and a fine equal to 30% to 50% of the evaded tax under Law No. 91 of 2005; ETA additionally assesses the underpaid tax, a 2% monthly late-payment surcharge, and a deficiency penalty of up to 40%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations risk personal joint liability for the company's unpaid tax and penalties; ETA may issue travel bans and asset freezes, and criminal conviction may result in imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer-pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, additional corporate income tax at 22.5%, statutory penalties, monthly delay surcharge, and administrative fines for missing Local File or Master File documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the transfer-pricing principal may be held jointly liable for the additional tax where they knowingly approved fabricated margins; severe cases expose them to criminal sanctions and travel restrictions during collection.
Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Claim

Statutory sanction: ETA denies treaty benefits, collects statutory WHT at 10% for dividends and 20% for interest/royalties, imposes penalties, and recharacterizes the arrangement under anti-avoidance and beneficial-ownership rules.

Director personal liability: Local directors and in-house legal officers who implement the conduit structure can be personally assessed for short-paid withholding tax and face civil restitution; the Egyptian company's responsible officers may be subject to travel bans and criminal prosecution.
AML / Foreign Exchange Evasion

Statutory sanction: Violations of Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 and CBE foreign-exchange circulars attract criminal fines, forfeiture of funds, revocation of licenses, and imprisonment; repeated breaches are treated as aggravated AML offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly use parallel financial channels are personally liable for civil restitution and fines; courts may impose travel bans, asset seizures, and imprisonment for directors implicated in AML violations.
