Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — https://www.eta.gov.eg (e-invoicing portal: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg); customs counterpart: Egyptian Customs Authority via the NAFEZA Single Administrative Window (https://www.nafeza.gov.eg).

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the LLC with GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) or the local Commercial Registry to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate and notarized Articles of Association. (2) Within 30 days of CR issuance, file the unified tax registration application with the ETA. (3) Submit the CR, Articles of Association, registered office lease/title deed, legal representative's national ID or passport and power of attorney, and a board resolution appointing the tax representative. (4) Receive the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) digital Tax Card; the same TIN governs corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and customs declarations. (5) Where taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 annually, register for VAT and obtain a VAT certificate through the same ETA portal; exporters should register immediately even below the threshold to access the 0% export rating and input VAT recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone optional. An Egyptian LLC is a resident corporate entity taxed at 22.5% CIT on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders are taxed on a territorial (Egyptian-source income) basis. No pass-through (check-the-box) classification exists under Law 91/2005. Manufacturing operations inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone), qualified free zones, or under the Investment Law No. 72/2017 may qualify for corporate tax holidays (up to 5 years extendable) or investment tax deductions (30-50% of capital expenditure).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box; statutory elections include: (i) group taxation election for 100%-owned resident subsidiaries (Law 91/2005 Art. 53); (ii) depreciation method election (straight-line or accelerated for certain machinery); (iii) investment incentive election under Investment Law No. 72/2017 (special deduction vs. free-zone treatment); (iv) 'Golden License' consolidated licensing election (unified approval, customs, and VAT treatment through the GAFI single window); (v) foreign tax credit election under applicable double tax treaties; (vi) VAT input tax deduction method election under Law 67/2016 (direct attribution vs. pro-rata recovery rules); (vii) free-zone goods import/export documentation election (Closed List regime).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual turnover of rated (taxable) supplies in Egypt; mandatory registration once the threshold is met, with exporters permitted and encouraged to register voluntarily below the threshold to claim input VAT refunds. Registration is also mandatory for foreign entities supplying taxable goods or services in Egypt through a fixed establishment or an authorized agent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies under Art. 19 of Law 67/2016 to: (1) tangible goods physically exported from Egypt, evidenced by an approved customs export declaration; (2) toll manufacturing / inward processing for foreign principals where finished goods (e.g., manufactured submarine fiber-optic cable) are re-exported within ETA-prescribed periods; (3) services directly related to exported goods or international transportation (e.g., subsea cable-laying services to non-residents); (4) services performed in Egypt but consumed outside Egypt by non-resident customers; (5) supplies delivered in free zones or SCZone qualifying regimes; and (6) export-oriented supplies into bonded warehouses. The exporter must prove actual physical removal of goods and receipt of foreign exchange.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) issued by Egyptian Customs via NAFEZA
  • Full set of Bills of Lading issued in the exporter's name by a licensed maritime carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing credit of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Signed commercial invoice and export contract with incoterms and payment terms
  • Packing list, insurance certificate, and, when required, the Certificate of Origin
  • Manufacturing or inward processing declaration (for toll manufacturing) and ETA refund support schedule

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing for all registered taxpayers under ETA Decree No. 436/2021 (B2B and B2G): every invoice must be generated in JSON, digitally signed (HMAC), and submitted via ETA-certified REST APIs to the e-invoicing portal; upon validation the ETA issues a unique acceptance code (UUID) before the invoice is legally recognized (near-real-time fiscal clearance). Physical revenue stamps are abolished for invoicing and replaced by an encrypted quick-response digital stamp affixed to each invoice (e-stamping). The parallel e-Receipt mandate (B2C) is being phased in under Decree No. 264/2023; non-compliant invoices are rejected and input VAT deductions are disallowed.

The ETA performs desk and on-spot audits of export zero-rating claims before refund disbursement. Penalties and recovery exposures: (i) repayment of the VAT clawed back with interest at the CBE lending rate, plus an additional tax penalty of 30% to 100% of the evaded tax (Law 67/2016 Arts. 68-70); (ii) criminal prosecution (imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and fines of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000) for fraudulent or forged export documentation, false bills of lading, or fictitious remittance certificates (Arts. 74-76); (iii) revocation of the 0% rate, denial of pending refunds, and suspension of the taxpayer's e-invoicing registration for repeated violations; (iv) retrospective reassessment and clawback within a 5-year statute of limitation from the tax due date; (v) where foreign currency is not repatriated within 180 days (unless an authorized CBE/ETA extension is obtained), the refund right lapses and input VAT previously refunded is reclaimed with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and has operated a managed-to-market-determined flexible exchange-rate regime since March 2024. Current-account transactions, including profit remittances, dividends, trade settlements, royalties, and services payments, are fully convertible without prior CBE approval but remain subject to documentary review by the mandated reporting bank as prescribed under CBE Financial Inclusion and Treasury Directives. Capital-account transactions, such as initial equity contribution, intra-group shareholder loans, debt servicing, and disposal proceeds, are lawful for companies registered under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and are repatriable upon presentation of audited financial statements and a CBE tax-clearance certificate evidencing settlement of applicable Egyptian withholding taxes. Practical friction persists in the form of periodic USD liquidity tightness in the Egyptian banking sector; therefore, maintaining pre-approved import/export purpose codes, accurate BOP classifications, and clean source-of-funds documentation is essential to avoid clearing delays. No local-currency surrender requirement derives from the convertibility regime, and non-resident shareholders may hold their invested capital proceeds in foreign-currency accounts within the banking system pending repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE Instruction No. 2 of February 2022, as amended, requires resident exporters to repatriate proceeds from exported goods to a licensed Egyptian bank within a maximum statutory window of 6 months from the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date. For services and turnkey telecom infrastructure contracts, the repatriation ceiling is 3 months from date of completion, extendable to 12 months with the CBE's prior written approval on the strength of the underlying sovereign contract. Once repatriated, exporters may retain 100% of the foreign-currency proceeds in onshore foreign-currency accounts without any legal obligation to convert into Egyptian pounds; conversion, if undertaken, must occur through a licensed bank at the prevailing interbank market rate. The company must attach to each export shipment a CBE Export Proceeds Form, the customs export declaration, the commercial invoice, and proof of shipment; the collecting bank issues an Export Proceeds Registration for each transaction to permit subsequent use of funds for import settlements, dividend repatriation, or hedging.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Foreign Exchange Regulation issued by its Board of Directors. The CBE is the sole licensing, supervisory, and enforcement authority for banks, currency exchange companies, and payment service providers in Egypt. It operates the interbank FX market, maintains the national SWIFT/RTGS settlement infrastructure, and is the mandatory counterparty for balance-of-payments reporting by all licensed financial institutions. The General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) registers equity inflows but exercises no independent FX-control mandate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW, with only non-core ancillary utility. The CBE does not recognize offshore digital banks, virtual-account EMIs, or global fintech treasury platforms as acceptable settlement venues for a resident Egyptian manufacturing LLC. All statutory FX obligations, including export-proceeds repatriation, capital injection, and import payments, must pass through a CBE-licensed bank under Law No. 194 of 2020. An offshore fintech EMI may serve as a minor blockchain-based payments supplement for travel, marketing services, or nominal subscriptions, but it may neither receive export proceeds nor effect LC-backed settlements, and its use above EGP 100,000 per single operation triggers mandatory CBE reporting and, in practice, rejection by the appointed external auditor. The legally viable approach is to maintain a physical Tier-1 local bank relationship, with a secondary digital access layer provided by the bank's own corporate internet-banking and host-to-host channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All incoming foreign direct investment must be routed through the licensed Egyptian banking system via SWIFT. The recommended protocol is as follows: (1) the EG LLC opens a dedicated non-resident investment sub-account at a CBE-licensed Tier-1 commercial bank, explicitly annotated for foreign equity injection; (2) the offshore shareholder issues an SWIFT MT103 citing the beneficiary as the LLC, using the purpose code 'FDI Equity Subscription' together with the notarized board resolution number and the shareholder reference; (3) the receiving bank validates the UBO register, source of funds, and the CBE capital-registration declaration furnished by counsel, then credits the account as 'BOP 1101-E Equity Capital Inflow'; (4) the bank issues an FX transaction receipt and inward-remittance certificate, which the LLC's legal counsel uses to refresh the GAFI ownership register; (5) conversion into EGP for local operating expenses is effected at the interbank market rate, executed through recognized dealing platforms and settled over Egyptian RTGS/CBE infra-2022 rails; (6) the bank must be provided the approved investment file and the valid tax-card before any part of the contribution is applied to operational expenditure.Outbound trade payments for subsea cable production inputs (copper conductors, PE insulation, fiber-optic units, steel armoring wire, gel compounds) should be cleared through the following rails in hierarchy: (1) Usance or sight Documentary Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) opened by the Egyptian bank with confirmation add-on from a top-tier International Bank (HSBC, Citi, MUFG, or Standard Chartered) to eliminate country-bank credit friction; the LC is the default rail for shipments exceeding USD 1.0 million because it simultaneously protects the foreign supplier and becomes eligible for export-backed refinancing; (2) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfer for low-value high-frequency deliveries below USD 1.0 million, with full CBE BOP classification as 'Product/Service Import Payment', supported by proforma invoice, vendor contract, and import-duty proof where applicable; (3) Egyptian bank's own cross-currency ledger transfer for payments directed to free-zone suppliers, which requires a CBE free-zone clearance certificate; and (4) for strategic sovereign-backed procurement, a domestic stand-by letter of credit or bank-acceptance draft issued by the Tier-1 bank against a sovereign payment guarantee. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW, with only non-core ancillary utility. The CBE does not recognize offshore digital banks, virtual-account EMIs, or global fintech treasury platforms as acceptable settlement venues for a resident Egyptian manufacturing LLC. All statutory FX obligations, including export-proceeds repatriation, capital injection, and import payments, must pass through a CBE-licensed bank under Law No. 194 of 2020. An offshore fintech EMI may serve as a minor blockchain-based payments supplement for travel, marketing services, or nominal subscriptions, but it may neither receive export proceeds nor effect LC-backed settlements, and its use above EGP 100,000 per single operation triggers mandatory CBE reporting and, in practice, rejection by the appointed external auditor. The legally viable approach is to maintain a physical Tier-1 local bank relationship, with a secondary digital access layer provided by the bank's own corporate internet-banking and host-to-host channels.
