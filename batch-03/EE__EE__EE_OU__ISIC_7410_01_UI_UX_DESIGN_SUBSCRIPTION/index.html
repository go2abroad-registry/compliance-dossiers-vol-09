Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation: OÜ is a tax-resident capital company; Estonian CIT is distribution-based, meaning undistributed profits are taxed at 0% and distributed profits at 20% (gross-up equivalent 25%); profits distributed to non-resident shareholders are also subject to Estonian dividend taxation at 20% unless treaty relief applies. No pass-through or transparent classification is available for an OÜ.

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Estonia; ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically under domestic law. Available elections: (i) regular-distribution election to tax qualifying dividends to resident individuals and treaty-protected EEA/non-resident individuals at 14% plus a deferred 7% tax at subsequent distribution; (ii) VAT cash-basis (payments-basis) election for qualifying small taxable persons; (iii) Union OSS / non-Union OSS election for remote and electronic services, including UI/UX design subscription supplies to EU consumers; (iv) optional election to use a shorter VAT return period where administrative convenience is demonstrated.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
22%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Resident taxable persons and foreign persons with establishment in Estonia: EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover threshold; intra-Community distance sales of goods: EUR 10,000 annual threshold; non-resident persons without an Estonian establishment have no de minimis threshold and must register prior to any taxable supply of services or goods in Estonia.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods: 0% VAT applies where the goods are dispatched or transported to a non-EU destination and leave the EU within 180 days, or to an EU member state as an intra-Community supply (customer VAT ID required). Services: Estonian VAT is not charged (0% / outside scope) where the place of supply of the UI/UX design subscription service is outside Estonia under the B2B place-of-supply rule (customer's seat of business); the supplier must possess solid documentary proof of the customer's identity, VAT registration or OECD residence, and the commercial substance of the service. Supplies made through Stripe Billing or Wise rails do not alter the analysis.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Estonia is a full member of the European Economic and Monetary Union, and the euro is fully and freely convertible on both current and capital account. The EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Articles 63–66 TFEU) apply without any residual exchange-control approval or licensing mechanism. There are no restrictions on profit remittance, distribution of dividends, or repatriation of capital; the only fiscal consideration is Estonia's corporate income tax system under which retained profits are tax-free but distributions are taxed at 20% on gross distributed amounts (or reduced rates under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable tax treaty).

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds in Estonia. Export receipts generated by the OÜ may be held in any currency in an Estonian bank account or any EEA-licensed account without obligation to convert them to euro. Under EU single-currency rules and Estonian anti-money-laundering law, the sole obligations are accurate bookkeeping, appropriate transaction purpose coding, and tax reporting to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. For CJEU and OW authorities, all EUR accounts are inside the TARGET2 system; foreign-currency accounts are subject to normal bank commercial terms, not exchange-control approvals.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), integrated with Finantsinspektsioon (Financial Supervision Authority) for banking supervision and prudential oversight.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
High for euro-denominated subscription collections, contractor payouts, and cross-border disbursements. EU-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe SA, Monese, and Holvi can be onboarded remotely and are passportable into Estonia under the EU E-Money Directive. They are legally permitted to execute payments, issue and manage digital e-money accounts, and safeguard client funds in pooled accounts, but they cannot offer credit facilities, overdrafts, or investment products. For the low-risk UI/UX subscription model, an EMI is operationally viable as the primary settlement rail for high-frequency, low-value recurring invoices; however, for large capital injections, shareholder distribution flows, or legal treasury commitments, a full commercial bank account meeting EBA liquidity and correspondent-banking standards is preferable to avoid counterparty concentration and to satisfy counterparties conducting enhanced due diligence.

Root cause: Non-resident e-residency company with a foreign shareholder and no physical office in Estonia; underwriting flags cross-border ownership and zero-substance management.

        
Defense evidence: Certified copy of the OÜ registration excerpt (Äriregister), share register, board resolution appointing the director, and powers of attorney; passport copies of all shareholders in executive positions and beneficial owners; proof of residential address; an ownership and control declaration; a notarized statement of source of wealth with 12-month bank statements from the home jurisdiction; tax residence certificate; detailed business plan for the UI/UX design subscription agency; signed client and supplier contracts; a professional portfolio demonstrating actual design service delivery; evidence of e-Residency usage; and an irrevocable commitment to annual Estonian tax filings with an approved tax advisor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Shareholder capital injections into the OÜ are processed without exchange-control approval via SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-origin funds or via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA inbound wires. The remittance instruction must identify the transaction as an equity injection or shareholder loan using a structurally precise purpose code: for capital contributions, use SWIFT Category 'CapiContribution' or 'CUBU' (Capital Investment in non-financial corporations) in the purpose code field; for shareholder loans, use 'LOAN' and include the loan agreement reference. To prevent rejection, the bank statement narration should clearly read: 'Payment on behalf of the shareholder towards the establishment / increase of share capital of OÜ [name], legal entity identifier and registry code.' Supporting documents must be pre-submitted to the receiving bank through its corporate online portal: an up-to-date excerpt from the Estonian Commercial Register showing the authorized share capital and the capital increase resolution, a notarized board resolution approving the transfer, a source-of-funds statement, and a copy of the subscription agreement. The inbound funds can then be credited and booked either to the paid-in share capital in the Register or to a shareholders' current account / subordinated loan ledger. No minimum share capital threshold applies to an OÜ, but prudent capital modeling for real subscriptions, accounts receivable collections, and software-subscription procurement costs is expected by Eesti Pank-supervised banks.For the UI/UX design subscription business, outbound trade disbursements consist primarily of subcontractor design fees, stock photography licensing, SaaS tool subscriptions, cloud compute charges, and occasional royalty payments. The optimal clearing rails are: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer for all euro payees in the EEA, carrying the UETR and a structured remittance reference; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA contractors and foreign licensors, with strict compliance against EU sanctions, US OFAC secondary sanctions, and FATF lists; and (iii) commercial bank payment undertakings or escrow services for large milestone settlements. Letters of credit are not standard for intangible design services and are generally disfavored due to documentation burdens; where required by a foreign client, an MT700 documentary credit with a clean presentation of engine-invoice, proof of delivery report, and signed electronic acceptance is commercially feasible. All outbound wires should be supported by a signed invoice, a task acceptance report, VAT treatment documentation (including reverse-charge justification for B2B services across EU borders), and a fully populated OFAC/sanction screen. This rail blueprint preserves the audit trail required for Estonian corporate tax compliance and distributable profit calculations.Deliverable FX forward contracts between EUR and USD, GBP, SEK, and NOK with a licensed EEA credit institution or MiFID-regulated broker; such contracts are permissible without Eesti Pank approval but are subject to EMIR mandatory clearing and margin posting if the counterparty is a financial counterparty above the clearing threshold.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) transactions for currencies where an onshore deliverable market is unavailable, using a cash-settlement convention referenced to a published spot rate; permitted for hedging subscription revenues from emerging-markets clients, but limited by bank credit appetite and the absence of an official Estonian FX control regime.European-style FX options (both bought and sold) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/JPY, executed OTC under an ISDA Master Agreement or on a regulated exchange; standard hedge accounting documentation is required to prevent speculative characterization.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) employed to align long-term design-subscription receivables with corresponding purchase obligations; exposure must be supported by a Board-approved treasury policy and EMIR reporting to a registered trade repository.Exchange-traded FX futures on major convertible currencies as a short-term roll-and-hedge tool, cleared through an EU CCP and documented under a brokerage clearing agreement; used only where the underlying subscription cash flow is explicit and periodic.

              TSD Section 1 - Profit Distributions
              Declaration of gross dividends and deemed profit distributions paid to each shareholder, computation of the tax base at 20/80 or 14/86, and per-recipient identification data including residence indicator.
              Non-resident shareholders must be itemised individually; the OÜ applies the domestic statutory rate; treaty-reduced rates are effective only where the payer holds a valid tax residence certificate (TRC) and beneficial ownership declaration prior to filing.
              Late filing penalties up to EUR 1,000 per declaration under MKS § 129; default interest of 0.03% per day on unpaid tax under MKS § 128; deliberate false declaration triggers a 50% tax fraud surcharge under MKS § 154 and criminal liability under Penal Code § 390 (fine or imprisonment up to 5 years).
            

            
            

              TSD Section 2 - Fringe Benefits and Non-Business Perquisites
              Declaration of fringe benefits, gifts, entertainment expenses exceeding statutory limits, and other deemed distributions to owners, directors, and employees; taxed at the 20/80 rate.
              Benefits provided to non-resident directors are regarded as deemed distributions with identical 20/80 mechanics; an arm's-length cost allocation and supporting contemporaneous records must be retained.
              Late filing penalties up to EUR 1,000 per declaration under MKS § 129; default interest of 0.03% per day on unpaid tax under MKS § 128; deliberate false declaration triggers a 50% tax fraud surcharge under MKS § 154 and criminal liability under Penal Code § 390 (fine or imprisonment up to 5 years).
            

            
            

              TSD Section 3 - Payments to Non-Residents
              Withholding tax on interest, royalties, management, consultancy, and agency fees paid to related non-residents at the statutory 20% rate.
              The Estonian-resident payer is the withholding agent; a treaty-reduced rate requires the recipient's TRC, a beneficial ownership statement, and, for IP royalties, evidence of real substance of the recipient.
              Late filing penalties up to EUR 1,000 per declaration under MKS § 129; default interest of 0.03% per day on unpaid tax under MKS § 128; deliberate false declaration triggers a 50% tax fraud surcharge under MKS § 154 and criminal liability under Penal Code § 390 (fine or imprisonment up to 5 years).
            

            
            

              TSD Section 4 - Employment Income and Social Taxes
              Gross payroll, flat 20% employee income tax, unemployment insurance premiums, and funded pension contributions, with per-employee disclosure and monthly aggregate reconciliation.
              Remote employees outside Estonia remain taxable in Estonia unless a foreign permanent establishment or EU/EEA social-security coordination applies; A1 certificates and PE analyses must be kept on file.
              Late filing penalties up to EUR 1,000 per declaration under MKS § 129; default interest of 0.03% per day on unpaid tax under MKS § 128; deliberate false declaration triggers a 50% tax fraud surcharge under MKS § 154 and criminal liability under Penal Code § 390 (fine or imprisonment up to 5 years).
            

            
            

              Balance Sheet - Equity and Distributable Reserves
              Discloses share capital, statutory reserves, retained earnings, and net distributable profit; forms the legal basis for determining lawful distributions under the Commercial Code.
              The equity solvency test must be satisfied before declaring dividends to non-resident owners; distributions sourced from non-equity funds are unlawful and are recharacterised as taxable deemed dividends at 20%.
              Failure to file triggers coercive fines from the Commercial Register (typically EUR 200 per imposition, repeatable), loss of good standing, and forced dissolution proceedings; the MTA may issue an estimated assessment based on assumed outbound distributions.
            

            
            

              Income Statement - Subscription Revenue Recognition
              Accrual-based recognition of design-as-a-service subscription income under Estonian GAAP; disclosure of deferred revenue, contract liabilities, and client concentration.
              Export design subscription revenues from foreign clients are not subject to Estonian income tax absent a local permanent establishment; no withholding applies to the export service fees themselves.
              Failure to file triggers coercive fines from the Commercial Register (typically EUR 200 per imposition, repeatable), loss of good standing, and forced dissolution proceedings; the MTA may issue an estimated assessment based on assumed outbound distributions.
