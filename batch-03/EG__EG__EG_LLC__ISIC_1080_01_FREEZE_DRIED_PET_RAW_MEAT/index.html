Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The EG LLC is a juridical person subject to Egyptian corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide taxable income, with a foreign tax credit for foreign-source income; no fiscal transparency is available. Shareholders are taxed separately: a final 10% withholding tax applies to dividends actually distributed, and for an LLC retaining after-tax profits without a distribution resolution within six months of the fiscal year-end, the ETA imposes a 10% deemed-dividend tax on 50% of the net after-tax profit. Egypt applies a unitary national tax system with no sub-national income tax distinction. The standard onshore classification is applied in this dossier; an alternative elective free-zone status (e.g., Suez Canal Economic Zone) under Law 83/2002 would be a separate investment decision and is not the default classification.

Available election forms

Egypt has no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch entity election; the LLC is classified as a taxable corporation by statute. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal-year-end election (calendar-year default, subject to prior ETA approval); (ii) Small Business Tax Regime election under Law 25/2020 applying a final presumptive turnover-based tax for annual turnover of EGP 500,000 to EGP 2,000,000, generally unsuitable for an export manufacturer because input-VAT recovery is forfeited; (iii) dividend-resolution election, whereby the shareholders resolve an actual distribution within six months of the fiscal year-end, activating the 10% final withholding tax and avoiding the ETA deemed-dividend tax on retained profits; (iv) VAT input-apportionment election for mixed-use expenses, subject to ETA approval; and (v) a CBE-approved extension election for the 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline in exceptional force-majeure circumstances.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services triggers mandatory VAT registration (VAT Law 67/2016, Article 27). An export-oriented freeze-dried pet-food manufacturer must register on the first taxable supply regardless of threshold to claim input-VAT credits on raw meat materials, processing utilities, packaging, fixed assets and export-related services. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services to Egyptian consumers are separately required to register once annual supplies to Egypt exceed EGP 500,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 21(1) of the Egyptian VAT Law 67/2016, tangible goods physically exported from Egypt are zero-rated at 0% provided that: (a) the seller is a VAT-registered Egyptian taxpayer; (b) the export is made under an Egyptian Customs export declaration through an Egyptian exit port or airport; (c) the foreign-currency export proceeds are received in Egypt by the exporter's CBE-licensed bank within the mandatory 180-day repatriation period; (d) the ETA-cleared e-invoice reflects the 0% rate and the export transaction; and (e) the full customs, transport and banking documentation is retained for ETA audit. Freeze-dried pet raw meat and meal kibble falls within Chapter 2309/HS 2309.10 and qualifies for export zero-rating as a physical export of finished goods, provided the consignment clears Egyptian Customs (NAFIZA) and is loaded for carriage to a non-resident customer. Offshore services physically performed and consumed outside Egypt may also be zero-rated or treated as outside the VAT scope under Articles 20-22 of the VAT Law, but they are secondary to the zero-rated physical export of the manufactured pet-food goods in this dossier.

  • Customs Export Declaration processed through the NAFIZA single window, showing the exporter code, the HS 2309.10 commodity classification, net mass, value and the Egyptian exit port or airport, together with the customs release number
  • Bill of Lading (original clean-on-board set) or Air Waybill evidencing physical carriage of the freeze-dried pet-food consignment out of Egypt to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the CBE-licensed bank documenting the foreign-currency export proceeds and the date of their credit to the exporter's Egyptian bank account within the 180-day window
  • Zero-rated VAT e-invoice (XML with ETA clearance code and QR) matching line-by-line the customs declaration, commercial invoice and export contract
  • Export sales contract or purchase order, final commercial invoice, certificate of origin, and the GOEIC/General Veterinary Organization export health certificate required for animal-origin pet food

Real-time fiscal clearance is mandatory for all VAT-registered enterprises in Egypt. Every B2B invoice must be transmitted to the ETA e-invoicing platform (invoicing.eta.gov.eg) in XML format, digitally signed using an ETA-compliant electronic signature certificate, and cleared by the ETA before or at the time of issuance; the ETA returns a unique system-approved UUID and a machine-readable QR code for the final legal invoice. Integration is via REST API with OAuth2 authentication or SFTP bulk upload. B2C supplies are covered by the parallel e-receipt system. An invoice not cleared by the ETA is legally ineffective for input-VAT deduction, export zero-rating and customs audit evidence. Non-compliance penalties start at EGP 20,000 per un-cleared invoice, and can escalate to suspension of the taxpayer's e-invoicing account and denial of input-VAT recovery for the audit period.

The ETA audits export zero-rating claims by cross-matching NAFIZA customs export declarations, the CBE export-proceeds register, bank statements and the e-invoice archive. If the exporter fails to prove physical export, fails to evidence receipt of the foreign-currency proceeds in Egypt within 180 days (or any CBE-approved extension), or presents incomplete or false documentation, the ETA retroactively re-characterizes the supply at the standard 14% VAT and assesses the difference plus unified monthly simple delay interest at 1.5% (capped at 100% of the tax due) under Tax Procedures Law 206/2020, together with administrative fines which may reach EGP 100,000 per non-compliant invoice. Fraudulent zero-rating, including forged bills of lading, false customs declarations or manufactured inward remittance certificates, constitutes tax evasion under Articles 38-39 of VAT Law 67/2016 and carries criminal exposure of imprisonment and a fine of up to EGP 1,000,000 per violation, joint and several liability of the LLC's managers and UBOs, attachment of bank accounts, and cancellation of the exporter's e-invoicing and customs trader status.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP is market-convertible on current-account items since the CBE's 6 March 2024 float and unified interbank rate: trade payments, service fees, royalties, and dividend remittances from audited distributable profits may be settled freely through authorized banks after documentary, customs, and tax-clearance verification. Capital-account convertibility is administrative: inbound FDI equity must be declared to the CBE under the balance-of-payments direct-investment code and funded via a local authorized bank; profit and capital exits require audited financial statements, ETA tax clearance, proof of the mandatory statutory reserve build-up under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 (typically 5% of annual distributable profits), and a CBE foreign-currency transfer declaration. Practical hurdles are correspondent-bank de-risking and bank-level liquidity, not blanket exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE export-receipts framework (mid-2023 revision), proceeds from Egyptian manufactured exports must be credited to the exporter's account at an authorized bank within 270 days from the bill-of-lading/customs declaration date; capital goods receive an extended window. No mandatory conversion ratio is currently imposed - the LLC may retain 100% of proceeds in a foreign-currency account or voluntarily convert; however, every shipment must have a matching customs Form M/A-C entry and final invoice, and non-arrival of proceeds within 270 days triggers CBE credit sanctions, restriction of foreign-currency facilities, and auditor report qualification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary, banking supervision, and foreign exchange authority under Law No. 88 of 2003, the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, and Law No. 38 of 1994 on Foreign Exchange; coordinated for investment registration and tax clearance with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary settlement rail; Medium as a supplementary rail for Platform_Pay and marketplace collections. Egypt's Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 requires all payment-service providers to be licensed by the CBE; non-resident digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) hold no Egyptian banking license, cannot open CBE-supervised accounts, and cannot participate in the CBE-operated EG-RTGS reconciliation and settlement system. They may only support offshore receipts that are subsequently repatriated by bank transfer, which will be flagged as third-party or aggregator flows. Recommended architecture: local licensed PSP (Fawry or Paymob) for domestic and platform pay-ins into the LLC's main operating account, and a Tier-1 bank for all FX settlement; any offshore wallet must be disclosed in the AML/KYC file to avoid downstream bank refusal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI equity is wire-transferred via SWIFT MT103/TT exclusively to the LLC's dedicated capital escrow account at a Tier-1 authorized bank (NBE, Banque Misr, or CIB). The remitter's instruction and SWIFT cover must cite: beneficiary EG_LLC; bank account details; purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Participation in Egyptian LLC' under the CBE balance-of-payments direct-investment classification; and reference to the GAFI license and Commercial Register number. The receiving bank performs the CBE inward-transfer declaration, verifies the share subscription and board resolutions, and credits the escrow only after issuance of the bank share-capital deposit certificate and the updated Commercial Registry extract. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary settlement rail; Medium as a supplementary rail for Platform_Pay and marketplace collections. Egypt's Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 requires all payment-service providers to be licensed by the CBE; non-resident digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) hold no Egyptian banking license, cannot open CBE-supervised accounts, and cannot participate in the CBE-operated EG-RTGS reconciliation and settlement system. They may only support offshore receipts that are subsequently repatriated by bank transfer, which will be flagged as third-party or aggregator flows. Recommended architecture: local licensed PSP (Fawry or Paymob) for domestic and platform pay-ins into the LLC's main operating account, and a Tier-1 bank for all FX settlement; any offshore wallet must be disclosed in the AML/KYC file to avoid downstream bank refusal.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 1 million aggregate related-party transactions in a tax year triggers the mandatory transfer pricing disclosure form filed with Form No. 50; EGP 10 million aggregate related-party transactions triggers the preparation of a full Local File; membership in a multinational group with consolidated group turnover above EGP 200 million triggers a Master File, pursuant to ETA Decree No. 553 of 2018 and subsequent ETA TP guidelines; the ETA may also request contemporaneous documentation for smaller transactions under risk assessment.

Economic substance scope: Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations do not apply in Egypt; however, Egyptian tax law applies a substance-over-form doctrine, beneficial-ownership requirements for treaty relief, and a general anti-avoidance rule under Article 30 of Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended by Law No. 96 of 2015. The EG_LLC must demonstrate substantive Egyptian operations and physical presence: board meetings, executive management, purchasing decisions, production, payroll, warehouse and banking transactions conducted in Egypt; inter-company service fees, royalties and interest are disallowed if no real activity, benefit or support to income generation is established.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Ex-officio assessment by ETA with full 22.5% CIT plus 2% monthly late-payment surcharge on the unpaid balance, a fine of double the evaded tax, a fixed fine of EGP 1,000 to EGP 10,000, publication of the taxpayer in the Egyptian Taxpayers Register, and criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Signing officers, directors and beneficial owners who directed or signed false returns may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax, subjected to asset seizure and travel bans, and sentenced to imprisonment of up to 3 years; the corporate veil is pierced where fraudulent intent is proven.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA recharacterizes the transactions under the general anti-avoidance rule, disallows inter-company deductions, raises the assessed taxable income at 22.5% plus the 2% monthly surcharge, and may refer the case to the General Authority for Investment for deregistration and license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties; travel bans and closure of Egyptian bank accounts apply; directors may be barred from serving on any Egyptian board for up to 5 years.
Profit Stripping through Excessive Interest and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the full deduction and recalculation of CIT at 22.5%; penalty on the Egyptian payer equal to the under-withheld WHT amount and an additional fine of up to 50% of the unreported remittance; monthly surcharges accrue until settlement.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the distribution of dividends out of artificially reduced profits are personally liable for the shortfall in tax; remitting banks freeze further cross-border transfers; the responsible officers face criminal prosecution and imprisonment of up to 2 years.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian AML Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended, financial penalties of EGP 100,000 to EGP 2,000,000, forfeiture of the transferred value, freezing of the entity's accounts, and referral by the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit to the public prosecutor.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors who authorized or failed to disclose the wire structures face criminal imprisonment of 6 months to 7 years, confiscation of assets, placement on the Egyptian sanction list, and permanent restriction from holding banking mandates in Egypt.
CIT Non-Filing and Disappearance from Commercial Register

Statutory sanction: ETA issues a decreed estimated assessment on presumed profit, applies the 22.5% rate plus 2% monthly surcharge, levies fixed penalties up to EGP 40,000, suspends the Commercial Registry certificate, blocks import/export entitlements, and initiates compulsory liquidation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid payroll taxes and WHT amounts not remitted; absence of the ETA tax clearance certificate imposes travel bans and prevents any sale or transfer of the company; managers may be personally sued for damages by creditors and investigated for criminal non-compliance.
