Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the articles of association fix the share capital from €1 upward. The share capital must be fully paid before or at registration via bank transfer to the company's temporary settlement account or, in the e-Residency flow, to the RIK collection account of the e-Business Register. No escrow account is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the registered office must maintain the occupancy permit (kasutusloba) issued by the local government, a fire safety self-assessment filed with the Rescue Board (Päästeamet), serviced extinguishers and clear evacuation signage. Occupational health and safety: a written workplace risk assessment (riskianalüüs) and an occupational health doctor service agreement are mandatory under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus); the employer must pre-notify the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) of the workplace and of each employee before work commences. Civil defense obligations are limited to emergency evacuation floor plans, annual staff instruction, and compliance with the Emergency Act (Hädaolukorra seadus) for office premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the articles of association fix the share capital from €1 upward. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA); taxpayer portal is e-MTA (e-Maksuamet) accessible at https://emta.ee; commercial registry integration via the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK).

E-government filing portal

A foreign juridical person first obtains an Estonian registry code (äriregistri kood) by registering a branch or establishing an Osaühing (OÜ) through the e-Business Register. The ten-digit registry code serves as the national tax identification number for corporate income tax. For VAT purposes, a non-established taxable person supplying goods or services in Estonia must apply for a separate VAT identification number via the e-MTA portal (Form KMD) prior to the date of first taxable supply, unless the intra-Community distance sales threshold of EUR 10,000 has not been exceeded. Non-established applicants without a permanent establishment in Estonia may be required to appoint a locally resident tax representative; certificates are issued electronically and amendments are filed via e-MTA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not provide a check-the-box regime. The statutory default for all resident OÜs is distribution-based corporate income tax: undistributed profits are not taxed; a taxable distribution triggers corporate income tax at 22/78 or 20/80 on the gross distribution (the applicable gross-up rate depending on the year and distribution type). An OÜ may elect to account for income tax on advances received from an associated enterprise, and may elect for VAT grouping under Section 27 of the Estonian VAT Act if the 25% financial, economic and organisational integration conditions are met. Hybrid mismatch elections follow the mandatory ATAD anti-abuse rules; no elective pass-through classification is available for a commercial limited company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable turnover for taxable persons established in Estonia; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied digital services (one-stop-shop, OSS). Non-established foreign taxable persons are registerable from the first taxable supply in Estonia unless they fall within the EU-wide distance sales threshold or the import one-stop-shop rules; a VAT return must be filed for any month in which there is a supply tax point.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT when the goods are physically transported outside the European Union within 90 days from the supply date, and the exporter retains comprehensive proof of removal. Supply of services to a taxable person established outside the EU (including registered-agent, offshore secretarial and corporate compliance services) is zero-rated or outside the scope of Estonian VAT when the place of supply is outside Estonia under the general B2B place-of-supply rule, provided the recipient is in a third country and the services are not attributable to a permanent establishment in Estonia. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated and outside-scope transactions is fully recoverable through the standard VAT refund procedure; no VAT invoice is required for an offshore-service supply when the place of supply is outside Estonia, but the supplier must hold an export ledger containing commercial and payment evidence.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD), Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note, Inward remittance certificate from the receiving bank, commercial invoice, packing list, proof of transport completion, delivery confirmation from the non-EU recipient, and contemporaneous export-register entry in the EMTA e-MTA system.

Estonia does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, nor digital stamping. Invoices must be issued within seven calendar days of the supply date or payment-in-advance date. All VAT returns (KMD), recapitulative statements, and export declarations are filed electronically through the e-MTA portal. E-invoicing (PEPPOL BIS, UBL 2.1, or equivalent) is mandatory for invoicing Estonian public-sector entities and is widely accepted for B2B; documentary proof must be retained for seven years and produced on EMTA request. No pre-approval or electronic clearance requirement applies to private sector sales.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are rejected on audit and the VAT is reassessed at the prevailing standard rate (24.0% as of 1 July 2025) with default interest at the Bank of Estonia refinancing rate plus 10 percentage points per annum. Deliberate falsification of export documentation constitutes a criminal fiscal offence; penalties may reach EUR 1.6 million for legal persons, and directors or tax representatives may become jointly and severally liable. The ordinary VAT assessment period is extended from six to seven years for cases involving false zero-rating claims, and any contested refund or credit is clawed back together with the refusal of pending or future refund requests.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has been a euro-area member since 1 January 2011; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange controls preventing repatriation of dividends, liquidation proceeds, loan repayments, or service fees. The OÜ may invoice and settle in any currency. Profit distribution is governed by the Estonian Commercial Code and requires a signed decision by the shareholders' meeting, auditor approval if the annual report is audited, and compliance with the net asset test. From a tax perspective, Estonia applies a cash-flow corporate income tax (CIT) system: undistributed profits are tax-free; distributed profits are generally taxed at 20% (or 14% for regular profit distributions). No withholding tax is levied on dividend payments to non-residents under Estonian national law if the recipient is a resident of a country with which Estonia has a tax treaty, or if the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies. No exchange-control certificate or central bank permission is required for wire transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia has no statutory regime requiring exporters to surrender, convert, or repatriate foreign exchange proceeds within a specified timeframe. Exporters may hold foreign currency indefinitely. However, for statistical purposes, resident enterprises must report material foreign-currency and external receivables/payables balances to Eesti Pank through the monthly/annual balance-of-payments reporting system. There are no penalties for non-repatriation of export income.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) - the central bank of the Republic of Estonia and a member of the Eurosystem. It is responsible for currency stability, payment systems, and compiling balance-of-payments statistics. Exchange control regulation is harmonised at the EU level; Eesti Pank acts as the national competent authority for capital flow reporting and monetary and financial statistics. The Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) is separately responsible for AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions and financial service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium with legal constraints. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and fintech platforms, e.g., Monese, Wise Business, or up-and-coming B2B EMIs with an EEA passport, may open accounts for an Estonian OÜ remotely with only a brief e-ID/JIT or video-KYC check. These EMIs are viable for small-ticket inbound/outbound wires, business card issue, and FX conversion. However, they are not credit institutions and are generally not permitted to hold client funds for escrow or payment intermediation purposes without a separate payment-institution licence. Furthermore, banks commonly reject transactions from an EMI-owned aggregated IBAN if the commercial invoice contains registered-agent/corporate-services wording. For ISIC 8299 registered-agent or secretarial services, the transaction profile (low initial capital, high frequency of recurring retainer payments) can be compliantly handled by an EMI, but the acceptance decision is not guaranteed. Therefore, for escrow-account responsibilities or high-value settlement of acquisition agreements, a Tier-1 banking institution must be used.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital injections and intra-group loans must be remitted by international wire transfer using the SWIFT MT103 format. In field 70 (Remittance Information), the transferor must state one of the following purpose codes in English or Estonian to facilitate bank KYC and statistical classification: 'SHARE CAPITAL – OÜ REGISTRATION' or 'SISSEMAKSE OSAKAPITALI'. For compliance with AML, the sending bank must be a regulated credit institution; money-remittance company transfers are generally rejected. The receiving Estonian bank will require an explanation on the source of funds (e.g., declaration of the transferor, and if the transfer exceeds EUR 10,000, a copy of the share subscription agreement). For borrowings, the purpose code 'LAEN – INTERCOMPANY LOAN' must be inserted, and an executed loan agreement containing principal, interest, maturity, and use of funds must be provided to the bank. All payments are processed before 16:00 CET; EUR transfers within the EEA clear via SEPA, while non-EUR transfers are cleared via correspondent banks (usually through Frankfurt, London, or New York).Outbound trade payments for subcontracting, software licensing, professional fees, and operational rent shall be executed via SWIFT MT103 (single payment) or SEPA credit transfer for euro-denominated payments to an EU counterparty. For high-value transactions (above EUR 50,000), the bank may require source documentation: pro-forma invoice, signed contract, and customs declaration if goods. If a commercial letter of credit (LC) is used, the issuing bank in Estonia (e.g., Swedbank or SEB) creates the LC in accordance with UCP 600 and sends it via swift MT700. The beneficiary presents documents through its own bank; the Estonian bank will honour the LC upon compliant presentation through the SWIFT MT752/MT754 sequence. For cross-border receivables of secretarial fees, the OÜ may also accept SWIFT-mediated payments on a net basis, and must retain the MT199 confirmation message from the receiving institution for audit trail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 100,000 EUR (total controlled transactions value)

Economic substance scope: ESR applies to holding companies and IP regime entities; registered agents must demonstrate adequate substance for any qualifying activities

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties including fines up to 30,000 EUR or imprisonment up to 5 years; civil restitution of evaded taxes

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable; possible imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification from management roles
Shell Company / Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to 6,400 EUR; potential deregistration; back taxes with interest

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors face civil claims, fines, and criminal sanctions for complicity
AML / KYC Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 6,400 EUR; license revocation; criminal liability under AML Act

Director personal liability: Personal fines, imprisonment for severe cases, and disqualification
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 50% of understated tax; interest on adjustments; possible criminal charges

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for tax losses caused by non-compliance
