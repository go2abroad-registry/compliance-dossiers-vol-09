Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); official portal: e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee) with digital filing services at e-maksuamet; company registry: e-Business Register (https://ariregister.rik.ee)

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders incorporate the OÜ in the e-Business Register (e-Residency digital ID accepted); upon entry, the Commercial Register automatically assigns an 8-digit registry code (registrikood), which constitutes the statutory tax identification number for all Estonian tax accounts. For VAT purposes, submit application KÄR in e-MTA (signed via ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID, or e-Residency card); the MTA issues a VAT number in format EE plus 9 digits within 5 working days. A non-resident without a fixed establishment must register from the first taxable supply made in Estonia unless the supply is reverse-charged to a taxable customer or covered by OSS; non-EU persons must appoint an Estonian tax representative (VAT Act section 19). An Estonian address or a designated contact person must appear in the registry; use of an e-Residency service-provider address is accepted for EU/EEA founders without an Estonian branch.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - OÜ is a resident entity subject to the Estonian distributive-profit model (Income Tax Act): annual retained profit is exempt (0% CIT); only profit distributions trigger entity-level income tax at 20/80 of the net dividend (effective 20% of gross), reduced to 14/86 for regular qualifying distributions up to the average of taxable profits distributed in the preceding three years. Undistributed profits accumulate tax-free indefinitely; no capital duty, no branch tax, no net wealth tax; the regime is incompatible with pass-through classification. The entity maintains worldwide tax residence in Estonia and is taxed exclusively at distribution in the shareholder chain. — Foreign founders incorporate the OÜ in the e-Business Register (e-Residency digital ID accepted); upon entry, the Commercial Register automatically assigns an 8-digit registry code (registrikood), which constitutes the statutory tax identification number for all Estonian tax accounts. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - OÜ is a resident entity subject to the Estonian distributive-profit model (Income Tax Act): annual retained profit is exempt (0% CIT); only profit distributions trigger entity-level income tax at 20/80 of the net dividend (effective 20% of gross), reduced to 14/86 for regular qualifying distributions up to the average of taxable profits distributed in the preceding three years. Undistributed profits accumulate tax-free indefinitely; no capital duty, no branch tax, no net wealth tax; the regime is incompatible with pass-through classification. The entity maintains worldwide tax residence in Estonia and is taxed exclusively at distribution in the shareholder chain.

Available election forms

Estonian law provides no check-the-box or electable entity classification. Available elections: (a) reduced 14/86 CIT rate for regular distributions (Income Tax Act section 50(5)), a de facto election exercised through the dividend resolution and annual tax return; (b) VAT group registration for closely linked persons (VAT Act section 14(8)) with joint and several liability; (c) OSS/IOSS election for intra-Community distance sales and B2C electronic services; (d) participation-exemption election on disposal of shares, preserving CIT neutrality outside the 20/80 distribution chain; (e) no hybrid-mismatch election - mandatory ATAD anti-hybrid rules apply to B2B SaaS structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover in a calendar year for resident taxpayers and non-residents with a fixed establishment in Estonia; registration is mandatory within 5 working days after the threshold is exceeded. For non-residents without a fixed establishment there is no threshold, and registration is due from the first taxable supply unless the supply is reverse-charged to an Estonian taxable customer or falls under OSS. EU-wide EUR 10,000 OSS threshold applies to cross-border B2C digital services; cross-border B2B SaaS supplies to foreign taxable persons do not create an Estonian VAT registration obligation because the place of supply is at the customer establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border B2B Enterprise SaaS is outside the scope of Estonian VAT under the general place-of-supply rule (VAT Act section 11a(1); Council Directive 2006/112/EC article 44): the service is deemed supplied where the customer is established, and the customer accounts for reverse-charge VAT, so no Estonian zero-rating claim is required. True 0% rating applies only to exports of goods (article 146), intra-Community supplies of goods (article 138) with a valid customer VAT ID, and services physically attached to exported goods. The OÜ may reclaim Estonian input VAT on domestic costs (hosting, legal, accounting) under the standard KMD return; export-related invoices must identify the customer as a taxable person, state the reverse-charge reference, and exclude Estonian VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EXA) - required only for physical goods; not applicable to pure SaaS software exports
  • Commercial invoice carrying customer VAT ID, reverse-charge notation per article 226 of the VAT Directive, and full service description
  • Signed SaaS contract / subscription agreement (AUP) identifying the B2B enterprise customer and service scope
  • Service delivery evidence - access logs, API usage telemetry, user-activation reports, or deployment certificates
  • Bank or payment-rail settlement confirmation (Stripe or Wise) evidencing receipt of consideration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate only if requested by the Estonian bank for documentation of cross-border inflows; no statutory FX-surrender certificate exists in Estonia

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies to B2B transactions. Mandatory structured e-invoicing (EN 16931/Peppol) is required only for invoices issued to Estonian public-sector entities (B2G). For B2B, the electronic filing system requires submission of the VAT return KMD together with the KMD INF annex, which reports transaction-level data for all domestic B2B sales and purchases (counterparty VAT IDs, invoice numbers, net amounts) via the e-MTA portal or API. Voluntary B2B e-invoicing is widely used; invoices must be issued within 7 calendar days of the supply; automated MTA data matching across domestic counterparties is applied.

If zero-rating or out-of-scope treatment is retroactively rejected by the MTA, the OÜ is assessed Estonian VAT at 22% with late-payment interest of 0.06% per calendar day (circa 21.9% annualized) from the original due date. The general assessment limitation period is 3 years; it extends to 5 years in cases of suspected tax evasion and up to 10 years for criminal tax fraud. A corporate fine of up to EUR 8,000 per violation may be imposed (Taxation Act section 154); deliberate export-claim fraud (Maksupettus) is a criminal offence punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment for natural persons and by pecuniary punishment for legal persons. Clawback covers the full assessed VAT, interest, fines, and loss of future zero-rating treatment; statutory 7-year record-retention duties apply, and automated cross-border exchange of information (DAC6, CRS, VIES, customs data) is used to detect artificial export structures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Estonia is a Eurozone member and applies the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (TFEU Article 63). Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted, with no licences, quotas, surrender or administrative conversion requirements. Dividend distributions and profit remittances are freely transferable out of Estonia subject only to standard corporate formalities, documentary evidence, bank source-of-funds checks, sanctions screening and, where relevant, EU capital-market reporting thresholds. No withholding tax is imposed on dividends at domestic law, but the bank will require a board resolution, UBO confirmation, financial statement substantiation and QF/WHT forms where tax-treaty relief is claimed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory surrender or conversion rules. Estonian law and the Eurosystem legal framework do not impose any statutory deadline for repatriation, nor any minimum conversion ratio into EUR, for export proceeds. Export invoices may be collected and retained in any freely usable currency, and the only requirement is statistical reporting to Eesti Pank as part of the balance-of-payments survey where the annual reporting thresholds are exceeded. For a B2B SaaS exporter receiving EUR via SEPA or USD via SWIFT, no FX conversion is compelled either at receipt or before onward use.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), central bank and member of the Eurosystem; prudential banking supervision is delegated to Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority), with AML/CFT supervision shared with the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High but conditional. EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions, such as those passporting from Western/Northern Europe under PSD2, may open business accounts and issue virtual IBANs to Estonian OÜs after a passporting notification to Finantsinspektsioon. They are well-suited for cross-border SaaS collections from global customers, multi-currency settlement and FX conversion at competitive rates. Operationally feasible rating is High for payment collection and treasury pooling, but an EMI must not engage in deposit-taking or Estonian retail lending; funds must be safeguarded. Non-EEA or offshore EMIs are rated Low for full banking functionality and Medium only for ancillary collection rails, because Estonian law and EU passporting rules require a branch, agent, or passporting arrangement within the EEA before they can lawfully provide payment services to Estonian clients.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injections are routed through SEPA credit transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for foreign currency to the OÜ's Estonian bank account, using the ISIN-based purpose code 'Direct investment' under the Eurosystem balance-of-payments framework where the underlying share subscription is notified. The remittance instruction must reference the share subscription resolution and shareholder name; the receiving bank will require a completed source-of-funds questionnaire, existing UBO database, payment reason such as 'Sissemakse aktsiakapitali/kontosse' (capital contribution), and, for higher-risk jurisdictions, a certified copy of the remitter's passport, supporting transaction trail, and confirmation that neither the remitter nor the underlying funds are sanctioned. The bank may hold those funds temporarily in a capital escrow sub-account before releasing them to the operating account after verification of the notarised subscription deed and registry filing.For non-EEA vendor payments and service fees, the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire transferred from the OÜ's operational account in EUR, USD or GBP, linked to a corresponding reason-for-payment code (trade, service, licence, dividend). For EEA-denominated EUR disbursements, SEPA Credit Transfer via painful.001 XML is sufficient, settled through TARGET2 via the Estonian banking matrix. Large-value trade settlements, where goods are involved or where counterparty risk exists, are secured through a commercial Letter of Credit issued or advised by an Estonian tier-1 bank under UCP600, usually with MT700/MT710/MT720 messaging and deferred-payment or sight-draft terms. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on gross profit distributions, computed as 20/80 of the net dividend; equivalent to 25% effective tax on pre-distribution retained profit. Undistributed and reinvested profits of an Estonian OÜ are taxed at 0% until distributed.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory profit-distribution charge is 20% of the gross distribution, expressed in Estonian law as 20/80 of the net amount paid to the shareholder. Estonia does not mechanically label this a non-resident dividend withholding tax; the OÜ is the taxpayer. For a shareholder receiving a net dividend from pre-tax retained profits, the effective burden is 25%. Domestic participation exemption and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive can reduce the effective Estonian source tax for qualifying corporate shareholders, often to 0% when a 10% participation threshold is met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonian law does not prescribe a fixed monetary threshold for Local File or Master File preparation for a standalone OÜ. The Tax and Customs Board may require transfer-pricing documentation for any controlled transaction during an audit. As a practical compliance threshold, maintain OECD-aligned contemporaneous documentation when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed EUR 50,000. Larger MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more must file Country-by-Country Reports with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the general anti-abuse clause in the Estonian Income Tax Act and Tax Procedure Act authorises disregarding artificial legal arrangements lacking real business substance. A registered OÜ is generally tax-resident in Estonia, yet the company must demonstrate genuine presence through strategic management in Estonia, employees, office premises, business records, and substantive economic functions where treaty benefits are claimed. E-residency alone creates no tax substance and does not immunise a SaaS business from foreign permanent establishment, CFC, or anti-abuse challenges. For in-scope multinational groups, OECD Pillar Two GloBE rules may apply at the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Disguised Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a deemed profit distribution plus 20/80 corporate income tax, daily 0.06% arrears interest, tax misdemeanour fines, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines, confiscation, and imprisonment up to three or five years in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise such distributions may be jointly and severally liable under Estonian insolvency rules when the OÜ becomes unable to pay tax debts; criminal conviction can carry travel bans, asset seizure, disqualification from board service, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / E-Residency Abuse Without Substance

Statutory sanction: The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may disregard the arrangement under anti-abuse and general substance rules, impose distribution-tax assessments, deny DTA benefits, and expose the OÜ to foreign CFC taxation. Commercial Register proceedings can compulsorily dissolve a shell company for persistent failure to file annual reports or for operating contrary to the registered business.

Director personal liability: Board members and beneficial owners may be held personally liable for the company's tax and public-law debts, struck off as directors, barred from re-registering in Estonia, fined, and criminally prosecuted if the sham was used as an instrument of tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation in SaaS Value Chain

Statutory sanction: The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may adjust taxable income under the arm's-length principle, impose 20/80 distribution tax on disguised profit extraction, apply daily interest and tax penalties, and impose criminal fines where intentional mispricing is proved.

Director personal liability: CFO, CEO, and board members who sign or implement sham intercompany contracts face personal fiduciary liability, unlimited statutory damages, disqualification, and criminal liability for tax evasion; the foreign parent may also be subject to Estonian CFC-type attribution for diverted profits.
AML / Payment Stack Abuse

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act exposes the OÜ to supervisory fines, forced suspension of payment services, freezing of funds by the Financial Intelligence Unit, and in severe cases criminal penalties for money laundering with imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers, money-laundering reporting officers, and board members face personal fines, administrative penalties, confiscation, travel restrictions, disqualification from payment-industry roles, and imprisonment for intentionally permitting laundered funds to pass through the SaaS treasury.
