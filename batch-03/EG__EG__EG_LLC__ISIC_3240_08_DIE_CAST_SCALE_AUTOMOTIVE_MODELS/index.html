VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies (mandatory registration threshold); voluntary registration permitted below threshold; Suez Canal Economic Zone licensed projects may access VAT suspension or investment incentive treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside Egypt, including die-cast scale automotive models, qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the exporter holds a valid taxpayer card, the export occurs within the statutory timeline from the triggering supply, and the export is substantiated by customs release and foreign-currency receipt. Services directly related to the exported goods, certain offshore services, and supplies to qualifying free-zone operators may also be zero-rated where supported by ETA-recognized documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACID/ACS release by the Egyptian Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds into an Egyptian bank account
  • ETA zero-rating declaration form and export sales contract
  • Original purchase invoices for input VAT credit substantiation

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts through the ETA platform, with phased enrollment by registered taxpayer turnover segments. Taxpayers must issue invoices as structured XML/JSON via ETA-approved APIs or the ETA portal; each invoice is validated in real time, assigned a unique QR-code fiscal stamp, and receives an official invoice number before transmission to the customer. Non-integration, off-line issuance, or failure to obtain fiscal clearance attracts statutory penalties.

Zero-rating refund claims are subject to comprehensive documentary desk audit by the ETA. Under Articles 69–70 of VAT Law No. 67/2016, fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims trigger recovery of the entire tax avoided plus a penalty of up to 100% of underpaid or falsely claimed tax, imprisonment of up to five years, and retrospective clawback of previously refunded amounts. The ETA must settle valid refund claims within 45 days, failing which it bears 10% annual interest in favor of the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt accepts IMF Article VIII obligations, making EGP convertible for legitimate current-account transactions (trade, dividends, interest, royalties, and services) through CBE-licensed banks, while capital-account transactions (equity injections, intercompany loans, securities) remain subject to CBE declaration, GAFI registration, and bank-level documentary vetting. Practical profit-remittance hurdles include: (i) requirement for audited financial statements, tax clearance certificate, and a board resolution authorizing dividend distribution; (ii) CBE-imposed foreign-currency declaration forms for each outward transfer; (iii) residual FX liquidity rationing during balance-of-payments stress, which historically caused accumulation of bank queues for hard-currency remittances; and (iv) post-March-2024 exchange-rate unification, banks price near market levels but still apply enhanced due-diligence scrutiny to foreign shareholder distributions. Capital contributed through the banking system is afforded repatriation protection under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, provided the inflow is registered with GAFI and the supporting inward-transfer certificate is retained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Law No. 194 of 1959 and applicable CBE circulars, the Egyptian exporting LLC must repatriate export proceeds into a CBE-licensed bank account in Egypt within five months from the date of export documentation; in standard market conditions no fixed statutory percentage of proceeds must be surrendered to the CBE, and the exporter may lawfully retain 100% of export receipts in foreign-currency accounts. Nevertheless, the CBE holds discretionary authority to issue temporary mandatory-conversion directives during reserve emergencies (as occurred with the 50% conversion requirement applied to export receipts in 2023), at which point the LLC must convert the directed percentage into EGP within the stipulated window (generally 30 days from receipt). The exporter must submit the prescribed export-proceeds declaration and related shipping documents to the receiving bank, and any repatriation shortfall or late repatriation may trigger administrative fines, denial of import-financing facilities, and adverse classification under the bank's internal risk-rating system.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), established under Law No. 88 of 2003 (Central Banking and Monetary System Law), exercising sole regulatory authority over banking licensing, foreign-exchange policy, and cross-border payments enforcement in Egypt, in coordination with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and the Egyptian Customs Authority. The Exchange Control Law No. 194 of 1959 (as amended by Law No. 87 of 2019) remains the foundational statute governing repatriation, export proceeds, and currency declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM (auxiliary collection channel only). Egypt does not maintain a local banking-license or EMI-license category that permits offshore digital banks (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) to issue Egyptian IBANs or hold EGP balances with CBE clearing access. Such offshore EMIs may be used as secondary receivables-consolidation pulpits for e-commerce and PayPal-derived proceeds, but they cannot replace a Tier-1 Egyptian commercial bank for statutory export repatriation, trade finance, or CBE compliance. Legal constraints are material: all export proceeds must ultimately flow into an Egyptian licensed-bank account; invoicing through an offshore EMI creates beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds mismatches; and many EMI platforms exclude Egyptian entities from their terms of service. The compliant design is to designate a local bank as the primary treasury concentration point, use EMIs only for small-value consumer payments, and execute daily or weekly SWIFT MT103 sweeps to Egypt while maintaining a full reconciliation ledger for the CBE and tax authorities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must enter Egypt exclusively through a CBE-licensed bank using SWIFT MT103 wire transfer; the beneficiary must be the Egyptian LLC's Capital Escrow Account, and the remittance information (Field 70) must clearly identify the transaction as an equity capital contribution under Law No. 72 of 2017 and the GAFI registration reference number. The receiving bank will issue an inward-transfer certificate after applying CBE declarations and source-of-funds checks; this certificate must be retained as the essential document for future lawful repatriation of invested capital. For clarity, currency exchange of the injected USD/EUR into EGP operating liquidity is executed at the bank's published FX rate on the value date, with the foreign-currency side preserved in a separate USD account for import obligations. Any injection exceeding relevant CBE reporting thresholds triggers an automatic declaration to the Economic Balance monitoring database; the funds cannot be transformed into intercompany loans without a separate CBE-approved loan registration.The compliant outbound clearing matrix is: (i) SWIFT MT103 for low-value supplier remittances, freight payments, mold-tooling fees, and royalties — executed after the bank's documentary due-diligence review of the commercial invoice, bill of lading/AWB, and ACI customs release; (ii) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (MT700) for bulk zinc-alloy ingot imports and high-value Chinese tooling contracts, with preferred tenor of 90-120 days to match production and cash-conversion cycles; (iii) documentary collections (D/P or D/A) as intermediary rails for smaller UAE/Turkey suppliers; and (iv) EGP RTGS transfers for domestic overhead, wages, and customs-duty settlement. All outward transfers require accurately completed bank forms and purpose codes; the bank will reject any payment associated with false declarations or inconsistent goods classifications.
