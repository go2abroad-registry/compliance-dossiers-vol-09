Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Nasr City, Cairo; unified tax registration portal at www.eta.gov.eg and e-invoicing services at einvoice.eta.gov.eg.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide income; no pass-through or transparent entity election exists for LLCs under Egyptian law. Dividends are subject to a 10% withholding tax at distribution. The territorial scope is a modified worldwide method with foreign tax credit; income of an Egyptian resident LLC earned abroad through a permanent establishment is generally taxable in Egypt with unilateral credit relief.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Egyptian tax law. The ETA applies substance-over-form, effective management (Egyptian 'center of management'), and the 'subject to tax' condition under Article 50 of the Tax Procedures Law 206/2020, which denies deduction of payments to a non-resident entity if the amount is not subject to tax at a comparable level in the foreign jurisdiction. Elections are limited to the grouping of tax periods, the simplified small-taxpayer regime, and the VAT refund option for zero-rated suppliers; no protective passthrough elections are available to a single-member LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory); voluntary registration allowed below threshold, including for zero-rated exporters seeking refunds; a presumptive simplified small-taxpayer regime applies to qualifying businesses with turnover up to EGP 2 million under Law 152/2020, but it does not override the exporter's zero-rating rights.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods (including CO2-laser-cut wooden puzzles) are zero-rated under Article 16 of Law 67/2016 provided the exporter submits: an authenticated customs export declaration proving physical departure from Egypt, transport documents, sales contract bearing international trade clauses, and proof that the consideration has been received from a non-resident in foreign currency (or in EGP through the official banking channel). Offshore services consumed outside Egypt also qualify for zero-rating. The claim is substantiated through an 'export proof file' that must be preserved for at least 5 years and is audited by the ETA before any refund is authorized.

  • Customs Export Declaration (بيان تصدير) authenticated by the Egyptian Customs Authority through the NAFEZA/ACI system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by an international carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank confirming receipt and crediting of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Certificate of Export Proceeds (Form 4) issued by the exporter's bank confirming collection of the payment and legation with the Central Bank of Egypt repatriation rules
  • Sales contract (translated legalized copy), commercial invoice, and packing list

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing for all B2B exports and domestic sales: the taxpayer must push every invoice to ETA via API (XML/JSON) for real-time clearance and receipt of a UUID and QR code before the transaction is considered compliant; e-receipts are mandatory for B2C sales since tax phase 2022; all exported goods must also obtain an ACI number via the NAFEZA one-stop customs portal at least 48 hours before loading; non-compliance triggers the penalty scale under Article 40 of VAT Law 67/2016 (EGP 50,000 to EGP 2,000,000 per violation) and the per se disallowance of input VAT deduction on non-cleared invoices.

The ETA and Central Bank of Egypt cross-check export files with NAFEZA shipment data, bank-backed reporting, and customs exit records; any false or inflated zero-rating claim triggers: (i) full refund clawback with additional penalty of 50% to 170% of the VAT refunded (Article 40 VAT Law); (ii) reversal of the zero rate and assessment of 14% VAT with delay interest computed at 2% per month (minimum EGP 100 and capped at 100% of the tax); (iii) criminal liability, including imprisonment and confiscation, in cases of forged documents or fraudulent export schemes; (iv) suspension of the exporter's tax card and e-invoicing certificate, with the manager and licensed tax adviser held jointly liable under the Tax Procedures Law 206/2020.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains de jure current account convertibility for EGP, having accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; the CBE liberalised the exchange rate in March 2024 and now operates a flexible, interbank-driven currency regime. Capital account transactions are not fully convertible: inward FDI and foreign borrowing must be routed through CBE-licensed banks and registered with GAFI (General Authority for Investment). Profit remittance is permitted without prior CBE approval only where the foreign investment has been properly registered, foreign currency entered Egypt through the banking system, corporate tax and 10% dividend withholding tax have been settled, and audited financial statements support the dividend distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the March 2024 FX reform, no statutory percentage of export proceeds must be converted into EGP; the CBE rescinded the earlier incremental surrender ratios (5%-15%) in January 2023. Exporters must still channel all export proceeds through an Egyptian bank and receive the funds into a foreign currency account within the statutory repatriation period of 90 days from the bill of lading date (extendable in documented exceptional cases). Proceeds may be retained in USD/EUR, used for import settlements, or sold into EGP at the prevailing interbank rate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), also exercising foreign exchange administration authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and the Egyptian Investment Law No. 72 of 2017.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 for Local File; EGP 20,000,000 for Master File based on aggregate cross-border related-party transactions in the fiscal year; additional sector-specific thresholds may apply.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations regime akin to UAE; however, GAAR provisions (Income Tax Law Article 99), CFC rules, and MLI principal-purpose tests require economic substance. A laser-cutting LLC claiming asset depreciation and e-commerce revenue must have real personnel, workshop space, equipment, risk-taking, and management decisions in Egypt; otherwise the ETA can disregard the entity, deny treaty benefits, and recharacterize transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment up to five years and a fine of not less than EGP 1,000,000 or at least twice the evaded tax; civil liability for the full underpaid tax plus a 2% monthly delay tax and all incurred audit expenses.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and persons signing false certificates are jointly and severally liable; travel bans, asset freezes, passport withdrawal, and disqualification from board roles are applied on a personal basis.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: ETA will recharacterize the transaction at arm's length, allocate profits to the Egyptian LLC, reclassify payment as royalty/management fee subject to 20% withholding, impose an administrative penalty of 15% of the adjustment plus monthly delay tax of 2%.

Director personal liability: De facto and shadow directors are personally co-liable; denial of treaty benefits under MLI triggers recovery against the foreign intermediary and its Egyptian principal; travel restrictions can be imposed pending payment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Mandatory penalty of 15% of the adjusted transfer-pricing difference plus the monthly 2% delay tax; evidence submitted late after ETA request may be excluded; recalcitrant taxpayers face naming and publication.

Director personal liability: The board members and the tax agent who certified incomplete TP files are personally exposed; piercing the corporate veil is permitted for wilful and grossly negligent omissions.
AML / Payment Routing Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002 and Anti-Money-Laundering decree amendments, imprisonment of three to seven years and a fine of EGP 1,000,000 to EGP 5,000,000; confiscation of all instrumentalities and proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and treasury officers are individually criminally liable for arranging split payments; personal bank accounts may be frozen and the individuals placed on the Egyptian Travel Ban List while the investigation is pending.
