Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), a semiautonomous body within the Egyptian Ministry of Finance; official e-service portals include https://www.eta.gov.eg (unified tax registration, filings, and TIN issuance) and https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg (electronic invoicing and e-receipt clearance engine).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a unified statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the ETA's electronic registration module. The LLC's authorized Egyptian resident manager (or legal representative holding a notarized, legalized power of attorney) files the following bundle: (i) updated Commercial Registry extract showing incorporation of the LLC; (ii) notarized and legalized articles of association (MoA/AoA) and board resolution appointing the representative; (iii) proof of registered office/lease address in Egypt; (iv) passports of shareholders and managers; and (v) a certified bank statement showing the subscribed share capital account (no statutory minimum capital applies to the LLC). The ETA verifies the file, issues the unified TIN and activates the LLC as an established taxpayer for CIT, VAT, withholding, payroll, and stamp taxes; VAT registration under the same TIN is automatic once the annual taxable turnover exceeds the EGP 500,000 threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - The Egyptian LLC is a juridical resident entity fully subject to entity-level corporate income tax under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005, as amended. It is taxed on worldwide profits (a territorial/production rule also captures Egypt-sourced income), at the tiered CIT schedule of 19% on the first EGP 10 million of annual taxable income and 22.5% above that band. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Egyptian LLC is a juridical resident entity fully subject to entity-level corporate income tax under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005, as amended. It is taxed on worldwide profits (a territorial/production rule also captures Egypt-sourced income), at the tiered CIT schedule of 19% on the first EGP 10 million of annual taxable income and 22.5% above that band. Egypt does not recognize pass-through taxation for LLCs; distributions are not deductible and profit-bearing distributions are within the statutory dividend rules.

Available election forms

Egypt has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms are: (i) the Investment Law No. 72/2017 regime election between the Inland Tax-Incentive Zone, the Free-Zone regime, or the Special Economic Zone regime for projects approved within the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone); (ii) the option to register voluntarily for VAT below the mandatory EGP 500,000 threshold in order to preserve input-VAT credit and export-refund rights; and (iii) treaty-relief elections applying to non-resident counterparties through the ETA's advance double-tax-treaty reduction procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supply turnover for mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration is available below the threshold, and the same unified TIN is used as the VAT ID.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods from Egypt to a destination outside Egypt qualifies for the 0% VAT rate under Law 67/2016 where the exported equipment physically leaves Egyptian customs territory, the EG LLC is the exporter of record on the customs export declaration, the zero-rated invoice is issued and cleared through the ETA e-invoicing platform, and the export price is received and repatriated in convertible foreign currency through an Egyptian licensed bank within the statutory 180-day FX deadline. Export-related technical services (offshore installation, commissioning, and training) may also be zero-rated only if the place-of-supply rules assign the services to the foreign customer with performance occurring outside Egypt; services physically performed inside Egypt remain standard-rated or exempt as provided.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA electronic declaration) duly stamped and released by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill / international waybill) naming the EG LLC as shipper and a foreign consignee
  • Zero-rated commercial invoice transmitted and QR-stamped in the ETA e-invoicing system
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Packing list and non-preferential/preferential certificate of origin accompanying the shipment
  • Signed foreign sales contract and purchase order evidencing the commercial counterparty

VAT-registered businesses must transmit all invoices (B2B) and receipts (B2C) in the ETA-mandated structured XML/JSON format, in real time, to the ETA clearance API (https://api.invoicing.eta.gov.eg). The ETA performs immediate schema validation, digital-signature verification, and value/Rule-ID checks, returning a unique invoice UUID and a digitally signed QR stamp (two-dimensional fiscal seal) that must appear on the issued document. Unstamped/unaccepted invoices are legally non-compliant and input VAT may not be deducted. ERP-integrated filing, certified fiscal clearance portals, and e-receipt generation are compulsory phases already applicable to manufacturing exporters; no analogue fiscal-journal hardware stamping is applied.

Export zero-rating claims are cross-checked by the ETA against ASYCUDA customs release data, the e-invoice repository, and the banking FX-inflow records maintained by the Central Bank of Egypt. If the exporter fails the export-evidence chain, the proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, or the true value is understated, the ETA retroactively re-characterizes the supply as domestic at the standard 14% VAT rate, assesses the tax together with a monthly delay penalty (2% per month of the assessed VAT), and may impose VAT-evasion fines up to EGP 500,000 and imprisonment. Additional consequences include denial of input-VAT refund, customs wrongful-export penalties, and automatic blocking of the company's e-invoicing account and export licenses until the assessed liabilities are settled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt is in a market-clearing, flexibly managed exchange-rate regime since the full float measures enacted in 2024. Current-account transactions are broadly convertible for bona fide commercial flows: imports of machinery, spare parts, royalties, and service fees are settled against genuine contracts, pro-forma invoices, and the ACI customs order without case-by-case central-bank pre-approval. Capital-account entries are administratively registered rather than prohibited: foreign-direct-investment capital for the LLC must enter through an authorized bank, be credited to a capital entry account, and be recorded with the CBE and GAFI to establish the basis for future remittances. Profit repatriation for a foreign shareholder is lawful under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and is gated by (i) the tax clearance certificate confirming settlement of the 22.5% corporate income tax under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and applicable dividend withholding tax, (ii) the LLC's audited financial statements, and (iii) bank records evidencing the original inward remittance. Practical hurdles are liquidity-driven: during interbank scarcity windows banks may queue USD transfers, so the LLC should secure a committed USD repatriation facility and a rolling, documented dividend policy from the outset.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods shipped from Egypt must be repatriated into the exporter's account at an authorized Egyptian bank within 90 days from the bill-of-lading / on-board date under the CBE Foreign Exchange Regulations implementing Law No. 194 of 2020 and the ACI/Nafeza framework. A documented extension up to 180 days is available for capital-goods transactions — relevant to ISIC 2819 integrated laser cutting systems — where the underlying sales contract lawfully provides a deferred payment schedule and the bank accepts the commercial contract as evidence. The exporter may credit the proceeds to an Egypt-domiciled foreign-currency retention account or convert them into EGP at the interbank market rate; no statutory flat surrender percentage applies in the current flexible-rate policy, although the CBE may issue temporary directives imposing a minimum conversion ratio during FX-stress episodes. Non-compliance, such as proceeds parked offshore or settlement outside the statutory window, triggers bank compliance alerts, exclusion from new import/export trade financing, and civil sanctions. The LLC must therefore execute export sales under irrevocable letters of credit or documented telegraphic-transfer terms with collection milestones inside the repatriation window.

Central bank supervision: The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) is the sole foreign-exchange supervisory authority in the Arab Republic of Egypt, exercising its mandate under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Foreign Exchange Regulations issued by the CBE Board of Directors. The CBE licenses authorized banks, supervises the interbank foreign-exchange market, maintains the external position and capital-movement registry, and jointly operates the Advanced Cargo Information (ACI / Nafeza) platform with the Customs Authority for import-payment verification. The General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) administers the inward foreign-investment registration file that conditions future capital and profit repatriation rights.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low — offshore digital EMI accounts (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, MultiPass and similar) are not acceptable as principal corporate treasury or export-collection accounts. The CBE repatriation rules require export proceeds to be collected into an authorized Egyptian bank within the statutory window; routing receivables to a foreign EMI would constitute a repatriation breach, render the receipts invisible to the CBE/GAFI registration records required for future profit remittances, and create tax and audit mismatches in the Egyptian books. A narrow ancillary use is permissible for collecting low-value deposits from regional distributors via locally licensed fintechs under the Payments Law No. 18 of 2019, subject to immediate onward transfer to the tier-1 bank account and full SWIFT reconciliation. The blueprint mandates a single primary tier-1 bank relationship, with domestic fintech payroll solutions used only for local salary and petty-cash flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital is injected via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the correspondent / Nostro account of the Egyptian collecting bank, with the payment order carrying the LLC's commercial-register number, tax identification number, and the CBE purpose classification for direct investment / equity subscription. The funds are credited to a segregated capital entry account and the bank issues a certificate of inward remittance, which the LLC files with GAFI to register the foreign-investment percentage; this certificate is the valuation benchmark for all future dividend, repatriation, and liquidation remittances. The MT103 must not reference loans, collateral, or personal purposes — loans above the CBE threshold require separate CBE external-financing registration and trigger thin-capitalization limitations. The beneficiary account name must match the LLC's incorporation file exactly to avoid rejection. After credit, the LLC may retain USD or convert to EGP at the interbank market rate; the bank's foreign-currency certificate must be permanently retained to substantiate exit remittances.All outbound trade payments are cleared through the ACI/Nafeza import-control platform: the LLC must obtain a registered customs-order number for each shipment before the bank will execute any transfer or LC. Principal rails for the ISIC 2819 program are: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for spare parts and advance payments within bank limits, marked with the capital-machinery import purpose code and payable only to registered suppliers; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 in USD, EUR, or CNY in favor of the Chinese or EU component supplier, at sight or with usance up to 360 days, typically margin-funded at 15 to 25 percent, and incorporating transport documents, packing lists, and inspection certificates; (3) Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for standardized low-value components where LC charges are uneconomic. For Chinese suppliers, the bank's USD/CNY dual-clearing corridor and CIPS-vs-SWIFT fee comparison should be evaluated. Each outbound payment must be reconciled within a 5 percent variance against the customs declaration; larger variances create arrears and suspension of trade-finance availability.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP foreign-exchange forwards executed by licensed Egyptian banks under the CBE Foreign Exchange Regulations, permitted only against demonstrated underlying import, export, or registered investment exposure; typical tenors are 1 to 360 days with a 10 to 20 percent collateral margin and documentary triggers including LC drafts, purchase orders, and export contracts.OTC cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP) for balance-sheet hedging of the equity bridge and capex financing, subject to CBE notional reporting and an economic-substance test tied to the underlying bank facility.Vanilla FX options (calls and puts) offered by tier-1 banks such as CIB, HSBC Egypt, and NBE to cap the USD cost of the import pipeline; premiums are cash-settled in EGP and exotic or leveraged structures are prohibited for non-financial corporate clients.Offshore non-deliverable forwards in EGP are prohibited for Egyptian resident corporates: the CBE does not recognize offshore NDFs as a permissible hedging instrument for residents. The eligible substitute is a combination of onshore deliverable forwards and an FX collar with the relationship bank, priced against the CBE interbank fixing.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low — offshore digital EMI accounts (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex, MultiPass and similar) are not acceptable as principal corporate treasury or export-collection accounts. The CBE repatriation rules require export proceeds to be collected into an authorized Egyptian bank within the statutory window; routing receivables to a foreign EMI would constitute a repatriation breach, render the receipts invisible to the CBE/GAFI registration records required for future profit remittances, and create tax and audit mismatches in the Egyptian books. A narrow ancillary use is permissible for collecting low-value deposits from regional distributors via locally licensed fintechs under the Payments Law No. 18 of 2019, subject to immediate onward transfer to the tier-1 bank account and full SWIFT reconciliation. The blueprint mandates a single primary tier-1 bank relationship, with domestic fintech payroll solutions used only for local salary and petty-cash flows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% for Egyptian-resident limited liability companies under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005; a non-resident company's Egyptian permanent establishment is subject to the same 22.5% rate on Egypt-source profits; extractive petroleum/mining activities are taxed separately at 40.55% where specifically applicable.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax is imposed under Law No. 91 of 2005 on dividend and profit distributions made by an Egyptian limited liability company to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident, except distributions made out of profits already exempted or subject to the preferential free-zone regime. Liguidation distributions attributable to accumulated profits in excess of paid-up capital may be taxed as deemed dividends at the same 10% rate; capital gains arising on liquidation are taxed separately at 22.5%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is triggered where a taxpayer's annual controlled transactions with related parties exceed EGP 20,000,000; the Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EGP 500,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting is required where the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EGP 5,000,000,000. A TP disclosure schedule must accompany the corporate income tax return even when no full Local File is required. The ETA may request contemporaneous documentation below these thresholds in a risk-based audit.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no precisely parallel Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the ETA vigorously applies substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance, and place-of-effective-management tests. A fiber-laser cutting machine manufacturer claiming Egyptian CIT benefits, free-zone tax exemption, treaty relief, or an Egyptian permanent establishment allocation must maintain real premises, production equipment, qualified engineers, independent board decisions, employment records, and active operational bank accounts in Egypt. A Spanish mailbox LLC with nominal staff and no local laser-equipment integration will be disregarded or recharacterized, with all related foreign sales reattributed to the Egyptian entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for two to five years plus a fine equal to the tax evaded, a discretionary fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000, confiscation of goods and bank balances linked to the evasion, and civil assessment of the full tax deficiency with the 2% monthly late-payment surcharge up to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who sign or cause false returns to be filed are jointly and severally liable for the tax, surcharges, and fines. The ETA may impose travel bans, freeze personal assets, and pursue criminal imprisonment against responsible officers.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ETA makes a primary adjustment to the arm's length profit, denies deduction of the underpriced cost or overstated intercompany payment, assesses the resulting 22.5% corporate income tax with 2% monthly surcharge and, in cases of false documentation, refers the conduct to criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved intercompany pricing without economic justification are personally liable for the additional tax if they were aware or should have been aware of the artificial pricing; travel bans and seizure of personal assets are possible when the company fails to pay.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GAFI and ETA jointly revoke the free-zone license, re-import duties are demanded, and accumulated undisclosed profits are taxed at 22.5% with penalties; the LLC may be struck off under the Egyptian Companies Law after investigations into false declarations.

Director personal liability: Where the shell is used as a façade, directors and shareholders can be pierced through the corporate veil and held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax duties and customs; personal travel bans, immigration alerts, and criminal sanctions apply for fraudulent free-zone licensing.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Law No. 80 of 2002 and its executive regulations impose fines ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 500,000, imprisonment for five to fifteen years, confiscation of the laundered funds, and freezing of all related bank accounts and trade permits.

Director personal liability: AML obligations are imposed on directors and compliance officers; a willfully blind director may be convicted of money laundering, barred from leave, placed on the CBE blacklist, and all personal assets connected to the transaction may be confiscated.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA holds the Egyptian LLC liable as the withholding and paying agent for the full withholding tax amount, plus 2% monthly late surcharge and non-deductible fines; deliberately mischaracterizing payments as nontaxable gives rise to criminal tax evasion liability.

Director personal liability: The managing director and any responsible board member are personally liable for the withheld but unremitted amounts; where the company is illiquid, the ETA may enforce directly against directors' assets and impose travel bans pending criminal proceeding.
