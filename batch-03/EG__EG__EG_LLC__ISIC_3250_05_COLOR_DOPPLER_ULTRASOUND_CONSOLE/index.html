Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated if goods physically leave Egypt and are covered by verified export customs declarations; direct international transport services and ancillary export services qualify. Services provided to non-residents and effectively consumed outside Egypt may also qualify for 0% VAT, subject to ETA conditions and supporting contracts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACL/DEC form) stamped and signed by customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian bank
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Proof of VAT zero-rating justification per ETA guidelines

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts through ETA-approved digital channels. Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to retrospective VAT assessment, input VAT credit clawback, monthly late payment surcharge (up to 2% per month), and penalties under Law No. 67 of 2016 and Tax Fraud Law, including fines up to EGP 250,000 and imprisonment; customs and e-invoice integration records are primary audit evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is not freely convertible in the practical treasury sense. Current-account payments, including legitimate imports, service fees, dividends, loan servicing, and other business payments, are generally permitted through licensed banks subject to documentary support, sanctions screening, tax status, customs evidence, and bank liquidity. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment, capital reductions, intercompany loans, and repatriation of invested capital, require enhanced documentation and bank review. Foreign shareholders may generally remit dividends and proceeds from a lawful capital reduction or liquidation after approval of financial statements, satisfaction of tax and corporate requirements, and submission of supporting resolutions and bank records. Banks may delay execution because of foreign-currency liquidity, enhanced due diligence, incomplete beneficial-ownership evidence, unpaid taxes, unresolved customs matters, or inconsistent transaction classifications. No blanket guarantee exists that a bank will provide foreign currency on demand.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt does not generally impose a universal statutory requirement that every exporter surrender a fixed percentage of export proceeds or convert them into Egyptian pounds. Export proceeds should be collected and handled through licensed banks in accordance with applicable CBE instructions, banking procedures, customs records, and export-control requirements. The bank normally requires linkage between the export declaration, commercial invoice, transport documents, certificate of origin where applicable, collection or letter-of-credit terms, and the incoming funds. A single universally applicable statutory repatriation deadline and mandatory conversion ratio should not be assumed; applicable timing may depend on the product, export regime, customs treatment, financing structure, bank policy, and current CBE or customs instructions. The company should obtain written confirmation from its servicing bank before each export program and maintain a reconciliation of export declarations, receivables, collections, and any outstanding balances.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020. Foreign-exchange transactions are conducted through licensed banks and authorized financial institutions. Import, export, customs, and investment documentation may also involve the Egyptian Customs Authority, the General Organization for Export and Import Control, and the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for core treasury operations. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution generally cannot replace an Egyptian licensed bank for local collection, Egyptian-pound accounts, statutory capital accounts, import settlement, customs-linked payments, payroll, tax payments, or regulated foreign-exchange execution. Offshore EMI accounts may be used for limited incoming or outgoing commercial payments only where the Egyptian servicing bank accepts the structure, the payment purpose is lawful, correspondent-bank controls are satisfied, and full source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership, invoice, contract, and tax documentation is available. The company should not use personal accounts, unlicensed payment intermediaries, informal value-transfer channels, or account structures designed to circumvent CBE controls.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends or profit shares distributed by an Egyptian LLC to a non-resident shareholder under Article 58 of Law No. 91 of 2005. Qualifying free-zone profits distributed into reinvestment under GAFI-approved terms may benefit from exemption treatment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Egyptian TP disclosure form and Local/Master File documentation are required when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions exceeds EGP 10,000,000. The ETA may nonetheless require documentation below this threshold where the taxpayer's profit margin has no reasonable business justification or the controlled price deviates materially from the arm's-length range.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the ETA and administrative courts apply substance-over-form, beneficial-ownership analysis, and Art. 49-55 anti-abuse principles. A shell LLC with no real management, employees, premises, or contract risk in Egypt is re-characterized or disregarded, causing full re-assessment, DTA denial, and possible licence revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Invoicing of Medical Imaging Exports

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment at best judgment, monetary fine of up to three times the evaded tax, imprisonment of up to five years, attachment of bank accounts, suspension of tax card, and denial of export clearance if the tax status is unresolved.

Director personal liability: Directors and the local legal representative are personally and jointly liable for evaded tax plus penalties where fraud is established. The court may order travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from board service.
Artificial Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the legal entity under judicial anti-avoidance doctrine, re-attribution of all profits to the foreign principal, denial of treaty benefits, liability for full statutory tax plus late-payment surcharges, and potential administrative dissolution.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign declarations without substance face civil restitution for the unpaid tax and penalties; fraud-based findings remove limited-liability protection and enable asset freezing and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA best-judgment transfer-pricing adjustment, gross-up of adjusted income, statutory fine of up to EGP 1,000,000 for missing documentation, and publication or blacklisting of the non-compliant taxpayer.

Director personal liability: The finance director and CEO may be held personally liable for the documentation fine where they certified false or missing intercompany price analyses. Where the omission is accompanied by concealment, criminal liability attaches.
Money Laundering / Payment Structuring Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Freezing and confiscation of all proceeds under Law No. 80 of 2002, criminal fines of EGP 10,000-100,000, imprisonment up to seven years, cancellation of the medical-device trading or manufacturing licence, and prohibition from future ETA clearance.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve the payment structure are treated as principals in the money-laundering offence. The corporate veil is pierced for their personal assets, travel documents can be seized, and civil liability covers the full transaction value.
