Competent corporate registry

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), RT I 1995, 26, 355, as amended; applicable sections: §§ 132–177 (Osaühing/private limited company), § 135 (share capital), §§ 139–142 (founding and filing of registration notice), § 143 (management board), § 187 (entry in the Commercial Register).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required at formation; an OÜ may be founded with €0 or €1 nominal capital. If the fixed share capital is below €2,500, contributions may be made only in cash and must be fully paid before filing. No escrow account is statutorily required, but paid-in capital must be evidenced as credited to a payment account in the company's name (or held by a notary/custodian). For a licensing business, a €2,500 paid-up capital is recommended to preserve dividend distribution flexibility.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for a virtual registered office without staff. If physical premises are leased, the OÜ must file a fire safety notice with the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) before occupancy and complete a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus); structural load certification under the Building Code is required only where significant structural alterations are made.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); central digital portal: e-MTA (e-Maksuamet) at https://www.emta.ee with XML/JSON machine interfaces; company and tax registration are effectuated through the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister/e-Business Register) at https://ariregister.rik.ee

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders holding an e-Residency digital ID or a notarized authorization may complete fully electronic incorporation and obtain the OÜ registry code, which automatically constitutes the national business/TIN number and is notified ex officio to the MTA; the registry code is prefixed with 'EE' upon VAT registration. Sequence: (1) obtain e-Residency card or appoint a local contact person; (2) file incorporation in the e-Business Register (state fee EUR 265; no statutory minimum share capital); (3) automatic income-tax registration with MTA is completed within the same workflow; (4) file VAT registration via e-MTA (Form KPR/KPR-2) upon exceeding EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover or earlier voluntarily; (5) register as employer via e-MTA before the first payroll; non-established foreign entities in border cases act through a fiscal representative for VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the OÜ is an opaque, tax-resident legal person taxed on profit distributions only (classic Estonian distribution-based CIT): undistributed earnings are permanently exempt; distributions are charged at 20/80 (25% effective on net) and 14/86 for regular distributions; territorial scope: no income tax on retained profits and no reserve taxation; Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable — e-Residency is an administrative digital-identity program, not a tax exemption; Pass_Through_Eligible: not available for an OÜ

Available election forms

Check-the-box and hybrid-mismatch elections do not exist under Estonian law; entity classification is statutorily fixed. Available statutory elections: (a) reduced 14/86 distribution rate where the distributed amount does not exceed the average of the preceding three years' distributions (Income Tax Act § 50(2)); (b) intra-group VAT group registration (VAT Act § 27) for closely affiliated taxable persons; (c) Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for B2C electronically supplied services to EU consumers; (d) option to move from monthly to quarterly VAT accounting where statutory turnover conditions are met; (e) ATAD anti-hybrid rules are mandatory and allow no elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover (VAT Act § 3) for Estonian-established persons; nil threshold for non-established suppliers; EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold for intra-EU distance sales and B2C digital services, above which Union OSS registration is required

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of goods: 0% VAT with full input-VAT recovery where transfer of title to a non-EU recipient is evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documents. Digitally supplied royalty-free stock music: the licensing service falls outside the scope of Estonian VAT wherever the place of supply is non-Estonian — B2B licenses to non-Estonian business customers are taxed in the customer's jurisdiction under reverse charge; B2C sales to non-EU consumers are outside EU VAT; B2C sales to EU consumers are taxed at the consumer's Member State rate via OSS. To claim 0%/outside-scope treatment, the supplier must hold a valid VAT ID/customer status declaration and two non-conflicting proofs of customer residence or business establishment; input VAT on export-related purchases is refundable through the Estonian VAT return or the EU VAT REF procedure for non-established refund claimants. Estonia imposes no distinct FX-repatriation statute (euro area); the 180-day convention tracks standard export-receivables and documentary evidence practice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX) and electronic export acceptance — mandatory only for physical media; for digital music, substitute with server download logs, license activation records, and CDN geolocation reports
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR — physical goods only; substituted for digital services by IP address logs and digital delivery confirmation
  • Signed license agreement or clickwrap EULA indicating licensee type (B2B/B2C), territory, and usage rights, plus written customer location declaration
  • VAT registration evidence of the counterparty (VIES confirmation for EU business customers; self-certification for non-EU business customers)
  • Two non-conflicting proofs of customer location (IP address, billing address, bank country, payment-processor country code)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and Stripe/PayPal settlement and bank statements evidencing collection of the export consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or fiscal-memory requirement exists in Estonia; VAT returns are mandatorily filed electronically through e-MTA (XML/JSON API supported); e-invoicing in a universal European/Peppol format is mandatory for B2G supplies (since 2017) and is standard in commercial practice; B2B e-invoicing is currently voluntary but is in scope of the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age (ViDA) reform, with digital reporting and e-invoicing expected to phase in from approximately 2025–2030.

MTA may disallow export/outside-scope treatment and reclassify turnover as domestic supply, assessing 22% VAT for up to 3 years (6 years where under-declaration is material or procedural, and through criminal limitation for fraud), with daily late-payment interest of 0.06%, an additional surcharge on tax deficiencies, and refusal of input VAT deduction leading to retrospective clawback of refunds. Intentionally false export documentation constitutes tax fraud under Penal Code § 351, subject to fines up to EUR 1,600,000 for legal persons and imprisonment up to 5 years for responsible individuals.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full member of the euro area (euro adopted on 1 January 2011) and the functional currency of the Estonian OÜ is the euro. The euro is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions; there are zero exchange-control quotas, licensing thresholds, or prior-authorisation requirements for currency conversion, profit remittance, royalty repatriation, or intra-group funding. Article 63 TFEU guarantees the unhindered free movement of capital within the EU/EEA, and Estonia has no residual national capital-control statute. Profit remittance is effected exclusively through the Estonian corporate income tax system: undistributed profits are taxed at a rate of 0%, while dividends and deemed profit distributions to shareholders attract 20% corporate income tax at the moment of distribution (or the reduced 14% rate on regular, low-distribution dividends), payable to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet). No FX clearance, inward/outward declaration, or central-bank conversion certificate is required for dividend transfers, royalty payments, or loan repayments. The only hurdles are documentary and tax-compliance related: the OÜ must make annual beneficiary owner filings, maintain an up-to-date share register, and, for non-resident shareholders, monitor the tax residency classification to ensure correct withholding treatment under the Estonian Income Tax Act and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive rules; Estonia does not apply withholding tax on dividends paid to EU/EEA treaty-resident corporate shareholders that meet the participation-exemption conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio for export earnings or cross-border licensing/license proceeds. An OÜ engaged in royalty-free stock music licensing may lawfully retain its foreign-currency revenue on multi-currency accounts indefinitely and is free to convert such balances into EUR at any time via the servicing credit institution. The only relevant statutory obligations are tax-reporting obligations under the Estonian Income Tax Act (advance payments, annual returns), the VAT informational return for cross-border digital services and distance-selling of intangibles (OÜ must self-assess reverse-charge VAT for B2B sales of digital content within the EU if applicable, or use the EU OSS scheme for B2C digital services), and the external-statistics reporting (BNR) captured automatically by Estonian banks for transactions above the statistical threshold under ECB/Eurostat methodology. No documentary proof of export proceeds is required for treasury operations.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); banking supervision is conducted by the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon), while Eesti Pank governs balance-of-payments statistics and euro liquidity operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for operational collections and micro-payout automation; Low for core treasury custody. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions chartered under PSD2 in another EEA Member State (e.g., Lithuanian EMI licences now under the ECA/Free-standing payment regime, Estonian Tuum, and other passporting fintechs) may passport into Estonia without obtaining a local e-money licence from Finantsinspektsioon. Such EMIs lawfully provide EUR IBAN settlement accounts, SEPA Credit Transfers, Instant SEPA rails, SWIFT MT103/MT102 agency services via correspondent banking, and virtual corporate cards. They may additionally provide merchant-of-record wrappers that integrate with Stripe and PayPal in layered treasury architecture. Legal constraint: an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot accept deposits, extend lending facilities, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide sanctioned correspondent access beyond its own banking partners; a nested-banking structure exposes the OÜ to the risk of EMI insolvency, suspension of passporting, or account freezes under second-tier AML scrutiny by Finantsinspektsioon. Consequently, the EMI rail is viable only for daily royalty settlement and batch redistribution to licensors, while the primary house account, foreign-exchange forward line, LC instrument capability, and Statutory Free Reserve custody must reside with a tier-1 commercial bank. The OÜ must contractually ensure the EMI service agreement includes Estonia's applicable consumer/business protection metrics, 7-figure insured safeguarding under PSD2 requirement, and read-access to transaction data for the Estonian e-Tax Board reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The OÜ has no statutory minimum capital under Estonian law, but a practical initial paid-in share capital of EUR 2,500 is benchmarked to stabilise bank onboarding and liquidity ratios. The shareholder wires the contribution from their personal or corporate account by SWIFT MT103 (for non-EUR currency) or SEPA Credit Transfer (for EUR) to the OÜ's IBAN under the bank-operations format EE38 2200 2210 0123 4567 890. The payment must be tagged with ISO 20022 purpose-category 'EQUI' (Equity Participation) or EBA/EBC purpose code 'S310' for cross-border investment statistics, and the payment reference must recite 'Osakapitali sissemakse / Member Share Capital Contribution'. The bank automatically files external-statistics (BNR) declaration to Eesti Pank, and no prior central-bank authorisation is required. Supporting verification: (i) executed capital increase resolution, (ii) bank confirmation evidencing crediting, and (iii) updated Commercial Register (Äriregister) entry confirming the paid share capital. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for operational collections and micro-payout automation; Low for core treasury custody. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions chartered under PSD2 in another EEA Member State (e.g., Lithuanian EMI licences now under the ECA/Free-standing payment regime, Estonian Tuum, and other passporting fintechs) may passport into Estonia without obtaining a local e-money licence from Finantsinspektsioon. Such EMIs lawfully provide EUR IBAN settlement accounts, SEPA Credit Transfers, Instant SEPA rails, SWIFT MT103/MT102 agency services via correspondent banking, and virtual corporate cards. They may additionally provide merchant-of-record wrappers that integrate with Stripe and PayPal in layered treasury architecture. Legal constraint: an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot accept deposits, extend lending facilities, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide sanctioned correspondent access beyond its own banking partners; a nested-banking structure exposes the OÜ to the risk of EMI insolvency, suspension of passporting, or account freezes under second-tier AML scrutiny by Finantsinspektsioon. Consequently, the EMI rail is viable only for daily royalty settlement and batch redistribution to licensors, while the primary house account, foreign-exchange forward line, LC instrument capability, and Statutory Free Reserve custody must reside with a tier-1 commercial bank. The OÜ must contractually ensure the EMI service agreement includes Estonia's applicable consumer/business protection metrics, 7-figure insured safeguarding under PSD2 requirement, and read-access to transaction data for the Estonian e-Tax Board reporting.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on gross distributed profits (20/80 of net dividend); 0% on retained and reinvested profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% of gross dividend (20/80 of net dividend); reduced to 14% gross (14/86 net) for qualifying regular distributions.

TSD must be submitted and tax paid no later than the 10th day of the month following the month in which the distribution or payment was made. The fiscal-year annual report is a separate filing with the Commercial Register due within six months of the financial year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when specified controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 250,000 in a tax year. Master File is required where the Estonian group member is part of a group with consolidated annual turnover above EUR 50 million. Country-by-Country Reporting applies when the ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not applicable in Estonia. Estonia enforces substance through corporate residency rules based on place of incorporation and place of effective management, EU ATAD CFC rules, and judicial substance-over-form doctrines. An e-Residency OÜ engaged in royalty-free stock-music licensing must maintain genuine board meetings, commercial decision-making and operational capacity in Estonia to avoid foreign CFC attribution and to substantiate any DTA claim in the shareholder jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Profit Distribution Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment of the undeclared distribution tax, late-payment interest of 0.06% per calendar day, penalty surcharge up to 30%, criminal fines for legal persons up to EUR 500,000, and imprisonment for up to five years for significant tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly under-declare distributions or conceal profit distributions are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax, penalties and interest; travel bans and imprisonment may accompany criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Artificial Retention Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of the 0% retention benefit, recharacterization of retained funds as deemed distributions, imposition of 20% CIT plus a penalty surcharge of up to 30%, and compulsory strike-off or liquidation by the Commercial Register.

Director personal liability: Directors can be compelled to satisfy deemed-distribution tax from their private assets when the company is insolvent; board members who tolerate shell inactivity breach fiduciary duties and face claims from creditors and shareholders.
Anti-Money Laundering / KYC Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Estonian Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act of up to EUR 400,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons; additional FIU sanctions, confiscation of assets, and suspension of the marketplace payment licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and money-laundering reporting officers face personal fines up to EUR 40,000 and imprisonment of up to eight years for willful or grossly negligent money-laundering facilitation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Tax authority TP adjustment with interest at 0.06% per day, penalty surcharge up to 30% of the additional assessment, and separate monetary fines up to EUR 1,600 for each unsubmitted document; such failure further disqualifies the taxpayer's pricing positions in litigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure statutory TP documentation or sign misleading benchmark studies are personally liable for penalty amounts, and in cases of deliberate falsification, criminal liability for tax evasion may attach.
