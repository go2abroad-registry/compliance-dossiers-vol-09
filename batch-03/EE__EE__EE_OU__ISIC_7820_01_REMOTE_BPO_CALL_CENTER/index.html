Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital; an Osaühing may be formed with a nominal share capital of €1 or more. No escrow or blocked bank account is required by the Commercial Register; the founders must state the subscribed capital in the Articles of Association and declare the contribution method in the registration application.

Funding & unlock path: The leased office must hold a valid structural occupancy and fire-safety certification under the Building Code and Fire Safety Act; the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) must be notified where fire-safety documentation is required. The employer must register the workplace with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon), carry out a workplace risk assessment, and maintain emergency evacuation and occupational health and safety arrangements.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up share capital; an Osaühing may be formed with a nominal share capital of €1 or more. No escrow or blocked bank account is required by the Commercial Register; the founders must state the subscribed capital in the Articles of Association and declare the contribution method in the registration application.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); secure e-services portal e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee).

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) through the Estonian e-Business Register and obtain the Estonian registry code, which serves as the general tax reference number. The OÜ then applies online via e-MTA for an Estonian VAT identification number where taxable supplies reach or are expected to reach the mandatory threshold; voluntary VAT registration is also available. E-Residency digital identity can be used to sign and file all registration documents remotely.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign shareholders incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) through the Estonian e-Business Register and obtain the Estonian registry code, which serves as the general tax reference number. The OÜ then applies online via e-MTA for an Estonian VAT identification number where taxable supplies reach or are expected to reach the mandatory threshold; voluntary VAT registration is also available. E-Residency digital identity can be used to sign and file all registration documents remotely.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a check-the-box regime. The OÜ is statutorily treated as an opaque corporate taxpayer subject to deferred entity-level income tax on profit distributions. Available mechanisms include voluntary VAT registration below the statutory threshold, optional VAT group treatment for closely linked Estonian entities, fixed-year VAT filing elections, and the domestic application of ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules for cross-border payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period. Cross-border B2B services supplied to non-resident taxable persons do not count toward the threshold unless the supply is deemed to be located in Estonia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, the 0% rate applies to export supplies evidenced by customs export documentation confirming the goods left the EU within the legally prescribed period, or to intra-Community supplies evidenced by valid ECSL statements and the purchaser's valid Estonian or other EU VAT ID. For the subject offshore BPO call-center services supplied to non-resident business customers, the general rule places the supply at the customer's business establishment, so the supply is outside the scope of Estonian VAT and is invoiced with reverse-charge or no Estonian VAT; the OÜ must retain contemporaneous evidence of the customer's taxable status, business-establishment location, and the commercial reality of the cross-border service.

  • Customs Export Declaration or EX1 movement certificate (for goods)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or airway bill proving physical departure from the EU (for goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 reference evidencing receipt of foreign consideration
  • Master service agreement and itemized service delivery reports describing the remote BPO activity
  • Customer VAT registration proof or VAT-number validation result confirming non-resident taxable-person status

Estonia mandates electronic invoicing for B2G contracts in the European standard EN 16931 format. VAT returns, annexes, and all tax communications must be filed electronically via e-MTA. There is no nationwide real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control license, no API stamping, and no local SAF-T filing obligation; the fiscal authority relies on periodic VAT returns rather than transaction-level pre-clearance.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims are reassessed as taxable Estonian supplies at 22% VAT plus 0.06% daily arrears interest from the original due date. The Tax and Customs Board can retroactively disallow export treatment, cancel refunds, and impose fines under the Tax Information Act; deliberately false declarations or forged export evidence can trigger criminal tax-fraud liability, debarment from e-MTA electronic refund channels, and a statutory three-year limitation period for reopening past VAT periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general exchange controls, capital-account restrictions, or restrictions on routine conversion of lawful funds. EU Treaty protections generally provide for free movement of capital, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, tax rules, and applicable reporting obligations. Current-account receipts and payments, including BPO service fees, may generally be received and remitted without central-bank approval. Dividends and other distributions may be paid abroad when validly declared under Estonian company law, supported by distributable equity and proper corporate records, and subject to Estonian distribution taxation and the recipient’s applicable tax treatment. Banks may require contracts, invoices, ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, or tax documentation before processing transactions. No general profit-remittance approval or mandatory conversion into euros applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no general export-proceeds repatriation deadline, surrender requirement, or mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign-currency service proceeds may be retained in permitted accounts or converted commercially. The OÜ must maintain accurate accounting records and comply with applicable financial reporting, tax, sanctions, and transaction-monitoring requirements.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s central bank and participates in the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. Financial supervision is principally conducted by the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon); payment-system and financial-crime obligations also arise under EU and Estonian law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium to High for routine EUR and supported-currency collections and payments, subject to the provider’s country, sector, ownership, and transaction-risk acceptance. An Estonian OÜ may seek service from an appropriately authorized and passported EU/EEA electronic-money institution, including Wise where the relevant service and entity are available. EMI balances are not bank deposits and are not covered by deposit-guarantee schemes; safeguarding is not equivalent to deposit insurance. Providers may restrict cash services, currencies, payment corridors, account features, or transactions and may request enhanced due diligence. Maintain a licensed bank account as the primary treasury and contingency channel; do not assume that an offshore EMI must accept the company.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits from 1 January 2025, computed as 22/78 of the net amount distributed. Retained and reinvested profits are not subject to corporate income tax merely because they are earned.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% dividend withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian resident company. The distributing company ordinarily pays corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net distribution. Transitional rules may impose 7% withholding on certain dividends paid to resident or non-resident individuals from qualifying dividends taxed under the former 14/86 regime.

Submit TSD and pay the associated tax by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the relevant payment or taxable event occurred. Where the deadline falls on a non-working day, the statutory deadline rules apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment and recovery of unpaid tax and statutory interest; administrative or misdemeanor sanctions may apply. Intentional tax fraud may constitute a criminal offence, with sanctions determined by the applicable Criminal Code provisions and facts.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal administrative or criminal liability where statutory elements are met, including intentional participation or breach of specific duties. Tax arrears are not automatically transferred to directors; recovery from a responsible person requires the statutory basis and procedure. Imprisonment may apply to qualifying criminal conduct; travel bans are not an automatic tax penalty.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length conditions, corporate income tax on the resulting deemed distribution where applicable, interest, and possible administrative or criminal consequences for deliberate misreporting.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own intentional or otherwise legally actionable conduct and may incur civil liability for breach of duties. Corporate liability remains separate unless a statutory basis for personal responsibility is established.
Failure to file or pay TSD liabilities

Statutory sanction: Tax recovery, daily statutory interest on overdue tax, and applicable enforcement or administrative sanctions; deliberate conduct may trigger criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for company arrears. Personal liability may arise under the Taxation Act or other law where the statutory conditions for breach of duty, intentional conduct, or wrongful distribution are met.
Unlawful distributions and insolvency-related misconduct

Statutory sanction: Possible recovery or restitution of unlawful distributions, civil claims, insolvency proceedings, and administrative or criminal sanctions where the conduct meets the relevant statutory elements.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for breach of fiduciary and statutory duties and may be required to compensate the company or creditors. Criminal liability and imprisonment require satisfaction of the applicable offence elements; veil piercing is not automatic.
Shell structure, sham services, or artificial management

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax benefits, recharacterization under domestic anti-avoidance rules, tax assessments and interest, and possible penalties or criminal prosecution for fraud. Foreign jurisdictions may independently assert residence, permanent establishment, payroll, VAT, or withholding obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil or criminal liability for knowing participation, false statements, or breaches of duty. Estonian incorporation does not prevent foreign enforcement or establish a director's personal liability without an applicable legal basis.
AML, sanctions, and payment-rail misuse

Statutory sanction: Account restrictions, transaction rejection or freezing, reporting and enforcement action, administrative or criminal penalties, and possible confiscation under applicable Estonian, EU, and foreign laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible individuals may face personal regulatory or criminal liability where they knowingly participate or breach applicable duties. Travel restrictions and imprisonment require a specific legal basis and are not automatic consequences of a compliance breach.
