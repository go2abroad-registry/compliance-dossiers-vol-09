Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The legal minimum share capital for an Osaühing is EUR 1.00; the capital is deemed paid either by transfer to the company's bank account or by a founder's written confirmation in the digitally signed formation dossier. No escrow account is required for online registration.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises must comply with the Occupational Health and Safety Act and the fire safety requirements under the Rescue Act. Employers must carry out a workplace risk assessment, arrange fire safety training and emergency evacuation plans, and ensure structural safety of the leased premises. No separate civil defense permit is required for consultancy services.

Paid-in capital rule: The legal minimum share capital for an Osaühing is EUR 1.00; the capital is deemed paid either by transfer to the company's bank account or by a founder's written confirmation in the digitally signed formation dossier. No escrow account is required for online registration.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, 'MTA'), operating under the Ministry of Finance. Primary digital channels: e-MTA portal (https://emta.ee), e-Business Register/Company Registration Portal (https://ariregister.rik.ee), and the state e-Residency programme (https://www.e-resident.gov.ee). Corporate income tax filings (Form TSD), VAT returns (Form KMD/KMDINF), annual declarations, and customs submissions are all lodged through e-MTA in XML or web-entry mode.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Non-resident founders obtain an e-Residency digital ID card (police background check, in-person biometric collection at an Estonian embassy or Police and Border Guard Board office). Step 2: File the OÜ formation notice in the e-Business Register via the Company Registration Portal, signed with the e-Residency card; no minimum share capital applies (Commercial Code amendment reflecting minimum_capital_requirement=false), and 100% foreign ownership is permitted. Step 3: On registration, the Tartu County Court registry department generates the 8-digit business registry code and automatically notifies the MTA; the OÜ is thereby simultaneously enrolled as a resident corporate taxpayer and receives its tax number (identical to the registry code). Step 4: The OÜ registers for VAT separately through Form KMR in e-MTA if its taxable supplies in Estonia exceed EUR 40,000 per calendar year or if the place-of-supply rules subject its services to Estonian VAT; voluntary VAT registration is permitted. A non-resident supplier with an Estonian VAT obligation and no local seat may be ordered to appoint a domestic tax representative (Tax Administration Code, Maksukorralduse seadus).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based participation exemption). The OÜ is a resident legal person taxed under the Estonian deferred CIT model — undistributed and unreasonably retained profits are taxed at 0%; a taxable event arises only upon distribution or deemed distribution. Distributions are generally taxed at 20/80 of the net amount (effective 20% of the gross payment); a 14/86 rate (effective approx. 14%) applies to the portion of 'regular dividends' not exceeding the average of taxable distributions paid in the preceding three tax years. Qualifying dividends to an EU/EEA corporate parent holding at least 10% of shares are exempt from Estonian withholding under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; other non-resident payees face withholding at 20/80 (or 14/86 if the regular-dividend conditions are met). Cross-border management and consulting fees paid to the OÜ by foreign clients create no Estonian source income tax exposure at the service level; only the subsequent dividend act triggers CIT. No CFC charge applies to the OÜ's foreign-market-entry consulting profits unless profits are funneled into a resident PE abroad. — Step 1: Non-resident founders obtain an e-Residency digital ID card (police background check, in-person biometric collection at an Estonian embassy or Police and Border Guard Board office). Step 3: On registration, the Tartu County Court registry department generates the 8-digit business registry code and automatically notifies the MTA; the OÜ is thereby simultaneously enrolled as a resident corporate taxpayer and receives its tax number (identical to the registry code). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based participation exemption). The OÜ is a resident legal person taxed under the Estonian deferred CIT model — undistributed and unreasonably retained profits are taxed at 0%; a taxable event arises only upon distribution or deemed distribution. Distributions are generally taxed at 20/80 of the net amount (effective 20% of the gross payment); a 14/86 rate (effective approx. 14%) applies to the portion of 'regular dividends' not exceeding the average of taxable distributions paid in the preceding three tax years. Qualifying dividends to an EU/EEA corporate parent holding at least 10% of shares are exempt from Estonian withholding under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; other non-resident payees face withholding at 20/80 (or 14/86 if the regular-dividend conditions are met). Cross-border management and consulting fees paid to the OÜ by foreign clients create no Estonian source income tax exposure at the service level; only the subsequent dividend act triggers CIT. No CFC charge applies to the OÜ's foreign-market-entry consulting profits unless profits are funneled into a resident PE abroad.

Available election forms

Estonian law does not provide a US-style check-the-box or entity re-characterisation election; no general hybrid mismatch elective regime is available beyond the ATAD 2 mandatory mismatch limitation rules in the Income Tax Act. Available statutory choices include: (i) VAT group registration under VAT Act § 14(6) for closely linked Estonian entities (joint and several liability), electable by the EE_OU to pool administrative burden where an Estonian sister entity exists; (ii) voluntary VAT registration before the EUR 40,000 threshold to preserve input VAT deduction rights on start-up costs; (iii) regular-dividend qualification strategy — paying taxable distributions at least annually so that a 14/86 band crystallises over the three-year average; (iv) appointment of a tax representative for VAT or customs (e-Enterprise EORI application); (v) decision on the e-invoice channel (Peppol, EDI, or e-MTA XML); and (vi) option to file group VAT returns where applicable. The EE_OU should maintain an explicit written dividend policy (annual pay-out aligned to the three-year averaging rule) and a VIES-validation record for all EU B2B clients to support the 0% export treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable supplies made in Estonia (VAT Act § 3(1)); EU distance-selling suppliers of goods into Estonia face a EUR 10,000 annual pan-EU threshold below which home-country rules apply; non-EU suppliers of goods to Estonia are registrable from the first supply with no de minimis threshold; B2B services supplied by a foreign supplier to an Estonian business customer are reverse-charged and therefore do not trigger the supplier's registration. For the EE_OU's ISIC 7020 consulting services to non-resident business clients, the Estonian nexus is generally absent because the place of supply is the customer's country; Estonian VAT registration only becomes mandatory if the OÜ makes domestic-standard-rated supplies exceeding EUR 40,000 or supplies services to domestic non-business customers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For cross-border B2B consultancy (ISIC 7020), Estonian VAT is 0% (effectively outside the scope of Estonian VAT) where the place of supply is the customer's state: (i) an EU business customer — VAT Act § 7(2) and EU VAT Directive Art. 44 — provided the customer's VAT identification number is validated in VIES at the time of supply; (ii) a non-EU business customer — the supplier must hold a valid foreign business tax number and reasonable supporting evidence of the customer's entrepreneur status (business registration extract, official confirmation). If the client is a non-business person resident in Estonia, place of supply is Estonia and 22% VAT applies; if the client is a non-business person outside the EU, consulting services follow the B2C general rule (place of establishment of the supplier) so Estonian 22% VAT normally applies unless a specific relocation of place-of-supply rule for services performed abroad is met. Input VAT on costs attributable to B2B export services (SaaS subscriptions, travel, market data) remains fully deductible in the KMD return, generating a refundable balance where no domestic output VAT exists; VAT on costs attributable to exempt supplies would be non-deductible. Export of tangible goods (not the primary activity of the EE_OU) qualifies for 0% under VAT Act § 17(6)-(8) only if goods leave the EU within 90 days of supply, evidenced by the customs export declaration in ETSF.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document, SAD) lodged in Estonia's ETSF customs-declaration system, plus the exporter's EORI number and, if claimed, EUR.1 or ATR movement certificates for goods consignments; for pure consulting services, the substitute evidence bundle is: executed service contract, sales invoice with ISO 20022 remittance reference, and an acceptance report certified by the foreign client.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (for goods) or, for services, a dated digital delivery log evidencing that the consultancy deliverables (reports, feasibility studies, licensing-assistance milestones) were released to the non-resident recipient outside Estonia.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement showing receipt in EUR from the foreign customer's account and clearly reconciling the payment to the specific invoice; Estonian law sets no separate forex repatriation certificate, but MTA audit practice requires bank-level proof of cross-border payment flow.

No continuous transaction control (CTC) fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime exists in Estonia for B2B invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G supplies: public-sector invoices must be submitted in structured XML format via Peppol (EN 16931 compliant) through the e-MTA interoperability service. Real-time digital reporting is obligatory only for B2C cash-register transactions: retail, hospitality, and other cash sellers must use fiscal cash-register software that transmits each receipt (KMA records) electronically to the MTA immediately upon issue. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary (Peppol, EDI, or e-MTA XML); there is no unique invoice-registration code or API clearance stamp. The EE_OU may adopt Peppol BIS 3.0 to satisfy the forthcoming EU ViDA/electronically-supplied information standardisation expectations.

The MTA may re-characterise a zero-rated B2B service as a domestic taxable supply where the customer's VAT ID is invalid, the place-of-supply evidence is incomplete, or the service is performed in Estonia for a domestic benefit, leading to retrospective VAT assessment at 22% plus interest at 0.06% per day and a misconduct fine (tax proceedings) of up to EUR 1,000 for an incorrect declaration; intentional evasion (maksupettus, Penal Code § 397) can trigger criminal liability with up to 5 years' imprisonment, confiscation, and extended audit periods. The statutory audit window is 3 years for a correctly filed tax return and 5 years where bad faith or omission is established. For related-party export fees, transfer-pricing adjustments may reclassify under-compensated services as a deemed profit distribution, attracting 20/80 CIT and potential withholding; the MTA routinely cross-checks the VIES validation date, the customer's bank country, and the substance of the deliverable. The EE_OU must retain the complete five-part evidence bundle (contract, invoice, acceptance, VAT-ID validation, bank receipt) for at least 7 years; the customs-adjacent records for goods are retained 10 years to withstand clawback and criminal-investigation timelines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia, as an EU member state and Eurozone participant, maintains full convertibility for both capital and current accounts. Profit remittances are unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/KYC and beneficial ownership compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds under Estonian law. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts without conversion to local currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank)
