Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital required; an OÜ may be incorporated with €0 share capital. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box elections. Estonia applies EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules through the Income Tax Act. No fiscal unity election is available; each OÜ is taxed independently. The 20% corporate income tax is triggered only upon profit distribution, with statutory rate elections embedded in the dividend distribution resolution; a special reduced effective rate applies to undistributed profits (0% in any given tax year before distribution).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover in Estonia; EUR 10,000 for intra-Community distance sales; import VAT registration and reverse charge apply to non-established foreign suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exports of goods to non-EU destinations evidenced by physical exit within 3 months of dispatch, intra-Community supplies, and services directly tied to exported goods or international transport. Regulatory consulting services (FDA 510(k)/CE Mark) supplied B2B to non-Resident clients fall outside Estonian VAT via place-of-supply rules and reverse charge, not zero-rating; evidence includes client business status and service contract.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD EX1/EX3)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR waybill / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FX repatriation evidence)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or cash-register cloud mandate. E-invoicing is voluntary and aligned with European Standard EN 16931 and the Estonian eArve (e-invoice) format; VAT returns are filed via e-MTA on a monthly or quarterly basis.

MTA conducts export-focused VAT audits with document diligence; unjustified zero-rating triggers VAT clawback, daily interest at 0.06%, late-payment surcharges up to 100% of the tax benefit for deliberate evasion, retrospective assessment periods of 3 years (7 years for intentional misconduct), and criminal liability up to 5 years imprisonment under Penal Code § 401 for fraudulent export refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia, being a euro-area member since 1 January 2011, maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions. The regime is governed by EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (TFEU Articles 63–66) and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act (Välisvaluutaseadus), which contains no general exchange-control approvals, no advance licensing, and no quantitative limits on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of capital. Profit remittance from an Osaühing (OÜ) is subject to Estonian income tax only upon distribution (20% regular rate, or 14% on previously taxed dividends under §50 Income Tax Act), and no central-bank or FI permission is required to effect the payment. The only practical hurdles are standard banking AML/KYC verification, sanctions screening, and documentary support evidencing the underlying transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no statutory surrender, retention, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds, including cross-border consulting income. There is no requirement to sell foreign currency to the central bank or any authorised financial institution. Receipts in EUR can be freely held or converted at market rates; the obligation is limited to proper accounting under Estonian GAAP/IFRS and reporting to the Tax and Customs Board (MTA) for VAT and corporate income tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the euro-area single monetary policy

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate for EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that are passporting into Estonia under PSD2/E-Money Directive. Non-EU/offshore EMIs without an EU passport have no viable access to Estonian clearing rails and will generally be blocked by correspondent banks. Estonian fintech/EMIs, such as LHV-backed services, can offer multi-currency ledger accounts and faster API integration, but they are not a substitute for a tier-1 commercial bank. For FDA/CE regulatory consulting, the primary recommendation is to maintain a core operational account in a credit institution; an EU EMI may be used only for auxiliary card processing or sub-account segregation.

Defense evidence: A narrowly scoped advisory agreement listing permitted FDA 510(k)/CE marking consulting only (drafting technical files, advising on clinical evaluation, regulatory strategy, quality management systems), explicitly excluding services such as clinical trial patient recruitment, medical device trading/distribution, and offshore incorporation; customer due-diligence portfolio including beneficial ownership of all client entities; sanctions screening logs; transaction-chain documentation from source of funds; an internal know-your-customer procedure; audit trail from the ISO 13485 quality management system; records of continuous regulatory training; credible references from previous EU/US clients; demonstrable tax substance in Estonia (e.g., local pension contributions, rent invoices, telephone/VoIP infrastructure, director business insurance).
Sudden inflow of large invoice payments from overseas medical-device or pharmaceutical firms that are not immediately reconciled with signed consulting contracts

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial OÜ share capital contribution, funds must be wired in EUR via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA, if within EEA) from the investor's or shareholder's bank to the Estonian corporate account. The payment reference should read 'Share capital payment under Estonian Commercial Code' and must align with the shareholder resolution and e-Business register filing. The applicable OECD/Baltic econometric purpose code is typically 'B08' (capital contributions) or the EU BOP code 'E020' (foreign direct investment within EU), depending on the sending bank's classification. Estonia's central authorization authority, Eesti Pank, requires no pre-clearance under the Foreign Exchange Act; however, the receiving bank will verify compliance with AML/KYC, source of funds, sanctions, and beneficial ownership. All inbound injections need full corporate wiring instruction (SWIFT BIC, IBAN), the shareholder's passport copy, and a notarised/certified copy of the resolution increasing share capital if the injection exceeds the initial amount. The bank may request evidence that the funds have been lawfully earned and that no shell-company typologies are triggered.Outbound disbursements for trade and operating costs (e.g., subscription fees, subcontractor payments, software procurement, and consultancy fees) are primarily executed via SEPA Credit Transfers (within the EU) and SWIFT MT103 payments outside the eurozone. For larger vendor contracts, particularly with non-EU medical device manufacturers, a documentary Letter of Credit under UCP600 issued by Swedbank, SEB, or LHV can provide settlement security; the LC requires a professional commercial invoice, packing list, and in the case of regulatory consulting, a signed statement of work / deliverable acceptance certificate. Applicable purpose codes for outgoing payments are 'B22' for professional and consulting fees, 'B12' for licence fees/royalties, or 'B07' for trade settlement of goods under standard Bank of Estonia classification. No surrender or hedging obligation applies to such disbursements because EUR is the company's functional currency, but for cross-currency vendor obligations the bank will execute a spot or forward foreign exchange transaction with no central-bank approval requirement.EUR/USD and EUR/GBP outright forward contracts (up to 12 months) through tier-1 credit institutions such as Swedbank, SEB, or LHVNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in emerging-market currencies (e.g., USD/BRL, USD/INR, USD/ZAR) to hedge underlying client collections or vendor costs without settling principal in the illiquid currencyCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for longer-term exposure, executed under ISDA master agreements with investment-grade bank counterpartiesEUR/USD or EUR/GBP currency options (vanilla call/put, zero-cost collar) if the ultimate client receivables are linked to regulatory milestone payments in those currenciesTime-option forwards (flexible drawdown) for variable consulting fee flows, requiring no take-or-pay obligation beyond underlying invoices
