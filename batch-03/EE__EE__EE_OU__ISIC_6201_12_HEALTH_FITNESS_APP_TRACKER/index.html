Name reservation / lock period

Estonia does not generally provide a separate statutory company-name reservation period equivalent to a 30-day reservation system. The applicant should conduct an e-Business Register search, trademark search through the Estonian Patent Office and EUIPO, domain-name search, and conflict review before filing. If the registrar rejects the proposed name, obtain the written decision and legal grounds, correct or replace the name, and submit a revised filing. An administrative challenge may be filed with the competent court under Estonia's administrative court procedure within the applicable statutory deadline stated in the decision, generally 30 days from notification unless the decision specifies another period. The applicant may also seek judicial review of an unlawful registry decision; filing does not create name priority unless expressly granted by the registry.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); online portal e-MTA at https://www.emta.ee/eng

E-government filing portal

For an Estonian OÜ: register the company with the e-Business Registry (e-Äriregister), which issues a registry code; the same code is the mandatory tax identification number for VAT and CIT purposes. To obtain a VAT identification number, file VAT application Form KAT with the MTA via e-MTA. Mandatory registration is required within 5 working days of exceeding the €40,000 turnover threshold; voluntary registration is also available. Non-resident entities selling goods or services in Estonia without an EU establishment must either register with the MTA directly, appoint a fiscal agent, or use the applicable Union/Non-Union OSS scheme to avoid a local registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For an Estonian OÜ: register the company with the e-Business Registry (e-Äriregister), which issues a registry code; the same code is the mandatory tax identification number for VAT and CIT purposes. To obtain a VAT identification number, file VAT application Form KAT with the MTA via e-MTA. Mandatory registration is required within 5 working days of exceeding the €40,000 turnover threshold; voluntary registration is also available. Non-resident entities selling goods or services in Estonia without an EU establishment must either register with the MTA directly, appoint a fiscal agent, or use the applicable Union/Non-Union OSS scheme to avoid a local registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for Estonian OÜs; the corporate income tax on distributions is mandatory. Available elections include one-off decision on VAT group treatment (konsolideerimisgrupp), voluntary VAT registration under the €40,000 threshold, and the option to apply the reduced withholding rate on dividends under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No hybrid mismatch elections are permitted; ATAD-consistent anti-hybrid rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 (annual taxable turnover); non-residents have no threshold and must register for any taxable supply in Estonia unless covered by reverse charge or OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% VAT on exported goods, the exporter must hold a valid customs export declaration (SAD) establishing that the goods physically left the EU, together with commercial transport and payment evidence. For exported digital services B2B, zero-rating (reverse charge) applies when the recipient is a taxable person in another EU member state (valid VAT number) or outside the EU. For B2C digital supplies via app stores, the platform is the deemed supplier; the developer does not claim zero-rating but may rely on the OSS scheme or the platform's compliance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for physical exports)
  • Commercial invoice showing 0% VAT and incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement for the export sale
  • Copy of the customer's VAT number (EU B2B) or contract/service agreement (non-EU B2B)
  • Proof of digital service delivery (download logs, app analytics)

Estonia does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. VAT returns are filed electronically via e-MTA; e-invoicing in a structured format is mandatory for invoices issued to the Estonian government. The MTA provides an API for automatic filing and access to tax data.

Tax audits can be opened within 3 years (6 years in cases of misreporting or suspected deliberate fraud). If a zero-rating claim fails substantiation, VAT is reassessed at 20%, plus default interest of 0.06% per day. A misstatement penalty of up to 40% of the deficiency applies; fraud penalties may rise to 100%, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud can result in imprisonment up to 5 years. The MTA can also suspend the taxpayer's VAT registration.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a member of the Eurozone and EU; the euro is a fully convertible currency with no capital or current account restrictions. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted and subject solely to standard corporate tax compliance and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds under EU law; all receipts may be freely converted to any currency and repatriated without statutory timelines or ratios.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) and European Central Bank (ECB)

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 per related-party transaction type in a fiscal year triggers the preparation of a Local File; a Master File is required when the group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 50 million; a Country-by-Country Report threshold of EUR 750 million group turnover applies under the OECD/ATAD framework.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime akin to offshore financial centres; however, substance requirements are embedded in the Tax Procedure Act, the Income Tax Act, and the Treaty Abuse Convention. An OÜ must demonstrate real economic presence and business purpose in Estonia (registered office, active management, employees, premises, or credible local outsourcing) to avoid being reclassified as a shell/conduit company, to qualify for treaty benefits, and to withstand anti-avoidance challenges. The Central Tax and Customs Board may disregard artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance and recharacterise the transaction as a taxable distribution at 20%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Disguised Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a deemed dividend subject to 20% (or 14%) corporate income tax plus late-payment interest at 0.06% per day; a tax fraud fine of up to EUR 32,000 for the company under the Tax Procedure Act; criminal prosecution under Penal Code §§ 389–391 for intentional tax evasion, carrying a fine or imprisonment up to 5 years for responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate income tax and associated interest if they participated in or failed to prevent the abusive distribution; the court may impose a travel ban where the tax debt exceeds EUR 5,000; in flagrant cases, personal assets may be seized through veil-piercing, and directors face a disqualification order of up to 10 years and imprisonment up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residency or treaty benefits; deemed profit distribution tax at 20% on the entire income stream; forced deregistration from the Commercial Register; penalty of up to EUR 5,000 for misleading the registry; recalculated VAT consequences if the company is found to have no effective establishment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes up to the full evasion amount if they certified false in the annual report or acted in bad faith; a court may issue a restriction on acting as a director for up to 3 years; directors of a shell entity can be subject to personal sanctions, including asset seizure and a ban on leaving Estonia during tax criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable profits in Estonia; additional 20% corporate income tax on the adjusted deemed distribution; late-payment interest; a penalty of 5% of the additional tax for incorrect filing (minimum EUR 40); if false documentation is submitted, the fine can reach EUR 20,000 per offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign TP documentation knowing it to be materially false are personally liable for the resulting tax deficiency and penalties; TP adjustments can trigger director disqualification under Company Law; in cross-border fraud cases, imprisonment up to 5 years and extradition risk apply.
AML / Money Laundering through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act penalties: criminal fines up to EUR 1.6 million for legal persons; confiscation of proceeds and instrumentalities; imprisonment for natural persons up to 5 years; administrative penalty for breaches of the AML to up to EUR 16,000; mandatory suspension from the Register where the company is deemed a mule vehicle.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable for failure to adopt adequate AML controls, even without intent; they face imprisonment up to 5 years, civil restitution to the state for laundered funds, travel bans, and a 15-year prohibition on managing legal entities; personal assets may be frozen pre-conviction.
Late Filing or Under-declaration in TSD

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalty of up to 5% of the unpaid tax (minimum EUR 40); forced tax assessment based on best judgement; late-payment interest at 0.06% per day; if the under-declaration exceeds EUR 1,000 and is intentional, a criminal fine up to EUR 32,000 and imprisonment up to 1 year for negligent reporting.

Director personal liability: The director responsible for tax filings is jointly and severally liable for the company's withheld/unpaid tax if the company is insolvent; a travel ban applies where tax arrears exceed EUR 5,000; continued gross negligence warrants a court order prohibiting the individual from serving as a director for up to 5 years.
