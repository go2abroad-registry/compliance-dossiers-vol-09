Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), operating under the Egyptian Ministry of Finance and governed by the Unified Tax Law No. 30 of 2023 (consolidating income tax rules formerly under Law No. 91 of 2005) and by Value Added Tax Law No. 67 of 2016, as amended by Law No. 3 of 2023. The ETA integrated self-service portal (https://eta.gov.eg) provides TIN onboarding, corporate income tax registration, VAT registration and filing, e-invoicing and e-receipts clearance, and export VAT refund workflows. Entities located in the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE) or other General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) free zones interface additionally with GAFI for zone licensing and annual facility-fee administration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC manufacturing agricultural drone sprayers, the statutory procurement sequence is: (1) incorporate the LLC with GAFI or the SCZONE authority and obtain the commercial registration certificate (qayd tijari) from the Commercial Registry; (2) file a tax card (al-bitaqa al-daribiyya) application through the ETA e-services portal, submitting the articles of association, commercial registration extract, board resolution appointing a resident tax representative (mumathil dharibi), registered-office title or lease, and proof of identity for all non-resident shareholders; (3) receive the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the VAT registration certificate from the ETA, which are issued through the same onboarding workflow; (4) complete simultaneous registration on the ETA e-invoicing platform and with the Egyptian Customs Authority on the Advanced Cargo Information (ACI) system as an exporting operator; (5) undergo ETA technical inspection or Large Taxpayer Office assignment where projected export turnover exceeds the large-taxpayer threshold. Standard issuance takes approximately 5 to 10 business days for the TIN and tax card, and the tax card must be kept aligned with the commercial registration renewal cycle and any change in shareholders or registered address.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident incorporated entity taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Unified Tax Law No. 30 of 2023 (headline corporate rate 22.5%, with small-business progressive brackets in limited cases). Where the LLC holds a GAFI or SCZONE free-zone license with an export-only mandate, a Tax_Exempt_Zone variant applies, substituting corporate income tax with an annual facility charge of generally 1% to 3% of export-derived sales value; VAT zero-rating on outward supplies continues to operate separately. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident incorporated entity taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Unified Tax Law No. 30 of 2023 (headline corporate rate 22.5%, with small-business progressive brackets in limited cases). Where the LLC holds a GAFI or SCZONE free-zone license with an export-only mandate, a Tax_Exempt_Zone variant applies, substituting corporate income tax with an annual facility charge of generally 1% to 3% of export-derived sales value; VAT zero-rating on outward supplies continues to operate separately. Egyptian LLCs are not eligible for pass-through or partnership look-through classification.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The Unified Tax Law No. 30 of 2023 contains limited statutory elections: (i) family companies, being entities whose capital is held exclusively by natural persons from a single family, may elect to be taxed as tax-transparent partnerships with income imputed to the individual family members rather than taxed at the entity level; (ii) companies licensed in free zones may elect between the Public Free Zone regime, replacing income tax with the 1% to 3% annual facility charge on gross export sales, or the Private Free Zone regime, preserving standard entity-level taxation while enjoying customs duty relief and input VAT exemptions; and (iii) registered exporters may elect the small-business turnover-based presumptive tax bracket where statutorily eligible. Hybrid-mismatch entity elections are not recognized under Egyptian tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies where the person's annual taxable turnover of goods or services exceeds EGP 500,000 within the preceding 12 months (Article 16 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016). An export-only LLC producing agricultural drone sprayers must register for VAT regardless of turnover in order to claim input VAT credits and periodic refunds on export zero-rated sales. Registration of non-resident suppliers carrying on economic activity in Egypt through an actual or deemed establishment is likewise required. Customs-side ACI operator registration is administratively separate from the VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported agricultural drone sprayer units, spare parts, and related hardware applies under Articles 12 and 13 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 and its Executive Regulations where: (i) the goods physically depart Egyptian customs territory supported by a customs-approved export declaration; (ii) the buyer is a non-resident person and the invoiced consideration is receivable in convertible foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system; and (iii) the prescribed export documentation bundle is maintained in auditable form. Offshore services, including drone calibration, remote agronomy analytics, and route-planning software supplied to a recipient outside Egypt, may also qualify for zero-rating where the service benefit is realized outside Egypt. In all cases, the LLC must hold the valid export authorization for dual-use UAV technology issued by the competent Egyptian defense trade-control authorities, otherwise the zero-rating claim fails at the completeness review stage. Zero-rated supplies are declared on the monthly VAT return, preserve input VAT recovery on imported components, and any residual credit is refundable under the ETA refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document, SAD/Form 41) referencing the ACI export pre-declaration number issued by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Truck Waybill endorsing the shipment to the non-resident buyer or the buyer's nominated bank
  • Inward Foreign-Currency Remittance Certificate (bank call notice or tanfiz receipt) evidencing that the USD or EUR sale proceeds were credited to the LLC's Egyptian bank account
  • Commercial invoice and the duly executed export sales contract with the non-resident purchaser, including INCOTERMS, UAV technical specifications, and spare-parts schedule
  • Certificate of Origin issued by the competent Egyptian chamber of commerce or industrial registration authority

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance: every tax invoice must be transmitted in XML or JSON to the ETA e-invoicing platform through a certified REST API gateway, authenticated with public-key infrastructure digital certificates, validated against ETA fiscal business rules, and stamped with a Unique Identification Code (UUID) and a cryptographic QR code before the invoice may be issued to the customer. E-receipts are additionally mandated for B2C cash register transactions under the phased e-receipt program. Input VAT is legally recoverable only when the related e-invoice has received ETA fiscal clearance. Penalties for issuing invoices without clearance start per-invoice and escalate up to EGP 50,000 per document in repeat cases, with risk of VAT certificate revocation and criminal referral for forged clearance codes. Export invoices relating to agricultural drone shipments must appear in the monthly e-invoice stream and be reconciled with the official customs export declaration during VAT refund review.

Export zero-rating refund packages are jointly reviewed by the ETA Large Taxpayer Audit Directorate and the Customs Export Reconciliation Unit; red flags include related-party sales, artificial intermediation, recycled components, inflated invoice values, and foreign proceeds retained offshore beyond the repatriation deadline. Legal consequences for fraudulent zero-rating or falsified export documentation include: (i) under Article 69 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, premeditated tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of not less than double the amount of tax evaded; (ii) retrospective clawback of all input VAT credits and refunds, compounded by monthly delay interest of 2% on VAT arrears and an additional administrative penalty of up to 100% of the disallowed amount; (iii) suspension or revocation of the tax card and customs clearance privileges; (iv) public listing on the ETA non-compliance register; and (v) referral to the Tax Prosecutor under the Unified Tax Law No. 30 of 2023. The tax prescription period for proven evasive zero-rating extends to 6 years from the end of the relevant tax period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a market-determined floating EGP regime since 6 March 2024 under Law No. 194 of 2020. Current-account transactions (goods, services, royalties, dividends) are freely convertible via CBE-licensed banks at interbank rates without prior approval. Capital-account flows are permitted but require GAFI/CBE registration of the inbound investment, an inward-investment certificate from the receiving bank, and Egyptian Tax Authority clearance for outbound profit remittance. Dividend repatriation is subject to a 10% statutory withholding (or treaty-reduced rate upon filing a beneficial-ownership certificate); practical friction arises from USD interbank liquidity and documentary verification of the funded capital, not from statutory quotas.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Law No. 155 of 2023 abolished the former mandatory repatriation-and-surrender regime under Law No. 118 of 1975. Statutory timeline: no fixed conversion deadline; export receipts may remain in the exporter's onshore foreign-currency account or in a licensed offshore account, subject to a 5-year CBE balance-of-payments reporting horizon. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% — the EG_LLC retains 100% of USD/EUR receipts and may deploy them directly for import payables, loan servicing, reinvestment or dividends. Compliance requires a NAFEZA export declaration, GOEIC export-register card, commercial invoice, bill of lading/AWB, and CBE bank acknowledgment for statistical registration.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

LOW. Egypt's Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves foreign-exchange, documentary collections, and trade settlements to CBE-licensed banks. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Currencycloud, Airwallex-type) are not recognised settlement counterparties; wires carrying a non-bank BIC are rejected or escalated to enhanced due diligence. Such rails are acceptable only for immaterial operating expenses (software, travel), never for export collections, LC settlement, or ECGF-guaranteed transactions. All treasury clearing must be bank-to-bank via SWIFT.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity enters exclusively via a CBE-licensed bank. The remitter must be the shareholder of record; SWIFT MT103/202 must carry remittance code 'Direct Investment — Equity Contribution' (CBE BoP code 3285) and reference the EG_LLC commercial-register number and GAFI dossier. The receiving bank credits the FCY account and issues an inward-investment certificate (CBE Form-II) anchoring capital registration, future repatriation, and tax basis. Conversion to EGP is executed at the daily interbank rate. Shareholder loans must be supported by a notarised facility agreement and CBE external-debt registration to permit interest capitalisation and outbound servicing. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: LOW. Egypt's Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves foreign-exchange, documentary collections, and trade settlements to CBE-licensed banks. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Currencycloud, Airwallex-type) are not recognised settlement counterparties; wires carrying a non-bank BIC are rejected or escalated to enhanced due diligence. Such rails are acceptable only for immaterial operating expenses (software, travel), never for export collections, LC settlement, or ECGF-guaranteed transactions. All treasury clearing must be bank-to-bank via SWIFT.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard corporate income tax rate on net taxable profits under Article 51 of Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended (banks and Suez Canal/oil categories taxed at 40%-45%; not applicable to an industrial LLC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on cash dividends and other profit distributions paid by an Egyptian LLC to resident and non-resident shareholders (Article 55, Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 64 of 2015).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Annual CIT return due within four months after the close of the fiscal year (Article 60, Law No. 91 of 2005). For a calendar-year LLC, the deadline is 30 April of the following year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 1 million: mandatory annual TP Disclosure Form to accompany the CIT return when the aggregate of controlled transactions in a tax category exceeds this amount. EGP 10 million: aggregate related-party transactions trigger the preparation of Local File and Master File in line with the ETA Transfer Pricing Guidelines (Presidential Decree No. 467 of 2018). MNE groups with consolidated group revenue above EGP 22 billion (approx. EUR 750 million) must file a Country-by-Country Report; all documentation must be in Arabic/English and retained for six years.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not administer a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing, but the ETA applies a broad substance-over-form and GAAR doctrine (Article 43, Law 91/2005; Article 5 of Law 30/2023). An Egyptian LLC is automatically tax-resident by incorporation; the use of offshore board meetings, nominee shareholders or an accommodation office does not defeat Egyptian source taxation. The Free Zone regime requires a fixed registered premises, Egyptian employees, real local activity and an audited account trail for the 10-year exemption; a shell holding or letterbox distribution company exporting drones through Egypt will be treated as fictitious and the profits re-attributed to the foreign beneficial owner, without the free-zone benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Commercial Documentation

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for three to five years and a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000 (Article 143, Law 91/2005); payment of the evaded tax in full plus monthly interest of 1% (up to a cumulative additional levy of 100% of the evaded tax); seizure of movable and immovable assets; cancellation of the tax card and e-invoicing registration.

Director personal liability: The general manager, executive directors and authorised signatories are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt; the ETA may obtain a preventive travel ban and asset-freeze order against each responsible person; criminal conviction results in disqualification from board membership and potential custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax administration disregards the corporate entity, re-characterises the transactions, and assesses tax on the beneficial owner at 22.5% on the deemed Egyptian-source profit, with surcharges up to 100% of the tax and penalties for late filing; free-zone tax holidays are revoked and export incentives clawed back; the trade register and tax licence may be struck off.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are personally liable for the unpaid corporation tax and penalties on a civil basis; they may be criminally prosecuted for falsifying corporate records and for aiding tax evasion; travel bans and a prohibition from holding any Egyptian corporate directorship follow a conviction.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The ETA reassesses the related-party transactions under the arm's-length principle, upward-adjusts the taxable income, and applies the 22.5% rate on the difference; the assessment window extends six years; monthly interest of 1% accrues on the deficiency; non-compliance in repeat audits can trigger anti-evasion fines of up to EGP 20,000 per false declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors approving transfer prices without a benchmark report are jointly and severally liable for the primary tax adjustment; involvement in falsified documentation lifts the corporate veil and leads to civil restitution, an ETA travel ban and criminal prosecution for tax fraud in aggravated cases.
Anti-Money Laundering and Hard-Currency Violations

Statutory sanction: Penalties under AML Law No. 80 of 2002 reach imprisonment of up to 15 years and fines of up to EGP 1 million; the EMLCU may freeze the company's bank accounts and the CBE may blacklist the exporter and suspend its import/export registry, depriving the drone manufacturer of foreign currency allocations and expunging export incentives.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors face personal criminal liability, custodial sentences, mandatory restitution of the undeclared foreign currency and civil fines; the Ministry of Justice may issue a travel ban and prevent the responsible persons from managing financial entities in Egypt.
