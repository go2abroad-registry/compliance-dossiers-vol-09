Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA – مصلحة الضرائب المصرية); unified national tax portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipt platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; customs export clearance and documentation administered by the Egyptian Customs Authority through the NAFIZA single window: https://www.nafiza.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI, https://www.gafi.gov.eg) and obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate from the Commercial Registry of the Ministry of Trade and Industry. 2) File the unified tax registration application with ETA within 30 days of registration or commencement of activity, attaching the notarized Articles of Association, commercial registry extract, premises title/lease, and identity documents of owners/managers; ETA then issues a permanent Tax Identification Number (TIN), the unified Tax Card (بطاقة ضريبية) covering income tax, payroll/salary tax, stamp tax and VAT, plus a separate VAT registration certificate under Law 67/2016. 3) Register as an exporter with the Egyptian Customs Authority in NAFIZA and obtain the exporter code/electronic signature. 4) Complete ETA e-invoicing onboarding (XML/JSON API integration or web portal) before issuing the first invoice. 5) Foreign non-resident ownership is permitted with no minimum capital requirement; if the business operates as a branch or permanent establishment of a foreign entity, a local fiscal representative must be appointed for registration, filing and e-invoicing; pure non-resident exporters without Egyptian nexus need only register for Egypt-source withholding obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. Under Income Tax Law 91 of 2005, an Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the general corporate rate of 22.5%; no pass-through or partnership-style attribution is available. The regime is non-territorial (worldwide income with foreign tax credit relief). The business is not situated in a licensed tax-exempt investment zone (Suez Canal Economic Zone incentives apply only to projects licensed inside that zone), so standard national CIT and VAT rules apply.

Available election forms

Egypt does not offer check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections; an LLC is per se a corporate taxpayer under Law 91/2005 and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (i) election of a non-calendar fiscal year subject to ETA approval; (ii) SME simplified presumptive taxation under Law 152/2020 for qualifying businesses with turnover up to EGP 40 million; (iii) foreign tax credit election for foreign-source income instead of a deduction; (iv) advance pricing agreements and mutual agreement procedures with the ETA Transfer Pricing unit (Ministerial Decree 661/2018); (v) intercompany dividend participation relief for dividends received from resident companies subject to normal Egyptian tax. BEPS Action 2 hybrid-mismatch rules have not been enacted; ETA may apply the statutory general anti-avoidance rule and transfer-pricing provisions to recharacterize hybrid instruments and artificial structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration threshold: EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover or taxable supplies in the preceding 12 months; voluntary registration from EGP 100,000. Exporters and importers must register regardless of turnover, and registration is required before commencing taxable activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0%) applies to: (a) goods physically exported outside Egypt by a VAT-registered exporter under a NAFIZA customs export declaration, with title passing to a non-resident buyer and payment received in foreign currency through Egyptian banking channels; and (b) exported or offshore services rendered to a non-resident for effect and use wholly outside Egypt, with remuneration received in convertible currency. Conditions: the goods must leave Egypt or the services must be consumed abroad no later than 180 days from the date of the invoice or supply; the exporter must hold a valid tax card and exporter code; failure to satisfy the export or repatriation timeline retroactively converts the zero rating to 14% output VAT.

  • NAFIZA Customs Export Declaration (بيان جمركي تصديري) stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or FX bank credit advice from an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract or non-resident purchase order
  • Exporter registration certificate and VAT tax card extract

All VAT-registered taxpayers, including exporters, are onboarded to the ETA e-invoicing system. B2B invoices must be transmitted in real time to ETA using the unified XML/JSON schema via API or web portal; ETA validates the invoice and returns a unique UUID and 2D barcode/QR code without which the invoice is fiscally invalid and input VAT credit is denied. B2C transactions fall within the phased e-receipt system where applicable. No physical fiscal stamp or banderole applies to resin figurines, and no dedicated fiscal clearance hardware is required beyond ETA e-invoice validation; export invoices must be reconciled with NAFIZA export declarations and the matching FX receipt.

ETA cross-checks e-invoices, NAFIZA export declarations, bank FX receipts and CBE exchange-control records. If zero-rated goods are not exported within the 180-day window, or export proceeds are not repatriated through Egyptian banks within the Central Bank of Egypt deadline, ETA claws back the zero rating and assesses 14% output VAT plus monthly delay penalties and statutory interest from the original invoice date. Fraudulent claims, such as false export declarations, fictitious inward remittances, or re-importation of exported goods, attract criminal penalties under VAT Law 67/2016, including imprisonment and fines of not less than 100% and not exceeding 200% of the tax evaded, mandatory repayment of any refunded VAT, revocation of the VAT/Tax Card, and NAFIZA/GOEIC blacklisting of the exporter and its responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-float regime under Law No. 194 of 2020. Current-account transactions for genuine trade and service payments are generally settled at the interbank market rate without a prior surrender requirement, but each transaction must pass through an authorized bank. Capital-account transactions, including share capital injections, capital increases by non-residents, and subsequent divestment or dividend remittances, are technically permitted but are subject to a documentary evidence chain and CBE/GAFI validation. Dividend and branch profit remittance is allowed to the extent that the LLC meets annual audit, tax clearance, Central Vigilance Organization reporting, and supports the outgoing transfer with the original inward MT103 or central bank foreign-exchange receipt. Full free convertibility is absent because non-resident funds may be directed into a special ‘private free account’ or temporarily held in local foreign-currency accounts, but any conversion to EGP or remittance triggers bank-level compliance, CBE daily reporting, and in practice the bank may require an underlying hedge-eligible trade or investment transaction document. Profit remittance hurdles include: outstanding tax balances, pending value-added-tax filings, absence of audited financial statements, or mismatched legal form/goods descriptions in the custom duties declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Pursuant to CBE General Rules for Foreign Exchange Business issued under Law 194/2020, export proceeds must be repatriated to an Egyptian licensed commercial bank and credited to the exporter’s account within 180 days from the date of the bill of lading, airway bill, or equivalent transport document. The mandatory conversion ratio is 50% of the foreign-currency export proceeds, which must be immediately converted to EGP at the prevailing interbank rate; the remaining 50% may be retained in a foreign-currency account for working-capital purposes, imports, or debt-service obligations. Exemptions apply only where the exporter holds an authorized import financing allocation, a CBE approved offsetting foreign-currency liability schedule, or the export arises from a duty-free / free-zone operation with separate CBE FX authorisation. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window exposes the LLC to CBE penalties, limitation of bank credit lines, and an automatic reconciliation order against the proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – General Department for Foreign Exchange Supervision and the Foreign Exchange Committee

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary clearing rail and Medium as an ancillary collection gateway. Egyptian law and CBE regulations recognize only licensed Egyptian banks and licensed exchange houses as authorized deposit-taking and FX transaction entities. Offshore digital EMI accounts (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, or similar) are not permissible as the statutory receiving account for export proceeds or for settlement of inbound equity capital, because such accounts are not within CBE supervision and do not produce the documentary chain required for Egyptian tax and foreign-exchange clearance. The practical use case is limited to enabling overseas pre-order deposits and PayPal/Stripe payouts, provided the LLC maintains full visibility of the ultimate account holder and routes surplus balances through a licensed correspondent bank into an Egyptian bank account within 90 calendar days. For statutory export and treasury purposes, a bank-held foreign-currency account in Egypt is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident member initiates the capital injection by SWIFT MT103 from a bank outside Egypt to the LLC's authorized Egyptian bank foreign-currency account, remitting exactly USD/EUR to the entity and using a purpose code that aligns with equity injection into an Egyptian limited liability company, such as “Direct Investment – Equity Capital” under the CBE’s daily balance reporting codes. The receiving Egyptian bank applies a two-step control: first it matches the inward transfer to the LLC’s GoV/Registry credentials, an entry from the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) certificate, and the banking signature card; second it obtains a copy of the capital-declaration statement and records the purpose under CBE’s electronic reporting system. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary clearing rail and Medium as an ancillary collection gateway. Egyptian law and CBE regulations recognize only licensed Egyptian banks and licensed exchange houses as authorized deposit-taking and FX transaction entities. Offshore digital EMI accounts (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, or similar) are not permissible as the statutory receiving account for export proceeds or for settlement of inbound equity capital, because such accounts are not within CBE supervision and do not produce the documentary chain required for Egyptian tax and foreign-exchange clearance. The practical use case is limited to enabling overseas pre-order deposits and PayPal/Stripe payouts, provided the LLC maintains full visibility of the ultimate account holder and routes surplus balances through a licensed correspondent bank into an Egyptian bank account within 90 calendar days. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions: crossing this threshold mandates attaching TP disclosure forms TP-1/TP-2 to the Form No. 19 return and maintaining contemporaneous documentation. Master File and Local File obligations apply for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EGP 3,000,000,000; the ETA may demand TP documentation below the threshold where transactions involve low-tax jurisdictions or a foreign shareholder without substance.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to UAE/Caribbean regimes. Instead, the ETA applies substance-over-form doctrines, the beneficial-ownership tests embedded in Egypt's DTA network, CFC rules introduced by Law 26/2020, and GAAR anti-abuse principles. An ISIC 3240 resin-statue LLC must maintain verifiable economic substance: physical production premises, resin/mold inventories, Egyptian payroll for casting and painting staff, export/import customs records, and board minutes of actual management meetings in Egypt. A passive 'letterbox' LLC claiming treaty benefits while acting as a conduit for foreign shareholders will be denied DTA relief, face deemed-PE attribution, and risk having its limited-liability shield pierced.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Under-Declaration of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2-5 years plus a fine equal to 100%-200% of the evaded tax under Law 91/2005 and Unified Tax Procedures Law 67/2023; automatic upward assessment; mandatory confiscation of production equipment; immediate loss of all SME preferential rates.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorised managers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and withheld amounts; criminal conviction triggers asset seizure, travel bans, bank-account freezes, and disqualification from serving on boards of Egyptian companies.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CIT adjustment by the ETA using TNMM or profit split with an uplift penalty of 30% of the additional tax; separate fixed fines under the 2020 amendments; listing on the ETA non-compliant taxpayer register; reversal of underlying expense deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors signing knowingly false TP declarations are personally liable for the penal uplift and are exposed to criminal referral for filing false tax documents.
Disguised Distributions / Shareholder Loan Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of outstanding balances as deemed dividend distributions subject to 10% WHT plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge (capped at 80%); CFC attribution of passive income; interest disallowance under thin-cap rules.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are statutorily required to withhold 10% dividend WHT at the time of payment; failure to withhold creates personal liability for the full withheld amount, interest, and penalties, enforceable through personal bank attachment.
Shell Company Structure / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of payments as taxable dividends or services; 20% WHT imposed on the gross amount; CFC taxation on shifted profits; the foreign entity may be delisted from the Egyptian commercial registry and the Egyptian LLC's deductions are reversed with evasion penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Egyptian LLC and the foreign shell face criminal fraud liability under the Egyptian Penal Code and AML Law; Egyptian travel bans are routinely issued in collection proceedings; corporate veil piercing is available where the LLC is operated as an instrument of fraud.
AML / Payment-Rails Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: AML Law 80/2002 as amended: imprisonment of 3-7 years and a fine of EGP 50,000-1,000,000 for natural persons; for legal persons, fines up to EGP 2,000,000 plus confiscation; the ETA treats unreported payment-rail inflows as deemed revenues subject to full evasion penalties and 22.5% CIT on the entire amount.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly executing or benefitting from unreported payment rails are prosecuted as principals; bank accounts are frozen, corporate e-invoice permits are suspended, travel bans are issued, and directors are barred from re-licensing business activities in Egypt.
