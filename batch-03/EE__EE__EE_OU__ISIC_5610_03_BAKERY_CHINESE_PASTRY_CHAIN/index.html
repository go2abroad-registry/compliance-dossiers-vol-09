Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); primary electronic portal: e-MTA.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian Osaühing obtains its statutory legal-entity identifier through registration in the Estonian Commercial Register. The Commercial Register code is used as the principal domestic tax identifier. The company establishes access to e-MTA using an accepted electronic identification method and registers for applicable tax accounts. VAT registration is requested from MTA through e-MTA or the prescribed application process, with submission of the legal name, Commercial Register code, registered address, business activities, expected taxable supplies, management and contact details, bank information where requested, and supporting evidence of genuine economic activity. A non-resident entity without an Estonian registered entity must register with MTA where it carries on taxable activities or has a permanent establishment in Estonia. A foreign entity may need an Estonian tax representative for VAT compliance where required by Estonian law, particularly where it is established outside the European Union. MTA may request contracts, premises information, invoices, accounting records, supply-chain evidence, and identification of directors or beneficial owners before granting a VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an Osaühing. The Osaühing is a separate legal person and is generally subject to corporate income tax when profits are distributed or treated as distributed, rather than when profits are earned and retained. Applicable elections and treatments may include dividend and profit-distribution treatment, qualifying intra-group distributions, fringe-benefit treatment, business-purpose classification of expenses, and treaty relief. EU and Estonian anti-hybrid-mismatch rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion where hybrid instruments or entities produce double deductions or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes. No election converts an Estonian Osaühing into a fiscally transparent entity for general Estonian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover arising in Estonia from the beginning of the calendar year. A taxable person generally must apply for VAT registration when the statutory threshold is exceeded; certain cross-border supplies, acquisitions, imports, fixed-establishment activities, and transactions subject to special rules can require registration independently of the threshold. The 24% standard rate applies from 1 July 2025; reduced rates and exemptions apply only where statutory conditions are satisfied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier can demonstrate that the goods left the EU customs territory and that the supply satisfies the Estonian VAT Act and customs requirements. The supplier must retain the customs export declaration and reliable transport or delivery evidence linking the goods, purchaser, invoice, and export. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% only where the customer has a valid VAT identification number in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains required transport and recapitulative-statement evidence. Services are not automatically zero-rated because they are described as offshore services. The place-of-supply rules determine taxation: qualifying B2B services supplied to a customer established outside Estonia may be outside the scope of Estonian VAT, while specified services have special location rules. A 0% claim must be supported by contemporaneous commercial, transport, customs, and customer-status evidence.

  • Electronic customs export declaration and export MRN, including release-for-export and exit confirmation where available
  • Commercial invoice identifying the Estonian supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment, and delivery terms
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier export record
  • Purchase order, sales contract, delivery evidence, and proof linking the exported goods to the zero-rated invoice
  • Evidence of payment or settlement where relevant to substantiate the transaction and commercial reality
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence to another EU Member State, and required recapitulative statement
  • Inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory Estonian export zero-rating document and Estonia does not impose a general foreign-exchange certificate requirement

Estonia does not generally impose universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory submission of every B2B invoice to MTA. VAT invoices must satisfy the statutory content, numbering, issuance, and record-retention requirements. Electronic invoices are permitted if authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and legibility are maintained. Structured e-invoicing is used in public-procurement and government-facing processes through recognized channels, and contractual or sector-specific requirements may apply. There is no general Estonian VAT digital-stamping requirement for ordinary domestic invoices. VAT returns and related schedules are filed electronically through e-MTA, generally by the 20th day of the following month.

Estonian law does not impose a general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. MTA may deny or reverse a 0% VAT treatment where export, transport, customer-status, or business-purpose evidence is incomplete or unreliable. The resulting VAT, interest, and applicable penalties may be assessed retrospectively under the statutory assessment period, subject to the applicable limitation rules. Incorrect VAT returns can lead to tax reassessment, late-payment interest, and misdemeanour or criminal liability where conduct involves intentional fraud, false documents, concealment, or material tax evasion. Administrative penalties depend on the specific breach and applicable procedural provision; fraudulent claims may also trigger recovery of improperly refunded VAT, audit costs where legally available, and referral to investigative authorities. Records should generally be retained for the statutory accounting and VAT periods, with longer retention where an audit, dispute, or special legal requirement applies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro (EUR) as sole legal tender. There is full convertibility on both current and capital account items. Profit remittances, dividends, and branch repatriations are not subject to foreign exchange controls, conversion restrictions, or withholding taxes. The only impediments are non-exchange-based AML/KYC documentary hurdles applied at the commercial banking layer. For a foreign-owned bakery master franchise OÜ, payment of franchise royalties, central kitchen management fees, and dividend distributions is unrestricted, subject only to board resolution and source-funds evidence to the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no statutory timeline for inbound export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may maintain multi-currency accounts, retain foreign currency indefinitely, and convert to EUR at market rates. SEPA and SWIFT settlement are unconditional, but banks may apply standard economic substance review and frozen-account safeguards in exceptional money-laundering or sanctions cases.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting as the central bank and member of the Eurosystem; foreign exchange and capital movement oversight is aligned with the European Central Bank and EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viable as supplementary payment infrastructure for EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions, e.g., those authorized in Estonia by Finantsinspektsioon or passported under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Feasibility rating: Medium for primary corporate accounts; High for payout automation, international supplier disbursements, and card-acquiring settlement. Legal constraints: EMIs are not full credit institutions; they cannot hold capital escrow accounts in the same legal sense, cannot provide lending, and are subject to safeguarding requirements. Major Estonian tier-1 banks may classify certain high-volume or high-risk EMI activity as correspondent-like and impose restrictive due diligence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on distributed profits (calculated as 20/80 of net distribution); 0% on retained and reinvested profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory withholding tax for dividends paid by an Estonian resident OÜ to a non-resident corporate shareholder; instead, the company pays corporate income tax at 20% (or 14% reduced rate) on distribution. No separate dividend withholding tax is imposed at the shareholder level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly TSD filings due by the 10th day of the month following the reporting month. The annual income tax return for non-resident branch and other taxpayers is due by 31 March of the following calendar year. For a calendar-year accounting period, the annual information return and corporate income tax declaration are due with the annual report filing under the Commercial Code, typically by 30 June; the MTA may require filing by 30 April or as published in the official deadline schedule.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Form TSD – Section 1: Resident legal person profit distribution (dividends) – code 2111 Total gross amount of dividends or other profit distributions made during the reporting month that are subject to taxation at 20% (gross-up 20/80). For a non-resident corporate shareholder, no Estonian CIT is withheld at the company level on dividends if the company is a resident OÜ; the distribution must be reported in this line for the 20/80 corporate income tax accrual. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonia applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as incorporated through §50(4)–(5) of the Income Tax Act and the General Part of the Taxation Act §84. There is no statutory monetary threshold in domestic law for the Local File / Master File; however, the MTA may require transfer pricing documentation for any transactional value that is not at arm’s length. Practically, the MTA’s TP documentation order requires documentation for cross-border related-party transactions that individually or in aggregate exceed €1,500,000 annually for goods and €500,000 annually for services, royalties, and interest. A Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least €50,000,000 and transactions falling above the thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the Crown Dependencies or UAE; however, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under §83 of the General Part of the Taxation Act is actively applied to disregard transactions lacking economic substance. The MTA may recharacterize a structure or deny treaty benefits under the OECD BEPS Action 6 principal purpose test where a non-resident shareholder, franchise royalty arrangement, or financing structure exists primarily for tax avoidance. For a bakery master franchise, the Estonian OÜ must demonstrate real operational substance: physical central kitchen facilities, personnel, contracts, and management decisions located in Estonia. The 'management and control' test under the Commercial Code and the analysis of the place of effective management under §6 of the Income Tax Act determine corporate residence; if the OÜ is managed from abroad, it may become a tax resident of another state and lose Estonian CIT advantages on retained profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion on undeclared profit distributions and artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under the Penal Code §§364–366 (tax fraud): up to 5 years’ imprisonment for intentional non-payment of significant tax; fine from €200 to €1,600 for the natural person and up to €2,000,000 for the legal entity. MTA additionally levies the unpaid tax plus penalty interest at 36.5% per annum and an additional tax penalty of up to 50% of the tax (Taxation Act §154).

Director personal liability:
Disguised profit distribution as deemed director compensation

Statutory sanction: Deemed employment income at market rate; 33% social tax and 20% income tax on wages are assessed retroactively, plus penalty interest and a fine under §152 of the Taxation Act. Under commercial law, the recipient director is jointly liable to restore the withdrawn amounts to the OÜ in insolvency.

Director personal liability:
Missing or falsified transfer pricing documentation (royalty payments to master franchisor)

Statutory sanction: The MTA may disregard the royalty payment and add 20/80 corporate income tax on the distribution deemed to have occurred, applying the arm’s length standard under OECD TP Guidelines. Failure to produce documentation upon request triggers a non-compliance penalty under §153 of the Taxation Act of up to €25,000 and penalty interest. Under the Penal Code, intentionally false documentation may lead to criminal tax fraud charges.

Director personal liability:
Shell company / economic substance failure of e-Residency business structures

Statutory sanction: The company can be deemed resident of China or the shareholder’s jurisdiction under domestic law or treaty tie-breaker analysis, exposing the entire retained profit to Chinese CIT (generally 25%) and dividend withholding at the shareholder level. Estonia imposes compulsory deletion of the OÜ from the Commercial Register for failure to submit annual reports; directors (including e-resident foreign directors) are barred from acting and may become personally liable for the company’s tax debts if they have grossly violated management duties under §620 of the Law of Obligations Act. Criminal conversion may apply if the structure is used for money laundering under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act: up to 7 years’ imprisonment.

Director personal liability:
Non-compliant e-residency e-Resident corporate reporting evasion

Statutory sanction: MTA ex officio tax assessment at up to the highest reasonable turnover (Taxation Act §§93–94), plus 20/80% CIT and social tax on uncompensated personal use, 33% social tax on any reclassified salary benefits, fixed penalty of €200 per missing return, address and bank account seizures, and dissolution; a travel ban under the Aliens Act may be imposed for arrears exceeding €2,000. Directors’ disqualification under the Commercial Code lasts up to five years.

Director personal liability:
Money laundering and AML failure in payment rails (Master_Royalty_Wire / POS)

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (RAH), fines up to €1,200,000 for legal persons and imprisonment up to 7 years for responsible persons. The FIU (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) can freeze assets. Additionally, under the Commercial Code shareholders are not personally liable, but the court may pierce the corporate veil only in cases of fraudulent misuse; restitution under the Law of Obligations Act §620 applies to directors as joint and several liability.
