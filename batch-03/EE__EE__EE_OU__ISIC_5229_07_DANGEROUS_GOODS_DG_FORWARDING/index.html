Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, MTA); e-services portal e-MTA at emta.pria.ee / www.emta.ee. Company registration is administered through the Äriregister (Commercial Register) by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident founder incorporates the Osaühing (OÜ) under the e-Residency programme; the Commercial Register issues the 8-digit registry code, which is the statutory corporate TIN. VAT registration is applied for exclusively through the e-MTA portal using the registry code and a designated contact person for non-resident applicants. Registration becomes mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 per calendar year, or earlier if the company voluntarily registers to recover input VAT or to issue Estonian VAT invoices; MTA issues a VAT ID in the format EE followed by 9 digits, generally within 5 working days of a complete application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the OÜ is a resident company subject to Estonia's deferred and distribution-based corporate income tax. Undistributed profits are taxed at 0%; regular profit distributions are taxed at 20/80 (increasing to 22/78 from 1 January 2025); deemed, irregular and non-business distributions are taxed at 25/75; regular dividends paid to resident natural persons may qualify for the reduced 14/86 rate. No pass-through eligibility exists. Foreign PE profit distributions may be exempt under the participation exemption where the foreign profits have been taxed abroad.

Available election forms

Estonia does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a pass-through election. Available statutory elections include: (i) the election to apply the reduced 14/86 CIT rate to regular profit distributions meeting the statutory 'regular distribution' pattern; (ii) VAT group treatment for resident companies with at least 51% control that elect to file as a single taxable person; (iii) EU OSS/IOSS elections for distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services; (iv) advance pricing agreement applications to MTA. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD apply by operation of law, requiring self-assessment and adjustment without any elective mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover per calendar year (VAT Act §3), being the economic turnover threshold that triggers mandatory Estonian VAT registration; voluntary registration is permitted for businesses below the threshold and by non-resident suppliers; a separate EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold applies to cross-border distance sales and electronically supplied services under the OSS/IOSS regimes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to supplies of goods physically dispatched from Estonia to a third country (e.g., Norway, Switzerland, Ukraine, the United Kingdom) and to international transport services and ancillary loading, unloading, handling and dangerous-goods preparation services where such services are directly connected to an export or import journey. Eligibility requires proof that the goods left EU customs territory within the statutory period (MTA administratively expects removal within 3 months of dispatch), that a valid customs export declaration (SAD/EX with MRN) exists, and that the DG freight services are supported by transport contracts naming a foreign consignee and destination.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX form) bearing a valid Movement Reference Number (MRN) issued by Estonian Customs within the MTA
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Master/Air Waybill evidencing the final third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or SWIFT MT103 evidence showing settlement and foreign-exchange repatriation into Estonia
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract and Incoterms terms of sale
  • Dangerous-goods consignment file: ADR/IATA/IMDG dangerous-goods declaration, UN-certified packing certificate, container/vehicle packing certificate and competent-authority approvals
  • Signed contract of carriage (CMR road consignment note, air waybill or sea waybill) and terminal delivery receipt from the destination port or warehouse

Estonia does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or SdI-type continuous transmission mandate. Mandatory structured e-invoicing applies in the B2G segment (invoices to public-sector buyers) since 30 April 2019; since 1 July 2022 all taxable persons must be technically able to receive electronic invoices. VAT compliance is performed by filing the monthly KMD return, with the KSD annex and customs declaration supplement, electronically through the e-MTA portal by the 20th day of the following month. EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations apply to intra-EU supplies.

MTA audits export zero-rating retrospectively and places the burden of contemporaneous documentary proof on the taxpayer. If goods are not removed from the EU within the statutory period, if the MRN or transport evidence is missing, or if FX settlement is not received and repatriated within the operational 180-day benchmark, MTA will re-classify the supply as domestic, assess VAT at 22% on the gross invoice, charge late-payment interest at 0.06% per calendar day (approximately 21.9% per annum) from the original due date, and may impose a penalty of up to 30% of the assessed amount, or up to 100% where deliberate concealment is established. Fraudulent export zero-rating in dangerous-goods and suspected carousel traffic triggers criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code §390 (tax fraud, punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment), joint and several liability of directors and officers, and retrospective customs-duty and excise clawback. DG misdeclarations are cross-checked with the Transport Administration and environmental authorities and may additionally result in suspension or revocation of the statutory dangerous-goods forwarding licence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Estonia has been a eurozone member since 1 January 2011 and maintains unrestricted cross-border capital flows under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU Capital Free Movement Directive (88/361/EEC). There are no exchange control approvals, no quotas, and no separate exchange rate regime. Profit repatriation is burdened only by the corporate income tax layer at distribution: 20% of gross dividend (i.e., 20/80 of net distribution), or 14% for regular distributions (shown as 14/86 net effective). No withholding tax further applies to dividends paid to non-residents. Funds may be held in EUR or any foreign currency, and no requirement exists to convert export receipts into EUR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Estonia does not impose any mandatory surrender, repatriation deadline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters retain full freedom to hold receipts offshore or in multicurrency accounts. However, Estonian AML/CFT legislation (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, as amended) requires complete documentation of the commercial underlying for wire transfers exceeding EUR 15,000. For dangerous goods (DG) forwarding, the carrier or freight-forwarding company should also maintain all transport and customs documentation to satisfy due diligence under the EU AML Directive.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) under the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) acting jointly with Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority), with the European Central Bank as the ultimate monetary authority for the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for ancillary treasury operations. Estonian e-Residency and a modern regulatory climate permit onboarding with licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Monese (UK/EE), Wise Europe (BE), Payoneer, or Finom (NL) for operational sub-accounts, payroll, or supplier payments. However, the primary corporate account and all high-value DG trade settlements must be held with a full EU credit institution (licensed under CRR/CRD IV) to ensure settlement finality and FD-like deposit protection (EUR 100,000 per depositor under Estonian Deposit Guarantee Fund). As a legal constraint, EMIs are not permitted to provide credit or hold client funds for settlement accounts in a way that exposes them to market risk; the DG forwarding company cannot rely on an EMI for Letter of Credit operations or SWIFT MT103 with true payment guarantee. For the main treasury function, an EMI should only be used for low-value, high-frequency disbursements where the aggregate balance does not exceed EUR 50,000.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection into the Estonian OÜ must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with an appropriate purpose code. For a share capital contribution (minimum nominal no longer required, but if used, any amount allowed, no legal minimum since 15 March 2019), use ISO 20022 purpose 'COST' (capital) or 'A0100' (direct investment) as commonly accepted by Estonian banks. The correspondent banking instruction must include the beneficiary account IBAN (EE38XXXX...) and the reference 'OÜ share capital / contribution according to board resolution dd.mm.yyyy'. For shareholder loans, purpose 'S0100' (loans) or 'LEND' (loan). Verification chain comprises: (a) the original notarized board resolution, (b) an updated share register or notarial acceptance, (c) a certificate from the Estonian Commercial Register confirming share increase, (d) identity of the remitting bank must be on the beneficiary bank's allowed list; if the wire comes from a non-SWIFT country, an intermediary bank in Germany or France may be required. No Estonian Central Bank permit is necessary for any inbound foreign capital, but the beneficiary bank must be given 7 days' prior notice for any inbound transfer exceeding EUR 100,000 to allow sanctions screening and source-of-funds validation.Clearing rails for outbound DG trade disbursements are segmented by value and instrument: (1) SWIFT MT103 – used for operational payments to subcontractors, freight forwarders, terminals, and customs brokers; the standard execution window is T+0 to T+1 for EUR SEPA payments, and T+2 for cross-border currency transfers (e.g., USD) via correspondent banks. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) – the mandatory rail for DG trade settlements above EUR 50,000 or for jurisdictions on the bank's watchlist; the applicant (Estonian OÜ) will be asked to post collateral (cash margin or escrow facilities) at 30%-100% depending on the counterparty country. The full documentary chain must include the Dangerous Goods Declaration and IMDG/ADR certificate as LC documents. (3) For long-term charter or multi-year transport contracts, a standby LC (SWIFT MT760) or bank guarantee is used. (4) Direct debit (SEPA DD) is not used for cross-border third-party payments. All outbound wires above EUR 15,000 require e-invoice or contract reference and the bank may impose a 24-hour positive-pay verification window for first-time counterparties. For payments to sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions, the bank may only process after a successful OFAC and UN/EU sanctions scan and may require a pre-payment letter of indemnity.FX Forwards (outright or physical delivery) under ISDA 2002 or a local master agreement with the same bank; the maximum tenor is normally 12 months without collateral for counterparties with investment grade rating; longer tenors require a Credit Support Annex.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permissible for currencies where a full delivery forward is prohibited or unavailable (e.g., RUB, CNY via offshore, or TRY); settlement is conditioned on a mutual ISDA with a reliable fixing source (e.g., WM/Reuters for the relevant currency).Cross-Currency Swaps (XCCY) – permitted for aligning a newly contracted EUR revenue profile with an existing USD or SEK liability; the contract may be a simple floating-to-floating or fixed-to-floating swap, documented under form IFEMA 2020.FX Options – vanilla call/put options may be purchased (premium paid upfront) without a bank limit; selling options is only permitted under a full trading credit line and is not recommended for a DG forwarding operation without a central treasury.Currency Swaps with the Central Bank – available only for member state institutions; not applicable to private OÜs. The Estonian OÜ may alternatively use the Eurosystem's standing facility but only via a licensed credit institution.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for ancillary treasury operations. Estonian e-Residency and a modern regulatory climate permit onboarding with licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Monese (UK/EE), Wise Europe (BE), Payoneer, or Finom (NL) for operational sub-accounts, payroll, or supplier payments. However, the primary corporate account and all high-value DG trade settlements must be held with a full EU credit institution (licensed under CRR/CRD IV) to ensure settlement finality and FD-like deposit protection (EUR 100,000 per depositor under Estonian Deposit Guarantee Fund). As a legal constraint, EMIs are not permitted to provide credit or hold client funds for settlement accounts in a way that exposes them to market risk; the DG forwarding company cannot rely on an EMI for Letter of Credit operations or SWIFT MT103 with true payment guarantee. For the main treasury function, an EMI should only be used for low-value, high-frequency disbursements where the aggregate balance does not exceed EUR 50,000.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed retained profits; 20% on profit distributions (14% reduced rate for regular dividend payments meeting the statutory three-year regular distribution history, and advanced/limited cases as prescribed by the Estonian Income Tax Act)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ; the economic internal tax is the corporate-level 20% distribution tax (or reduced 14% regular rate), which is final and not a non-resident withholding tax

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 3,000,000 annual turnover of the Estonian resident entity triggers Local File preparation; Master File is required when consolidated global group revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000; taxpayers below these thresholds may still be summoned by the tax authorities to justify cross-border related-party transactions on request.

Economic substance scope: Estonia is not a traditional ESR offshore jurisdiction, but the Estonian Income Tax Act § 14(11) codifies the arm’s length principle; the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD), the general anti-abuse rule, CFC rules, and the Estonian Commercial Code require genuine economic substance, including physical office or dispatch presence, locally licensed dangerous-goods specialists, and actual decision-making; an OÜ used purely as a zero-percent tax wrapper without operating substance is subject to recharacterization and deemed distribution treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Under-reported Freight Revenues

Statutory sanction: All evaded corporate income tax collected; mandatory late-payment interest at 0.06% per day; administrative fine of up to EUR 10,000 per unreported item; criminal tax evasion conviction carries imprisonment up to 5 years (with a serious criminal group aggravator up to 10 years) and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Authorized representatives and actual decision-makers are jointly and severally liable for the OÜ’s unpaid taxes when the underpayment stems from intentional accounting manipulation; courts may order asset preservation, travel bans, disqualification from managing commercial companies for up to 3 years, and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit Structure

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities apply GAAR and recharacterize the artificially allocated profits as hidden profit distributions, adding 20% income tax (or 14% reduced rate if applicable) plus penalties of up to 50% of the additional tax; the tax claim may be elevated to criminal money-laundering or tax-evasion categories if systematic.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual beneficial owners cannot shield behind the OÜ veil where the entity is demonstrated to be a sham: civil liability for all attributable taxes, restitution of diverted payments, and statutory prohibition of managing board offices; in aggravated cases criminal sentencing includes imprisonment generally 1 to 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, fines to legal persons can reach EUR 1,600,000 up to 10% of annual turnover; the favored remedial enforcement action is suspension/revocation of the forwarding license and immediate transaction blockage.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement or silently allow deficient KYC/AML controls are criminally liable in their personal capacity; possible consequences include disqualification, travel restrictions, fines, and up to 8 years’ imprisonment for large-scale money laundering in a commercial setting.
Operating Without Dangerous-Goods Transport License

Statutory sanction: Administrative coercive fine issued by the Estonian Transport Administration: up to EUR 3,200 per inspection and immediate service prohibition; recurrence results in criminalization under the Estonian Penal Code Article 372 with imprisonment up to 3 years and mandatory destruction or safe-repacking of the hazardous shipment.

Director personal liability: The certificate holder is personally responsible for transport safety; in the event of a leak, casualty, or environmental damage, directors face civil-damages restitution, seizure of personal assets, prohibition from serving as a director in the transport sector for 3 years, and imprisonment for endangering public safety.
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjusts related-party income, imposes the Estonian corporate income tax on the adjusted deemed distribution at 20% or reduced 14% regular rate, charges 0.06% per day interest, and applies a fine of up to EUR 4,000 for defective TP documentation; serious deliberate tax fraud prosecution is available.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing declarations, approve intercompany invoices, or suppress profitability evidence are personally liable for the tax increase; the court may pierce the corporate veil, order restitution, ban the individual from board memberships for up to 3 years, and impose imprisonment up to 5 years where the conduct is classified as deliberate fiscal fraud.
