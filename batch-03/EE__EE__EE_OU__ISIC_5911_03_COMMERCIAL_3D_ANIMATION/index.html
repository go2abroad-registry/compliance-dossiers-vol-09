Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); online portal: e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee/et/eraklient/e-maksuamet); commercial registration via the e-Business Register (https://www.rik.ee/en/e-business-register)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File incorporation of OÜ (private limited company) with the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) through the e-Business Register; no minimum capital required. Step 2: The assigned Estonian registry code automatically constitutes the tax identification number (TIN) for income tax, VAT, and social tax purposes; no separate TIN certificate is issued. Step 3: After registration, the entity is auto-enrolled in the e-MTA digital tax account where all tax cards and returns are managed. Step 4: Non-resident legal entities without a permanent establishment must apply separately to the MTA for an Estonian VAT identification number (KMKR) before making taxable supplies. Step 5: Foreign shareholders/directors must submit notarized copies of constitutional documents, register the beneficial owners in the e-Business Register, and appoint a contact person with an Estonian address for service of documents; e-Residency digital ID cards allow fully remote digital filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies a uniquely deferred entity-level corporate income tax system: undistributed and reinvested profits are taxed at 0%, while profit distributions trigger income tax at 20/80 of the net dividend (effective 25% gross-up); no check-the-box pass-through regime exists for OÜs. Statutory elections available: (i) election to apply the reduced 14/86 dividend tax rate for regular distributions if dividends are paid consistently for at least three preceding years (qualifying 'regular distributions'), subject to an immediate 7/93 withholding on gross distribution; (ii) election to pay income tax on advance dividends according to the same distribution schedule; (iii) irrevocable election to file consolidated group taxation is unavailable at domestic level but group relief is limited to intragroup transfers; (iv) elective application of CFC rules is not available, but the ATAD-aligned Estonian CFC rules apply automatically to foreign subsidiaries of Estonian OÜs in low-tax jurisdictions; (v) hybrid mismatch elections must align with Council Directive (EU) 2016/1164 and are mandatory filings under the e-MTA report, not optional elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable turnover (supplies of goods and services where the place of supply is Estonia); registration is mandatory from the first day of the following calendar month after the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration permitted for suppliers, distance sellers, and non-residents

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies on supplies dispatched or transported outside the EU provided the goods are exported within 3 months of supply and the supplier retains transport/export evidence; the export must be substantiated before filing the VAT return. Services: 0% VAT applies to B2B services where the customer is a taxable person in another EU member state or a non-resident business, applying the general place-of-supply rule that places the supply at the customer's establishment (reverse charge); for supplies to non-EU business customers, the place of supply is outside the EU and the supply is outside the Estonian VAT scope (Finland-style zero-rating available); for 3D animation/VFX services provided to foreign advertising or media companies, the 'service linked to immovable property, B2B, electronic-services' rules must be assessed; input VAT on costs is fully deductible against zero-rated supplies, and any excess credit is refunded via the e-MTA auto-refund within 30-60 days after the VAT return deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX1/SAD) accepted by Estonian or other EU customs office, showing exit from the EU
  • Transport documentation: Bill of Lading (B/L), CMR, or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical movement
  • Commercial invoice with the sales contract and Incoterms clause
  • Inward payment evidence and FX settlement records (SWIFT/Wise credit confirmation)
  • Foreign customer's VAT registration number (EU) or certificate of business presence (non-EU) for zero-rated services
  • Service delivery records: signed delivery acceptance, workstation usage logs, master files, and IP transfer documents

Estonia operates a Real-Time Economy (RTE) mandate: all electronic tax invoices (B2B and B2G) must be issued and receivable in structured XML format compliant with EU standard EN 16931; B2G e-invoices must be submitted to the e-MTA platform; invoice data is cleared through the e-MTA e-account, but there is no real-time fiscal stamping or physical digital stamp requirement; instead, the MTA uses pre-filled VAT return datasets, digital audit files, and API-based e-invoice exchange (e.g., PEPPOL network); monthly VAT returns and SIMM (statistical/intra-EU) reports are filed electronically via API or e-MTA portal; cash-register fiscalization is required for all physical sellers via certified fiscal software with mandatory remote data transmission to the MTA; for cross-border digital services, the OSS (One Stop Shop) must be used with quarterly electronic filing; penalties apply for non-compliant e-invoice formats, including rejection of input VAT deduction.

The MTA maintains specialized export audit desk reviews; if export zero-rating is claimed without full documentation, the VAT is retrospectively clawed back at the standard 22% rate plus default interest at 0.06% per calendar day; deliberate false zero-rating is prosecuted as tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code (§392) with fines up to EUR 3,200 for individuals and EUR 4,000 for legal entities, imprisonment up to 3 years, and retrospective reversal of input VAT deductions for up to 5 years; VAT Act §94(4) imposes a penalty of up to EUR 3,500 for each incorrect return; cross-border controls use the ECRIS and VIES databases; suspicious FX-routing through intermediary jurisdictions in excess of the 180-day repatriation benchmark triggers transfer-pricing adjustments and the MTA's anti-avoidance powers to reclassify the arrangement as hidden profit distribution, which immediately creates 20/80 corporate income tax liability on the deemed dividend.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Section 1 – Remuneration paid to individuals Gross salaries, bonuses, management board fees and other employment remuneration paid to Estonian resident employees, with boxes for individual income tax (20%), social tax (33%), unemployment insurance (employer 0.8%, employee 1.6%) and funded pension (2%). Remuneration for work physically performed in Estonia is reportable even when paid to a non-resident employee; the employer must withhold 20% individual income tax unless a DTT or EU social security coordination (A1/E101) provides otherwise. Late or missing TSD triggers MTA enforcement fines (up to EUR 600 per outstanding declaration) and coercive monetary penalties; unpaid distribution tax accrues penalty interest at 0.06% per calendar day (approx. 21.9% p.a.); intentional concealment of distributions constitutes criminal tax evasion under Penal Code section 389, punishable by fine or up to five years' imprisonment.
Section 2 – Fringe benefits Non-cash benefits (housing, company car, insurance, equipment for personal use) provided to employees and board members; taxable at 20% individual income tax plus 33% social tax calculated on a grossed-up value. Fringe benefits granted to non-resident directors or foreign contractors are reportable only where the benefit constitutes Estonian-source employment income; otherwise no local declaration applies, but the cost may be disallowed as a hidden distribution if lacking business purpose. Late or missing TSD triggers MTA enforcement fines (up to EUR 600 per outstanding declaration) and coercive monetary penalties; unpaid distribution tax accrues penalty interest at 0.06% per calendar day (approx. 21.9% p.a.); intentional concealment of distributions constitutes criminal tax evasion under Penal Code section 389, punishable by fine or up to five years' imprisonment.
Section 3 – Payments to non-residents subject to withholding tax Gross payments to foreign persons for royalties, interest and services (e.g., overseas 3D rendering subcontractors, licensed CGI software, animation libraries); boxes for statutory 20% withholding tax, DTT reduced-rate relief, and EU Interest and Royalties Directive (IRO) 0% exemptions. For cross-border animation services and IP licensing, the OÜ must withhold 20% unless the beneficial owner obtains DTT/IRO relief; the TSD requires entry of the treaty article, TRC reference and relief code; failure triggers reassessment plus penalty interest. Late or missing TSD triggers MTA enforcement fines (up to EUR 600 per outstanding declaration) and coercive monetary penalties; unpaid distribution tax accrues penalty interest at 0.06% per calendar day (approx. 21.9% p.a.); intentional concealment of distributions constitutes criminal tax evasion under Penal Code section 389, punishable by fine or up to five years' imprisonment.
Section 4 – Dividend distributions and distribution-based CIT Gross dividends declared and paid to shareholders; calculated corporate income tax at 20/80 of the net dividend, with the 14/86 reduced regime for regular distributions within the preceding three-year average; separate box for the 0% Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption. Estonia imposes no additional shareholder-level dividend withholding tax; the OÜ reports and pays the 20/80 or 14/86 tax in the TSD of the distribution month even if the sole shareholder is a foreign e-Residency investor; hidden profit distributions must be grossed-up and reported in the same section. Late or missing TSD triggers MTA enforcement fines (up to EUR 600 per outstanding declaration) and coercive monetary penalties; unpaid distribution tax accrues penalty interest at 0.06% per calendar day (approx. 21.9% p.a.); intentional concealment of distributions constitutes criminal tax evasion under Penal Code section 389, punishable by fine or up to five years' imprisonment.
