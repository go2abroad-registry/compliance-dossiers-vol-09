UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (RahaPTS), including §§ 9-10 on beneficial-owner identification and §§ 20-23 on customer due diligence, risk assessment, identification and verification; Commercial Register Act (Äriregistri seadus), provisions governing beneficial-owner data filed with the Commercial Register; International Sanctions Act (Rahvusvahelise sanktsiooni seadus); applicable European Union directly effective restrictive-measures regulations; Financial Intelligence Unit and Tax and Customs Board guidance. An Estonian OÜ must file beneficial-owner information with the Commercial Register. The beneficial owner is generally the natural person who ultimately owns or controls more than 25% of shares, voting rights or ownership, or exercises control by other means; where no such person can be identified after documented reasonable measures, the senior managing official is recorded under the statutory fallback rule.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is generally permissible for an Estonian OÜ if it is a genuine registered address at which official notices can be received and the company has a compliant contact arrangement when required, including a contact person where the management board is located outside Estonia. A virtual office does not replace the requirement for accurate registry information, accessible records, accounting capability, management availability or actual commercial substance. For a programmatic advertising exchange or SSP platform, the structure should maintain documented decision-making, contracts, accounting records, technical governance, data-protection controls, advertising-compliance procedures and payment controls. A virtual office alone is insufficient evidence of Estonian tax residence or effective management.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) – e-MTA portal (https://e-mta.emta.ee).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities creating an Osaühing (OÜ) are first entered in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri) via the e-Business Register (https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee). The issued registry code (8-digit) serves as the general tax identification number (TIN). VAT registration is a separate step: application is filed through the e-MTA portal. Non-established businesses without an Estonian seat must register as 'non-resident' and obtain an EE VAT number if they engage in taxable Estonian supplies, distance sales, or electronic/digital services. Upon application, the MTA issues VAT ID (format EE + registry code). The e-MTA portal supports digital ID (e-Residency card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities creating an Osaühing (OÜ) are first entered in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri) via the e-Business Register (https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee). The issued registry code (8-digit) serves as the general tax identification number (TIN). VAT registration is a separate step: application is filed through the e-MTA portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists for OÜ. The Estonian standard is the deferred corporate income tax system (retained profits are 0%, profits distributed are taxed at 20/80 of net amount, effectively 20%). No hybrid mismatch election is available outside ATAD-driven adjustments (Press Release No. 1). The OÜ may elect VAT group treatment with related Estonian entities (access to group VAT) and may elect annual VAT reporting if turnover is under the threshold. For non-resident suppliers, the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme is available for EU distance sales of goods and electronic/digital services, replacing separate VAT registrations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 (calendar-year taxable turnover threshold). Once exceeded, mandatory VAT registration in Estonia. Non-resident VAT liabilities arising from certain cross-border services (e.g., programmatic ad services to Estonian taxable persons under reverse charge) do not require registration solely for reverse-charged onward supplies; however, any own taxable supply to non-taxable persons in Estonia triggers registration at the first euro unless covered by OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% rate applies to: (i) export of goods physically shipped outside the EU, evidenced by customs export declarations; (ii) services supplied to a non-EU business customer (B2B) where the place of supply is outside the EU (e.g., advertising services for a US-based client); (iii) services relating to goods exported to outside the EU; (iv) B2C electronic/digital services supplied to persons in third countries (outside the EU) where the place of supply is outside the EU. For programmatic ad-exchange/SSP services, zero-rating is allowed for qualifying B2B supplies to non-EU taxable persons, subject to documentary proof of non-EU status and effective enjoyment outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document / e-customs export acceptance)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/air waybill) confirming export and showing consignor/consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank documentary evidence of payment for the exported service/goods
  • Written contract or SOW explicitly evidencing the supply
  • Proof of the recipient's non-EU VAT number or equivalent official registration (e.g., US EIN, UK VAT if applicable post-Brexit, UAE TRN)
  • IP address logs, service delivery logs, and server data demonstrating the advertising service is delivered outside the EU

Estonia does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for e-invoices. E-invoicing based on EN 16931 is the desired norm for B2B/G2B. VAT returns must be filed electronically via e-MTA (monthly or quarterly based on turnover). There is no API-based real-time clearance, but taxpayers must retain structured e-invoice data for audits.

If zero-rating is claimed erroneously or converted to export fraud, the MTA imposes repayment of under-declared VAT (22% of supply), plus late-payment interest (0.06% per day). Fraudulent zero-rating claims are punishable under the Penal Code – tax evasion of a large amount carries a fine up to EUR 800,000 for legal persons and imprisonment for up to 5 years for responsible individuals. Administrative fines up to 3× the evaded tax and prohibition from claiming refunds for up to three years can apply in aggravated cases. Retrospective audits may extend 5–7 years in suspected fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is part of the euro area (currency code EUR). Capital and current account transactions are fully convertible under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63–65, the EU Capital Movement Directive, and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Law framework. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on payment of dividends, branch profits, redemption of capital, or assignment of receivables. Estonian resident legal entities must, however, report statistical data to Eesti Pank for balance-of-payments purposes; dividends from an OÜ are subject to distribution taxation at 20% or 25%, but no foreign-exchange or remittance approval is required. Suspicious transactions require STR filing under the AML Act, but not FX permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation deadline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export, licence, or platform receipts in Estonia. EU regulations only require accurate bookkeeping, invoices, beneficial ownership records, and balance-of-payments/portfolio investment reporting to Eesti Pank where monthly transaction thresholds are exceeded. EUR and foreign-currency export proceeds may remain on resident bank accounts indefinitely; transactions with sanctioned jurisdictions are blocked.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) and Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority), acting as the integrated central bank, prudential supervisor and AML/CFT supervisor under the Credit Institutions Act, the Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act, and the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions as a secondary account, and Low for offshore/third-country EMIs. A PSD2-licensed EMI may passport into Estonia and provide multi-currency IBAN, SEPA CT/SDD, and corporate cards, but it cannot issue an Estonian credit institution licence, provide commercial documentary credits, or open a reimbursable settlement account at Eesti Pank. Under Section 77 and subsequent provisions of the Anti-Money Laundering Act, funds held by an operator must be safeguarded in a pooled account at an authorised credit institution, and the EMI cannot replace the bank relationship required for DSP/SSP platform settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Transfer via SWIFT MT103 for customer-originated capital injection or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover; SEPA CT is accepted for euro-denominated minor contributions. The payment must state the Estonian registry code of the OÜ as beneficiary and include reference to the shareholder's contribution agreement; for balance-of-payments classification, use the appropriate purpose code for 'equity capital/direct investment' when the ECB/Eesti Pank reporting is completed by the resident bank. Verification dossier: a capital contribution resolution, updated share capital register, bank's KYC/AML check on the remitter, sworn statement of source of funds, and the transaction must be registered in the e-Business Register within the statutory timelines. Non-resident loan advances are treated as debt liabilities and require an interest/repayment schedule to satisfy transfer-pricing rules under the Estonian Income Tax Act.SWIFT MT103 for international customer credit transfers, MT202 COV for correspondent clearing, and MT700/MT707 for commercial letters of credit and amendments.
