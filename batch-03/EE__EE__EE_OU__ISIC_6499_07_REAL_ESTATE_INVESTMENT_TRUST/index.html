Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Osaühing (Commercial Code § 140, minimum capital requirement abolished by the 2011 amendment). The initial share capital is fixed by the Articles of Association and may be as low as €0. Proof of payment must be lodged with the registry: a bank confirmation of deposit of the subscribed capital to a transit custody account opened in the name of the OÜ being formed, or a sworn auditor's valuation report for non-monetary contributions (Commercial Code § 143(4)). No escrow agent is legally mandated; the bank deposit confirmation constitutes the statutory proof of capital formation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: obtain a rescue-standards inspection certificate and approved operational fire safety documentation (fire safety review and evacuation plan) from the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) under the Rescue Act; structural clearance: building permit or construction permit decisions through the local building authority and entry in the Building Register (Ehitisregister) for any structural alterations or load-bearing modifications; occupational clearance: register workplaces with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon), implement risk assessments, and issue workplace health and safety clearance certificates for all occupied common areas of the property portfolio in compliance with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Osaühing (Commercial Code § 140, minimum capital requirement abolished by the 2011 amendment). The initial share capital is fixed by the Articles of Association and may be as low as €0. Proof of payment must be lodged with the registry: a bank confirmation of deposit of the subscribed capital to a transit custody account opened in the name of the OÜ being formed, or a sworn auditor's valuation report for non-monetary contributions (Commercial Code § 143(4)). No escrow agent is legally mandated; the bank deposit confirmation constitutes the statutory proof of capital formation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, 'MTA'); central e-filing portal e-MTA (https://emta.ee); commercial registry codification via the Estonian Business Register (Äriregister, https://ariregister.rik.ee).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign investor establishing an OÜ: (1) file incorporation in the Äriregister (electronically via notary or e-Residency digital ID) to obtain an 8-digit registry code, which functions as the entity's statutory TIN; (2) foreign legal persons performing Estonian activity without a local subsidiary may instead register a branch under the Commercial Code, also obtaining a registry code; (3) the registry code is automatically reported to MTA; no separate general tax-identification application is required for corporate taxpayers; (4) for VAT liability, submit an electronic VAT-registration application in e-MTA once the EUR 40,000 annual taxable-supply threshold is exceeded, or before the first taxable supply where the operator is non-resident; MTA issues a distinct EE-prefixed VAT number; (5) a real-estate pooled vehicle that qualifies as an alternative investment fund must separately obtain an activity/fund licence from the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon); no fiscal representative is mandatory for foreign VAT registrants except where MTA requires a security on a case-by-case basis.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - The OÜ is a resident corporate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act; undistributed profits are taxable at 0%, so rental income retained in the REIT bears no corporate income tax until distribution; distributed profits are effectively taxed at entity level at 20% (20/80 gross-up), reduced to 14% (14/86) for regular dividend payments; non-resident shareholders are subject to the same distribution-based withholding of 20% unless treaty relief applies. Territorial scope: Estonian resident entities are taxed on a distribution basis over worldwide profits only when distributed; non-resident persons are taxed on Estonia-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The OÜ is a resident corporate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act; undistributed profits are taxable at 0%, so rental income retained in the REIT bears no corporate income tax until distribution; distributed profits are effectively taxed at entity level at 20% (20/80 gross-up), reduced to 14% (14/86) for regular dividend payments; non-resident shareholders are subject to the same distribution-based withholding of 20% unless treaty relief applies. Territorial scope: Estonian resident entities are taxed on a distribution basis over worldwide profits only when distributed; non-resident persons are taxed on Estonia-source income. No separate statutory REIT exemption exists in Estonia; an OÜ holding income-generating real estate is treated under the standard distribution-tax model, while a contractual AIF/REIT-like fund would be regulated under the Investment Funds Act.

Available election forms

(1) Regular-dividend reduced-rate election: an OÜ with a qualifying distribution history may apply the 14/86 rate to regular dividend distributions (Income Tax Act § 50); (2) VAT option-to-tax for immovable property: a taxable person may elect to apply VAT to otherwise exempt letting or transfer of commercial property (VAT Act § 16), thereby allowing full input-VAT recovery; the election is declared to MTA and is binding; (3) no check-the-box or transparent-entity election exists in Estonian law; ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch rules deny double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; (4) treaty-based reduction/exemption of the 20% dividend withholding tax may be activated by the recipient shareholder's residence certificate and treaty claim.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover in a calendar year for an Estonian-resident OÜ (VAT Act § 3(1), threshold applicable since 1 May 2022); EUR 10,000 threshold for intra-Community distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services subject to One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election; non-established foreign suppliers have no turnover threshold and become registrable from their first taxable supply in Estonia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% rate is available only where: (i) goods are physically dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU (VAT Act § 27) and the export is duly evidenced; (ii) goods are supplied intra-Community to another EU taxable person holding a valid VAT ID (VAT Act § 25), subject to EC Sales List submission; or (iii) services are supplied B2B with the place of supply in another jurisdiction, triggering reverse charge there. For a real-estate investment trust, rental and property-management services relating to Estonian immovable property are deemed located in Estonia and cannot benefit from zero-rating; letting of land and buildings is VAT-exempt under VAT Act § 16(2), unless the taxable person exercises the option to tax, in which case rent becomes taxable at the standard VAT rate with full input-VAT recovery.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic single administrative document/SAD) bearing a unique MRN issued by the customs office of export
  • Transport evidence confirming physical exit from Estonia/EU - Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note with exit-office stamps/trackers
  • Commercial invoice, underlying sale contract, and bank/foreign-remittance confirmation (inward remittance certificate) evidencing the foreign buyer and payment flow
  • For intra-Community supplies: validated customer VAT-number printout, EC Sales List filing confirmation (KMD-INF) and delivery/acceptance documentation

Estonia does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API stamping or digital-visa system for invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory only for invoices addressed to Estonian public-sector bodies, using PEPPOL/EN 16931-compliant structured formats; for B2B transactions, paper and PDF invoices remain lawful provided all 19 statutory VAT particulars are included. Retail, hospitality and catering businesses must use certified online cash registers that transmit fiscal records; immovable-property lettings carried out by a REIT fall outside this cash-register mandate. VAT returns (KMD), annexes (KMD-INF) and EC sales lists must be filed electronically in e-MTA on a monthly or quarterly cycle.

Estonian law does not codify a strict 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline; however, MTA audit practice applies a six-month prudential benchmark, and unexplained non-receipt or non-repatriation of export consideration within 180 days shifts the evidentiary burden to the taxpayer and supports denial of the 0% rate. The MTA may assess VAT at the standard rate together with late-payment interest of 0.06% per calendar day and an additional penalty of up to 25% of the assessed tax under the Tax Administration Act; the general limitation period is 3 years, extended to 6 years for deliberate concealment or tax fraud. Criminal tax fraud under Penal Code § 397 is punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment, or up to 5 years where committed on a large scale or by an organised group.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full member of the Eurozone (since 2011) and the EU single market. The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on profit repatriation, and no requirement for central bank approval for cross-border transfers. Dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be freely remitted, subject only to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and tax reporting obligations. The REIT structure must comply with the Estonian Investment Funds Act and the Commercial Code, but no FX conversion hurdles exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Estonia imposes no surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. All foreign currency proceeds may be retained in any currency or converted freely. The euro is the sole legal tender, but foreign currency accounts are permitted. No statutory timeline for repatriation exists; however, for tax purposes, transfer pricing and thin capitalization rules apply to cross-border related-party transactions.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) – the central bank and member of the Eurosystem; foreign exchange administration functions are shared with the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) and the Ministry of Finance for sanctions and capital movement oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Estonia is a pioneer in e-Residency and digital banking, but the REIT is a regulated entity (requires a license from Finantsinspektsioon under the Estonian Investment Funds Act). Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, or other EU-licensed EMI/PSP) can be used for ancillary payment processing, but they are not suitable as primary treasury accounts for a licensed REIT due to regulatory expectations for a local correspondent banking relationship and the need for a licensed credit institution to hold client funds. The REIT must maintain its main operational account with a licensed Estonian or EU credit institution. EMIs may be used for disbursements but not for capital escrow or dividend distributions to investors without additional KYC/AML scrutiny.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections (share capital, investor subscriptions, or loans) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA (for EUR transfers within the EEA). The payment must include a clear purpose code (e.g., 'Capital subscription – REIT units' or 'Shareholder loan'). For non-EUR currencies, use SWIFT with appropriate currency codes. The bank will require a copy of the subscription agreement and the Finantsinspektsioon license. For loans, a loan agreement and transfer pricing documentation must be in place. No FX conversion is required if the transfer is in EUR; if in foreign currency, the bank will convert at market rate. The bank must verify the source of funds and the UBO of the sending entity.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For real estate acquisitions and operational expenses, use SWIFT MT103 for international wires, SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within the EEA, and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for cross-border construction or large vendor contracts. For dividend distributions to investors, use SWIFT MT103 or SEPA, with the purpose code 'Dividend distribution'. The bank will require a board resolution and tax clearance (if applicable). For ACH (target payment rail), Estonia uses SEPA Direct Debit and SEPA Credit Transfer; ACH is not a local term but SEPA serves the same function. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, or other EU-licensed EMI/PSP) can be used for ancillary payment processing, but they are not suitable as primary treasury accounts for a licensed REIT due to regulatory expectations for a local correspondent banking relationship and the need for a licensed credit institution to hold client funds. The REIT must maintain its main operational account with a licensed Estonian or EU credit institution. EMIs may be used for disbursements but not for capital escrow or dividend distributions to investors without additional KYC/AML scrutiny.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on gross profit distributions (20/80 of net distribution); reduced rate of 14% on qualifying regular dividends

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% as a classical shareholder-level withholding tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonian domestic law does not codify a specific monetary threshold mandating Local File or Master File. In practice, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board may request transfer pricing documentation for any material controlled transaction; a commonly accepted compliance trigger is EUR 250,000 of annual controlled transaction value per category, and comprehensive Local File documentation is ordinarily prepared for related-party revenues above EUR 1,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory for consolidated groups with annual revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the OECD substance-over-form doctrine is embedded in the Estonian General Anti-Abuse Rule (Income Tax Act section 84) and the 2015 BEPS principal purpose test. For an OÜ structured as a real estate investment trust, the e-MTA and Estonian courts will disregard artificial arrangements that lack genuine economic activity, local management control, physical presence, real estate management functions, and adequate capitalisation. Without such substance, treaty privileges and the deferral of distribution tax are denied, and the income is re-attributed to the true beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Administratively, the tax authority imposes a fine up to 100% of the evaded tax plus default interest at 0.06% per calendar day. For intentional tax evasion, the Estonian Penal Code section 385 provides a monetary penalty of up to EUR 300,000 and imprisonment of up to five years when the evaded amount is significant. The company may also be denied liquidation and tax settlement advantages.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion become personally and severally liable for the company's tax arrears under section 15 of the Tax Information Act; travel bans and asset seizure orders may be imposed; conviction leads to disqualification from management board positions for up to three years and a criminal record.
Shell Company and Artificial Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: The e-MTA may recharacterise the company as a sham under the Estonian GAAR, disregard the juridical entity, impute all rental income and capital gains directly to the beneficial owner, assess distribution tax with a 30% penalty and daily default interest, and deny DTA benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to repay any wrongful distributions under the Commercial Code, and if the company becomes insolvent, they may face civil restitution claims from creditors and the bankruptcy trustee. Criminal liability for fraud under Penal Code section 392 may result in imprisonment of up to three years and a ban on acting as a director of any Estonian legal person for up to five years.
Profit Repatriation Abuse and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The distribution is recharacterised as taxable profit distribution under Income Tax Act section 50; the e-MTA assesses 20% distribution tax on the full amount, adds a late-payment penalty of 2%, and applies a fine of up to EUR 10,000 if false declarations were made. Reciprocal DTA relief is revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise distributions when the company's net assets are below the legal capital are personally liable for the shortfall to the company under section 203 of the Estonian Commercial Code. If criminal intent is established, imprisonment of up to four years and severe director disqualification are possible.
AML and Sanctions Violations in Real Estate Transactions

Statutory sanction: The Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act provides administrative fines of up to EUR 400,000 for individuals and up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of total turnover for legal persons. Real estate assets of the trust may be frozen, and the OÜ may be forcibly wound up. Money laundering under Penal Code section 394 is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and confiscation of the subject property.

Director personal liability: Directors and money-laundering reporting officers face personal fines of up to EUR 10,000 per infringement. In serious cases, criminal proceedings lead to imprisonment, travel bans, revocation of e-Residency digital ID privileges for non-resident directors, and disqualification from holding compliance or management positions in Estonian financial and real estate sectors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: The e-MTA applies the arm's length principle and makes a transfer pricing adjustment. The adjusted excess amount is treated as a deemed dividend distribution subject to 20% distribution tax, plus default interest of 0.06% per day and an administrative fine of 30% of the additional tax due. The payer may also lose deduction of the disallowed expense for trade income purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute fabricated transfer pricing agreements are personally jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax underpayment. In cross-border insolvency scenarios, the bankruptcy trustee may claw back amounts under the Estonian Bankruptcy Act, and the Prosecutors may institute criminal proceedings for false certification and tax evasion with imprisonment up to five years for grossly disproportionate transactions.
