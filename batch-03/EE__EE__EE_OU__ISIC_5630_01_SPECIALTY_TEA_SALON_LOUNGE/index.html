Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an OÜ. Founders determine the share capital and contribution terms in the articles and formation documents. Contributions may be deferred if the documents so provide; unpaid contributions remain payable, and statutory restrictions apply to distributions while contributions remain unpaid. No incorporation escrow account is generally required. A bank or payment institution may require evidence of capital payment for its own onboarding procedures.

Funding & unlock path: Before opening, ensure the premises have the required building-use documentation and are suitable for the intended public and food-service use. Obtain any required local building or use permits and required technical inspections. Comply with occupational health and safety duties, including workplace risk assessment, under the Occupational Health and Safety Act; no universal separate workplace-clearance certificate applies to every tea lounge.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA); electronic tax services are provided through the e-MTA portal. The Estonian Business Register assigns an OÜ its registry code, which is also used as its domestic tax identification number.

E-government filing portal

For an Estonian OÜ, complete incorporation in the Estonian Business Register and obtain its registry code; no separate general corporate income-tax registration is ordinarily required. Apply to EMTA for VAT registration through e-MTA or the prescribed application, supplying the entity's registry details and information or evidence supporting its taxable business activity. EMTA generally decides a VAT-registration application within five working days. A foreign taxable person making supplies for which Estonia requires registration must apply to EMTA; a non-EU/EEA applicant may be required to appoint an Estonian tax representative, subject to applicable mutual-assistance arrangements and statutory exceptions. Obtain an Estonian VAT identification number before making transactions for which registration is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For an Estonian OÜ, complete incorporation in the Estonian Business Register and obtain its registry code; no separate general corporate income-tax registration is ordinarily required. Apply to EMTA for VAT registration through e-MTA or the prescribed application, supplying the entity's registry details and information or evidence supporting its taxable business activity. EMTA generally decides a VAT-registration application within five working days. A foreign taxable person making supplies for which Estonia requires registration must apply to EMTA; a non-EU/EEA applicant may be required to appoint an Estonian tax representative, subject to applicable mutual-assistance arrangements and statutory exceptions. Obtain an Estonian VAT identification number before making transactions for which registration is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An OÜ is treated as a taxable legal person; Estonia has no US-style check-the-box election to classify an OÜ as a pass-through. Corporate income tax is generally imposed when profits are distributed or otherwise subject to a statutory deemed-distribution rule, rather than on retained accounting profits. No general hybrid-mismatch classification election changes the OÜ's domestic entity status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply with the place of supply in Estonia in a calendar year; the threshold is generally measured from the beginning of the calendar year. Separate rules can require registration without reliance on this threshold, including specified cross-border transactions and supplies by non-resident taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU may qualify for 0% VAT where the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier retains evidence that the goods left the EU. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% subject to the recipient's valid VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required reporting. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore: the place-of-supply rules determine whether the service is outside Estonian VAT, while specific statutory exemptions or zero rates may apply in defined cases. A tea lounge's domestic food, beverage, and hospitality supplies are generally taxable at the applicable Estonian rate unless a specific statutory rule applies.

  • Export customs declaration and evidence of exit from the EU customs territory, including the relevant customs confirmation or electronic export result
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier confirmation
  • Customer order, contract, commercial invoice, and proof linking the exported goods to the supplier's sale
  • For qualifying intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative reporting
  • Payment records and, where relevant to substantiation, bank evidence of receipt; Estonia does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a condition of export zero-rating

Estonia does not generally require real-time VAT invoice clearance, advance fiscal authorization, or digital stamping for ordinary domestic business-to-business invoices. Structured e-invoicing is required for invoices to public-sector entities under applicable public-procurement rules. For private-sector transactions, electronic invoicing is generally subject to the recipient's agreement or applicable statutory entitlement to request e-invoices. VAT invoices and accounting records must satisfy statutory content and retention requirements; VAT returns are filed electronically through e-MTA.

The supplier must substantiate the statutory conditions for the 0% rate and retain supporting records. If the conditions or evidence are insufficient, EMTA may assess VAT at the applicable rate, together with statutory interest and applicable procedural penalties. Intentional tax fraud or document falsification may trigger criminal liability under Estonian law. A VAT refund may be denied, adjusted, or recovered where the underlying entitlement is not established or is later found invalid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has full current-account and capital-account convertibility as an EU and euro-area member state. There are no exchange control laws, no prior FX permits, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the conversion or repatriation of profits by resident or non-resident shareholders. Dividends may be freely remitted subject to the Estonian distributed-profits tax regime: a regular distribution is subject to 20% corporate income tax at the level of the OÜ, reduced to 14% for regular distributions if specified conditions are met. This tax is separate from FX convertibility and does not require Bank of Estonia approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio in Estonia. Export and service receipts may be retained in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency indefinitely. For statistical balance-of-payments reporting, Eesti Pank or the commercial bank may require transaction-purpose coding for cross-border transfers above certain de minimis thresholds, but there is no legal obligation to surrender, convert, or time-limit export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem; prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment institutions is delegated to Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority). No separate foreign exchange administration authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions passported into Estonia; Medium for non-EEA offshore digital EMIs. Legal constraints: non-EEA EMIs cannot rely on an Estonian banking licence and may not provide an Estonian IBAN settlement account unless they operate through a licensed Estonian credit institution or hold a passported e-money licence under EU Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2. For a specialty tea salon lounge, an EU-licensed EMI is viable as a supplementary cross-border payment and FX conversion rail, but the primary treasury and merchant settlement account should remain at a supervised Estonian credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a lawful shareholder capital injection into the Estonian OÜ, route funds by SWIFT MT103 if the remitter is outside SEPA, or by SEPA Credit Transfer if the remitter is within the EU/EEA. The beneficiary field must show the OÜ's Estonian IBAN (EE38... format) and bank BIC. The remittance information should clearly state 'share capital contribution' or 'equity investment' and should refer to the share register entry. Estonia imposes no exchange-control approval, but the receiving bank will require source-of-funds documentation for amounts above EUR 10,000, and for foreign direct investment statistics the bank will complete Eesti Pank balance-of-payments coding, typically under the 'direct investment – equity capital' category. The bank may require a shareholders' resolution, an updated share register, and a notarised certificate of good standing if the remitter is a corporate entity. No capital escrow account is mandatory under the Estonian OÜ legal framework, but if the articles require share capital over EUR 2,500, the contribution must be evidenced via the bank statement before registration with the Commercial Register.For trade settlements from the OÜ, use SEPA Credit Transfer for all euro-denominated payments within SEPA, and SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-euro payments. For high-value tea or ceramic procurement, a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700/MT707) opened by an Estonian bank is the standard clearing rail. Outbound USD, GBP, SEK, or other currency payments require a pre-negotiated FX conversion through the bank's FX desk. The bank will require an underlying invoice, purchase order, or contract and a transaction-purpose code (trade payment / goods purchase). Any profit distribution or shareholder loan repayment must be separately identified as 'dividends' or 'loan repayment' and must be processed through the local statutory withholding and distributed-profits tax reporting framework.Deliverable spot and outright forward FX contracts in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/SEK, and other freely convertible currencies with an EEA-licensed credit institution.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps) for medium-term treasury exposures, provided the OÜ has documented underlying commercial exposure and the counterparty is EU/EEA supervised.European-style FX options (purchased options) for hedging procurement costs and payment volatility, executed under ISDA documentation with a licensed bank.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) strictly limited to currencies that cannot be delivered onshore (e.g., certain Asian or Latin American non-convertible currencies) and only with a regulated EU/EEA investment firm or bank under an ISDA/CSA arrangement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained/undistributed profits; 20% of gross distribution (20/80 on net, i.e. 25% effective) on distributed profits; reduced 14% of gross (14/86 on net, ca. 16.28%) for qualifying regular distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

No separate classical dividend withholding tax; the distributing Estonian OÜ bears CIT at 20/80 of the net distribution (14/86 on regular qualifying distributions), which operates as the final source tax on repatriation

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (aggregate annual cross-border controlled transactions) for Local File; EUR 250,000 for financial transactions; Master File required at group consolidated revenue of EUR 50,000,000 or more; Country-by-Country Report at consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation analogous to offshore hubs, but OECD/BEPS substance concepts apply through tax residence (place of effective management under TüS Section 6), GAAR and the MLI principal-purpose test. An OÜ without real management, employees, premises or operational activity in Estonia faces DTA benefit denial, foreign CFC attribution, MTA reclassification, and de-risking by banks and payment processors

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Cash Suppression

Statutory sanction: Back assessment of CIT at 20/80, VAT, social tax (33%) and late-payment interest; administrative fine up to EUR 1,600; criminal liability under Penal Code Section 152-1 for tax fraud with company fines up to EUR 200,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years

Director personal liability: Director faces personal criminal prosecution, disqualification from management, travel-ban risk during examination, and civil liability for the full amount of tax evasion alongside joint and several restitution with the company
Shell Company / E-Residency Abuse

Statutory sanction: MTA reassessment of undeclared distributions, denial of treaty benefits, forced deregistration of the e-resident OÜ, inclusion in cross-border exchange of information (FATCA/CRS), and cancellation of the commercial register entry on public-interest grounds

Director personal liability: Directors who control the sham structure are personally exposed to civil recovery, cross-border tax audits in the shareholder's country of residence, covered-person liability for intentional misrepresentation, and criminal sanctions for forgery of substance documentation
AML / CTF Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act up to EUR 400,000 for legal persons, public naming by the Financial Intelligence Unit, and suspension or revocation of the activity licence for the tea salon

Director personal liability: Directors are personally fined up to EUR 160,000, may be disqualified from board positions, and may face imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional money laundering predicate offences
Disguised Dividend / Fringe Benefit Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the expense as a taxable distribution with CIT at 20/80, recalculated social tax and unemployment insurance, 33% social security surcharge, plus daily late-payment interest and penalties

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the restored tax debt; fraudulent concealment elevates the case to Penal Code prosecution with imprisonment risk and prohibition on acting as a director
Nominee Directorship / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 400,000 under AML rules, forced amendment of the Beneficial Ownership Register, prohibition of dividend distribution pending registry correction, and cancellation of financial institution relationships

Director personal liability: Nominee and hidden principals face joint criminal liability for false declarations, personal restitution for any tax or third-party losses, travel restriction during investigation, and imprisonment for falsification of corporate documents
