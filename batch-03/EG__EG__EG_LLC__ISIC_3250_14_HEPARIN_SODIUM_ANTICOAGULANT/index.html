Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, unified taxpayer registry and e-services portal at www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must file the unified ETA registration application with notarized incorporation documents, board resolution, POA, and legal representative identification to obtain a consolidated 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) covering income tax, VAT, payroll, and stamp duty; ETA e-service account activation with credential approval is mandatory before electronic invoicing enablement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Egyptian LLC is a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax (Law 91/2005), VAT (Law 67/2016), and stamp tax; no pass-through or territorial exemption is available by default. — Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must file the unified ETA registration application with notarized incorporation documents, board resolution, POA, and legal representative identification to obtain a consolidated 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) covering income tax, VAT, payroll, and stamp duty; ETA e-service account activation with credential approval is mandatory before electronic invoicing enablement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Egyptian LLC is a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax (Law 91/2005), VAT (Law 67/2016), and stamp tax; no pass-through or territorial exemption is available by default.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Egyptian law; eligible small and micro taxpayers may elect presumptive income tax application, and qualifying businesses may request ETA approval for cash-basis VAT accounting; transfer pricing, thin-capitalization, and withholding rules are mandatory rather than elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies for mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration permitted below threshold under Law 67/2016 Executive Regulations

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (including fractionated heparin sodium API) are zero-rated under VAT Law when ownership transfers to a non-resident purchaser and the goods physically leave Egypt under customs export procedures; related transportation, handling, and processing of imported crude for re-export may also qualify when supported by complete export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (statutory exit certificate)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Foreign Purchase Order or export contract
  • Inward Foreign-Currency Remittance Certificate evidencing FX repatriation to Egypt

ETA e-invoicing is mandatory for phased-in categories of taxpayers; e-invoices must be submitted in real time to the ETA platform through certified ERP/API integration, validated and QR-stamped before issuance to the buyer, and e-receipts apply to B2C transactions; rejected or unstamped invoices are deemed legally non-issued for VAT deduction purposes.

ETA audits export zero-rating claims with strict documentary review; failure to substantiate export within statutory deadlines results in assessment at 14% VAT with late-payment surcharge, and false, forged, or duplicated export documentation triggers criminal fines and imprisonment, customs penalties, retrospective clawback of input VAT credits, interest, and possible AML sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is not freely convertible under a full capital-account liberalisation model. Current-account transactions, including import/export payments and ordinary trade settlements, are generally permitted through CBE-licensed banks at the official market rate, subject to documentary verification. Capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment subscriptions, shareholder loans, debt securities, acquisitions and profit repatriation routed through capital accounts) require CBE and/or General Authority for Investment (GAFI) approval and a licensed bank's exchange-control screening. Profit remittance from an Egyptian LLC is treated as a current-account transfer provided there are current audited annual accounts, a valid commercial register and tax card, evidence of payment of corporate income tax and withholding tax, CBE Form 14/declaration, proof of source of the dividends or realised profits, and no outstanding tax clearance certificate. In practice, divergent official and parallel exchange rates and periodic USD liquidity squeezes can delay settlement; therefore the treasury plan must maintain margin buffers and align remittance schedules with CBE allotment windows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations issued pursuant to the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Foreign Exchange Law No. 38 of 1994 (as amended), the full invoice value of exports from Egypt must be repatriated through a CBE-licensed bank and credited to an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the date of shipment (Bill of Lading date). No mandatory fixed percentage conversion into EGP applies under the post-2016 liberalised regime; the exporter may either maintain the proceeds in a foreign-currency account or sell the foreign currency to an authorised bank at the prevailing official exchange rate. The bank is required to obtain the underlying export documents (commercial invoice, Bill of Lading, customs export declaration and import/export card) and must report any unrepatriated export proceeds to the CBE. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window renders the company liable to penalties and administrative suspension of export and import facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) acting through the Foreign Exchange Licensing Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI licences (e.g. Lithuania, UK FCA, Singapore) are not recognised by the CBE as principal bank licences and cannot be used for statutory Egyptian corporate treasury, tax payments, capital subscription or trade settlement. Such EMIs may only be used as auxiliary receivable-collection tools where the funds are ultimately routed into a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account. A Company incorporated in Egypt is legally obliged to maintain its operating bank account in Egypt with a licensed institution under the CBE banking and AML/CFT framework; reliance on offshore EMIs would create withholding tax, exchange-control and bank-reconciliation non-compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment equity injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the foreign shareholder's own bank account to the Egyptian LLC's capital account held at a CBE-licensed bank. The initiating bank must reference the underlying purpose as 'Direct Investment – Capital Subscription' in the payment message, and the receiving bank must complete the CBE exchange-control declaration and match the amount against GAFI's authorised capital share structure. The company must provide a notarised capital subscription deed, a CBE Inward Transfer Form or bank-specific purpose-of-payment certificate, the GAFI certificate, and a certified board/partner resolution approving the call of capital. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions in the tax year exceed EGP 20,000,000; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 200,000,000; simplified documentation is acceptable for controlled transactions below EGP 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) in Article 35 of the Income Tax Law, coupled with treaty Limitation on Benefits clauses. Although Egypt does not issue separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like OFC regimes, the ETA can re-characterize arrangements lacking commercial substance, disallow treaty benefits, and attribute profits to the Egyptian economic employer. For pharma extraction activities, physical plant, qualified staff, procurement decisions and risk-bearing functions must be demonstrably located in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full evaded tax plus a penalty up to 100% of evaded tax, administrative fine between EGP 100,000 and EGP 500,000, charge over corporate assets, and criminal prosecution under Article 133 of Law 91/2005.

Director personal liability: Directors, CFO and tax counsel may be jointly and severally liable for the full tax shortfall and fines; senior officers face travel bans, seizure of personal assets, criminal imprisonment for up to 3 years, and disqualification from holding board positions in Egypt.
Shell Company / Artificial Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, reclassification of the payment as taxable dividend (10% WHT) or royalty/interest (20% WHT) plus default interest, and penalty equal to 20% of under-withheld tax; criminal fine up to EGP 200,000 for filing false declarations.

Director personal liability: The directors of the parent and the Egyptian LLC are jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax; personal penalties of up to EGP 100,000, travel ban, and criminal liability for false attestation before the Egyptian Tax Authority.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable profit under the arm's length principle, additional tax of up to 30% of the adjustment, an automatic monetary penalty of 10% of the resulting additional tax due (minimum EGP 100,000), and interest accrual.

Director personal liability: Senior management faces civil liability for the amounts adjusted, could be personally surcharged for negligent failure to supply records upon ETA request, and, in repeated violations, may be barred from approving cross-border transactions in Egypt.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 80/2002, for Money Laundering: seizure and confiscation of the proceeds of crime, fine from EGP 50,000 to EGP 5,000,000, and imprisonment from 3 to 7 years; banks are required to freeze accounts and report suspicious transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable if money laundering is facilitated by the company; personal asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment are ordered; professional banking licenses may be revoked and business permits for pharmaceutical export cancelled.
Customs / VAT Evasion on Cross-Border Shipments

Statutory sanction: Customs fine up to 100% of the duty evaded, release price adjustment; VAT penalty under Law 67/2016 of 25%-100% of the tax, plus monthly default interest; customs bond cancellation and administrative suspension of import/export codes.

Director personal liability: Company officers who knowingly sign false customs declarations are subject to penalties of up to EGP 100,000, imprisonment, and restriction from travelling abroad; the business license of the chemical fractionation entity may be revoked by the Ministry of Health and ETA.
