Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Procedure for a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: complete company incorporation with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI); obtain commercial register and tax card from the local tax office; submit VAT registration application under Law No. 67 of 2016 and corporate tax registration under Law No. 91 of 2005 through the Egyptian Tax Authority; appoint a resident tax agent where required; receive the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN), VAT certificate, and e-invoicing credentials. No minimum capital requirement applies and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted for LLCs.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Procedure for a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: complete company incorporation with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI); obtain commercial register and tax card from the local tax office; submit VAT registration application under Law No. 67 of 2016 and corporate tax registration under Law No. 91 of 2005 through the Egyptian Tax Authority; appoint a resident tax agent where required; receive the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN), VAT certificate, and e-invoicing credentials. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no general check-the-box election. An LLC is automatically treated as a resident corporation for Egyptian income tax purposes. Available elections include: consolidated tax return filing for wholly-owned corporate groups under Article 56 of Law 91 of 2005; optional participation in the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt pilot phase before mandatory rollout; and tax treaty based relief elections. No hybrid mismatch election is available in the domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for compulsory VAT registration of resident suppliers; foreign digital and e-commerce suppliers selling into Egypt become registrable upon their first taxable supply to Egyptian consumers, with no general turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of tangible goods, including children's wooden blocks and educational toys, qualifies for 0% VAT where the goods physically leave Egyptian customs territory, are sold to a non-resident customer, and the foreign currency sales consideration is credited to an Egyptian bank account. Offshore services and related logistics may also be zero-rated if supplied for foreign consumers and supported by export invoices. The exporter must retain sufficient proof to substantiate the foreign nature of the transaction, and paid input VAT on exported goods may be claimed as a refund through ETA after clearance.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

All B2B invoices and B2C receipts issued by registered businesses must be reported to the Egyptian Tax Authority in real time through the integrated e-invoicing and e-receipt platform. Invoices are digitally signed, encrypted, stamped with a QR code and validated by ETA fiscal clearance before becoming legally effective for tax purposes. Mandatory integration via ERP-API connection or ETA web portal applies; non-compliant invoices are rejected or treated as unissued as a 25% sanction in specific cases.

The Egyptian Tax Authority performs targeted audits of export zero-rating claims. Where the Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading or Inward Remittance Certificate reveals a false export, INR misprocurement, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the mandatory 180-day deadline, the zero-rated status is revoked, VAT is clawed back at 15%, late payment penalties accrue at 2% per month or fraction thereof, monetary fines may reach triple the tax due, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Law No. 67 of 2016 can result in imprisonment. Additionally, GAFI and customs authorities may suspend export permits and deny future zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a de facto managed flexible exchange-rate regime (Liberálised exchange rate under CBE Circular 2024-03) with full current-account convertibility for trade, services, and profit remittances that satisfy documentary due-diligence requirements. The Egyptian pound (EGP) is not freely convertible outside the formal banking system for capital-account items; capital outflows, foreign-direct-investment exits, and dividend repatriation above defined thresholds require documentation of source, audited financial statements, and CBE-approved bank attestation that applicable Egyptian taxes and levies have been settled. Profit remittance is legal through authorized banks, but practical hurdles include foreign-currency clearing queues, residual bank-level balance-sheet capacity constraints, and the requirement to substantiate that retained earnings have been fully taxed and are not impaired by undistributed statutory reserves.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE's unified export proceeds repatriation regime, an exporter must receive the full invoice value in convertible currency through an authorized domestic bank within 120 calendar days from the bill-of-lading date (or earlier if the underlying export contract specifies a shorter period). Statutory surrender is not fixed by law; rather, CBE imposes an operational foreign-exchange allocation condition under which, during periods of market imbalance, banks must require the export-receiving corporate to convert 50 percent of the repatriated proceeds to EGP at the prevailing interbank market rate, with the remaining 50 percent available for retention in a foreign-currency account or for financing permitted imports. With the 2025 flexible-exchange-rate reforms, this 50-percent conversion mechanism is currently applied selectively only when the CBE declares a temporary standing facility; otherwise, 100 percent of export cash proceeds may be held in foreign currency, provided the exporter maintains a compliant export screening dossier (sales invoice, customs export declaration, and CBE repatriation certificate).

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), Exchange Control Directorate, Banking and Monetary Recovery and Treasury Operations Department; the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU) is the competent AML/CFT supervisory unit for banking institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and fintech payment settlement providers may not obtain direct CBE clearing or settlement membership without a duly licensed Egyptian payment-services branch or a binding technical sponsorship agreement with a licensed local payment aggregator. Amazon Pay and Stripe can only function as downstream receivable collection rails, with final settlement routed into a local partner bank account through a licensed payment processor or local beneficiary bank, not as standalone banking counterparties for liquidity management. Any EMI holding client funds for the Egyptian LLC must be restricted to an auxiliary receivable-clearing function, never as a treasury vault.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The corporate income tax return and the final tax payment are due within four months following the end of the fiscal year. For a 31 December year-end the statutory deadline is 30 April. Advance corporate income tax payments are due on a quarterly basis based on the prior year's tax liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 20 million annual gross turnover with cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EGP 2 million; contemporaneous Local File/Master File documentation must be prepared and submitted to the ETA within 30 days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. The ETA applies the substance-over-form doctrine, beneficial ownership conditions, and an anti-abuse provision under Law 91/2005. A passive Egyptian LLC that distributes dividends or royalties without physical offices, employees, or substantive decision-making functions may be disregarded for treaty purposes and its payments recharacterized as Egyptian-source taxable income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Financial Arrangements

Statutory sanction: The ETA levies a fine of 100%–150% of the evaded amount, adds a monthly late-payment charge up to 25%, and refers the matter to the public prosecutor. Conviction carries imprisonment for up to five years, debarment from public procurement, and revocation of the commercial license.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent declarations are criminally prosecuted, face a personal travel ban for tax debts above the statutory threshold, and are exposed to civil restitution through piercing-the-veil claims if corporate profits were diverted to shareholders.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of EGP 100,000–EGP 500,000 for missing documentation, plus discretionary arm's-length price adjustment with an additional 20% tax and default interest from the original due date. Repeat non-compliance escalates to criminal referral for concealment of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the transfer pricing master file or authorization letter are personally exposed. Egregious non-market pricing may be treated as simulation, giving rise to criminal liability, travel bans, and collection of the unpaid tax from the general manager's personal assets.
Shell Company and DTA Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reapplication of the 20% statutory WHT on the recharacterized dividend, imposition of a GAAR assessment with an additional fine of up to 25% of the tax, and administrative freezing of bank accounts, with penalties up to EGP 5,000,000.

Director personal liability: If the structure is found to be a sham, the corporate veil is pierced; directors and shareholders may be personally assessed for the unpaid tax and are subject to travel bans, asset freezing, and criminal prosecution for committing a crime to reduce tax.
AML and Consumer Product Regulatory Infraction

Statutory sanction: Under the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law (Law 80/2002, as amended), fines range from EGP 10,000 to EGP 5,000,000 and imprisonment for at least six months for laundering operations. Regulatory breaches may suspend the commercial license and trigger customs penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors identified in the AML audit are criminally prosecuted, face travel restrictions, and their personal assets are subject to forfeiture as the proceeds of crime. The entity is blacklisted from domestic and cross-border banking and from Suez Canal Economic Zone facilities.
