Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permitted. The Estonian Commercial Code requires only that an OÜ have a registered office (registreeritud asukoht) in Estonia, meaning an actual street address to which official communications, court documents, and supervisory correspondence can be delivered. For a low-risk software publisher (ISIC 5820), a professional virtual-office or serviced-office address that includes the physical street name, letterbox, scanning, and forwarding arrangements is acceptable, provided the address is not a mere PO Box without legal mailbox service. The virtual-office provider must be contractually mandated to retain all registered mail for at least 30 days and notify the board member electronically. The management board remains responsible for ensuring the address is continuously valid, and any change must be filed in the e-Business Register within 30 days. Virtual offices without any permanent human presence are acceptable for this industry but are inadvisable if the company later seeks an Estonian credit institution account or substantial physical substance for VAT thresholds.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an Osaühing; founders may set share capital at zero or any amount expressed in shares. No escrow or blocked bank account is mandatory. If share capital is fixed above zero, monetary contributions must be transferred to the company's bank account upon incorporation or by the due date stated in the Articles of Association.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for a purely digital company. If a physical office is staffed, the employer must undertake a workplace risk assessment under the Estonian Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus), obtain fire safety documentation from the Rescue Board for the premises, and ensure the building occupancy and structural load compliance under the Buildings Act (Ehitusseadustik).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, ETCTB); e-MTA portal at https://e-mta.emta.ee; e-Business Register at https://www.ariregister.rik.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ) is formed in the e-Business Register using an e-Residency digital ID or an authorized signatory. The Registrar issues the company's 8-digit Registry Code, which serves as the tax registration number for corporate income tax. For VAT, the OÜ must apply to the ETCTB via the e-MTA portal (paper form VTA also available) and, if eligible, receives a VAT identification number prefixed by 'EE'. Non-established foreign entities with a place of supply in Estonia may register for VAT directly under the reverse-charge regime without a local PE. Registration is generally granted within a few working days; no minimum capital is required for an OÜ.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an OÜ is an Estonian tax-resident company subject to the Estonian corporate income tax system on distributed profits; undistributed profits are not currently taxed, and there is no general accrual-based corporate income tax. For ISIC 5820 hyper-casual game publishing, no statutory industry license is required; the entity remains within the standard CITA profit-distribution regime.

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style 'check-the-box' entity election. The relevant statutory mechanism under the Corporate Income Tax Act is the ability to retain profits indefinitely, thereby deferring shareholder-level taxation until actual distribution. Regular distributions are taxable at the corporate level; dividends from resident subsidiaries may be exempt under participation-exemption conditions. EU ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules are implemented, so cross-border instruments or entities with inconsistent classifications may be recharacterized or denied deductions. An OÜ may also elect the regular accrual scheme only in limited circumstances, none of which change the default distribution-based CITA treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supplies (excluding VAT) per calendar year for Estonian-established taxable persons; voluntary VAT registration is permitted below this threshold. Non-established suppliers are subject to reverse-charge or OSS rules without a local turnover threshold; the EU distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 applies to cross-border B2C supplies of digital game services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports to non-EU destinations are zero-rated when the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of Estonia/EU and documentary proof of export is retained. B2B services supplied to a taxable person outside Estonia are generally outside the scope of Estonian VAT under the place-of-supply rules; B2C digital services rendered to non-EU consumers are not subject to Estonian VAT unless the reverse-charge or OSS mechanism applies. Advertising proceeds from AdMob/AppLovin or similar programmatic ad networks collected from foreign principals are treated as B2B supplies to non-resident taxable persons and therefore fall outside Estonian VAT, provided the recipient business status and beneficial use are documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX/NCTS movement record)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate or SWIFT credit advice evidencing settlement
  • Payment or platform statement identifying game monetization and ad-network counterparties

Estonia does not impose a universal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for B2B VAT invoices. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2G contracts and is exchanged through the PEPPOL network. MTA accepts structured XML invoices and e-declarations through the e-MTA portal and public API endpoints, but this is not a pre-clearance/fiscal authorization process. Taxable persons must retain full invoice records and submit periodic VAT returns electronically.

ETCTB audits zero-rated export claims by matching internal VAT returns with customs export data and can retrospectively reverse 0% treatment if the required export evidence is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent. Reassessment exposes the taxpayer to additional VAT at 22%, statutory late-payment interest, and administrative fines for false or erroneous declarations. Repeated or intentional misapplication may lead to criminal tax-fraud prosecution, with penalties including pecuniary fines and imprisonment in severe cases. The statutory audit and clawback window generally extends up to five years from the end of the relevant tax period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under full EU capital account convertibility with no restrictions on inbound capital, outbound remittances, or profit distributions to non-resident shareholders. Current account transactions face no controls; profit remittances are unrestricted provided tax filings and beneficial ownership registers are maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds required. Foreign exchange transactions must be documented and reported to the Bank of Estonia if monthly value exceeds EUR 50,000 per transaction or aggregate thresholds under EU AMLR; conversion occurs at market rates with no statutory ratios.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank) and European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High viability for EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMI) under Payment Services Directive and Estonian Financial Supervision and Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) oversight; legal constraints include minimum capital of EUR 20,000, mandatory licensing, and enhanced AML/KYC for cross-border payouts.
