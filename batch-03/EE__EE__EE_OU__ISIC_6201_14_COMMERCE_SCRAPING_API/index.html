Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Business Register (Äriregister / e-Äriregister), Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus), operated through the e-Business Register portal, with notarial co-assistance for paper filings.

Governing statute

Commercial Code of the Republic of Estonia (Äriseadustik, RT I 1995, 26, 355), Part IV 'Osaühing' §§ 135–178 (esp. § 136 minimum capital, § 140 contribution rules, § 141 foundation agreement, § 142 articles, § 163 registration application, §§ 179–180 management board); Business Register Act (äriregistri seadus) §§ 20–24; General Part of the Civil Code Act §§ 30–31.

Formation / constitutional filings

Digital Registration Notice (online incorporation application 'Reserve name and found OÜ') submitted via the e-Business Register; for paper path — notarially authenticated Foundation Agreement and registration application.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Legal minimum share capital for an OÜ is EUR 2,500 (≈ USD 2,700), fully subscribed by shareholders. Per Commercial Code § 140(1)2, no bank escrow deposit is required at incorporation: the paid-up contribution may be deferred up to ten (10) years, provided the articles expressly state the deferred payment schedule. Contributions already made must be confirmed by the bank's confirmation of credit. No notary escrow is mandatory for e-registration with digital signatures.

No industrial license required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-services portal: e-MTA (e-Maksuamet), www.emta.ee

E-government filing portal

A domestic Osaühing (OÜ) is automatically registered for national tax identification upon incorporation in the Commercial Register (Äriregister), with no separate application step for attribution of the TIN. A foreign entity derives an Estonian VAT ID by filing the cross-border registration application via the e-MTA portal; mandatory registration applies within 5 working days of exceeding EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover in Estonia, EUR 10,000 on intra-Community acquisitions, or before the first taxable import into Estonia. Non-resident entities below the threshold may apply voluntarily for provisional VAT registration; if no permanent establishment or Estonian address exists, the Tax and Customs Board may require appointment of a VAT fiscal representative. e-Residency enables the entire registration flow digitally from abroad, subject to lodgment of a local contact person and registered address obligations for the Estonian company.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A domestic Osaühing (OÜ) is automatically registered for national tax identification upon incorporation in the Commercial Register (Äriregister), with no separate application step for attribution of the TIN. A foreign entity derives an Estonian VAT ID by filing the cross-border registration application via the e-MTA portal; mandatory registration applies within 5 working days of exceeding EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover in Estonia, EUR 10,000 on intra-Community acquisitions, or before the first taxable import into Estonia. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; legal entity form is conclusive for tax classification. Resident OÜs are taxed under the deferred distribution-based model (undistributed profits carry 0% current taxation, distributed profits taxed at 20% gross / 25% effective). Available elections include: (i) the reduced 14/86 regular-distribution rate for distributions not exceeding the average taxable distribution base of the preceding three years; (ii) elective prepayment of income tax on planned future distributions; (iii) VAT group election for related Estonian entities under the Value Added Tax Act; and (iv) hybrid mismatch neutralization rules under ATAD are mandatory and cannot be elective, denying double non-taxation outcomes for hybrid instruments and hybrid entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for domestic supplies; EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance-sale threshold for B2C digital services via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS); voluntary registration admissible below these limits

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to destinations outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate when dispatched or transported out of EU territory within 90 days of the time of supply, supported by qualifying customs and transport evidence. For the target SaaS/web-scraping and proxy-API business, services supplied to a business customer established and effectively operating in a third country qualify for the 0% export rate of services under the VAT Act; the taxpayer must demonstrate a genuine cross-border B2B chain. Data services consumed within Estonia or by EU end consumers do not qualify for 0% and must be taxed through the standard rate or OSS as applicable. The exporter must also satisfy VAT record-keeping and, for goods, the customs export declaration, and must repatriate consideration from the foreign buyer within the statutory period to substantiate that a true outside-EU supply occurred.

  • Customs export declaration (EX1 or electronic SAD) certified by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board
  • Bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill definitively proving dispatch from the EU
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of payment from the non-EU buyer
  • Executed contract, purchase order, and zero-rated invoice package for the foreign customer
  • Intrastat declaration (for intra-Community supplies) where applicable
  • Ledger substantiation linking the zero-rated supply to the underlying export transaction (VAT Act Section 17 and Section 18)

No real-time fiscal clearance, central stamping, or mandatory API stamping regime applies in Estonia. Electronic invoicing is lawful and standard via X-Road; B2G suppliers to the public sector must issue PEPPOL-compliant e-invoices. Issuers must respect the 7-day invoice issuance rule and preserve invoice authenticity/integrity controls (whether via EDI, qualified electronic signatures, or business-process documentation). The e-MTA portal prefills VAT returns from X-Road invoice data; programmatic API-based submission of VAT returns is available for compliance automation, but no fiscal-digital stamp duty or continuous transaction control certificate is imposed on ordinary data-service businesses.

Customs and indirect tax audits combine high-risk data analytics and customs export database cross-matching (e-MTA, INTRASTAT and EU VIES exchanges). Under-documented or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger reassessment at the 20% standard VAT rate, late-payment interest of 0.06% per day, and a fraud-prevention tax penalty of up to double the evaded amount. Intentional false documentation additionally constitutes tax fraud under Section 402 of the Estonian Penal Code, carrying fines and up to 5 years' imprisonment for aggravated or significant evasion. Inward remittance failure breaches the export substantiation bundle and triggers retroactive clawback of all input VAT recovered on the related cost base; the general assessment limitation period is 3 years, extended to 7 years for non-disclosed tax obligations and up to 10 years in fraud cases, thereby exposing stale refunds to reopened review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is the lawful currency; the exchange system is fully liberalised with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Estonia applies the EU free movement of capital framework, committing to unrestricted profit, dividend, royalty and liquidation-proceeds repatriation for residents and non-residents. There are no prior-authorisation requirements for cross-border transfers, no surrender or retention rules, and no central bank conversion obligations. The only indirect restraint is the Estonian distributed-profit taxation model: dividends are taxable at 20/80 (net/gross) upon distribution, or 14/86 for regular dividends, with relief available under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (for qualifying EEA corporate shareholders holding 10% or more for at least 12 months). This is a tax effect, not a currency impediment; settlements flow immediately through TARGET2 and SEPA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply. Estonian legislation and EU/ECB regulations impose no surrender, repatriation timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds or services income. Invoices for cross-border web-data services may be denominated in EUR, USD or any freely convertible currency, and balances may be held in external accounts. The only statutory duty is statistical: payment instructions must enable the Estonian bank to complete Eesti Pank balance-of-payments reporting, including an economic-purpose code and the underlying invoice/contract reference.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) and the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a payment settlement bridge; LOW as a primary banking/escrow relationship. Foreign electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Europe SA, Revolut Payments UAB, Monese) may passport payment services into Estonia under PSD2 and FIC regulation without a local deposit-taking licence. They can issue an IBAN, execute SEPA and SWIFT payments, and support USDC off-ramps via regulated VASPs. Legal constraints: (i) EMI accounts are not deposit guarantees covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund; (ii) the EMI, not the Principality, is responsible for AML/CFT, and the merchant's Estonian company profile is subject to the EMI's internal risk scoring; (iii) public web-scraping/API governance models and cryptocurrency-linked flows are frequently declined by standard EMIs, requiring a prior written compliance acceptance; (iv) if the company receives USDC as a direct redemption rail, the EMI onboarding will require evidence that the company itself is not a VASP or that an EU-licensed VASP performs conversion; (v) for statutory-paid capital escrow, a licensed Estonian bank is still required because the Commercial Register relies on the bank's written confirmation of the contribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual CbC Report: mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more. Local File / Master File: no statutory numerical threshold under Estonian domestic law; the MTA may require EUTPD-aligned transfer-pricing documentation on request for any controlled transaction, and failure to produce contemporaneous documentation shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to the UAE or Caribbean ESR regimes. Substance obligations arise indirectly: (i) place of effective management establishes residence under ITA § 6; (ii) the EU ATAD GAAR and Estonian anti-abuse rules deny treaty/beneficial-ownership advantages to artificial arrangements; (iii) MTA guidance requires genuine commercial substance for e-Residency-based OÜs engaged in digital/API commerce. An entity incorporated in Estonia is always an Estonian tax resident regardless of physical presence, but a substance-less conduit is vulnerable to CFC taxation abroad, treaty-benefit denial, and recharacterization by the MTA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unreported API and Crypto Revenue

Statutory sanction: Penal Code § 394 tax fraud: fine for a legal person of EUR 5,000 to EUR 10,000,000; imprisonment of the responsible natural person up to 5 years (7 years when aggravated); MTA additionally exacts 0.06% daily late interest and reassesses the hidden distribution at 20/80 with deemed-dividend charges.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders may be personally convicted and imprisoned; the court may impose a business ban of 1–5 years; where the OÜ is insolvent, the MTA can pursue personal secondary liability and claw-back civil restitution for the unpaid tax.
Hidden Profit Distribution / Shareholder Loan Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as a taxable distribution under ITA at 20/80 with 0.06% daily late-payment interest; administrative fines for misdeclaration; prosecution under Penal Code § 394 if concealment is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for the difference between actual tax and the assessed deemed-distribution tax where the company cannot pay; personal restitution claims by the MTA; potential criminal conviction and disqualification from managing companies.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Conduit Arrangements

Statutory sanction: MTA adjustment of controlled transactions to the arm's-length standard; the adjusted amount is taxed as a deemed distribution at 20/80 plus daily interest; CbC and documentation sanctions apply in group situations; criminal liability arises where false documentation is submitted.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false transfer-pricing records face personal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; the corporate veil may be pierced for restitution of the tax shortfall if the OÜ is stripped of assets via the conduit structure.
AML / VASP Non-Compliance in Crypto Acceptance

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) administrative fines up to 10% of annual turnover of the legal person (statutory caps apply under RahaPTS); permanent prohibition of the crypto-payment activity; freezing and confiscation of crypto assets; money-laundering offenses under Penal Code § 392 carry imprisonment of up to 8 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally sanctionable; FIU may impose asset freezes and travel restrictions; an individual with criminal AML exposure faces imprisonment of up to 8 years, forfeiture of personal assets, and a business ban; insurance coverage for such liability is typically null.
