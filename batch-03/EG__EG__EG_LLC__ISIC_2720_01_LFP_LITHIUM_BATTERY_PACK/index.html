Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) — One-Stop-Shop (OSS); Commercial Registry office integrated within the OSS and the Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade.

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended (LLC provisions under Articles 51–66), read with the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations; VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 for tax registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association / Incorporation Contract (Aqd Al-Taasis) and GAFI application form for incorporation and Commercial Registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for LLC formation under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; the capital is fixed by the Articles of Association, and the initial subscribed portion must be deposited into a provisional capital escrow-style bank account in Egypt before GAFI approval. Once the Commercial Registration is issued, the deposit is released to the company.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Directorate fire safety certificate under Law No. 152 of 2020; structural load inspection certificate from an approved engineer for battery racks and storage areas; Ministry of Manpower Workplace Safety and Occupational Health clearance; completion of building code and fire rated construction inspections required before operation commences.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA); official e-portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (e-invoice and e-receipt clearance APIs administered by ETA); Egypt Customs Authority (ASYCUDA World) for export licensing and customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC with GAFI (One-Stop Shop), obtain the Commercial Registration extract and industrial license for ISIC 2720 (LiFePO4 battery pack assembly). Step 2: Submit the ETA Unified Taxpayer Registration Application, with notarized Arabic-translated Memorandum and Articles of Association, board resolutions, proof of premises, passports of managers, and GAFI file. Step 3: ETA validates the file and conducts premises/activity verification; one single TIN, VAT registration certificate, withholding-tax file, and e-invoicing credentials are issued. Step 4: Open local foreign-currency bank accounts and register the export proceeds undertaking with the authorized Egyptian bank. The mandatory processing period is up to 15 business days; making taxable supplies before TIN issuance triggers late-registration penalties and denial of input-VAT recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not offer a check-the-box regime; the LLC is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a resident corporation under Law No. 91 of 2005 (22.5% CIT). Pool of statutory elections available to an LLC: (i) Public Free Zone election replacing standard income tax with a 1% annual turnover lump-sum tax for industrial free-zone projects; (ii) Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) and other investment-law incentive packages including Golden License instruments under Law No. 72 of 2017; (iii) the option under Article 26 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 to regularize intercompany markups and input-VAT apportionment; (iv) voluntary VAT registration and voluntary e-invoice/e-receipt onboarding ahead of mandatory phase; (v) application of Egypt's CbCR/TP documentation elections for transfer-pricing compliance (affecting tax returns).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 (compulsory registration once annual taxable supplies exceed this threshold per Article 17, Law No. 67 of 2016; Egyptian LLCs in the battery manufacturing chain normally exceed the threshold and register immediately; voluntary registration available for lower-turnover producers seeking input-VAT recovery).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of LiFePO4 battery packs qualify for 0% VAT under Article 21 of Law No. 67 of 2016 if: the goods leave Egyptian territory under a validated export customs declaration; the seller is the registered VAT supplier; the buyer is a non-resident and title passes abroad under the agreed Incoterms; and the full export price is received in convertible foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system. Supporting testing/engineering services supplied for goods delivered abroad are zero-rated when contractually bundled and invoiced to the non-resident buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACN/ASYCUDA printout endorsed by Egypt Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (original or telex release) or Air Waybill naming the overseas consignee
  • Commercial Invoice and Arabic/English Packing List
  • Export Contract / Purchase Order (with Incoterms 2020 clause) and Certificate of Origin on demand
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) / Foreign Currency Bank Credit Advice from an Egyptian authorized bank proving settlement of the export invoice
  • ETA Article 21 Export Evidence Form and, if required by the bank, the Central Bank of Egypt foreign-exchange repatriation undertaking

The ETA mandates real-time electronic clearance for all B2B and B2G invoices under ETA Decisions No. 492/2021 and No. 200/2022. Every invoice must be generated in an ETA-certified system and transmitted via API to the ETA validation engine before issuance; a cleared invoice receives a unique QR code and UUID (stamp). Manufacturing exporters operate with mapping of ISIC 2720 and HS 8507 codes; any deviation causes rejection codes and automatic blockage of outward clearance. Offline or cash-register invoices are inadmissible for VAT refunds and expense deductibility.

The ETA audits export zero-rating claims within a 5-year limitation period. Insufficient documentation shifts the burden to proof, and a failed/forged export file triggers: (i) clawback of the 14% VAT differential plus a 2% per-month delay penalty capped at 30%; (ii) administrative fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax under Articles 42-49 of Law No. 67 of 2016 as amended; (iii) imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible officers under Egyptian Penal Code evasion rules; (iv) VAT deregistration, e-invoice account suspension, and customs blacklisting of the LLC; and (v) joint and several liability of directors, the tax representative, and the receiving bank in cases of forged remittances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains current account convertibility for trade and services; capital account transactions are subject to CBE registration and approval. Profit remittance is permitted as transferable profits under ratified treaties, but requires tax clearance, audited financials, and CBE confirmation of the underlying FDI.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations, export proceeds must be repatriated and credited to an Egyptian authorized bank within 120 days of shipment. No mandatory conversion ratio is imposed; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency, sold at interbank rate, or used for admissible import settlements.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low. Offshore digital EMIs cannot open direct settlement accounts or issue e-money in Egypt without CBE licensing under Law No. 18 of 2019. They may only integrate as referrers or white-label providers through CBE-licensed banks, not as independent clearing channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

FDI capital must be wired from the corporate bank account of the non-resident investor via SWIFT MT103 to the LLC’s capital escrow account maintained at a CBE-authorized bank. The remittance must carry the BOPS purpose code for direct investment ('Capital Subscription – FDI') and be matched against the GAFI investment registration. The receiving bank verifies the transaction, issues a repatriation entitlement certificate, and may convert to EGP at the interbank rate or hold in USD in a margin account.Outbound supplier settlements are cleared via: (a) SWIFT MT103 T/T for import payments ≤ USD 100,000 per transaction with certified proforma invoice and CD (Customs Declaration); (b) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (L/C) for larger equipment and cell purchases, issued by the bank against a signed purchase order and import information sheet (Form 4); all payments are subject to CBE Form 5 export/import filing and local tax clearance.Deliverable FX forwards (USD/EGP) with maturities up to 24 months for trade and investment hedge exposures.FX swaps for short-term balance-sheet liquidity management (up to 12 months) executed with onshore authorized banks.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CIRS) for long-term project financing, subject to CBE prior approval and underlying capital transaction evidence.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of annual taxable profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 10% on dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company. A 5% rate may apply to qualifying portfolio dividends relating to listed shares where statutory conditions are satisfied. Treaty rates may reduce the rate, subject to the relevant treaty, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse provisions, and procedural compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within four months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year company, the ordinary deadline is 30 April of the following year. The return must be filed electronically where the taxpayer is within the electronic-filing population.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation generally includes a Local File and, for qualifying multinational enterprise groups, a Master File and Country-by-Country Report. Common statutory thresholds are approximately EGP 8 million annually for related-party goods transactions and EGP 2 million annually for related-party services, financing, and intangible-asset transactions, subject to the transaction categories and regulations applicable to the filing year. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are generally relevant where the multinational group’s consolidated revenue reaches approximately EGP 3 billion, with CbC filing or notification obligations potentially applying to the Egyptian constituent entity. Thresholds and filing mechanics must be confirmed for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not generally operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Nevertheless, Egyptian tax authorities may challenge arrangements lacking commercial substance under transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership standards, permanent-establishment concepts, treaty anti-abuse provisions, and general deductibility requirements. The Egyptian LLC should maintain real local management, employees or appropriately documented outsourced functions, production assets, inventory controls, operational premises, decision-making records, bank activity, customer and supplier evidence, and commercial risk allocation consistent with its declared battery-pack manufacturing functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax-evasion liability may include imprisonment, commonly ranging from six months to five years under applicable tax legislation, and a fine that may be linked to or equal to the tax sought to be evaded, in addition to the underlying tax, compensation, administrative penalties, and confiscation or collection measures.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, authorized signatories, accountants, and other responsible persons may face personal criminal liability where they directed, authorized, participated in, or knowingly facilitated the conduct. Civil recovery, attachment of assets, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may arise under applicable tax and criminal-procedure rules; limited liability does not protect deliberate fraud.
Failure to file or pay corporate income tax

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines may apply for non-filing, late filing, or electronic non-compliance, with enhanced penalties for repetition. Unpaid tax attracts statutory late-payment compensation and may lead to enforced collection, account attachment, liens, and restrictions on tax-clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and signatories may be prosecuted where non-compliance is intentional or involves falsification. Personal liability is fact-dependent and may include fines, criminal sanctions, civil restitution, asset attachment, and travel restrictions.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian Tax Authority may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, impose additional tax, late-payment compensation, documentation penalties, and withholding tax, and may apply penalties for inaccurate disclosures or failure to maintain required files.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal exposure where they knowingly approved artificial pricing, false documentation, or concealed related-party dealings. Civil restitution and criminal exposure depend on proof of intent, participation, and resulting tax loss.
Customs and import-tax misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include reassessed customs duties and VAT, fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods, suspension of import privileges, customs prosecution, and tax reassessment where the misdeclaration affects corporate income tax or withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, customs brokers, and responsible employees may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for intentional smuggling, document fraud, or customs evasion.
Improper dividend or profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The company may face dividend recovery, withholding-tax assessment, corporate tax adjustments, late-payment compensation, accounting and company-law penalties, and banking or foreign-exchange compliance scrutiny.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions may be required to restore distributed amounts, compensate the company or creditors, and may face criminal or administrative sanctions where the distribution involved fraud, insolvency, false accounts, or tax evasion.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC may become liable for the unwithheld tax, late-payment compensation, filing penalties, interest, and penalties for inaccurate or missing withholding returns. The tax authority may deny the related deduction.

Director personal liability: Responsible signatories and finance officers may face personal penalties or criminal exposure for deliberate non-withholding, falsified treaty documentation, or misappropriation of amounts withheld from recipients.
Shell company, sham transaction, or lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The authority may disregard the arrangement, deny deductions, recharacterize payments, impose withholding tax and corporate tax, revoke incentives, challenge treaty benefits, and refer suspected fraud or money laundering for prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil restitution, asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment where sham arrangements, fraud, proceeds of crime, or intentional tax evasion are proven. Corporate limited liability does not shield persons who used the entity as an instrument of unlawful conduct.
Anti-money-laundering and beneficial-ownership violation

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include suspicious-transaction reporting failures, regulatory fines, account freezing, transaction blocking, license consequences, confiscation, and criminal prosecution under Egyptian anti-money-laundering legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, beneficial owners, and employees may face personal fines, asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or failure to perform legally required controls.
False invoicing and electronic-record manipulation

Statutory sanction: The company may face VAT and income-tax reassessment, invoice penalties, denial of deductions and input-tax credits, electronic-invoicing sanctions, interest, and criminal fraud or tax-evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Authorized signatories, finance personnel, and directors who knowingly approved false invoices may face fines, civil recovery, asset attachment, travel bans, and imprisonment.
