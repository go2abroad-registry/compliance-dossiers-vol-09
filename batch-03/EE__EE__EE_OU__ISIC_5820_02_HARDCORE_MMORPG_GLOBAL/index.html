Competent corporate registry

Registry Department of Tartu County Court (Tartu Maakohtu registriosakond), operating through the electronic e-Business Register (e-äriregister / ettevõtjaportaali), which is administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus, RIK).

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik, RT I 1995, 26, 355), Chapter V on the private limited company / Osaühing, §§ 135–202; applicable sections include § 136 (legal personality), § 140 (share capital), § 142 (contents of the articles of association), § 144 (payment of contributions), § 158 (management board) and § 176² (restricted distribution if capital below EUR 2,500). Registration procedure is governed by the Business Register Act (Äriregistri seadus) §§ 2, 9–12, and the State Fees Act (Riigilõivuseadus) fee schedule; e-Residency digital identity is established under the Police and Border Guard Board Act framework.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Entry in the Business Register (registreerimisavaldus / äriregistri registrikanne), submitted as the digital formation form for an Osaühing on the e-Business Register portal and digitally signed with a qualified e-Residency or Estonian digital identification signature.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital: an Osaühing may be formed with registered share capital from EUR 0.01. Under Commercial Code § 140(1), a monetary contribution must be paid within the term stated in the articles, which cannot exceed one year from registration; non-cash contributions require a valuation statement. If registered share capital is below EUR 2,500, profit distributions are restricted until share capital and unrestricted equity together exceed EUR 2,500 per § 176²(1). There is no mandatory escrow account rule; monetary contributions can be credited to a dedicated temporary bank collection account opened for the OÜ before registration, but shareholders remain liable for unpaid contributions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No US-style check-the-box regime exists in Estonia. An OÜ is entity-level taxed under the Estonian corporate income tax model: profit is exempt until distributed or deemed distributed; distributions are taxed at the payor at 20/80 (20% of gross distribution) or 14/86 for regular dividends meeting the statutory regular-distribution balance conditions; the 14/86 election is available on qualifying distributions. Optional elections with MTA include (i) formation of a VAT group by closely connected Estonian taxable persons, (ii) VAT small-business exemption below the EUR 40,000 turnover threshold, (iii) EU Union One Stop Shop (OSS) registration for intra-Union B2C digital services and Non-Union OSS for EU consumer digital services where applicable. The ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply statutorily without elective carve-out; cross-border hybrid entities and instruments require mandatory self-adjustment.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
22%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover in Estonia (calendar-year basis with forward-looking 12-month assessment) for Estonian-established taxable persons; non-established foreign suppliers are registrable from the first taxable supply without threshold; for intra-Union B2C digital services a EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold applies, above which Union OSS registration is required unless the supplier opts for per-Member-State registration.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods: physical export from Estonia to a third-country destination is zero-rated upon proof of dispatch and customs release within 3 months. Services, including MMORPG digital supplies: (i) B2B supplies of digital services to customers established abroad, including EU customers holding a valid VAT ID, fall outside the scope of Estonian VAT and are effectively zero-rated in Estonia, with reverse charge applying to the customer's jurisdiction; (ii) B2C supplies of game subscriptions, virtual items and in-app purchases to consumers residing outside the EU are outside Estonia's VAT jurisdiction and effectively 0% rated; (iii) B2C supplies to private consumers in another EU Member State are not zero-rated and are subject to the destination Member State's VAT, payable via the Union OSS; (iv) App Store / Google Play payouts in the usual structure constitute the platform operators' deemed B2C supplies to the final consumer, with the Estonian OÜ rendering a B2B digital service to Apple Distribution International or Google Ireland Limited, zero-rated in Estonia and reverse-charged in Ireland. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated and export transactions is recoverable through periodic KMD returns; sustained refund positions are subject to MTA pre-refund verification. The public commercial model requires no gaming license in Estonia.

Estonia does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital stamping regime. Structured e-invoicing (EN 16931-compatible e-arve / PEPPOL) is mandatory only for B2G invoices issued to Estonian public-sector entities; B2B e-invoicing is voluntary although commercially standard. All VAT returns (Form KMD and annex KMD INF) must be submitted electronically via e-MTA; the MTA provides official REST APIs for return submission, VAT register queries and e-invoice data exchange. Invoices must satisfy EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC content rules and be retained for at least 7 years.

      
The MTA conducts targeted post-clearance audits of export zero-rating claims; for digital businesses, scrutiny focuses on divergence between OSS filings and App Store / Google Play payout flows, substance of the stated recipient, and completeness of the 180-day FX repatriation documentation. Where zero-rating is not substantiated, the MTA retroactively assesses output VAT at 22% on gross consideration, disallows related input VAT, applies statutory interest at 0.06% per day from the original due date, and imposes an administrative penalty for untaxed supplies under the Taxation Act. Intentional or grossly negligent schemes are treated as tax fraud under Section 389 of the Penal Code, exposing responsible management to imprisonment up to 5 years and the legal entity to a daily-rate-based pecuniary punishment. Excess input VAT refunds linked to rejected export evidence are clawed back with interest, and repeat violations may result in enhanced audit frequency and suspension of VAT refund priority.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Full convertibility under the euro (EUR), the national currency since 1 January 2011. Estonia applies the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and has no exchange-control regime restricting current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance, including dividend distributions, is unrestricted from an FX perspective; however, corporate income tax on distributions (generally 20% on the net distribution, 14% on regular dividends meeting statutory criteria) applies under the Estonian Income Tax Act. No central-bank approval, currency repatriation licence, or FX allocation is required for an MMORPG-operating OÜ receiving Apple App Store / Google Play proceeds and paying global suppliers and royalties in EUR or foreign currency.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None applicable. Estonia repealed its Foreign Exchange Act upon euro adoption and imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender or mandatory conversion of export receipts or foreign-currency proceeds. The OÜ retains full freedom to hold EUR or foreign-currency accounts with an authorised credit institution, provided transaction reporting obligations under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (Rahapesu ja terrorismi rahastamise tõkestamise seadus) and general tax-declaration requirements are satisfied.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem; monetary policy and foreign exchange operations are coordinated by the European Central Bank (ECB). The Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) regulates credit institutions and payment institutions for licensing and AML/CFT purposes.

            Fit: High

Use of an offshore (non-EU/EEA) EMI is legally constrained: providing payment or e-money services in Estonia without a Finantsinspektsioon licence or an EU passport notification constitutes an administrative and potentially criminal offence under the Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act; the OÜ should avoid maintaining treasury accounts with unlicensed offshore EMIs because correspondent-banking terms and Estonian payment-account rules do not recognise such providers.
