Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); tax administration is conducted principally through the e-MTA portal. Company incorporation and statutory registry-code issuance are handled through the Estonian Commercial Register (e-Business Register) administered by the Ministry of Justice and Digital Affairs.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian Osaühing obtains its statutory legal-entity identifier by incorporation in the Estonian Commercial Register. The assigned registry code is generally used as the entity identifier in tax administration and tax filings. The company must activate access to e-MTA, appoint authorized representatives, and register as an employer where it pays employment income. VAT registration is separate: the company submits an application to the MTA, identifying its business activities, management, bank account, expected taxable turnover, contracts, and evidence of genuine economic activity. Mandatory VAT registration generally arises when taxable turnover generated in Estonia exceeds EUR 40,000 in a calendar year; voluntary registration is available before the threshold. A foreign entity without an Estonian registered office may require registration for Estonian tax obligations, including a permanent establishment, employer, or VATable activity. An EU-established taxable person may generally apply directly. A non-EU taxable person may be required to appoint an Estonian tax representative, subject to applicable mutual-assistance rules and statutory exceptions. VAT identification is issued in the format EE followed by nine digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no elective pass-through classification for ordinary corporate income tax purposes. An OÜ is a separate taxable person, but Estonia generally defers corporate income tax until profit is distributed or treated as distributed. From 1 January 2025, distributed profits are generally subject to corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net distribution. Retained and reinvested profits are generally not subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the time of earning. Estonia applies EU anti-hybrid-mismatch rules and related-party transfer-pricing rules; these are statutory anti-avoidance regimes rather than elective classification mechanisms. Non-resident entities may become taxable through an Estonian permanent establishment or source-based rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover arising in Estonia during a calendar year. The threshold does not eliminate registration obligations arising under special rules, including certain intra-Community acquisitions, reverse-charge transactions, distance sales, imported goods, or electronically supplied services. The standard VAT rate is 24% from 1 July 2025; the previous standard rate was 22%. Reduced rates may apply to specified statutory categories and are not generally applicable to SaaS, email infrastructure, hosting, or digital advertising services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the exporter can substantiate exportation through customs records and transport evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% VAT where the customer is VAT-identified in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier has the required VAT identification and reporting evidence. For B2B services, including SaaS, email API, hosting, and infrastructure services, the supply is generally located where the business customer is established and may be invoiced without Estonian VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism; this is technically an outside-scope or reverse-charge treatment rather than a domestic 0% export rate. B2C electronically supplied services are generally taxable where the consumer is located and may require OSS registration or local VAT compliance. Input VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, business-use evidence, and compliance with VAT return and record-retention rules.

  • Electronic customs export declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN), accepted or released by Estonian Customs
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment, and delivery terms
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier tracking record, or carrier confirmation
  • Evidence that the goods exited the European Union, including customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic export evidence
  • Sales contract, purchase order, packing list, and proof of delivery where relevant
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and EC Sales List reporting
  • For exported services: customer business-status evidence, contractual description of the service, customer location evidence, invoices, payment records, and evidence supporting the applicable place-of-supply rule
  • Bank payment evidence may support commercial substantiation, but Estonia generally does not require a separate inward remittance certificate for export VAT treatment

Estonia does not generally impose real-time invoice clearance or universal B2B e-invoicing for ordinary domestic transactions. VAT invoices may be issued electronically if statutory invoice-content requirements are met and the recipient can receive and process them. Structured electronic invoicing is required or operationally expected for specified public-procurement and public-sector transactions, subject to applicable procurement and accounting rules. There is no general requirement for a digital tax stamp, real-time API submission, or continuous transaction control for the stated OÜ business. VAT returns and related schedules are filed electronically through e-MTA. EU One Stop Shop (OSS) and Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) systems may apply to qualifying cross-border B2C supplies.

Estonia has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation requirement or mandatory deadline for bringing export proceeds into Estonia. A 0% VAT claim may be denied or clawed back if export, transport, customer-status, place-of-supply, or accounting evidence is missing, contradictory, fabricated, or inconsistent with customs data. The taxpayer may become liable for unpaid VAT, statutory late-payment interest, correction of VAT returns, repayment of improperly claimed input VAT, and administrative penalties under the Taxation Act and Value Added Tax Act. Deliberate false documentation, concealment, or fraudulent VAT claims may result in criminal investigation, criminal liability, recovery of tax-related damages, and restrictions on VAT registration or tax-refund processing. The MTA may audit records, customs data, payment flows, customer location, IP or service-delivery evidence, and related-party arrangements. There is no fixed universal clawback period stated in this dossier; assessment and limitation periods depend on the nature of the tax obligation, conduct, and applicable Estonian procedural law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under full eurozone convertibility rules. Both capital and current accounts are unrestricted; profit remittances to EU/EEA shareholders face no regulatory hurdles or approval requirements.

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies. Service exports fall under liberalized EU single-market regime; foreign-currency receipts may be retained in any permitted account without conversion.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% (20/80 effective rate on gross distributions; 0% on retained and undistributed profits)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on gross profit distributions (20/80 effective); the 14% reduced rate applies to qualifying regular distributions to resident individuals; non-resident corporate shareholders may benefit from DTA or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, typically reducing the rate to 0% or a lower treaty rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with non-resident related parties, as per MTA regulatory practice; documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 30 days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code similar to offshore financial centres; however, the Estonian General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), ATAD-implemented provisions, and EU jurisprudence impose a substantive substance requirement. A shell OÜ used solely to channel email-infrastructure SaaS income through zero-tax retained profit will be denied treaty benefits and potentially recharacterized as having made taxable distributions. Real personnel, physical premises, business decision-making, and operational activity within Estonia are required to withstand tax authority challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Back taxes plus 0.06% daily default interest; administrative penalty up to €7,500; criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code §396 with mandatory tax surcharge; imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or knowingly facilitate false declarations are personally liable for restitution of the unpaid corporate tax. Criminal proceedings may result in personal travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as a board member; the corporate veil may be pierced where fraud or abuse of the limited-liability structure is proven.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA or EU-directive benefits; recharacterization of undrawn profits as constructive distributions subject to 20% corporate income tax and late-payment interest; administrative fine up to €7,500; potential administrative strike-off of the entity; refusal of future e-Residency or trade license applications.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid tax if the corporate shell is an instrument of abuse; travel bans and personal asset seizures can be imposed in enforcement proceedings; criminal liability for tax fraud arises where the shell is deliberately designed to evade Estonian distribution tax.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing the taxable base by disallowing excessive deductions; recharacterization of adjusted amounts as hidden profit distributions subject to 20% corporate income tax; 0.06% per day default interest; administrative penalty up to €7,500; criminal referral for wilful transfer pricing fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or prepare transfer pricing documentation knowing it is false are jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalties. If the arrangement is deemed artificial for tax avoidance, criminal tax evasion sanctions may apply personally, including imprisonment and personal seizure orders.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: AML Act sanctions of up to €5 million or 10% of annual turnover; asset freezing and confiscation orders; withdrawal of payment-institution or collection licences; administrative penalty of up to €200,000 for natural persons responsible for AML compliance; criminal prosecution under Penal Code §394 for money laundering with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to implement AML policies, appoint no AML compliance officer, or approve transactions with suspicious counterparties face personal criminal fines, travel bans, personal asset attachment, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where the OÜ is used as a laundering vehicle.
