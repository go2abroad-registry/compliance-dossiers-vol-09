Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) — central tax and customs administration; e-MTA self-service portal: https://e-mta.emta.ee; official website: https://www.emta.ee; entity registry: Estonian Centre of Registers and Information Systems via the e-Business Register (https://ariregister.rik.ee).

E-government filing portal

STEP A — Incorporate the OÜ online in the e-Business Register (Äriregister). The registry issues a unique 8-digit registry code (registrikood), which serves as the company's permanent national TAX ID/TIN; no separate TIN certificate is issued. STEP B — Appoint a management board (juhatus); board members authenticate via ID-card, Mobile-ID, or the e-Residency digital ID; foreign founders may execute through a notarised power of attorney. STEP C — Mandatory VAT registration: file the VAT registration application with the MTA through e-MTA as soon as Estonia-taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed EUR 40,000 per calendar year; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. STEP D — For cross-border digital services to EU consumers, register in the Union OSS scheme through the Estonian e-MTA/OSS portal (quarterly declaration), which avoids individual VAT registration in other EU member states. STEP E — Receive the VAT certificate (maksukohustuslasena registreerimise tõend) showing the EE-prefixed VAT number (format EE+8 digits). No customs or excise codes are required for a digital web-fiction subscription platform.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the OÜ is a non-transparent legal person. Estonia applies a deferred entity-level corporate income tax: undistributed profits are entirely untaxed (0% annual CIT), but profit distributions, fringe benefits, gifts, and deemed non-business expenses trigger entity-level CIT at a 25% headline burden (computed as 20/80 of the net distribution). Territorial principle: an Estonian-resident OÜ is taxed on distributions regardless of where profits were earned; a foreign company whose place of effective management is in Estonia is likewise treated as Estonian-resident. The OÜ is not a pass-through entity and is not located in a tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Relevant statutory options: (1) voluntary VAT registration before the EUR 40,000 threshold (VAT Act); (2) Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for intra-EU distance sales and B2C electronic/digital services instead of separate member-state VAT registrations; (3) optional treatment of a foreign legal person as Estonian tax-resident when its place of effective management lies in Estonia (Income Tax Act); (4) application to the MTA for a binding advance tax ruling on classification, place of supply, and zero-rating (Tax Procedure Act); (5) automatic hybrid-mismatch neutralisation under ATAD I/II (no taxpayer election — an OÜ that is transparent in the foreign shareholder's jurisdiction or that involves hybrid financial instruments is adjusted symmetrically to prevent double non-taxation, including via CFC rules in the Estonian shareholder's hands).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year for Estonian-resident taxable persons (mandatory VAT registration once taxable Estonia-supplies exceed or are expected to exceed this turnover; voluntary registration earlier is permitted). Non-resident suppliers registered in Estonia have no monetary threshold — any taxable supply chain in Estonia (other than via OSS) triggers VAT liability. For intra-EU distance sales and B2C electronic services, the Union-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies: below EUR 10,000 of such cross-border supplies, Estonian domestic VAT applies to an Estonian-resident supplier; above it, the OSS scheme must be used and VAT accrues in the EU consumer's member state at that state's rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated if the goods physically leave Estonia for a destination outside the EU, supported by customs export documentation and transport evidence; the OÜ must hold such evidence before filing zero-rated output in its VAT return. Intra-EU B2B dispatches are zero-rated when the customer supplies a valid EU VAT ID and the goods are dispatched to another member state, with an EU Sales List (ECS) filed. For services: B2B services exported to a taxable person outside Estonia (EU or third-country) are zero-rated (place of supply at the customer's establishment) upon obtaining the customer's valid VAT number or foreign-tax evidence. B2C e-services (web-novel subscriptions): (a) to non-EU consumers — outside the scope of Estonian VAT (no zero-rating claim necessary); (b) to EU consumers — Estonian VAT is displaced by the destination-state VAT through the OSS scheme; (c) when sold via Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, the app-store operator may be the deemed supplier of the electronic service, making the operator — not the OÜ — the VAT collector for the end-supply; the OÜ's contractual and payout documentation must evidence the B2B relationship with the app store and the app-store commission and remittance terms.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/MRN Movement Reference Number) evidencing the goods' release for export from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or CMR/air waybill (land/air) or courier consignment note proving physical exit from Estonia to a third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103) evidencing receipt of the export consideration and, where applicable, FX repatriation to the Estonian bank account
  • Commercial invoice, transport instructions, export contract, and insurance certificate attributable to the specific zero-rated supply
  • Substitute evidence for digital services: web-subscriber agreement, IP-geolocation and subscriber-residence logs, Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console payout reports, OSS quarterly returns, and VIES validation screenshots for intra-EU zero-rated B2B supplies

No real-time fiscal data clearance, fiscal stamping, or mandatory API reporting to the MTA exists for B2B/B2C invoicing. Tax invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format, and must be retained for 7 years. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only for invoices addressed to Estonian public-sector bodies (B2G), which must be issued in a structured European standard (EN 16931) format, typically delivered via the PEPPOL network. The file formats in private commerce are unrestricted. Forward planning: the EU ViDA package is expected to impose mandatory structured e-invoicing and digital VAT reporting for cross-border B2B supplies (projected 2028–2035), so the web-fiction platform's billing software (Apple IAP / Google Play Billing exports and OSS XML returns) should already support standard e-invoice export and machine-readable audit files.

The MTA audits zero-rating claims by matching each 0% invoice to transport, customs, OSS, and payment records; missing or divergent documentation causes the supply to be recharacterised as domestic with VAT at 22%, plus statutory arrears interest on the late tax (currently 0.06% per day under the General Part of the Tax Code) and defaulting-filer penalties. Fraudulent or knowingly incorrect zero-rating and OSS misuse attract: (i) reassessment and clawback of the full unpaid VAT and input-VAT recovery; (ii) administrative fines; (iii) criminal liability under the Penal Code for tax fraud (maksupettus) — fines on the legal person and imprisonment of responsible directors up to 5 years, with longer terms in aggravated cases; and (iv) revocation or suspension of the VAT number and OSS access, with cross-border exchange of information through the EU OSS network. Limitation and re-opening rules follow those in force under the Tax Procedure Act and the Penal Code, including extended assessment windows where deliberate concealment is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is the sole legal tender. Full convertibility applies to both current account and capital account transactions. Estonia imposes no exchange control restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, shareholder loans, or subscription receipts, subject only to general EU anti-money laundering reporting thresholds and tax withholding obligations at source (normally none for distributions to EU/EEA tax residents; 20% withholding on dividends to non-treaty jurisdictions unless treaty relief applies). Since the OÜ is subject to the 0% undistributed profit regime, profit remittance is taxed upon distribution at 20/80 (22/80 from 2025) of the net dividend unless a participation exemption or treaty rate reduces it. No hurdles exist for converting subscription revenues from USD or other currencies into EUR; conversion is free and immediate at market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia applies no statutory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds. EU free movement of capital and payments (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63–65) guarantees unrestricted retention, use, and repatriation of receivables. Exporters may keep foreign currency in designated accounts indefinitely and are only required to report certain balance-of-payments statistical data to Eesti Pank for monthly/direct reporting thresholds (in accordance with ECB Balance of Payments regulation and Eesti Pank statistical requirements).

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — the central bank of Estonia, a euro-area institution operating within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for daily liquidity operations; LOW for primary treasury settlement. Estonian-resident OÜs may open accounts with EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Monese, Holvi, and specialised B2B platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Payoneer) via the EU single passport. These accounts are legally valid under the EEA Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and the Payment Services Directive (PSD2), and can support SEPA instant payments, business debit cards, and IBANs in the OÜ’s name. However, EMIs generally do not provide credit facilities, physical bank guarantees, or deep FX hedging desks, and they may impose additional due diligence on high-volume consumer payment aggregators. A tier-1 licensed bank account remains the necessary anchor for capital injection, trade-related documentary instruments, and corporate governance credibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol for shareholder capital injection and shareholder loans: - For EUR-denominated injection: SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) from the shareholder’s bank to the OÜ’s IBAN (LT/EE-prefix) with purpose code 'CAPT' (capital contribution) or 'LOAN' (intercompany debt). Under current SEPA rules, no FX control or pre-authorisation is needed; the receiving bank will only ask for supporting corporate resolution if the transfer exceeds €50,000. - For non-EUR injection (USD, GBP): SWIFT MT103 with an explicit underlying purpose code in field 72 (e.g., /CAPP/ for capital payment, /LOAN/ for intercompany credit). The sending bank will require a funds-availability statement and the beneficiary bank may request the OÜ’s board resolution and registry excerpt. Tax filing to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA) is not required at entry, but the injection must be recorded in the OÜ’s balance sheet and, for shareholder loans exceeding €100,000, an intercompany loan agreement with arm’s-length interest rate must be kept to avoid thin-capitalisation scrutiny. - Verification: All inbound payments are screened by the Estonian FIU and Eesti Pank under EU payment screening rules. The OÜ must retain the payment message, the contract/decision authorising the transaction, and the shareholder’s bank confirmation for 5 years. A capital account opening report to the Commercial Register is filed through the e-Business Register within 1 month after the contribution is credited.Clearing rails for operational outflows: - For standard supplier payments within SEPA: SEPA Credit Transfer (MT103-equivalent on SEPA XML) — final settlement in real time in euro; no FX risk. Purpose code suffices as 'SUPP' or 'ROYA' when paying authors/content licences. - For non-EUR royalties and contractor payments (e.g., USD): SWIFT MT103 messages via tier-1 banks, with purpose code 'ROYAL' or 'LICEN'; bank converts EUR at agreed spot or via a standing instruction thereafter. - For large trade settlements requiring documentary assurance (e.g., purchase of marketing tech hardware or exclusive licences > €100,000): Commercial Letter of Credit (irrevocable, at sight / deferred) issued by the client’s bank and advised/confirmed by a Baltic correspondent. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for daily liquidity operations; LOW for primary treasury settlement. Estonian-resident OÜs may open accounts with EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Monese, Holvi, and specialised B2B platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Payoneer) via the EU single passport. These accounts are legally valid under the EEA Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and the Payment Services Directive (PSD2), and can support SEPA instant payments, business debit cards, and IBANs in the OÜ’s name. However, EMIs generally do not provide credit facilities, physical bank guarantees, or deep FX hedging desks, and they may impose additional due diligence on high-volume consumer payment aggregators. A tier-1 licensed bank account remains the necessary anchor for capital injection, trade-related documentary instruments, and corporate governance credibility.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% (gross-up basis 20/80 of net distributions; 0% on retained undistributed profits)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Estonia does not impose a separate recipient-level dividend withholding tax. The resident OÜ is the taxpayer and bears the statutory distribution tax: 20% (20/80 gross-up on the net dividend) as the standard rate, and 14% (14/86 of the net dividend) for regular distributions to resident and non-resident individuals within the three-year average regular distribution limit. Non-resident recipients receive the dividend net of the OÜ-level tax with no further source-state withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of cross-border controlled transactions with related parties acts as the MTA's practical audit trigger for requiring contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation; EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers mandatory Country-by-Country reporting. Estonia has no fixed statutory Local File/Master File threshold, but the MTA may demand OECD-aligned documentation within 15 business days when the EUR 1,000,000 threshold is exceeded or when transactions involve low-tax jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: Estonia applies a substance-based anti-avoidance doctrine embedded in the distribution-based corporate tax: expenses lacking a real business purpose or economic substance are treated as deemed distributions subject to 20% tax. Section 84(8) of the Income Tax Act empowers the MTA to disregard artificial transactions and to recharacterize the OÜ as a conduit if its Estonian management is nominal. E-residency does not confer tax residency benefits; the OÜ must maintain actual management, employee presence, business records, and decision-making functions in Estonia. Payments to related parties that are not substantiated by real services, IP development, or financing functions are treated as hidden dividends with no deduction and a full 20% tax liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Undeclared Distributions and Hidden Dividends

Statutory sanction: Back-assessment of the 20% distribution tax on the entire grossed-up hidden amount; late payment interest of 0.06% per day; imputed tax-fraud fines under Penal Code section 1531 of up to EUR 1.2 million for corporations and criminal fines for individuals; Customs and Tax Board enforced seizure of bank accounts; publication of tax debtors.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize hidden distributions are personally liable under Commercial Code section 187(2) for restitution to the company of the dissipated value; criminal conviction for aggravated tax evasion (Penal Code section 1532) carries imprisonment up to 7 years and mandatory disqualification from acting as a director; travel bans and cross-border arrest warrants issued against e-resident shareholders abusing the OÜ structure.
Shell Company and Sham Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the OÜ as a foreign-controlled permanent establishment with retroactive taxation at 20% on deemed distributions; invalidation of internal invoices and cost allocations; GAAR application under Income Tax Act section 84(8); forced deregistration of the entity from the Commercial Register; denial of tax residence and loss access to Estonian double-tax treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors incur civil liability for unpaid corporate taxes under the tax-goods liability regime; intentional abuse of the e-Residency scheme constitutes a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years; entry bans and e-residency digital identity revocation are issued under the Identity Documents Act; directors of wound-up entities are separately liable for the company's unpaid tax arrears to the extent of distributed assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Artificial Profit Extraction

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment restoring arm's-length results; the upward adjustment to taxable distributions is taxed at 20%; additional penalty interest at 0.06% per day; administrative fine up to EUR 32,000 under the MTA's supervisory rules; mandatory audit for habitually non-compliant MNE groups; CbC exchange sanctions for failure to file the EUR 750 million reporting threshold.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false transfer-pricing documentation are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax adjustment; criminal sanctions apply for document forgery and tax fraud; OECD Mutual Agreement Procedure access is denied for deliberate contraveners; directors face professional disqualification and personal insolvency consequences for civil restitution claims of up to the entire adjusted amount.
Anti-Money Laundering and Platform Payment-Rails Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal persons or 10% of annual turnover under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; freezing and confiscation of platform revenues and bank balances; prohibition of the consumer payment license for willful non-compliance; criminal forfeiture of proceeds and punitive fines under Penal Code section 394.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and board members are personally exposed to fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for laundering of proceeds; directors of payment intermediaries face civil damages to injured subscribers; travel bans and asset seizure orders are routinely issued in parallel AML investigations; recurring violations trigger automatic disqualification from serving as a director in any Estonian legal entity.
