Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum share capital is mandated by Estonian law for an OÜ. The share capital is determined by the Articles of Association. At least 25% of the share capital must be paid up in cash prior to registration, with the balance payable within one year. No escrow account is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) — e-MTA digital portal at https://e-mta.emu.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign founder may obtain an Estonian e-Residency digital ID, then incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) through the e-Business Register (https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee) with the 8648 registry code assigned as the statutory Estonian business TIN. Following incorporation, apply to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board for a VAT identification number via e-MTA upon exceeding the €40,000 annual taxable turnover threshold or on a voluntary basis; foreign entities without a local permanent establishment may register directly for VAT under the non-resident VAT registration procedure and, for EU distance sales of digital services, under the OSS non-Union scheme.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies a distribution-based entity-level corporate income tax under Section 50 of the Income Tax Act: no tax on retained or reinvested profits, with tax triggered only upon profit distributions (20% regular rate, 14% qualifying regular distribution rate, 25% for non-specified or high-risk distributions). Elections available include: (1) periodic 80% tax consolidation for resident parent-subsidiary groups, (2) VAT group registration election for closely related resident persons, (3) OSS/IOSS scheme election for cross-border B2C digital services, and (4) ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch neutrality election requiring timely documentation in the annual tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of annual taxable turnover (Section 7 Value Added Tax Act); foreign suppliers of e-services to Estonian consumers are deemed taxable in Estonia from the first euro unless covered by the OSS Union scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT on physical deliveries outside the EU/EFT if transported out of Estonia within the statutory period and evidenced by collective transport documents; intra-EU B2B dispatches zero-rated when the customer holds a valid destination-state VAT ID declared on a recapitulative statement. Services: virtual concert/digital metaverse services qualify for zero-rating only when the place of supply is outside the EU (business customer with non-EU establishment or private consumer outside EU); B2C sales to EU consumers are taxed at 22% through OSS or domestic Estonian VAT without zero-rating eligibility.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/EX1 and MRN receipt)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Estonia
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer record showing receipt of export consideration
  • Sales contract and CRM ticket-token ledger confirming the digital concert transaction
  • Proof of the customer's non-EU permanent establishment or billing address (for services)
  • Foreign customer VAT ID confirmation printout (for intra-EU B2B dispatches)

Estonia mandates electronic invoice receipt capability for all taxable persons (B2B and B2G), with mandatory PEPPOL BIS 3.0 e-invoicing for public-sector invoices. Real-time fiscal clearance is not imposed; instead MTA operates continuous transaction-level analytics through the e-MTA API (X-Road), the mandatory electronic registration of cash registers with fiscal mark encryption, and automated VAT return pre-fill. Monthly or quarterly VAT returns are submitted electronically via e-MTA, and inbound EU B2C digital tickets/e-vouchers are declared via the OSS periodic return.

MTA may re-characterise zero-rated exports as taxable domestic supplies, claw back the 22% VAT together with interest at the statutory tax surcharge rate (currently 0.03% per day) and impose a late-payment penalty. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute a criminal tax offence under the Penal Code Section 397-2, punishable by fines up to EUR 400,000 or imprisonment up to five years for significant amounts; tax fraud declarations also trigger retrospective adjustment of all prior VAT periods, withdrawal of OSS eligibility, and publication of the sanction under MTA's compliance risk scheme.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia, as a Eurozone member, maintains full current and capital account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU). There are no bespoke FX controls, no restrictions on profit repatriation, and no capital gains lock-up periods. Dividends from an Osaühing are taxed at the time of distribution at the standard corporate income tax rate (20/25% with relief), and the withholding tax framework is fully aligned with EU directives. The only compliance gates are statistical reporting to Eesti Pank (Balance of Payments) and the general anti-money-laundering framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. Export receivables may be held in foreign currency, but the commercial accounts must be maintained in EUR under the Accounting Act. All proceeds should be reported via the ESTA (Estonian Tax and Customs Board) and Eesti Pank's direct investment and current account surveys if thresholds are met.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) and the European Central Bank (Eurosysteem)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating with an Estonian branch or cross-border passport, such as Monese, Payhawk, and Selected, can provide ancillary payment accounts, virtual IBANs, and multicurrency wallets. However, treasury-heavy operations with substantial cross-border ticket settlement and USDC-to-fiat conversion are better served by traditional banks. Offshore digital EMIs with no EEA passport are generally not legally viable for onboarding an Estonian OÜ, and their accounts will not be treated as compliant bank references for licensing or investor due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections should be transmitted as SWIFT MT103 (for foreign currency) or SEPA CT (for EUR). The payment narrative must reference the company registry code and the purpose code corresponding to equity subscription under Eesti Pank's Balance of Payments classification (typically code 528 for direct investment equity or code 529 for reinvested earnings). A signed share subscription agreement, an updated commercial register extract, and a share capital confirmation from the management board must be delivered to the receiving bank. If USDC is the source of funds, the coins must be converted to EUR through a licensed VASP and then transferred via SEPA to the operational account; the bank demand the VASP transaction history and a source-of-funds breakdown to prove the final fiat remitter.Outbound trade settlements for virtual event production, artist payments, metaverse software licensing, and distribution should primarily use SWIFT MT103, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer, or Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) where a bank guarantee is required. Correspondent banking is facilitated through SWIFT's network via LHV, Swedbank, and SEB, with TARGET2 used for instant euro settlement. Multilateral netting is recommended to minimise cross-border fees.
