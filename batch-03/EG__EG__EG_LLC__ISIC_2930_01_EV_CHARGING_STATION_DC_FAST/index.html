Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - the national revenue authority of the Arab Republic of Egypt under the Ministry of Finance; official e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipt platforms: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg and https://ereceipt.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - an Egyptian LLC is a resident corporate taxpayer under Law No. 91 of 2005, taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the standard 22.5% corporate rate; no pass-through eligibility exists; distributions are not deductible; the entity is subject to full CIT, salary tax, withholding tax, and VAT compliance; special territorial or tax-exempt treatment is not automatic and requires a qualifying investment license under Law No. 72 of 2017 or registration in an approved free zone / investment zone / SCZone regime.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and has no hybrid-mismatch entity election. The principal statutory elections available are: (1) optional VAT registration for annual taxable turnover between EGP 50,000 and EGP 500,000; (2) consolidated Egyptian corporate tax group filing for subsidiaries held directly or indirectly at least 95%; (3) recognition-pattern election for long-term construction contracts (percentage-of-completion vs completed-contract) as permitted by the Income Tax Law and its executive regulations; (4) selection of the general tax regime vs the incentivized investment/free-zone/SCZone regime under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 for eligible manufacturing entities; and (5) depreciation method elections within the ETA-prescribed fixed-asset depreciation schedules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is triggered when the value of taxable supplies exceeds EGP 500,000 during the preceding 12 months; optional or voluntary registration is available when annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 50,000; the registration application must be submitted to the ETA within 30 days after the mandatory threshold is crossed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating at 0% VAT applies to goods physically exported outside Egypt and to certain services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Egypt. The exporter must hold a valid VAT registration certificate; goods must exit Egyptian customs territory under a valid export customs declaration with ownership and risk transferred to the non-resident buyer; consideration must be paid in convertible foreign currency and repatriated through an Egyptian licensed bank; and the exporter must retain the full statutory file comprising customs, transport, contract, and inward remittance evidence. Input VAT attributable to exported goods and related overheads is reclaimable through monthly periodical returns via offset against output VAT or by direct cash refund after ETA verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export SAD / customs export entry) stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority - mandatory
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical exportation and destination - mandatory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-currency credit advice issued by an Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds - mandatory
  • Commercial export invoice and underlying sales contract or purchase order - supporting
  • Certificate of Origin where required by the destination market or financing bank - supporting
  • Input VAT tax invoices and supporting overhead documents matching the exported goods for the refund claim - supporting

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing under ETA Decision No. 646 of 2020, with e-receipt obligations for B2C retail sales. Registered taxpayers must use ETA-approved billing software integrated via ETA REST APIs, transmit every B2B invoice in the prescribed XML schema to the ETA e-invoicing platform for real-time validation and fiscal clearance before issuance to the buyer, and embed a unique ETA-issued UUID and QR code stamp on the final invoice PDF. Non-cleared, duplicate, or off-portal invoices are deemed invalid and trigger rejection, monetary fines, and ETA information-technology audits.

ETA performs desk reviews, field audits, and cross-verification of customs, bank, and e-invoicing data on every zero-rated export and VAT refund claim. Missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent documentation reverses the 0% rate to the standard 14% VAT on the full export value, denies the input VAT offset or refund, and opens the file to retrospective reassessment for up to 5 years (extended in fraud cases). Financial sanctions include monthly late-payment surcharges on the reassessed VAT, statutory civil fines of EGP 10,000-50,000 under Law No. 67 of 2016 for false declarations and documentation, removal from the authorized e-invoicing and export facilitation regimes, and criminal liability including imprisonment for fraudulent refund claims or forgery of export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a unified managed-float exchange rate regime (post-March 2024), yielding de jure and de facto convertibility on the current account (trade in goods/services, royalties, dividends to non-residents) with minimal documentary hurdles. The capital account remains segmented: full convertibility for portfolio inflows and FDI under GAFI registration, repatriation permitted upon bank confirmation of certified underlying transactions (audited financials, tax clearance), and residual administrative scrutiny for outbound payments with a value exceeding USD 100,000 per transaction. Profit remittances are lawful via banks using the 'Form 15 (Dividend Repatriation)' mechanism plus CBE-prescribed code 'D/L' declarations, but banks may impose operational delays during episodic USD liquidity constraints. Egypt imposes no general capital controls tax, yet all FX transactions must be routed through CBE-licensed banks; inter-company loans require CBE approval when tenor < 2 years or when creditor-in-substance is a related offshore entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE Circular (Implementation Rules of the Central Bank and Banking System Law) mandates full repatriation (no mandated surrender/conversion percentage) of export proceeds into an Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the bill of lading date (CBE Regulation No. 759/2017 as amended; CBE circular of Dec 2022 suspended forced conversion). However, per CBE Directive of March 2024 (eliminating the 100% surrender ratio), exporters retain full discretion to keep USD/foreign currency balances in Export Proceeds Accounts for up to 120 days, without default obligation to sell to the CBE. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline triggers bank-initiated CBE reporting, potential port/import blacklisting, and forfeiture of FX-dealing privileges; the LLC must file a detailed 'Export Proceeds Repatriation Statement (Form-Export/01)' quarterly for any shipment above USD 50,000.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, Foreign Exchange Regulatory Department; co-regulatory oversight by the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) for non-bank financial instruments and the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) for capital entry/exit recording.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW — Egyptian law (CBE Law No. 194/2020, Article 221 under banking exclusivity) prohibits offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer) from directly opening Egyptian corporate accounts or providing domestic payment services without a CBE payment-facilitator license and an Egyptian physical presence; local fintech licensing (e.g., via CBE's Regulatory Sandbox) exists but does not cover multi-jurisdictional treasury clearing. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (a) SWIFT MT103 wire from parent to the Egyptian operational account with payment purpose code 'GAFI-C01 (Equity Capital Contribution)' and reference to the serialized GAFI Investment Certificate; (b) entry via CBE's single secured ledger; (c) confirmation within 3-5 business days through the 'Egyptian FX Capital Entry System (FX-E)' after submission of Form (Capital/)GAFI-1 and the notarized capital deposit certificate from the receiving bank; (d) the LLC must separately file a 'Capital Entry Verification' with CBE and corresponding GAFI shares-issue registration, converting the inflow to issued share capital (minimum not required, but statutory requirement to register actual subscribed amount with GAFI). Prohibition: equity injection may NOT be received through an offshore account or a money-service business intermediary; the sole legal route is a direct wire to a CBE-licensed bank account held in the LLC's name.Standard clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 for non-LC operational payments (freight, spare-part imports, partially paid services, salaries above EGP threshold) routed through the corporate bank's direct custody and CBE-reportable F-Form; (b) SWIFT MT700 (Commercial Letter of Credit) for high-value component shipments from China/Germany — irrevocable, confirmed or unconfirmed by the Egyptian bank, with UCP600 governing, terms typically 60-120 days usance, payable against a full set of clean-on-board bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate-of-origin; (c) SWIFT MT202/COV for correspondent-bank interbank settlement during cross-BDC transfers; (d) Egypt-QIZ/GAFTA expedited payment rail for inputs sourced under qualified industrial zone rules. All outbound FX disbursements require: the CBE Unified Declaration Form (UD-1), underlying commercial contract, tax certificate confirming withholding tax on services, and, for USD > 10,000 per individual shipment, a bank discretion under 'import verification' requiring 6-month track record of PFI (Proforma Invoice) and customs release proof.Onshore deliverable EGP/USD FX Forwards (CBE-registered, tenor up to 180 days, available only against genuine trade transactions with documentary evidence; CBE Law 194/2020 permissions incorporated into authorized bank Treasury Standard, as reaffirmed by the March-2024 unpeg directive)Offshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) on EGP, permitted for Egyptian corporates only via international broker/legally equivalent contract with a licensed global bank (e.g., LSE-China bilateral deal); settlement reference to CBE-published central or interbank rate; no physical delivery in EGP; effective after the 2024 free-floating alignmentCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) between EGP and USD/EUR/CNY — permissible for long-tenor capital expenditure hedging of the EV-charger infrastructure, conducted onshore with a licensed Egyptian bank needing to demonstrate underlying public-project or capital commitment (rate-swap legs net-settled quarterly)Offshore-linked synthetic options (call/put spreads) subject to bank's internal collateral requirements; outright options are generally unavailable on EGP domesticallyCommodity-price downside hedges via handled exchange-traded futures (nickel/copper/aluminum) through international broker accounts (separate from CBE approval) — not classified as FX instruments but embedded in the treasury policy for raw-material cost protection

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW — Egyptian law (CBE Law No. 194/2020, Article 221 under banking exclusivity) prohibits offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer) from directly opening Egyptian corporate accounts or providing domestic payment services without a CBE payment-facilitator license and an Egyptian physical presence; local fintech licensing (e.g., via CBE's Regulatory Sandbox) exists but does not cover multi-jurisdictional treasury clearing. Offshore EMI accounts may be used purely as auxiliary receivables collectors for non-Egyptian buyers, but transferring proceeds into Egypt triggers full repatriation rules; compliance risk is HIGH if the LLC relies on such rails as its principal settlement channel. Recommended architecture: local CBE-licensed bank as the treasury spine; offshore EMI usage strictly limited to EU/UK collection with daily sweeps to the Egyptian operational account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: aggregate related-party transactions above EGP 10 million per tax year. Master File: group consolidated revenue above EGP 1 billion. Country-by-Country Report: consolidated group revenue of approximately EGP 18 billion (EUR 750 million equivalent). A TP Disclosure Form (Model 40) is mandatory with the Model 200 return whenever controlled transactions are present.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the ETA enforces anti-abuse rules under Article 30 of Law No. 91 of 2005, the OECD beneficial ownership test, and substance-over-form principles when granting Investment Law incentives, Free Zone/SEZ rates, or treaty WHT reductions. The EG_LLC must demonstrate physical manufacturing facilities, qualified employees, board decision-making, and genuine EV charger assembly operations in Egypt; a letterbox company cannot retain the 10% SEZ rate or treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Underreporting Income)

Statutory sanction: Fine of 2 to 5 times the evaded tax plus imprisonment of up to 5 years (Article 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005); automatic reassessment with 2% monthly delay surcharge and asset attachment.

Director personal liability: Court-ordered joint restitution of evaded tax and penalties, travel ban, disqualification from board service, and imprisonment of the de facto manager.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: ETA denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of payments as domestically sourced income taxed at 20% withholding, delay surcharges, and administrative fines up to EGP 5 million; commercial blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable as statutory withholding agents for unpaid WHT, with criminal fines, travel bans, and up to 5 years' imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA TP adjustment to the arm's length profit, 2% monthly surcharge on the additional tax, and fines of EGP 50,000 or more; default profit-rate estimation if documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives personally liable for non-filing of statutory TP schedules; penalty assessments may be collected from directors' personal assets in cases of gross negligence.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80 of 2002 (amended): fines up to EGP 2 million, confiscation of goods and proceeds, and imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for repeat offenders.

Director personal liability: Involved officers are personally prosecuted; travel bans, asset freezing, and criminal confiscation ordered by the National Anti-Money Laundering Council.
Fiscal Non-Filing and Payment Default

Statutory sanction: Late-filing fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000; 2% monthly late-payment surcharge; automatic ETA assessment and blocking of bank accounts and customs clearance.

Director personal liability: Travel ban imposed by court order when unpaid corporate tax debt exceeds EGP 100,000; criminal prosecution for flagrant and repeated non-compliance.
