Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); electronic tax services are provided through the e-MTA portal.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ receives a registry code upon entry in the Estonian Commercial Register; that code is used as its domestic tax identifier. A foreign company without an Estonian subsidiary must apply to the MTA for VAT registration where statutory registration grounds arise, generally using the prescribed application submitted through e-MTA or to the competent MTA service office. A foreign taxable person with no Estonian fixed establishment may be required to appoint an Estonian tax representative, subject to applicable EU and treaty rules; a representative is generally not required for a taxable person established in another EU Member State. VAT registration is generally effective from the date prescribed by the MTA and does not itself establish a permanent establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An Estonian OÜ receives a registry code upon entry in the Estonian Commercial Register; that code is used as its domestic tax identifier. A foreign company without an Estonian subsidiary must apply to the MTA for VAT registration where statutory registration grounds arise, generally using the prescribed application submitted through e-MTA or to the competent MTA service office. A foreign taxable person with no Estonian fixed establishment may be required to appoint an Estonian tax representative, subject to applicable EU and treaty rules; a representative is generally not required for a taxable person established in another EU Member State. VAT registration is generally effective from the date prescribed by the MTA and does not itself establish a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style check-the-box election for an OÜ. An OÜ is treated as a separate taxpayer; undistributed profits are generally not subject to corporate income tax, while distributed profits and specified deemed distributions are taxed at the company level. Estonia applies EU anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD, including rules addressing certain hybrid mismatches; these are statutory rules, not elective entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of relevant taxable supply arising in Estonia in a calendar year for an Estonia-established person. The threshold generally does not protect a non-established person making taxable supplies in Estonia; registration may be required from the first such supply, subject to specific statutory exceptions and reverse-charge rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate may apply to goods supplied and dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU, provided the supplier substantiates the export and satisfies the Estonian VAT Act and EU VAT requirements. For customs brokerage services, the VAT treatment depends on the customer, place-of-supply rules, and the service's direct connection, if any, to export transactions; a service is not automatically zero-rated merely because it relates to an export. B2B services supplied to a taxable customer outside Estonia may instead be outside Estonian VAT under the general place-of-supply rule. Input VAT recovery is subject to ordinary deduction, documentation, and business-use requirements.

  • Export customs declaration and evidence that the goods exited the EU customs territory, such as an electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and transport documentation, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, or CMR, where applicable
  • Evidence identifying the exporter, goods, consignee, destination, and link between the supply and the customs export
  • For a customs brokerage service, brokerage agreement, service invoice, customs mandate, and records substantiating the service and applicable place-of-supply or zero-rating basis
  • Bank payment or remittance evidence where relevant to corroborate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general statutory prerequisite for Estonian export zero-rating

Estonia does not generally require real-time VAT invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or universal transmission of domestic B2B invoices to the tax authority. Structured e-invoicing is required for invoices to public-sector entities under applicable rules, and businesses may exchange structured invoices through supported channels, including Peppol. VAT returns and related information are filed electronically through e-MTA; ordinary invoices must satisfy Estonian VAT Act requirements.

Estonia imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated to Estonia. Unsupported or incorrectly reported zero-rating may result in assessment of unpaid VAT, statutory interest, correction of VAT returns, and administrative or criminal penalties depending on culpability and severity. The MTA may deny input VAT deductions or reassess tax where records do not substantiate the supply, export, or service treatment. Deliberate tax fraud may trigger criminal liability. No fixed export-specific clawback period or automatic penalty rate applies independently of the generally applicable tax assessment, interest, and enforcement rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general exchange-control regime or routine restrictions on current-account or capital-account transfers. Payments and capital movements are subject to EU law, sanctions, anti-money-laundering requirements, bank screening, and ordinary tax and customs documentation. Dividends and other lawful distributions may generally be remitted abroad without exchange approval, subject to company-law solvency and distribution requirements, applicable Estonian tax on distributions, withholding obligations where applicable, and any relevant treaty or EU-law conditions. Banks may request evidence of source of funds, beneficial ownership, business purpose, and tax or customs documentation before processing higher-risk payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory deadline or mandatory conversion ratio requires Estonian exporters to repatriate or convert export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual terms, sanctions, AML controls, tax reporting, and any applicable customs or trade-finance documentation.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s central bank and participates in the Eurosystem. Estonia applies the European Union framework for payments, capital movements, sanctions, and financial supervision; the European Central Bank and the Estonian Financial Supervisory and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) have relevant supervisory roles. The Estonian Tax and Customs Board administers customs and tax procedures, not general foreign-exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine SEPA and supported international payments, subject to each provider’s onboarding, country, currency, and customs-sector policies. An EU-licensed electronic money institution may provide payment or e-money services under its authorization and passporting rights; it is not necessarily a bank and may not provide deposit protection, credit, cash services, or trade-finance products. An offshore digital EMI without relevant EU authorization or passporting rights should not be relied upon to provide regulated services in Estonia. Maintain a licensed bank account for customs guarantees, payroll, tax payments, and continuity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% of gross distributed profit (levied on the distributing resident company; economically equivalent to 25% on net cash dividend: tax = 20/80 × net distribution)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at shareholder level. The 20/80 distribution profit tax is a corporate-level charge: on a net dividend of EUR 80, the OÜ pays EUR 20 (i.e., 20% of gross economic value, 25% of net cash). For certain regular payments to Estonian resident individuals, the rate is 14/86 (14% of gross economic value, approximately 16.28% of net cash).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annex 5 must list the foreign beneficial owner, jurisdiction of tax residence, and any applicable Treaty/Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption. Do not withhold on the remittance; pay the 20/80 company-level tax. Late tax accrues 0.06% interest per calendar day (approximately 24% annualized). Intentional non-filing or false filing may lead to criminal fine and imprisonment up to 5 years for tax evasion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 250,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions per each associated enterprise, requiring a Local File; Master File is mandatory for Estonian-resident group entities whose ultimate parent group revenue exceeds EUR 50 million; low-risk simple transactions may be documented in a less detailed mini-file.

Economic substance scope: Estonia applies OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and EU ATAD; the distribution tax system is a preferential regime under EU Code of Conduct. However, genuine economic substance is a precondition for treaty benefits, VAT registration, customs brokerage licensing, and e-Residency use. The OÜ must have decision-making in Estonia, a physical seat/address, employees/contract agents handling clearance, and actual infrastructure for bonded transit. The Tax and Customs Board has authority under Tax Code §84 to disregard artificial arrangements where the main purpose or one of the main purposes is tax avoidance and recharacterize transactions per OECD International Tax compliance standards. Economic substance is required for the Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption and any DTA beneficial-owner claim.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the 20/80 corporate distribution tax, arrears interest at 0.06% per day (approximately 24% p.a.), fine up to EUR 1,600 per Tax Code; criminal penalty for tax fraud under Penal Code §344: fine up to 180 daily rates or imprisonment up to 5 years; additional disqualification from customs brokering license.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax (Tax Code §84); criminal conviction can lead to up to 5 years imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as a director in any Estonian legal person for up to 5 years; repatriation restrictions for non-resident directors under EU arrest warrant mechanism.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement / e-Residency Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board may disregard the legal form (substance-over-form), treat the entire income as immediately distributed taxable profit at 20/80, add 0.06% daily interest, and revoke the customs brokerage license under the Customs Act/EU UCC licensing criteria; penalty for establishment without economic activity under the Commercial Code can include compulsory strike-off and liquidation.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution to the Customs Board for duties and taxes evaded; if the company trades while insolvent or submits false e-residency declarations, directors are personally liable; potential criminal liability under Penal Code §345 (money laundering) with imprisonment up to 3 years; confiscation of goods and assets.
