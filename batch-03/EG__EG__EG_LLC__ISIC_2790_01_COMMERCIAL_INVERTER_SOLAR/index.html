Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the LLC is a resident corporate taxperson under Law 91 of 2005, taxed on worldwide income at the standard 22.5% CIT rate; no pass-through or check-the-box classification is available. The manufacturing site may alternatively qualify for Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment if licensed within the Suez Canal Economic Zone or a Free Zone under Investment Law 72 of 2017, in which case export-related profits may be exempted while non-resident shareholders remain taxed only on Egypt-source income. Territorial scope therefore follows the corporate residency of the LLC and the situs of the manufacturing establishment.

Available election forms

No entity-classification elections exist under the Egyptian CIT code. Available binding elections: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the statutory threshold to begin recovering input VAT; (ii) Free Zone or SCZone investment-incentive election, including the 50% investment-cost deduction for green manufacturing projects under Investment Law 72 of 2017 as amended; (iii) depreciation and capital-allowance method election, which is irrevocable once adopted in the first CIT filing; (iv) registration in the state export-support program conditioned on full foreign-currency repatriation; and (v) hybrid-mismatch adjustment and documentation elections introduced by Law 30 of 2023 amending Law 91 of 2005, subject to economic substance and the general anti-avoidance rule.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover in Egypt for mandatory VAT registration (Law 67/2016, Art. 14); voluntary registration below the threshold is available for prospective exporters seeking input-VAT recovery; imported services trigger the reverse-charge obligation for the Egyptian recipient irrespective of the foreign supplier's turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of commercial grid-tie solar inverters and microinverters qualifies for the 0% VAT rate when (i) the goods physically leave Egypt under a customs export declaration, (ii) the invoice is issued as a zero-rated e-invoice stamped by the ETA, (iii) title and risk pass to the foreign buyer outside Egypt, and (iv) the exported value is received in foreign currency and repatriated through the Egyptian banking system. Directly connected international freight, handling, warranty, and offshore after-sales services, as well as supplies to licensed free zones and the Suez Canal Economic Zone, are also zero-rated. Input VAT incurred on production, materials, and logistics is fully creditable; where zero-rated exports exceed taxable outputs, the exporter claims a cash refund on the periodic VAT filing supported by the export documentation bundle.

  • Original stamped customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / Bayan) issued by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing the onboard shipment date and the foreign consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration and the ETA-stamped zero-rated e-invoice
  • Foreign currency inward remittance credit advice (SWIFT transfer entry or Inward Remittance Certificate) from the Egyptian bank
  • Copy of the zero-rated e-invoice bearing the ETA digital stamp and the 0% VAT product code

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing for B2B transactions and e-receipts for B2C transactions under ETA board decrees, phased by turnover tier and fully applicable to Egyptian manufacturers and exporters of solar power electronics. Taxpayers must connect an ERP or web portal to the ETA e-invoice clearance API, submit each invoice in the authorized XML or JSON fiscal schema, and obtain ETA validation with an encrypted QR digital stamp affixed to the invoice; invoices lacking the ETA stamp are non-compliant, disallow the buyer's input-VAT deduction, and carry administrative penalties. Digital-stamp verification is supported by the ETA Kareem verification channel, and electronic and fiscal invoice archives must be retained for five years.

The ETA review window extends five years over zero-rated export files and refund claims. Non-substantiation, sham or circular transactions, overstatement of FOB value, missing outward customs exit, or repatriation outside the Central Bank of Egypt statutory window exposes the exporter to: revocation of the 0% rate and reassessment at 14% VAT on the full invoice value, recovery of refunded input VAT with default interest, administrative fines up to 100% of the tax evaded and up to EGP 100,000 per violation set under Law 67 of 2016, delisting from the tax card and exporter support registries, joint personal liability of directors and officers, and criminal tax-evasion prosecution with imprisonment; penalties under the Customs Law No. 207 of 2020 apply concurrently for false export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP operates under a flexible, market-determined exchange-rate regime restored on 6 March 2024 under the IMF Extended Fund Facility; interbank USD/EGP rates are set by authorized dealing banks and published daily. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted for trade in goods and services: an authorized bank may sell FX against a CBE-prescribed documentation package (invoice, contract, customs declaration) without seeking CBE pre-approval. Capital-account convertibility remains controlled: equity injections must be registered with GAFI and notified to the CBE; intercompany loan principal and interest require CBE debt registration to secure future repatriation; portfolio flows are permitted only through regulated channels. Profit and dividend remittance from an Egyptian LLC is legally permissible once audited financial statements are filed, the corporate tax return and withholding declarations are settled, an Egyptian tax clearance certificate for the distribution year is obtained, and the bank's outward-remittance form records a valid CBE purpose category. In practice, remittance is executed against available bank FX liquidity and may be queued during periods of EGP tightness, so treasury must plan dividend payments around monthly interbank liquidity windows and give priority to trade settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be repatriated and credited to the exporting LLC's account at a CBE-licensed bank within the CBE-prescribed trade-settlement period, currently applied by Egyptian banks as 90 days from the customs export date (bill-of-lading or airway-bill date) under the CBE's FX settlement circulars implementing Law No. 194 of 2020. The mandatory conversion ratio is now 0%: the temporary CBE circular regime in force from 2022 to early 2024, which required exporters to surrender 100% of proceeds into EGP, was abolished by the 6 March 2024 flexible-rate reform. Exporters may therefore retain USD/EUR balances, sell to the bank at the interbank rate, or apply the proceeds against approved import and current payments. Each export receipt must still be supported by the customs export declaration filed through the National Single Window (NAFEZA), the commercial invoice, the payment order, and the bank's FX transaction form for CBE balance-of-payments reporting. Late repatriation without a CBE-accepted excuse restricts the exporter's own import FX access, triggers a higher AML risk rating, and may attract penalties under the Banking Law.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary and foreign-exchange regulator under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and its Executive Regulations. The Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU) operates under the CBE umbrella for AML/CTF supervision, while the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) administers foreign-direct-investment registration. The CBE issues binding FX circulars, authorizes licensed dealing banks, and collects balance-of-payments data on all cross-border flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

NOT VIABLE as a primary in-country treasury rail. Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves deposit-taking, FX dealing, and payment-system access to CBE-licensed banks and authorized payment institutions. Offshore electronic-money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud and similar) cannot maintain EGP accounts, issue Egyptian IBANs, clear domestic payments, or act as an approved intermediary for the LLC's export proceeds. Their acceptable use is limited to (i) collecting customer remittances in USD/EUR abroad and (ii) sweeping those funds by SWIFT MT103 into the CBE-licensed corporate account. All supplier settlements, statutory conversions, and CBE reporting must pass through the Egyptian banking system; routing any operational disbursement through an offshore EMI would be treated as a suspicious third-party clearing pattern and would trigger an EMLCU filing by the Egyptian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) before any transfer, register the foreign direct investment with GAFI and match the investment certificate to the commercial-registry capital amount; (2) instruct the foreign shareholder to wire USD/EUR via SWIFT MT103 using the CBE purpose category for inward direct investment — equity capital, placing in free-text field 72 the Egyptian Tax Identification Number, the commercial-registration number, the GAFI file reference, and the caption 'capital deposit of the Egypt LLC'; (3) upon receipt, the Egyptian bank executes the mandatory CBE inward transfer notification, enters the transaction in the balance-of-payments reporting system, and issues the capital-deposit certificate required for licensing; (4) the deposited funds are then released from the blocked capital account after the commercial registry and tax card are updated. Intercompany shareholder loans must be supported by a signed loan agreement, CBE debt registration to secure future principal and interest repatriation, and an MT103 earmarked 'intercompany loan — related party' accompanied by the group's transfer-pricing file. Cash or third-party pass-through funding is prohibited; every injection must flow directly from the registered shareholder's own account to the LLC's CBE-licensed account.The LLC should use two complementary rails. Primary rail — a confirmed irrevocable documentary letter of credit at sight or with a 60–180 day tenor, issued by the Egyptian bank to the inverter-component supplier's bank via SWIFT MT700 and settled by MT103 reimbursement upon presentation of a compliant document set (commercial invoice, certificate of origin, packing list, transport document, inspection certificate, and insurance certificate); this satisfies the Swift_LC target rail. Secondary rail — direct SWIFT MT103 wire-transfer for low-value prepayments, spare parts, and service fees, permitted only after the bank has pre-vetted the proforma invoice, purchase order, and the import registration filed on the Customs Authority's NAFEZA/ACI platform with a valid ACID number. Import customs registration must precede shipment to avoid the statutory penalty of up to 100% of the goods value. Settlement currency is USD or EUR through the correspondent network (for example JPMorgan, HSBC, or Citi), and each disbursement must be recorded on the bank's outward FX form with the CBE import purpose category; supporting documents are retained for six years. For payments to related-party suppliers, an executed intercompany agreement and transfer-pricing memorandum must accompany the FX request.Outright deliverable USD/EGP FX forward contracts with a maximum maturity of 180 days, executed exclusively against an underlying commercial transaction (trade payables, export receivables, or repatriation exposure) at the CBE-licensed dealing bank; speculative un-backed positions are prohibited.Non-deliverable EGP forwards (NDFs) booked offshore with an approved non-resident counterparty to hedge structural EGP depreciation risk where onshore market liquidity is insufficient; settlement is effected in USD outside Egypt and does not require conversion of Egyptian pound balances.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (USD/EGP or EUR/USD) linked to an existing term-loan or equipment-financing facility, subject to the CBE derivative eligibility test and board approval, to align debt service with USD export revenue.Zero-cost collar or participating FX option structures offered by the relationship bank for trade exposures, contingent on written confirmation that the transaction qualifies under the CBE's derivative product regime governing options.Natural hedging by retention of export proceeds in USD/EUR current accounts and systematic set-off against import disbursements, which the CBE expressly permits after the 2024 liberalization and reduces reliance on exchange-traded or OTC derivatives.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: NOT VIABLE as a primary in-country treasury rail. Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves deposit-taking, FX dealing, and payment-system access to CBE-licensed banks and authorized payment institutions. Offshore electronic-money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud and similar) cannot maintain EGP accounts, issue Egyptian IBANs, clear domestic payments, or act as an approved intermediary for the LLC's export proceeds. Their acceptable use is limited to (i) collecting customer remittances in USD/EUR abroad and (ii) sweeping those funds by SWIFT MT103 into the CBE-licensed corporate account. All supplier settlements, statutory conversions, and CBE reporting must pass through the Egyptian banking system; routing any operational disbursement through an offshore EMI would be treated as a suspicious third-party clearing pattern and would trigger an EMLCU filing by the Egyptian bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8 million of controlled transactions per related party/transaction category triggers mandatory Local File; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 1 billion; CbC Report is due when group consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 10 billion (ETA Transfer Pricing Guidelines 2020, Decree No. 228 of 2020).

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); nevertheless, the ETA enforces the arm's-length standard, beneficial-ownership requirements, substance-over-form doctrine, and General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) in Law No. 91 of 2005. Incentives and treaty benefits will be denied where the LLC lacks physical plant, operational payroll, and genuine commercial functions; the solar inverter manufacturer must demonstrate real production activities, staff, and risk assumption in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 1 to 5 years and a fine equal to 100% to 200% of the evaded tax, plus monthly surcharges of 2% on underpaid amounts; seizure of funds and inventory; ETA suspension of tax card and sealing of production premises.

Director personal liability: Signing directors and effective managers are jointly and severally liable for the uncovered CIT and penalties; the ETA may pierce the corporate veil and impose pretrial detention, travel bans, asset attachment, and personal bankruptcy where the entity is a sham.
Shell Company / Fictitious Corporate Existence

Statutory sanction: ETA reclassification of the entity as a sham, disallowance of incentive and holiday claims, retrospective CIT assessment at 22.5% plus 2% monthly interest, additional 25% penalty on unreported tax, and potential forced deregistration from the Commercial Register.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners have the corporate veil pierced for unpaid tax and penalties; travel bans, freezing orders of personal bank accounts, and disqualification from board positions follow under Egyptian execution law.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment adding the notional profit to the taxable base, 25% penalty on the under-reported tax, 2% monthly late-payment interest on the deficiency, and disallowance of the corresponding deductions; the ETA may also levy fines of up to EGP 500,000 for documentation failures.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany pricing declarations without contemporaneous substantiation may be personally joint and several for the evasion-style penalty; repeated violations trigger criminal prosecution and personal travel restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering & Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines of EGP 50,000 to EGP 200,000 under Law No. 80 of 2002 (amended), imprisonment of responsible officers for 1 to 3 years, freezing of the LLC's bank balances by the Economic and Financial Crimes Unit, and seizure of trade assets related to solar inverter transactions.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and nominee directors are criminally liable and may face compound forfeiture of proceeds plus imprisonment; travel bans and asset-confiscation orders may be executed pending trial, and the corporate veil is disregarded for true beneficial ownership.
