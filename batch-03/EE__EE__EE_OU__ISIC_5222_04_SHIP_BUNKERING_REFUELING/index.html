Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet), an agency of the Ministry of Finance; primary digital portal: e-MTA (https://e-mta.emta.ee); corporate registration executed in the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) with automatic issuance of the national tax number. Fuel excise and customs supervision for marine bunkering is administered by the same Board, including the excise exemption regime for energy products supplied to vessels in international service under the Alcohol, Tobacco, Fuel and Electricity Excise Duty Act (ATKEAS).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident foreign entity / newly incorporated OÜ: (1) incorporate the OÜ in the e-Business Register using an e-Residency digital ID or notary; the 8-digit registry code issued at incorporation is the permanent TIN for corporate income tax, withholding tax and statistical identifiers; (2) register for VAT via Form K submitted electronically through the e-MTA portal; the VAT number EE + 9 digits is issued normally within 5–10 business days; (3) where the foreign shareholder has no fixed establishment in Estonia, appoint a domestic VAT representative under the VAT Act to guarantee tax liabilities; (4) obtain the excise licence/registration for the storage and supply of mineral fuel and apply for the excise duty exemption certificate for international-voyage bunkers (ATKEAS); (5) where bunkering is performed within a customs free zone (e.g., Muuga, Sillamäe, Paldiski South Harbour), register the operator status with the Tax and Customs Board and maintain operating records under the Union Customs Code. All procedures are performed fully online without physical appearance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — an Estonian OÜ is a tax-resident corporate entity under the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus) operating under the distributed-profits tax system: retained and reinvested profits are exempt from corporate income tax; tax arises on distributions and deemed profit distributions at 20/80 (25% effective on net amount) and at the preferential 14/86 (≈16.3% effective) for regular dividends meeting statutory conditions. Territorial scope: worldwide income may be attributed to the Estonian resident entity, while the timing of taxation is deferred to distribution; participation-exemption and treaty rules apply to foreign permanent establishments and foreign dividends. Pass_Through_Eligible is not available under Estonian law; CFC, interest-limitation and hybrid-mismatch rules transposing ATAD apply mandatorily. Customs free zones do not confer corporate income tax exemption, only relief from customs duties and VAT/CIT neutrality in limited circulation scenarios. — Non-resident foreign entity / newly incorporated OÜ: (1) incorporate the OÜ in the e-Business Register using an e-Residency digital ID or notary; the 8-digit registry code issued at incorporation is the permanent TIN for corporate income tax, withholding tax and statistical identifiers; (2) register for VAT via Form K submitted electronically through the e-MTA portal; the VAT number EE + 9 digits is issued normally within 5–10 business days; (3) where the foreign shareholder has no fixed establishment in Estonia, appoint a domestic VAT representative under the VAT Act to guarantee tax liabilities; (4) obtain the excise licence/registration for the storage and supply of mineral fuel and apply for the excise duty exemption certificate for international-voyage bunkers (ATKEAS); (5) where bunkering is performed within a customs free zone (e.g., Muuga, Sillamäe, Paldiski South Harbour), register the operator status with the Tax and Customs Board and maintain operating records under the Union Customs Code. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Estonian OÜ is a tax-resident corporate entity under the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus) operating under the distributed-profits tax system: retained and reinvested profits are exempt from corporate income tax; tax arises on distributions and deemed profit distributions at 20/80 (25% effective on net amount) and at the preferential 14/86 (≈16.3% effective) for regular dividends meeting statutory conditions. Territorial scope: worldwide income may be attributed to the Estonian resident entity, while the timing of taxation is deferred to distribution; participation-exemption and treaty rules apply to foreign permanent establishments and foreign dividends. Pass_Through_Eligible is not available under Estonian law; CFC, interest-limitation and hybrid-mismatch rules transposing ATAD apply mandatorily. Customs free zones do not confer corporate income tax exemption, only relief from customs duties and VAT/CIT neutrality in limited circulation scenarios.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; entity classification is statutory. Available mechanisms: (1) election to apply the 14/86 preferential rate on regular dividend distributions (compared to the arithmetic average of dividends distributed in the prior three years, subject to statutory ceilings); (2) VAT group election under the VAT Act, permitting closely connected Estonian entities to be treated as a single taxable person for VAT purposes; (3) voluntary VAT registration below the €40,000 threshold to recover input VAT on pre-operational expenditure, recommended for export-oriented bunkering operations; (4) advance binding rulings from the Tax and Customs Board on the VAT/excise characterisation of bunkering supplies and zero-rating eligibility; (5) no elective hybrid-mismatch shelters — ATAD anti-hybrid rules are compulsory and prevail over domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year for Estonian-resident taxable persons; mandatory registration from the first euro for non-resident entities and for any person supplying excise goods, including marine fuel; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold and is commercially essential for bunkering businesses to recover input VAT on fuel purchases. Supplies within a customs free zone may be treated as outside the territorial scope of VAT, subject to customs warehouse/free-zone procedures.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Two independent zero-rating grounds apply to marine bunkering: (A) Export of goods — 0% VAT where bunker fuel is transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU, evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration; (B) Supplies for international maritime transport — 0% VAT for fuel, lubricants and ship stores supplied to vessels engaged in international navigation, including ocean-going ships departing an Estonian port on a foreign-going voyage with the bunkered fuel on board. Supplies to vessels operating exclusively in Estonian territorial or internal waters, or between Estonian/non-EU-only ports without an international character, remain standard-rated at 20%. The corresponding excise duty exemption for energy products supplied as stores for international-voyage vessels applies under ATKEAS. The supplier bears the burden of proving the physical export or the foreign-going voyage through contemporaneous, consistent documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic EX declaration lodged via the national Customs Declaration System/ECS under the Union Customs Code)
  • Bill of Lading (or sea waybill / through bill of lading evidencing discharge at a non-EU port)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103/202 payment evidence from the foreign charterer/buyer within the contractual term)
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) under MARPOL Annex VI stating fuel type, quantity, IMO number and supplier licence data
  • Port log / port clearance certificate confirming the vessel's physically documented departure from Estonian territorial waters to an international destination
  • Charter party / fixture note or voyage order establishing the international navigation character of the specific voyage
  • Commercial invoice and sale contract specifying export-oriented Incoterms (EXW/FOB/DAP) and the destination
  • Vessel tracking evidence (AIS data or harbour pilot log) retained for audit verification

Mandatory B2G e-invoicing via PEPPOL (EN 16931) since 2019; sectoral B2B e-invoicing mandates (construction, telecommunications, utilities) phased in from 2023, with a national rollout extending to general B2B e-invoicing. VAT returns (Form KMD), EC Sales Lists, Intrastat filings and periodic VAT summary data are submitted exclusively through the e-MTA portal or XML/JSON API; no real-time transaction-level fiscal clearance or digital stamping analogous to the Italian SDI model is currently in force. Operational cash-register data is transmitted in real time for B2C receipts, and customs declarations for bunker fuel entering or leaving free zones and customs warehouses are filed electronically via the national Customs Declaration System (NCTS/ECS).

The Tax and Customs Board conducts risk-based, sector-specific export audits for bunkering operators focused on four indicators: (i) physical movement of fuel (BDN, tank dipping, AIS track); (ii) commercial plausibility and arm's-length pricing; (iii) full receipt of export consideration from the foreign counterparty; and (iv) the vessel's actual departure beyond Estonian territorial waters without intervening domestic port calls. If zero-rating substantiation fails, the Tax and Customs Board retrospectively assesses VAT at 20% on the gross supply, applies an interest surcharge of 0.06% per day (approximately 21.9% per annum) running from the due date of the original return, and levies late-payment penalties of up to 2% of the assessed amount per month, capped at 30% of the tax amount. Negligent misdeclaration gives rise to a tax misdemeanour punishable by a fine of up to €1,200 for individuals and €4,800 for legal persons; deliberately false zero-rating claims or forged export documents constitute criminal tax fraud under the Penal Code and are punishable by up to five years' imprisonment together with criminal confiscation of the evaded tax. The parallel excise exemption for international-voyage bunkers is clawed back under the same interest and penalty structure whenever vessel tracking data contradict the declared international voyage.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies. Estonia adopted the euro on 1 January 2011 and maintains a completely liberalized capital and current account under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There is no separate national currency and no exchange-control clearance or central bank permit for foreign-currency or EUR transactions. Capital inflows, equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend remittances, trade payments, and loan repayments may be freely routed. Undistributed profits of the OÜ are not taxed until distribution, which supports treasury retention. Profit distributions are subject to the Estonian corporate income tax on distributions: the distributing OÜ pays 20% of the gross dividend (effective 20/80 on the net amount), with a reduced rate (14/86 net) available for regular dividends meeting statutory conditions, while dividends to EU/EEA resident corporate shareholders attract no further Estonian withholding. Dividends to non-resident individuals are generally subject to 20% withholding, subject to treaty relief. The only operative remittance hurdles are bank-level enhanced due diligence, sanctions screening, source-of-funds documentation, and the automatic balance-of-payments statistical reporting performed by the bank to Eesti Pank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. Estonian legislation imposes no statutory timeline, repatriation duty, surrender quota, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, including proceeds from marine bunker fuel sold to international shipping. Receipts may be retained in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in Estonia or offshore. The bank must report the underlying external transaction to Eesti Pank for balance-of-payments statistics and must apply standard anti-money laundering (AML), counter-terrorist financing (CFT), and sanctions screening, but no central bank permission or conversion requirement is applied to the export proceeds of ship bunkering operations.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), a constituent member of the Eurosystem within the European Central Bank and the national authority for payments, monetary operations, currency in circulation, and balance-of-payments statistics. Estonia has no national foreign exchange administration because the euro is the sole legal tender; exchange-control supervision is therefore embedded in Eurosystem law and Eesti Pank's prudential and sanctions remit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) in Estonia, including Estonian-licensed entities and EU/EEA EMIs passported under the Second Electronic Money Directive, can open electronic-money accounts remotely, which is attractive for e-Residency corporate structures. However, for ship bunkering and refueling, EMIs should be treated as auxiliary rather than primary treasury rails. Legal and commercial constraints include: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit or provide documentary trade finance instruments such as letters of credit or banker guarantees; (ii) most EMI payment acceptance policies classify petroleum product supply, marine fuel distribution, and cash-to-master transaction chains as high-risk and therefore block or severely cap transactions; (iii) correspondent clearing through EMI partner banks may fail for vessel counterparties in high-risk flag registries or for sanctioned-adjacent maritime routes; and (iv) standby letters of credit required by bunker suppliers cannot be issued by an EMI. A full-service commercial bank account remains mandatory for the Stage 4 liquidity and clearing structure.
