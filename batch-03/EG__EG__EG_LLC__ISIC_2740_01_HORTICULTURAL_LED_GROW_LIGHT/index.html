Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The statutory minimum paid-up share capital for an LLC is EGP 50,000 (approx. USD 1,600). The full capital amount must be subscribed by the founders at signing and paid into a temporary 'blocked' escrow bank account opened in the name of the company-in-formation at a bank licensed by the Central Bank of Egypt. The resulting Initial Bank Deposit Certificate must be produced to the Notary Public and to the Commercial Registry; the blocked funds are released to the operating corporate account only after the Commercial Registration Certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the General Administration of Civil Defense (Al-Himaya Al-Madaniya) verifying automatic fire suppression, emergency egress capacity, fire compartmentalization and storage flammability controls; a Structural Load and Building Compliance Certificate from a licensed engineering consultancy confirmed by the local municipal engineering department for the assembly-line equipment weight and electrical loads; and an Occupational Safety Clearance from the Manpower Inspectorate of the Ministry of Manpower and Immigration under Labour Law No. 12 of 2003, verifying electrical grounding, machine guarding, lockout-tagout procedures, soldering-station ventilation, harmonic lighting workplaces and emergency exit signage. These clearances are mandatory preconditions for the issuance and annual renewal of the IDA Industrial Operation License.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-determined managed float (fully liberalized since 6 March 2024), with the EGP trading against the USD at consolidated bank-quoted rates. Current-account convertibility is broadly unrestricted for bona fide trade in goods and services; capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, profit distribution, dividend repatriation) are permitted but are subject to documentary verification by an Egyptian authorized bank, issuance of internal foreign-exchange declarations, prior corporate and tax clearances, and registration of the investment certificate with GAFI and the CBE. Profit remittance hurdles are materially reduced but a residual USD liquidity risk and the requirement to present a valid tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, and a board resolution adopting the distribution remain the principal practical impediments. Fully offshore convertibility of EGP is denied; foreign exchange outside licensed banks is prohibited and carries criminal penalties under Law No. 194 of 2020.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulatory instructions (as applied subsequent to the March 2024 unification of the exchange rate), 100% of export proceeds arising from the manufacture of horticultural LED grow-light fixtures must be repatriated into Egypt through a licensed bank and credited to the exporter's designated export-receipt account; no percentage is permitted to be retained offshore. The statutory surrender window is generally 90 days from the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date; extensions up to 360 days may be granted by the exporter's bank on documented commercial terms (deferred payment letters of credit or open-account terms). Proceeds received in a foreign currency may be (i) converted into EGP at prevailing interbank rates, or (ii) held in a foreign-currency account, but the exporter must present the customs export declaration, shipping documents, and CBE form when surrendering. Failure to repatriate within the approved period exposes the entity to non-tariff penalties, bank de-risking, and loss of access to foreign-currency facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) jointly with the Egyptian Money Laundering Combating Unit (EMLCU) and the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Digital cross-border EMIs (including Airwallex and similar platforms) are not independently licensed in Egypt as banks or payment institutions. They may function only as non-resident invoicing and payout service providers through CBE-licensed sponsor banks or as collection-side FX aggregation tools for receivables received abroad; the in-country payroll, statutory VAT/Tax Payment, social insurance contributions, and export-proceeds surrender obligations must be discharged through a domestic Egyptian bank account. No EMI may open a direct local account in EGP or process CBE-regulated inward remittances without a licensed Egyptian banking partner, and the CBE's Retail Payment Services and Electronic Money Regulations (issued under Law No. 194 of 2020) impose licensing prerequisites for payment initiation, account information, and digital wallet services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign capital (equity or shareholder loan) must be wired via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the Egyptian bank's foreign-currency account, denominated ordinarily in USD or EUR, with the beneficiary account classified under the capital-escrow or foreign-investment account scheme. The remittance must carry the sender's bank reference with the purpose-of-payment field stating 'Capital Contribution / Investment Capital' or 'Shareholder Loan', and the Egyptian receiving bank requires a post-receipt inward-transfer declaration, a board resolution of the Egyptian LLC subscribing the shares, the GAFI investment registration certificate or free-zone license, the company's Commercial Registry (CR) extract, Tax Card, and a CBE exchange-control form executed by the authorized bank officer. For shareholder loans, the bank will additionally require a signed shareholder loan agreement with an arm's-length interest rate, a repayment schedule, and confirmation that the loan is subordinated to the bank's own facilities where credit exposure is sought. Funds must not be commingled with operational collections prior to conversion into EGP.Trade settlements for outbound disbursements (component imports and payments to foreign distribution partners) are executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 straight transfer for open-account or advance payments, provided full underlying trade documentation and a CBE import-form are submitted, (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit issued in favor of foreign suppliers (SWIFT MT700), (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A), and (iv) low-value global payouts via licensed sponsor-bank-integrated fintech rails such as Airwallex exclusively for foreign currency balances held offshore and not requiring EGP conversion. For imports above the CBE threshold, the importer bank must verify the purchase order, proforma invoice, pricing benchmark, shipping terms, and customs declaration; advance payments exceeding statutory percentages require enhanced due diligence and sometimes bank-level authorization. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (Law No. 91 of 2005, Article 58); the distributing Egyptian LLC must withhold the 10% dividend distribution tax from amounts payable to both resident and non-resident shareholders, with no exemption for non-residents absent a ratified tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final return Form No. 35 is due within four months following the end of the tax year (fiscal year). Quarterly estimated returns Form No. 34, together with advance tax payments, are due within one month after the close of each of the first three quarters.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the aggregate annual value of related-party transactions exceeds EGP 15 million. Master File: mandatory where total related-party transactions exceed EGP 30 million or the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds EGP 100 million per fiscal year. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR): required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EGP 18 billion, aligned with the OECD's EUR 750 million filing threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply standalone Economic Substance Regulations modeled on the UAE/IFC regime; however, the ETA enforces an equivalent standard through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR), the beneficial ownership requirement under tax treaty provisions, and the arm's length principle under Article 30 of Law No. 91 of 2005 and Law No. 1 of 2022. A manufacturing LLC claiming the SCZone tax holiday or treaty benefits must maintain actual business premises in the zone, employ the relevant workforce, undertake real production of horticultural LED fixtures, and retain full records of its economic activities. Transactions with a related party that has no substantive business are disregarded, and the associated income is re-attributed to the Egyptian taxpayer with late-payment surcharges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Article 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005 and Law No. 1 of 2022: imprisonment for a term of up to five years and/or a fine of up to EGP 1,000,000; civil assessment of the full evaded tax plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge capped at 100% of the tax; confiscation of falsified documents and assets derived from the evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false returns or direct book-keeping manipulation become jointly and severally liable for the civil tax debt, face criminal conviction, a travel ban, and freezing of personal bank accounts, and may be barred from serving on any Egyptian company board for up to five years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA disregards the entity for tax purposes; denial of all tax holidays and treaty benefits; re-assessment on a deemed profit basis at 22.5% with 2% monthly delay surcharge; potential administrative strike-off from the Commercial Register; referral to the Public Prosecutor for operating a sham company.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil allows the ETA and creditors to attach the directors' personal assets; the Anti-Money Laundering Unit may impose travel bans and asset freezes for the duration of the criminal investigation; additional liability for fraud against minority shareholders and foreign tax authorities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer pricing adjustment under the arm's length principle using CUP/TNMM, involuntary assessment of additional taxable income at 22.5%, application of a 2% monthly delay surcharge, and a documentation penalty of up to 1% of the relevant related-party transaction value for failure to produce a Local/Master File; repeat offenders face criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the non-arm's length pricing are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; travel bans are routinely applied when the ETA issues a criminal tax evasion referral; professional director disqualification may be imposed by the Egyptian Financial Regulatory Authority.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Dividend / Royalty / Interest WHT

Statutory sanction: The un-withheld tax is immediately assessed against the distributing entity; a monthly delay surcharge of 2% on the un-remitted WHT (capped at 100%) and an additional administrative fine under Law No. 1 of 2022 ranging from EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000; the ETA may also charge the entire obligation against the company's bank accounts by executive order without a court judgment.

Director personal liability: Withheld taxes retain a public-fund character; directors who divert or fail to remit them are personally liable for the full amount and may be prosecuted for misuse of public funds, leading to imprisonment, seizure of personal assets, and a strict travel ban pending full repayment.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach / Structuring of Export Proceeds

Statutory sanction: Money laundering felony under Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended): imprisonment for 3 to 7 years and a fine of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000; seizure of all funds and assets related to the offense; mandatory license revocation for the company; possible blacklisting by Egyptian banks and loss of access to the SWIFT system in Egypt.

Director personal liability: Directors who opened or controlled the foreign shell accounts are criminally liable as principals; they face cumulative sentences, full confiscation of personal and corporate assets, automatic passport revocation, and an international travel ban; extradition may be requested from the foreign jurisdiction hosting the intermediary account.
