Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital since the 2011 amendment of Section 136 - an Osaühing may be incorporated with EUR 0 or EUR 1 share capital; no escrow account or notarial capital deposit is mandated by law. If capital is paid up, the contribution must be credited to a bank account before filing. In banking practice, deposit of at least EUR 2,500 is routinely expected for merchant acquiring and lease agreements.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: Rescue Board (Päästeamet) inspection and compliance with the Rescue Act (PäästeSeadus), including automatic fire alarm (SUJ), fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, and evacuation signage. Structural: the premises must hold a building Use and Operating Permit (kasutusluba) from the local municipality's building authority confirming structural load, electrical safety, and fire-resistance compliance. Occupational: registration of the restaurant workplace with the Labor Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus), mandatory risk assessment (riskianalüüs), staff HACCP food safety training certificates, and heat-stress and ventilation controls for kitchen workers.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital since the 2011 amendment of Section 136 - an Osaühing may be incorporated with EUR 0 or EUR 1 share capital; no escrow account or notarial capital deposit is mandated by law. If capital is paid up, the contribution must be credited to a bank account before filing. In banking practice, deposit of at least EUR 2,500 is routinely expected for merchant acquiring and lease agreements.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); online tax administration portal: e-MTA.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ is identified for tax administration by its Estonian Commercial Register code. To obtain a VAT number, submit a registration application to the MTA through e-MTA or an authorized representative, providing the company code, business activity, evidence of intended taxable activity, and requested registration date. The MTA generally decides within five business days and assigns an EE-prefixed VAT number if approved. A foreign company with no Estonian fixed establishment must register when liable for Estonian VAT; the domestic turnover threshold generally does not protect a non-established supplier making taxable supplies in Estonia. A supplier established outside the EU generally must appoint an MTA-approved fiscal representative unless the MTA grants an exception. A nonresident employer or other taxable person may also have separate employer or tax-register obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An OÜ is a separate taxpayer; Estonia has no general US-style check-the-box election to treat an OÜ as fiscally transparent. Corporate income tax is generally imposed when profits are distributed or otherwise treated as distributed, rather than on retained accounting profits. No general hybrid-mismatch election changes the OÜ's default classification; applicable anti-hybrid rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion under their statutory conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of relevant taxable supply arising in Estonia in a calendar year for a supplier established in Estonia. Non-established suppliers may be required to register from their first taxable supply in Estonia, subject to applicable reverse-charge, place-of-supply, and special-scheme rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A qualifying supply of goods transported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may be zero-rated if the supplier substantiates the export and satisfies the Estonian VAT Act. Supplies of goods dispatched to another EU Member State are governed by separate intra-Community supply conditions. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: VAT treatment depends on place-of-supply rules, customer status, and any applicable exemption. Restaurant and catering services are generally taxed where physically performed; meals supplied in Estonia are not converted into zero-rated exports merely because the customer or payer is foreign.

  • Export sales invoice and records identifying the goods, customer, and transaction
  • Customs export declaration and customs confirmation of exit from the European Union, including the MRN where applicable
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as a bill of lading, CMR, or air waybill
  • Proof of payment or other commercial correspondence where needed to corroborate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general Estonian statutory prerequisite

Estonia does not generally require real-time VAT invoice clearance, digital tax stamping, or tax-authority API validation for ordinary domestic B2B or B2C invoices. E-invoicing rules apply in specified contexts, including public-sector transactions; from 2025, a legal-person recipient may request structured e-invoices under applicable accounting legislation. Invoices and accounting records must satisfy statutory content, retention, and accessibility requirements. VAT returns are generally filed electronically through e-MTA.

Estonia imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. If export conditions or evidence are inadequate, the MTA may deny zero-rating, assess VAT, and charge statutory late-payment interest. False declarations, fabricated evidence, or improper VAT refunds may also result in administrative fines or criminal liability under applicable law. The MTA may audit records and recover incorrectly refunded or unpaid tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility. Estonia is a euro-area member state since 1 January 2011 and maintains complete current-account and capital-account openness under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital). There are no exchange controls, no FX approvals, no import/export payment licensing requirements on the Estonian side, and no statutory restriction on profit remittance from an Osaühing (OÜ). The only economically material hurdle is the Estonian distributed-profits tax regime: undistributed profits are tax-free, while dividends and other profit distributions are taxed at 20/80 of the net distribution (or 14/86 for regular dividend distributions under Tulumaksuseadus sections 50-52). For the overseas direct-operated hotpot restaurant, the host-country FX regime and local profit-remittance rules are the operative external constraints; Estonia imposes none. Practical approval barriers are therefore bank-side KYC, source-of-funds verification, aggregated cash-flow reconciliation for restaurant receipts, and transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border intercompany payments, rather than any statutory convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no statutory surrender ratio, no forced conversion into EUR, no repatriation deadline, and no special settlement account requirement for export proceeds. The OÜ may lawfully retain foreign-currency receipts (including intercompany royalties, condiment-supply invoices, management fees, and branch-profit sweeps) in any bank worldwide. The only compliance overlay is statistical: a resident enterprise must file Eesti Pank balance-of-payments returns when its cross-border transactions exceed EUR 50,000 in a single payment or aggregate above EUR 130,000 per reporting quarter, with correct BPM6-based purpose codes on each payment instruction. In addition, all outbound invoices (condiments, kitchen equipment, franchise royalties) must be priced at arm's length and supported by transfer-pricing documentation to avoid recharacterization as constructive dividends under the Estonian Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) - the national central bank within the Eurosystem, with foreign-exchange and monetary policy competence delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB). AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the integrated financial supervisor Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) and by the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) operating within the Police and Border Guard Board. Cross-border statistical surveillance and balance-of-payments reporting mandates are administered by Eesti Pank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a secondary multi-currency payment and FX-conversion rail, LOW as a standalone treasury spine. EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passport into Estonia under PSD2 and the E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Payhawk, and Estonian-licensed EMIs supervised by Finantsinspektsioon). The OÜ may open such an account remotely using the e-Residency card and digital signature. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot accept deposits beyond incidental e-money, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and cannot provide bank guarantees; (ii) e-money safeguarding does not carry the statutory deposit guarantee (Tagatisfond) protection available at a credit institution; (iii) high-volume restaurant POS/QR collections must be acquired through a host-country Tier-1 merchant bank; and (iv) EMI transaction-monitoring systems may freeze cross-border flows pending source-of-funds proof. Recommended architecture: the Tier-1 Estonian bank owns the statutory account, trade-finance, and FX hedging spine; the EMI is used solely for corridor FX conversion and small-value working-capital payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant funding path for an OÜ that directly operates an overseas hotpot restaurant: (1) Equity injection - remit from the shareholder via SWIFT MT103 (or TARGET2/SEPA where the shareholder is EU-based) crediting the OÜ operational account, with SWIFT Field 70 stating 'CAPITAL INJECTION - EQUITY - [commercial registry code]'. Estonia imposes no minimum paid-in capital, but a dedicated Capital Commitment (blocked) sub-account is advisable where the host-country business license requires proof of paid-in capital. (2) Shareholder loan - documented by a signed loan agreement, arm's-length interest (e.g., EURIBOR plus an OECD-compliant margin), subordination where applicable, and balance-of-payments classification as direct-investment loan (BPM6 DI category). (3) China-corridor injections require the Chinese investor to complete SAFE outbound FDI registration before remitting; the remitting bank will require the FDI certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH as a secondary multi-currency payment and FX-conversion rail, LOW as a standalone treasury spine. EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passport into Estonia under PSD2 and the E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Payhawk, and Estonian-licensed EMIs supervised by Finantsinspektsioon). The OÜ may open such an account remotely using the e-Residency card and digital signature. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot accept deposits beyond incidental e-money, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and cannot provide bank guarantees; (ii) e-money safeguarding does not carry the statutory deposit guarantee (Tagatisfond) protection available at a credit institution; (iii) high-volume restaurant POS/QR collections must be acquired through a host-country Tier-1 merchant bank; and (iv) EMI transaction-monitoring systems may freeze cross-border flows pending source-of-funds proof. Recommended architecture: the Tier-1 Estonian bank owns the statutory account, trade-finance, and FX hedging spine; the EMI is used solely for corridor FX conversion and small-value working-capital payouts.
