Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — the single national agency administering income tax, VAT, stamp tax, e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance; official portal: www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipt system at invoicing.eta.gov.eg; incorporation and customs interfaces co-administered with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and Egypt Customs via the NAFEZA single-window.

E-government filing portal

For the local manufacturing LLC: (1) incorporate with GAFI and obtain the Commercial Register extract; (2) file the ETA tax-registration application (articles of association, commercial register certificate, board resolution, director IDs, registered-office lease) at the competent ETA office or via the ETA e-portal; (3) obtain the unified corporate TIN and the separate VAT registration number under Law 67/2016; (4) register in the ETA e-invoicing system and receive the ETA-issued PKI digital-signature certificate; (5) for exports, obtain the customs export code and activate ACID/NAFEZA export-declaration filing. A foreign entity without an Egyptian permanent establishment obtains no income TIN, but any non-resident supplier of taxable goods/services in Egypt or of digital services to Egyptian consumers must VAT-register online regardless of establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election; the LLC is compulsorily entity-level taxable under Law 91/2005. Statutory elections available: (i) consolidated group income-tax filing where a resident parent holds at least 51% of capital and voting rights for a full tax year (Art. 41, Law 91/2005); (ii) simplified turnover-based tax regime for qualifying micro/small enterprises (Law 152/2020); (iii) voluntary VAT registration before the EGP 500,000 mandatory threshold; (iv) special SCZone/free-zone tax regime election limited to newly zone-licensed entities, not available to a mainland LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration is triggered by EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods/services in Egypt on a preceding-12-months basis (Art. 23, Law 67/2016); non-resident suppliers of digital services and other cross-border taxable supplies are registrable irrespective of local turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available only to VAT-registered exporters and is document-driven. Goods: physical export from Egypt within the statutory shipment window evidenced by the final customs export declaration. Services: supplies to non-resident recipients wholly performed and consumed outside Egypt, including freight and ancillary services for exported goods. The exporter must issue a 0%-rated export e-invoice, retain the customs/shipping archive, and prove receipt of proceeds in an approved foreign currency through an authorized Egyptian bank or permitted inward transfer. Export zero-rating preserves input-VAT credit on attributable purchases, with excess credits refundable via the periodic VAT return and ETA refund verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (final Bayan Makhragi linked to the ACID/NAFEZA export filing)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit voucher proving receipt of export proceeds in an authorized Egyptian bank in approved foreign currency
  • Zero-rated export invoice issued through the ETA e-invoicing system with the EXPORT flag and validation token
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order and sales contract retained in the statutory archive

E-invoicing is mandatory for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be issued through the ETA Integrated E-Invoicing System in the ETA XML VAT-IRD/EIC v3.x profile via (a) direct REST API, (b) ETA user portal, or (c) EDI batch filing. Each invoice is digitally signed with an ETA-issued PKI certificate and, upon near-real-time validation, receives a unique GUID validation token and QR/2D barcode constituting the fiscal clearance stamp. Export invoices carry the EXPORT flag and the customs export-declaration reference. Under the e-receipt programme, B2C transactions require real-time clearance, with the ETA acceptance receipt obtained before or at the point of sale; invalid checksums, missing signatures, late submissions and non-conforming XML are rejected and generate audit flags, fines and suspension of e-invoicing status.

ETA risk engines cross-match export e-invoices against customs declarations (NAFEZA/ACID), Central Bank of Egypt FX-inflow data and VAT returns. Export zero-rated positions are retroactively examinable within the general 5-year assessment window; failed exports are recast as taxable local supplies at 14% VAT, triggering a 2% per month late-payment penalty (capped at 80% of assessed tax), a 30% administrative penalty on the tax difference for incorrect returns without intent, and a 100% penalty plus criminal referral for deliberate fraud involving forged export documentation or sham invoices. Additional Law 67/2016 fines for unregistered supply and e-invoicing non-compliance apply; exchange-control violations, including failure to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day window without CBE-approved extension, may revoke zero-rating approvals, suspend customs export privileges, and cause CBE administrative penalties and AML/CFT risk downgrades.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a durably flexible exchange-rate regime for the EGP following the March 2024 parity unification; the CBE intervenes only to counter disorderly market conditions, and no dual-rate or multiple-currency-practice restrictions remain in force. Current-account convertibility is substantially liberalized: trade proceeds, service fees, dividends, interest, and royalties are transferable after standard documentary vetting by an Authorized Dealer. Capital-account convertibility remains registration-based: inbound equity and shareholder loans must be booked through an Authorized Dealer under the CBE Investment Manual, creating a registered capital-inflow record that is the legal precondition for future profit remittance and capital repatriation. Profit-remittance hurdles for the EG LLC are: (i) CBE-registered capital-inflow evidence; (ii) audited financial statements and a shareholders' resolution declaring the dividend; (iii) an Egyptian Tax Authority clearance certificate confirming settlement of the 10% dividend withholding tax (treaty-reduced where eligible); (iv) an external auditor's confirmation of distributable reserves; and (v) submission of the bank's foreign-transfer application, processed by the CBE-approved bank within 5-10 working days. Post-float interbank depth has materially reduced the historical 6-12 month remittance queues, although availability of USD liquidity in the banking system remains the operating constraint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from Egyptian manufacturing operations must be received through an Authorized Dealer into a designated Export Proceeds Account within 5 working days of the remittance date; routing proceeds outside the banking system voids VAT zero-rating (export refund) and customs duty-drawback entitlements. Under the CBE's post-float instructions (Circular No. 18 of 2024 as subsequently amended), 50% of each export receipt must be converted into EGP at the prevailing interbank rate upon crediting; the remaining 50% may be retained in a foreign-currency account for bona fide current-account needs (component imports, royalties, foreign salaries) with a statutory 12-month retention window renewable upon evidence of trade-cycle continuity. The CBE export declaration (customs export form) must accompany the credit, and the bank-issued conversion certificate supports the exporter's EGP liquidity and VAT-export refund claims. Exporters that are simultaneously importers may offset a permitted portion of proceeds against their own import invoices (export-import offset) with prior CBE acknowledgment. For an entity licensed under SCZONE free-zone status, the 50% conversion obligation is superseded by the free-zone banking protocol, which permits 100% foreign-currency retention of export earnings provided settlement executes through a licensed zone bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary authority, banking supervisor, and foreign-exchange control regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and its Executive Regulations. FX operations, trade-payment vetting, and investment-inflow registration are executed exclusively through CBE-authorized banks (Authorized Dealers). GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) and SCZONE (General Authority for Suez Canal Economic Zone) interface for investment licensing; all FX operations outside licensed banks are prohibited under Egyptian law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low — not viable as a primary or standalone local banking rail. Egypt's banking-reservation provisions (Law No. 194 of 2020) confine deposit-taking, FX purchase/sale, and payment-account origination to CBE-licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, etc.) hold no Egyptian banking license, cannot issue EGP IBANs, and cannot serve as receiving banks for export proceeds, because the CBE export-surrender regime mandates settlement through an Authorized Dealer in Egypt. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder executes a board-approved equity contribution, records the capital increase in the Commercial Register, and instructs its bank to send SWIFT MT103 in USD or EUR to the EG LLC's Capital Escrow Account opened at the Authorized Dealer. The remittance-information field must carry the CBE purpose code for inbound foreign direct investment (equity) together with the LLC's commercial-registration and GAFI numbers. On receipt, the Authorized Dealer credits the account, executes FX conversion (if instructed) at the interbank rate, files the CBE investment-registration record through the bank's CBE corporate reporting portal, and issues the inward-investment registration notification — the legal precondition for subsequent dividend and exit-capital repatriation. For in-kind (equipment) contributions of laparoscopy-tower production lines, the value must be evidenced by a CBE-acceptable external-valuation report and Egyptian customs import documentation matching the GAFI-approved in-kind contribution schedule; the board resolution and amended commercial register must reflect the capital increase before the bank credits any excess contribution. All inbound funds must remain traceable through the EGP or FCY operational account to the project purposes stated in the founding certificate.Trade settlements for raw-material and component procurement (optics, camera modules, surgical instrument parts) clear through two approved rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for prepayments and open-account supplier payments, each accompanied by the CBE Import Declaration (Customs Form 4), the supplier invoice, and the Ministry of Health import license; and (ii) documentary Commercial Letters of Credit via SWIFT MT700/MT707 issued by the Egyptian dealer bank and advised or confirmed by a first-class overseas correspondent, typically 90-120-day usance, collateralized under the company's trade-finance facility. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are permissible for lower-value consignments. Outbound FX payments are constrained by the CBE's 'no payment before importation' rule: pre-import transfers require an advance-payment permit validated by the bank, and import settlements must correspond to customs-cleared values evidenced by the customs release document. EGP-denominated interbank transfers settle through the CBE's RTGS system; same-day treasury sweeps between the EGP operational account and the USD trade account are executed through the dealing bank's spot FX desk.Onshore deliverable FX forward (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP, tenors 1-12 months) contracted with an Authorized Dealer against documented trade exposure (import LC or export order) under standard Egypt Bankers' Association master-agreement terms.Onshore FX swap (spot versus forward, USD/EGP) used to align working-capital currency flows between EGP collections and USD import payments, executed under the dealing bank's FX swap line.Cross-currency interest rate swap (CCIRS) on medium-term equipment-finance facilities (USD or EUR debt swapped to EGP cash flows), offered by licensed dealer banks with CBE risk-management notification and board authorization.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) on EGP: not available onshore; residual EGP depreciation risk may be hedged by non-resident counterparties in the offshore NDF market, while the Egyptian entity itself should use onshore deliverable forwards to remain fully compliant with CBE regulatory requirements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low — not viable as a primary or standalone local banking rail. Egypt's banking-reservation provisions (Law No. 194 of 2020) confine deposit-taking, FX purchase/sale, and payment-account origination to CBE-licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, etc.) hold no Egyptian banking license, cannot issue EGP IBANs, and cannot serve as receiving banks for export proceeds, because the CBE export-surrender regime mandates settlement through an Authorized Dealer in Egypt. EMIs are complementary only: upstream supplier payments in the EU/US/Asia, low-value distributor disbursements, and USD/EUR liquidity required for non-Egypt invoicing. Collection of end-customer payments from Egyptian hospitals or importers through an EMI would breach CBE import-declaration and export-proceeds rules.
