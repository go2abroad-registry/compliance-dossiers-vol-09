Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoice and receipt portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg; customs single-window counterpart: NAFEZA (National Single Window for Foreign Trade).

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card (Bitaka Dhareebiya) from the Egyptian Tax Authority. The procedure begins after incorporation with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) by filing a unified tax registration application at the competent ETA Tax Office or through GAFI's One-Stop-Shop. Required documents include the commercial register extract, notarized and authenticated Memorandum/Articles of Association, board resolution appointing legal and tax representatives, power of attorney for the local tax representative, shareholders' identity documents (including passports for non-resident shareholders), office lease contract, and bank account opening details. After issuance of the TIN/Tax Card, the LLC must enroll in the ETA's mandatory electronic invoicing platform, obtain a separate VAT certificate under Law No. 67 of 2016 if taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold or upon voluntary registration, and register for customs exports through the ACID/NAFEZA system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None under Egypt's income tax law: there is no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch classification election for an Egyptian LLC; the entity is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a resident Egyptian company under Law No. 91 of 2005. Under the VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, low-turnover businesses may voluntarily elect VAT registration before reaching the mandatory threshold, and exporters must elect the zero-rated export treatment by submitting the full export documentation package; however, such documentary election does not change the entity's tax status. Special economic or free-zone incentives are granted only by express investment approval, not by unilateral election.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
14%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services for a resident Egyptian LLC; importers require VAT registration upon import regardless of threshold; non-resident suppliers without a fixed establishment in Egypt may be subject to reverse-charge mechanisms or mandatory VAT registration depending on the nature, place, and recipient of the supply.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Egyptian VAT Law zero-rates qualifying goods physically exported outside Egypt to a foreign destination, as well as closely connected services supplied for such exports. For surgical suture needle assembly, the LLC must be a registered VAT taxpayer and the direct exporting party named in the contract and customs declaration. The goods must be physically dispatched from Egypt through customs to the foreign buyer, title and risk must pass under Incoterms, and the sale proceeds must be received in foreign currency and credited to an Egyptian licensed bank account in accordance with Central Bank of Egypt repatriation rules. The zero-rated invoice must be issued through the ETA e-invoicing platform before shipment, and all supporting export documents must be contemporaneously preserved for ETA audit and VAT refund claim validation.

The ETA has made electronic invoicing (and phased e-receipts) mandatory for registered taxpayers. The LLC must integrate its billing/ERP system with the ETA e-invoicing infrastructure using approved API endpoints or certified JSON/XML connectors, issue invoices in real time before or at supply completion, transmit the invoice to the ETA for validation and clearance, and print or publish a QR-coded invoice with the ETA-issued acceptance identifier. Export invoices must be classified under the zero-rated export invoice type and linked to customs and foreign-currency remittance evidence; failure to pass ETA real-time validation suspends export VAT refund clearance and may trigger penalties.

      
The ETA reconciles export VAT claims with the customs NAFEZA/ACID declarations, the e-invoicing platform, and the Central Bank of Egypt foreign-currency receipts. Any inconsistency, missing document, or failure to repatriate proceeds within 180 days will cause the ETA to disallow the zero-rating, re-characterize the supply as taxable, and assess VAT at 14%, plus statutory late-payment additions and administrative fines under the VAT Law. Intentional false zero-rating claims, forged bills of lading, fake inward remittance certificates, or wrongful refund collection constitute tax evasion under Law No. 206/2020 and are subject to criminal penalties including imprisonment of the responsible officers, monetary fines, and full recovery of the refunded tax plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Egypt maintains a managed-floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility for genuine trade and service transactions. Capital-account convertibility is conditional: foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, intercompany loans and foreign-currency financing require registration with GAFI, CBE reporting and, in some cases, prior authorisation. Profit remittance by a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC is legally permissible after the entity obtains an audited annual financial statement, files the statutory corporate income tax return, secures a valid tax clearance certificate and presents proof of the original foreign-currency remittance into Egypt. Practical hurdles include periodic US-dollar liquidity stresses in the interbank market, CBE scrutiny of remittance purpose codes, real-estate/property fair-value documentation, translation/apostille requirements for shareholder corporate records, and a mandatory source-of-funds justification when the shareholder is an offshore holding company. Entities operating inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE) generally benefit from simplified net-foreign-currency-verification procedures and expedited profit repatriation, but the LLC in the ordinary Egyptian tax regime must still comply with standard CBE regulations.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
For an Egyptian LLC exporting surgical suture needle assemblies, the CBE requires that export proceeds be repatriated to an Egyptian licensed bank within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration, subject to a shorter 45-day window for certain strategic goods and a longer 180-day period only for projects expressly approved by the CBE. The mandatory conversion ratio is currently a minimum of 50% of the export proceeds: within five business days of the foreign-currency credit, the beneficiary bank must convert at least 50% into Egyptian pounds on behalf of the exporter. The remaining 50% may be retained in a permitted foreign-currency account for trade-related imports, commissions, freight, debt service, or public-interest uses. Exporters operating under SCZONE free-zone or a qualified free-zone certificate may apply to the CBE for a netting-based surrender arrangement, under which only 10% to 20% of total export proceeds must be converted, provided the zone authority confirms the annual net foreign-currency inflow position.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – the sole statutory regulator for currency issuance, foreign exchange policy, banking licensing, balance-of-payments reporting and cross-border payment systems under the Banking Law No. 194 of 2020. The CBE exercises its foreign-exchange supervisory authority through the Foreign Exchange Committee and coordinates with the Egyptian General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Egyptian Customs Authority for trade settlement compliance.

Low to Conditional. Offshore digital banking and electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex and similar non-bank PSPs cannot act as the primary settlement or capital-escrow system for the Egyptian LLC. Egyptian law only permits payment services to be rendered by CBE-licensed banks or CBE-licensed Microfinance/PayTech companies; a foreign EMI lacks a branch or representative office in Egypt and cannot file CBE trade receipts, issue Egyptian official receipts, or operate an Egyptian balance-of-payments purpose code. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        All inbound equity injections must be transferred via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COVER for substantial shareholder loans) to the LLC's Capital Escrow Account held at an Egyptian CBE-licensed bank. The remitting bank and the beneficiary bank must apply the correct Balance-of-Payments purpose code – typically 'EQ' (direct investment equity) for ordinary share capital and 'LD' or 'LC' for shareholder loans. The bank will require, before crediting any capital injection: an updated Egyptian commercial registry extract recording the share issue or capital increase; a notarised and Arabic-translated board resolution or shareholders' general meeting resolution; the GAFI/Investment Authority notification; the source-of-funds questionnaire; and, where the remittance exceeds the bank's internal limit, a three-year audited financial statement of the remitting shareholder. The account should not be used for operating trade transactions until the commercial registration is updated with the new paid-in capital; any use before the required administrative filing would create an AML audit exception. The escrowed amount should be kept in the original currency unless the bank is instructed to convert to EGP for working-capital injection; conversion requires a board resolution and a CBE-approved corporate bank relationship.Trade payments and supplier settlements for the surgical suture needle assembly operation should be cleared through the following rails: (i) direct T/T settlement via SWIFT MT103 against proforma invoice or commercial invoice; (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (MT700) for large raw-material purchases, especially for imported polyglycolic acid absorbable suture polymers or custom-blend stainless steel needles; and (iii) periodic board-approved intercompany settlements using MT103 with underlying service agreements. In all cases the bank will verify that the export/import transaction is supported by a valid sales contract, proforma/commercial invoice, packing list, customs declaration, bill of lading / air waybill, and, for LC issuance, collateral arrangements such as cash cover or a working-capital credit line. Any FX conversion for outbound payments is executed at the CBE interbank rate on the value date, and the bank may request a purpose-code declaration 'IP' for imports or 'PF' for services before the wire is released. Dividends to non-resident shareholders should be routed through an operational account after tax clearance and audited financial evidence are provided; the clearing rail is a standard MT103 wire to the foreign parent's bank.Outright USD/EGP forward contracts with CBE-licensed Egyptian banks, provided the underlying exposure is a documented trade receivable/payable, dividend forecast, or capital commitment of the LLC.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the official EGP rate in comparable jurisdictions, executed through a CBE-licensed bank acting as an intermediary, purely for hedge purposes and not for speculative trading.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP or EUR/EGP) with approved Egyptian banks for long-term balance-sheet hedging of foreign-currency borrowings or fixed-asset purchases.Plain-vanilla FX options with maturities not exceeding the underlying commercial exposure horizon, subject to bank product suitability and CBE negative-list restrictions on naked speculative transactions.Flexible forward structures (forward with optionality or window forwards) where the bank's treasury confirms the hedging is backed by commercial cash flows, capacity expansion capex, or future dividend exposure.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Conditional. Offshore digital banking and electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex and similar non-bank PSPs cannot act as the primary settlement or capital-escrow system for the Egyptian LLC. Egyptian law only permits payment services to be rendered by CBE-licensed banks or CBE-licensed Microfinance/PayTech companies; a foreign EMI lacks a branch or representative office in Egypt and cannot file CBE trade receipts, issue Egyptian official receipts, or operate an Egyptian balance-of-payments purpose code. Cross-border EMIs may be used only as auxiliary disbursement rails for low-value operational overheads, marketing subscriptions, or non-controlled ancillary procurement, provided the originating funds were lawfully obtained from a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account. For formal import settlement, export collection and statutory dividend payments, the LLC must use its Egyptian bank's correspondent SWIFT network.
