Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Estonia imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital before registration: an Osaühing may be incorporated online with share capital from EUR 0 to any amount; the market-standard reference is EUR 2,500. If the share capital is below EUR 2,500 upon formation, the OÜ must transfer at least one-fifth of its annual net profit to the legal reserve until share capital plus reserves reach EUR 2,500, and shareholder distributions are restricted until that threshold is met. No escrow account is required; subscriptions are credited to the company's payment account or a notary's deposit account, evidenced by a bank or notarial certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet / MTA) – e-MTA digital portal at emta.ee / e-maksuamet.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first secure an Estonian registry code by registering as a non-resident legal person or a branch in the Estonian Commercial Register (e-Äriregister), with e-Residency enabling fully remote establishment. For VAT purposes, the foreign entity applies to the MTA through e-MTA using Form KMD (VAT return) and the relevant registration application; the Tax Board issues a resident V.A.T. number or a non-resident VAT registration number under the 'EE' prefix. Registration is mandatory when the foreign entity makes taxable supplies in Estonia and either has no Estonian seat or performs services/electronically supplied services to Estonian private consumers in excess of the statutory threshold. A permanent establishment in Estonia also triggers local VAT and registry-code obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Osaühing (OÜ) is an entity-level taxpayer subject to Estonia's unique corporate income tax regime: undistributed annual profits are not taxed at the entity level; corporate income tax at 20/80 (effective 20% on gross distributions) is imposed upon profit distribution, deemed distributions, fringe benefits, gifts, and transfer-pricing adjustments. A reduced rate of 14/86 (effective 14%) applies to regularly distributed dividends paid out of prior taxed profits. Eligible foreign hybrid entities may nonetheless benefit from Estonian tax treaty and ATAD-driven hybrid mismatch rules, which disallow deduction for payments treated as deductible in Estonia but not taxable or treated as income in the counterparty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover (calendar year) for Estonian-resident taxable persons; non-resident suppliers of digital services to Estonian consumers must register when their intra-annual cross-border distance or electronically supplied services exceed EUR 10,000 (Union-wide threshold). Foreign businesses without an Estonian residence making any taxable supplies in Estonia are required to register from the first taxable supply, unless the supply is only to a VAT-registered Estonian counterparty who applies reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where goods are dispatched or transported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside the European Union, and the supplier retains documentary proof that the goods left the EU within 3 months. Goods supplied into another EU Member State are treated as zero-rated intra-Community supplies when the customer holds a valid VAT identification number in that Member State and the dispatcher files an EC Sales List. Services: export-related services (transport, ancillary services, processing, repair, and services directly connected to exportation) qualify for 0% when performed, and digital/telematics services in the logistics-SaaS scope are zero-rated only if supplied to a non-EU customer or, for business customers, subject to reverse charge in the EU jurisdiction of the customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX - Single Administrative Document / electronic customs declaration via ECS), including the MRV (Movement Reference Number) proving goods exited the EU
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (proof of transport to a destination outside Estonia and outside the EU), or CMR road transport consignment note for intra-EU container/truck movements where export occurs in another Member State
  • Commercial invoice or proforma invoice defining the goods, value, exporter, and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing that hard-currency export proceeds have been or will be repatriated and booked in Estonia
  • Optional but strongly recommended: customs transit documentation (NCTS), insurance certificate, packaging list, and attainment of IMDG/ADR compliance for logistics fleet equipment if applicable

Estonia does not operate per-transaction real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction reporting for standard B2B invoices. All VAT obligations are discharged through periodic e-MTA filings (Form KMD) and European Sales Lists / Intrastat data. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory under the European standard EN 16931 (e-invoice format as PEPPOL or equivalent) for invoices to Estonian public-sector bodies. For commercial B2B e-invoicing, PEPPOL-compliant or XML structured e-invoices are recommended and accepted; private-sector invoice exchange is bilateral. No digital stamping or pre-clearance of individual invoices is imposed by the Tax and Customs Board.

The MTA may retroactively deny a zero-rating claim if documentary substantiation is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or does not conclusively prove physical export within 3 months. Estonian tax law imposes a value-added tax surcharge at a rate of 0.06% per day on under-declared VAT in addition to interest, and a deliberate tax-fraud penalty of up to 30% of the under-declared amount (or 45% for repeat offences). Where fines and penalties are assessed jointly, the aggregate fiscal liability may reach the unpaid VAT itself plus penalty interest retroactive to the original due date. Criminal liability for intentional VAT evasion (including forged export documents, backdated bills of lading, or sham repatriation certificates) carries a potential fine, imprisonment, or both. The MTA coordinates with EU OLAF and national customs authorities to audit export records; fraudulent zero-rating claims also trigger the termination of the exporter's fixed-period customs authorization and loss of the right to apply the 0% rate for a subsequent period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under full EU and Eurozone convertibility regime. Capital account transactions are unrestricted for residents and non-residents. Current account items including profit remittances face no convertibility restrictions or hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted at prevailing market rates without any statutory timeline or conversion ratio obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank)
