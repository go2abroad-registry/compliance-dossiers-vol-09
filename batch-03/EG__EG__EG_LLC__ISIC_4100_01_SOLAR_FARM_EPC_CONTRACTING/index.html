Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; official e-services portal: www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must first register with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Commercial Registry to establish the LLC; thereafter apply to the ETA for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the e-filing portal. VAT registration is mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; application requires the commercial registration certificate, constitutional documents, proof of address, authorized signatories, and expected turnover declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entity must first register with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Commercial Registry to establish the LLC; thereafter apply to the ETA for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the e-filing portal. VAT registration is mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; application requires the commercial registration certificate, constitutional documents, proof of address, authorized signatories, and expected turnover declarations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are physically shipped out of Egypt within the prescribed period and the exporter retains documentary proof of shipment and CBE value. Export services (including certain offshore services) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident and consumed entirely outside Egypt, supported by a valid export contract, foreign-currency transfer, and ETA declaration. The sale must be evidenced by an invoice stating 'export' status and customs export documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Export Invoice
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Foreign Supply/Purchase Contract
  • Packing List

Egypt has implemented a mandatory electronic invoicing system administered by the ETA. Registered taxpayers generally issue electronic invoices through the ETA E-Invoicing Platform; large taxpayers with annual turnover exceeding EGP 50 million must transmit invoices in near-real time to the ETA server for validation and digital stamping. The system uses digital certificates and QR codes to ensure authenticity and integrity. Phasing is by ministerial decree.

Claiming zero-rating without lawful export will trigger a VAT clawback of 14% plus late interest, and may be classified as tax evasion under Law 67/2016. Penalties range from one to three times the tax legally due, plus imprisonment, and potential loss of tax registration status. ETA audits cross-check customs and CBE remittance records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime administered by the CBE. Current account transactions - trade in goods, trade-related services, project remittances, interest, and fees - are generally convertible through licensed banks provided the underlying transaction is supported by documentation required under the Egyptian exchange-control rules. Capital account transactions are not freely convertible: inward foreign direct investment must be registered with GAFI and CBE-approved banks to obtain repatriation entitlements; profit remittances and dividend distributions are permitted under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 once the company is tax-compliant, the annual statutory audits are filed, and the bank has verifiable evidence of the profit source. Practical hurdles include bank-level FX prioritisation, CBE clearance queues for larger remittances, and the need for end-beneficiary declarations, particularly when the remittance is for procurement from a non-OECD supplier. Egyptian EPC contractors should assume a minimum 7-15 business-day settlement latency for large outbound FX payments and should structure intercompany loans with CBE registration to preserve future recoverability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the current CBE consolidated guidelines, Egyptian exporters are required to repatriate the full invoice value of export proceeds into an Egyptian-licensed bank account through the formal banking system; no statutory percentage surrender or forced conversion to EGP is generally imposed. In practice, 100% of proceeds must be received within the payment term agreed in the underlying contract and must not remain outside Egypt; banks require the export's shipping documents, customs export declaration, and a signed exporter undertaking confirming that proceeds will be credited to the exporter's CBE-rated account before goods are released at destination. For solar equipment re-export or intercompany services, a CBE-compliant export invoice and transfer pricing file must accompany the receipt to avoid re-characterisation as a capital account flow. Mandatory conversion applies only to the settlement of EGP-denominated statutory obligations, such as taxes and customs duties, where the exporter chooses to use withheld FX, or when the exporter voluntarily converts to EGP under an initiative or facility granted by the CBE. The CBE retains the right to require temporary conversion ratios during balance-of-payments crises, but no fixed ratio is currently in force.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Capital & Monetary Sector, Foreign Exchange Control Department, acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 concerning the Central Bank and the Banking System, together with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for foreign investment registration and the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) for non-bank financial cross-border activity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital banks, electronic money institutions, and non-bank payment processors cannot legally maintain Egyptian-resident corporate accounts, issue local EGP routing numbers, or settle Egyptian tax and customs liabilities. They may be used only for incidental non-resident fee collection and pre-import project-management services; all project cash flows and statutory obligations must ultimately pass through a CBE-licensed commercial bank. The CBE does not recognise virtual IBANs or offshore EMI accounts as valid execution rails for export proceeds or import payments. Any fintech solution must operate as a pure information layer over the banking system, with the Egyptian LLC maintaining legal account ownership at a licensed bank.

Intercompany shareholder loans or capital increases funded from abroad, where a bank may classify the flow as suspicious round-tripping or attempted capital flight.

Root cause: Frequent cash withdrawals or third-party payment instructions from the Egyptian operating account to pay unbanked site labour or local suppliers, which is a common red flag for shell-labour or informal economy financing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders must be routed through the official banking channel using SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for larger transfers) with the field 70 statement clearly stating 'CAPITAL INCREASE - EGYPTIAN LLC - GAFI REGISTRATION [GAFI-file-number]'. The remitting bank must be a licensed bank outside Egypt and the receiving bank in Egypt must be identified in the CBE's approved list of banks. Prior to the transfer, the Egyptian LLC must file the relevant GAFI capital-increase statement and receive a CBE/GAFI tracking reference, which is to be quoted on the inward remittance statement. On receipt, the Egyptian bank will issue an inward remittance certificate ('CR certificate') identifying the transaction as foreign direct investment; this certificate is the legal foundation for future profit repatriation and capital refund. Shareholder loans must be structured as documented intercompany loans, with a signed facility agreement, market-based interest rate, and a repayment schedule; the loan must be reported to the CBE on the same FX declaration form. No cash deposits or third-party cheque deposits can replace the regulated wire route for foreign currency injections into an Egyptian entity.The LLC's treasury should make outbound trade disbursements primarily by SWIFT MT103 for subcontractor invoices, consulting fees, insurance premiums, and small-value supply contracts; the payment instruction must include the purpose of payment, the Egyptian import/export form number where applicable, and the supplier's full bank account details. For large-scale procurement of solar panels, inverters, and engineering equipment, the incumbent bank should issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (confirmed or unconfirmed as appropriate) using SWIFT MT700, with the beneficiary being the original equipment manufacturer or its first-tier authorised distributor; a commercial LC provides the seller with a bank obligation and permits the Egyptian EPC contractor to defer cash outflow while protecting the treasury against payment without delivery. For emergency emergency-part shipments, a cash-against-documents arrangement under the bank's tour-channel protocol may be used, but it must be supported by a bill of lading, inspection certificate, and customs import declaration. All outbound disbursements exceeding USD 250,000 must be pre-cleared by the bank's international division to ensure that the CBE's FX position is topped-up and that the remittance does not enter a queue. MT101 and locally generated formats are not recommended for cross-border settlements; the only acceptable execution rails for counterparties outside Egypt are SWIFT MT103, MT202, MT700, and, for urgent working-capital movements, cover transfers through the bank's nostro accounts.Deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts between the Egyptian LLC and a licensed Egyptian bank, permissible only to hedge genuine current-account cash flows (project milestone receipts in USD/EUR and imports of solar equipment); no speculative positions are allowed under CBE bank-limit rules.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/EGP executed offshore through the parent treasury or a banking counterparty, used because the EGP is not fully convertible offshore; the Egyptian LLC may enter into such NDFs only where the underlying exposure is documented and where the settlement does not involve onshore delivery of EGP.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) between the Egyptian LLC and a licensed international bank in the CBE-listed offshore financial centre, permitted for hedging the currency and interest-rate mismatch on project-finance borrowings; the swap must be aligned with the financing agreement's notional amount, maturity, and interest-recalculation index.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on gross dividends distributed by an Egyptian LLC to a non-resident shareholder. Distributions by an LLC to non-resident legal owners are treated as dividends under Article 60 of the Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the close of the fiscal year. For a taxpayer using the calendar year, the deadline is 30 April of the following year; for a fiscal-year company, the deadline is the last day of the fourth month following the fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 for aggregate controlled transactions in the fiscal year, which triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File; a Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 10,000,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting is additionally required when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 10,000,000,000 and the ultimate parent is resident in an ETA-recognised jurisdiction. Transactions below the threshold must still be documented in the taxpayer's working papers to support the arm's-length assertion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the Egyptian GAAR and beneficial-ownership judicial doctrine require actual, verifiable economic substance. A solar-farm EPC contracting vehicle must maintain a substantive office in Egypt, employ senior project-level management and engineering staff, issue milestone certificates, control procurement and subcontracted works, and hold financial accounts with Egyptian banks. Without such factors, the ETA may disregard the Egyptian LLC and allocate all EPC profits to the foreign parent, disallow incentive deductions, and refuse reduced-treaty WHT benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Business Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Imposition of a fine equal to 150% of the evaded corporate tax amount, payment of all due tax and delay tax at 1.5% per month, criminal imprisonment for a period of not less than three years, and confiscation of overseas bank accounts traceable to the evasion.

Director personal liability: All signatories and directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties. Civil restitution may be enforced against personal assets; foreign directors are subject to travel bans, international asset-seizure orders, and extradition arrangements under bilateral judicial cooperation treaties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may adjust the taxable income to the recommended arm's-length amount and impose an additional 30% penalty on the extra tax assessed. Continued non-production of required documents carries a daily surcharge of 0.5% of the tax due, up to the tax amount itself, and a fine not exceeding EGP 1,000,000 for the responsible officer.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial managers who sign the TP documentation and income tax return are personally liable for the accuracy of the supporting database. If the tax authority proves deliberate falsification, personal liability extends to unpaid tax, penalties, and criminal imprisonment for up to five years.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Under the Egyptian GAAR, the ETA may disregard the legal existence of the shell corporation, recharacterise the entire EPC income as income of the beneficial foreign parent, deny the claimed tax holiday, and impose a penalty of 100% of the underpaid tax. The investment licence is cancelled and the company struck off the commercial register.

Director personal liability: The veil of incorporation may be pierced to hold directors, shadow-owners, and shareholders personally liable for the entire tax liability plus penalties. Directors additionally face travel bans, entry bans in the Gulf and MENA region, and criminal prosecution for fraud in obtaining tax incentives.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The payer is jointly and severally liable for the entire withholding tax not remitted. A fine equal to 100% of the uncollected withholding amount is imposed, along with monthly delay tax of 1.5%, and the underlying payment becomes non-deductible for corporate income tax purposes. In criminal cases, imprisonment for up to five years may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors, finance officers, and legal representatives of the Egyptian LLC are personally liable as deemed collectors for the unremitted WHT. Personal bank accounts and assets may be seized, and an arrest warrant or travel ban may be issued pending full restitution.
