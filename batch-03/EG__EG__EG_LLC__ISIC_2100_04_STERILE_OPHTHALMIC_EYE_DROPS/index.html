Egyptian law does not prescribe a general minimum capital amount for an LLC. The founders must state the capital, quota ownership and payment arrangements in the constitutive documents. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian-resident LLC is a juridical person taxed at the entity level under Law No. 91/2005; no pass-through treatment exists. Standard CIT at 22.5% applies to worldwide income of resident companies, with a unilateral foreign tax credit for foreign-source income; branches of foreign companies are taxed on Egypt-source income only. A GAFI free-zone licence may substitute an annual export-linked fee for CIT/VAT where output is substantially exported.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Egyptian law. Available statutory elections: (i) free-zone investment licence election under Investment Law No. 72/2017 (annual fee instead of CIT/VAT, subject to GAFI approval and export-orientation criteria); (ii) tax-neutral merger/reorganisation election under Law No. 91/2005 with ETA pre-approval and carry-over of tax attributes; (iii) voluntary VAT registration below the mandatory threshold; (iv) election of accelerated fixed-asset depreciation on the first tax return under ETA-permitted methods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover triggers mandatory VAT registration; manufacturers, importers, and exporters must register regardless of turnover (the sterile ophthalmic manufacturer is therefore mandatorily VAT-registered). Voluntary registration is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods (including sterile ophthalmic eye drops manufactured under ISIC 2100) are zero-rated at 0% VAT when the registered exporter physically removes the goods from Egypt and receives the foreign-currency proceeds. Input VAT on raw materials, sterile packaging, blow-fill-seal consumables, water-for-injection systems, and qualifying production utilities is creditable and refundable through the ETA export-refund mechanism (statutory processing target: 45 days from a complete application). Exported services and certain international transport services are also eligible for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Shaka / C22) stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical shipment from an Egyptian port
  • Export Commercial Invoice matched to the ETA e-invoice carrying the 0% VAT code
  • CBE-certified Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate or bank account statement proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Sales contract and purchase order, plus EDA batch-release certificate for pharmaceutical exports

Mandatory phased e-invoicing and e-receipt regime (ETA rulings since 2020), fully extended to all VAT-registered taxpayers. B2B invoices are submitted in structured JSON/XML per the ETA schema; synchronous API-based clearance returns an acceptance UUID or a machine-readable rejection code; a PKI digital signature from the government-issued certificate is embedded in every invoice; B2C e-receipts must be transmitted in real time through the ETA e-receipt system; export documents must be correlated with the NAFEZA customs single window.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt jointly pre-audit export refund files; zero-rating and refunds are clawed back if export proceeds are not received within the six-month (180-day) CBE repatriation deadline. On clawback, VAT at 14% is recaptured with a late-payment addition of 1% per month (minimum EGP 500/month) under Article 107 of Law No. 67/2016. Fraudulent zero-rating — false customs declarations, forged remittance slips, inflated export values, or input-VAT abuse through shell supply chains — exposes the entity and its tax representative to fines of EGP 5,000–100,000, imprisonment of up to five years, exclusion from e-invoicing and export-support programmes, and AML enforcement under Law No. 80/2002, together with retrospective VAT and CIT assessments.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since March 2024, Egypt maintains a CBE-managed flexible exchange-rate regime backed by the IMF Extended Fund Facility; the Egyptian Pound (EGP) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (trade, services, profit and dividend remittances) through authorized banks at the market rate. Capital-account transactions remain subject to prior CBE authorization for certain outflows; non-resident equity capital invested under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 is registered with the General Authority for Investment and Foreign Trade (GAFI) to guarantee future repatriation rights. Profit remittance requires tax clearance and documented audited financial statements; no mandatory conversion-ratio restrictions on retained foreign-currency account balances apply to a licensed LLC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current CBE framework (post-March 2024), export proceeds must be repatriated into Egypt through authorized banking channels within 90 days of shipment date; 100% of the proceeds may be retained in an exporter's foreign-currency account (Account B or equivalent) and used for permitted import settlements, debt service, or conversion to EGP at the prevailing interbank rate. The earlier mandatory surrender requirement (historically up to 50% conversion) is no longer in force; documentary evidence (bill of lading, invoice, customs declaration ACID/ACI number) must accompany the credit entry for CBE statistical reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and the Foreign Exchange Regulations issued thereunder, with supplementary supervision by the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low — Egypt does not operate a fintech/EMI banking-license tier; non-bank payment service providers operate only as CBE-approved payment facilitators. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, etc.) cannot act as the primary settlement bank for corporate collections in Egypt, cannot open EGP accounts without a licensed local banking partner, and lack access to CBE clearing and the Egyptian interbank infrastructure. Viable complement: use an EMI solely as a downstream collection/concentration tool for low-value global pharmacy-retailer receipts, with periodic upstream settlement to the licensed CBE bank account via SWIFT. All statutory accounting, tax, trade-facilitation, and FX conversion must occur through Egyptian authorized banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must open the LLC's foreign-currency account (Account B type) with a CBE-licensed bank (recommend CIB/NBE) and transfer funds by SWIFT MT103 wire. The transfer must carry the CBE/BoP purpose code '621 — Foreign Direct Investment in Egypt — Equity Capital' (mapped to the GAFI registration number); a copy of the GAFI certificate of incorporation, capitalization table, and board resolution appointing the signatories must be filed with the receiving bank before credit is posted to the account. The CBE requires the receiving bank to issue a Foreign Investment Certificate (FIC) of the inflow for later-profit and capital repatriation. Immediately after credit, the account may be converted to EGP at the day's interbank central rate or kept for trade settlements; each conversion is recorded in the CBE's FX tracking system (NOST/EDDS). Any EGP deviation exceeding the bank's daily reporting threshold requires transaction narrative and underlying documentation.High-value cross-border disbursements for sterile packaging films, pump-occluders, and API/active-pharmaceutical-ingredient purchases shall settle via (a) SWIFT MT103 payment orders for prepayments below USD 50,000 or against invoice with up-to-90-day terms, and (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) issued on CBE-bank templates (ISBP/URR725) for larger consignments, routed as MT700/MT707. All import payments must be made through the LLC's CBE-licensed import register (Importer Account 'B') and accompanied by an EDA import permit for pharmaceutical components, the supplier's proforma invoice, and the customs ACID/ACI number; Egyptian banks have no mandatory LC quota, and cash-against-documents (CAD) settlement is permitted. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is mandatory when aggregate controlled transactions with related parties exceed EGP 10 million annually; a Master File becomes mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 100 million. CbC reporting thresholds mirror Egyptian OECD-compliant requirements.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies anti-avoidance and substance-over-form rules under the Income Tax Law and ETA practice. A foreign-owned LLC will be respected only if it has real presence, local management, personnel, production activity, bank accounts, business records, and risk retention in Egypt. Transactions routed through low-tax or shell entities without commercial purpose may be recharacterized under Egypt's general anti-abuse rule, equivalent to Economic Substance Regulations principles.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Payment of the additional tax plus a penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, criminal fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, tax-card suspension, and bank account attachment.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly participated in the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, subject to travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Defects

Statutory sanction: ETA recharacterization and adjustment of taxable profits to the arm's-length result, assessment of additional 22.5% CIT, 2% monthly late-payment surcharge, and penalties up to 100% of the neglected tax.

Director personal liability: The director or finance officer who signs false transfer-pricing declarations may be personally assessed, subjected to travel restrictions, and held responsible for unpaid withholding tax on cross-border payments.
Shell Company and Beneficial Ownership Shams

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity for tax purposes, denial of treaty and investment incentives, reassessment of the branch or permanent establishment at the full 22.5% CIT rate, and fines for under-declared profits.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and signatories can be personally liable for unpaid tax, disqualified from acting as directors, and hit with travel bans and asset freezes pending financial restitution.
AML, Economic Sanctions, and Corrupt Payment Violations

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Law No. 80/2002 with imprisonment up to 10 years and fines up to EGP 1,000,000 for money laundering; ETA may disallow the related expenses, freeze bank accounts, suspend the tax card, and cancel the commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors, the compliance officer, and beneficial owners may be prosecuted individually and face criminal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and personal liability for penalties imposed on the Egyptian LLC.
