Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is mandated for an LLC under the amended Companies Law; in practice, the declared share capital (customarily EGP 50,000 or more) must be fully subscribed and deposited in a bank account in Egypt pending registration, with a bank capital deposit certificate produced to the GAFI OSS.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate ('Shahadat Al-Amn Al-Sina'i') from the General Administration of Civil Defense; requires a building completion certificate, fire-alarm and extinguisher layout certified by an engineering office, gas-suppression plans for battery energy storage and EV charging areas, and workplace clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 and Ministerial Decree No. 211 of 2003.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is mandated for an LLC under the amended Companies Law; in practice, the declared share capital (customarily EGP 50,000 or more) must be fully subscribed and deposited in a bank account in Egypt pending registration, with a bank capital deposit certificate produced to the GAFI OSS.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; electronic services portal at www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign manufacturing entity must first complete commercial registration in Egypt (or obtain investment/Free Zone approval), then file an ETA Tax ID Card application (Form No. 1/260) with the Commercial Registry extract, power of attorney, notarized incorporation documents, board resolution appointing local fiscal representative, and proof of the registered Egyptian office. The ETA issues a 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) also serving as the VAT registration number. Foreign entities with no permanent establishment but carrying on taxable activities through a local branch or representative are equally required to obtain a TIN before consummating revenue-generating transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign manufacturing entity must first complete commercial registration in Egypt (or obtain investment/Free Zone approval), then file an ETA Tax ID Card application (Form No. 1/260) with the Commercial Registry extract, power of attorney, notarized incorporation documents, board resolution appointing local fiscal representative, and proof of the registered Egyptian office. The ETA issues a 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) also serving as the VAT registration number. Foreign entities with no permanent establishment but carrying on taxable activities through a local branch or representative are equally required to obtain a TIN before consummating revenue-generating transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box regime. LLCs are statutorily taxed as juridical persons at entity level. Available mechanisms include application for Free Zone company status (0% corporate tax on qualifying export activity) under Law 83 of 2002, election to use the investment incentives in Article 23 of Law 72 of 2017 (Importation and Exportation Law), and notification-based elections for tax groups under Law 91 of 2005. Hybrid mismatch restraint follows the OECD/G20 BEPS alignment as incorporated by Egyptian transfer pricing and thin capitalization rules, but no standalone check-the-box election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services (mandatory threshold); voluntary registration permitted below this threshold. Separate mandatory e-invoicing registration thresholds apply based on turnover size per ETA phased implementation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is available for: (i) physical goods physically exported concurrently with sale in accordance with the Import and Export Law and customs law, provided export occurs within the statutory time frame; and (ii) services provided to non-residents for direct benefit and consumption abroad. The supplier must be the exporter, retain an export customs declaration evidencing dispatch from Egyptian customs territory, and, for services, document the offshore recipient and actual use outside Egypt. Goods exported to Free Zones or final export within bonded warehouses may also be zero-rated when the full customs export cycle is completed. Zero-rating is not available for supplies used, consumed, or re-delivered inside Egypt.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (showing HS/ISIC code, Exported Value, exporter TIN)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill with Egyptian port of loading and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency through Egyptian banking channels
  • Commercial Invoice signed by exporter
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin (where applicable)

Egypt mandates near-real-time e-invoicing through the ETA centralized clearance platform. Registered entities above the phased threshold must issue invoices in XML/Audit File format via ETA-certified APIs or the online portal, obtain an ETA authentication token, and pass automatic validation before the invoice is legally acceptable. The platform generates a unique invoice UUID and QR code (at the line-entity level) enabling fiscal clearance and matching of declared output VAT. E-receipt mandates for B2C transactions follow a phased rollout; non-compliance blocks acceptance of the VAT return and attracts daily late-filing penalties.

The ETA and Customs Authority jointly scrutinize export claims through cross-matching customs ledgers, bank remittance records, and e-invoice outbox data. Fraudulent or falsely substantiated zero-rating triggers reassessment of VAT at 14% on the exported value, a minimum 30% additional tax penalty, interest at the statutory rate, and criminal sanctions up to imprisonment for tax evasion. Retrospective clawback extends to any VAT refund or credit reclaimed on the zero-rated supply, and the exporter may also face suspension from the e-invoice clearance system and revocation of the VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) is a partially convertible currency. Current-account transactions, including dividends, royalties, and trade-related payments, are generally free upon compliance with CBE anti-money-laundering and documentation rules. Capital-account transactions, such as equity injections, loans, and intercompany balances, require CBE/GAFI registration and are subject to liquidity-driven temporary approval gates. Profit remittance is permitted on the basis of audited financial statements and tax clearance, but in periods of FX shortage the CBE may ration hard currency and impose prioritization queues for manufacturing inputs, spare parts, and energy. The enterprise should structure all inbound investments as registered inward remittances with a clear 'investment purpose' code and maintain immutable documentation of original capital entry, retained earnings, and statutory reserves to facilitate fully unreserved exit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations and Executive Regulations of Law No. 194/2020, exporters must repatriate 100% of export proceeds to an Egyptian licensed bank within 180 days of the date of shipment for manufactured goods (extendable to 270 days with CBE approval for certain long-cycle capital equipment). The entire proceeds must be received in an acceptable convertible currency through the banking system. As part of the central bank's liquidity management, the CBE may impose mandatory conversion of 100% of such proceeds to EGP at the officially published rate; however, exporters that are registered manufacturers with outstanding FX import requirements may apply for retention in a foreign-currency account for up to a CBE-determined percentage of proceeds to fund documented raw-material and spare-part imports. Detailed shipping documents, collection advice, and CBE export-registration forms must evidence each shipment. Non-compliance triggers suspension of export registration, fines, and denial of future FX allocations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Foreign Exchange Department, Licensing and Supervision; General Authority for Investment (GAFI) for capital inflow registration and repatriation certification; Ministry of Trade and Industry for export-registration related trade finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for offshore digital EMIs. Egypt does not recognize offshore e-money institutions as regulated local deposit-taking or clearing entities, and CBE prohibits foreign EMIs from accessing the national payment and clearing rails without a CBE-issued license or bilateral arrangement with an Egyptian bank. Licensed local fintech providers (e.g., Paymob, MNT-Halan, and CBE-authorized payment aggregators) can support corporate collections within Egypt, but cannot function as a substitute for a licensed commercial bank account for LC issuance, FX conversion, or large-value SWIFT settlements. Offshore EMIs may be used downstream for vendor payments outside Egypt only if they are fully compliance-licenced in their own jurisdictions, but funds must first clear through an Egyptian onshore bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and shareholder-loan injections must be routed through a CBE-licensed Egyptian commercial bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages. Onboarding the remittance requires a completed CBE Form 27 (or the bank's e-Form equivalent), the FIRR (Foreign Investment Registration Receipt) issued by the receiving bank, and GAFI registration of the increase in issued capital. The beneficiary bank will perform a 'know your customer' review, source-of-funds verification, and sanctions screening before crediting the capital escrow account. Purpose code: '113 — Equity Capital Investment' for equity, '112 — Intercompany Trade Loans' for shareholder loans, and '1000 — Investment in Local Enterprises' as the general investment category. For sums above the CBE reporting threshold, the bank issues a certificate of inward remittance and will file the transaction with CBE's Balance of Payments system. The legal entity must preserve the FIRR, bank closing notices, and GAFI certificate to support future dividend repatriation and disinvestment proceeds.Trade settlements for imported battery components, chassis systems, and production machinery are cleared through SWIFT MT103 (direct documentary collection or open account) and commercial letters of credit issued by an Egyptian licensed bank, typically via SWIFT MT700. For LCs, the issuing bank transmits the credit to the beneficiary's advising bank and settles maturing obligations against conforming bills of lading, inspection certificates, and Origin-Specific Customs Extras. Obligor instructions must tag each payment with CBE purpose codes: '210 — Imports of Machinery and Equipment', '211 — Imports of Raw Materials', '212 — Spare Parts', and '300 — Services and Technical Fees'. Disbursements denominated in USD or EUR are executed at the CBE published or interbank rate; payments above the delegated limit may require a CBE import declaration (partial prior approval). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax under Article 56 of Income Tax Law 91/2005 on profit distributions made by an Egyptian resident LLC to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident; no CIT deduction is allowed for the distributed amount. A non-resident corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the LLC's capital may qualify for a reduced dividend rate under an applicable double tax agreement (commonly 5% to 10%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return must be filed within four (4) months after the close of the fiscal year (Income Tax Law 91/2005, Article 29); for a calendar-year LLC, the filing deadline is 30 April of the following year; the ETA may extend the deadline on justified application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and the related-party transaction disclosure schedule are mandatory when the aggregate annual value of the Egyptian entity's controlled transactions with non-resident and resident related parties exceeds EGP 16 million; a Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EGP 1 billion; a Country-by-Country Report is required when the group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EGP 4.5 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). All documentation must be contemporaneous and produced to the ETA within 30 days of a formal request, and the annual related-party disclosure schedule must accompany or be filed within 30 days after the CIT return.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not administer a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but economic substance is enforced through: (i) the general anti-avoidance rule in Article 30 of Law 91/2005 and the tax-avoidance decrees implementing BEPS, (ii) 'beneficial owner' and limitation-of-benefits style assessments under Egyptian double tax agreements, (iii) permanent-establishment attribution rules requiring premises, personnel, decision-making, and operational risk in Egypt, and (iv) GAFI and ETA powers to revoke licenses, tax cards, or Investment Law incentives if the LLC fails to commence or continue genuine manufacturing activity. An EV bus OEM is therefore expected to maintain a functioning production plant, employed workforce, inventory management, and substantive board oversight in Egypt rather than invoicing-only arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution for tax evasion with imprisonment of one to five years and fines ranging from EGP 5,000 to EGP 100,000 or up to double the evaded tax, plus recovery of the principal tax, a 3%-per-month additional tax capped at 100% of the tax due, cancellation of tax incentives, and publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: LLC managers and signatories are personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax where the fraud is attributable to their fault; the ETA may obtain court-ordered travel bans and asset freezes against delinquent directors, and managers who knowingly sign false returns face imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA-imposed transfer pricing adjustment to arm's-length profits, additional tax at 3% per month on the underpaid CIT capped at 100%, fixed administrative fines of EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000 for nondisclosure, recharacterization of mispriced payments as dividends or royalties subject to withholding, and loss of treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve artificial transfer prices may be jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax, withholding deficiencies, and penalties; the Egyptian commercial manager can be personally liable for the LLC's failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation and for obstructing ETA audits.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of double tax agreement benefits, recharacterization of payments as fully taxable Egyptian-source income with 10-20% withholding leakage, disallowance of expense deductions, application of the 3%-per-month additional tax to uncovered liabilities, revocation of the tax card and GAFI operating license, and mandatory liquidation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Under Companies Law 159/1981 and the Unified Tax Procedures Law, directors who orchestrate shell arrangements are personally accountable for the LLC's unpaid tax and withheld amounts; their personal assets and bank accounts can be attached, and travel bans may be imposed pending settlement.
AML Violation / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Anti-Money Laundering Law 80/2002: imprisonment of three to seven years and fines of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000, confiscation of laundered funds and the buses or proceeds involved, closure of the offending facility, and delisting from Egyptian import/export registries.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the beneficial owner face arrest, pre-trial detention, travel bans, and personal confiscation orders; legal representatives who fail to implement AML and know-your-customer controls across letter-of-credit and SWIFT payment rails are criminally liable alongside the company.
Withholding Tax Default / Unauthorized Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The full unwithheld amount becomes immediately collectible from the distributing LLC, plus a 3%-per-month late-payment tax capped at 100% of the amount, fixed penalties under Law 25/2020, and criminal prosecution of managers for deliberate non-deduction; unlawful distributions render the distribution void and recoverable by creditors.

Director personal liability: LLC managers are personally liable for unrecovered withholding taxes, face travel bans and asset freezes in ETA collection proceedings, and, where distributions were made while insolvent, owe restitution to the company and its creditors with potential disqualification from directorship.
