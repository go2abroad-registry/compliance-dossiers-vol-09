Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estland Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-MTA portal at https://emta.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with taxable presence in Estonia must register as a non-resident taxpayer with the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistr) and obtain an Estonian registry code (juridiline isikukood). For VAT purposes, a non-resident undertaking must file Form KR-2 through e-MTA or submit paper documentation to the MTA regional service office, including proof of incorporation, board member identification (passport/ID), and evidence of Estonian taxable activity. The MTA issues a VAT number (KMKR number) within 5–10 working days after verification. Non-resident entities may also appoint a fiscal representative, but this is not generally mandatory for EU entities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity with taxable presence in Estonia must register as a non-resident taxpayer with the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistr) and obtain an Estonian registry code (juridiline isikukood). For VAT purposes, a non-resident undertaking must file Form KR-2 through e-MTA or submit paper documentation to the MTA regional service office, including proof of incorporation, board member identification (passport/ID), and evidence of Estonian taxable activity. The MTA issues a VAT number (KMKR number) within 5–10 working days after verification. Non-resident entities may also appoint a fiscal representative, but this is not generally mandatory for EU entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Estonia does not offer check-the-box or similar entity classification elections. Corporate income tax (Tutimaks) is imposed at 20% (with reduced rates for certain distributions) only upon profit distribution; undistributed profits are not taxed. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply with no elective opt-out mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for domestic supplies; EUR 10,000 annual cross-border distance/e- commerce threshold under the EU Mini One Stop Scheme

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU are zero-rated if the exporter holds proof of dispatch to a third country. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated when the customer provides a valid EU VAT number and goods are dispatched within the EU. Electronically supplied services, including EMR SaaS, are zero-rated when supplied to non-taxable persons outside the EU, and services provided to a business customer outside the EU are outside the scope of Estonian VAT. Valid documentary evidence of place of supply and business status is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX/SAD)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch
  • Proof of VAT identification of the buyer (VAT number for intra-EU; tax identification for external customers)
  • Foreign bank statement or inward remittance certificate evidencing payment

No real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoice stamping mandate. VAT returns are filed electronically via e-MTA. E-invoicing is optional and based on EN 16931; for VAT refund substantiation, invoices must be preserved in a secure electronic form and made available to MTA upon request. No API access is obligatory for taxpayers; MTA invites integration via its open-data interfaces.

The MTA conducts post-clearance audits with retroactive examination. Fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger reassessment of VAT, interest at the statutory rate, a penalty of up to 10% of the assessed tax liability, and criminal liability for tax evasion in serious cases. False documented substantiation (e.g., forged export invoices or false VAT numbers) may result in total clawback, ₠100–₠640 fines for light breaches, and up to five years’ imprisonment for material fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under the EU full convertibility regime. Both capital and current accounts are unrestricted; no prior approval required for profit remittances or capital repatriation to non-resident shareholders. All FX transactions subject solely to standard AML/KYC and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Receipts may be credited directly to foreign currency accounts without conversion. No statutory timelines, ratios, or central bank requirements apply; voluntary surrender available but not obligatory.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs. EU PSD2 licensing, local payment institution registration, and AML obligations restrict remote digital wallet operations; local bank partnerships required for compliant liquidity and FX services.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The TSD for each calendar month must be submitted by the 10th day of the following month; the related corporate income tax (20/80 or 14/86 of net distribution) and any 7% recipient withholding tax are due and payable by the same date. The standard fiscal year is the calendar year; audited or unaudited annual accounts are filed with the e-Commercial Register within 6 months after the fiscal year-end, which serves as a de facto annual compliance checkpoint.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
TSD Annex 4, Line 1 - Gross profit distributions Total gross amount of dividends or deemed profit distributions approved and paid in the reporting month. The tax base is the net cash or in-kind benefit received by the shareholder, grossed up in accordance with Income Tax Act erga distribution rules. Each non-resident shareholder must be itemized by name, registry or personal ID code, country of tax residence, shareholding percentage, and date of acquisition. A valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) is required to apply any treaty-reduced rate at filing; absent a TRC, the statutory 20/80 rate must be applied and treaty relief claimed by refund. Late payment accrues penalty interest of 0.06% per calendar day. Late filing of the TSD attracts a per-form penalty of up to EUR 1,200 for legal entities plus a late-filing surcharge of up to 5% of the tax due. Failure to declare taxable distributions or the deliberate understatement of the gross distribution amount is treated as tax concealment and may result in increased tax assessments, fines of up to 100% of the under-declared tax, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud.
