Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-MTA online portal at https://www.emta.ee

        
E-government filing portal
Incorporation of an OÜ in the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) automatically issues the 8-digit registry code, which is simultaneously the legal person's tax reference number (TIN). For VAT, the OÜ must register in e-MTA once taxable turnover reaches EUR 40,000 in the current or any preceding 12-month period; a newly formed entity applies for VAT registration by the 15th day of the month following the trigger, and may voluntarily register earlier if it intends to recover input VAT or supply taxable B2B services. The Estonian VAT registration number has the format 'EE' followed by a 9-digit code derived from the registry code. Foreign entities without an Estonian seat or permanent establishment supplying taxable services or goods in Estonia must apply for non-resident VAT registration through e-MTA without any turnover threshold; however, pure B2B cross-border services may be reverse-charged to the Estonian customer, in which case no supplier registration is required. A foreign company with a taxable permanent establishment in Estonia must also register for corporate income tax under the Taxation Act and comply with e-MTA filing obligations.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (deferred-liability model): Estonian resident OÜs pay 0% corporate income tax on undistributed and reinvested profits; income tax is triggered upon distribution at rates of 20/80 (20% effective on gross) for ordinary dividends and 14/86 (14% effective on gross) for regular distributions satisfying the statutory three-year distribution pattern. Immediate 20/80 taxation also applies to fringe benefits, gifts, representation expenses, and non-business-related payments. Territorial scope: resident companies are subject to Estonian taxing jurisdiction on worldwide income, but the 0% undistributed-profit rate permanently defers entity-level taxation until profit extraction. — Incorporation of an OÜ in the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) automatically issues the 8-digit registry code, which is simultaneously the legal person's tax reference number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation (deferred-liability model): Estonian resident OÜs pay 0% corporate income tax on undistributed and reinvested profits; income tax is triggered upon distribution at rates of 20/80 (20% effective on gross) for ordinary dividends and 14/86 (14% effective on gross) for regular distributions satisfying the statutory three-year distribution pattern. Immediate 20/80 taxation also applies to fringe benefits, gifts, representation expenses, and non-business-related payments. Territorial scope: resident companies are subject to Estonian taxing jurisdiction on worldwide income, but the 0% undistributed-profit rate permanently defers entity-level taxation until profit extraction.

        
Available election forms
Estonia does not operate a check-the-box, fiscal unity, or hybrid-mismatch election regime. Relevant statutory elections available to an OÜ: (i) voluntary VAT registration under §3(2) of the VAT Act even below the EUR 40,000 threshold; (ii) election to use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for intra-Community distance sales and certain B2C services; (iii) election of the 14/86 regular-distribution rate for qualifying dividends; (iv) no group taxation election exists — each OÜ is assessed independently. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are implemented through the Tax Information Exchange Act and do not create elective relief for mismatches.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
20%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 40,000 taxable turnover during the current or preceding 12-month period for resident taxable persons; no threshold for non-resident suppliers (registration required before making any supply taxable in Estonia); EUR 10,000 intra-Community distance-selling threshold triggering Estonian VAT unless declared through the EU OSS. For the anti-dumping defense consulting service line, most cross-border B2B engagements fall under reverse charge at the recipient's establishment and do not count toward the Estonian threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT when the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of supply, evidenced by a customs export declaration (SAD/MRN), a transport document, and the commercial invoice. Intra-EU B2B supplies of goods are zero-rated if the customer's valid VAT number is obtained and the goods are dispatched to another Member State with VIES declaration. For services (ISIC 7490 trade-remedy and anti-dumping defense consulting), the place-of-supply regime determines zero rating: B2B services supplied to a taxable person outside Estonia are taxable in the customer's country under reverse charge, producing effectively 0% Estonian output VAT. To substantiate this, the OÜ must hold the customer's VAT registration number (EU) or a certificate of taxable-status/tax residence (third country), the signed professional-services engagement contract, itemised time/fee and performance records (e.g., Commission hearing attendance, written submissions, case files), and a bank receipt of cross-border payment. B2C services supplied to non-taxable foreign recipients are generally taxable in Estonia at 20% unless the services are actually used and enjoyed outside the EU, which requires documentary proof of performance location; legal consulting for anti-dumping proceedings in Brussels may be treated as performed at the customer's seat or at the place of effective use, but MTA will apply substance-over-form scrutiny.

No real-time fiscal clearance, pre-approval, digital stamping, or mandatory API transaction reporting applies in Estonia. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G invoices (PEPPOL / EN 16931 format, effective since 30 March 2019); B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary and is facilitated through the PEPPOL network. VAT returns are filed electronically via e-MTA by the 20th day of the month following the tax period; customs export declarations are processed through the EU ECS and e-MTA systems. The MTA operates post-audit control rather than continuous transaction clearance.

      
MTA audit limitation period is 3 years from the tax declaration deadline, extended to 5 years (and up to 7 years in suspected fraud) for under-declared VAT and zero-rating claims. If export zero-rating is disallowed, MTA will assess output VAT at 20% on the gross consideration, add late-payment interest of 0.06% per calendar day (approximately 21.9% per annum) from the original due date, and may impose a fine of up to EUR 32,000 on the company and EUR 1,200 on individual officers for tax offences. Intentional submission of false export documentation or fraudulent zero-rating triggers criminal liability under Penal Code §4081 (tax fraud, imprisonment up to 5 years) and unlimited retrospective clawback unaffected by the 3-year statute. Estonia imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is retained solely as a due-diligence anchor because receivables outstanding beyond 180 days weaken the payment-evidence chain and increase the risk of MTA substance-over-form challenge and retrospective VAT assessment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Full and unrestricted convertibility. Estonia has adopted the euro as legal tender since 1 January 2011 and maintains no exchange-control legislation or central-bank approval regime for foreign-currency transactions. Free movement of capital and payments is guaranteed under Articles 63-65 TFEU and implemented domestically without residual restrictions for current-account or capital-account operations, including profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, loan servicing, and acquisition of foreign exchange by resident companies. The OÜ may hold EUR and foreign-currency accounts freely and convert receipts without declaration except for physical cash movements equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000, which trigger a customs/AML declaration duty, and standard balance-of-payments reporting obligations borne by the banking counterparty. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is subject solely to Estonia's distribution-based corporate income tax: 20/80 of the net dividend (i.e., 20% gross) at distribution, reducible to 14/80 for regular distributions within prescribed limits, and zero in practice for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU), provided the beneficial-ownership and substance conditions are met; no central-bank permission or FX license is required for such outbound dividend payments.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None applicable. Estonia imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no compulsory conversion ratio, and no mandated timeline for export proceeds or service income. As a euro-area member fully integrated into EU capital-flow liberalization, the Republic neither obliges legal-defense consulting enterprises (ISIC 7490) to repatriate foreign-currency earnings nor prescribes conversion into EUR. Service fees for anti-dumping trade-defense work received in USD, GBP, or other convertible currencies may be retained in hard-currency accounts indefinitely. The sole residual compliance obligations are (a) cash-declaration at borders for sums at or above EUR 10,000 per person per movement, (b) sanctions and embargo screening of counterparties under EU and domestic law, and (c) monthly balance-of-payments reporting by the servicing credit institution to Eesti Pank, in which the enterprise does not participate directly.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the central bank and monetary authority of the Republic of Estonia, operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; prudential supervision of credit institutions is exercised jointly with the Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) under the unified supervision framework.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium. Estonian-licensed and passporting European e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2 — e.g., Wise, Monese, Paysera, Holvi, Bunq, and Ponto — may legally onboard an EE OÜ remotely and issue IBAN-based accounts. This is an adequate secondary clearing rail for SEPA Credit Transfers, SEPA Instant Credit Transfers, card acquiring, and small-value receipts from foreign anti-dumping clients. However, EMIs are legally incapacitated for (i) providing overdraft facilities or trade credit, (ii) issuing or confirming commercial letters of credit, standby LCs, or bank guarantees, (iii) holding true escrow balances with depositor-preference protection equivalent to a credit institution, and (iv) executing large SWIFT MT103 wires above internal velocity thresholds, which many EMIs decline for professional-services clients with multi-jurisdictional beneficial owners. Recommendation: use an EMI purely as an ancillary liquidity rail; the primary banking relationship must be a tier-1 licensed Estonian credit institution for capital escrow, client-fund segregation, and high-value cross-border settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        For the OÜ's initial share capital, Estonian law imposes no statutory minimum since the 2021 Commercial Code amendment, and capital may be contributed by a non-resident shareholder without restriction. The compliant protocol is: (1) the founding shareholder instructs a SWIFT MT103 transfer from its home bank to the OÜ's pre-registration capital account at LHV Pank AS (or the operational account at Swedbank/SEB) using the beneficiary IBAN in EUR, with the mandatory payment reference 'Osakapitali sissemakse / Share capital contribution' together with the shareholder's registry code; (2) the remitting bank must state the EU balance-of-payments purpose code 'M1 — Direct investment: equity capital (inward)' for reporting to Eesti Pank; (3) for amounts exceeding EUR 10,000, the OÜ must provide source-of-wealth and source-of-funds documentation (bank statement of the contributor and audited financials or salary/tax records of an individual shareholder); (4) the receiving bank verifies that the remitter matches the shareholder listed in the e-Äriregister filing; (5) the bank issues a capital-receipt confirmation to the notary or e-Business Register for completion of registration; (6) after registration, capital is freely commingled into the operational account or held as a designated equity buffer. For inbound consulting-fee payments, the same MT103 rail is used with purpose code 'CS — Other business services' or 'SJ — Legal services' in the bank's BOP classification; the wire should reference the client and invoice number to satisfy AML transaction monitoring.The OÜ's outbound clearing stack is tripartite. (i) EU/EEA disbursements (expert witnesses, economic consultancies, translation agencies, customs laboratories, courier and filing fees): SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer using ISO 20022 pain.001 messages — instant or T+1 settlement at near-zero cost, subject to a EUR 100,000 per-transaction cap for instant transfers; for larger sums use high-value SEPA with cut-off discipline. (ii) Non-EEA disbursements (foreign counsel in Beijing, Delhi, Ankara, Hanoi, or Washington DC): SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer routed through the tier-1 bank's correspondent network; the covering instruction must disclose the beneficiary bank BIC and the settlement purpose code (e.g., 'Other business services — fees and honoraria'); each wire is screened against EU/UN/OFAC sanctions and matched to an invoice and case-file reference; for disbursements exceeding EUR 50,000, a confirmation of underlying service delivery (expert report engagement or court filing) is retained. (iii) Client-fund and escrow operations: where the OÜ receives advance funding for regulatory deposits or customs-bond premiums on behalf of a client, such funds are segregated in a client-money escrow sub-account at SEB Pank AS or LHV Pank AS and settled onward by MT103 with the payment narrative naming the beneficiary and the EU proceeding reference (e.g., 'Anti-dumping proceeding AD123 — provisional duty deposit'); commercial letters of credit and standby LCs (SBLCs) are permitted only as arranged contingent facilities for clients requiring court or customs bonds, issued by the tier-1 bank solely against full collateralization or an approved credit line, and are not part of the OÜ's own operational rail.FX Spot (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) — permitted for immediate conversion of inbound client fee receipts; no underlying documentation required beyond the bank's FX ticket and client reference.Outright FX Forwards — permitted up to 12 months tenor under a bank master agreement or ISDA/CSA, strictly matched to committed or highly probable client receivables and scheduled expert disbursements; prohibited for speculative open positions; requires board resolution and an ALCO-style hedging policy.FX Swaps (spot-versus-forward or tomorrow-next) — permitted for liquidity smoothing between the receipt date of foreign-currency client funds and the value date of downstream expert payments; must be unwound against a named invoice.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) — permitted for contingent exposures above EUR 500,000 (e.g., multidivisional defense cost pools in USD/GBP/CNY) with a maximum tenor of 24 months, fully collateralized, and accompanied by a documented exposure study.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permitted only for hedging nondeliverable currencies linked to actual client engagements (notably CNY, INR, and, where licit, RUB as an offshore hedge), on a deliverable-versus-nondeliverable basis, capped at 1.0x the underlying exposure, and prohibited in all cases of speculation or netting of uncommitted expectations.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Estonian-licensed and passporting European e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2 — e.g., Wise, Monese, Paysera, Holvi, Bunq, and Ponto — may legally onboard an EE OÜ remotely and issue IBAN-based accounts. This is an adequate secondary clearing rail for SEPA Credit Transfers, SEPA Instant Credit Transfers, card acquiring, and small-value receipts from foreign anti-dumping clients. However, EMIs are legally incapacitated for (i) providing overdraft facilities or trade credit, (ii) issuing or confirming commercial letters of credit, standby LCs, or bank guarantees, (iii) holding true escrow balances with depositor-preference protection equivalent to a credit institution, and (iv) executing large SWIFT MT103 wires above internal velocity thresholds, which many EMIs decline for professional-services clients with multi-jurisdictional beneficial owners. Recommendation: use an EMI purely as an ancillary liquidity rail; the primary banking relationship must be a tier-1 licensed Estonian credit institution for capital escrow, client-fund segregation, and high-value cross-border settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EUR 3 million annual turnover; or controlled transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 per related party transaction (or EUR 100,000 aggregate with same related party)

      
Economic substance scope: Estonia applies general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and substance requirements for treaty benefits; no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but requires real economic activity, adequate staff, and premises to claim tax residency and treaty access; shell companies without substance are subject to denial of treaty benefits and potential reclassification

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 1.2 million or 10% of turnover; additional tax plus 0.06% daily interest; criminal prosecution for intentional evasion

        
Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years

      

      
      

        Shell Company
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, additional tax assessment up to 20% of distributed amounts; fines up to EUR 320,000

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for tax debts; travel bans and disqualification from serving as directors for up to 3 years

      

      
      

        AML (Anti-Money Laundering)
        
Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; suspension of license; criminal liability for money laundering

        
Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; imprisonment up to 8 years, asset forfeiture, and travel bans
