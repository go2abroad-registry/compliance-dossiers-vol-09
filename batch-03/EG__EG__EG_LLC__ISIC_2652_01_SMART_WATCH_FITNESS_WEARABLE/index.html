Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-float exchange-rate regime. EGP is generally convertible for current-account transactions, including trade settlements and profit/dividend remittances, subject to supporting documentation and clearance of any tax obligations. Capital-account transactions, such as foreign-direct-equity injection and repatriation of paid-in capital, are routed exclusively through licensed Egyptian banks and require prior or contemporaneous registration with GAFI and, in certain cases, CBE approval. Dividend/profit remittance is permitted without prior CBE approval, provided audited financial statements, a tax clearance stamp or declaration, and the statutory corporate formalities are completed. Actual convertibility can be constrained by temporary USD liquidity tightness, so treasury planning must factor in the CBE's foreign-currency auction and interbank order flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign-exchange regulations, export proceeds must be received and credited to a licensed Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the date of the export declaration issued by Egyptian Customs. The exporter may apply for an extension of up to a total of 360 days on trade-backed, case-by-case grounds. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%: since the November 2016 EGP flotation, export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in the exporter's local bank account. However, the full amount of the export-related receipts must be repatriated into the Egyptian banking system within the statutory or extended timeline; retaining funds abroad or using offshore digital EMI accounts as a permanent repository is non-compliant.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Business) have medium viability for collecting USD/EUR export receivables as a supplementary treasury front-end, but they have low viability as a primary treasury rail inside Egypt. CBE regulations do not license these institutions as banks or authorize them to clear through Egyptian payment infrastructure; they cannot open local EGP IBANs, issue EGP-denominated settlement, or act as the repatriation account for export proceeds. All export proceeds must finally land in a licensed Egyptian bank within the 90-day repatriation window. Therefore, the compliant design is to use an offshore EMI only for preliminary invoicing/concentration and then to wire the net proceeds via SWIFT MT103 into the Egyptian operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) Register the investment with GAFI under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 to create a recognized inward-capital file; (2) hold a board resolution designating the specific Egyptian bank account as the capital-receipt account; (3) instruct the sending bank to issue a SWIFT MT103 with a GAFI file reference and the Egypt CBE purpose code for 'Foreign Direct Investment — New Capital Participation'; (4) ensure the beneficiary bank receives the wire into the foreign-currency account and issues a certificate of inflow or foreign-currency declaration; (5) after receipt, file the capital-inflow statement with GAFI and CBE so that future dividend remittance and capital repatriation will be honoured. For in-kind capital (machinery, inventory), obtain a CBE-attested valuation certificate and a commercial import declaration before the contribution is counted as paid-in capital.For importing components used in smart-watch manufacturing, the treasury clears outbound payments through two principal rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 for open-account or prepayment trade settlements, using Egyptian CBE purpose code for 'trade settlement' and attaching a proforma invoice, LC reference, or purchase contract; and (b) commercial documentary Letters of Credit (sight or usance) opened through the Egyptian bank for high-value or contractually secured supplies. All outbound trade payments are subject to CBE-backed documentary examination to verify that the commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and insurance certificate align with the declared transaction. For export receipts, the corresponding inbound rail is a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign buyer into the Egyptian receiving account; the treasury must verify the CIB/e-code or account syntax to prevent rejection.Onshore deliverable FX forward contract — permitted for hedging a contracted underlying trade, repatriation, or dividend obligation, typically with a tenor of up to 12 months, offered by licensed Egyptian banks against WBR (interbank) rates.Onshore FX swap — used to convert temporary USD/EGP cash mismatches on capital flows and working-capital cycles, subject to underlying exposure documentation and bank credit approval.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) — accessible to non-resident counterparties and, through licensed Egyptian banks, as a mirror structure for residents where deliverability is not required by the CBE.Cross-currency option or swap — permissible for large approved USD/EGP transactions where the bank's market-risk committee documents the hedged item as a confirmed trade/capital flow; plain-vanilla FX options on EGP are not generally permitted onshore.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions in the tax year triggers the mandatory Local File; higher group revenue and transaction thresholds apply for Master File and Country-by-Country reporting under the Egyptian Tax Authority regulations.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies substance-over-form and general anti-abuse principles under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Free-zone and treaty benefits are denied where an Egyptian LLC lacks real business premises, personnel, accounting substance, or genuine decision-making; a shell company booking smart-watch sales without actual manufacturing or distribution substance will be re-characterized by the ETA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misreporting of Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005: imprisonment of three (3) to five (5) years, fines up to the tax evaded, confiscation of concealed assets, plus monthly delay surcharge of 2% on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or authorize fraudulent filings may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax debt. The ETA may pierce the corporate veil, issue travel bans, and remit the case for criminal imprisonment sanctions.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA re-characterization of taxable income to the arm's length result, additional tax at 22.5% plus 2% monthly delay surcharge, and administrative fines for incomplete or absent TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's length transactions without a valid TP study risk personal assessment if the arrangement is found to be an illegal advantage or a misuse of corporate form. Financial sanctions, civil restitution, and director-level criminal referral may follow.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Dividends / Royalties / Interest

Statutory sanction: The payer remains legally liable for the un-withheld tax; the ETA assesses full tax recovery, a monthly delay surcharge, and fines for non-remittance. Repeated violation may trigger criminal prosecution for willful non-payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct payments abroad without withholding or who conceal the beneficial recipient may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax. Travel restrictions and imprisonment can be imposed for fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company / Anti-Money-Laundering Abuse

Statutory sanction: Anti-money-laundering penalties under Law No. 80 of 2002, including imprisonment, fines up to EGP 1,000,000, freezing of bank accounts, and confiscation of funds tied to illegal cross-border transactions.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing for AML offenders: directors, shareholders, and beneficial owners may be personally liable, subjected to travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment in addition to civil restitution of laundering proceeds.
