Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an Osaühing; a company may be formed without initial capital, but if the subscribed share capital is not fully paid, the founders are jointly and severally liable for unsatisfied obligations up to the subscribed amount. Separately, the Estonian EMI licensing regime requires initial capital of at least EUR 350,000 (or EUR 125,000 for limited electronic money issuance) as a condition for obtaining the Finantsinspektsioon licence.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and certification by the Estonian Rescue Board (Päästeamet) for the registered office premises, including compliance with the Fire Protection Act and the Building Code; structural load certificates from a licensed civil engineer if leasing any commercial floor spaces; and occupational health and safety clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, covering risk assessment, workplace ergonomics, electrical safety, and first-aid facilities.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an Osaühing; a company may be formed without initial capital, but if the subscribed share capital is not fully paid, the founders are jointly and severally liable for unsatisfied obligations up to the subscribed amount. Separately, the Estonian EMI licensing regime requires initial capital of at least EUR 350,000 (or EUR 125,000 for limited electronic money issuance) as a condition for obtaining the Finantsinspektsioon licence.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, MTA); filing and registration portal: e-Maksuamet / e-Toll (https://e-maksuamet.emta.ee).

E-government filing portal

The statutory TIN for an OÜ is the 8-digit Commercial Register registry code (Äriregister). Foreign founders typically use e-Residency digital ID to incorporate the company online at the e-Business Centre. After registration, the company files its tax card and annual data through e-MTA; VAT ID (KMKR) is applied for at MTA once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 or for voluntary registration; a foreign entity with an Estonian permanent establishment or VAT nexus must separately obtain Estonian registry/VAT coding.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — with deferred taxation element: Estonian corporate income tax (CIT) is charged on profit distributions, gifts, disallowed expenses and exit taxation, while retained profits are untaxed at entity level until distributed; pass-through election is unavailable for OÜ. — The statutory TIN for an OÜ is the 8-digit Commercial Register registry code (Äriregister). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — with deferred taxation element: Estonian corporate income tax (CIT) is charged on profit distributions, gifts, disallowed expenses and exit taxation, while retained profits are untaxed at entity level until distributed; pass-through election is unavailable for OÜ.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (1) CIT taxable unit (consolidated group) election for parent and 100% direct/indirect subsidiaries; (2) VAT group registration; (3) reduced 14/86 dividend distribution rate where the company has previously paid CIT on distributed profits; (4) application of ATAD hybrid mismatch and interest deduction limitations at default (no elective opt-out).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year (mandatory registration cross-threshold; voluntary VAT registration available below threshold; non-resident suppliers without establishment in Estonia are subject to special reverse-charge/registration obligations for certain services).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% when goods are dispatched outside the EU and export evidence (customs export declaration plus transport documents) is retained; services: cross-border B2B supplies to non-EU taxable persons are outside Estonian VAT/zero-rated under place-of-supply rules. Caution for EMI: virtual IBAN, e-wallet, and payment services are VAT-exempt financial services (Art 135 VAT Directive), not zero-rated; hence input VAT is generally non-deductible unless a special exemption/elective grouping applies.

  • Customs export declaration (EX/SAD) accepted by customs
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR/air waybill/rail consignment note) evidencing transport out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing payment from the non-EU customer
  • Commercial invoice / VAT invoice
  • Export contract or purchase order; customs-approved warehouse or exemption documentation where applicable

No continuous transaction control or digital stamping. Mandatory B2G e-invoices via the Peppol network; the e-MTA accepts structured e-invoices; retail/cash-register sales must be recorded through MTA-certified virtual cash-register software with real-time data transmission to the MTA (KAPI system); all VAT invoices must contain the statutory EU VAT invoice elements.

MTA audit window: 3 years from the filing due date (extended for defective declarations). False export zero-rating converts supply to taxable domestic supply: VAT assessed at 22% with default interest of 0.06% per day; statutory tax-fraud penalty (up to 10% of the under-declared tax liability) plus late-payment interest at 0.06% per day; extensive or intentional concealment may trigger criminal tax-evasion proceedings under the Penal Code with fines and custodial sentences; refunds are clawed back and set-offs denied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area member state. The euro is fully convertible; there is no foreign exchange control law. Current account payments are unrestricted, and capital account transactions within the EU/EEA are free under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Estonian Credit Institutions Act. Profit remittance from an Estonian OÜ is treated as a distribution subject to the 20/80 corporate income tax regime at the moment of distribution; no withholding tax applies to dividends paid to non-residents. No prior governmental approval, payment authorisation or FX licence is required for capital injections, loan repayments, trade payments or dividend remittances in euro or foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters and EMIs may freely hold, convert or transfer proceeds in any currency. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations may apply for central-bank data collection.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — central bank and monetary authority; Finantsinspektsioon (Financial Supervision Authority) is the integrated Estonian licensing and AML supervisory authority for credit institutions, payment institutions and e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. An Estonian-licensed EMI can access SEPA Clearing and T2 settlement indirectly through a licensed Estonian bank; a non-bank may also become a direct SEPA participant on a voluntary basis but must satisfy Eesti Pank and SEPA scheme rules. EEA-authorised EMIs may passport into Estonia under PSD2 and rely on their home-state license. Non-EEA offshore EMIs are not permitted to provide payment services or issue e-money in Estonia without an Estonian branch authorization or passport; such entities cannot hold a client-negative balance or issue e-money to Estonian residents. Legal constraint: the holding of e-money and the provision of payment services to Estonian customers require a license from Finantsinspektsioon, a valid passport under PSD2, or the use of a licensed Estonian credit institution as an agent/safeguarding host.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an Estonian OÜ, inbound share capital is remitted in euro via SEPA Credit Transfer or in foreign currency via SWIFT MT103. There is no Estonian exchange-control approval or registration required. On the payment instruction, the remittance information field should clearly state 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION / SHARE CAPITAL' in Field 70 of MT103 or the ISO 20022 RemittanceInformation in pain.001. For compliance verification, the bank will require the notarised share subscription decision, the incorporation registry extract from the Estonian Commercial Register (e-äriregister), the register of shareholders, and confirmation that the amount matches the subscribed share capital. Inbound currency conversion, if the payment is in USD or GBP, is freely available through the receiving bank on commercial terms.Euro-area trade payments use SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) with ISO 20022 XML, final settlement in TARGET2/T2. Payments to non-SEPA beneficiaries use SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary bank and trade-reference details. For larger or higher-risk goods transactions, commercial documentary letters of credit are issued under SWIFT MT700/MT720; collections use documentary collection instructions. The clearing rail hierarchy should be: 1) SEPA CT for EU/EEA and EEA-adjacent euro payments; 2) T2/TARGET2 for high-value domestic or cross-border euro settlement; 3) SWIFT MT103 for USD, GBP, CHF and other non-euro payments; 4) MT700/MT720 for documentary credits where goods are being financed. An Estonian EMI cannot settle directly at T2 unless it becomes an indirect participant via a clearing bank; in practice, the operating account at AS LHV Pank, AS Swedbank or AS SEB Pank is used as the domestic clearing vault.EUR/USD FX Forward executed with an EEA-licensed credit institution or investment firm under ISDA/CSA termsEUR/GBP FX Swap and EUR/CHF FX Swap for short-term liquidity balancingEUR/SEK FX Forward for regional merchant settlement hedgingCross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CIRS) for longer-term treasury or loan coupon hedgingFutures on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP or EUR/CHF listed on EU regulated markets, subject to margin and EMIR clearing obligationsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are generally unnecessary for euro exposures because official EUR deliverability is unrestricted; NDFs in non-EUR emerging-market currencies may be used with an EU-authorized dealer but trigger no special Estonian restriction

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained/reinvested profits; 20/80 of net profit distributed (25% of net dividend, i.e. 20% of gross distribution) as entity-level corporate income tax; reduced rate 14/86 of net (16.279% of net, i.e. 14% of gross) for regular dividends meeting Income Tax Act section 50 conditions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

No separate withholding tax imposed on the recipient; the Estonian entity-level distribution tax is the final source tax. Standard rate: 20/80 of net dividend (25% of net or 20% of gross dividend). Reduced rate: 14/86 of net dividend (16.279% of net or 14% of gross dividend) for regular dividends satisfying Income Tax Act section 50 conditions

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

By the 10th day of the calendar month following the reporting month; if the 10th is a weekend or Estonian public holiday, the deadline is extended to the next business day (Tax Information Act section 30)

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the end of the fiscal year (Commercial Code section 179); actual profit distribution must then be declared in Form TSD by the 10th day of the month following payment

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File/contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is required where the aggregate value of controlled transactions between the OÜ and a single related party exceeds EUR 250,000 per calendar year (Tax Information Act section 55(2)); a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000; irrespective of these thresholds, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board may demand comparable documentation within 60 days of a formal request

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the distribution-based CIT system presupposes genuine economic substance. Under the Electronic Money Institutions Act, the OÜ must be managed and controlled from Estonia – actual board meetings, AML/MLRO decision-making, treasury management and software/operational oversight must be located in Estonia. If the Tax Authority or Financial Supervision Authority determines that an OÜ lacks real substance and is a shell routing funds without local decision-making, the authorities may recharacterise income as a deemed distribution subject to 20/80 CIT (Income Tax Act section 50(2)) and revoke the EMI licence

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via undisclosed profit distributions or artificial shareholder loans

Statutory sanction: Assessment of overdue 20/80 CIT on the full disguised distribution; daily late interest at 0.06%; administrative penalty up to EUR 2,600 per false declaration; criminal prosecution under Penal Code section 397 for tax fraud with fines up to EUR 50,000 for a legal person and confiscation of the unjustified benefit

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and interest where the evasion resulted from a breach of management duties; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment up to 3 years, disqualification from serving as a director for up to 3 years, and travel-ban/asset-freeze measures during the criminal proceedings
AML/CTF violations in e-money reserve management

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (RahaPTS) and the Electronic Money Institutions Act, supervisory penalty up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; withdrawal of the EMI licence; prohibition on EU passporting; criminal sanctions for money laundering with imprisonment of the responsible natural persons

Director personal liability: Directors and the AML Compliance Officer (MLRO) may be personally liable for supervisory fines if the violation resulted from gross negligence; money laundering conviction carries imprisonment from 1 to 8 years (Penal Code section 394), permanent director disqualification, and civil restitution of losses incurred by customers or the payment system
Artificial intra-group financing and transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment increasing the OÜ's effectively distributable profit, followed by deemed-distribution CIT at 20/80; daily interest at 0.06%; documentation penalties of up to EUR 10,000 per failure; potential criminal liability for false invoicing

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length agreements are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax assessment if they acted with knowledge or gross negligence; professional disqualification and prohibition on holding management positions in licensed fintech entities may be imposed by the Financial Supervision Authority
Shell-company abuse and artificial avoidance

Statutory sanction: Application of Estonian CFC rules: the undistributed income of the low-tax controlled company is deemed distributed and taxed at 20/80; refusal to apply the reduced 14/86 dividend rate; licence revocation by the FSA for breaching the fit-and-proper and substance requirements of the EMI licence

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors may have the corporate veil pierced under commercial-law jurisprudence if the OÜ is deemed an artificial façade; personal liability for unpaid taxes, debts and customer-loss restitution; travel bans and entry bans may be imposed pending criminal investigation for serious economic crime
