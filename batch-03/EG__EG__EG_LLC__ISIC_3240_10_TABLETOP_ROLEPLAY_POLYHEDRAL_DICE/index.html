VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies of goods; EGP 250,000 threshold for certain services per Executive Regulations. Importers and exporters are required to register irrespective of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods physically removed from Egypt where the registered exporter supplies such goods under genuine export transactions, the proceeds in foreign currency are repatriated to an Egyptian licensed bank, and all statutory export declaration and Customs release formalities are completed. Services consumed wholly outside Egypt may also qualify under ETA guidance.

  • Egyptian Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill), signed commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign contract/purchase order plus Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing export proceeds credited to an Egyptian bank account in hard currency

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt system: all B2B invoices must be issued in XML format, transmitted in real time to ETA via approved API or cabinet portal, carry a QR code, and obtain automated ETA validation codes before being deemed fiscally compliant.

ETA may conduct retrospective audits on export files, bill of lading authenticity and remittance evidence. Failure to substantiate zero-rating or failure to repatriate proceeds within 180 days results in reversal of the 0% rate, VAT assessed at 14% on the invoice value, monthly delay penalty of 2% (capped at 180%), and potential fines up to 100% of the tax differential. Deliberate fraudulent documentation triggers criminal penalties under Law No. 67/2016 and Tax Procedures Law No. 96/2016.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the CBE decision of 6 March 2024, the EGP operates under a unified, freely floating interbank exchange regime with no parallel market; Egypt adheres to IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility. Current-account flows (trade settlement, service payments, royalties, and profit remittances) are convertible upon submission of standard banking documentation without discretionary CBE licensing. Capital-account flows are asymetric: inward direct investment into an Egyptian LLC is liberalized and requires no prior CBE approval, whereas outward resident direct investment and cross-border resident lending remain subject to CBE authorization. Profit and dividend repatriation to non-resident shareholders is uncapped subject to: (i) the CBE FX Transaction Form, (ii) audited financial statements and the general assembly dividend resolution, (iii) tax clearance evidencing the 10% dividend withholding tax under Law 91/2005, and (iv) the Foreign Capital Import Certificate evidencing the original hard-currency injection. Proceeds retained in onshore domiciled USD/EUR accounts are unrestricted; any sale of foreign currency must occur through licensed banks at interbank rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports (ISIC 3240 polyhedral dice), foreign-currency proceeds must be credited to a licensed Egyptian bank within a maximum of 180 days from the date of the customs export declaration filed through the NAFEZA unified electronic window; extensions require the bank's prior approval and documented payment terms. The mandatory surrender ratios applied in 2022 (100%, later reduced to 50%) were abolished on 6 March 2024; exporters now retain 100% of export receipts in onshore domiciled foreign-currency accounts and may convert to EGP at their own commercial discretion. Marketplace receipts from Kickstarter, Stripe, and PayPal constitute statutory export proceeds and must be repatriated through the Egyptian banking system, reconciled to the corresponding NAFEZA export declaration; accumulation of proceeds in offshore accounts beyond the statutory window contravenes Law 194/2020 and is punishable by imprisonment and fines of up to EGP 10 million.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary, banking and foreign-exchange regulatory authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), which superseded the 1951 CBE statute and the 2003 Banking Law; all FX transactions must be executed through CBE-licensed banks or licensed exchange bureaus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection from the non-resident shareholder is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's foreign bank to EG_LLC's USD account at the Egyptian bank; the wire narrative must state 'Capital Increase / Subscription to Paid-In Capital of EG_LLC' and correspond to the bank-selected CBE FX Transaction Form category for Capital Account - Direct Investment in a Resident Enterprise. The verification bundle comprises: (i) GAFI-registered capital-increase notification in the Unified Commercial Registry; (ii) shareholders' resolution authorizing the increase; (iii) notarized subscription deed in Arabic; (iv) updated ownership schedule confirming non-resident shareholding percentages; (v) source-of-funds statements from the sending bank; (vi) the LLC tax card. No prior CBE license is required for a cash equity increase; no mandatory conversion to EGP applies. Upon credit, the bank issues the Foreign Capital Import Certificate, which is the operative document for future dividend and capital repatriation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

27.5% on the first EGP 80,000,000 of annual taxable profit and 22.5% on the tranche above EGP 80,000,000 (Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 as amended by Law No. 26/2020). For an EG_LLC with taxable profit below EGP 80M, the applicable marginal rate is 27.5%; the commonly quoted Egyptian headline rate of 22.5% applies only to the excess.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross dividends distributed by an Egyptian LLC to shareholders, resident or non-resident (Article 56(3) of Law No. 91/2005). The LLC is the statutory withholding agent; the tax is due at distribution irrespective of the shareholder's residence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when the aggregate value of related-party transactions (purchases, sales, services, royalties, interest, intercompany financing) in a tax year equals or exceeds EGP 30,000,000. Master File mandatory when the worldwide multinational group (including the EG_LLC) reports consolidated annual revenue of at least EGP 6,000,000,000. Country-by-Country Report applies when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Even below the Local File threshold, the Form 7 TP annexure is mandatory for any controlled transaction, and contemporaneous arm's-length documentation must be held available for ETA inspection.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations form (i.e., no UAE-style ESR return) is applicable in Egypt; however, substance is enforced through the GAAR in Article 30 of Law No. 91/2005, the beneficial ownership test under the DTAs, and Egyptian CFC rules. The LLC must maintain a physical office in Egypt, active management exercised within Egypt, adequate staff, and production/distribution activity commensurate with its turnover. If the ETA or the EMLCU determines that the entity lacks commercial substance and is used as a conduit, the transactions may be re-characterized, treaty benefits denied, and non-resident shareholders assessed as having a permanent establishment in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 195/2022 amending the tax evasion regime: imprisonment for 1 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 100,000 up to EGP 20,000,000, in addition to recovery of the evaded tax, the 2% monthly delay surcharge, and ETA best-judgment assessment with penalties up to 100% of the tax difference.

Director personal liability: The general manager and directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax where the evasion was executed on their instruction; Egyptian courts pierce the corporate veil in fraud cases, ordering restitution from personal assets, preventive property seizure, travel bans (listings at the ETA collection department), and imprisonment of the responsible natural person.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA adjustment of Egyptian taxable profit under the most appropriate arm's-length method, additional CIT at 27.5% on the upward adjustment, 2% monthly delay surcharge, and administrative fines under the ETA TP documentation rules (including daily fines for late or incomplete TP filing). Deliberate false documentation is prosecuted as tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify the TP annexure with materially false data are personally liable for the adjusted tax difference and surcharges if the entity cannot pay; willful profit-shifting with fraudulent intent exposes them to evasion penalties, travel bans, and joint civil liability.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of all artificial intercompany fees; re-characterization under Article 30 GAAR; denial of treaty benefits on dividend/royalty payments for lack of beneficial ownership; the ETA may assess the non-resident parent directly on an Egyptian permanent establishment basis; company dissolution and commercial registry strike-off under Companies Law No. 159/1981.

Director personal liability: Nominee or 'front' directors are treated as co-debtors for the tax assessed at 27.5% on deemed Egyptian-source income; courts may order personal asset confiscation and travel bans; participation in a sham arrangement with knowledge of evasion may trigger criminal liability.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violation

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80/2002 as amended by Law No. 36/2024: imprisonment of 1 to 7 years and fines from EGP 100,000 to EGP 2,000,000 for laundering proceeds; the EMLCU may order freezing of the LLC's accounts, blacklisting, and withdrawal of banking facilities.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML compliance are personally subject to criminal penalties where the LLC was used as a conduit for funds without identifying beneficial owners or suspicious transaction reporting; personal assets may be seized and international travel prohibited during investigation.
Dividend WHT Evasion / Misreporting of Distributions

Statutory sanction: The LLC is the primary obligor for the unwithheld 10% WHT plus the 2% monthly delay surcharge and a fixed fine of EGP 1,000 to EGP 10,000 for each unfiled withholding return; disguised payments are re-characterized as dividends and treated as non-deductible.

Director personal liability: Under Article 11 of Law No. 91/2005, managers and directors are jointly and severally liable with the LLC for all unwithheld taxes and surcharges; ETA may issue collection orders, travel bans, and preventive seizure against personal assets until full settlement.
