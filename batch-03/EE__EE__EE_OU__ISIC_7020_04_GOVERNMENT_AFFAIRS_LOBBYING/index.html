Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-MTA portal: https://emta.ee/en/

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity or its Estonian OÜ submits the commercial registry application to the Registry Department of Tartu County Court; after registration, the company is automatically assigned a national registry code (Registrikood) and tax code. VAT registration is made separately through e-MTA if taxable supplies exceed EUR 40,000 annually or if the entity makes first taxable supply in Estonia; non-resident persons may obtain a VAT ID through a fiscal representative. A corporate income tax account is automatically created; no further TIN application is needed except for non-resident entities requiring a TIN for Estonian withholding taxes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity or its Estonian OÜ submits the commercial registry application to the Registry Department of Tartu County Court; after registration, the company is automatically assigned a national registry code (Registrikood) and tax code. VAT registration is made separately through e-MTA if taxable supplies exceed EUR 40,000 annually or if the entity makes first taxable supply in Estonia; non-resident persons may obtain a VAT ID through a fiscal representative. A corporate income tax account is automatically created; no further TIN application is needed except for non-resident entities requiring a TIN for Estonian withholding taxes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; Estonian law applies a single corporate level tax on distributed profits. Taxpayers may elect the preferential 14% rate for regular dividend distributions (i.e., dividends paid at least once per tax year and currently subject to full distribution tax treatment) versus the standard 22%/20% rate for irregular distributions. Hybrid mismatch rules derived from ATAD are mandatory and override contractual or structural election preferences; no elective tax consolidation or transparency election is available for an OÜ.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the export of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, as well as to supplies of services where the place of supply is determined to be a non-EU third territory (e.g., services tied to immovable property in a third country, or services physically performed outside the EU). For goods, zero-rating requires actual removal from Estonia within the EU fiscal framework, evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration accepted by customs authorities and proof of transporting the goods to a third country. For services, zero-rating is allowed when the customer is a taxable person in a third country or a non-taxable person resident in a third country, subject to substantive verification under the VAT Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic ECS/ AES record)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR, air waybill for non-maritime transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment statement from the foreign client
  • Contract for services / purchase order describing the nature, place and legal basis of the export

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping for domestic B2B invoicing. All value-added tax returns must be filed electronically via the e-MTA portal; invoices issued in electronic form must guarantee authenticity of origin and integrity of content. A Taxable Person must retain XML or PDF/A documents with a qualified electronic signature when used cross-border. Public sector procurement mandates the use of e-invoices in the national e-invoice network.

Application of 0% rate is strictly scrutinised. If export documentation is incomplete or transactions are found to lack genuine commercial substance, the tax authority retroactively assesses 22% VAT (or the applicable rate) on the supplies, adds interest at the statutory default rate (10% per annum from 2025), and applies an additional payment up to 20% of the calculated tax amount as a penalty. Deliberate VAT fraud gives rise to criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code, with fines up to three times the evaded amount and imprisonment up to four years; directors may have personal civil and criminal exposure for tax clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general exchange-control regime restricting current-account payments, capital transfers, foreign investment, or repatriation of profits. Cross-border payments remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, bank due diligence, tax rules, and any applicable reporting obligations. An OÜ may distribute profits to non-resident shareholders subject to Estonian company-law requirements and applicable corporate income tax on distributions; the receiving jurisdiction may impose additional tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no general statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may ordinarily be retained in foreign-currency accounts or used for permitted payments, subject to contractual, sanctions, tax, accounting, and bank compliance requirements.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (the Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s national central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets monetary policy for the euro area. Estonia applies applicable European Union payment-services, prudential, sanctions, and financial-market rules. Eesti Pank is not a general foreign-exchange approval authority for ordinary corporate transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR collections and payments where the provider is authorized in the European Economic Area and serves Estonian companies. An electronic-money institution account is not a bank deposit account, and safeguarded funds do not carry the same deposit-guarantee protection as eligible bank deposits. Provider acceptance, transaction limits, correspondent access, permitted jurisdictions, and enhanced due diligence can constrain use. Do not rely on an offshore EMI as the sole treasury account or as a substitute for a bank account needed for company, tax, or operational requirements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed profits; 20% on profit distributions (20/80 gross-up) and a reduced 14% rate for qualifying regular distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% corporate income tax on gross distributions (25% of net cash); preferential 14% rate on qualifying regular dividends; no separate shareholder-level withholding tax under domestic law

The reduced 14% rate applies to the corporate layer; non-resident shareholders claiming treaty rates must separately present the TRC; the reduced domestic rate does not automatically lower treaty withholding if the treaty states a different rate. TSD Line 5 — Corporate income tax payable Calculated tax at 20% on the grossed-up distribution (25% of net dividend) or 14% on the grossed-up regular distribution (16.28% of net dividend). This tax is a company-level tax and is paid by the OÜ, not withheld from the shareholder. Ensure the e-MTA payment order uses the correct reference number (DBP). TSD Line 6 — Fringe benefits and non-business expenses treated as distributions Benefits granted to shareholders or employees and expenses not related to business are subject to corporate income tax as deemed distributions. For non-resident recipients, the taxable base is the fair market value of the benefit; arm's length valuation evidence must be retained. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 per related party for cross-border services; EUR 250,000 per related party for goods and financial transactions. Master File obligation arises when the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000. MTA may also require documentation for transactions with tax-haven entities regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply Economic Substance Regulations in the offshore sense, but substance is a decisive criterion for tax residency and treaty entitlement. An OÜ engaged in government affairs lobbying must maintain a permanent local office, board meetings in Estonia, local payroll, contracts with clients, and a genuine decision-making center; otherwise, the MTA may reclassify it as non-resident or apply the principal purpose test and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Reporting Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax, interest at 0.06% per day, penalty 30% of the tax deficiency (50% for deliberate concealment). Criminal prosecution under Penal Code § 389: fine up to EUR 1,500,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years; prohibition on operating a business for up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax if the evasion results from their decision-making. Courts may impose director disqualification, travel bans, and civil restitution of concealed profits; deliberate evasion carries custodial sentences of up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Sham Structure Lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of Estonian GAAR and OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test: disregard the artificial structure, re-filing at 20% corporate tax, penalty up to 50%, withdrawal of tax residence certificate, and compulsory deregistration if the company has no lawful activity.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly operated a sham; criminal charges for money laundering or tax evasion; severe cases lead to imprisonment and freezing of personal assets; travel bans are routinely imposed during investigation.
Bribery and Unlawful Lobbying Payments

Statutory sanction: Under Penal Code § 402, bribery of a public official is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 500,000. The Anti-Corruption Act provides for confiscation of the value of the bribe, revocation of any lobbying licence, and publication of the court judgment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be sentenced to imprisonment and become personally liable for civil damages; disqualification from serving as a director for 5 years; travel bans and seizure of passports apply pending trial.
Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance in Lobbying Retainers

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, fines for legal entities can reach EUR 1,000,000; repeated or serious violations trigger licence revocation, closure of bank accounts, and inclusion in the public AML offender register.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines up to EUR 300,000, disqualification from management for up to 5 years, and imprisonment up to 3 years for negligent disregard of AML obligations. Civil liability lies to victims injured by the laundered funds.
