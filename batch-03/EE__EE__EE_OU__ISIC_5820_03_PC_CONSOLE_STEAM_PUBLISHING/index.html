Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital is required for an Osaühing; the Articles of Association may fix share capital as low as EUR 0, with a minimum nominal value of EUR 0.01 per share. The Commercial Code does not mandate a notarized escrow account; capital contributions may be paid post-registration into the company's own bank account or pre-registration into the founder's dedicated settlement account. Non-monetary contributions require a statutory external valuation report (Section 145(2)) filed with the registry. Any dividend distribution is prohibited until share capital is fully paid.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: before occupying office premises, the employer must submit an electronic fire safety self-inspection declaration (tuleohutus) to the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) and ensure fire extinguishers, evacuation plans, and staff fire training. Occupational safety: the employer is required to prepare a written workplace risk assessment (riskianalüüs), arrange occupational health check-ups, and register employees in the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon). Structural clearance: the office building must have a valid building usage permit (kasutusluba); any dedicated server room with heavy equipment requires a structural engineer's floor load certification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); digital portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet) - https://emta.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable Estonian activities must register either (a) as a non-resident taxable person with the MTA under Section 20(5) of the VAT Act, obtaining an Estonian VAT identification number (KMKR) listed on VIES, or (b) as a branch or subsidiary in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) via the e-Business Register single window; the MTA then issues a taxpayer registry code (registrikood) which serves as the TIN for corporate income tax, withholding tax, and VAT purposes. E-residents may incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) fully remotely without physical presence in Estonia.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based deferred taxation: undistributed profits are taxed at 0% corporate income tax; distributed profits are taxed at the entity level at 20/80 of net distribution, equal to 25% effective rate on the gross distribution; foreign permanent establishment profits of an Estonian resident company are exempt). — A foreign entity conducting taxable Estonian activities must register either (a) as a non-resident taxable person with the MTA under Section 20(5) of the VAT Act, obtaining an Estonian VAT identification number (KMKR) listed on VIES, or (b) as a branch or subsidiary in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) via the e-Business Register single window; the MTA then issues a taxpayer registry code (registrikood) which serves as the TIN for corporate income tax, withholding tax, and VAT purposes. E-residents may incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) fully remotely without physical presence in Estonia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based deferred taxation: undistributed profits are taxed at 0% corporate income tax; distributed profits are taxed at the entity level at 20/80 of net distribution, equal to 25% effective rate on the gross distribution; foreign permanent establishment profits of an Estonian resident company are exempt).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Estonia. Statutory elections include: (1) reduced 14/86 corporate income tax rate on regular/habitual dividends paid to resident natural persons out of previously taxed profits, subject to the three-year equalisation rule; (2) voluntary VAT registration for suppliers below the EUR 40,000 threshold; (3) election of VAT filing frequency (monthly or quarterly) by small registrants; and (4) application of the ATAD anti-hybrid and CFC rules as directly applicable statutory provisions without elective opt-out; taxpayers may select accounting and structuring treatments within statutory boundaries to avoid hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for resident businesses per calendar year. There is no threshold for non-resident suppliers without an Estonian establishment; they must register for VAT before making taxable supplies in Estonia unless the supply falls under the Union OSS scheme or the reverse charge. Intra-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to Estonian non-taxable consumers exceeding the EUR 10,000 Union-wide distance-selling threshold are declared via OSS and do not require separate Estonian VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of goods to a destination outside the EU qualifies for the 0% VAT rate under Section 15(1)(6) of the VAT Act where title has passed and the goods have been transported out of the EU; the supplier must obtain and retain proof of export within three months. Services directly connected to exported goods (transport, loading, ancillary supplies) are also zero-rated. For the Steam/console publishing scope (ISIC 5820): B2B digital and licence supplies to non-resident EU customers are zero-rated under the place-of-supply rule; B2C digital services to EU consumers are taxed in the consumer's member state under the OSS scheme (Estonian 22% VAT does not apply); B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside the scope of Estonian VAT, requiring retention of evidence of the consumer's location and residence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX; electronic export declaration accepted by the MTA Customs, including AES/ICS records)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing dispatch from the EU
  • Commercial export invoice and underlying sales or publishing contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT/MT103 proof of receipt of export proceeds
  • Warehouse dispatch order, digital delivery log, or inventory/end-customer certificate for digitally supplied content
  • Carrier or freight forwarder statement of export (or export confirmation)
  • Estonian VAT return and KMIF sales list records evidencing the zero-rated or out-of-scope treatment

Estonia operates no continuous transaction control (CTC) regime and no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based invoice authorisation requirement for domestic B2B invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory only in the B2G segment (PEPPOL e-invoice format). VAT returns and intra-EU sales lists (KMIF) are filed electronically through the e-MTA portal, which supports machine-readable XML submission and API integration. Cross-border OSS/IOSS declarations are transmitted to the MTA under EU regulations and exchanged via CESOP and VIES.

Export zero-rating claims are cross-checked by the MTA against customs export declarations, carrier records, and banking data. If mandatory documentation is missing, false, or the foreign-sales proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day period, the MTA retrospectively reverses the 0% rate, re-asserts 22% output VAT on the gross consideration, applies penalty interest at 0.06% per day, and imposes tax fraud surcharges (up to 50% of the evaded tax) under the Tax Information Act. Criminal sanctions under Penal Code Section 403 may include fines and imprisonment of up to five years for aggravated intentional tax fraud. The Estonian zero-CIT undistributed profit regime does not shield the VAT liability; directors may be personally liable where the VAT debt arises from a distribution in violation of capital maintenance rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro has been the sole legal tender since 1 January 2011; Estonia maintains no residual foreign-exchange-control statute, and both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU free-movement-of-capital acquis. Profit remittance by an Osaühing (OÜ) faces no FX permission or repatriation hurdle: dividends may be distributed in EUR or any other freely convertible currency with immediate transfer abroad. The only financial cost is the Estonian corporate income tax imposed at the moment of distribution (normally 20/80 gross-up under Income Tax Act sections 50 and 51, with the reduced 14/7 regular-distribution regime for qualifying private-company dividends), plus bank-level AML verification of the ultimate beneficiary. Practical friction is therefore documentary and compliance-driven rather than regulatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Estonia imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation-timeframe obligation on export proceeds, including Steam and PlayStation Store payout receipts. Foreign-currency receivables may be retained indefinitely in any EEA-authorized credit institution or in a third-country bank subject to sanctions screening and ECB Balance-of-Payments statistical reporting thresholds. The material constraints are the EU/EEA sanctions framework (notably asset freezes concerning Russia and Belarus) and the FIU's suspicious-transaction reporting duties under MLTFPA.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is the national central bank and the Eurosystem member responsible for monetary policy execution, euro cash supply, payment-system oversight, and balance-of-payments statistics. Prudential and AML/CTF supervision of banks and payment institutions is shared between the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon, FSA) and the Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo, FIU) operating under the Police and Border Guard Board pursuant to the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (MLTFPA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium, subject to EEA passporting under PSD2. Authorized electronic money institutions (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer and similar EMIs) may passport into Estonia and issue EUR IBANs and USD payout accounts without a local banking licence, which is operationally useful for Steam and PlayStation revenue collection. However, EMIs are not covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund, cannot operate statutory escrow or pledged client-funds accounts, and frequently apply stricter merchant underwriting to high-volume digital-goods revenue. EMIs should be deployed only as ancillary clearing rails, never as the sole treasury bank, and each EMI deposit must remain below counterparty-risk limits set by the OÜ board.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans from non-Estonian investors must enter Estonia through regulated EU payment messaging. In EUR, use a SEPA Credit Transfer to the OÜ's IBAN at the treasury bank, carrying the ISO 20022 purpose code 'INTC' (intra-company payment) or 'LOAN' for shareholder loans, with the OÜ registry code quoted in the Remittance Information. In USD, GBP or other non-EUR currencies, route via SWIFT MT103, inserting the ECB Balance-of-Payments statistical purpose category (Direct Investment - Equity Capital or Other Investment - Loans) in Field 77B Regulatory Reporting and the board resolution or loan agreement reference in Field 70. The receiving bank will typically hold initial credits for 1-3 business days pending automated and manual verification of the Commercial Register extract, beneficial-owner declaration, investor source-of-funds statements, and the signed loan or subscription documents; the OÜ must be prepared to produce a capital-increase filing receipt from the e-Business Registry if statutory capital is enlarged.Outbound flows comprise developer royalties, localization or licensing fees, VAT and corporate-income-tax settlements, dividend distributions, and refunds. For EEA beneficiaries, use SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR with next-working-day value and the appropriate ISO 20022 purpose code (SUPP for supplier payments, ROYA for royalties, TAXS for tax, DIVI for dividends). For non-EEA beneficiaries, use SWIFT MT103 with a Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) under SWIFT gpi, confirming Standard Settlement Instructions with the payee bank and executing EUR/USD conversion at the treasury bank's spot rate. Commercial Letters of Credit are not standard for digital services; instead the normal instruments are MT103 direct settlement, bank guarantees for material co-development milestones, and MT798 envelope messaging for documentary receivables financing. Dividend remittances must attach the board distribution resolution and the e-MTA tax reference so the 20% (or 14/7 qualifying) Estonian corporate income tax withheld can be remitted to the Maksu- ja Tolliamet by the 10th day of the following month.EUR/USD outright forward contracts (plain vanilla, tenors up to 24 months) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the bank's standard FX master terms, eligible for the EMIR non-financial-counterparty clearing thresholdEUR/USD FX swaps (spot-forward combinations) for rolling operational liquidity and short-tenor treasury optimization without creating residual open FX positionsVanilla EUR/USD FX options (purchased, premium paid in EUR) with tenor aligned to Steam and PlayStation payout cycles, subject to MiFID II appropriateness assessment and board derivative-risk policyCross-currency interest rate swaps (EUR/USD XCCY) to match USD-denominated platform receivables against EUR-denominated wage, tax, and accountancy liabilitiesNon-deliverable forwards in EUR/USD are generally not offered onshore and are economically redundant because the euro is fully convertible; any offshore NDF would trigger EMIR trade reporting, ESMA post-trade transparency, and margin obligations without regulatory benefit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, subject to EEA passporting under PSD2. Authorized electronic money institutions (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer and similar EMIs) may passport into Estonia and issue EUR IBANs and USD payout accounts without a local banking licence, which is operationally useful for Steam and PlayStation revenue collection. However, EMIs are not covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund, cannot operate statutory escrow or pledged client-funds accounts, and frequently apply stricter merchant underwriting to high-volume digital-goods revenue. EMIs should be deployed only as ancillary clearing rails, never as the sole treasury bank, and each EMI deposit must remain below counterparty-risk limits set by the OÜ board.
