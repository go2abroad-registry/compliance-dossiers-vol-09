Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by Law No. 159 of 1981 for Limited Liability Companies; however, founders must subscribe 100% of the issued quotas and the full subscription amount must be deposited in a temporary blocked escrow account with a Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)-licensed bank before registration. The escrow funds are released only upon issuance of the GAFI incorporation certificate and the Commercial Register extract. For pharmaceutical and industrial applicants, GAFI and the Industrial Development Authority expect a capital and financing plan demonstrating at least USD 200,000 equivalent for construction, equipment and working capital; no mandatory installment payment percentage is applied where the entire subscription is covered by the escrow deposit certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and extinguishing system certification is required from the General Directorate of Civil Defense of the Ministry of Interior, covering process areas, solvent and ethanol storage, cleanroom infrastructure, and emergency egress arrangements; a structural load inspection certificate for the plant building must be issued by a licensed structural engineer and approved by the governorate building authority; occupational safety clearance is issued by the Ministry of Manpower - Safety and Occupational Health Administration after verification of emergency exits, first-aid and occupational health programs, personal protective equipment matrix, noise and vapor exposure monitoring, and hazardous materials storage controls; pressure apparatus and sterilization equipment must be inspected and certified prior to operation under the technical regulations applicable to pharmaceutical engineering installations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is a fiscally resident juridical person subject to Law 91/2005; no check-the-box pass-through; non-resident shareholders suffer final 10% dividend withholding at entity level).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election in Egypt. Statutorily available elections: (i) group/consolidated corporate filing for Egyptian parents with wholly-owned Egyptian subsidiaries on ETA preconditions (Art. 61 Law 91/2005); (ii) unified small/micro business regime election under Law 24/2024 on turnover thresholds; (iii) declining-balance depreciation election on fixed assets; (iv) free-zone / SCZONE special status election under Law 83/2002 and Investment Law 72/2017; hybrid-mismatch interest-limitation outcomes of Decree 494/2020 are mandatory, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover; registration application must be filed within 60 days after the threshold is exceeded; exporters and pure intra-group suppliers may register voluntarily below the threshold to secure zero-rating refund entitlement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Egyptian customs territory within 180 days of the taxable supply qualify for the 0% export rate if title passes to a non-resident buyer and payment is documented in freely convertible foreign currency; qualifying services rendered to non-resident recipients and consumed wholly outside Egypt are also zero-rated; SCZONE/free-zone subcontract processing and re-export operations qualify under the applicable investment-zone rules; the exporter must file the export-correlation statement and obtain ETA clearance acknowledgement to sustain refund claims.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD form) bearing ECA export registration number
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical exit from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian bank proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • ETA-cleared electronic VAT export invoice matching the customs declared value
  • Sales contract / purchase order and applicable certificate of origin or product release documents for the liposomal vaccine adjuvant.

ETA mandates real-time electronic invoice clearance through the Ragit fiscal platform (XML schema, multi-code QR, digital stamping) for all phase-in-mandated taxpayers; B2C e-receipts are also mandatorily issued once phased in; REST/API or portal integration is compulsory, and invoices failing ETA clearance are legally void for input VAT deduction and trigger automated audit risk flags. Electronic sales registers must be retained and queryable for at least five years.

Risk-based ETA export audit cross-checks customs SAD records, bank remittance certificates and cleared e-invoices; missing substantiation triggers retrospective clawback of the 0% zero-rating at the standard 14% VAT rate plus default interest and administrative fines; fictitious or inflated export declarations constitute VAT evasion (Law 67/2016) punishable by imprisonment and fines up to double the evaded tax, with additional CBE enforcement, including blocking or boycotting the exporter's banking facilities until export proceeds are repatriated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a market-determined managed float, most recently liberalized in March 2024 under the IMF Extended Fund Facility framework; current-account convertibility (trade in goods, freight, royalties, and periodic service charges) is fully operational for documented transactions routed exclusively through CBE-licensed banks and must be supported by customs declarations, invoices, and IMF Balance-of-Payments purpose codes. Capital-account convertibility is conditional rather than prohibitive: inward foreign-direct-equity subscription and outbound dividend/profit remittance require documentary sequencing composed of audited financial statements, ETA tax clearance certificates for the profit remittance authorization, CBE bank intermediated execution, and prior registration of the foreign shareholding in the Commercial Registry. Practical hurdles are liquidity-driven FX prioritization in the interbank matching engine, ETA clearance issuance timelines, and bank-level compliance documentation; no fixed statutory ceiling on profit repatriation exists post-2024 float.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current CBE regulations applicable to licensed export houses, 100% of export proceeds must be repatriated into a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the customs export declaration date (bill-of-lading or airway-bill date) for goods, and within the contractual credit period not exceeding five years for capital equipment financed by usance instruments. Exports must be pre-registered on CBE export Form No. 1 and reconciled electronically through the CBE automated banking reporting platform; while the mandatory conversion quota has been relaxed after the March 2024 float—allowing exporters to hold foreign currency in their CBE-licensed accounts or convert at market rates—the repatriation deadline remains a hard statutory requirement, and non-compliance subjects the exporter to fines, suspension of bank credit facilities, and cancellation of export registration privileges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the exclusive monetary and foreign-exchange regulator under Law No. 88 of 2003 (Central Bank and Banking Sector Law), exercising delegated supervisory authority over licensed commercial banks, the interbank FX market, and the national payment/clearing infrastructure; complemented by the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) for FDI entry registration and the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) for capital-account remittance tax clearances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a treasury backbone: the CBE Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and CBE Resolution No. 109 of 2020 require any entity issuing accounts, wallets, or payment instruments in Egypt to hold a CBE license, and offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or comparable platforms) cannot clear EGP through the national Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system nor maintain CBE-regulated local accounts for the EG_LLC. Offshore EMIs may function only as ancillary inbound collections for sales receipts in limited volumes, but any use must be ring-fenced from capital injections, EGP payroll, tax payments, and documentary trade settlements; the CBE applies deemed-agent scrutiny to payment instructions routed through unregulated aggregators, elevating AML profile and threatening bank account durability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI equity wire transfers must be intermediated by a CBE-licensed commercial bank using the statutory IMF BPM6 purpose codes for direct investment equity injection (E41/E42 series as mapped by the CBE automated reporting system) and settled via SWIFT MT103 to the designated capital escrow account of the EG_LLC. The bank must file the CBE inward-remittance notification within the same business day through the CBE Automated Foreign Exchange Information system, and the injection must be reconciled against the GAFI investment certificate, the notarized LLC share subscription resolution, and the Commercial Registry capital endorsement. Shareholders' loans and subordinated debt are permissible under the E47/E48 codes with loan-term documentation and CBE interest-rate caps; non-compliance with purpose-code accuracy risks classification as ineligible proceeds, mandatory re-routing, or forced repatriation.Outbound settlements for raw-material imports (active lipid excipients, microfluidics consumables, sterile packaging) should be executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 payment orders for small open-account transactions supported by CBE import registration forms, or (ii) commercial letters of credit (sight followed by usance) for high-value recurring procurements with confirmed bank avalization. Each disbursement must be matched to the NAFEZA customs single-window declaration, the product tariff code alignment (HS 3002, 3824, 3926 lines), and an CBE-issued Import Registration Form (IRF) number before payment release. The import LC must be opened in favor of the supplier with invoice, packing list, transport document, certificate of origin, and the mandatory Egyptian pre-shipment inspection certificate if applicable; post-December-2022 CBE reform permits both LC and direct documentary collection modes, removing the previous LC-only mandate.Onshore deliverable EGP/USD FX forwards (sight and term up to 12 months) executed with a CBE-licensed corporate treasury counterparty under ISDA and credit-support documentationPlain-vanilla cross-currency EGP/USD swaps aligning floating-rate liabilities with fixed EGP funding and vice versa, approved by the CBE treasury desk and marked-to-market monthlyOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in EGP booked through London or New York sell-side dealers for the non-resident parent entity, legally permissible outside CBE clearing provided such trades are not novated into the Egyptian banking systemEGP/USD FX call-spread collars and zero-cost option structures protecting profit-remittance cash flow, permitted under CBE derivatives licensing constraints when bank-approved and backed by real trade exposure

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as a treasury backbone: the CBE Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and CBE Resolution No. 109 of 2020 require any entity issuing accounts, wallets, or payment instruments in Egypt to hold a CBE license, and offshore fintech EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, or comparable platforms) cannot clear EGP through the national Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system nor maintain CBE-regulated local accounts for the EG_LLC. Offshore EMIs may function only as ancillary inbound collections for sales receipts in limited volumes, but any use must be ring-fenced from capital injections, EGP payroll, tax payments, and documentary trade settlements; the CBE applies deemed-agent scrutiny to payment instructions routed through unregulated aggregators, elevating AML profile and threatening bank account durability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions (turnover); for cross-border financing services or interest-bearing loans, the threshold is EGP 2,000,000 (based on ETA TP guidance). Master File must be prepared when transactions exceed EGP 25,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies a General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) and the economic substance doctrine under Law No. 91/2005, Article 30. Companies must have real tangible substance (staff, premises, risk-bearing activity, and active decision-making) to be respected for tax purposes. Free Zone entities and Special Economic Zone entities must maintain economic substance to retain preferential tax treatment under Investment Law No. 72/2017. A shell company without substantive operations faces recharacterization and denial of treaty/incentive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Fines equal to 150% of the evaded tax, plus a daily interest surcharge of 2% per month; imprisonment for a term of three to seven years and additional fine of EGP 10,000–EGP 50,000 under Law No. 91/2005 Article 133.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are personally jointly liable for corporate tax amounts and criminal penalties if they knowingly authorize or participate in the fraudulent activity; can face travel bans, asset freezes, confiscation of personal property, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ETA will adjust the taxable profit under the arm's length standard; impose a deficiency assessment of 20% of the additional tax plus 2% monthly interest; in aggravated cases, penalties increase to 40% of the additional tax; documentation failure triggers strict liability for the same fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax adjustment if they signed the return and cannot prove lack of knowledge; includes civil restitution and prohibition from serving on any company board in Egypt.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the transaction, full denial of treaty benefits, application of 22.5% corporate tax plus 20% penalty, and retroactive revocation of free-zone tax incentives; for Free Zone violations, administrative fines and closure of facility.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for abusive structuring; may be subject to imprisonment of up to three years, confiscation of assets, and recovery of previously granted tax holidays.
Failure to Withhold and Remit WHT

Statutory sanction: Liability for the full amount of unre mitted WHT plus a penalty of 20% of the under-withheld amount and monthly interest of 2%; administrative fine of up to EGP 10,000; criminal prosecution in cases of willful non-collection.

Director personal liability: Directors who were responsible for the cross-border payment may be held personally liable for the withheld taxes and fines; travel bans and criminal conviction possible.
