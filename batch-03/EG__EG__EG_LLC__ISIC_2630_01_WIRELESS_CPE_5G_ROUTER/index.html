Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for an LLC under Companies Law 159/1981. The capital is set by the members in the Articles and must be fully subscribed. Contributions in Egyptian pounds or foreign currency may be transferred into the company's Egyptian bank account; no escrow account is statutorily mandated for a non-free-zone LLC.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt. Official e-portals: www.eta.gov.eg (tax-card, TIN, e-filing), einvoice.invoice.gov.eg (central e-invoicing fiscal clearance), ereceipt.invoice.gov.eg (retail e-receipts). Foreign investment incorporation and one-stop-shop processing: General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), www.gafi.gov.eg. Customs interface: Egypt Customs Authority via Nafeza/ACI systems.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate the EG LLC before GAFI/commercial registry: legalize foreign shareholding and board documents, appoint a resident general manager, establish a registered office. (2) Within 30 days of commercial registration, submit the unified tax-registration application to the competent ETA district office, attaching the commercial registry extract, premises title or lease, municipality license, import/export registration, NTRA or telecom-manufacturing license (where applicable for ISIC 2630 equipment), and ID cards or powers of attorney of authorized signatories. (3) ETA issues a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card covering corporate income tax, VAT, stamp tax, and payroll registration; VAT registration is ex lege mandatory once annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000. (4) Enroll in the ETA e-invoicing platform, obtain API and identity credentials, and certify the ERP or billing engine for XML/JSON invoice clearance before first supply. (5) If the plant is licensed inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) or a GAFI free zone, the zone authority issues a special tax and customs code with distinct export procedures; Egypt is a unitary state, so no sub-national tax registration is required. Typical lead time: 2-6 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The LLC is a resident juridical person under Companies Law No. 159/1981 and Investment Law No. 72/2017; it is per se a corporate taxpayer under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 on worldwide income at the applicable CIT rate (currently 22.5% for juridical persons), with no pass-through or distribution deduction. Territorial_Scope nuance: foreign-currency and foreign PE profits of resident companies are creditable but subject to Egyptian tax; non-resident enterprises without an Egyptian PE are taxed only on Egyptian-source income, while a PE or branch is taxed on attributable Egyptian-source income at the same CIT rate. Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment is not default; it is available only by statutory license (e.g., SCZone or GAFI free-zone industrial registrations), and the tax-card classification remains a normal resident taxpayer until a zone exemption decision is issued.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a US-style check-the-box or elective hybrid-mismatch regime; LLC classification is fixed by law. Available statutory elections and options: (i) depreciation policy election for production machinery and telecom-manufacturing fixed assets (straight-line or accelerated subject to ETA approval); (ii) Investment Law No. 72/2017 incentive elections, including state-approved investment cost deductions and zone-based incentives for new industrial projects in designated governorates and economic zones upon GAFI approval; (iii) VAT input-credit recovery elections, including pro-rata and special allocation methods for mixed taxable or exempt activities; (iv) fixed statutory loss carry-forward of 5 years under Article 24 of Law 91/2005, with no group relief or consolidation election for LLCs; (v) general anti-abuse rules under Article 30 of Law 91/2005 permit ETA recharacterization of hybrid or avoidance structures, but no hybrid-mismatch corrective-election mechanism is enacted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 (Article 16, Law 67/2016); voluntary registration when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 100,000; compulsory registration irrespective of turnover for importers, exporters, and economic-zone operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating under Article 9, Law 67/2016 applies to physical exports of goods by a VAT-registered manufacturer or exporter to buyers outside Egypt on transfer of title at FOB or CIF point of exit; wireless 5G CPE routers (ISIC 2630) qualify as tangible exported goods. Cumulative conditions: (i) the export invoice is a valid ETA-cleared e-invoice; (ii) goods actually leave Egypt through an Egyptian customs port evidenced by an approved export declaration; (iii) export shipment occurs within one year from the date of supply; (iv) export proceeds are received in foreign currency in an Egyptian bank account within CBE repatriation deadlines; (v) input-cost documentation substantiates manufacturing cost and the refund set-off. Offshore services supplied to non-residents require separate service-specific analysis; supplier-consignment or sham export arrangements do not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Shikan) with customs exit stamp and Nafeza/ACI matching record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international carriage to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank confirming receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

Universal e-invoicing mandate through the ETA central clearance platform (einvoice.invoice.gov.eg). Every VAT invoice must be generated as an XML/JSON file, digitally stamped by the ETA crypto-stamp (UUID + QR code), and submitted via API or an ETA-certified billing system for real-time fiscal clearance within 24 hours of issuance. Unstamped or non-cleared invoices are legally invalid for input-VAT recovery and export zero-rating. A separate B2C e-receipt system applies mandatorily to retail merchants and telecom or electronics sellers. New TIN holders receive e-invoicing credentials at registration; non-compliance triggers per-invoice fines, suspension of clearance credentials, and audit triggers.

ETA export-refund desks audit zero-rating claims by cross-matching e-invoice clearance data, customs export declarations, shipping manifests, and bank FX remittance records; warehousing, factory, and documentary inspections may be ordered. False, inflated, or sham export claims are treated as tax evasion and trigger: (i) retrospective clawback of the 0% rate and assessment of 14% VAT on the full invoice value; (ii) monthly late-payment surcharge at the CBE discount rate plus 2% (Article 27, Law 67/2016); (iii) evasion penalties of up to EGP 1 million plus imprisonment of up to 5 years (Article 78, Law 67/2016; Article 137, Law 91/2005); (iv) additional customs-fraud penalties, confiscation, and exporter blacklisting under Customs Law No. 207/2020; and (v) permanent loss of zero-rating and refund privileges and suspension from CBE export-proceeds facilities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt adopted a market-determined, flexible exchange-rate regime in March 2024 under an IMF-supported program; the EGP trades freely through authorized dealer banks and is fully convertible for documented current-account transactions (trade, services, royalties, freight). Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment injections, shareholder loans, and profit repatriation, are convertible but require GAFI registration, audited financial statements, tax clearance, and CBE-compliant inward/outward purpose codes. Profit remittance requires board-approved distribution, statutory audit reports, and confirmation of no outstanding tax liabilities; the 2022-2023 repatriation queue has largely cleared but documentary review remains a period cost.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All goods-export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt within 90 calendar days from the shipment date (per CBE circulars under Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020) and credited to an account at a CBE-licensed authorized dealer bank. Exporters may lawfully retain 100 percent of proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at Egyptian banks or convert voluntarily on the interbank market; no fixed compulsory conversion-to-EGP ratio currently applies. Extension of the 90-day window requires CBE prior approval; services and free-zone exporters are partially exempt. Non-repatriation triggers import payment blocks, penalties under the Foreign Exchange Law, and possible suspension of FX dealing privileges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary, foreign-exchange, and banking licensing authority under the Central Bank and Banking Sector Law No. 88 of 2003 (as amended) and the Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020. The CBE licenses authorized dealer banks, operates the interbank FX market, issues exchange-control circulars, approves treasury/derivative transactions, and enforces AML/CFT obligations under Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. The CBE Payment Services Law No. 18 of 2019 and its licensing regulations prohibit unlicensed e-money or payment institutions from holding, clearing, or settling EGP on behalf of Egyptian resident entities. Offshore digital EMIs and virtual-account providers (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) cannot issue Egyptian IBANs, cannot route EGP-denominated trade settlements, and cannot satisfy the statutory export-proceeds repatriation rule, which is satisfied only through CBE-licensed authorized dealer banks. Such EMIs may be used only as ancillary non-EGP collection vehicles outside Egypt; core treasury liquidity must remain onshore.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed only through CBE-licensed authorized dealer banks using SWIFT MT103 messages. The instruction must carry the correct CBE inward-remittance purpose code for equity contribution/capital increase and reference the GAFI-approved subscription or capital increase resolution. The bank requires: (i) updated commercial registry extract reflecting the new capital; (ii) board resolution authorizing the issuance/allotment; (iii) shareholder subscription agreement and payment statement; (iv) GAFI filing confirmation; (v) tax card and account opening forms. Funds are credited to a designated capital-contribution collection account, held in USD or converted to EGP at the interbank rate, and released to the operational account only after GAFI and commercial registry registration evidence is delivered. Source-of-funds and BO verification precede any credit; the transaction is reported to the CBE through the bank's periodic FX and balance-of-payments filings.All import disbursements for components and materials used in 5G CPE manufacturing must be executed through an authorized dealer bank using: (i) commercial letters of credit (issuance via SWIFT MT700; settlement via SWIFT MT103 upon conforming presentation); (ii) documentary collections (DP/DA) with the bank as collecting agent; or (iii) direct telegraphic transfer (MT103) where the beneficiary is a pre-approved supplier under the bank's trade facility and goods are cleared or contractually documented. The LLC must hold a current importer registration in the CBE importer registry, valid tax and importer codes, and file each consignment in the Customs ACI (Advance Cargo Information) system. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within four months after the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year LLC, the filing deadline is 30 April. Quarterly advance CIT payments are due within the last day of the month following each quarter.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly instalments are due by 30 April, 31 July, 31 October, and 31 January for a calendar-year taxpayer; each instalment is normally based on 25% of the final tax payable for the preceding tax period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 4,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions trigger the mandatory Egyptian Local File; a Master File is additionally required where the MNE group's consolidated global turnover exceeds EGP 500,000,000. CbC reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EGP 25 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but Law No. 91 of 2005 and its TP rules impose an economic-substance test. A non-resident-owned LLC producing 5G CPE routers in Egypt must show real premises, technical staff, production records, inventory, and decision-making functions. The ETA may disregard or recharacterise a shell LLC that merely routes sales, and in such case, it will re-attribute profits to the foreign principal, impose penalties, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 91 of 2005 and Law No. 67 of 2016 (VAT/customs), conviction carries a penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, fines of EGP 5,000–50,000, monthly interest, and imprisonment of between two and seven years; the ETA may reopen assessments for five years.

Director personal liability: Signing directors and managers are jointly civilly liable for the evaded tax, interest and penalties; asset attachment, travel bans and custodial sanctions apply. The ETA will pierce the corporate veil if the LLC was operated as a mere conduit for tax avoidance.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's-Length Default

Statutory sanction: The ETA will effect an arm's-length adjustment and impose a penalty of up to 30% of the additional tax (or more if concealment is proven), an administrative fine for no/insufficient TP documentation (up to EGP 100,000), and statutory late-payment interest on the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's-length contracts without business substance or failed to instruct TP compliance can be made personally liable for the additional tax when the company cannot pay; travel bans may be issued during recovery proceedings.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the legal personality of the LLC under anti-abuse rules, re-source the revenue to the foreign beneficial owner, assess a 100% evasion penalty, impose customs/VAT penalties on importation, and revoke the commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and the local shareholder representative may be held jointly liable for the avoided tax, and criminal referral can be made for aiding a sham entity as an instrument of tax evasion.
AML / Counterterrorist Financing Violations

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with Egypt's AML Law No. 80 of 2002 triggers fines, confiscation of the funds involved, and imprisonment of up to 15 years; the Financial Regulatory Authority may freeze bank accounts and cancel licences, while telecom trade licences become suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who have acted in bad faith or without due diligence face personal criminal exposure, confiscation orders, travel bans, and are barred from serving on future boards; extradition may be available under bilateral mutual legal assistance treaties.
