Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an Osaühing; the share capital is set in the Articles of Association and may be as low as €1. Monetary contributions must be paid in full within 10 years of registration unless the Articles require immediate payment; no state-mandated escrow account, but a bank confirmation of payment is required where the founders elect notarial paper registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), electronic filing and registration via the e-MTA portal (https://e-mta.emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign person establishes the Osaühing (OÜ) in the Estonian e-Business Register (e-äriregister, ariregister.rik.ee); the registry issues the 8-digit registry code (registrikood), which doubles as the corporate tax identification number for income tax, employer withholding, and customs purposes. VAT registration is a separate mandatory step: a resident OÜ must register in the VAT register (KMD INF application through e-MTA) before exceeding €40,000 taxable turnover in a calendar year; a non-resident entity may voluntarily apply for a limited-scope Estonian VAT ID without a local permanent establishment, with no minimum threshold. Customs bond and transit authorisations (NCTS T1 comprehensive guarantee or TIR full guarantee) must be obtained separately from MTA Customs Department, and each vehicle in the bonded fleet requires a road transport operating licence and GPS-based customs seal protocol. Secure access to all portals requires an ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID, or e-Residency digital ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies a distribution-based corporate income tax model: retained profits are not taxed at OÜ level, and only distributed profits attract income tax (20% standard, elective 14% rate on regular dividend distributions not exceeding one-third of the prior year's distributed taxable amount). No US-style check-the-box or generic pass-through election exists for an OÜ. A mandatory annual (periodic) taxation regime applies only to credit institutions, pension funds, and certain financial undertakings; general companies cannot elect into it. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD (implemented in Estonian Income Tax Act §§50(4)–(15)) apply mandatorily and contain no elective opt-out. CFC rules apply per statute without election. A group may elect for VAT group registration under VAT Act §16 if sharing close economic relationships, and customs operators may elect between NCTS single guarantee and TIR carnet coverage.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro (EUR) as its sole legal tender and maintains full free convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control laws, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on profit repatriation, dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds, subject only to EU sanctions and AML/CFT regulations. Non-resident investors may freely transfer capital in and out, and corporate funds can be remitted abroad without prior approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds. Export receipts in any currency may be retained, credited to bank accounts, or converted to EUR or any other currency at market rates without time limits or central bank authorization.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) – the central bank of Estonia and member of the Eurosystem

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Estonian commercial banks offer integrated corporate banking, while EU/EEA licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) can passport into Estonia under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) without a separate Estonian banking license. Cross-border EMIs may provide euro-denominated IBANs and payment accounts, but are generally not viable for customs bond escrow, high-value trade settlement, or documentary credit issuance. Legal constraints include stricter AML/KYC expectations under Estonian FIU oversight, possible restrictions on accepting customer cash, and the need for an agency or branch registration if providing domestic services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for a capital increase or shareholder loan into an Estonian OÜ: inbound SWIFT MT103 / SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) from a foreign shareholder. No Estonian exchange control permission is required; however, the bank will apply standard KYC and payment verification. For statistical purposes, the transfer must be identified with the payment purpose code 'EESA – Share capital contribution' or equivalent according to the Estonian balance of payments reporting manual. Where the contribution exceeds the threshold for reporting, a written board resolution and an up-to-date shareholder register must be provided. The receiving bank will verify the ultimate beneficial owner and often require proof of the shareholder's corporate existence and beneficial ownership.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for EUR payments within the EEA, using the Baltic-Nordic IBAN network; (2) SWIFT MT103 (Customer Credit Transfer) for cross-border payments outside the EEA, including suppliers, fuel cards, drivers' expenses, and customs authorities; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 / UCP600) for structured high-value cargo or procurement transactions, especially when documentary compliance is required; (4) Bank guarantees and standby LCs (SWIFT MT760) to secure customs bonds, terminal fees, or contractual performance. All outbound disbursements are subject to the bank's transaction monitoring and must be supported by valid transport documentation (CMR, invoices, customs bond declarations).FX Forwards (Outright EUR/foreign currency forwards with a licensed credit institution)Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for illiquid currencies supporting defined trade flowsFX Swaps / Currency Swaps (including cross-currency interest rate swaps for long-term financing)
