Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik) of Estonia, sections 135-181 and 212-270 (Osaühing); sections 140 and 148 (management board); sections 56-78 (commercial register registration); section 33 (legal capacity upon registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-MTA portal, https://www.emta.ee.

E-government filing portal

The OÜ is first entered in the Commercial Register (Äriregister), which assigns the official registry code (registrikood). That registry code is the statutory tax registration number; no separate TIN is issued. Using an e-Residency digital ID or Estonian ID-card, the legal representative opens the e-MTA account, registers the company, and files statutory declarations electronically. VAT identification is requested through e-MTA when annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000, or immediately where supplies trigger Estonian VAT without a turnover threshold. No notarised or in-person step is required for e-residents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or flow-through election is recognised by Estonian law; the OÜ is a resident legal person and income tax is deferred at entity level until distributions. Elective choices relevant to a VC GP include (i) VAT grouping with fellow Estonian taxable persons where a controlling/subordinated relationship exists; (ii) application of the reduced regular-dividend rate under the Income Tax Act when distributing to resident individuals; and (iii) where permitted under EU ATAD, alignment of hybrid instrument or permanent establishment characterisation with treaty and anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover; non-resident taxable persons with Estonian supply nexus are required to register from first supply with no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Estonia to a third country qualify for the 0% rate where physical export is proven; intra-EU supplies are exempt if the customer has a valid EU VAT ID and the goods are dispatched to another Member State. For services supplied to a non-EU fund, Estonian VAT is zero-rated or outside scope where the place-of-supply rules place the service in the recipient's jurisdiction. Management and advisory services rendered by the VC GP to non-Estonian funds must evidence the fund's foreign establishment and fully taxable status. If the fund qualifies as a special investment fund under Estonian/EEA law, management services may be VAT-exempt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EXE) and exit confirmation; for intra-EU supplies, VIES-verifiable VAT ID and Intrastat/GPL movement records.
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch outside Estonia or the EU; for digital services, service performance and place-of-use logs.
  • Commercial invoice, fund management agreement, bank SWIFT credit note or inward remittance certificate proving payment from the foreign counterparty.

Estonia mandates structured e-invoicing (PEPPOL BIS / EN 16931) for invoices addressed to public-sector entities; B2B e-invoicing is common but not statutorily mandatory. There is no real-time tax-authority fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no fiscal memory requirement. VAT returns are filed electronically via e-MTA; VAT and Intrastat declarations are submitted through the same portal.

The Tax and Customs Board may post-audit export files; unsubstantiated zero-rating or exemption is re-invoiced at 22% VAT, plus late-payment interest of 0.06% per calendar day and an administrative tax fine of up to EUR 1,200 for tax offences. Fraudulent export claims can lead to criminal liability and retrospective clawback of input VAT. For cross-border VC GP fees, failure to prove non-resident recipient status triggers VAT, withholding and CIT adjustment, including ATAD hybrid mismatch consequences.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia adopted the euro on 1 January 2011; it has no independent national currency and therefore no exchange-control approval mechanism. The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Article 63 prohibits all restrictions on cross-border capital movements between EU Member States and third countries, subject only to EU sanctions and public-policy safeguards. There are no meaningful profit-remittance hurdles for an Osaühing (OÜ): retained profits are taxed at 0% until distributed; dividend distributions are subject to Estonian corporate income tax at 20% of the net payment (effective 20/80 gross-up), reduced to 14% (14/86) for regular distributions of the accumulated reserve, with participation-exemption relief available for certain corporate shareholders. No separate dividend withholding tax is collected by the paying bank because the distribution tax is levied at the OÜ level; tax-treaty relief would apply if the Estonian distribution tax were characterized as an Article 10 tax in a reciprocal convention. Capital injections, redemption proceeds, carried interest, and management fees may be repatriated or retained in any currency without central-bank prior authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation tenor, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds, management fees, capital calls, or any other inward cross-border receipts. Export and service revenue may be retained in EUR or any convertible currency without restriction. Conversely, all resident enterprises must maintain standard accounting records and banks report cross-border payment statistics to Eesti Pank solely for balance-of-payments compilation; the practical obligation is to annotate each wire with the correct purpose code, not to remit funds to the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank. Since 1 January 2022, the former Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) has been integrated into Eesti Pank, which is therefore the single prudential supervisor, money-laundering reporting supervisor for banks, and balance-of-payments statistics authority. The Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) acts as the AML/CFT analysis unit under the Police and Border Guard Board.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rated Medium for ancillary flows and Low as a primary treasury bank. Wise Business, Revolut Business, and other EU/EEA e-money institutions (EMIs) passported into Estonia under Directive 2009/110/EC can provide fast multi-currency settlement, SEPADirectFX, corporate cards, and API-driven transaction reporting. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: e-money funds are safeguarded rather than deposited, the 100,000 Tagatisfond (Estonian Guarantee Fund) deposit guarantee does not attach, and EMIs cannot issue documentary credits, standby letters of credit, or hold a licensed fund depositary mandate. For a Venture Capital General Partner classified as Medium_Risk_Enhanced, the legally robust venue is a full-service bank for capital-call custody and investor funds, with the EMI restricted to operational outflows such as travel expenses, portfolio-company reimbursements, and minor FX conversions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Fund constitutive documents (Limited Partnership Agreement, Private Placement Memorandum, side letters, subscription agreements); Ariregister extract showing the OÜ's articles of association; AIFM authorization or small-AIFM registration/notification issued by Eesti Pank, or a senior counsel legal opinion confirming the applicable exemption under the Estonian Fund Management Act; an investor inventory under NDA with name, jurisdiction, regulatory status, and source-of-wealth; the GP's written AML/CFT policy, risk-assessment matrix, and compliance-officer appointment certificate.

Transit/pooling risk: capital-call wires originate from the bank accounts of LPs in multiple countries and may reference the fund name rather than the remitter's legal name, raising suspicion of money-laundering through pooling or third-party payable-through accounts.
