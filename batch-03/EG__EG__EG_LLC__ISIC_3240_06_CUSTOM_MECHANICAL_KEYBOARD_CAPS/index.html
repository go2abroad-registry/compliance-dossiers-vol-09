Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Law No. 159 of 1981; the share capital must be fully subscribed at formation, and the cash portion must be deposited in a licensed Egyptian bank and evidenced by a banker's certificate. Capital may be paid in cash or in kind based on audited valuations.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense fire safety certification from the General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) after on-site inspection of the factory (fire extinguishers, evacuation routes, alarms, storage of plastic polymer raw materials); occupational workplace clearance from the Ministry of Manpower Industrial Safety Department under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003: machine guarding on injection molding presses, extraction ventilation, personal protective equipment, and noise exposure within applicable limits (90 dB A / 8-hour shift); plus structural and building permit inspection clearance under the Unified Building Law No. 119 of 2008 covering the production hall load-bearing floors and electrical installations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official national tax authority portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing clearance portal at https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; National Single Window for Foreign Trade 'Nafeza' used for customs export declarations and trade facilitation.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains its statutory tax identity through the GAFI one-stop-shop procedure: (1) incorporate the LLC in the Commercial Registry and obtain the commercial registration certificate; (2) file the incorporation documents and legal address with the Egyptian Tax Authority to obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card; (3) register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 within 30 days of reaching the EGP 500,000 annual taxable-turnover threshold, or immediately upon voluntary registration; (4) register on the ETA/Nafeza platform for electronic invoicing and customs identity; and (5) open a corporate EGP and foreign-currency bank account with an Egyptian bank to receive and evidence export proceeds in hard currency.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains its statutory tax identity through the GAFI one-stop-shop procedure: (1) incorporate the LLC in the Commercial Registry and obtain the commercial registration certificate; (2) file the incorporation documents and legal address with the Egyptian Tax Authority to obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card; (3) register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 within 30 days of reaching the EGP 500,000 annual taxable-turnover threshold, or immediately upon voluntary registration; (4) register on the ETA/Nafeza platform for electronic invoicing and customs identity; and (5) open a corporate EGP and foreign-currency bank account with an Egyptian bank to receive and evidence export proceeds in hard currency.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election; tax classification is determined by the legal form. Available statutory elections for the LLC include voluntary VAT registration below the statutory threshold, election of investment-incentive deductions and Free Zone status under Investment Law No. 72/2017 subject to GAFI approval, and taxation on an approved accounting-period or cash basis as permitted by the Tax Procedures Law. Consolidated corporate tax returns are not permitted for Egyptian LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration at EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods and services; voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold. Zero-rated export supplies count toward the turnover threshold for registration testing. Non-resident suppliers of electronic services are subject to separate ETA registration rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The LLC may apply the 0% VAT export rate where: (i) the physical goods are declared in the National Single Window and actually leave Egypt within 90 days of the supply/invoice date or within an ETA-approved extended window; (ii) a valid customs export declaration, export commercial invoice, and transport document evidence the removal; (iii) the buyer's full consideration is paid in freely convertible foreign currency and credited to the LLC's Egyptian bank account within the Central Bank of Egypt's 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline; and (iv) the LLC files zero-rated VAT returns with complete supporting documentation. Failure to achieve the 90-day export results in reclassification as a domestic 14% VAT supply with reversal of input VAT credits.

  • Customs Export Declaration (lodged and cleared through the National Single Window for Foreign Trade 'Nafeza')
  • Commercial Export Invoice clearly stating the 0% VAT rate
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing hard-currency proceeds credited to the Egyptian bank account
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin (as required by destination-country customs)
  • Foreign sales contract or purchase order (retained for ETA audit evidence)

ETA e-invoicing is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses on B2B transactions. Each invoice must be transmitted in near-real-time (statutorily within 24 hours of issuance) via the ETA API in JSON/XML format, cleared by the central clearance portal, and digitally stamped with the ETA QR code before legal effectiveness. Invoices that are not cleared or stamped are not deductible by counterparties and expose the issuer to administrative fines. B2C e-receipts are being rolled out in phases for retail transactions.

The ETA may retrospectively audit export zero-rating claims for up to five years, cross-checking Nafeza customs declarations, cleared e-invoices, transport documents, and CBE inward-remittance statements. Where the required export documentation bundle is incomplete or the foreign-currency consideration is not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, zero-rating is clawed back retroactively: the LLC is reassessed at 14% VAT on the export invoice, input VAT credits are reversed, and late-payment interest plus administrative fines are imposed. Forgery or deliberate misrepresentation of customs, transport, or banking evidence is prosecuted as tax evasion under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 and the Customs Law, carrying criminal sanctions including imprisonment, financial penalties, and suspension of e-invoicing privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains liberalized current-account convertibility for trade in goods and services under the unified exchange-rate regime adopted on 6 March 2024. Capital-account flows, including foreign equity injection, shareholder loans, and profit repatriation, are permitted but remain documentary-controlled and must be executed through CBE-licensed authorized banks. The Egyptian pound (EGP) is a market-discovered interbank currency subject to depreciation pressure; profit remittances require tax clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority and the servicing bank's confirmed FX liquidity position. No systematic blockage exists on current-account payments, but banks may prioritize outbound transactions based on prevailing USD/EUR liquidity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate 100% of export proceeds into Egypt through a CBE-licensed authorized bank within 120 days from the date of the shipping document (bill of lading). As of the March 2024 FX liberalization, there is no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio; exporters may retain proceeds in USD or EUR, apply them to import payments, or sell them at the interbank market rate. Non-compliance with the repatriation window triggers CBE penalties and elevated tax-audit scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting as the unified regulatory authority for banking conduct, foreign-exchange administration, and clearing system supervision under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% for private-sector resident limited liability companies

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on distributions of profits from the Egyptian LLC to shareholders, including non-resident corporate shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be submitted within four months after the close of the fiscal year; quarterly advance CIT payments are due within one month after the end of each calendar quarter of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million in controlled transactions during the fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File preparation; Master File is required where the worldwide group has consolidated annual revenue exceeding EGP 200 million; Country-by-Country Reporting is compulsory when group consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 20 billion and the group operates in Egypt.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation of the UAE type exists in Egypt; however, the Income Tax Law imports a general anti-abuse rule requiring arrangements to have real commercial substance and economic justification. The Egyptian Tax Authority will treat a pure paper entity lacking staff, premises, or active business functions as not entitled to treaty relief on dividends, interest, or royalties and may also disregard transactions whose sole purpose is tax avoidance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion / Double Accounting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the full evaded tax plus a penalty equal to 100%–200% of the evaded amount, monthly late-payment interest, confiscation of assets, and criminal imprisonment up to five years under Law 91/2005.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general manager who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties; the courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and a personal criminal sentence of up to five years, and may prohibit the director from holding board positions in Egypt.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Related-Party Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Upward TP adjustment of taxable income, additional 22.5% CIT on the adjustment, monthly interest, and an administrative penalty of up to 25% of the adjusted tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length contracts or direct the concealment of TP information may be personally joint- liable for the additional assessed taxes and subject to restraint orders and bank account garnishments during audit.
Shell Company / DTA Benefit Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, reapplication of statutory withholding rates, assessment of unpaid WHT, late-payment interest, and a fine of up to 20% of the under-withheld tax.

Director personal liability: The directors and beneficial owners who structured the sham arrangement can be made personally liable for the additional withholding tax; travel bans may be imposed during criminal investigation, and distributed funds may be seized if the arrangement is treated as money laundering.
AML / Informal Funds Movement

Statutory sanction: Fines under Egyptian AML Law No. 80/2002 ranging from EGP 50,000 to EGP 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years; tax deductions supported solely by undisclosed mental contracts may be disallowed entirely.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecutable for non-compliance with beneficiary due diligence and for deliberately routing corporate funds through their personal accounts; during a criminal case the court may order a travel ban, house arrest, and seizure of the personal assets of the directors.
