Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Since 1 January 2011, an Osaühing may be founded with no minimum paid-up capital (share capital of €0 up to €2,499.99); in that case no escrow account or pre-payment certificate is required. If the share capital is set at €2,500 or more, the full amount must be paid into the company's collection bank account during the foundation procedure and evidenced by a bank certificate or payment order submitted with the application. Contributions in kind require an independent auditor's report. A no-minimum-capital OÜ must observe the net asset restriction in § 176(2) of the Commercial Code and must not make distributions while its equity is below the share capital plus mandatory reserve.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance per §§ 15–17 of the Fire Safety Act (Tuleohutuse seadus): annual fire safety check, certified fire extinguishers, evacuation plan, and notice to the rescue authority for the office premises. Structural load review under the Building Code is required if the office floor load exceeds 2.5 kN/m². Occupational health and safety clearance per §§ 12–13 of the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus): written risk assessment, workstation ergonomic evaluation, and registration of the workplace with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) before hiring the first employee.

Paid-in capital rule: Since 1 January 2011, an Osaühing may be founded with no minimum paid-up capital (share capital of €0 up to €2,499.99); in that case no escrow account or pre-payment certificate is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) — official taxpayer portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee); e-Residency digital administration portal: e-resident.gov.ee

E-government filing portal

For an Osaühing (OÜ), register the company in the Estonian Commercial Register through the e-Business Register using an e-Residency digital ID; the company is then assigned a registry code serving as its tax identification code. The same registry code is used for corporate income tax and VAT. If the OÜ will have taxable turnover in Estonia, the management board must submit a VAT registration application through e-MTA before the obligation arises or within 3 working days after the turnover threshold is exceeded. A foreign entity without an Estonian commercial registration that nevertheless incurs a direct or indirect tax liability must apply to MTA for assignment of an Estonian tax account/registry code before filing any return or making any tax payment. Physical presence is not required for registration; an Estonian-registered agent or board member may execute the e-residency workflow.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; an OÜ is per se an entity-level taxpayer. Corporate income tax is levied at the entity level only on distributions of profit, deemed distributions, and non-business expenses; retained/undistributed profit is taxed at 0%. The profit distribution resolution is the taxable event, not an elective regime. Within the indirect tax sphere, Estonian VAT Act permits election to register/join a VAT group for separate taxable persons, and a small business may opt into the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) for cross-border supplies. Hybrid mismatch treatment is mandatory under ATAD/Council Directive (EU) 2016/1164 and Estonian Income Tax Act implementing rules; no elective opt-in or opt-out is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for domestic supplies in Estonia; EUR 10,000 annual cross-border distance sales/e-services threshold triggering destination-country VAT under OSS for suppliers in other EU member states

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating applies where physical goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the exporter before the supply is made, no further processing occurs in Estonia, and the exporter holds reliable documentary evidence of export. For services: electronically supplied services, software licenses, and simulated casino game services supplied to a business established outside the EU are outside the scope of or zero-rated for Estonian VAT under the place-of-supply rules, provided the supplier obtains two independent non-conflicting items of evidence of the customer's location/status (e.g. IP-country evidence, billing address, bank origin, VAT ID or business registration, contractual seat). Services supplied to EU consumers are not zero-rated and must be taxed through OSS at the destination member state's VAT rate; the 'export zero-rating' mechanism applies only to true third-country exports and qualifying B2B place-of-supply transfers.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic SAD/EXA, EXS/GMS consignment or equivalent accepted by Estonian Customs/MTA)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / air waybill / postal receipt proving physical removal from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment settlement proof evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign counterparty
  • Commercial invoice and sales/export contract stating the foreign buyer, Incoterms, and place of delivery
  • Transport and delivery supporting documents (packing list, warehouse certificate, third-party logistics receipt)

Estonia does not impose a real-time tax-authority clearance or digital stamping regime for general B2B invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory for public-sector contracting entities, and private-sector buyers may not refuse an e-invoice if issued in the national e-invoice standard (XML-based). The MTA operates the e-MTA filing environment and accepts periodic aggregated VAT/EC Sales data through its portal and API-based telecommunication channels; there is no per-invoice real-time validation or SAF-T pre-clearance requirement.

The MTA may retrospectively deny zero-rating if the documentary mileposts are incomplete, inconsistent, or obtained after the statutory tax assessment period; the exporter must then output standard-rate 22% VAT on the invoice value, plus late-payment interest of 0.06% per day from the original VAT due date and an administrative penalty up to EUR 3,200 per false taxable declaration. Where fraudulent export arrangements are found — including fabricated export documents, sham customs declarations, circular payments, or artificial reversal of EU export evidence — criminal tax evasion penalties under Penal Code §§ 389–390 may apply, with imprisonment up to 5 years and disqualification of the management board. Estonia imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days in cross-border law; however, MTA auditors assess long-outstanding export receivables beyond about 180 days as evidence that the supply and payment may not be genuine, triggering clawback of the claimed 0% rate, recharacterization as an Estonian-taxable domestic supply, and recovery of standard VAT.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible under both current and capital account categories. Estonia is an Article VIII IMF member with a complete absence of exchange controls. Dividends, shareholder loans, interest, royalties, and freely distributed profits can be repatriated without prior central bank permission or tax clearance. There are no restrictions on maintaining foreign-currency accounts in Estonia or abroad. The social casino business model is treated as a fully permissible service activity under EU free movement of capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Residents may retain foreign-currency receivables indefinitely either in local or foreign bank accounts. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations apply; a declarant threshold of EUR 50,000 per transaction or reporting filters under the NBU? No threshold per Estonian regulation. Legal entities must file periodic BOPS forms to Eesti Pank when international transaction values exceed EUR 12,500, but these are purely information-based, not permission-based.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) in coordination with the European Central Bank; prudential conduct of banks is supervised by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervisory Authority).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 in cross-border controlled transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File; Master File required when the Estonian entity's group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million; CbCR applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore centres, but substance analysis is embedded in GAAR (Tax Procedure Act §110), DTA entitlement, and permanent establishment risk. A social casino OÜ must have a local registered office, functioning board decisions, a resident director/manager, and actual economic activity (even if digital) to preserve 0% retained profit treatment and avoid forced recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Recoverable tax arrears plus 0.06% per day late payment interest; administrative fines up to EUR 15,000; criminal tax evasion under Penal Code §152/§153 exposes a legal person to a fine up to EUR 240,000 and individuals to imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false tax declarations or knowingly caused the omission are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall; courts may issue travel bans, asset attachments, and confiscation of proceeds; in insolvency, personal liability extends to the full unpaid corporate tax.
Artificial Shell Arrangement / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of GAAR under Tax Procedure Act §110: the structure is disregarded, profits are attributed to the de facto founder, resulting in 20% distribution CIT plus fines and interest; compulsory deletion from the Commercial Register and cancellation of VAT registration

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally ordered to pay the entire tax shortfall as restitution; travel bans and exclusion from acting as board members for up to 3 years; criminal liability if the artificiality is used to launder funds.
AML / Money Laundering (unlicensed payments and anonymous wallets)

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Anti-Money Laundering Act, supervisory fines up to EUR 1.2 million; revocation of payment institution or virtual currency service authorisation; money laundering itself carries imprisonment up to 5 years and forfeiture of criminal proceeds

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally prosecuted for money laundering and are subject to civil recovery of laundered assets; supervisory/AML officers risk a ban from management functions in any Estonian licensed entity, and travel restrictions while under investigation.
Transfer Pricing / Intercompany Payment Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing the taxable distribution base, late payment interest at 0.06% per day, and documentation-related fines up to EUR 3,200 for the first offence and EUR 9,600 for a repeat offence; tax criminal proceedings if intentional

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the adjusted tax amount if they signed the annual report and knowingly approved non-arm's-length payments; personal jail time up to 5 years for deliberate false documentation; board membership suspension may follow a conviction.
