Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Business Register, administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK) under the Ministry of Justice and Digital Affairs.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), including sections 136-147 on private limited companies, sections 145-146 on formation and contributions, and sections 149-152 on shares and share capital; Regulation (EU) 2017/1129 and Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 apply where relevant to securities offerings and crowdfunding services.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for entry in the Commercial Register (avalduse esitamine äriregistrisse), submitted through the e-Business Register portal or by notarised application.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 101 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an OÜ. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 0.01. Share capital contributions may be monetary or, where permitted, non-monetary. Monetary contributions must be paid as required by the formation documents before registration unless the founders use the statutory deferred-contribution procedure; deferred contributions remain the shareholders' liability and are not held in a mandatory escrow account. A separate escrow account is not generally required, although banks or payment institutions may impose account-opening conditions.

Funding & unlock path: No manufacturing clearance is normally required for a digital office. The premises must satisfy local building-use, fire-safety, evacuation, electrical, and occupational-health requirements. Fire-safety documentation or inspection may be required by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet), particularly for a new or altered premises. Structural-load inspection is required where construction, fit-out, server equipment, or material alterations affect the building. Workplace compliance must follow the Occupational Health and Safety Act, including risk assessment, emergency procedures, and employee training.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an OÜ. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 0.01. Share capital contributions may be monetary or, where permitted, non-monetary. Monetary contributions must be paid as required by the formation documents before registration unless the founders use the statutory deferred-contribution procedure; deferred contributions remain the shareholders' liability and are not held in a mandatory escrow account. A separate escrow account is not generally required, although banks or payment institutions may impose account-opening conditions.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), digital portal e-MTA (e-mta.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity may obtain a statutory Estonian TIN and VAT ID by registering through the e-MTA portal using e-Residency digital ID or via the VN10 paper form; the application requires company constitutive documents, proof of foreign registration, a local contact for VAT purposes, and, if not established in Estonia, a local tax representative or fiscal representative; registration is effective from the date specified in the certificate and is mandatory before taxable activities commence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity may obtain a statutory Estonian TIN and VAT ID by registering through the e-MTA portal using e-Residency digital ID or via the VN10 paper form; the application requires company constitutive documents, proof of foreign registration, a local contact for VAT purposes, and, if not established in Estonia, a local tax representative or fiscal representative; registration is effective from the date specified in the certificate and is mandatory before taxable activities commence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available under Estonian law; an OÜ is always an entity-level taxpayer for corporate income tax purposes, but may elect to apply the 14% reduced tax rate on regular dividend distributions and may use the ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch elections where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover for taxable persons established in Estonia; €150,000 distance sales threshold for non-established EU suppliers using the OSS scheme; non-established foreign suppliers become registrable as of first taxable transaction when no EU regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU, or to supply of services directly connected with exported goods or to non-resident clients where the effective use and enjoyment occurs outside Estonia; the supplier must obtain and retain the customs export declaration, transport documentation, proof of dispatch, and, for services, written evidence of the non-resident status and the actual offshore benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/ECS) and transit movement certificate
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or airway bill evidencing outside-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation proving payment receipt
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • e-MTA export transaction log and VAT return entry

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital signature mandate exists; B2B e-invoices must be issued within 7 days of the supply, and the invoice digital file must be retained in an accessible, auditable format; Peppol and e-invoice interoperability are recommended for VAT deduction substantiation, and overall electronic transmission and archival of customs and cross-border documents must anticipate e-MTA inspection.

Estonian tax authorities may retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT and related input VAT deductions if export proof is incomplete within 3 years of the return date; intentional fraud attracts fines up to 200% of the tax evaded, statutory penalty payments up to €10,000, and criminal prosecution; inadequate documentation triggers immediate VAT reassessment plus interest at the statutory rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a Eurozone member; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation for non-residents. The EU free movement of capital provisions apply, ensuring unrestricted cross-border flows subject to standard AML/CFT reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. Exporters may retain proceeds in EUR or any other currency, and there are no timelines for repatriation or conversion. Estonia adheres to EU regulations, which prohibit such controls.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) and the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for licensing and AML oversight

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for payment processing and multi-currency accounts. Estonian-licensed EMIs (e.g., Holvi, Monese) or EU-passported EMIs can provide segregated client accounts, FX conversion, and SWIFT/SEPA rails. However, for a regulated crowdfunding portal, the EMI must comply with the Estonian Crowdfunding Act and hold a license from the Financial Supervision Authority. EMIs are not substitutes for a full banking license but can serve as ancillary liquidity providers.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Complex beneficial ownership structure (multiple layers, offshore entities)

Root cause: Complex beneficial ownership structure (multiple layers, offshore entities)

Defense evidence: Provide full corporate registry extract (Äriregister), UBO declaration with percentage holdings, organizational chart, and source of wealth documentation for all ultimate beneficial owners.
High-risk jurisdiction exposure (investors or counterparties from FATF grey-listed countries)

Root cause: High-risk jurisdiction exposure (investors or counterparties from FATF grey-listed countries)

Defense evidence: Implement enhanced transaction monitoring, obtain independent legal opinion on source of funds, and provide evidence of robust AML/KYC policies aligned with Estonian AML Act and EU directives.
Unclear source of client funds for escrow accounts

Root cause: Unclear source of client funds for escrow accounts

Defense evidence: Maintain segregated client accounts with clear audit trails, provide contractual agreements with investors, and submit proof of licensing under the Estonian Crowdfunding Act (or MiFID II if applicable).
Rapid cross-border transaction volume without prior banking relationship

Root cause: Rapid cross-border transaction volume without prior banking relationship

Defense evidence: Present a detailed business plan, projected transaction flows, and a compliance manual covering transaction limits, velocity checks, and sanctions screening. Engage a local AML officer and provide evidence of staff training.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfers via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA (for EUR) using purpose code 'P001' (equity investment) or 'P002' (loan). For non-EUR, use SWIFT with appropriate purpose codes (e.g., 'G300' for equity). Verification includes KYC on the remitter, source of funds declaration, and compliance with the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act. Escrow accounts must be clearly labeled as 'Client Funds' and segregated from operational accounts.Clearing rails for trade settlements include SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers, commercial letters of credit (LC) via MT700, and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated payments. For high-value or cross-border transactions, use correspondent banking networks. All disbursements must be subject to dual authorization and AML screening.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for payment processing and multi-currency accounts. Estonian-licensed EMIs (e.g., Holvi, Monese) or EU-passported EMIs can provide segregated client accounts, FX conversion, and SWIFT/SEPA rails. However, for a regulated crowdfunding portal, the EMI must comply with the Estonian Crowdfunding Act and hold a license from the Financial Supervision Authority. EMIs are not substitutes for a full banking license but can serve as ancillary liquidity providers.
