Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or purely digital registered address is not generally accepted for an LLC intended to obtain an Egyptian Drug Authority manufacturing license. The company must maintain a physical registered office in Egypt, and the designated office address must appear in the Commercial Registry and tax card. For ISIC 2100 CMO/CDMO activities, a domestic physical presence including registered premises and operational substance is required under the Unified Investment Law and the Egyptian Drug Authority facility licensing requirements; however, GAFI may accept a leased serviced office as the registered address for the initial incorporation stage only if the lease agreement identifies the premises as the principal place of business and GAFI is satisfied that actual commercial substance is present. A virtual mailbox without evidence of management activity will not suffice for registration, tax registration, or licensing.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Egyptian Companies Law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC; however, the Egyptian Drug Authority (EDA) requires a minimum paid-in capital of EGP 2,000,000 or as stipulated in the current EDA licensing guidelines for pharmaceutical active ingredient manufacturing. The subscribed capital must be deposited in a blocked bank account in Egypt, evidenced by a bank capital deposit certificate, and released only after the final incorporation certificate and commercial registration are obtained.

Funding & unlock path: The company must receive the Fire Safety and Civil Defense Clearance from the General Administration of Civil Defense of the Ministry of Interior, including review of fire suppression systems, emergency exit capacity, and chemical storage segregation. A structural load and suitability clearance from a licensed engineering consultancy is required for the laboratory fit-out. Occupational safety and health clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 and the EDA GMP facility standards is necessary, covering ventilation, fume hoods, personal protective equipment, chemical hygiene plans, and medical fitness examinations for workers handling hazardous substances.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

General Authority for Taxation (GAT) under the Egyptian Ministry of Finance

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities operating in Egypt, including branches or permanent establishments of foreign companies, must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the General Authority for Taxation. The procedure involves submitting a formal application through the Egyptian Tax Authority portal or via authorized intermediaries, accompanied by supporting documents such as commercial registration certificate, memorandum of association, and proof of legal entity status. Processing typically requires 15-30 business days upon complete submission.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying offshore services are eligible for zero-rating under Egyptian VAT law, provided the supplier demonstrates export activity and repatriation of foreign currency proceeds to Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent proof of foreign currency repatriation

Mandatory participation in the Egyptian Electronic Invoicing System (EIS) for all VAT-registered taxpayers, requiring real-time transmission of invoice data to the Egyptian Tax Authority via API integration, issuance of digital stamps, and compliance with fiscal clearance protocols for audit and payment purposes.

Fraudulent claims for zero-rating may result in penalties equivalent to 200% of the erroneously claimed VAT amount, plus potential criminal prosecution; retrospective clawback of zero-rated VAT and associated tax liabilities if export documentation is deemed insufficient upon audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates an IMF-flanked flexible/managed-float regime for the Egyptian pound: current-account transfers are de jure unrestricted, while capital-account transfers require bank-level verification of the underlying corporate act (capital subscription contract, loan facility agreement, or dividend-approved general assembly minutes) rather than a formal ex-ante CBE licence. Since the March 2024 rate unification, the CBE removed the parallel/street-rate differential, abolished the former import-payment priority queues, and authorised licensed banks to buy and sell foreign currency freely at market rates for genuine trade and investment purposes. The principal practical hurdles for the EG_LLC are procedural: (i) every purchase of USD or EUR from an Egyptian bank requires a completed CBE FX order form and documentary proof of need (invoice, contract, or tax assessment); (ii) dividend and profit remittances require the statutory annual audit, audited financial statements, general assembly dividend allocation, corporate tax return and clearance certificate, and a bank source-of-wealth review; (iii) Egyptian-sourced revenue from domestic sales may not lawfully be routed to an offshore account in lieu of repatriation through an Egyptian bank. The convertibility profile for a CDMO is therefore 'convertible with documentary friction' rather than fully free or capital-controlled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Regulatory baseline (CBE Decree No. 10 of 2016, as amended): every physical export shipment by the EG_LLC must be settled to Egypt within 90 days following the date of customs release of the goods, i.e., the full invoice value must be credited to a licensed Egyptian bank account in USD/EUR or converted EGP. No mandatory percentage conversion or surrender-to-the-CBE requirement is currently in force; 100% of export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency retention account or sold to the bank at the prevailing interbank rate. For contract-manufactured pharmaceutical/biotech products with long post-shipment release cycles (e.g., peptide API batches subject to client stability protocols), the exporter may apply through the collecting bank for an extended settlement period, which the CBE customarily grants up to 8 months for processed manufactured goods and, where the commercial contract contains payment milestones, up to 12 months. The exporter must file within the settlement window the CBE export settlement file (Form C/E) together with the bill of lading, commercial invoice, export customs release certificate, and, for USD receipts, the beneficiary bank credit advice. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero, but export proceeds cannot be deposited into any non-Egyptian EMI or unlicensed foreign account without prior notification to the collecting Egyptian bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary, exchange-rate, and foreign-exchange supervisory authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), acting through the CBE Board of Directors Foreign Exchange Regulations (Decree No. 10 of 2016, as amended by CBE Board Decree No. 412 of 2022 and subsequent circulars implementing the March 2024 unified flexible exchange-rate framework). The General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) co-administers equity-flow recording and investment licensing, while the Egyptian Tax Authority enforces the tax-clearance prerequisite for FX release on profit and dividend remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary clearing rail. Under Law No. 18 of 2019 on Electronic Payment and Transfer of Money and CBE e-payment licensing rules, the CBE and Egyptian commercial banks may not treat an offshore EMI (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Mercury, or Payoneer) as a permitted beneficiary channel for EG_LLC export-proceeds repatriation; export funds must land in an account at a licensed Egyptian bank in the entity's name, and using an offshore EMI as the collection account would breach the 90-day repatriation rule and invalidate the Form C/E settlement file. Feasibility for ancillary treasury use: MEDIUM. The LLC may open an offshore EMI wallet strictly as a disbursement-only tool for non-EGP operating costs (online purchase of chromatography columns, Fmoc amino-acid supplies, analytical kits, and laboratory consumables), funded by documented transfers from the Egyptian bank FX retention account. Binding restrictions: (i) the EMI account must never collect trade proceeds or client invoice payments; (ii) monthly funding cannot exceed the bank-approved working-capital threshold; (iii) each transfer must carry purpose codes matching the LLC's statutory books; and (iv) the EMI must be named as an approved beneficial payee in a standing instruction registered with the Egyptian bank.

Annual Corporate Income Tax Self-Assessment Return Within 4 months after the close of the fiscal year; for a 31 December fiscal year, by 30 April of the following year $0 USD Late filing and payment penalties accrue at 2% per month of the underlying tax due, in addition to interest at 18% per annum; continued failure permits estimated desk assessment and criminal prosecution of directors under Law 91/2005.
Annual Payroll and Salary Withholding Tax Reconciliation Annual reconciliation and settlement due by 28 February covering the preceding calendar year's payroll withholdings $0 USD Late reconciliation attracts a fixed penalty of EGP 1,000–5,000 and monthly interest on undeposited withholdings; persistent failure carries criminal referral and possible closure of the licensed CDMO facility.
