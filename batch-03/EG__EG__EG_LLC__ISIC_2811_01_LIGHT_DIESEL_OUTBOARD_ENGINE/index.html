Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For an LLC under Law 159 of 1981: minimum issued capital of EGP 50,000, fully subscribed at formation; at least 25% of the capital must be paid in cash or in kind into a temporary corporate bank account prior to GAFI registration, and the balance must be paid within five years. No mandatory escrow beyond the temporary bank deposit certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Civil Defense Authority (Ministry of Interior) under Civil Defense Law No. 24 of 1962, covering extinguishers, fire pumps, hydrants, and evacuation plans; structural load verification certificate for the factory floor, mezzanines, and engine test cells from the competent municipality; occupational health and safety clearance from the Ministry of Manpower under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003, including workplace risk assessment, personal protective equipment, and first-aid provisions.

Paid-in capital rule: For an LLC under Law 159 of 1981: minimum issued capital of EGP 50,000, fully subscribed at formation; at least 25% of the capital must be paid in cash or in kind into a temporary corporate bank account prior to GAFI registration, and the balance must be paid within five years. No mandatory escrow beyond the temporary bank deposit certificate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is partially convertible. Current-account payments - trade-related, royalties, services, and profit remittances - may be processed through authorized banks after documentary review, tax clearance, and verification against CBE anti-money laundering rules. Capital-account inflows must be registered with the CBE under Law No. 194 of 2020; profit repatriation additionally requires an audited financial statement, a tax clearance certificate, and confirmation of no foreign-currency claims. Settlement timeliness can be affected by national FX liquidity, bank credit lines, and the CBE's priority list.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated to Egypt through an authorized bank within 90 days after shipment date (with limited extension for counter-trade or staging). Under current CBE directives applying post-2024 float, mandatory conversion ratios have been substantially relaxed; however, a bank may still impose a reasonable conversion quota on EGP for operating needs. Exporters must file customs export declarations, commercial invoices, and the CBE's export proceeds tracking forms; un-repatriated proceeds are subject to statutory penalties and potential trade-license impact.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE); the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) provides FDI registration coordination.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW (offshore EMIs) / MEDIUM (CBE-licensed onshore fintechs). Offshore digital EMIs cannot open Egyptian EGP settlement accounts, provide local clearing, or obtain CBE forex allocations; their use is limited to ancillary receivables collection outside Egypt and is subject to corporate bank consent. Onshore platforms holding a CBE Payment FinTech license may support API-driven collection and multi-currency pass-through, but hedging, LC issuance, and statutory foreign-currency accounts remain the exclusive domain of authorized banks. Thus, an industrial exporter should rely on licensed banks for core clearing; EMIs are only complementary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: Mandatory where related-party transactions exceed EGP 10 million in aggregate; Master File: required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EGP 100 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies for groups with consolidated revenue above EGP 25 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no formal Economic Substance Regulations styled after UAE/ESR, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 102 of Law No. 91/2005) and ETA guidance require that intercompany arrangements reflect genuine commercial and economic substance; shell structures, circular transactions, and artificial allocation of risks or investments may be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Fine of EGP 20,000–200,000 and imprisonment of 2–5 years under Article 119 of Law No. 91/2005, in addition to recovery of the full avoided tax, 2% monthly late-payment surcharge, and up to 50% late-filing penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and signatories involved in or benefiting from evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax; the corporate veil is pierced, with civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and custodial sentences imposed.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ETA may re-assess taxable income at arm's-length amounts, deny excessive interest deductions, apply a 2% monthly surcharge on underpaid tax, and levy an additional non-disclosure penalty of up to 35% of the adjusted tax (Executive Regulations).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve pricing without robust transfer-pricing studies may be personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall; Egyptian courts can hold them as participants in concealment, with travel bans and seizure of personal assets being available.
Shell Company / Abuse of Legal Form (Economic Substance Failure)

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule invalidates the transaction and re-characterises income to the true beneficial owner; the entity may be fined or subjected to compulsory liquidation and administrative sanctions.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and hidden beneficial owners are personally liable for taxes, fraud penalties, and criminal prosecution when the shell is used as a vehicle for evasion or unlawful distribution.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002, fines up to EGP 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years, plus mandatory asset freezing, confiscation of laundered funds, and revocation of export/import licences.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face civil restitution, personal travel bans, disqualification from corporate board service, and imprisonment where they knowingly facilitate or fail to file suspicious-transaction reports with the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Unit.
