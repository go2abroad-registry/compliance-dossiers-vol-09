Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Registries Department of the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK) - e-Business Register (Äriregister), Lõõtsa 4, 11415 Tallinn; digital service at register.rik.ee.

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik) of 15 February 1995, as amended - Chapter 8 'Private Limited Company' (Osaühing), Sections 135-142 (foundation, share capital, legal effect of registration), Sections 167-172 (management board); Business Name Act (Ärinimeseadus) for name rules; State Fees Act (Riigilõivuseadus) for registry fees (EUR 265 electronic filing / EUR 350 paper filing; USD equivalent calculated at 1 EUR = 1.125 USD).

Formation / constitutional filings

Registration application for entry in the commercial register (avaldus äriregistrisse kandmiseks) submitted electronically, accompanied by the foundation agreement (asutamisleping) and articles of association (põhikiri), executed by digital signature or notarized.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 10 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital: the Commercial Code permits formation of an OÜ with zero or any positive nominal capital fixed in the articles. If a positive amount is fixed, monetary contributions must be paid by the term determined in the foundation agreement (outer statutory limit: ten years from registration). No escrow or blocked-account requirement is imposed by Estonian law; in-kind contributions require an independent valuation by an authorized auditor.

Funding & unlock path: No dedicated civil-defense clearance applies to office premises. Mandatory workplace measures: (i) occupational health and safety internal-control procedure under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus), covering risk assessment, first aid, and workstation ergonomics; (ii) fire-safety inspection and fire-action plan under the Rescue Act (Päästeseadus) coordinated with the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) for the leased office; (iii) verification of the landlord's building-use permit and structural-load safety in the e-construction register (Ehitisregister) before occupation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital: the Commercial Code permits formation of an OÜ with zero or any positive nominal capital fixed in the articles. If a positive amount is fixed, monetary contributions must be paid by the term determined in the foundation agreement (outer statutory limit: ten years from registration). No escrow or blocked-account requirement is imposed by Estonian law; in-kind contributions require an independent valuation by an authorized auditor.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - ISIC 6201 (computer programming activities) is an unlicensed economic activity under the Economic Activities Code (Majandustegevuse seadustik); no industrial licensing board has jurisdiction over software development or payroll-SaaS platform operations.

Category: No operating license or activity permit (tegevusluba) is prescribed for the SaaS payroll service. Closest regulatory touchpoints: (i) personal-data processing for payroll must comply with the Personal Data Protection Act (Isikuandmete kaitse seadus) and the GDPR, requiring only an Article 30 record of processing; (ii) the platform is not a credit or payment institution, so no financial-services license under the Credit Institutions Act or Payment Institutions Act applies; (iii) payment-rail execution remains the responsibility of the client's licenced bank.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, 'MTA'); official website: https://www.emta.ee; secure self-service portal: e-MTA (https://e-mta.emta.ee). All corporate tax, VAT, customs, OSS, Intrastat and statistical filings are submitted digitally through e-MTA; non-resident users authenticate using the Estonian e-Residency digital ID card for qualified electronic signatures.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of an Osaühing (OÜ) via the e-Business Register (Äriregister, https://ariregister.rik.ee), the MTA automatically registers the company in the tax register and assigns a national corporate tax registration code (registrikood: an 8-digit base code with a check digit). Foreign founders and board members must first obtain an Estonian e-Residency smart card (or an Estonian personal identification code) to sign incorporation documents and access e-MTA. For VAT registration: file the KÄID application in e-MTA; registration is mandatory where taxable turnover in a calendar year reaches or foreseeably exceeds EUR 40,000, or where intra-EU acquisitions or distance sales trigger registration; the MTA issues a VAT number in the format 'EE' + 9 digits (e.g., EE100123456). Foreign entities without a local seat may register as non-resident VAT payers for reverse charge, EU VAT refund procedures and the One-Stop-Shop, appointing an authorised Estonian representative where required. On registration, resident OÜs are automatically enrolled in Estonia's distributed-profit corporate income tax model: undistributed retained earnings are taxed at 0%, and only profit distributions are taxable at 20/80 or 14/86 rates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no check-the-box or discretionary entity-classification regime; classification is statutory. Available elections: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the EUR 40,000 threshold, granting input VAT deduction rights (VAT Act § 3(3)); (ii) VAT group registration for closely linked resident persons (VAT Act § 9); (iii) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) Union and non-Union schemes for cross-border distance sales and B2C electronically supplied services, including payroll SaaS subscriptions; (iv) election to apply the reduced 14/86 corporate income tax rate on regular profit distributions (Income Tax Act § 50(3)); (v) participation-exemption claims on dividends and capital gains from foreign subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are automatically neutralised under Estonia's ATAD implementation; no election to apply or disapply hybrid rules exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year (VAT Act § 3(1)); registration is mandatory from the moment the threshold is exceeded or when business activity is begun with a clearly foreseeable turnover in excess of EUR 40,000; voluntary registration is permitted. Reduced VAT rates of 9% and 5% apply to designated goods and services; the standard rate has been 22% since 1 July 2024.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods are zero-rated where the goods are physically removed from the EU and the exporter holds customs or equivalent transport evidence (VAT Act § 15). For the Payroll SaaS Platform (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541214), the supply is an electronically supplied service: (a) B2B supply to a non-EU customer is outside the scope of Estonian VAT (place of supply is the customer's country) and is effectively 0%; (b) B2B supply to an EU customer in another member state is not subject to Estonian VAT, with reverse charge applied by the customer; (c) B2C supply to a non-EU consumer is outside VAT scope (0%); (d) B2C supply to EU consumers is taxed at the destination member state's VAT rate via the OSS Union scheme; (e) only supplies to Estonian-established customers (B2B or B2C) attract Estonian VAT at 22%. Zero-rated treatment requires a compliant invoice, proof of customer establishment (EU VAT number, non-EU business registration or official ID), and electronic delivery records such as access logs, usage reports, or handover certificates.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD / EX) with TARIC classification and MRN reference
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading (sea), CMR (road), or Air Waybill (air) evidencing dispatch from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 proof of foreign-currency receipt from the foreign buyer
  • Export contract, commercial invoice, and electronic service-delivery evidence (access logs, usage reports, or handover certificates)

No real-time fiscal clearance, government authorisation API, or digital stamping mandate is currently in force in Estonia for B2B or B2C transactions. E-invoicing is mandatory only for invoices issued to Estonian public-sector entities under EU Directive 2014/55/EU (EN 16931 / PEPPOL format). B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary but is widely adopted. The e-MTA portal supports electronic VAT returns, VIES, Intrastat, and bulk XML/API uploads, but does not operate a transaction-level pre-clearance system. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, mandatory e-invoicing and digital transaction reporting are expected around 2030; no such mandate is operational today.

The MTA applies risk-scored selection (outlier margins, repeated threshold extensions, TARIC anomalies, missing FX evidence) to audit zero-rating claims. The statutory reassessment window is 3 years from the end of the tax period, extended to 5 years where intentional tax evasion is established. If the exporter cannot substantiate customs exit, real economic activity, or receipt of payment within 180 days from supply, the MTA recharacterises the supply as taxable in Estonia, assesses VAT at 22%, and applies late-payment interest of 0.06% per day (circa 21.9% per annum) from the original due date. Fraudulent export zero-rating triggers criminal prosecution under the Estonian Penal Code tax-fraud provisions (Penal Code §§ 397-1 – 397-2): natural persons face imprisonment of up to 7 years in aggravated cases, and legal persons face fines of up to EUR 1,600,000, confiscation of proceeds, retrospective clawback of VAT refunded, cancellation of the VAT registration, and public disclosure of sanctions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 annual aggregate for controlled goods transactions; EUR 25,000 annual aggregate for controlled services transactions

Economic substance scope: No general ESR regime in Estonia. However, tax treaty benefits and Parent-Subsidiary / Interest-Royalty Directive relief require real economic substance, local management decisions, physical presence, payroll and IT servers in Estonia. Estonian tax authorities apply OECD BEPS substance tests, especially for digital payroll SaaS platforms.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Back taxes plus tax surcharge at 0.06% per day; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000; imprisonment up to 7 years

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause false declarations are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes; travel bans can be imposed pending criminal proceedings; personal bankruptcy and disqualification from board service
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Tax recharacterisation of excessive payments as constructive dividends taxed at 20%; additional corporate income tax; late interest 0.06% per day; penalty up to 50% of underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's length pricing may face civil liability for the tax loss; in severe intentional cases, criminal liability for tax fraud with imprisonment up to 7 years
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief; dividend taxed at 20% rather than 0%; fines for incorrect TRC usage; forced deregistration of the company by the commercial registry; director disqualification

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes if the company's separate legal personality is disregarded (asset tracing); travel bans applicable for non-resident directors under extradition arrangements for tax offenses
AML and Illegal Payroll Processing

Statutory sanction: Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act fines up to EUR 2,000,000; revocation of payment institution license; prohibition of platform operations

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability up to 7 years imprisonment; civil liability for AML fines; travel ban imposed by the Financial Intelligence Unit pending investigation
