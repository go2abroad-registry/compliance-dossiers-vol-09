Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an OÜ; it may be formed with zero share capital or a nominal share capital fixed in the articles. No escrow or court deposit is required. If a positive monetary share capital is declared, the contribution must be paid into a bank account in the company's name (or evidenced as an authenticated contribution in kind) before or at registration, as agreed in the foundation deed.

Funding & unlock path: For the registered office, the lessor's building permit/occupancy certificate and fire-safety certificate must be current; the employer must perform a workplace risk assessment and maintain occupational-health clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act if staff are engaged in Estonia. No supplementary civil-defense clearance is required for a stock-photo/AI-content platform.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); administrative portal e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee/eng); company registration portal: Estonian Business Register (https://ariregister.rik.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign founder incorporates an OÜ (Osaühing) online in the Estonian Business Register using an e-Residency digital ID; no minimum share capital is required and the registry code is issued immediately. The registry code is the permanent business TIN used by the MTA for corporate income tax, VAT and social tax reporting. The company activates its e-MTA account and submits the TSD payroll/income tax declaration; separately, VAT registration (voluntary or compulsory) is applied for on the KMD form. MTA issues the VAT identification number in the format EE followed by a numeric block. Non-resident suppliers of digital stock-photo services may instead use the EU OSS portal for EU B2C e-supplies; if a local Estonian VAT registration is indispensable, a non-established taxable person registers through e-MTA and must appoint an authorized tax representative for compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a Eurozone member state with full euro adoption since 2011. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions pursuant to EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital. Estonian law imposes no foreign-exchange licensing requirement for ordinary trade receipts, capital injections, dividends, or intellectual-property royalty outflows. Profit remittances are unrestricted in form and currency; however, corporate distributions are taxable at the level of the distributing Osaühing under Estonia's distribution-based corporate income tax regime (generally 20% of gross distribution, or 14% for regular distributions under the reduced rate conditions). Non-resident profit repatriation therefore encounters no FX-control hurdle, but board-approved distribution resolutions, tax residency certificates, and withholding-tax reporting are expected by the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia maintains no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency without surrender obligations. Banks nevertheless apply BPM6 statistical-reporting and sanctions-screening procedures; a stock-media export platform may aggregate cross-border receivables through licensed payment institutions without any obligation to convert or repatriate within a prescribed period.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the European System of Central Banks, with banking licensing and AML supervision jointly administered by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority) and the Financial Intelligence Information Unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-high for duly licensed EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions passporting into Estonia under the E-Money Directive; offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport may not lawfully hold settlement funds or issue payment accounts in Estonia. For the stock-photo marketplace, an EU-licensed EMI is suitable for payouts to global contributors and API-driven collections but should remain secondary to a tier-1 Estonian commercial bank as the primary treasury and capital-escrow institution. The AML burden for digital platforms with non-face-to-face onboarding remains elevated; payment institutions may impose additional velocity monitoring and source-of-funds documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the Estonian OÜ are executed via SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pain.001 messages with the purpose code S130 or equivalent equity-investment classification. The remitting bank must include the OÜ's registered business name and Eesti e-Business Register registry code; supporting documentation comprises board resolution for share issuance, share subscription agreement, updated share register, and tax-control declarations. No Eesti Pank or Finantsinspektsioon approval is required; the receiving bank completes only enhanced due diligence, sanctions filtering, and Bank-of-Estonia statistical reporting. If the subscription price is received in a foreign currency, the bank may credit the euro capital account using the ECB reference rate or a contracted spot rate.For EEA-based contributors and trade creditors, disbursements clear through SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer via TARGET2 and EBA CLEARING rails. For non-EEA jurisdictions, SWIFT MT103 and MT202 correspondent-banking messages are used, supported by CBPR+ ISO 20022 migration standards. Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 are reserved for physical-collateral or high-value visual-asset licensing transactions requiring documentary payment security.
