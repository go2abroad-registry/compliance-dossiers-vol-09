Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egypt Tax Authority (ETA) – Al-Hay'ah Al-Dharibiyah Al-Masriyah; a semi-autonomous body under the Ministry of Finance established by Law No. 73/2018, merging income tax, VAT, stamp duty and e-invoicing functions. Portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipt clearance portal: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg (RASEMED).

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian LLC (EG_LLC) engaged in dental CAD/CAM block manufacturing obtains its unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT ID as follows: (1) incorporate with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and extract the Commercial Registry certificate; (2) file ETA Tax Registration Form No. 2 (Corporate Bodies) with Articles of Association, certified translation, power of attorney to the appointed local tax representative, registered-office lease, valid legal IDs and bank confirmation; (3) register for VAT within 30 days of the turnover trigger; (4) activate RASEMED e-invoicing sub-accounts and digital-certificate credentials. GAFI one-stop-shop issuance is 3–5 business days; standard ETA issuance is 3–4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the LLC is a separate juridical person under Companies Law 159/1981 and a tax resident under Article 3 of Income Tax Law 91/2005 (legal seat or place of effective management in Egypt); it is taxed in its own name at the 22.5% corporate rate on worldwide income with no pass-through to shareholders.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity elections exist in Egyptian law; entity-level taxation is mandatory. Available tax-character elections include: cash-basis accounting for turnover up to EGP 20 million; spreading/amortisation of pre-incorporation and pre-production expenditures; accelerated depreciation and investment-incentive allowances under Law 72/2017; VAT cash-basis for turnover below EGP 3 million; transfer-pricing contemporaneous-documentation de minimis election below EGP 5 million of related-party transactions; and election to join the ETA VAT export-refund fast-track scheme.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 — mandatory registration when annual taxable supplies of goods and services, actual or expected, exceed EGP 500,000 in any 12-month period (Art. 18, VAT Law 67/2016); voluntary registration from EGP 50,000, and exporters may register below the threshold to preserve zero-rating and refund rights.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 11 of VAT Law 67/2016, a dentist-facing or milling-centre-facing supply of zirconia blocks is zero-rated where the exporter proves physical export from Egypt to a foreign destination, sale to a non-resident purchaser, dispatch within the lawful shipment window, and receipt of proceeds in foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system. Export documentation must be contemporaneous; missing export proof converts the transaction to the standard 14% rate with VAT declared in the month of re-characterisation.

  • Customs export declaration (Form C1) bearing ETA stamp and ACI/Nafeza platform reference
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR Road Consignment Note naming the Egyptian LLC as consignor
  • Inward foreign-currency remittance certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian bank for 100% of the export proceeds
  • Executed sale contract / commercial invoice and packing list
  • ETA-validated e-invoice (XML + QR) referencing the customs export declaration number
  • Manufacturing certificate and tariff-classification justification (HS 9021.29 dental-material line) for the CAD/CAM zirconia blocks

Mandatory and real-time: all B2B invoices, including zero-rated dental block exports, must run through the ETA RASEMED platform. JSON/XML files must be submitted synchronously to the ETA REST validation gateway; ETA returns a validation code and UUID embedded in a PDF/A3 with the ETA electronic seal and QR matrix only after successful clearance. Zero-rated invoices must reference the customs export declaration and ACI number. Rejected or non-validated invoices carry no fiscal effect and block the VAT refund; e-receipt publishing is additionally required for domestic dental-lab B2C sales.

Export zero-rating claims are high-risk audit targets; ETA and the Customs Authority scrutinise the full chain. Defective proof or late export re-characterises the supply at 14%, triggering a 2% monthly delay fine (Art. 30, Law 206/2020, capped at 100% of principal), an additional tax penalty of up to 100% of evaded tax with a minimum EGP 5,000 fine (Art. 76, VAT Law 67/2016), and, for forged documents or fraudulent claims, imprisonment of 3–5 years plus fines of 1–5 times the evaded tax (Art. 80, VAT Law 67/2016). Retroactive clawback of any refund paid, statutory interest, exclusion from the VAT refund fast-track for 1–3 years, and, in customs-related fraud, penalties and forfeiture under Customs Law 207/2020 apply; export-tax-rebate amounts are also recoverable with interest where the claimant is debarred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP has operated a managed-flexible regime since the March 2024 CBE and IMF Extended Fund Facility reforms; the previous import-priority FX queue was abolished. Current-account convertibility (trade settlements, service fees, dividends, and profit remittances) is unrestricted in principle but subject to documentary verification: each transfer requires the registered economic-ownership evidence, underlying invoices or audited financials, tax clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority (especially for dividend/profit repatriation), and a CBE-designated currency-declaration form completed by the licensed bank. Capital-account convertibility (foreign equity injection, shareholder loans, liquidation proceeds repatriation) is permitted but transaction-level: the inflow must be routed through a CBE-licensed bank and correctly purpose-coded, and the bank records the foreign-currency deposit evidence that forms the basis for later capital and dividend repatriation rights. Residual hurdles are documentary (tax clearance, audited financials, beneficial-ownership transparency, and matching of trade documents) rather than absolute quantity limits; during 2022-2023 FX shortages the CBE's discretionary surrender and prioritization rules caused settlement delays, but those were removed in the unified interbank market reforms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of manufactured goods, including dental zirconia blocks, must repatriate 100% of export proceeds into Egypt through the banking system within 180 days from the shipping date (bill of lading or airway bill); CBE may shorten this window to 90 days during BoP stress periods, as it did in 2022-2023. The exporter may retain the foreign currency in a USD/EUR export-receipts account and deploy it for permitted import-payment and trade purposes; mandatory full surrender into EGP is not a standing rule, but any local-currency settlement must be at the prevailing interbank rate via the CBE-licensed bank. The export transaction is reconciled against the Nafeza single-window customs filing and the exporter's Commercial Registry scope, and failure to repatriate within the statutory window exposes the entity to CBE penalties, suspension of banking facilities, and inclusion of the exporter's name in negative AML lists.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the sole monetary authority and banking/FX regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), acting with the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) on capital-market movements and with the Ministry of Finance on exchange-rate and external-debt policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. CBE regulatory architecture requires all FX conversion, export-proceeds receipt, and repatriation flows to pass through CBE-licensed banks; electronic money institutions (EMIs) hold only e-money licenses and cannot independently execute SWIFT cross-border FX settlements. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, MultiPass, Payoneer) may aggregate receivable collections but the legal settlement leg for Egyptian exporters must terminate at a local licensed bank; foreign EMIs cannot onboard the Egyptian LLC as a direct clearing customer for EGP balances. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity and shareholder loans must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (TT) to the LLC's CBE-licensed bank, with strict narration specifying 'Equity Injection — EGY LLC' or 'Shareholders Loan — EGY LLC' and referencing the Commercial Registry number. The receiving bank completes the CBE currency-declaration form and issues an inward-transfer receipt evidencing the FDI inflow, which is the legal basis for future dividend, interest, and capital-repatriation flows. All inbound high-value wires (over the CBE-recognized threshold) require pre-notification to the bank one business day in advance; the bank verifies the remitter's beneficial-ownership documentation, the LLC's IDA license, and the board resolution authorizing the capital increase before crediting the account.Import settlements for zirconia powder, milling tools, and packaging materials use SWIFT MT103/TT for advance payments with the declared purpose code 'Import Payment — Machinery' or 'Import Payment — Raw Materials', and documentary letters of credit (sight LC via SWIFT MT700) for large purchase contracts; commercial collections (documents against payment) are accepted for intermediate value ranges. Export collections for dental CAD/CAM blocks flow from overseas distributors via SWIFT MT103 or bank-guaranteed payment undertakings; the exporter presents the export invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and conformity/CE/FDA technical file to the bank for proceeds repatriation and Nafeza customs matching. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 9,000,000 (total value of controlled transactions with domestic and foreign related parties during the taxable year) triggers mandatory preparation of a TP disclosure form (Form B) and full Local File / Master File documentation.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the Gulf-style form, but Egyptian tax law applies the substance-over-form doctrine and a general anti-abuse rule (Article 53, Law 91/2005); a contract-manufacturing entity with no local staff, office, production, or storage may be recharacterised as a conduit or sham, leading to disallowance of intercompany payments, deemed dividends, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or Incomplete Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 100%–200% of evaded tax under Law 91/2005, Article 133, plus imprisonment up to 5 years; monthly surcharge of 2% on unpaid tax; seizure and freezing of bank accounts; suspension of the manufacturing licence and customs privileges.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for evaded taxes and penalties; courts may impose travel bans, freezing of personal assets, and imprisonment when fraud is proven; civil restitution orders strip the corporate veil for sham LLCs.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: ETA adjustment to arm's-length value; disallowance of deduced payments; additional tax at 22.5% on recharacterised dividends; penalty of 20% on underpaid tax plus monthly surcharges; potential criminal referral for deliberate mispricing.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally sanctioned or executed mispricing without a defensible benchmark study may be jointly and severally liable for underpaid tax and penalties; veil-piercing may be applied to hold individual assets; travel bans and disqualification from holding board positions can be imposed.
AML / Financial Reporting (Commercial Shell Structuring)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 3 to 15 years and fine up to EGP 10,000,000 under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended); confiscation of laundered funds; forced closure of the manufacturing facility and export licence revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable if they knowingly participate in laundering or concealment; personal asset freeze, travel ban, and disqualification from managing any Egyptian company are imposed; civil liability extends to full restitution of concealed funds plus interest.
