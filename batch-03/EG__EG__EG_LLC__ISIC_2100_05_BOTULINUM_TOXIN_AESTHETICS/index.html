Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - One-Stop Shop for Company Formation, operating under the Ministry of Investment, in coordination with the Commercial Registry Department of the Ministry of Justice and the local notary public.

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 4 of 2023 (governing joint-stock companies, partnerships limited by shares and limited liability companies), Title on Limited Liability Companies covering formation, share capital, quota transfer, management and registration; Commercial Registry Law No. 34 of 1976; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; anti-money-laundering beneficial-ownership declaration under Law No. 80/2002 as amended by Law No. 199/2020.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association of the Limited Liability Company (Eaqd Ta'sis), executed in Arabic, notarized before a licensed notary public, and filed electronically through the GAFI Investor Portal (investinegypt.gov.eg).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 107 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed under Law No. 4 of 2023; founders may freely determine the share capital for an LLC and may contribute in cash or in kind. The declared capital must be evidenced by a bank capital-deposit certificate (Shahadat Al-Ida) issued by an Egyptian licensed bank; no escrow account is statutorily mandated. For the biopharmaceutical use case, the Egyptian Drug Authority will additionally require proof of financial capacity commensurate with the fermentation, lyophilization and cold-chain facility build-out and process validation budget.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and means-of-escape certification from the Civil Defense Administration of the Ministry of Interior covering the lyophilization and cold-storage areas; structural load-bearing inspection certificate for the freeze-dryer chambers and cold-chain vaults; and occupational-health workplace clearance from the Ministry of Manpower under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003, imposing mandatory biological-risk medical surveillance for all production and quality-control personnel.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed under Law No. 4 of 2023; founders may freely determine the share capital for an LLC and may contribute in cash or in kind. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, operating under the Unified Tax Law No. 91/2024 (which unified income tax, VAT and stamp duty procedure). Official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-Invoice clearance platform: https://einvoice.invoicing.eta.gov.eg; e-Receipt platform: https://ereceipt.invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of the Egyptian LLC with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) one-stop shop and issuance of the commercial registry certificate, the LLC must file the unified tax registration application with the ETA within 30 days. The ETA assigns a single Unified Tax Number (UTN) which replaces the former separate income tax TIN, VAT ID and tax card. VAT registration is also mandatory within 60 days of annual taxable supplies exceeding EGP 500,000 (VAT Law No. 67/2016, Art. 20). Foreign shareholders and managers may execute filings through notarized and consular-legalized powers of attorney; no Egyptian residency or board majority condition applies to tax registration. The UTN is a precondition for ETA e-invoice clearance, customs export declarations and VAT refund claims.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident entity taxed on worldwide net income at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5%; no statutory pass-through regime exists. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident entity taxed on worldwide net income at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5%; no statutory pass-through regime exists. Facilities established within the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) may qualify for a preferential 5% territorial corporate tax treatment, but the statutory classification remains entity-level, not transparent.

Available election forms

None comparable to US check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections: the Unified Tax Law attributes income to the LLC as a mandatory separate taxable person. Available procedural elections are limited to (i) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold to recover input VAT; (ii) choice of a corporate tax year matching the financial year (with ETA approval if not 31 December or 30 June); (iii) application for advance pricing agreements (APA) and specific tax rulings; and (iv) treatment of a pure contractual joint venture as transparent as opposed to incorporation of a joint venture company. Hybrid arrangements are re-characterized by the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) without taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies once annual taxable supplies of goods and services exceed EGP 500,000 (VAT Law No. 67/2016, Art. 20). Exporters are registrable regardless of threshold because an export is itself a taxable supply at the 0% rate; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold for input VAT recovery purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0% VAT) applies to the export of goods, including botulinum toxin formulations (HS 3002), under Art. 22 of VAT Law No. 67/2016 provided that: (i) the goods physically leave Egypt under customs control within the Executive Regulation period (generally 90 days from issuance of the tax invoice); (ii) the invoice and contract are concluded with a non-resident buyer; (iii) the consideration is paid in convertible foreign currency and actually received through an Egyptian bank account; (iv) the customs export declaration, transport documents and banking receipts match the invoice details; and (v) the export invoice receives ETA e-invoice clearance. Qualifying offshore services to non-residents (e.g., cold-chain logistics, laboratory testing, R&D) may also be zero-rated where the service is performed for and benefits a non-resident and is not consumed in Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs statement/'Bayan' exit declaration) with HS Code 3002 and cold chain details, endorsed by Egypt Customs
  • Clean on-board Bill of Lading or Master/House Air Waybill evidencing physical export to the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice and signed export sales contract or purchase order (Arabic or English) stating Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Currency Receipt Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency
  • Certificate of Origin where required by the destination country or preferential regime (COMESA, AfCFTA, GAFTA)
  • Egyptian Drug Authority (EDA) export authorization and GMP/quality certificates for the regulated biologic (botulinum toxin)

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing for B2B supplies and phased e-receipts for B2C supplies apply to all VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted in real time to the ETA clearance platform via a certified ERP/API integration using structured XML/JSON, digital certificates, a unique UUID and a QR stamp; only ETA-approved invoices are tax-compliant and entitle the buyer to input VAT deduction. Rejected or uncleared invoices trigger no-deduction consequences. Tax records, XML files and integration logs must be retained for 5 years in Arabic or English, and ETA analytics cross-match invoice data with customs and banking information.

Export VAT refunds are subject to ETA risk-scored desk and on-site audits; refund applications are processed within approximately 60 days of completeness, but disbursement is frozen if customs, transport or banking evidence is missing or inconsistent. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day Central Bank of Egypt deadline, or any misstatement of the export declaration, triggers retroactive clawback of the 0% treatment, assessment of 14% VAT on the gross export value, late-payment interest at 2% per month, and fines of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000 per violation. Fraudulent or forged zero-rating documentation constitutes tax evasion punishable by imprisonment from 1 to 5 years plus the statutory fines and mandatory repayment of the suppressed tax and interest; the ETA may also suspend the exporter's e-invoice clearance profile, record a tax lien on the LLC's assets, and publicly blacklist the non-compliant exporter.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt adheres to current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII, but maintains a controlled capital-account framework. The EGP is not freely tradable offshore and all investor capital inflows, dividend remittances, loan servicing, and trade-related transfers must pass through CBE-licensed banks operating in Egypt. Profit/remittance hurdles: the repatriating entity must provide audited financial statements, a valid tax clearance certificate confirming settled Egyptian corporate and withholding taxes, supporting board resolutions, and the CBE-designated foreign-currency application forms. During periods of FX scarcity the CBE has enacted prioritization rules (e.g., Decree No. 89/2022 requiring bank approval for certain import transactions) which can delay outbound USD/EUR transfers; current liquidity environment is interbank-determined with a managed-flexible exchange rate, but treasury must monitor CBE circulars for temporary transfer restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: export proceeds must be repatriated and credited to an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the bill of lading date. Mandatory conversion ratio: 100% of export receipts must be transferred through the Egyptian banking system as wire transfers; offshore retention or deferral beyond the statutory window is prohibited without a CBE-specific exemption. Export receipts are credited to a foreign-currency export account and may be retained in FX, used for documented obligations, or converted to EGP at the prevailing interbank rate; in periods of heightened FX shortage the CBE may activate temporary mandatory-currency-orientation requirements for a stated percentage of proceeds, which treasury must track prospectively.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE; Al-Bank Al-Markazi Al-Masri), operating as the sole monetary authority, issuer of the EGP, administrator of the Unified Exchange Control Rules, and licensor of all banking, foreign-exchange, and payment-service activities under Banking and Central Bank Law No. 194 of 2020, together with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for capital-entry registration and the Egyptian Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) for non-bank oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. CBE Law No. 194/2020 restricts deposit-taking, foreign-exchange execution, and cross-border payment-facilitation to licensed banks and licensed PSPs; offshore digital EMI providers without an Egyptian banking branch cannot clear EGP, cannot receive CBE-mandated export proceeds, and cannot act as the statutory channel for tax or loan payments. An offshore EMI may only serve as a secondary supporting rail for small working-capital disbursements in hard currency outside Egypt, but it must never be used for EOF/export-proceed repatriation or any CBE-regulated RE form. If an EMI is used, treasury must pre-clear the arrangement with the CBE relationship bank to avoid shadow-banking findings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI equity and shareholder loans must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the overseas shareholder's bank to the Egyptian bank's nostro/correspondent account, bearing a precise purpose-code reference (Foreign Direct Investment, Equity Contribution or Shareholder Loan under the CBE Unified Exchange Control Rules) and the GAFI investment-file registration number. The receiving bank executes an inward-remittance FX/EGP receipt and credits the Capital Escrow Account pending full corporate KYC and CBE registration; post-registration funds are transferred to the Operational Account. Verification bundle: certified board resolutions; executed share-subscription agreement; source-of-wealth statements; audited parent financial statements; copies of the EDA/GAFI permits and tax card; apostilled passports of the UBOs; and any CBE-specific acknowledgment if the sector is deemed strategic or if the injected amount exceeds local transaction-reporting thresholds.Operational payments (freight, insurance, consultants, cold-chain audits, marketing) use SWIFT MT103 routed through the CBE bank after each underlying invoice is validated against the import registration and tax-file requirements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Egyptian TP law, a taxpayer must prepare a Local File and a Master File where controlled transactions with related parties exceed EGP 15,000,000 in aggregate per fiscal year; for smaller controlled transactions between EGP 6,000,000 and EGP 15,000,000, a summary TP disclosure form and abbreviated documentation are required; the 2024 ETA TP guidelines require a contemporaneous benchmark and economic-substance review for all high-risk related-party transactions including botulinum toxin intellectual property licensing

Economic substance scope: Egyptian tax law and administrative practice require the existence of real operational, financial and strategic substance; a botulinum toxin aesthetics LLC operating in Egypt must show actual laboratory premises, trained staff, cold-chain infrastructure, procurement decisions, quality-control procedures, manufacturing risk, and the capacity to bear entrepreneurial risk; mere contract manufacturing or passive routing of licensing income to a foreign affiliate triggers recharacterization under the anti-avoidance doctrine, and taxpayers are expected to satisfy the economic substance requirements analogous to those prescribed for investment-free-zone companies: physical presence, qualified employees, and substantive management in Egypt

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Article 133 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 imposes imprisonment of not less than two years and not more than five years and a fine equal to the full amount of evaded tax, or one of these penalties; the court additionally orders payment of the tax shortfall plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge; for large corporate tax evasion, the amount of the fine is doubled

Director personal liability: Directors, managers and shareholders who sign or approve false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penal surcharges; courts have authorized travel bans, seizure of personal bank accounts, attachment of personal shares, and disqualification of the director from acting as a manager of any Egyptian company for up to five years
Shell Company and Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The ETA will disregard the LLC's separate existence, treat the foreign parent as having a permanent establishment in Egypt, deny treaty relief, reclassify remitted royalties as taxable Egyptian-source distributions, apply a 10% dividend withholding tax, and impose a late-payment surcharge of 2% per month on unpaid tax; criminal fines range from EGP 500,000 to EGP 5,000,000 for repeatedly facilitating tax avoidance through artificial entities

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and controlling shareholders are exposed to personal liability for the corporate tax debt when the impenetrable shell is used as a fraud instrument; civil courts may pierce the corporate veil and order restitution of all underpaid taxes, the Ministry of Interior may issue a travel ban, and criminal conviction for obstruction of tax authority oversight carries imprisonment of up to three years
AML and Special-License Violation for Bio-Botulinum Toxin

Statutory sanction: Violations of Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 and Cabinet Decree No. 16 of 2019 carry administrative fines from EGP 200,000 to EGP 5,000,000 per violation; the Egyptian Drug Authority may revoke the special biological license, and the Central Bank of Egypt may block the LLC's bank accounts; criminal sanctions include imprisonment of up to five years for laundering proceeds from a high-risk licensed business

Director personal liability: The compliance officer, the managing director and every board member who approved the suspicious payment are individually liable to the Financial Intelligence Unit; personal accounts can be frozen, foreign travel is banned, and directors may be held personally liable for the forfeiture of all proceeds traced to the unlicensed transfer; imprisonment of up to five years and permanent prohibition from managing financial or medical-business entities may be imposed
