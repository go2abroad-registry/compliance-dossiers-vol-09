Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital is separately escrowed for registration in the e-Business Register; the founding shareholders may declare a nominal share capital as low as EUR 1.00. However, if the declared capital exceeds EUR 2,500 or includes non-cash contributions, a bank payment confirmation or an asset valuation report by an authorized auditor must be attached. A commercial bank account must be opened in the company's name to receive any capital contribution; the registry does not require a dedicated escrow account unless in-kind contributions are used.

Funding & unlock path: For the registered office premises, a fire safety certificate or a valid fire inspection under the Rescue Act (Päästeseadus) is required. Structural load inspections are the landlord's obligation and are evidenced by the building use permit. If employees are engaged, an occupational health and safety clearance is required under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-service portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Osaühing (OÜ) must first register the company in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister). Upon registration, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board automatically issues a tax identification number (TIN) and a VAT payer identification number (if VAT registration is required or elected). For e-Residency, the process is fully digital: submit incorporation documents via notarized digital signature, obtain the registry code (which serves as the tax ID), and then activate the e-MTA account using the e-Residency digital ID card. Foreign shareholders do not require a local tax ID for company formation; the company's registry code is the primary taxpayer identifier.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an Osaühing (OÜ) must first register the company in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister). Upon registration, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board automatically issues a tax identification number (TIN) and a VAT payer identification number (if VAT registration is required or elected). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Estonian domestic law. All resident companies are taxed at the corporate level only on distributed profits. An OÜ may elect to become a VAT payer voluntarily if below the mandatory threshold. There is no hybrid mismatch election; however, a transparent partnership regime may apply to certain foreign hybrid entities, and Estonian tax law provides for a participation exemption election on capital gains, which is mandatory in effect if the holding period and ownership thresholds are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year (taxable turnover of supplies of goods and services subject to VAT, excluding intra-Community acquisitions and reverse-charge transactions)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods outside the EU where the seller must retain proof that the goods left the EU within 90 days of dispatch. Services supplied to a non-EU business customer (B2B) are generally zero-rated under the general place-of-supply rule, provided the customer is identified as a taxable person with a valid VAT ID or equivalent. Services relating to immovable property outside Estonia and certain financial and insurance services are exempt without credit. For export zero-rating, the seller must demonstrate that the risk of loss and title have passed to the buyer and that the goods have physically exited the Union.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX1) with export customs acceptance or documentary proof of exit from Estonia and the EU
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, air waybill, or rail consignment note) evidencing dispatch from Estonia and arrival outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice (original) showing the OÜ's registry code, buyer's details, complete goods description, terms of delivery (Incoterms), and proof of zero-rating code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds into the OÜ's Estonia or EU-licensed bank account
  • Packing list and customs broker's dispatch summary

Estonia mandates B2B e-invoicing in the sense that all VAT invoices must meet structured data requirements when transmitted electronically. The e-MTA portal supports real-time exchange of VAT declaration data and the e-invoice standard is the European standard EN 16931. There is no continuous transaction control clearance system (no real-time fiscal stamping) for domestic invoices; however, customs export declarations must be digitally filed via the Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) through the e-MTA portal and the European Union's Import Control System 2 (ICS2) for imports. Payment service provider data on cross-border transactions is subject to automatic exchange under DAC7.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may retrospectively zero-rate export transactions if documentation is incomplete or false. Penalties include reversing the zero-rated VAT leaving the supplier liable for 22% VAT plus interest, and an administrative fine under the Taxation Act (MKS) of up to EUR 1,300 for an OÜ; in cases of intentional tax fraud or misrepresentation, criminal liability applies with fines up to EUR 800,000 or imprisonment. Also, unjustified VAT refund claims may be clawed back with a penalty surcharge of 20% of the underpaid tax or withheld refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Estonia's EU and eurozone membership eliminates any central-bank foreign-exchange approval, registration, or allocation regime. Capital movements, including share subscription, profit repatriation, investment liquidation, and cross-border family-office treasury flows, are unrestricted except for EU/UN sanctions, AML/CFT reporting obligations under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, and Eesti Pank statistical/Balance of Payments reporting thresholds. Profit remittance is not subject to FX permits or convertibility hurdles; the only fiscal impediment is the Estonian distribution tax (generally 20/80 gross-up, or 14/86 for regular low-taxed dividends) which applies to the distributing company and may require a withholding declaration for certain payments to non-residents, but no exchange-control ceiling or syndication is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Estonian Foreign Exchange Act and related regulations following euro adoption do not impose any mandatory surrender of export proceeds. All receipts may be retained freely in euro or converted into any fundable currency at prevailing bank rates. The only binding rules are: (i) compliance with EU/UN sanctions and asset-freezing measures; (ii) mandatory cash declaration and reporting thresholds pursuant to the Estonian Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, in particular cash cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 10,000; and (iii) Balance-of-Payments statistical reporting to Eesti Pank through the resident credit institution for large cross-border goods and services transactions.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — central bank and monetary authority — together with Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority) for banking supervision and AML/CFT enforcement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium to Low. A family office should not place treasury or securities custody with offshore digital EMIs (e-money institutions), though a licensed EMI holding an EU passport under PSD2 may be used for low-value operating disbursements. Key legal constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits, cannot grant credit, are not covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund, and are frequently treated as high-risk correspondents by Estonian tier-1 banks. As a result, direct fund flows to/from the SFO's primary banking relation must pass through the domestic banking system; EMIs may be used only as ancillary receiving accounts for select management-fee or reimbursement payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire-transfer protocol: the non-resident shareholder or family trust initiates a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer in EUR to the SFO's capital escrow account at an Estonian credit institution. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state the purpose code and a clear narrative, such as Direct Investment – Equity Capital / Share Subscription, together with the SFO's registry code and the invoice or share-certificate reference. For eurozone payments, the IBAN/BIC route is sufficient; for non-euro currencies, the receiving Estonian bank must convert the proceeds into EUR before crediting, and such conversion may be executed at market rate. Eesti Pank Balance-of-Payments statistics require the receiving bank to classify the inward transaction as direct investment or portfolio investment; therefore the SFO must provide an executed shareholders' resolution confirming the capital contribution, source-of-funds documentation, identity of the ultimate contributor, and beneficial-ownership declaration before value is posted. No central-bank approval or pre-registration is required for a euro-denominated equity contribution.Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for ordinary outbound payments including dividends, management fees, legal fees, investment purchases, and operating disbursements; (ii) SWIFT MT202 and MT202COV for bank-to-bank transfers and correspondent reimbursements when a nested correspondent chain exists; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit under ICC UCP600 for cross-border trade in physical goods if the family office holds operating subsidiaries, although direct trade LC usage is not core to the SFO mandate; (iv) SEPA Credit Transfer / SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for low-value euro expenses within the EEA; (v) TARGET2 settlement for high-value euro liquidity flows; and (vi) T2S (Target2 Securities) settlement via the chosen custodian for purchased securities. All outbound transactions above the AML threshold must include accurate purpose codes and be supported by the underlying corporate authorisation in order to satisfy Finantsinspektsioon expectations and correspondent-bank due diligence.Deliverable FX forwards (outright forwards) for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, EUR/SEK and other freely convertible currency pairs, executed with an Estonian or EU/EEA-regulated bank or broker under ISDA or ISDA/CSA documentationForeign exchange swaps (FX swap sale-and-repurchase structures) for short-term and intraday liquidity management, subject to EMIR clearing thresholds and internal board treasury policyCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for aligning long-term non-euro investment or funding exposures with the SFO's consolidated euro balance-sheet requirementCurrency options (EUR call/put and other conventional structures) purchased from or arranged through a licensed investment firm, with no speculative tenor beyond a 12-month board-approved collar or protective-strategy horizonNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for temporary situations where the underlying currency is non-convertible or has only a weak deliverable market; NDFs are permitted under Estonian law but are not typically available for euro trades and require explicit bank credit approval under EMIR/MiFID II execution rules
