Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital. As of 1 February 2011, an OÜ may be registered with share capital of as low as €0.01; if share capital is below €2,500, contributions must be made in money. Contributions are paid into the company's own bank payment account, not a state escrow. Deferred monetary contributions are permitted but must be fully paid within ten years from the Commercial Register entry, and the contribution deadline must be stated in the memorandum of association.

Funding & unlock path: Rescue Board (Päästeamet): the registered office must carry a current fire safety risk assessment and fire extinguishers per the Fire Safety Act. Building control authority: the rented premises must hold a valid usage permit (kasutusluba) issued by the local municipal building authority, which constitutes structural load and building-engineering clearance. Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon): before the first employee commences work, the company must complete a written work-environment risk assessment, arrange an occupational health physician contract, and conduct an ergonomic workplace audit for screen-based work under the Occupational Health and Safety Act.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, MTA); e-service portal: e-MTA (emta.ee). The Commercial Register electronic service is the e-Business Register (är.ee).

E-government filing portal

A resident OÜ is incorporated through the e-Business Register and immediately obtains an Estonian registry code (Äriregistri kood), which functions as the taxpayer identification number. VAT registration is separate: an entity liable for Estonian VAT must register with MTA through e-MTA, file form KMR for the VAT register, and receive an EE-prefixed VAT number. A non-resident digital service provider may register as a non-resident VAT payer in Estonia if it makes taxable supplies in Estonia and does not use the EU One Stop Shop; application is made via e-MTA using the non-resident VAT registration procedure. After registration, VAT returns (KMD) are filed monthly by the 20th day of the following month.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A resident OÜ is incorporated through the e-Business Register and immediately obtains an Estonian registry code (Äriregistri kood), which functions as the taxpayer identification number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; an OÜ is a taxable body corporate. The corporate income tax deferral is statutory: profits are not taxed until distribution, but a resident company may voluntarily decide on distributions giving rise to tax. Elective mechanisms available include: VAT group registration election, payment of dividend tax on current taxable income, application of the EU cross-border loss relief election, and advance pricing agreement requests with MTA. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD have been implemented, with limited elections to align tax-transparent treatment across jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for domestic supplies of goods and services; EUR 10,000 pan-EU distance-selling threshold; remote digital services to EU consumers are declared via OSS and do not require separate EE registration below the OSS threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported physically outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT if the export is evidenced by a Customs Export Declaration, proof of dispatch, and commercial documents. For software, VPN services, and digital encryption services: zero-rating applies when the place of supply is outside Estonia, i.e., the customer is a non-EU business or non-EU consumer; for EU business customers reverse charge applies; for EU consumers the OSS must be used. To prove place of supply for digital services, the supplier must hold two independent and non-contradictory pieces of evidence, such as customer residence, bank country, IP address, phone country code, or business register extract.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/EXS)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Bank Transfer Evidence
  • Commercial Contract and Invoice stating zero rating
  • Proof of physical exit from the EU
  • Customer location evidence (for digital services: IP, bank, business register)

Estonia imposes periodic VAT declaration through e-MTA, not real-time per-invoice clearance or digital stamping. There is no mandatory API-based continuous transaction control for domestic invoices. Electronic invoicing is accepted in standard formats (e-arve, UBL, EN 16931), and e-invoicing is required for supplies to Estonian public-sector contracting authorities. Taxpayers must retain full invoice data and make it available to MTA upon audit.

MTA may retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT where documentary evidence is inadequate, plus statutory late-payment interest (currently 0.06% per day). Misdeclared exports or fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to VAT reassessment at 20%, a tax evasion penalty under the Estonian Taxation Act, and criminal tax fraud liability under the Estonian Penal Code, including fines and imprisonment. Limitation periods for reassessment are generally three years in ordinary cases and seven years in cases of intention or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility with a complete absence of exchange controls. Estonia has been a euro-area member since 1 January 2011 and applies the EU Treaty free movement of capital under TFEU Article 63; non-residents may freely open and operate EUR and foreign-currency accounts without prior approval, quantitative limits, or repatriation licensing. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted; the Estonian corporate tax trigger is distribution rather than conversion: undistributed profits are taxed at 0%, regular dividend distributions at 14/86, non-regular distributions at 20/80, and cross-border dividends may be fully exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU or relieved under Estonia's double-tax treaty network. Capital injections, shareholder-loan repayments, service fees, royalties, and liquidation proceeds are freely repatriated in any currency. The only practical hurdle is anti-money-laundering source-of-funds verification by the banking counterparty, not any currency-control restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion quota, or repatriation timeline exists in Estonia. Exporters of services and goods may retain foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in resident or non-resident accounts, may net receivables against payables, and may assign receivables as collateral for trade finance without seeking Eesti Pank permission. The only residual obligations are statistical and indirect: credit institutions report Balance of Payments data to Eesti Pank, the OÜ must file annual accounts and maintain full evidence of export income for tax audit and transfer-pricing purposes under the Estonian Accounting Act, and VAT records must support the place-of-supply treatment of digital services (B2C via One-Stop-Shop/OSS, B2B via reverse charge). None of these constitute currency-conversion or export-capture rules.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) - central bank of the Republic of Estonia and Eurosystem member, responsible for monetary operations, financial stability, currency statistics, and TARGET2-Estonia settlement. Prudential licensing and supervision of credit institutions and fintech/e-money institutions is delegated to Finantsinspektsioon (the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority). AML/CFT supervision and financial-intelligence collection is vested in the Rahapesu Andmebüroo (Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit), operating within the Police and Border Guard Board under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (RahaPTS), transposing EU AML Directives and FATF Recommendations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for fast transaction-settlement rails, Medium-Low as a treasury-concentration solution. Under PSD2, EU-authorised e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Paysera UAB (Lithuania), Wise Europe (Belgium), Monese (Estonia), and iCard may onboard the OÜ fully remotely under EEA passporting into Estonia. Legal constraints: (1) EMI balances are safeguarded client funds, not bank deposits, and are excluded from the Deposit Guarantee Scheme; (2) EMIs cannot grant overdrafts or provide investment-grade FX and derivative execution; (3) VPN and anonymisation software is treated as an enhanced-due-diligence category, so EMIs may impose transaction limits, demand an external AML officer, or reject the merchant category outright; (4) the OÜ's settlement contracts with Stripe and Paymentwall must be reconciled with each EMI's prohibited-business list; (5) the recommended architecture is to use the EMI as a collection sweep-account settling weekly via SEPA into the LHV Pank AS tier-1 operational account, which remains the sole treasury control point and FX/hedging execution venue.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for an OÜ with zero statutory minimum capital: (1) the non-resident shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 from a personal bank account; MT103 field 50K must match the KYC-verified name and address, and field 70 cites 'Share capital contribution' or 'Shareholder loan per resolution [No.]' with cross-reference to the OÜ registry code; Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on distributed profits (tax equals 20/80 of the net dividend); 0% on retained and reinvested profits until actual or deemed distribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

No separate withholding tax is levied on dividend payments to shareholders. Instead, the distributing OÜ bears corporate income tax at 20% (20/80 of net distribution), reduced to 14% for regular distributions within the historical average. Outbound dividends therefore effectively incur a 20% statutory CIT charge at the corporate level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form TSD and payment of all declared corporate income tax, social tax, and withholding tax are due by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the profit distribution or other taxable unrecognized payment occurred.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 0 – Estonia imposes no stand-alone statutory Local File or Master File threshold; related-party transactions must nevertheless be priced at arm's length and substantiated upon tax audit. The Country-by-Country Reporting threshold applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations register akin to offshore financial centres, but the 0% retained-earnings deferral is a territorial benefit conditioned on real activity. The Estonian Tax Authority will deny treaty and domestic tax benefits to artificial structures lacking employees, premises, business decision-making, and operational risk exposure in Estonia, applying GAAR (Taxation Act § 29) and substance-over-form analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Mischaracterized Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment under CIT at 20/80 of the disguised distribution; late payment interest at 0.06% per day; penalty up to EUR 1,300 per false TSD filing; criminal fine against the legal entity up to EUR 2.5 million and up to 5 years' imprisonment for responsible individuals under Penal Code § 158.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for intentionally or grossly negligently caused tax arrears under the Taxation Act and Commercial Code § 315. Courts pierce the corporate veil where the OÜ is a sham, ordering personal confiscation of hidden profits, travel bans pending criminal tax proceedings, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Sham e-Residency Vehicle

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of retained profits as deemed distributions with immediate 20% corporate income tax; retroactive interest assessments; deregistration of the OÜ from the Commercial Registry; denial of Estonian VAT and DTA benefits; administrative penalties up to EUR 12,000 for false commercial registry declarations.

Director personal liability: Founders and board members face criminal liability under Penal Code § 385 for falsely certifying corporate data, disqualification as directors, revocation of e-Residency digital credentials, travel restrictions under preventive restraint orders, and personal recovery of the underpaid tax where the OÜ lacks assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's length recharacterization under the Estonian Taxation Act; upward adjustment of Estonian-source profits; reclassification of excess payments as deemed profit distributions subject to 20% CIT; late interest at 0.06% per day; documentation deficiency penalty up to EUR 1,300; criminal fines for intentional misdeclaration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under the Commercial Code for orchestrating transfer pricing that destroys the OÜ's tax base; criminal courts may impose imprisonment up to 5 years for tax fraud and freeze personal assets of the directors and shadow shareholders.
Anti-Money Laundering / Payment Railing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: State supervision measures under the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit, fines up to EUR 400,000 for medium-risk technology companies, suspension or forced closure of payment gateways, and AML foreign account referral to the criminal authorities; confiscation of proceeds linked to predicate offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML failures up to full disgorgement of gains; travel bans and extradition possible under EU mutual recognition; imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering facilitation under Penal Code § 394, regardless of the legal entity being technically licensed.
