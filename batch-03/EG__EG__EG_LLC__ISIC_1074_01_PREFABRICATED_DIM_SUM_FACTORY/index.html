Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Foreign LLC steps: (1) obtain Commercial Registry extract and investment license from GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones); (2) submit to ETA the unified tax registration application with constitutional documents, Arabic/Latin certified translations, board resolution appointing a resident tax representative, and notarized power of attorney; (3) obtain the Unified Tax Identification Number (TIN), tax card, and e-invoicing system credentials; (4) simultaneously register for VAT and employer/payroll obligations. TIN issuance is centralized and electronically generated after verification at the competent ETA branch, with Turnaround of 5–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 at the 22.5% corporate headline rate on worldwide income; non-resident branch taxed on Egypt-source income as a separate deemed entity; no pass-through classification exists for LLCs; SCZone/free-zone licensees may qualify for territorial exemption and a 1% annual charge on export turnover.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists. Available statutory elections: (i) small-business presumptive tax election at 14% on taxable revenue up to EGP 20 million under the 2023 tax facilitation package; (ii) free-zone election under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 substituting income tax with a 1% annual charge on export-related gross revenue; (iii) election for jointly held ventures to be taxed as a separate taxable entity; (iv) participation-exemption election for dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings; (v) advance pricing agreements and bilateral mutual agreement procedure for transfer pricing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration where annual taxable supplies of goods or services exceed EGP 500,000 (12-month look-back); voluntary registration available from EGP 50,000; importers and customs declarants must register irrespective of domestic turnover; registration is required before claiming export zero-rating and input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT only upon actual physical export outside Egypt evidenced by customs release, shipping documents, and bank remittance; the exporter must hold valid VAT registration, issue export invoices, and retain input tax invoices covering recoverable VAT; offshore services rendered to non-resident customers and performed entirely outside Egypt qualify for zero-rating where the benefit accrues outside Egypt. Frozen dim sum and dumpling products exported as processed food qualify as export of goods under ISIC 1074 manufacturing scope.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export customs invoice and release certificate issued via Egyptian Customs Authority/Nafeza)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual exportation and date of shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank receipt evidencing foreign-currency proceeds credited to the exporter's account within 180 days)
  • Commercial sales invoice, purchase order, and underlying export contract
  • VAT tax invoices for related input purchases (domestic or import) supporting the input VAT refund calculation

Mandatory B2B electronic invoicing through the ETA Electronic Invoicing System, phased in across registered taxpayers; invoices must be submitted in real time via certified ERP/API endpoints in JSON/XML with digital signature, validated against ETA reference data, and stamped with an acceptance code and machine-readable QR; e-receipts for B2C POS transactions are mandated in later phases; non-compliant taxpayers face penalties and suspension of tax clearance certificates.

ETA post-clearance audits verify export authenticity through customs release matching, shipping documents, and FX repatriation within the 180-day deadline; shipments lacking matched inward remittance or customs proof are re-characterized as taxable domestic sales subject to 15% VAT, mandatory clawback of input VAT credits, statutory monthly default interest, and late-payment penalties; fraudulent export documentation triggers criminal prosecution under Articles 38-40 of Law No. 67 of 2016 with imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to three times the evaded tax, plus debarment from e-invoicing, cancellation of the unified tax number, and customs red-flagging of the taxpayer's clearance profile.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has operated a unified flexible exchange rate regime since March 2024, restoring full interbank determination of the EGP against major foreign currencies. Current account transactions, including trade settlement and distributed profit remittances by non-resident shareholders of an Egyptian LLC, are freely convertible through CBE-authorized dealing banks, subject only to documentary compliance with the Unified Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and CBE reporting forms. Capital account inflows, notably equity injection or capital increases under the Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, remain subject to prior GAFI registration, bank confirmation of the purpose code, and post-transaction CBE statistical reporting. Profit repatriation hurdles are limited to settlement of Egyptian corporate income tax and applicable dividend withholding tax at the rate in force, verification of audited distributable reserves, and evidence that the statutory reserve requirement under Article 87 of Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 has been funded before distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Regulation No. 121 of 2019 as amended by subsequent CBE circulars, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt through an authorized bank and credited to the exporter's foreign currency account within a maximum of 5 months from the bill of lading date. The former mandatory conversion ratios into EGP (ranging from 50 percent to 100 percent of receipts) have been abolished following the March 2024 unification of the exchange rate; the exporter may retain the foreign currency or sell it at the interbank market rate. The CBE retains discretionary authority to reimpose mandatory surrender requirements during future balance-of-payments stress, and all repatriated receipts must be supported by customs declarations, commercial invoices, and the company's official export records at the Egyptian bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the exclusive foreign exchange administration and monetary authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and CBE Regulation No. 121 of 2019 on foreign exchange operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Egypt does not license offshore digital banks or foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) to hold statutory corporate funds or clear trade payments. Only CBE-licensed payment service providers under the Payment Services Law No. 18 of 2019, acting through a CBE-licensed settlement bank, may provide digital payment accounts, and such accounts are in practice reserved for retail or low-value domestic use. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, and similar platforms) cannot receive statutory export proceeds, cannot remit foreign dividends, and cannot satisfy the CBE export repatriation clock. They are usable only as an ancillary convenience for petty administrative payments outside Egypt; all statutory treasury movements must be routed through the Egyptian banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing of the foreign investor's equity injection: first, the LLC obtains a GAFI capital increase certificate, a shareholder resolution, and a purpose code declaration from the receiving CBE-authorized bank classifying the wire as a capital contribution. The remitting foreign bank then executes an SWIFT MT103 to the LLC's account at the Egyptian dealing bank, quoting the LLC full legal name, the commercial registration number, and the Egyptian branch SWIFT/BIC code. The receiving bank verifies that the credited amount matches the GAFI certificate, converts at the interbank market rate if EGP is required, allocates the amount to the capital escrow or capital sub-account, and files the CBE statistical return. Within 90 days the LLC must complete the share capital posting and re-register the commercial record, otherwise the funds are reclassified as shareholder loans and trigger deemed interest and transfer-pricing documentation obligations.Outbound trade settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for prepayments and confirmed invoices, and through documentary commercial letters of credit (sight or usance, denominated in USD or EUR) for food-grade ingredient imports. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000

Economic substance scope: The Egyptian Tax Authority does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework equivalent to those in the UAE or Bahrain; however, it enforces substance-over-form, generally accepted GAAR principles, beneficial-ownership requirements under double tax treaties, and requires companies to maintain actual managerial, operational, and payroll substance in Egypt to withstand treaty benefit and transfer pricing challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or Concealed Records

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years and a fine equal to the amount of evaded tax plus additional civil penalties of 100% to 200% of the assessed tax; the Egyptian Tax Authority may also freeze bank accounts, confiscate transportation assets, and suspend the factory's commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns, participate in off-book sales, or allow fraudulent export declarations to be lodged may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and fines, subject to travel bans (preventive seizure of passport), and may face criminal imprisonment alongside the company.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the shell arrangement for corporate income tax purposes, re-attribution of profits to the actual foreign parent, denial of treaty relief, and a penalty of 20% of the adjusted revenue plus interest; free-zone privileges can be revoked by GAFI where the entity lacks genuine local functions.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced if the shareholder-director used the LLC as an instrument of tax fraud; personal liability extends to restitution of evaded taxes, disqualification from board service in Egypt, and criminal proceedings involving administrative sanctions up to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian Tax Authority may perform a unilateral adjustment to taxable profit plus priming interest, impose a misdeclaration penalty between 20% and 200% of the undercharged tax, and levy administrative fines up to EGP 200,000 for documentation failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved fabricated related-party contracts or instructed the accountant to conceal the parent-supplier relationship may be personally liable for the assessed adjustment and penalties, and may be subject to criminal sanction if the misdeclaration is tied to fraudulent letters of credit or money laundering.
Fatal Withholding Tax Violation

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC is liable as payer for the uncollected withholding tax plus a delay surcharge of 2% per month and a fixed penalty of EGP 5,000; the non-resident recipient may be deemed ineligible for treaty benefits and become taxable in Egypt on a deemed assessed basis.

Director personal liability: The senior finance director and the chairman are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax because the law expressly imposes personal liability on the custodian of corporate funds who has not caused the tax to be withheld and remitted; criminal prosecution may include travel restrictions and confiscation of personal assets.
Anti-Money Laundering and Currency Evasion

Statutory sanction: Breach of Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 exposes the company and its responsible officers to fines ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000 and imprisonment of one to seven years; the Supreme Council for Economic Courts may order liquidation and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors personally engaged in the movement of proceeds through private overseas accounts are subject to arrest, free travel-bank confirmation, confiscation of passport, freeze of UAE and international bank relationships, and personal criminal convictions; the corporate veil is lifted to deem the company's assets as the director's fraudulent vault.
