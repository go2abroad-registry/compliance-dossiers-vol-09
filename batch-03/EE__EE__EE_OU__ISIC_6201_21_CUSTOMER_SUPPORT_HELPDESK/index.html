Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) via e-MTA (e-Maksuamet) portal at emta.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in Estonia must register for VAT with the Tax and Customs Board before beginning economic activity. The foreign entity obtains an Estonian/EU VAT identification number (format EE+9 digits) by submitting an electronic application on e-MTA, authenticated via e-Residency digital ID or via an authorised representative. The tax authority issues the VAT registration certificate normally within 5-10 business days. For a newly incorporated Estonian OÜ, the commercial register automatically issues a registry code (reg. no.); VAT registration becomes compulsory when taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold and may also be applied for voluntarily.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity not established in Estonia must register for VAT with the Tax and Customs Board before beginning economic activity. The foreign entity obtains an Estonian/EU VAT identification number (format EE+9 digits) by submitting an electronic application on e-MTA, authenticated via e-Residency digital ID or via an authorised representative. The tax authority issues the VAT registration certificate normally within 5-10 business days. For a newly incorporated Estonian OÜ, the commercial register automatically issues a registry code (reg. no.); VAT registration becomes compulsory when taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold and may also be applied for voluntarily.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies no check-the-box entity classification; an OÜ is automatically taxable at entity level. Available elections include VAT group registration, application for an advance tax ruling, conclusion of an advance pricing agreement, registration under the OSS for distance sales and electronically supplied services, and voluntary VAT registration. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD/Interest and Royalty Directive are mandatory and not elective in Estonia.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover for resident taxable persons; non-resident taxable persons have no monetary threshold and must register before commencing taxable activity; intra-EU distance sales and digital services to EU consumers are subject to a €10,000 cross-border threshold requiring OSS registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to a third country qualify for 0% if the supplier retains documentary evidence of physical removal from the EU within 90 days and files the customs export declaration. Cross-border services supplied to a business customer established outside Estonia are outside the scope of Estonian VAT or zero-rated under B2B place-of-supply rules, provided the supplier obtains the customer's VAT registration number or equivalent evidence of commercial establishment outside Estonia. SaaS/helpdesk services to EU consumers are subject to VAT at the consumer's Member State rate and must be reported via OSS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or CMR
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence
  • For services: executed service contract, scope description, and customer's business registration certificate or VAT registration evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping applies to ordinary B2B invoices. VAT returns, EC sales lists and customs declarations must be filed electronically through e-MTA. E-invoicing to Estonian public sector bodies must be Peppol BIS-compliant. e-MTA also provides a REST API for automated tax filing and customs clearance integrations.

Estonian customs and tax authorities may reassess VAT retrospectively for three years from the filing date, extended to six years for tax fraud or deliberate non-disclosure. Invalid export zero-rating claims result in VAT reassessment, interest at the statutory arrears rate, penalties of up to €3,200, and, in serious cases, criminal liability for tax fraud carrying imprisonment up to five years. Claimants also lose refund eligibility and may be subject to clawback of attributed input VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both capital and current accounts. Estonia acceded to the EU in 2004 and adopted the euro on 1 January 2011, removing all national exchange-control instruments. Free movement of capital is guaranteed by Article 63 TFEU without national derogation; no central-bank consent, no advance licensing, and no FX rationing applies to any transaction. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal and documentary only: dividend distributions trigger the 20/80 (standard) or 14/86 (regular distribution) corporate income tax at the moment of distribution, and the paying bank requires evidence of e-Maks tax settlement, a board resolution, and a UBO source-of-funds file. No Eesti Pank permission or FX approval is required for dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Estonia imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for SaaS export receipts or any other cross-border proceeds. USD/GBP subscription revenue may be held indefinitely in multi-currency accounts; conversion frequency is a matter of internal treasury policy only. Legal basis: elimination of all exchange controls upon EU accession (2004) and euro adoption (2011), with TFEU Article 63 governing capital flows.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — Eurosystem central bank; since 1 January 2025 consolidated prudential and conduct supervisor through the merger of Finantsinspektsioon; operates the TARGET2-Estonia settlement channel, issues credit-institution and payment-institution licences, enforces AML/CFT supervision, and administers cross-border balance-of-payments statistics under the Governor's Regulation No. 3.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as operational collections and vendor-payment rails; LOW-MEDIUM as the sole banking facility where statutory equity evidence, investor due diligence, or procurement credit checks require a relationship with a licensed credit institution. Legal constraints: EU/EEA EMIs must passport under PSD2 Article 11 through the single-licence notice to Eesti Pank or the home-state supervisor; funds are safeguarded, not DGSD deposit-protected; banks will flag accounts fed exclusively through EMI pass-throughs if the UBO cannot be matched to underlying commercial contracts. Viable providers for this OÜ: Wise Europe (multi-currency treasury accounts), Holvi (Finland, expense-card integration), and Paysera (Lithuania). EMIs are recommended strictly as secondary rails; the primary capital and distribution account should sit with an Estonian-tier-1 bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the gross amount of a taxable profit distribution, calculated by dividing the net distribution by 0.78; retained accounting profits are not subject to corporate income tax merely because they are earned.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder. The OÜ’s distribution-level corporate income tax remains payable at 22/78 of the net distribution from 2025. A 7% withholding tax may remain relevant for certain dividends paid to qualifying resident or non-resident natural persons from distributions subject to the former 14/86 regime under transitional rules; confirm the origin and tax history of the distribution before applying it.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 is a commonly relevant transaction threshold for Estonia’s detailed transfer-pricing documentation requirements, but it is not a standalone universal Local File or Master File trigger. Additional thresholds and entity criteria apply, including company size, group status, transaction type, and applicable Estonian regulations. Assess the current rules and the company’s facts before concluding that a Local File or Master File is mandatory.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. The OÜ must nevertheless maintain genuine business records and comply with transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, controlled-foreign-company, permanent-establishment, and applicable EU anti-tax-avoidance rules. An Estonian e-Residency card or registered address alone does not establish tax residence, substantive management, or treaty entitlement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, recovery of tax and statutory interest, administrative penalties, and potential criminal liability under Estonian law for conduct meeting the applicable criminal-offence elements. Criminal fines and other penalties depend on the offence and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal administrative or criminal liability where statutory elements are met. A director may also be liable for tax debt under the Taxation Act where the director intentionally or through gross negligence breaches duties and the statutory conditions for recovery are established. Imprisonment or travel restrictions are not automatic consequences of an ordinary filing error.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board may adjust taxable amounts, assess additional tax and interest, and impose applicable procedural penalties. The adjustment may increase tax on a distribution or other taxable transaction; Estonia does not generally impose corporate income tax on retained accounting profit solely as a transfer-pricing adjustment without a taxable event under domestic law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed under applicable tax-debt recovery rules if the statutory fault and causation requirements are met. Criminal liability requires conduct satisfying the elements of a criminal offence.
Undeclared employment, fringe benefits, or payroll taxes

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of tax and contributions, statutory interest, applicable administrative penalties, and potential criminal proceedings for sufficiently serious intentional conduct.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise under statutory tax-debt or criminal-law provisions where the required conduct and fault are established; it is not automatic solely because a company has an unpaid tax balance.
Money laundering and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal fines, confiscation, transaction restrictions, and other sanctions under the applicable Estonian and EU legislation; the penalty depends on the offence, value, intent, and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible persons may face individual criminal liability where the statutory elements are met. Civil recovery, asset restraint, or travel restrictions require an applicable legal basis and competent-authority action; they are not automatic consequences of an AML or sanctions red flag.
Shell-company or sham-residency arrangements

Statutory sanction: Possible denial of treaty benefits, reassessment under applicable residence, permanent-establishment, anti-abuse, and transfer-pricing rules, tax and interest recovery, and administrative or criminal sanctions where the conduct independently violates law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own false statements, breaches of statutory duties, or participation in unlawful conduct. Limited liability is not displaced merely because the company has foreign owners or limited Estonian operations.
