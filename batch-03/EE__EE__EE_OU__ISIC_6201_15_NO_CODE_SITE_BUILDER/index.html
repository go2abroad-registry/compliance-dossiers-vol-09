Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Äriregistri (Estonian Business Register), Registry Department of Tartu County Court (Tartu Maakohtu registriosakond), operating through the e-Business Register online portal (ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee).

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik, RT I 1995, 26, 355, as amended) - §§ 134-152 (Osaühing formation, capital and governance), § 136(1) (minimum share capital of €2,500), §§ 185-191 (management board); supplemented by the Information Society Services Act (Infoühiskonna teenuse seadus) and the Accounting Act (Raamatupidamise seadus).

Formation / constitutional filings

Foundation Agreement (asutamisleping) and Articles of Association (põhikiri) submitted as a single digitally signed Application for Entry in the Business Register (ettevõtte registreerimise avaldus); notarial authentication is available as an alternative route for founders without an e-Residency digital ID.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital is €2,500 (ÄS § 136(1)); no statutory escrow account is mandatory. Capital may be paid in full before filing or deferred by a board declaration for up to one year after registration, provided the full amount is stated in the articles of association; funds are typically held with an Estonian or EU-licensed bank or payment institution (e.g., LHV Pank, Wise).

Funding & unlock path: The leased office premises must hold a valid building use certificate (kasutusluba) with fire safety compliance under the Building Code (Ehitusseadustik), including fire extinguishers and unobstructed exits; workplace clearance follows the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus) covering a written occupational health service contract, first-aid equipment, and ergonomic workplace assessments for desk-based staff.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital is €2,500 (ÄS § 136(1)); no statutory escrow account is mandatory. Capital may be paid in full before filing or deferred by a board declaration for up to one year after registration, provided the full amount is stated in the articles of association; funds are typically held with an Estonian or EU-licensed bank or payment institution (e.g., LHV Pank, Wise).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet; MTA) for income tax, VAT, and customs; e-service portal e-MTA at https://e-mta.mta.ee; incorporation and registry-code issuance through the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister) at https://ariregister.rik.ee; e-Residency digital identity at https://www.e-resident.gov.ee.

E-government filing portal

Remote non-resident founders: (1) obtain e-Residency digital ID or Estonian personal ID code; (2) submit the OÜ incorporation application on e-Äriregister and receive the nine-digit registry code — the statutory TIN; the VAT number is the same code with the EE prefix (EE + 9 digits); (3) apply for VAT registration on e-MTA (RUTA/KATTR application) voluntarily or when EUR 40,000 calendar-year taxable turnover is exceeded; the MTA confirms the EE-prefixed VAT number electronically; (4) a foreign legal entity without an Estonian establishment selling to Estonian consumers may register as a non-resident VAT payer via e-MTA and may use the OSS/IOSS digital schemes; (5) the same e-MTA login handles KMD/KMD INF, TDF, and TSD filings; no notarisation or apostille is normally required for e-Resident digital applications; registry beneficial-ownership statements must be updated within 30 days of any change.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial-distribution model): retained and undistributed profits of the OÜ are untaxed (0% CIT); corporate income tax is self-assessed by the OÜ itself only upon profit distribution at 20/80 (20% of the gross distribution) for ordinary dividends and at 14/86 for regular distribution cycles meeting the statutory continuity test; no separate withholding is imposed at the Estonian source on shareholders, and treaty/Parent-Subsidiary relief may apply to cross-border dividends; foreign dividend income may qualify for the participation exemption at 10% holding; CFC and hybrid-mismatch rules operate at entity level on an attribution basis. — Remote non-resident founders: (1) obtain e-Residency digital ID or Estonian personal ID code; (2) submit the OÜ incorporation application on e-Äriregister and receive the nine-digit registry code — the statutory TIN; the VAT number is the same code with the EE prefix (EE + 9 digits); (3) apply for VAT registration on e-MTA (RUTA/KATTR application) voluntarily or when EUR 40,000 calendar-year taxable turnover is exceeded; the MTA confirms the EE-prefixed VAT number electronically; (4) a foreign legal entity without an Estonian establishment selling to Estonian consumers may register as a non-resident VAT payer via e-MTA and may use the OSS/IOSS digital schemes; (5) the same e-MTA login handles KMD/KMD INF, TDF, and TSD filings; no notarisation or apostille is normally required for e-Resident digital applications; registry beneficial-ownership statements must be updated within 30 days of any change.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial-distribution model): retained and undistributed profits of the OÜ are untaxed (0% CIT); corporate income tax is self-assessed by the OÜ itself only upon profit distribution at 20/80 (20% of the gross distribution) for ordinary dividends and at 14/86 for regular distribution cycles meeting the statutory continuity test; no separate withholding is imposed at the Estonian source on shareholders, and treaty/Parent-Subsidiary relief may apply to cross-border dividends; foreign dividend income may qualify for the participation exemption at 10% holding; CFC and hybrid-mismatch rules operate at entity level on an attribution basis.

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style check-the-box election. The operative election-like mechanisms are: (1) reduced 14/86 dividend rate — de facto election via the annual TDF distribution computation supported by evidence of the prior-cycle regularity; (2) voluntary VAT registration below the EUR 40,000 threshold; (3) OSS/IOSS election for intra-Community distance sales and B2C digital services, replacing multi-jurisdiction VAT registrations; (4) VAT grouping election for closely-linked resident taxable persons; (5) no hybrid-mismatch election — ATAD 2 rules in the Taxation Act apply automatically and mechanically; (6) e-Residency is an administrative identity mechanism, not a fiscal election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 aggregate calendar-year taxable turnover (all taxable supplies, including 0%-rated export supplies, excluding VAT-exempt supplies without credit) for compulsory registration; EUR 10,000 EU-wide turnover threshold for OSS/IOSS distance sales of digital services; voluntary registration permitted below both thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT on export supplies to final non-EU destinations where goods are dispatched from Estonia by the registered exporter within the statutory evidence period and customs exit is proven. Services of the no-code website builder: place-of-supply rules govern — B2B services to a customer established outside Estonia are outside Estonian VAT (reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction); B2C digital services to EU consumers are declared in the OSS at the consumer-state rate; B2C digital services to non-EU consumers qualify for 0% treatment as exported services provided two independent non-conflicting items of consumer-residence evidence (IP geolocation, billing country, card-BIN country, login telemetry) are documented. Absent such evidence, 20% Estonian VAT must be added on the fee.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX1 or eCustoms export movement) showing the MTA-registered export date and exit office
  • Transport document — Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note in the Estonian exporter's name as consignor
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and a 0%-VAT clause for the no-code/SaaS licence fees
  • Inward remittance evidence — SWIFT MT103, bank statement, or licensed PSP (Stripe/Paddle) disbursement report proving foreign-currency receipt into an Estonian bank account or PSP settlement account
  • Service agreement, licence order log, and delivery metadata (IP geo, seat location, login records) evidencing remote delivery of the site-builder platform to non-EU customers
  • KMD INF appendix of the VAT return listing each 0%-rated supply and reconciling to the accounting ledger
  • Consumer-residence proof for B2C non-EU supplies (IP address plus card BIN/billing state) retained for audit

No mandatory universal real-time e-invoice clearance or digital-stamping API is in force as of the dossier date; B2B/G2B e-invoicing is voluntary on the PEPPOL-based national e-arve standard. The MTA requires mandatory electronic filing via e-MTA (KMD, KMD INF, TDF, TSD) over HTTPS/web-service APIs and performs automated cross-matching of customs, bank, and VAT data. A phased statutory real-time electronic-sales-receipt (fiscalisation) regime is in legislative roadmap; until effective, remote no-code/SaaS businesses issuing only electronic platform invoices have no fiscal cash-register duty. Invoices and source records must be kept for seven years.

The MTA applies a documentary-chain and business-purpose review to all 0% export claims; mismatches between customs exit data, invoice parties, and financial flows, or failure to evidence foreign-currency repatriation within the 180-day covenant, support reassessment of the fee at 20% VAT with gross-up (20/80), daily late-payment interest at 0.06% per day, and administrative penalty calculations. Knowingly false declarations trigger criminal tax-fraud liability under the Penal Code (imprisonment up to five years for aggravated tax fraud), director personal liability, a retroactive reassessment window extended to five years, loss of the 14/86 distribution and OSS simplifications, and inclusion in the MTA's public risk and recovery registers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has full current and capital account convertibility as a euro-area Member State. There are no exchange control regulations, no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or capital proceeds, and no prior approval requirements for cross-border transfers under the Foreign Exchange Act. The Estonian corporate income tax system taxes only distributed profits (generally 20% or 14% for regular dividends), but the distribution itself is not subject to FX or capital control permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion regime applies. Exporters and service providers may retain revenue in any convertible currency, have no fixed timeline for repatriation, and are not required to convert foreign currency earnings into euros.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the European System of Central Banks; monetary policy and foreign exchange reserve management competence for euro-area matters are exercised by the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for licensed EU/EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) as supplemental operational rails. Legal constraints: the EMI must hold a valid payment institution or electronic money institution licence under the revised PSD2, must comply with Estonian AML/CFT and beneficial ownership transparency requirements, and must offer corporate accounts that can be opened using e-Residency digital identities or other qualified remote KYC. An EMI cannot replace a domestic credit institution for statutory settlement accounts, commercial letters of credit, or complex FX hedging arrangements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Unclear source of initial capital injection in an OÜ with zero minimum capital and no operating history.

Defense evidence: Bank-to-bank wire trail from the founder's personal or prior business account; sworn declaration of source of funds; if available, audited or accountant-prepared financial statements; tax residency certificates of the contributing shareholder; explanation of the commercial model (visual web template and low-code builder) and how revenue will be generated; share register and general meeting resolution recording the contribution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 (for non-EUR currencies) or SEPA Credit Transfer (for EUR) from the shareholder's bank to the OÜ's operational account at an Estonian credit institution. The payment must include in the Remittance Information field the phrase 'Share capital contribution (osakapitali sissemakse)' and the company's registry code. For a shareholder loan, the wire should state 'Shareholder loan (laen)' and be supported by a written loan agreement; any interest paid must respect Estonia's transfer pricing arm's length rules. Banks may require a board resolution, the register excerpt, and a UBO declaration before releasing funds. Statistical reporting is covered by the bank under the Balance of Payments framework using appropriate direct investment codes (e.g., S21 or S31).Primary clearing rails for B2B digital services are SEPA Instant Credit for euro-denominated invoices to EU/EEA clients, SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA business customers, and commercial letters of credit (UCP600-compliant) for high-value enterprise procurement contracts. For card-based customer acquisition, Stripe Billing and Paddle handle acquiring and settle into the OÜ's operational account via preferred payout rails (SEPA Credit or SWIFT). Payouts to contractors or service providers in non-EEA jurisdictions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 with appropriate purpose codes, and documentary evidence (e-invoices, contracts, fulfilment logs) should be retained for AML and tax purposes.European-style FX forward contracts denominated in EUR against major convertible currencies (USD, GBP, SEK, NOK, JPY)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies subject to convertibility restrictions or lacking a fully deliverable offshore marketCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) for longer-dated treasury risk managementVanilla FX options (call and put) purchased or written with a licensed credit institutionStandard spot contracts for operating cash flow conversion

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on distributed profits (0% on retained/undistributed profits; 14% preferential rate on regular dividends paid to residents and certain non-residents subject to conditions; 20/80 = 25% effective pre-withholding gross-up on full distribution, with statutory dividend withholding tax treated as the corporate income tax under Estonian system)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% corporate income tax on distributed profits imposed at the level of the Estonian OÜ; no additional separate withholding tax on dividends for most distributions; for dividends subject to the 14/7 regime: 14% corporate income tax on the distribution (with 7% withholding tax at the moment of payment for resident individuals; for non-residents, the 7% withholding is withheld by the OÜ, resulting in total 20/80 effective tax; non-resident corporate shareholders may be subject to 0% or reduced rates under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and DTA)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual TSD must be submitted by 30 April of the year following the tax period; monthly TSD declarations are due by the 10th day of the following month; quarterly declarations due by the 15th day of the month following the quarter; any profit distribution triggering income tax must be declared in the TSD submitted by the 10th day of the month following the month of distribution

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No domestic statutory monetary threshold for Local File / Master File in EUR; Estonia relies on the arm's length principle under Income Tax Act §50 and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; contemporaneous TP documentation (transfer pricing report) is recommended and should be prepared where related-party cross-border transactions exceed EUR 50,000 per category annually as a risk-management benchmark; MTA has not published a formal binding threshold, but requires documentation upon request if transactions with related parties exist, and significant TP audits trigger documentation orders; a formal Master File and Local File aligned with EU Transfer Pricing Directive (2022/2523) are increasingly expected, with the EU-wide filing threshold of EUR 50 million group turnover and EUR 500,000 related-party transactions for the new EU reporting framework (per CbCR and DAC6 annexes) applicable for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2024

Economic substance scope: Estonia has not enacted separate Economic Substance Regulations for domestic OÜs; however, anti-abuse substance requirements apply through: (1) the Estonian General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) - a transaction is disregarded when its sole or main purpose is tax benefit and no valid economic substance; (2) EU ATAD (Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive) implemented in the Income Tax Act, including interest limitation rule, CFC rules, exit taxation, and GAAR; (3) CFC rules under Income Tax Act §§68-68^8 targeting profits in low-tax jurisdictions (below one-third of Estonian headline rate, i.e. below 6.67%); (4) e-Residency does not replace physical substance; the OÜ must have a management board, registered address in Estonia, real business activity, and maintain proper books; participation exemption and dividend relief require a 10% long-term holding and substantive operational links; for no-code SaaS/template revenue models, the MTA may apply the 'permanent establishment' risk assessment framework when the effective management and decision-making occurs outside Estonia, rendering the OÜ fully tax-resident in another state if the place of effective management is not in Estonia; full substance requirement for VAT reverse-charge and e-commerce rules applies

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-declaration of Distributions

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment for 5 years (or 10 years for hidden profit distributions); penalty up to EUR 9,600 for the legal person; daily late payment surcharge of 0.06% on unpaid tax; criminal prosecution under Penal Code for tax fraud if the concealed tax amount exceeds EUR 15,000 per year (from 2020: EUR 10,000 for intentional evasion), with fines up to EUR 3.2 million for aggravated cases

Director personal liability: Personal secondary liability of directors under LaTS/Income Tax Act for taxes not paid from company funds where the director intentionally or negligently failed to settle tax debts at maturity; tax debts may be collected from the director's personal assets; travel ban (sõidukeelu) and prohibition on leaving Estonia may be imposed by the MTA for unpaid tax over EUR 3,000; imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated tax evasion
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the OÜ as tax resident in the state of effective management; full Estonian exit taxation and foreign-country assessment; refusal of treaty benefits; the MTA may deem the company a sham and assess taxes on a look-through basis; annual report submission failure leads to forced deregistration (the Commercial Register may delete the company after 1 year of non-compliance); consequential taxation of all accumulated profits at 20% plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors dissolve the veil if management functions were performed abroad; personal liability for unpaid tax on deemed distributions; ban on serving as a board member for up to 3 years; cross-border mutual assistance in tax recovery under EU Directive 2010/24/EU and OECD MAAC; criminal liability for false statements in the annual report
AML / UBO Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Warning, then penalty up to EUR 32,000 for entities under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; forced deregistration of the company if UBO data not filed; restrictions on bank accounts and payment gateways (Stripe/Paddle) due to AML KYC refusal; cross-border information exchange under DAC6 and OECD exchange of information

Director personal liability: Directors face fines up to EUR 32,000; in aggravated cases imprisonment up to 3 years; reputation blocklists in banking and fintech rails refuse onboarding and payouts; personal liability for damages caused by continuing an unregistered UBO
Transfer Pricing / Cross-Border Artificial Transactions

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment of taxable income with a penalty surcharge of 10% on the adjusted difference under the Tax Enforcement Act; interest at 0.06% per day; refusal of deduction of expenses to related party if documentation not provided; potential CFC inclusion for profits shifted to low-tax affiliates (below 6.67% effective tax rate); where the threshold for new EU TP reporting applies, failure to file Master/Local File yields penalties up to EUR 5,000 per missing document

Director personal liability: Directors jointly liable for corporate tax adjusted on TP corrections if they knowingly signed inaccurate transfer pricing documentation; personal surcharge up to 20% of the adjusted difference in aggravated cases; travel restrictions for unpaid MTA assessments; criminal prosecution if TP manipulation amounts to intentional tax evasion
Digital Sales / VAT and Payment Gateway Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT arrears for up to 3 years; late interest 0.06% per day; penalties up to EUR 3,200 for failure to submit VAT returns; MTA can issue cross-border EU VAT recovery orders and instruct Stripe/Paddle to block payment settlement; a confirmation of non-compliance may lead to suspension of the company from Simpli/OSS and mandatory full local VAT registration in member states

Director personal liability: VAT debt may attach to directors when they continued business despite insolvency and failed to declare VAT; joint secondary liability; personal surcharge; banning from board positions in companies active in cross-border e-commerce; criminal liability for intentional non-submission when turnover exceeds EUR 200,000
