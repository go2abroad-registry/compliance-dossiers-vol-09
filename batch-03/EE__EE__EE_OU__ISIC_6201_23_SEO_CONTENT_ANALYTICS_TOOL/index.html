Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum paid-up capital is required; an OÜ may be incorporated with EUR 0 share capital. If a positive share capital is declared, cash contributions must be placed on a special bank account and evidenced by a credit institution confirmation before registration; non-cash contributions require an audited/no-notarized appraisal unless exempted by law.

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certification required in the absence of a physical industrial site. A simple fire safety inspection by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) is required if a physical office is opened. Occupational health and workplace clearance requirements under the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) apply to office spaces, including electrical safety and ergonomic workstations.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum paid-up capital is required; an OÜ may be incorporated with EUR 0 share capital. If a positive share capital is declared, cash contributions must be placed on a special bank account and evidenced by a credit institution confirmation before registration; non-cash contributions require an audited/no-notarized appraisal unless exempted by law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (ETCB), official portal emta.ee

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing operations in Estonia must register the company with the Estonian Commercial Register and obtain a tax identification number (TIN) from the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. Upon registration, the entity receives a VAT registration number if applicable based on turnover thresholds. The process involves submitting incorporation documents, company statutes, and proof of address.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establishing operations in Estonia must register the company with the Estonian Commercial Register and obtain a tax identification number (TIN) from the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. Upon registration, the entity receives a VAT registration number if applicable based on turnover thresholds. The process involves submitting incorporation documents, company statutes, and proof of address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not offer check-the-box elections. However, qualifying micro and small companies may elect simplified tax regimes, including the micro-company tax regime (0% or 20% on revenue) or the small business tax regime (14% on revenue).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

40,000 EUR annual turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT rate applies to the supply of goods exported outside the European Union and to services supplied to non-EU customers where the service is not used or enjoyed in the EU. For goods, proof of actual export is mandatory.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory e-invoicing via the e-arireis system for all taxable persons. Invoices must be issued electronically, stamped with digital signature, and transmitted to the tax authority in real time or within 7 days. Non-compliance results in penalties.

Tax authorities may audit export VAT claims. Fraudulent zero-rating can result in full clawback of the VAT amount, interest charges, and administrative penalties up to 50% of the tax due.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia, as a full EU member state, operates under unrestricted capital account convertibility and current account convertibility pursuant to EU free movement of capital and payments directives. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without restriction, subject solely to standard corporate income tax withholding at source where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or conversion requirement applies to export receipts; all proceeds remain freely usable or remittable without FX conversion mandates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank)

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed and reinvested profits; 20% (as 20/80 on net distribution) on distributed profits, yielding an effective 25% gross-up rate for declared dividends.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on ordinary dividend distributions paid to non-residents; resident corporate shareholders are subject to the standard 20/80 CIT on any profit distribution out of untaxed reserves unless the participation exemption applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

10th day of the month following the month in which the taxable payment or distribution is made (e.g. a December 31 distribution is declared by January 10).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 – Employment income and social tax Aggregate gross wages and taxable benefits paid; social tax at 33%, unemployment insurance at 0.8% (employer) and 1.6% (employee), and individual income tax withheld at 20% on taxable payroll. For non-resident employees rendering services in Estonia, a 20% flat withholding rate applies in the absence of an applicable social security and double tax treaty exemption; submit the employee's certificate of residence to reduce withholding on salary. Late TSD filing incurs an administrative fine of up to EUR 2,000 per declaration; late payment of corporate income tax accrues daily interest at 0.06% (circa 23% p.a.); intentional non-declaration may escalate to criminal tax evasion proceedings and penalties of up to EUR 7,500,000 for a legal person.
Box 5 – Corporate income tax on distributed profits Taxable distributions declared and paid in the month: cash dividends, profit shares, deemed distributions (fringe benefits, gifts, non-business expenses) were taxed at 20/80 formula on the net amount; the resulting box amount is the calculated 20% CIT. For dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, Estonia applies the statutory 20% WHT unless a lower treaty rate is available. The reduced rate is applied at source only upon submission of a valid TRC before payment; otherwise 20% withheld and excess reclaimed via refund application to the Tax Authority. Late TSD filing incurs an administrative fine of up to EUR 2,000 per declaration; late payment of corporate income tax accrues daily interest at 0.06% (circa 23% p.a.); intentional non-declaration may escalate to criminal tax evasion proceedings and penalties of up to EUR 7,500,000 for a legal person.
Box 6 – Withholding tax on interest, royalties and management fees paid to non-residents Payments of interest, royalties, lease premiums, and certain management fees to non-resident persons are subject to a 20% WHT unless exempt under domestic law, the EU Interest & Royalties Directive, or a double tax treaty. Apply 20% WHT or 0% for EU/EEA associated companies under the EU Interest & Royalties Directive when the beneficial owner provides a TRC; for royalties arising from the SEO analytics software platform, confirm the place of economic use and the applicable treaty article before filing. Late TSD filing incurs an administrative fine of up to EUR 2,000 per declaration; late payment of corporate income tax accrues daily interest at 0.06% (circa 23% p.a.); intentional non-declaration may escalate to criminal tax evasion proceedings and penalties of up to EUR 7,500,000 for a legal person.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may require transfer pricing documentation where the annual aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds EUR 1,000,000 (Local File). A Master File may be demanded where the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000. There is no automatic filing requirement; the documentation package must be delivered within 60 days of an official request.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE or Bermuda. However, the Estonian GAAR and the judicial doctrine of substance-over-form apply to deny treaty benefits and deferral advantages where a company lacks real economic activities. The board must maintain a physical office, autonomous decision-making, qualified staff, and substantive value-add in Estonia; an artificial shell will be reassessed on deemed distributions and denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment on the concealed amount at a penalty rate of up to 100% of the underpaid CIT, plus late interest; for deliberate tax evasion involving more than EUR 12,000, criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code §417 carries a prison sentence of up to 3 years for a natural person, and a fine of up to EUR 10,000,000 for a legal person.

Director personal liability: The Tax Authority can pierce the corporate veil and hold the management board members jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax to the extent of the company’s unrecovered debt. Criminal conviction triggers travel bans, asset forfeiture, director disqualification, and in serious cases imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax-treaty benefits under the OECD principal purpose test; the Tax Authority applies the GAAR to treat the entity as a fiction, reassessing a deemed distribution of 20% on the full streamed amount. Administrative fines of up to EUR 200,000 can be imposed, and the Commercial Register may initiate strike-off of the shell entity.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the de novo assessed corporate income tax plus interest if they deliberately arranged the conduit structure. Repeated non-compliance leads to personal travel bans, a 5-year management ban, and potential seizure of personal assets in cross-border collection proceedings.
AML / Financial Obscurity

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, legal persons incur a financial sanction of up to EUR 4,000,000 or 10% of annual worldwide turnover; criminal money-laundering offences can be punished by up to 10 years’ imprisonment for the responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in laundering proceeds from the platform, or who tolerate weak AML controls, face personal criminal conviction, confiscation of corporate bank accounts, disqualification from board service, and direct liability to civil claims for restitution.
