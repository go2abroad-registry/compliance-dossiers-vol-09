Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a resident OÜ is charged corporate income tax at entity level only on distributable events, namely profit distributions, fringe benefits, gifts, representative expenses, and other non-business-related disbursements, in both cases at 20/80 of the net amount (25% effective on grossed-up base); regular periodic dividends to resident individuals may qualify for the reduced 14/86 rate subject to statutory conditions; undistributed retained earnings are deferred indefinitely at 0%. Territorial scope: a resident OÜ is subject to the charge on profits sourced worldwide, but taxation crystallizes only upon distribution or deemed distribution, with capital gains on securities of Estonian resident companies generally exempt at entity level; participation exemptions and CFC rules apply to foreign-sourced income. No tax-exempt zone status applies to the standard OÜ.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through classification exists; entity classification is statutory under the Estonian Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus). Available elections under domestic law include: (a) the election to treat dividends as 'regular periodic dividends' qualifying for the reduced 14/86 corporate income tax rate where distributions have been made in a consistent preceding-period pattern and recipients are resident individuals, subject to the statutory cap; (b) the election to apply the standard 20/80 rate to all other distributions without maintaining a dividend pattern; (c) the election to form a VAT group under VAT Act § 3, requiring prior written approval from the MTA; (d) for hybrid mismatch arrangements, ATAD II rules impose mandatory self-neutralization with no elective opt-out; no separate hybrid mismatch election form is available, and non-compliance is treated as an understatement of tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover in a calendar year for domestic supplies of goods and services; EUR 10,000 annual aggregate intra-Community distance sales threshold (EU-wide) applying through the One Stop Shop (OSS); mandatory registration is effective from the day the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary registration is permitted without minimum for holding companies and other EEA taxable persons established in Estonia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of goods applies where the OÜ supplies goods dispatched or transported to (i) a taxable person in another EU Member State who holds a valid VAT identification number, constituting an intra-Community supply, or (ii) a third country, constituting an export, in both cases requiring the goods to physically leave Estonia/EU territory and the OÜ to retain transport and dispatch evidence. Zero-rating of services applies under the general B2B place-of-supply rules where the recipient is a taxable person established outside Estonia and the place of supply is therefore outside the EU or in another Member State; intra-EU B2B services are taxable at destination via reverse charge system rather than zero-rated. Critically for ISIC 6430, discretionary trust administration, fiduciary custody, asset management, and related financial services are exempt from VAT as financial services under VAT Act § 16(2), which means they are exempt without input VAT recovery and cannot be zero-rated; only separately contracted and invoiced ancillary advisory, corporate administration, or legal consulting services may be zero-rated if they qualify as measurable B2B services to non-EU taxable persons and are not subsumed within the fiduciary function.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) submitted electronically through the Estonian ETS/ECS system, bearing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and supplier's Export Accompanying Document, together with confirmation of the physical exit of goods from the customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, underlying sales contract/pro forma invoice, specifying the exporter, consignee, Incoterms, quantity, and customs value
  • Transport evidence, consisting of a Bill of Lading (B/L) for ocean freight, CMR road consignment note, or Air Waybill (AWB) completed by the carrier, cargo receipt, or the freight forwarder's delivery confirmation showing the goods left Estonia and the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank account statement evidencing the international credit transfer of proceeds from the non-resident buyer/client, with corresponding SWIFT references tying the payment to the underlying export document

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory B2B e-invoicing API regime exists in Estonia as of this date. Electronic invoicing is mandated exclusively for B2G transactions through the PEPPOL network using EN 16931 standards since 1 July 2019. For B2B and B2C transactions, e-invoicing remains voluntary subject to recipient consent; VAT filings are submitted electronically via e-MTA, with structured API interfaces available to large taxpayers for bulk reporting; invoice data is not transmitted to the MTA in real time except for VAT Information Exchange System (VIES) summaries and sales listings exchanged with other EU Member States.

The MTA applies risk-based auditing, electronic selection, and exchange-of-information with EU customs and VAT authorities to verify zero-rating claims; failure to substantiate export dispatch, payment, or commercial substance results in retrospective VAT assessment at 22% (or the rate in force) on the full supply value plus penalty interest at 0.06% per day from the original due date, an additional tax surcharge of up to 30% of the understated tax under the Taxation Act, and clawback of any input VAT previously recovered. The limitation period for assessment is three years, extended to six years in cases of intentional tax avoidance or fraud. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, fabricated export documents, falsified B/Ls, and sham circular payment chains constitute tax fraud under Penal Code § 3971 and are punishable by imprisonment for up to five years, confiscation of proceeds, and disqualification of directors; aggravated cases involving organized or repeated conduct carry a maximum penalty of seven years' imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility without capital controls. Estonia adopted the euro effective 1 January 2011; foreign exchange transactions are unrestricted, and Estonia observes IMF Article VIII, OECD Capital Liberalisation Code, and Article 63 TFEU free movement of capital. Both current-account and capital-account convertibility are liberalised; there is no approval, licensing, or notification requirement for non-resident equity injections, fiduciary inflows, or onward trust disbursements. Profit repatriation by non-resident owners is taxed only at distribution: 0% on retained earnings, 20% on irregular distributions (14% plus 7% withholding for regular qualifying distributions), and no further withholding for EU/EEA beneficial owners; treaty relief applies for non-EU residents. Euro is legal tender; the OÜ may hold multi-currency accounts, but the functional accounting currency may be EUR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist. Estonian resident entities may retain foreign currency in external accounts indefinitely; there is no statutory deadline to repatriate or convert export receivables. The only compliance obligations are commercial-book documentation (e-invoice under the Accounting Act), one-time statistical reporting through the bank to Eesti Pank's balance-of-payments data collection, and standard automated AML transaction monitoring; none of these require conversion or surrender.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), a member of the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; macroprudential authority for FX statistics and reserve management; prudential banking supervision is delegated to the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) under the Financial Supervision Act; the Financial Intelligence Unit of the Police and Border Guard Board (Rahapesu Andmebüroo 'RAB') registers and supervises Estonian trust and company service providers (TCSPs) as obliged persons under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low to Medium. Estonia-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (e.g., Monese for EEA clients, PAYHOUND, and several neobanks) can legally onboard e-Residency companies, but their internal risk appetites almost universally reject ISIC 6430 discretionary offshore trust administration because of beneficial-ownership opacity and asset-protection features. Wise Business and other cross-border EMIs typically decline the TCSP category. A regulated Estonian bank such as LHV Pank remains the safest onboarding route; EMIs should be used only for operational escrow and settlement for incidental administrative expenses, never as custodians or clearing allocators of trust corpus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from non-resident shareholders: SWIFT MT103 in EUR (or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer if originating from an EEA bank) routed to the OÜ operational account, with ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPT' (capital) or 'INTC' (intra-company) and clear narrative 'Osakapitali sissemakse — share capital contribution'. Verification bundle comprises the current extract from the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri väljavõte) reflecting the increased share capital, an updated share register (osanike nimekiri), the articles of association amendment, and the bank's internal form confirming no Estonian exchange-control approval is required. Shareholder loans use purpose code 'LOAN' or 'INTC' supported by a signed loan agreement and arm's-length interest schedule, with thin-capitalisation documentation preserved for tax purposes. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium. Estonia-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (e.g., Monese for EEA clients, PAYHOUND, and several neobanks) can legally onboard e-Residency companies, but their internal risk appetites almost universally reject ISIC 6430 discretionary offshore trust administration because of beneficial-ownership opacity and asset-protection features. Wise Business and other cross-border EMIs typically decline the TCSP category. A regulated Estonian bank such as LHV Pank remains the safest onboarding route; EMIs should be used only for operational escrow and settlement for incidental administrative expenses, never as custodians or clearing allocators of trust corpus.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% corporate income tax on distributed profits, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution; retained and reinvested profits are generally taxed at 0% until distribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% Estonian withholding tax generally applies to ordinary dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ to a non-resident shareholder. The OÜ generally pays corporate income tax of 22/78 on the net distribution. The recipient's residence-country taxation, anti-abuse rules, and beneficial-ownership rules remain relevant.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no single universal transaction-value threshold that eliminates the arm's-length requirement. Estonian local-file and master-file obligations principally arise for specified large taxpayers, regulated financial institutions, listed companies, groups, and transactions with associated persons in low-tax jurisdictions. Common large-taxpayer indicators include approximately 250 employees, annual turnover of approximately EUR 50 million, or balance-sheet total of approximately EUR 43 million, subject to the applicable Estonian regulation and taxpayer category. A master file may additionally be required for qualifying multinational groups, including groups within the country-by-country reporting framework. Related-party transactions must be priced at arm's length even when formal documentation thresholds are not met.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a standalone generic Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains critical under the Estonian Income Tax Act, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership standards, anti-avoidance provisions, CFC rules, interest limitation rules, hybrid-mismatch rules, and the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework. An OÜ providing fiduciary or asset-protection services must demonstrate genuine management, qualified personnel, operational capacity, commercial decision-making, appropriate premises or controlled infrastructure, books and records, regulated-service authorization where required, and a real assumption of risks. An e-Residency card, registered address, nominee director, or bank account alone does not establish substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed fiduciary, trust, or company-service activity

Statutory sanction: Order to cease activity, licence or authorization refusal or revocation, administrative or misdemeanour fines, tax reassessment, confiscation of proceeds, and potential criminal liability where the conduct involves fraud, unauthorized financial activity, or repeated unlawful operations.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal civil liability for damage caused by unlawful management, reimbursement of improperly extracted assets, disqualification, and criminal liability where they knowingly organize or facilitate unauthorized activity. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are possible only under the applicable criminal or enforcement proceedings and are not automatic consequences of a licensing breach.
Tax evasion and concealed distributions

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment for unpaid corporate income tax and withholding tax, statutory interest, penalties, recovery costs, and potential criminal prosecution for intentional concealment of tax liability. Corporate criminal sanctions may include a substantial monetary penalty; natural persons may face a fine or imprisonment, with higher exposure for aggravated or large-scale conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual decision-makers may be jointly exposed to civil restitution, criminal liability, asset seizure, and enforcement restrictions where they intentionally participated in the evasion. The corporate form does not protect a director from liability for personal misconduct or fraudulent diversion.
Money laundering and terrorist-financing exposure

Statutory sanction: AML enforcement may include enhanced supervision, administrative fines, activity restrictions, licence revocation, confiscation, and criminal prosecution. Money laundering can result in significant fines and imprisonment, with aggravated penalties for organized, repeated, professional, or large-scale conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, beneficial owners, and other responsible persons may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, disqualification, and civil claims. Imprisonment and travel restrictions require statutory grounds and competent-authority proceedings.
Beneficial-owner concealment and nominee arrangements

Statutory sanction: Correction orders, administrative fines, AML enforcement, refusal or termination of banking relationships, licence consequences, tax reassessment, and criminal liability where concealment forms part of fraud, money laundering, or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors may be personally liable for false filings, concealment, and resulting losses. The corporate veil does not shield deliberate misrepresentation, unlawful nominee conduct, or misuse of company assets.
Related-party transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment, additional corporate income tax or withholding tax, interest, documentation penalties, denial of treaty benefits, and potential anti-avoidance recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for losses caused by intentional or grossly negligent transactions, particularly where the arrangement disadvantages the OÜ or its creditors. Criminal exposure arises if the pricing manipulation is part of deliberate tax fraud or falsification.
False accounting and sham documentation

Statutory sanction: Rejection of accounting evidence, tax reassessment, bookkeeping sanctions, fines, confiscation, and criminal prosecution for document fraud, accounting offences, tax evasion, or money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and accountants who knowingly approve or use false records may face personal civil claims, criminal fines, imprisonment, disqualification, and asset seizure.
Illegal distribution and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unlawful distribution, director-liability claims, insolvency proceedings, tax consequences, interest, and possible criminal liability for fraudulent asset diversion or creditor prejudice.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore company assets, compensate creditors, bear insolvency-related liability, and face disqualification or imprisonment where fraudulent intent or aggravated misconduct is established.
Sanctions evasion and prohibited cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: Payment blocking, asset freezing, administrative or criminal sanctions, confiscation, licence consequences, and potential international enforcement cooperation.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance personnel may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, asset seizure, disqualification, and imprisonment where they knowingly authorize or facilitate sanctions evasion.
Substance and permanent-establishment misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Tax residence challenge, foreign permanent-establishment exposure, transfer-pricing adjustment, denial of treaty benefits, penalties, interest, and duplicate taxation risk.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for inaccurate filings and regulatory breaches. Personal criminal liability generally requires intentional fraud, false documentation, money laundering, or another separately criminalized act.
