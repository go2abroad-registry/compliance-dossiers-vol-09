Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For online incorporation via the e-Business Register, an Osaühing may be registered with a declared share capital of at least EUR 0.01 (one share with nominal value); if traditional notarised paper filing is used, the minimum share capital is EUR 2,500. No escrow account is mandatory; capital may be contributed by bank transfer to the company's bank account before or after registration as stated in the Articles of Association.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Rescue Board, including fire load calculation, evacuation routes, and suppression systems; structural load inspection certificate issued by a licensed civil engineer certifying that the building can support inspection/testing equipment; workplace clearance from the Labor Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) confirming that occupational health, ergonomic, and sanitary conditions meet the Occupational Health and Safety Act.

Paid-in capital rule: For online incorporation via the e-Business Register, an Osaühing may be registered with a declared share capital of at least EUR 0.01 (one share with nominal value); if traditional notarised paper filing is used, the minimum share capital is EUR 2,500. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-MTA digital portal at eelring.emta.ee

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-controlled OÜ, the entity is registered in the Estonian e-Business Register online using e-Residency digital ID. The Estonian tax/corporate registration number is assigned upon incorporation, which also activates the tax account in the e-MTA. For VAT, the OÜ must file the VAT registration application (form VAT-R) through e-MTA; non-EU resident entities must appoint a fiscal/tax representative unless an EU reverse-charge or special scheme applies. VAT number is granted after submission and validation by the MTA (typically 3–5 business days).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-controlled OÜ, the entity is registered in the Estonian e-Business Register online using e-Residency digital ID. The Estonian tax/corporate registration number is assigned upon incorporation, which also activates the tax account in the e-MTA. For VAT, the OÜ must file the VAT registration application (form VAT-R) through e-MTA; non-EU resident entities must appoint a fiscal/tax representative unless an EU reverse-charge or special scheme applies. VAT number is granted after submission and validation by the MTA (typically 3–5 business days).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists for an OÜ; the entity-level CIT regime on distributions is statutory mandatory. Available elections include the reduced regular-distribution CIT rate (currently 14/86 for qualifying regular distributions), VAT group registration under VAT Act, and the EU OSS (One-Stop-Shop) schemes for cross-border services. No general hybrid mismatch election is available under Estonian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover for persons established in Estonia; non-established taxable persons with taxable supplies in Estonia must register regardless of threshold unless the supply is reverse-charged; €10,000 threshold applies to intra-EU distance sales and certain digital B2C services (via OSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT is available for goods exported from Estonia to a third country where transport/exports is evidenced by the customs export declaration (SAD/ECS) and transport documents. For services, 0% applies if the place of supply is outside Estonia, which for B2B testing/inspection services is generally the customer’s country of establishment; in such cases the customer self-accounts under the reverse-charge mechanism. Direct links between the service and exported goods may also trigger zero-rating if documentary proof is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ECS/SAD) confirming actual export
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR transport document
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance or bank wire evidence (proof of consideration received)
  • Incoterms clause and/or export contract as corroborating evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital stamping requirement for B2B invoices. B2G e-invoicing to the Estonian public sector is mandatory in structured PEPPOL-compliant format. The e-MTA supports electronic tax return and VAT file submissions over an authenticated API. Invoices must be retained for seven years, and B2B electronic invoices may be issued in any compliant e-invoice/PDF+XML format.

MTA will retroactively assess 22% VAT plus late interest where zero-rating is claimed without documentation proving export or foreign-customer place of supply within the required period. Penalties for negligent or fraudulent VAT understatement can reach 40% of the unpaid VAT, together with penalty interest; intentional evasion triggers criminal liability under the Penal Code (fine, imprisonment, and seizure). The statutory limitation for assessment and clawback is generally 6 years from the end of the tax period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under full capital and current account convertibility as an EU member state and Eurozone participant. No capital controls apply; profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation face no regulatory hurdles or approval requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds required under EU law. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts or used freely without statutory timelines or conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the net amount of a taxable profit distribution, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution. Undistributed profits are not subject to Estonian corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid to resident or non-resident shareholders where the distribution is taxed at the ordinary 22/78 corporate rate. A 7% withholding tax may apply to certain dividends distributed by a resident company where the dividend is paid from qualifying profits previously taxed at the former 14/86 rate, subject to the transitional rules and recipient circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no single transaction-value threshold that universally triggers both a Local File and a Master File. Detailed documentation requirements apply to specified categories of Estonian taxpayers, including qualifying large enterprises and groups, and may also apply to cross-border transactions involving associated persons in low-tax jurisdictions. The statutory large-enterprise criteria include employee, turnover, and balance-sheet thresholds; assess the applicable Estonian rules and the group’s consolidated position for the relevant year. The currency is EUR.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a general Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Estonian tax residence, permanent establishment, transfer pricing, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse, and business-purpose rules remain relevant. An OÜ should maintain evidence of actual decision-making, personnel, premises or operational arrangements, services performed, and commercial rationale consistent with its activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and false reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, statutory interest, administrative sanctions, and, where criminal-law elements are met, criminal penalties. Criminal liability and penalty ranges depend on the conduct, amount, intent, and applicable Penal Code provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability for their own intentional participation and may incur liability under tax and company law for breach of duties. Company debts do not automatically become directors' personal debts; veil-piercing or personal recovery requires a legal basis and proof. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are not automatic tax-collection consequences.
Transfer pricing misstatement

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board may adjust the taxable amount and assess tax and interest; penalties may apply for procedural violations or deliberate conduct. Maintain agreements, benchmarking, service evidence, cost allocations, and proof of commercial benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own misconduct or statutory breaches. A transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create personal civil or criminal liability.
Unlawful profit extraction or misclassified payments

Statutory sanction: Reclassification, corporate income tax, applicable withholding tax, interest, and potential administrative or criminal consequences where the statutory elements are met.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for breach of fiduciary or statutory duties and for their own participation in unlawful conduct. Shareholder repayment and director liability depend on the applicable company-law and insolvency rules.
Tax fraud, money laundering, or sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal prosecution, confiscation, regulatory reporting, account restrictions, and penalties under applicable tax, anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and criminal laws. The specific sanction depends on the offence and facts.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate may be prosecuted personally. Corporate status does not shield a director from liability for personal criminal conduct; travel bans and imprisonment require a competent authority's lawful decision and are not automatic.
Unlicensed or improperly represented inspection activity

Statutory sanction: Possible enforcement under the applicable Estonian sectoral and conformity-assessment rules, including orders to cease or correct activity and administrative or criminal consequences where prescribed. Confirm whether the actual service requires authorization; commercial factory quality-control inspection is not automatically a statutorily licensed activity.

Director personal liability: Personal liability depends on the director's own participation, statutory duties, and the relevant sectoral law; no automatic veil-piercing applies.
