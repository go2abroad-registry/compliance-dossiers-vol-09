Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), as amended, in particular Sections 135–140 (formation and share capital), 142 (application for registration), 146 (management board), 179–182 (Commercial Register proceedings); supplemented by the General Part of the Civil Code Act and the State Fees Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required at incorporation; the registered capital is fixed solely in the Articles of Association. A cash contribution, if stated, must be paid into the company's payment or escrow account before or immediately after registration in accordance with the Commercial Code. Client monies received for study-abroad and visa-advisory engagements must be held separately in a designated client escrow account under the Law of Obligations Act, and may not be treated as registered capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); official e-service portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee / emta.ee); company formation and registry code administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems via the e-Business Register (ariregister.rik.ee).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign parent establishing an OÜ: (1) incorporate the OÜ online through the e-Business Register using a qualified digital identity (ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID or e-Residency digital ID); the assigned registry code is the entity's statutory TIN. (2) Activate the e-MTA account under that registry code and file the mandatory beneficial-owner notification with the Commercial Register. (3) Apply for an EE-prefixed VAT identification number once taxable turnover reaches EUR 50,000 in a calendar year or voluntarily if input VAT recovery or VAT-compliant invoicing to cross-border clients is needed; non-resident persons submit the MTA's electronic VAT registration application and may appoint a tax representative. (4) No separate TIN is issued: the registry code simultaneously serves as corporate tax, employer withholding, customs and statistical identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box pass-through election exists in Estonia. The OÜ is subject to the entity-level deferred-distribution model: 20% corporate income tax is levied only on distributions (dividends, deemed profit distributions, fringe benefits and non-business disbursements), while undistributed profits remain tax-exempt. A statutory 'regular corporate income tax' election introduced by amendment to the Income Tax Act is available for qualifying companies predominantly owned by non-residents, which subjects accounting profits to an annual 20% entity-level tax and preserves foreign tax credit claims; the election is company-specific, irrevocable for a fixed term and requires shareholder consent. Hybrid mismatch and CFC rules under the Income Tax Act transpose the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive and restrict deduction or credit relief for arrangements characterised differently in the counterpart jurisdiction; neither the OÜ nor its shareholders can elect pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 50,000 (mandatory registration threshold for taxable turnover of Estonian and non-resident suppliers in a calendar year; EUR is the local currency)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Estonia to a non-EU destination qualify for 0% VAT where the export customs declaration (EXS/SAD) is released and the goods leave the EU within statutory time limits. Intra-EU dispatches qualify for 0% only if the customer's valid EU VAT number is held and the supply is reported in the VAT-ECL. For services, B2B supplies are beyond Estonian VAT where the customer belongs in another jurisdiction (reverse charge); B2C study-abroad, educational and immigration-advisory services are outside the territorial scope where the place of effective use and enjoyment is in a third country. The taxable person must substantiate the export by customer residency evidence, signed engagement letters identifying the recipient and jurisdiction, contemporaneous transport and waybill records, and banking proof identifying the remitter and the consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration - electronic Single Administrative Document (SAD/EXS) with definitive exit release
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR evidencing physical cross-border movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export consideration in foreign currency

Estonia does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, certified fiscal-storage devices or digital stamping. E-invoicing is voluntary and built on the national e-Arve XML standard aligned to Peppol BIS 3.0. VAT returns (KMD), recapitulative statements (VAT-ECL) and customs declarations are filed exclusively through the e-MTA API and portal; at tax audit, structured ledger data (SAF-T type) must be submitted electronically. Estonia supports the EU VAT in the Digital Age package; phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing will enter into force once the EU agreement is transposed.

Unsupported zero-rating is recharacterised by the MTA as a standard-rated domestic supply; the authority retrospectively assesses the output VAT, reverses corresponding input VAT deductions and charges penalty interest of 0.06% per day on the overdue amount. The ordinary audit window is three years from the due date, extended to seven years where concealment or fraud indicators exist. Knowingly false export documentation may constitute criminal tax fraud under Section 403 of the Penal Code (imprisonment up to five years for natural persons and monetary fines for legal entities), in addition to administrative tax fines, public blacklisting and enhanced future audit profiling; the business sustains a retroactive clawback of claimed input VAT for the full statutory assessment period together with all related interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is part of the euro area; the euro is fully convertible for both capital and current account transactions. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to foreign shareholders are generally free of exchange control approval, subject only to standard banking due diligence, corporate law solvency tests, and tax compliance (withholding tax may apply under national law unless treaty relief or EU Directive applies). Non-resident corporate accounts are permitted without require eur-denominated clearing in the EU real-time settlement system TARGET2. No pre-approval is required for cross-border payments under Estonian foreign exchange law, which retains restrictions only for sanctioned jurisdictions, politically exposed persons, and funds implicated by AML/CFT or international restrictive measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists in Estonia or under EU law to repatriate, surrender, or convert export proceeds. Estonian residents and non-residents may hold euro and foreign currency accounts freely. For an OÜ engaged in education advisory and immigration visa services, inbound fee receipts are treated as service export proceeds; there is no mandatory timeline for conversion or repatriation, although for tax efficiency proceeds may be retained or distributed subject to the standard 14/7 rule for qualifying dividends under the Estonian distributed profit tax system.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) in conjunction with the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for prudential and AML matters; currency regulation derives from EU Treaty provisions and Estonian legislation implementing the euro

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist
