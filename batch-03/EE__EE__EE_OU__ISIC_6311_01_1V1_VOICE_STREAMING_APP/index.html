Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); digital service portal e-MTA (https://e-mta.emta.ee); e-Residency business portal (https://www.e-resident.gov.ee); company registration through the e-Business Register (https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee).

E-government filing portal

A legal-entity tax identification for an Osaühing is the eight-digit Registration Code (registrikood) issued automatically by the e-Business Register upon incorporation; it serves as the statutory TIN for all tax filings and VAT returns. A foreign entity without an Estonian legal presence must (i) obtain an e-Residency digital ID card or appoint a local authorised representative, (ii) submit the VAT registration application through the e-MTA portal stating legal-entity details, economic activity codes (EMTAK/ISIC), and expected turnover, and (iii) receive a separate EE-prefixed VAT registration number; a non-EU-established trader must additionally appoint an Estonian fiscal representative unless it registers under the Union One-Stop-Shop or Import One-Stop-Shop procedure. No separate national TIN application exists beyond the registrikood.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a check-the-box election. An OÜ is by default an entity-level taxpayer under the distributed-profits regime: corporate income tax is levied only on profit distributions at 20% of the gross distribution (20/80 of the net amount). A 'regular distribution' qualifies for the reduced 14% gross rate (14/86 of net) for resident recipients, calculated by reference to distributions taxed in the preceding three years under section 50(2) of the Income Tax Act. Non-resident shareholders are subject to statutory 20% withholding unless a tax treaty reduces the rate. Hybrid-mismatch rules implementing EU ATAD/ATAD 2 apply to re-characterise entities for double-non-taxation avoidance, and Estonian CFC rules may treat undistributed passive income of certain controlled non-resident entities as deemed distributions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT registration of an Estonian-established taxable person; EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold for non-established digital sellers registering under the Union One-Stop-Shop; voluntary VAT registration is permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to goods physically exported by the supplier or on its behalf from Estonia to a third country, to specified international transport and related services, and to services whose place of supply is outside the EU/EEA under the place-of-supply rules. For the voice-streaming subscription service, B2C supplies to customers established outside the EU/EEA fall outside the scope of Estonian VAT (customer-location rule); B2C supplies to customers in other EU member states are taxable at the consumer's home-state VAT rate under the OSS scheme. The supplier must evidence the customer's country of establishment through payment metadata, device/network identifiers, subscriber registration data, and invoicing records.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX document with MRN and customs office of export stamp)
  • Commercial invoice including export exemption wording and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing physical dispatch to a third-country destination
  • E-invoice (e-Arve) or standard VAT invoice issued to the non-resident customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX statement evidencing cross-border receipt of payment
  • OSS quarterly return and KMDINF turnover detail lines where applicable as supplementary evidence

Estonia imposes no real-time government pre-clearance, no fiscal-stamping, and no mandatory clearance API for invoices. B2G invoices must be transmitted as structured e-invoices (UBL/e-Arve) through the national e-invoice network; B2B e-invoicing is widely used but not legally mandatory. All VAT returns (form KMD) and turnover-structure declarations (KMDINF) are filed exclusively through the e-MTA portal, with machine-to-machine integration available via the national X-Road data-exchange layer. VAT payments are due by the 20th day of the month following the close of the taxable period.

Fraudulent or sham zero-rating exposes the taxpayer to reversal of output VAT, restitution of all previously recovered input VAT, and statutory late-payment interest of 0.06% per day. Administrative fines of up to 5% of the tax amount may be imposed under the Taxation Act; criminal tax-fraud penalties under sections 403-405 of the Penal Code provide for up to five years' imprisonment together with confiscation for large-scale fraud. Retrospective assessments may be raised within the three-year statutory tax-assessment window, which is extended where declarations were omitted or materially false.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible. Estonia applies a liberal foreign exchange regime consistent with EU free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act. Both capital and current account transactions are substantially unrestricted. Profit remittances and dividend distributions require no exchange control approval, but are subject to the Estonian corporate income tax system (typically 20% at distribution, unless treaty or withholding relief applies). Cross-border payments are subject to balance-of-payments statistical reporting and EU anti-money-laundering information requirements, but no permit, conversion, or surrender requirements apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia does not mandate export proceeds surrender, minimum repatriation percentages, or mandatory conversion of foreign currency into euro. Export receipts may be held in any currency. For statistical and prudential supervision, Eesti Pank may require monthly balance-of-payments data from resident companies where cross-border payment volumes or positions exceed notification thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem; monetary policy and currency issuance are ultimately governed by the European Central Bank (ECB) for the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: High for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions operating under PSD2 passporting (e.g., an EMI licensed in another EEA state may provide payment services in Estonia). Feasibility: Medium for offshore non-EEA EMIs, which face severe legal constraints under EU anti-money-laundering rules and may fail Estonian bank counterparty due-diligence. Practical constraints include: difficulty linking Apple IAP / Google Play Billing merchant settlement into some EMI accounts; potential automatic laundering of funds if the EMI has no Estonian branch; and reluctance of domestic banks to accept foreign EMI as sole treasury rail. An OÜ should maintain an Estonian-regulated bank account as primary liquidity anchor, with selected EEA EMIs serving as supplementary FX and payout rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the OÜ's EUR account are routed by SWIFT MT103, MT202, or SEPA Credit Transfer from the shareholder's bank, depending on originating jurisdiction. The payment instruction must be accompanied by: (a) a clear narrative in field 70, e.g., 'Equity capital contribution - OÜ share capital'; (b) where the receiving bank requires a statistically meaningful purpose code, use the balance-of-payments code assigned by Eesti Pank for equity transactions (commonly 'S301' for issue of common equity) and for intercompany loans the corresponding loan-receipt code; (c) a certified copy of the Estonian Business Registry notice or e-Notary resolution approving the capital increase; and (d) a notarised or digitally signed shareholder declaration confirming the contribution is genuine, not a loan conversion, and that funds are sourced lawfully. Verification at the receiving bank also includes checking wire date, remitter-beneficiary legal entity match, and full-match residue on the company's IBAN; any missing purpose narrative triggers an AML hold.Trade settlements and operational disbursements use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in EUR for EU/EEA beneficiaries; SWIFT MT103 (single transaction) or MT101 (batch) for non-EU beneficiaries or currencies other than EUR; TARGET2/RTGS only for urgent high-value euro payments; and documentary instruments such as UCP600-compliant commercial letters of credit (MT700) for high-value infrastructure, software licensing, or content acquisition where supplier offering requires secured payment. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing proceeds net of applicable App Store tax withholdings are received to the OÜ's EUR account via SWIFT or SEPA from the respective payout headquarters; the OÜ must map those inbound reference IDs to the corresponding Google/Apple transaction reports and apply the correct inbound BOP code.Deliverable FX forwards (plain vanilla) in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/SEK with an EEA-licensed credit institution or eligible counterparty under ISDA 2002 and EMIR reporting/clearing obligations.FX swaps (swap points transaction) to convert temporary surplus foreign currency into EUR for operational liquidity while preserving future re-selling exposure.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS/CCIRS) under ISDA for financing assets or intercompany loans involving material non-EUR debt, subject to EMIR clearing thresholds if applicable.OTC and exchange-traded FX options (EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) providing asymmetric hedging for uncertain app-store payout volumes and advertising cost inflows.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for exotic currencies without a robust deliverable FX market (e.g., certain Latam, MENA, and Asian ring-fenced currencies) when the OÜ monetises products through those jurisdictions; NDFs on fully deliverable currencies such as EUR/USD are commercially unnecessary and generally not offered by Estonian-regulated banks.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed profits; 20% gross CIT on distributed profits (statutory formula 20/80 of net distribution)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

No separate shareholder-level withholding tax; the OÜ pays corporate income tax of 20% on the gross dividend (20/80 of net) at distribution; effective final burden on the shareholder is 20% of gross; reduced 14% (14/86) for regular distributions; 0% for a qualifying EU/EEA corporate parent under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% holding, ≥12 months)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold in the Income Tax Act; the MTA applies OECD TPG and the burden of proof is on the taxpayer for every cross-border controlled transaction. Practical de minimis used by MTA audits: approx. EUR 50,000 per related-party transaction per year. Mandatory CbC Report for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue ≥ EUR 750 million; Master File/Local File compliance is strongly recommended for any OÜ with recurrent intergroup flows (royalties, management fees, marketing recharge).

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no local Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) modelled on Cayman/BVI. However, the General Anti-Abuse Rule under the Estonian Tax Act (MKS § 82-83) disregards artificial transactions whose sole purpose is tax avoidance, and the EU ATAD GAAR (implemented) applies to abusive arrangements. An OÜ engaged in voice streaming must demonstrate real economic substance: local/adequate employees, actual decision-making of the board in Estonia, active management of app contracts and IP, and a functional profile matching the TNMM/CUP results. A letterbox OÜ with remote foreign management can be recharacterised as a tax resident of the shareholder's jurisdiction under the 'place of effective management' tie-breaker, or its income can be pulled into a foreign CFC charge, denying the 0% deferred-tax benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Hidden distribution/under-declaration of dividends

Statutory sanction: Additional CIT of 20/80 on the undisclosed distribution; late-payment interest 0.06% per day; tax penalty up to 3 times the avoided tax (MTA practice); criminal fine for the legal person from EUR 400 up to EUR 8,000,000 under the Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Director liable for restitution of the company's tax debt under Commercial Code § 376; disqualification from managing a company for up to 3 years; imprisonment up to 5 years (Penal Code § 390(1)); travel ban and asset seizure in cross-border cases.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterisation of the arrangement; deemed distribution of accumulated profits taxed at 20/80; denial of treaty benefits and of the e-Residency business registration; potential compulsory deregistration of the OÜ from the Commercial Register.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors jointly and severally liable for CIT, social tax and VAT arrears (Tax Act § 8-9 and Commercial Code § 376); prohibition from re-entering/using e-Residency digital identity; criminal conviction in the shareholder's home state for CFC non-disclosure.
Failure to File / Persistent TSD and INF 1 Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty up to EUR 600 per declaration; coercive fine (sun of up to EUR 200 per month) under MKS; interest accrual at 0.06% per day; tax authority can apply for forced collection against bank accounts and commercially register pledges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause non-filing face criminal tax fraud charges (imprisonment up to 5 years); the MTA may lift the corporate veil against the officer who was the 'de facto manager' and order civil restitution with personal bank seizure.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Excessive Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment; secondary adjustment is a deemed distribution of the excess payment subject to 20/80 CIT; late-payment interest; penalty up to 10% of the adjusted amount; loss of foreign tax credit in the payee state leading to double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's-length pricing may be required to reimburse the company for taxes paid as loss/damage; civil liability to the shareholders; criminal sanctions for falsification of tax data (imprisonment up to 3 years).
AML – Unlicensed Payment/Wallet Operation in the App

Statutory sanction: AML Act administrative fine for a legal person up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; confiscation of criminal proceeds; order to cease the unlawful activity; ban on app store accounts (Apple/Google enforcement).

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to EUR 200,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering offences (Penal Code § 394); prohibition from board membership; travel bans; extradition exposure through EU mutual recognition instruments.
