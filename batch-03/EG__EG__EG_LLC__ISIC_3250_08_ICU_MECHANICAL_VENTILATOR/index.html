Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; primary digital portal: egypttax.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing/e-receipts platform: invoicing.eta.gov.eg (FATOORA integration via REST APIs).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC must apply to the ETA for the unified Tax Identification Card (Unified Tax ID / 'Tax Card') by submitting Form 1 together with: (1) notarized and Arabic-translated certificate of incorporation and articles of association of the LLC; (2) Commercial Registry extract; (3) head-office constitutional documents and certificate of good standing of each non-resident shareholder; (4) power of attorney appointing the resident manager and tax accountant; (5) proof of registered seat (lease or title deed); (6) bank certificate confirming the LLC's Egyptian account; and (7) taxpayer profile including ISIC 3250 medical-device manufacturing activity. The ETA issues a single Tax ID used for CIT, VAT, payroll, stamp, and customs purposes. Non-resident suppliers without an Egyptian establishment are generally outside CIT nexus, but import VAT and e-invoicing obligations capture inbound sales of medical components; foreign exporters to Egypt obtain an ETA importer code/VAT registration for customs clearance only if they make taxable supplies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Egyptian law. Available statutory elections: (a) simplified SME tax regime under Law No. 152 of 2020 for micro, small, and medium enterprises below statutory turnover ceilings; (b) opting into a Public/Private Free Zone license or the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) regime, which replaces standard CIT with special reduced or zero tax treatment on qualifying export-oriented turnover; (c) voluntary VAT registration at EGP 150,000 taxable turnover; (d) electing the Exporters' VAT Refund Settlement and Input-Tax Offset mechanism; and (e) applying for an advance pricing agreement (APA) to stabilize transfer-pricing treatment on export margins.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies (mandatory registration under VAT Law 67/2016); voluntary registration at EGP 150,000 annual turnover. Manufacturing exporters of ICU ventilators are recommended to register immediately regardless of turnover to recover input VAT on imported electronic components, sub-assemblies, and raw materials.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available under the VAT Law for: (a) goods physically exported outside the customs territory of Egypt, supported by a valid customs export declaration and the Egyptian ACI (Advance Cargo Information) release record; and (b) services rendered to non-resident recipients where the benefit and use of the service occurs entirely outside Egypt. ICU mechanical ventilators designed, assembled, and exported by the LLC qualify as exported goods provided title and physical possession pass to the foreign buyer and the goods are not consumed or diverted within Egypt. The exporter must issue an e-invoice marked zero-rated with the underlying export contract, commercial invoice, and proof of dispatch; the corresponding input VAT on components and production inputs is reclaimable through the ETA export refund or mutual-settlement mechanism, subject to input-apportionment verification.

Yes. ETA mandates a phased e-invoicing (FATOORA) and e-receipts clearance regime. Registered taxpayers must integrate their ERP/accounting system with the ETA API gateway, submit standardized JSON/XML invoice/receipt files, obtain generation codes and validation report numbers in near-real-time, and stamp B2C e-receipts with ETA-issued QR codes. Any invoice failing automated validation is rejected, input VAT deduction may be disallowed, and the taxpayer is subject to penalty and risk-scoring sanctions. Medical-device manufacturers engaged in B2BG embedded supply into public tenders are automatically within the scope of the e-invoicing mandate from their assigned integration wave.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) jointly scrutinize export refund claims, documentary consistency, and FX repatriation. Fraudulent or overstated zero-rating exposes the exporter to: (i) retrospective clawback of all refunded input VAT with statutory default interest; (ii) monetary fines of up to double or treble the tax evaded under VAT Law 67/2016, plus additional penalties for submission of false documents; (iii) administrative suspension or cancellation of the Unified Tax ID, customs importer/exporter code, and medical-device export license; (iv) criminal prosecution of the general manager, tax representative, and responsible officers, including imprisonment; and (v) inclusion in the ETA public non-compliant taxpayer list, which blocks participation in government tenders and LC transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP operates under a managed float (CBE unified the interbank exchange rate in March 2024). Egypt accepts IMF Article VIII obligations, so current-account payments (imports, services, dividends) are freely convertible against documentary evidence; the capital account is liberalized but regulated: inward equity must be registered through a licensed bank via the CBE's Foreign Investment Registration system and a GAFI certificate to secure lawful repatriation rights; outward profit remittance requires a valid Tax Card, ETA tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements and the standard CBE profit-remittance package. Practical FX rationing tail-risk persists, so treasury must maintain an onshore USD buffer of not less than three months of operating expenses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion under the 2022–2024 liberalized regime: an Egyptian exporter retains 100% of USD export proceeds in Foreign Currency Accounts and may pay external suppliers or convert freely; export receipts must be repatriated through formal banking channels within 12 months of the bill-of-lading date and reported to the financing bank within 7 banking days of credit; each export movement must be declared through the Egyptian Single Administrative Document (SAD) integrated with the Advanced Cargo Information (ACI) platform prior to customs release.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the exclusive monetary, foreign-exchange and trade-finance supervisor under Central Bank and Banking Sector Law No. 194 of 2020; CBE Board Decrees No. 115/2022 (foreign-currency accounting), No. 50/2023 (banking conduct) and No. 589/2021 (AML/CFT unified rules) constitute the binding treasury framework.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of net taxable profit for Egyptian companies and Egyptian permanent establishments, subject to applicable special-zone, investment-law, or sector-specific rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding on dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company is the general domestic rate. A 5% rate may apply to qualifying dividends connected with listed shares or other statutory conditions. Treaty rates may reduce the rate, subject to the relevant Egyptian double-tax agreement and anti-abuse requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within four months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 30 April of the following year. A company using an approved non-calendar fiscal year generally files within four months after that fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where related-party transactions exceed EGP 8,000,000 in the relevant tax year, subject to transaction-category and current ETA requirements. A Master File and country-by-country reporting obligations may additionally apply to Egyptian entities belonging to multinational groups meeting the applicable consolidated-group revenue threshold, commonly EGP 3,000,000,000 or its prescribed equivalent under the current Egyptian rules. Thresholds and filing mechanics must be verified for the relevant filing year.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not generally operate a separate, standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to those of certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, Egyptian tax authorities may challenge arrangements lacking commercial substance under general anti-avoidance, sham-transaction, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, transfer-pricing, deductibility, and substance-over-form principles. The LLC should maintain Egyptian personnel, premises, production capacity, decision-making evidence, operational assets, contracts, inventory records, bank activity, and risk-control evidence proportionate to its ventilator manufacturing functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate concealment

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, statutory compensation or late-payment charges, administrative penalties, and criminal prosecution. Tax-evasion offenses may carry imprisonment and fines under the Egyptian Income Tax Law, with sanctions potentially increasing for repetition or aggravated conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, authorized signatories, and responsible tax agents may face personal criminal liability where they participated in, ordered, knowingly approved, or facilitated the conduct. Courts may impose restitution and collection measures; travel restrictions are possible through competent judicial or prosecutorial process and are not automatic.
False invoicing, fictitious transactions, and VAT manipulation

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions or credits, repayment of refunds, tax reassessment, penalties, interest or compensation, suspension of tax registration or electronic invoicing access, and possible criminal prosecution under tax and penal laws.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and signatories may incur civil and criminal exposure where knowledge, authorization, or failure of required supervision is established. Personal liability is fact-dependent and does not arise solely from holding office.
Transfer-pricing failure and profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to taxable income, possible secondary adjustment or withholding-tax exposure, documentation penalties, late-payment charges, and audit escalation. The ETA may reject unsupported deductions and impose penalties for inaccurate or incomplete disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may face personal exposure for fraudulent documentation or deliberate tax evasion. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments generally create corporate tax liability rather than automatic director liability.
Improper profit repatriation and withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unwithheld tax from the Egyptian payer, late-payment compensation, administrative penalties, denial of deductions, and possible joint-liability or collection proceedings against responsible persons under applicable tax procedures.

Director personal liability: Authorized signatories and finance officers may be pursued where they intentionally failed to withhold, misused treaty relief, or submitted false certificates. Travel restrictions or imprisonment require the applicable judicial or prosecutorial basis and are not automatic.
Customs, import, product-regulatory, and medical-device non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, confiscation, seizure, administrative fines, license suspension or cancellation, product recall, facility closure, and criminal penalties under customs, medical-device, health, and commercial laws.

Director personal liability: Directors, quality officers, customs representatives, and authorized signatories may incur civil, administrative, and criminal liability for knowing participation, false declarations, or failure to maintain required regulatory controls. Imprisonment and travel restrictions depend on the charged offense and judicial action.
Anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and suspicious-payment violations

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting failures, account or asset freezing, transaction rejection, regulatory sanctions, fines, license consequences, confiscation, and criminal prosecution under Egyptian AML and related laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability for knowing facilitation, concealment, obstruction, or negligent failure to implement mandatory controls. Asset freezes and travel restrictions may be ordered by competent authorities where legally justified.
Shell company, nominee ownership, and beneficial-ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Registration and licensing sanctions, tax reassessment, denial of treaty or incentive benefits, corporate and beneficial-owner disclosure orders, fines, and criminal liability for fraud, forgery, tax evasion, or money laundering where applicable.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners may be personally liable for fraudulent filings, concealment, or unlawful control. Courts may order restitution, asset recovery, and, where legally authorized, travel restrictions or imprisonment.
Corporate books, annual accounts, and tax-record destruction

Statutory sanction: Record-keeping penalties, estimated assessment, denial of deductions, audit extension, forced collection, administrative sanctions, and potential criminal prosecution for obstruction or intentional destruction of evidence.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible accountants may be personally liable for deliberate destruction, obstruction, or false certification. Corporate limited liability does not protect individuals from their own statutory or criminal violations.
