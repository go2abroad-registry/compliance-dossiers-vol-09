Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet; MTA), administered through the e-MTA electronic tax and customs portal. Companies are identified primarily by their Estonian Commercial Register code; VAT-registered persons receive an EE-prefixed VAT identification number.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ obtains its statutory identifier upon registration with the Estonian Commercial Register. The company establishes e-MTA access through an authorized management-board member, representative, or power of attorney. A non-resident company without an Estonian registered entity applies to MTA for non-resident taxpayer registration where it has an Estonian permanent establishment, taxable activity, withholding obligation, or other statutory basis. VAT registration is applied for electronically through e-MTA or in writing to MTA, supported by constitutional documents, commercial-register extracts, business-activity information, expected turnover, contracts or invoices evidencing taxable activity, and representative authorization where applicable. MTA may request additional evidence of genuine economic activity and may refuse or cancel VAT registration where the statutory conditions are not met.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An Estonian OÜ obtains its statutory identifier upon registration with the Estonian Commercial Register. The company establishes e-MTA access through an authorized management-board member, representative, or power of attorney. A non-resident company without an Estonian registered entity applies to MTA for non-resident taxpayer registration where it has an Estonian permanent establishment, taxable activity, withholding obligation, or other statutory basis. VAT registration is applied for electronically through e-MTA or in writing to MTA, supported by constitutional documents, commercial-register extracts, business-activity information, expected turnover, contracts or invoices evidencing taxable activity, and representative authorization where applicable. MTA may request additional evidence of genuine economic activity and may refuse or cancel VAT registration where the statutory conditions are not met.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no general elective transparent-tax treatment for a domestic private limited company. An OÜ is a separate legal person. Estonian corporate income tax is generally imposed when profits are distributed or treated as distributions, rather than when profits are earned or retained. Distributions made by an OÜ are generally subject to corporate income tax at the applicable statutory rate, currently calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution from 1 January 2025, subject to specific statutory rules. Treaty relief, EU parent-subsidiary relief, participation-related rules, and anti-avoidance provisions may affect withholding or recipient-level taxation; they are not entity-classification elections. Estonia has no general hybrid-mismatch election that converts an OÜ into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover arising in Estonia during a calendar year. The threshold concerns Estonian taxable supply and does not automatically cover every cross-border transaction. A non-resident may require VAT registration without relying on the threshold where it makes taxable supplies in Estonia, subject to place-of-supply rules, reverse-charge provisions, and applicable special regimes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Estonia or another EU Member State to a destination outside the EU may qualify for 0% VAT where the goods leave the EU customs territory and the supplier can substantiate the export with customs and commercial evidence. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for 0% VAT where the customer is VAT-identified in another Member State and the transport and reporting conditions are satisfied. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer or vessel is offshore. The place-of-supply rules must be applied by service category, customer status, location of performance, and use or enjoyment provisions. Certain services supplied to non-EU business customers may be outside Estonian VAT under the general B2B rule; qualifying services directly connected with export or import transactions may also receive specific treatment. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, business-purpose evidence, and compliance with VAT return and record-retention requirements.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and evidence of customs acceptance and exit from the EU customs territory, including the relevant export or exit confirmation
  • Commercial invoice, contract, purchase order, or service agreement identifying the parties, goods or services, consideration, and VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the transaction, such as bill of lading, sea waybill, charter-party documentation, cargo manifest, or carrier confirmation
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside the EU where relevant
  • Accounting records, VAT invoices, customer VAT identification evidence for intra-EU supplies, and correspondence substantiating the transaction
  • Bank payment evidence where available; an inward-remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory Estonian export-VAT document and Estonia has no general requirement to obtain such a certificate for export zero-rating

Estonia does not generally require universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or tax-authority pre-approval of every B2B invoice. VAT invoices must satisfy the Estonian Value Added Tax Act and may be issued electronically if authenticity, integrity, and legibility are maintained. Structured e-invoicing is required for specified public-sector transactions and may be required contractually or where the recipient is legally entitled to demand it. Invoices are generally reported through VAT returns rather than submitted individually for real-time clearance. There is no general VAT digital stamp requirement for ordinary domestic invoices. Customs declarations for imports and exports are filed electronically through the Estonian customs systems and may require electronic supporting records.

Estonia has no general foreign-exchange control or statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline. MTA may deny or retrospectively reverse 0% VAT where export, transport, customer-status, place-of-supply, or other statutory conditions are not proven. The resulting VAT, late-payment interest, and applicable penalties may be assessed through tax proceedings, with the normal statutory assessment and limitation rules applying. Incorrect VAT returns may generate administrative sanctions, and intentional tax fraud, false records, or fraudulent VAT refund claims may create misdemeanour or criminal liability under Estonian law. A refund or credit obtained without entitlement is recoverable, with interest and penalties; deliberate or materially negligent conduct may also trigger director, representative, or criminal liability depending on the facts.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia, as an EU member state and Eurozone participant, maintains full capital account convertibility with no restrictions on profit remittances or capital movements. Current account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, subject only to standard AML/KYC verification and tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds required under Estonian law. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted at the company's discretion. However, all transactions must comply with AML regulations, and large or unusual transactions may trigger Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited feasibility for EMI cross-border services due to regulatory constraints under EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (FSA) oversight. Offshore digital EMIs face restrictions on cross-border transfers and require full licensing; not viable for high-risk maritime salvage operations without enhanced compliance infrastructure.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the company's net distribution base, economically charged as 22/78 of the amount distributed from 1 January 2025; undistributed and reinvested profits are generally not subject to Estonian corporate income tax

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on ordinary dividends paid by an Estonian resident OÜ from 1 January 2025. The OÜ generally pays corporate income tax of 22/78 on the distributed amount. Transitional or legacy distributions taxed under former reduced-rate regimes require separate review.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD is due by the 10th day of the calendar month following the month in which the relevant payment or taxable event occurred. The annual report is generally due within six months after the end of the financial year and no later than nine months after year-end. A private limited company must retain reliable accounting records and file the annual report even if no corporate income tax is payable.
