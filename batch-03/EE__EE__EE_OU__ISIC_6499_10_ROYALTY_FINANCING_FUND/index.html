Competent corporate registry

Estonian Business Register, administered by the Registration Department of Tartu County Court and accessible through the e-Business Register portal.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), including sections 135-203 governing private limited companies, sections 138-140 governing formation and articles, and sections 145-146 governing management board and representation. The Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, the Creditors and Credit Intermediaries Act, and the Securities Market Act may apply depending on the financing products, customer profile, and activities actually conducted.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for entry of a private limited company in the commercial register, submitted through the Estonian e-Business Register, together with the articles of association and prescribed formation documents.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An OÜ may be established with share capital of at least EUR 0.01, subject to the statutory nominal-value and contribution rules. The articles may permit deferred payment of contributions where legally available; unpaid contributions remain enforceable against the shareholder, and statutory restrictions apply until contributions are fully paid. Contributions must be made in money or permitted non-monetary assets, with valuation and documentation required for in-kind contributions. The incorporation filing fee is separate from share capital.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial civil-defense clearance is required for ordinary office operations. Structural-load inspection is required only where the premises, equipment, storage, or alterations trigger applicable building or safety rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); primary digital portal: e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee/en/e-mta). Company incorporation and registry identifiers are administered through the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK) e-Business Register.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ normally receives an Estonian registry code upon registration in the Commercial Register. The registry code is generally used as the corporate tax identification reference. The company must activate or obtain access to e-MTA through an authorized management-board member or representative. VAT registration is submitted electronically to MTA, identifying the company, business activities, taxable supplies, management, bank account, accounting records, and evidence of actual economic activity. MTA may request supplementary evidence and may refuse or delay VAT registration where the business purpose or taxable activity is not substantiated. A foreign entity without an Estonian registered office or permanent establishment applies to MTA for the relevant non-resident tax registration and, where required, an Estonian VAT number. A non-EU taxable person generally must appoint an Estonian tax representative for VAT purposes unless an applicable statutory exception or international arrangement applies. The Estonian VAT number format is EE followed by the relevant registration number. Registration for employment taxes and social taxes is separately required when the entity has Estonian employees or taxable remuneration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no general elective pass-through classification for Estonian corporate income tax. An Estonian-incorporated OÜ is generally an Estonian tax resident and is subject to corporate income tax principally when profits are distributed or treated as distributed, including dividends, profit distributions, certain non-business expenses, fringe benefits, gifts, donations, and similar deemed distributions. From 1 January 2025, the general corporate income tax on distributions is calculated at 22/78 of the net distribution. Undistributed and reinvested profits are generally not taxed at the corporate level. E-residency does not itself confer individual tax residence or alter corporate tax residence. Treaty relief, EU directives, participation-exemption rules where applicable, and transfer-pricing adjustments operate as statutory rules rather than entity-classification elections. Hybrid mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive apply where their conditions are met; they do not create a voluntary classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply arising in Estonia in a calendar year. The threshold is assessed under the statutory place-of-supply rules and does not eliminate registration obligations for specific transactions or voluntary registration. The 24% standard rate applies from 1 July 2025; reduced rates and exemptions apply to specified supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier, purchaser, or an authorized representative transports or dispatches the goods out of the EU and the supplier can substantiate the export and the relevant customer and transaction details. Evidence normally includes the customs export declaration, exit confirmation, transport documents, commercial invoice, and accounting records linking the shipment to the supply. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% VAT only where the statutory EU conditions are satisfied, including transport to another EU Member State and a valid customer VAT identification number, with required reporting. B2B services supplied to a customer established outside Estonia are generally governed by place-of-supply rules and may be outside Estonian VAT rather than technically export zero-rated; evidence of the customer’s status and establishment is required. Financial services, including certain granting or negotiation of credit and related transactions, may be VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. Revenue-based financing, royalty financing, servicing, arrangement fees, and ancillary services must be legally characterized separately because exemption, taxable treatment, and place of supply may differ. Input VAT refunds require valid VAT invoices, a direct business connection, and compliance with deduction restrictions.

  • Electronic customs export declaration accepted by the competent customs authority
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent evidence that the goods left the European Union
  • Commercial invoice and contractual order or agreement
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier evidence
  • Evidence identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, quantity, destination, and transaction reference
  • Accounting records reconciling the export supply with the VAT return
  • Proof of payment where commercially available; an inward remittance certificate is not generally an Estonian statutory prerequisite for export VAT zero-rating
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification validation and transport evidence to the other EU Member State

Estonia does not generally impose universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or continuous transaction controls for ordinary domestic B2B transactions. VAT invoices must satisfy statutory content and record-retention requirements. VAT returns and related reporting are filed electronically through e-MTA, generally by the 20th day of the following month. Structured e-invoicing is used in public procurement and may be contractually or commercially required, but ordinary private-sector invoices are not generally subject to mandatory pre-clearance or digital stamping. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the Estonian customs systems and EU customs infrastructure. The company must retain reliable source records, transaction evidence, VAT calculations, and customs documentation for the statutory retention period.

Estonia does not generally impose a universal statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT treatment is tested primarily through customs exit evidence, transport records, contractual documents, customer data, accounting entries, and consistency with VAT returns. If 0% VAT is unsupported or incorrectly claimed, MTA may reassess VAT at the applicable rate, deny related input-VAT deductions, charge tax interest, and require correction of VAT returns. Administrative penalties may apply for failures to register, invoice, report, retain records, or provide information. Deliberate tax evasion, fabricated documents, or fraudulent refund claims may create misdemeanor or criminal exposure for the company and responsible individuals, in addition to recovery of the tax and interest. Refunds improperly paid may be clawed back under the applicable tax and administrative procedures. There is no general Estonian rule making receipt of export proceeds within 180 days a condition of VAT zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts; Estonia adopted the euro (EUR) on 1 January 2011 and maintains no exchange controls, prior-approval regimes, quotas, or repatriation licenses. Profit remittance is unrestricted: retained profits of an OÜ are untaxed, while distributions are taxed at source at the rates in force (20% regular distribution / 25% non-regular distribution), with possible exemption or reduction under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and Estonia's tax treaty network. No obstacle exists to inbound or outbound cross-border transfers for a royalty-financing fund.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, or statutory repatriation timeline on export proceeds or trade receipts; funds may be held in EUR or any freely convertible currency. Only statistical obligations apply: resident legal persons are subject to Eesti Pank Balance of Payments reporting, which is normally discharged by the servicing credit institution under Eesti Pank's statistical reporting regulation, with transaction-level detail for cross-border payments above the applicable thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the central bank of the Republic of Estonia and a member of the Eurosystem. Foreign exchange administration is governed by EU primary law (TFEU Article 63 on free movement of capital) and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act (Välisvaluutaseadus), which contains only residual rules on currency usage; no exchange-control licensing authority exists. The Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) licenses and supervises credit institutions and e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. EU-authorized e-money institutions (e-money license holders such as Monese, Wise, and Payoneer, passportable into Estonia under PSD2/EMD2) can provide SEPA IBANs, card acquiring, and euro disbursement rails for collecting digital-brand revenue share; however, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot lawfully hold fiduciary escrow balances, grant overdraft or liquidity facilities, or execute SWIFT/Vostro correspondent settlement. Most EMIs also internally decline ISIC 6499 revenue-based financing as lending-adjacent and impose turnover caps. Recommended architecture: EMI accounts used as collection sub-accounts and payout agents only, with all escrow, FX, and settlement liquidity maintained at a Tier-1 Estonian credit institution.

Recommended architecture: EMI accounts used as collection sub-accounts and payout agents only, with all escrow, FX, and settlement liquidity maintained at a Tier-1 Estonian credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, calculated as 22/78 of the net amount distributed, effective for distributions made from 1 January 2025. Undistributed profits are not subject to ordinary corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian company to a non-resident recipient, including a non-resident corporate shareholder, subject to anti-abuse, beneficial-ownership, and recharacterization rules. The Estonian OÜ generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the net distribution. Domestic rules applying before 2025 to certain dividends paid to resident individuals, including the former 7% withholding mechanism associated with the 14/86 regime, should not be applied to ordinary 2025 distributions without transitional-law verification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory detailed Local File and, where applicable, Master File obligations generally apply to qualifying large taxpayers meeting at least one statutory size criterion, including annual sales revenue of at least EUR 50,000,000, balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43,000,000, or an average of at least 250 employees, together with covered controlled transactions. Documentation may also be required for certain transactions involving non-residents, permanent establishments, preferential-tax jurisdictions, or other categories prescribed by Estonian law. A separate Country-by-Country Reporting obligation generally applies to an Estonian ultimate parent or surrogate parent of an MNE group meeting the EUR 750,000,000 consolidated-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a standalone broad Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the regimes of certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under the Estonian general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial-ownership principles, EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive measures, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, treaty principal-purpose tests, transfer-pricing rules, and anti-abuse provisions. A royalty-financing fund should maintain genuine decision-making, risk control, qualified personnel or outsourced control functions, financing capacity, books and records, commercial rationale, and demonstrable control over credit, currency, liquidity, and intellectual-property risks. An e-Residency card, registered office, nominee director, or payment account alone does not establish economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent reporting

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid tax, statutory interest, administrative fines, confiscation of proceeds, and criminal sanctions for intentional tax evasion. Depending on the amount and aggravating circumstances, a natural person may face a pecuniary punishment or imprisonment, and a legal person may face a substantial pecuniary punishment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for damages caused by breach of statutory duties, deliberate tax misconduct, unlawful distributions, or failure to preserve accounting records. Criminal conviction may result in imprisonment, confiscation, and ancillary restrictions. A corporate veil is not ordinarily pierced merely because tax is unpaid; personal exposure requires a statutory or fault-based basis.
Unlawful profit distribution and shareholder extraction

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax at 22/78 on the taxable distribution, interest, recovery of the unlawful payment, potential restoration of equity, and civil claims against recipients and responsible management. Insolvency-related transactions may be challenged and clawed back.

Director personal liability: Directors may face joint or several civil liability for unlawful distributions and damage to the company, creditors, or shareholders. Liability can extend to repayment, restitution, and insolvency proceedings; imprisonment is possible only where separate criminal conduct is established.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and related-party financing abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment, denial or reduction of deductions, recharacterization of debt as equity or a distribution, additional corporate income tax or withholding tax where applicable, interest, documentation penalties, and possible double-tax exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for negligent or intentional approval of non-arm's-length transactions and may face criminal exposure if the conduct forms part of intentional tax fraud, falsification, money laundering, or creditor prejudice.
Money laundering and AML non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, supervisory measures, business restrictions, confiscation, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or terrorist-financing conduct. Criminal sanctions can include substantial fines and imprisonment, with aggravated cases carrying significantly higher imprisonment exposure.

Director personal liability: Management and compliance officers may be personally liable for intentional or grossly negligent AML failures, obstruction, concealment, or participation in laundering. Travel restrictions, asset freezing, confiscation, and imprisonment may arise from criminal proceedings; a travel ban is not an automatic consequence of every AML breach.
Unlicensed financial activity or unauthorized fund management

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist orders, administrative fines, invalid or unenforceable arrangements in some circumstances, confiscation, regulatory enforcement, and criminal liability for unauthorized financial activity. Licensing requirements depend on the actual economic activity, investor structure, solicitation, risk profile, and applicable exemptions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be liable for unauthorized operations, investor losses, regulatory costs, and unlawful proceeds. Criminal prosecution may result in fines or imprisonment; travel restrictions may be imposed by a court where legally justified.
Shell-company, nominee, and beneficial-ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Registry sanctions, AML enforcement, account closure, enhanced tax audit, fines for inaccurate beneficial-owner information, denial of treaty benefits, tax reassessment, and possible criminal liability for fraud or money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for false registry filings, concealment, and resulting losses. Civil restitution, asset freezing, confiscation, and imprisonment may apply where the conduct constitutes a criminal offense. Travel bans are case-specific and court-imposed rather than automatic.
Accounting, recordkeeping, and annual-report failure

Statutory sanction: Fines, compelled filing, registry enforcement, tax assessments based on indirect evidence, denial of deductions, insolvency consequences, and possible criminal sanctions for falsified accounting or obstruction.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for damage caused by inadequate accounting, failure to file, or misleading financial statements. In insolvency, failure to preserve records or timely address insolvency may create direct civil and, in serious cases, criminal exposure.
Sanctions evasion and prohibited cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, asset freezing, administrative or criminal fines, confiscation, prohibition of business activity, and imprisonment depending on the breach and intent.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face personal criminal liability, asset freezing, confiscation, civil claims, and court-ordered travel restrictions where they authorize, facilitate, or conceal prohibited transactions.
