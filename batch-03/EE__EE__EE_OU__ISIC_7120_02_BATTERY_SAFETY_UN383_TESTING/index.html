Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital: the OÜ may be founded with share capital below EUR 2,500 or with zero capital contribution (Companies Act § 136(1¹)). If capital is below EUR 2,500, the company is subject to net-asset supervision – the management board must call a general meeting if net assets fall below one-half of share capital or below the EUR 2,500 statutory floor. Monetary subscriptions must be fully credited to the company's bank account before filing; no escrow or notary-trustee account is statutorily required. State fee for electronic registration is EUR 265 (approx. USD 289).

Funding & unlock path: Building usage permit (kasutusluba) issued by the municipal building authority after the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) fire-safety design review; structural load certificate for the drop-test rig and EMC chamber floor loadings; occupational working-environment risk assessment (töökeskkonna riskianalüüs) filed with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) under the Occupational Health and Safety Act; for operational EMC/radio test antennas, a radio-frequency spectrum usage authorisation from TTJA under the Radio Equipment Directive (RED).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board), operational portal: e-MTA (e-Maksuamet, https://e-mta.emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity without Estonian commercial registry presence obtains an Estonian VAT identification number by submitting the VAT registration application (form KÄR) through the e-MTA portal or by postal application to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. The application must declare the foreign firm's foreign tax identification number, intended Estonian taxable activities, any Estonian establishment or fixed place of business, and a local representative if no permanent establishment exists. In-country OÜ registration requires entry in the Commercial Register (äriregister) through the e-Business Centre; the registry code or national identification number serves as the base TIN, with a separate VAT number (format EE + 9 digits) issued by the Tax and Customs Board for VAT purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident entity without Estonian commercial registry presence obtains an Estonian VAT identification number by submitting the VAT registration application (form KÄR) through the e-MTA portal or by postal application to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. The application must declare the foreign firm's foreign tax identification number, intended Estonian taxable activities, any Estonian establishment or fixed place of business, and a local representative if no permanent establishment exists. In-country OÜ registration requires entry in the Commercial Register (äriregister) through the e-Business Centre; the registry code or national identification number serves as the base TIN, with a separate VAT number (format EE + 9 digits) issued by the Tax and Customs Board for VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies a distributed-profit corporate income tax system; an OÜ is generally taxable at entity level only upon profit distribution. No form-based check-the-box election exists for disregarded-entity classification under Estonian domestic tax law. Elections available include: (i) the decision to distribute dividends triggering 20% corporate income tax or reduced 14% dividend rate when distributions are made from previously taxed/regular distributions; (ii) VAT grouping election for closely linked Estonian taxable persons under the Value Added Tax Act; and (iii) application by a non-resident for VAT registration on a voluntary basis when below the compulsory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory Estonian VAT registration for an Estonian-established taxable person applies when taxable supplies exceed EUR 40,000 in a calendar year (excluding VAT); a non-established foreign taxable person generally has an immediate VAT registration obligation when making taxable supplies in Estonia that are subject to Estonian VAT, without benefit of the domestic turnover threshold, unless the services are not treated as supplied in Estonia or the reverse-charge mechanism shifts the obligation to the Estonian customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods physically exported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union, provided the exporter obtains and retains evidence of export within the statutory timeframe and the goods leave the EU within 3 months of supply. Export of services, including dangerous-goods testing and electromagnetic compatibility laboratory services provided to a non-resident business customer, is zero-rated or outside the scope of Estonian VAT when the place of supply is determined to be in the customer's country under the general B2B place-of-supply rule (customer establishment outside Estonia), provided adequate documentary evidence of the customer's status and place of establishment is retained. Supplies of services directly related to export goods (e.g. testing services in respect of goods to be exported outside the EU) may also qualify for zero-rating under the ancillary-service rule if conditions are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) – electronic customs declaration with movement reference number and exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or CMR/rail consignment note for land transport) showing exporter, consignee, destination outside EU, and route evidencing physical exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration in a foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and test report/service completion certificate (for export of laboratory testing services)
  • Evidence of transport contract and packing list (for goods), and if freight-forwarded, a non-negotiable FCR or carrier receipt confirming customs exit

Estonia does not currently mandate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance system, fiscal stamping, or government-authorisation platform for standard B2B invoices. VAT reporting is performed periodically through the e-MTA portal by submitting the VAT return (KMD) with attachments (VD, INF); sales and purchase ledger-level information must be transmitted electronically when required by the Tax and Customs Board. Electronic invoicing in the European standard format is accepted for public-sector invoices under Directive 2014/55/EU, but there is no mandatory API-based invoice clearance for domestic commercial invoices. Taxpayers must ensure invoice data integrity for VAT deduction and export proof purposes.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board scrutinises export zero-rating through cross-referencing customs MRN records, bank wire data, and transport carriers. If documentary proof of export is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent, the VAT initially zero-rated is clawed back at 22% together with interest of 0.06% per day from the original due date. Fraudulent or knowingly false export claims trigger tax fraud proceedings under the Taxation Act and Penal Code, with fines, denial of deduction, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for significant VAT fraud. Material repeat violations may additionally result in revocation of VAT registration, seizure of goods, and publication of the taxpayer in compliance-risk lists used for cross-border audit coordination.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general exchange controls, foreign-currency licensing requirement, capital repatriation approval, or mandatory conversion of ordinary commercial receipts. The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, payment-services rules, tax compliance, and bank transaction monitoring. An Estonian OÜ may receive foreign investment, intercompany loans, customer receipts, dividends, royalties, service fees, and trade payments from abroad without routine central-bank approval. Profit distributions are governed primarily by Estonian corporate tax and company-law rules rather than foreign-exchange controls. Since 1 January 2025, distributed profits are generally subject to Estonian corporate income tax calculated at 22/78 of the net distribution, subject to applicable exemptions and transitional rules. Dividend, interest, royalty, transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, and treaty considerations must be reviewed separately before remittance. Banks may request board resolutions, financial statements, tax evidence, contracts, invoices, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation of the distribution's legal basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in euros or foreign-currency accounts and may be received through SEPA, SWIFT, correspondent banking, card-acquiring, or other licensed payment channels. The company must retain commercial and accounting evidence supporting the transaction, correctly record revenue and receivables, comply with VAT and customs requirements, and respond to bank or authority requests concerning sanctions, economic purpose, source of funds, and beneficial ownership. Any sector-specific reporting, customs, statistical, or tax filing obligations remain separate from foreign-exchange convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank. Foreign-exchange transactions are also subject to directly applicable European Union financial-services, payments, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and reporting rules administered by relevant Estonian authorities, including the Financial Intelligence Unit and the Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for supplementary collections and payments; low as the sole treasury bank. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution, including Wise Europe SA where its product and risk policy permit the customer, may provide payment accounts, safeguarded funds, local account details, SEPA transfers, and selected foreign-currency services. An EMI is not a deposit-taking bank and normally does not provide the same deposit protection, lending, cash-management, escrow, documentary-credit, or full trade-finance capabilities as a commercial bank. Offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs may face correspondent restrictions, unsupported currencies, local licensing limitations, sanctions screening, delayed settlements, account closures, and inability to provide Estonian account functionality. Wise must not be represented as an Estonian bank or as a substitute for a regulated operating bank. Maintain a primary Estonian or EEA bank account, reconcile all EMI accounts to the general ledger, document beneficial ownership and source of funds, and avoid using personal accounts for company receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through the company's account at an Estonian or other EEA bank using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro funding or SWIFT for non-SEPA currencies. Use a payment reference identifying the OÜ, contribution type, shareholder or lender, and relevant corporate resolution. Before transfer, prepare the subscription or capital-contribution agreement, shareholder or board resolution, updated ownership information, lender agreement if debt-funded, source-of-funds evidence, and accounting treatment. Estonia generally does not require central-bank pre-approval or a foreign-exchange purpose code for ordinary equity or shareholder-loan funding. The receiving bank may nevertheless require a documented purpose, UBO verification, source-of-wealth evidence, sanctions screening, and confirmation that the funds are not third-party pass-through funds. For a capital contribution, ensure the payment and accounting treatment comply with the Estonian Commercial Code, the company's articles, and registry requirements; an OÜ may be established without a mandatory minimum share capital, but any declared share capital and unpaid contribution must be properly documented and paid. For shareholder loans, maintain arm's-length terms, maturity, interest, transfer-pricing support, withholding-tax analysis, and debt-capacity evidence. Reject funds from unrelated third parties unless legally documented and pre-cleared by the bank.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and sanctions screening. Use SWIFT MT103 or its current ISO 20022 equivalent for non-SEPA or non-euro payments, with complete originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, and purpose information. For larger or higher-risk purchases, use a commercial documentary letter of credit issued or confirmed by a bank under UCP 600, with compliant transport, insurance, invoice, packing, inspection, and certificate documents. Documentary collections may be handled under URC 522 where appropriate. Use bank guarantees or standby letters of credit only after reviewing the applicable rules, collateral, fees, and counterparty risk. Match each payment to an approved purchase order, contract, invoice, delivery evidence, customs or transport record where relevant, and sanctions or export-control screening. Avoid third-party payments, split payments designed to evade controls, unexplained round amounts, and payments to personal accounts. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained and reinvested profits; 20% on distributed profits (computed as 20/80 of the net distribution; equivalent to 20% of gross dividend). The zero rate applies only to undistributed earnings. The 5-year look-back/offset framework applies for calculation of the reduced 14% regular-dividend rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on gross dividend distribution (equivalently 20/80 of the net amount paid). A 14% rate (14/86 of net) applies to regular dividends paid to natural persons within statutory thresholds. DTA relief commonly reduces the rate to 0% for EU corporate holders under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (qualifying shareholding) and 5% for portfolio holdings; mixed-source distribution rules apply.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the 10th calendar day of the month following the taxable event. For example, a dividend resolved on 31 March must be reported by 10 April. When the 10th falls on a non-working day, the deadline moves to the next working day without surcharge.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the 10th day of January following the reporting calendar year, unless the taxpayer receives MTA permission for a different period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonian law does not define a fixed statutory monetary threshold for Local File/Master File. In practice, the MTA will require transfer pricing documentation when cross-border related-party transactions exceed €100,000 in a fiscal year, or when DTA relief is claimed. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750 million under EU Directive 2016/881. A formal TP documentation set is strongly recommended for any intercompany transaction with a foreign ultimate owner.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not impose offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Substance requirements are enforced through the Commercial Code: the OÜ must maintain a registered office, management board, and sufficient human and physical resources to perform its licence activity. An e-Residency virtual address does not substitute for substance. The Estonian General Anti-Abuse Rule and CFC rules in the Income Tax Act deny the 0% retained-profit regime where the entity is artificially constructed to avoid tax, including for battery testing labs with no local technical capacity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Taxation Act and Criminal Code §389(2), intentional tax evasion exceeding €20,000 attracts fines up to €1.5 million for legal persons and imprisonment up to seven years for responsible individuals. The MTA will also assess the omitted tax, add statutory interest at approximately 0.06% per day, and impose penalty tax of up to 30%.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false declarations are personally, jointly, and severally liable for unpaid taxes, subject to civil damages, asset seizure, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as directors for 3 to 10 years.
Shell Company / Artificial Zero-Tax Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The MTA applies the General Anti-Abuse Rule to deem the routed income as a distribution, imposing 20% CIT, social tax on disguised wages, and a penalty of 5% of the assessed sum. The Commercial Register may initiate compulsory dissolution or strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors and e-Residency users face cross-border criminal investigation, travel bans, and extradition for tax fraud. Non-resident directors risk permanent EEA entry bans and personal liability for the OÜ's unpaid debts in bankruptcy proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering / Invoice Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, the maximum fine for legal persons is €3.2 million and for natural persons €1.2 million; the testing license is suspended, and assets are subject to criminal confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the transaction are personally fined, banned from travel, and liable for up to four years' imprisonment. The Estonian digital signature on payment instructions creates an evidentiary presumption of personal direction and control.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The MTA adjusts the taxable distribution, applying 20% CIT on the undercharged amount, statutory interest, and penalty tax up to 30% of the additional assessment. The adjustment also increases the withholding tax on any onward distributed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed non-compliant intercompany agreements are personally named in the assessment and liable for the deficiency if company assets are insufficient. International cooperation allows the enforcement of travel bans and extradition for tax offenses exceeding the criminal threshold.
