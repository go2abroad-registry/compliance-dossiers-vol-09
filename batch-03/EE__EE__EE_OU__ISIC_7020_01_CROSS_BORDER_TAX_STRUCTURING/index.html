Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Osaühing; a zero-capital OÜ is permissible. If share capital is subscribed, the cash contribution must be paid in full to the company's bank account before filing, evidenced by a bank confirmation; no separate escrow account is required by Estonian law.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises must satisfy the fire safety requirements verified by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet), including fire extinguishers and an evacuation plan; structural load approvals are not required for ordinary office occupancy unless alterations are made to the building. Occupational health and safety clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act requires a written risk assessment, an appointed occupational health and safety representative, and compliance with workplace ergonomic and safety standards.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Osaühing; a zero-capital OÜ is permissible. If share capital is subscribed, the cash contribution must be paid in full to the company's bank account before filing, evidenced by a bank confirmation; no separate escrow account is required by Estonian law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-MTA portal: https://e-mta.emta.ee; e-Business Register (e-äriregister): https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders register an Osaühing (OÜ) via the e-Business Register using DigiDoc or an e-notary; no minimum share capital requirement (EUR 0.01 minimum in paper filings; zero minimum in electronic registration). MTA automatically issues the 8-digit registry code (äriregistri kood), which serves as the company's base TIN and is recorded on the commercial register certificate. A separate VAT identification number (EE + 9 digits) is obtained by filing the VAT registration form in e-MTA; registration is mandatory with effect from the first day of the calendar month following the month in which taxable turnover exceeded EUR 40,000 in the preceding 12 months. Non-resident entities without an Estonian seat may register for VAT directly via e-MTA (no local PE required) and must appoint a tax representative unless the EU OSS mechanism covers their distance sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies a deferred entity-level corporate income tax: undistributed profits are taxed at 0% and taxation arises only upon distribution. Statutory elections include: (i) the 14% reduced rate for regular distributions (14/86 gross-up) available to the extent distributions do not exceed the average of the three preceding years' taxable distributions; (ii) the 20% general rate on non-regular distributions (20/80 gross-up, i.e., 25% of gross); (iii) tax-neutral group contribution election (grupisisene sissemakse) between resident group companies; (iv) VAT grouping election under §27 of the Estonian VAT Act; (v) OSS election for EU distance sales; (vi) for non-resident founders, the place-of-effective-management attribution and permanent establishment rules under the Estonian Income Tax Act; hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by ATAD-mandated primary adjustment rules. No US-style check-the-box mechanism exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 (annual taxable supply threshold); non-resident EU sellers are subject to a EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold unless the OSS election is applied; intra-Community acquisitions by a non-registered person trigger registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to: (i) exports of goods transported outside the EU VAT area, provided title passes to a non-EU customer and the goods physically leave Estonia within 90 days of supply; (ii) intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered buyer with a valid EU VAT ID and VIES confirmation; (iii) services, including ISIC 7020 tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory, where the place of supply is outside the EEA under the B2B general rule (customer's established place of business) and the customer has no fixed establishment in Estonia benefiting from the service; (iv) ancillary transport services directly linked to export. For consultancy services to non-EU clients, the supplier must document the B2B character, the customer's non-resident seat, and the absence of Estonian fixed-establishment consumption to sustain the 0% place-of-supply treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX1 through the AES system validated by MTA customs)
  • Bill of Lading (maritime), Air Waybill, or CMR (road) endorsed by the third-party carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 / payment-routing evidence from the non-resident customer)
  • Commercial Invoice and executed Services Agreement (ISIC 7020 scope of work and place-of-supply determination)
  • EU VIES validation receipt (for intra-Community supplies) or OSS records (for distance sales)

Estonia employs a semi-mandatory e-invoicing model: B2G invoices must be issued as structured e-invoices via PEPPOL-compliant operators or the e-MTA portal; B2B e-invoicing is voluntary but VAT returns (KMD) and Intrastat declarations must be filed electronically via e-MTA APIs (X-tee infrastructure). There is no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping (unlike Italy or Spain); however, under the Tax Information Act §128, MTA may require machine-readable sales and purchase ledgers in real time for audits. The 'Smart VAT' system cross-validates invoice-level data with e-invoice repositories and customs declarations, enabling risk-based automatic withholding of export refunds.

MTA may claw back the zero-rating benefit where transport documents are materially inconsistent with the customs declaration, the 90-day export evidence window is missed without justification, or inward remittance is delayed beyond 180 days without commercial rationale; the clawback reinstates VAT at 22% plus statutory default interest of 0.06% per day (approximately 21.9% annualized). A tax supplement of up to 30% is imposed for concealment (MKS §156) and up to 60% for deliberate VAT fraud; criminal liability under Penal Code §414/§415 carries a fine of up to EUR 32,000 or imprisonment of up to 3 years. Directors and controlling shareholders may be jointly and severally liable in administrative 'fair tax' claims; for ISIC 7020 advisory fees, transfer pricing documentation and substance files are required to defeat a business-purpose or sham-transaction challenge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU law. The euro is the sole legal tender; no exchange-control licences, approvals, or registration requirements apply to cross-border payments or profit distributions. The Estonian corporate income tax system is a unique 0% undistributed-profits regime: an Osaühing pays income tax only upon dividend or deemed distributions, currently at 20% of the gross distribution (20/80 of the net distributed amount). A reduced 14% rate applies to qualifying recurring dividends paid from retained profits of prior tax years, subject to statutory caps and recipient conditions. Under the 2025-2026 fiscal reform enacted by the Riigikogu, the base distribution rate increases to 22% of gross distributions in 2025 and to 25% in 2026. Foreign shareholders may obtain treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief. Profit remittance therefore encounters no convertibility or approval hurdle; the only conditions are proper distribution mechanics, general meeting resolution, funds-sufficiency accounting, and CRS/WHT filing obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender, conversion, repatriation, or retention-period obligation applies to export receipts. Estonian regulation imposes no statutory timeline requiring banks to convert foreign currency into euro and no mandatory ratio for involuntary FX conversion. All receipts may be freely retained in any convertible currency in an Estonian or EEA-licensed account. The only compliance requirements are the standard payment-fraud screening, sanctions-list screening, and AML monitoring performed by the collecting bank.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the central bank of the Republic of Estonia and a member of the Eurosystem. Financial supervision of credit institutions is performed by the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon). Estonia does not operate a standalone foreign-exchange-control authority; exchange liberalisation follows from EU Treaty provisions (TFEU Art. 63) and the euro-area legal framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High as an operational overlay rail, Medium as a sole treasury bank. EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut, Monese, Bunq) can on-ramp an Estonian Osaühing under the EU passporting regime (PSD2) and are valuable for multi-currency collection and disbursement. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001 for SEPA-batched transfers) to the Osaühing's Estonian current account. The wire instruction must reference the Estonian registry code (reg.nr) and a precise purpose narrative, e.g., 'CAPITAL INCREASE PER SHAREHOLDER RESOLUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN AGREEMENT DATED [X]'. Equity contributions are notified to the e-Business Register on form V2 within one month of the shareholder resolution; no capital duty or stamp tax is levied in Estonia. Shareholder loans must be documented by a binding loan deed with arm's-length interest rate and maturity benchmarked under local transfer-pricing rules; interest paid to foreign shareholders is generally exempt from Estonian withholding tax under domestic law. All injected funds must originate from a regulated credit institution or verifiable commercial counterparty, with the source trail traceable to the UBO, to avoid triggering AML escalations.For consultancy advisory settlements, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for single cross-border payments and ISO 20022 pain.001 for batch files; interbank concentration uses MT202. For EUR-denominated transfers within the EEA, SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) provide near-instant settlement for operational payments. For engagements combining tax-data licensing or software deliverables with a sale-of-goods character, a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) confirmed by LHV Pank or another tier-1 Estonian bank provides payment assurance and mitigates counterparty credit risk.
