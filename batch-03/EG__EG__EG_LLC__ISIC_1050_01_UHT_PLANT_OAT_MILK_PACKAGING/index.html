Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An Egyptian limited liability company is generally treated as a separate taxable legal person and is not subject to a general United States-style check-the-box election. There is no broad statutory election allowing an ordinary Egyptian LLC to be treated as fiscally transparent for Egyptian corporate income tax. Special tax treatments may arise from qualifying free-zone, economic-zone, investment, or sectoral incentive regimes, but they require statutory eligibility and, where applicable, licensing or authority approval. Egyptian law does not provide a general hybrid-mismatch classification election for ordinary LLCs. Treaty-residence and beneficial-ownership positions are determined under applicable law and treaty provisions rather than by an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover is the principal VAT registration threshold for ordinary taxable persons. Registration may also be mandatory irrespective of turnover for specified activities, importers, exporters, and other categories prescribed by the VAT Law or its executive regulations. Voluntary registration may be available subject to ETA requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Egypt are generally subject to VAT at zero percent when the goods are exported outside Egyptian territory and the export is substantiated by customs records and the required commercial and payment evidence. The exporter should demonstrate that the goods physically left Egypt, that the exporter is properly registered where registration is required, that the export declaration corresponds to the invoice and shipment, and that the consideration and foreign-exchange evidence are consistent with the transaction. Exported services may qualify for zero rating where the statutory conditions are satisfied, generally requiring a nonresident recipient, use or enjoyment outside Egypt, payment in convertible foreign currency or other accepted evidence, and exclusion of services effectively used in Egypt. Domestic supplies to Egyptian customers, supplies consumed in Egypt, and transactions lacking adequate export evidence remain taxable at the applicable rate. Input VAT refunds or credits for export activities require valid purchase and import invoices, proof of payment where required, customs evidence, reconciliation to VAT returns, and compliance with ETA audit and refund procedures.

  • Customs export declaration or electronic customs release record showing the exporter, consignee, goods description, quantity, value, HS classification, and exit confirmation
  • Commercial export invoice corresponding to the customs declaration and VAT records
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, airway bill, or equivalent carrier evidence
  • Certificate of origin where required by the destination country, trade arrangement, or customs authority
  • Evidence of customs exit or loading and, where applicable, port, shipping, or export-system confirmation
  • Foreign-exchange and payment evidence, such as bank credit advice, inward remittance documentation, documentary collection records, or letter-of-credit settlement evidence, where required to substantiate the export consideration
  • Export contract, purchase order, or letter of credit identifying the foreign customer and delivery terms
  • VAT registration certificate, tax invoice or export invoice records, VAT return disclosure, and input-tax schedules supporting any refund claim
  • Proof of any required export, food-safety, sanitary, product, or manufacturing authorization applicable to packaged oat-milk beverages

Egypt has implemented mandatory electronic invoicing for in-scope business-to-business transactions and other specified taxpayers through the ETA electronic invoicing platform. Taxpayers must obtain ETA approval, register invoice profiles, use an approved integration method or portal, apply a valid electronic signature, issue structured electronic invoices containing prescribed supplier, customer, tax, item, value, and reference data, and transmit documents to the ETA in the required format. ETA validation, unique document identifiers, cryptographic signatures, and status controls operate as electronic authentication and clearance mechanisms. Electronic credit and debit notes must be issued for qualifying adjustments. Electronic receipts apply to specified business-to-consumer transactions through the electronic receipt system. The mandate is phased and depends on taxpayer category, transaction type, and ETA implementation notices; a plant should confirm its current onboarding deadline and whether its sales channels require both invoicing and receipt integration. Paper invoices generally do not support VAT recovery or compliant business invoicing where electronic issuance is mandatory.

The ETA may deny zero-rating or an input-VAT refund where export departure, customer status, payment, foreign-exchange, customs, invoice, or accounting evidence is incomplete or inconsistent. The resulting assessment may include output VAT at the applicable domestic rate, repayment of an improperly refunded or credited amount, statutory additional tax or late-payment charges, and interest or other amounts prescribed by law. False declarations, fictitious exports, forged customs or banking records, concealment of taxable turnover, or intentional evasion may trigger administrative fines and criminal sanctions under the VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 and the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 206 of 2020, including liability of responsible officers where statutory conditions are met. The ETA may also suspend or cancel electronic invoicing access, reject invoices, refer the case for prosecution, and conduct retrospective reassessment for the applicable statutory limitation period. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation period should be monitored against current Central Bank of Egypt, banking, customs, and export-control instructions because applicable deadlines and permitted extensions can vary by transaction, exporter, payment method, and regulatory notice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a flexible (de facto floating) exchange-rate regime since the CBE's 6 March 2024 market normalisation and IMF EFF-supported reform; the official EGP rate is unified with the interbank market. Current-account transactions (merchandise trade and services) are freely convertible but subject to documentary verification by authorized-dealer banks, including import-form declarations and export-proceeds repatriation tracking. Capital-account items - FDI equity, shareholder loans, dividends, and profit repatriation - are permitted for LLCs with non-resident ownership, subject to bank-level registration, CBE balance-of-payments statistical reporting, registration of the capital in the company file, and Egyptian tax-clearance documentation (audited financials, tax card, proof of tax filing and payment). Historical FX-availability queues for profit remittances have been substantially cleared since the 6 March 2024 float; residual procedural hurdles are documentary rather than regulatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE foreign-exchange framework in force after the March 2024 float, Egyptian exporters are required to repatriate 100% of the proceeds of exported goods (including UHT oat-milk beverages) to a foreign-currency account at an authorized Egyptian bank within 90 days from the date of the bill of lading or air waybill. There is no statutory mandatory conversion/surrender ratio; proceeds may be retained in USD/EUR or converted at the prevailing interbank rate. Netting, offsetting against import payments, or retention of proceeds abroad is prohibited; all proceeds must be received by wire through the banking channel and matched to the underlying export customs declaration and shipping documents.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - البنك المركزي المصري, sole monetary and foreign-exchange regulatory authority under Law No. 194/2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the LLC's Egyptian bank account (EGP Capital Escrow pre-incorporation or operational EGP/FCY account post-incorporation), referencing 'Equity Contribution - Share Capital - EG LLC'. Mandatory pre-checks include the GAFI incorporation certificate, the completed bank KYC file, the shareholder's board resolution approving the investment, and the LLC's Articles of Association; the receiving bank registers the inflow for CBE balance-of-payments statistics and issues an inward-transfer advice used by auditors. For intercompany loans or convertible funding, the loan must be documented and registered through the Egyptian bank (CBE loan registration) and remittance marked with the applicable purpose code for related-party financial loans; interest remittances attract 10% Egyptian withholding tax unless reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty. Recommended routing uses a correspondent bank with deep USD/EGP liquidity (e.g., CIB's USD correspondent via Citibank New York or HSBC's European corridor) with charges instructed 'SHA' to optimize clearing costs.Trade settlements for UHT packaging materials, aseptic cartons, films, fitments, and plant consumables must be executed via irrevocable documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700, sight or usance) opened through a CBE-authorized bank, supported by the proforma invoice, signed LC application, and CBE import-documentation registration; the international leg clears via SWIFT (MT103/MT700 message exchange) and the local EGP leg via the Egyptian National Clearing House (EGNCH) and RIIST real-time gross settlement system. For open-account, prepayment, and small-ticket supplier disbursements, SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers are permitted after the bank completes the importer's Declaration/Import-Filing form, purchases FX on the interbank market against the proforma invoice, and verifies Egypt's customs ACID registration for the shipment. The same clearing rails route freight-forwarder, insurance, duty, and excise payments; all outbound FX payments require the underlying trade dossier to remain on file for CBE examination.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP) - permitted by the CBE for genuine trade- and investment-related hedging up to the underlying exposure tenor (typically up to 12 months); requires supporting documentation including LCs, proforma invoices, or capital-transaction evidence; speculative dealing is prohibited.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDF) on EGP - available via licensed London/New York counterparties as a supplementary hedge where onshore bank forward tenors do not match the exposure; the NDF settles in USD outside Egypt and must be treated as a foreign instrument in the LLC's management and tax accounts.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP or EUR/EGP) - permissible to hedge long-term foreign-currency capital injections or equipment-financing exposures, executed under an ISDA master agreement with a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank and subject to the underlying exposure being verified against the CBE's derivatives and FX-risk-management guidelines.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low primary-account viability. CBE regulations (Law No. 194/2020 and FX-control instructions) require all corporate foreign-exchange transactions and statutory share-capital escrow deposits to be effected through CBE-authorized Egyptian dealing banks; offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not CBE-authorized dealers and cannot lawfully serve as the LLC's primary revenue or payments facility. EMIs may be used only as ancillary collection rails for low-value overseas receipts, provided funds are periodically swept via SWIFT to the Egyptian bank account; sustained use as the main treasury node creates legal and compliance exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File when annual related-party controlled transactions exceed EGP 10 million; mandatory Master File when the multinational group has consolidated revenue exceeding EGP 2 billion; Country-by-Country Report required when group consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies a substance-over-form and beneficial ownership doctrine rather than a UK/UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime. For the EG_LLC to retain treaty benefits, investment incentives, and the regular 22.5% CIT rate, the entity must demonstrate real factory assets, production staff, packaging line management, bank account activity in Egypt, and actual strategic decision-making in Egypt. A mere invoice-issuing shell will be re-characterized by ETA, stripped of DTAs, and treated as a taxable permanent establishment of its foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Payment of the evaded tax plus monthly delay surcharges of 2% per month, non-deductible fines, criminal prosecution before the Egyptian Economic Courts, seizure of bank accounts and factory assets, and imprisonment of responsible officers for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who sign false declarations or order accounting concealment may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; courts may impose civil restitution, travel bans, asset freezes, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company and Artificial Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA may disregard the corporate identity, re-attribute all oat milk profits to the foreign parent through a deemed permanent establishment, apply the 22.5% CIT plus branch-level WHT, deny treaty relief, revoke commercial registration, and impose penalties for false license declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate a nominee or facade structure may face derivative tax collection, personal exposure for unpaid corporate tax, travel restrictions, disqualification as managers, and civil liability to minority shareholders and creditors.
Money Laundering and Trade-Based Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Fines under the AML Law, freezing and forfeiture of involved bank accounts, reporting to the Egyptian Money Laundering Combating Unit (EMLCU), criminal prosecution, and exclusion from Egyptian trade and banking facilities.

Director personal liability: Senior managers who direct or knowingly facilitate trade-based money laundering are personally liable; sanctions include imprisonment, large criminal fines, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and prohibition from serving as directors.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Deficient TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, additional CIT at 22.5%, monthly delay surcharges, penalties equal to a percentage of the adjusted amount, and exclusion of the adjustment from deductible expenses; repeated violations escalate to criminal tax fraud referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP certifications or instruct artificial pricing may be personally assessed for the additional tax through veil-piercing, subject to civil restitution and travel bans, and may face criminal sanctions for making false declarations to ETA.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation and Withholding Tax Defaults

Statutory sanction: ETA reclassifies the payment and assesses the proper withholding tax, imposes monthly delay surcharges and penalties on the LLC as withholding agent, blocks future remittances through banking channels, and issues collection orders for unpaid WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or execute the under-withheld remittance are personally liable for the unpaid WHT debt and penalties; remedies include civil asset recovery, bank attachment, travel bans, and personal criminal liability where conduct was fraudulent.
