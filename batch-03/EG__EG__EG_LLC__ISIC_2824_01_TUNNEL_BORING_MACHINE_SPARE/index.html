Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a standard LLC engaged in manufacturing; the cash subscription declared in the Articles of Association must be deposited into a bank formation account prior to registration. Legal escrow is not required, but funds are blocked until the Commercial Registry Extract is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense clearance confirming fire alarm and suppression systems, emergency exits, hazardous material storage safety, and structural fire resistance. A licensed structural engineer certificate is required to verify floor loading for heavy machinery. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing platform: https://invoice.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must complete incorporation with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), then file a tax registration application with the Egyptian Tax Authority using the prescribed Form No. 1. Supporting documents include the notarized certificate of incorporation, commercial registry extract, articles of association, board resolution, accepted signature specimens, power of attorney or tax representative appointment, registered office lease, and identity documents of directors and shareholders. After document review and site verification, the ETA issues a permanent Tax Card (بطاقة ضريبية) containing the corporate tax ID number. The LLC must in parallel register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 if its taxable supplies are expected to exceed the threshold, and also register for employer-related payroll, stamp duties, and any applicable withholding tax obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation_With_Worldwide_Income; the Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax under Law No. 91 of 2005 on worldwide income. There is no pass-through treatment; profits are taxed at the corporate entity level. Non-resident shareholders receiving dividends are subject to dividend withholding tax at a rate of 10% unless a lower tax treaty rate applies.

Available election forms

Egyptian tax law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through elections for LLCs. Available mechanisms include selection of a non-calendar fiscal year with prior ETA approval, voluntary VAT registration before the mandatory threshold is crossed, election of VAT filing frequency subject to ETA classification, and use of advance pricing agreement and mutual agreement procedure mechanisms for cross-border related-party transactions. No domestic hybrid mismatch election exists; taxpayers must document compliance with BEPS Action 2 principles in cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of cumulative taxable supplies within any 12 consecutive months; imports are taxable with no registration threshold; non-resident suppliers of cross-border electronic services may become registrable under special attendance rules even below the threshold if supplying final consumers in Egypt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of tunnel boring machine spare parts qualify for 0% VAT if the registered LLC can prove actual and definitive exportation from Egypt under the Egyptian Customs Law. The goods must be physically shipped outside Egyptian territory, title and risk must pass to the non-resident buyer, and export proceeds must be received in Egypt in a freely convertible currency. Indirect export sales through an Egyptian intermediate supplier are also zero-rated when the final export is documented. The zero-rate applies to the export of goods and certain offshore services listed under the VAT executive regulations.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (Form SAD) endorsed by the Egyptian Customs Authority and showing the TBM spare-part commodity code, quantity, value, and outbound customs office of departure.
  • Bill of Lading or signed Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Egypt, the exporting party, and the final foreign destination; combined with shipping container/tracking records where applicable.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a bank licensed by the Central Bank of Egypt confirming receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign currency in the exporter’s Egyptian bank account within the statutory period.

Mandatory e-invoicing applies to all VAT-registered taxpayers, including the subject LLC. Invoices must be prepared in the ETA’s prescribed XML/JSON schema and transmitted in real time through a certified integration. Both seller and buyer must apply for an ETA qualification and obtain a unique multi-purpose digital identification. The ETA’s electronic clearance engine validates the invoice online, rejects non-compliant documents, and returns a unique QR-based authentication code. No legally valid invoice can be issued without real-time clearance. Fiscal data matching with customs declarations and banking records is automated.

The ETA uses the e-invoicing/customs/bank data lake to detect export zero-rating abuse. If any condition is not met within 180 days or the documentation is misstated, the 0% treatment is retrospectively revoked, VAT is assessed at 14% on the gross export value, and a delay penalty of 0.5% per month is added. Fraudulent export refund claims are subject to administrative penalties of up to 100% of the evaded tax, minimum fine provisions, imprisonment under Law No. 67 of 2016 and the Egyptian Penal Code, and disqualification from future VAT refund programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt uses a market-determined flexible exchange-rate regime (restored 2024). Current-account transactions (trade, services, dividend remittances) are broadly convertible via licensed banks subject to standard documents (invoice, Bill of Lading, tax clearance). Capital-account inflows (FDI, shareholder loans, foreign-currency financing) require CBE registration and issuance of an Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) establishing the legal capital basis. Profit-remittance hurdles are practical: audited financials, e-Tax clearance, Form 41 certificate, board minutes, and IRC-linked capital-entry evidence are mandatory; interbank liquidity is the main delay point.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to an Egyptian authorized bank within 180 days from Bill of Lading date and credited to the exporter's local account; offshore retention beyond that window is prohibited. A CBE circularic conversion and surrender ratio applies (prevailing benchmark: 50 to 100 percent of repatriated proceeds); the exporter must offer the foreign currency to the bank and may retain the unconverted balance only in a CBE-approved foreign-currency account under an approved treasury policy. Breach triggers refusal of new export documents, suspension of trade-finance lines, and enhanced CBE administrative scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Foreign Exchange Administration under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Banking and Banking Sector Law) and its Executive Regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for in-country treasury and clearing. Offshore electronic-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not licensed banks or authorized payment-service providers under Egypt's Payment Services Law No. 18 of 2020 and cannot hold EGP or access CBE settlement systems (RTGS, ACH, Meeza). EMIs can serve only as auxiliary overseas-receivable collection and visibility tools. All CBE-regulated trade, capital, and tax flows must settle through licensed Egyptian banks; an EMI-only treasury structure is legally non-viable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% for distributions of profits by Egyptian LLCs to tax-resident shareholders; for non-resident shareholders, Egyptian domestic law historically treats dividends as exempt from withholding when paid from a company whose profits are subject to Egyptian corporate tax. There is no separate statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions made by an Egyptian company to non-resident shareholders under Law No. 91 of 2005, provided the profits have borne the 22.5% CIT. Consequently, no WHT is applied to a dividend distribution from an Egyptian LLC. This is subject to anti-abuse rules where the distribution is disguised as an excessive interest, royalty, or service fee payment, in which case the payment is recharacterized and 20% withholding applies at source on treaty-covered or non-treaty income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return must be filed within 4 months from the end of the fiscal year (i.e. by 31 May for a 31 December year-end). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Egyptian TP Documentation thresholds under Ministerial Decree No. 524 of 2018 and subsequent amendments require a Local File and Master File where the related-party transactions per class exceed EGP 10 million annually (by aggregate category) or EGP 2 million for transactions with persons resident in a zero-tax jurisdiction. The previously proposed thresholds of EGP 5 million were superseded by EGP 10 million per transaction category. The Egyptian LLC in the heavy TBM spare-parts sector must prepare and retain contemporaneous documentation if the aggregate value of cross-border purchases from the foreign parent or affiliates exceeds EGP 10 million per annum, or if loans/royalties to related parties exceed EGP 2 million. Loss-making entities with intercompany revenue below materiality thresholds may still be notified by the ETA; functional, risk, and economic substance analyses must be prepared on a best-practice basis regardless of formal threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has currently issued draft/framework Economic Substance Regulations as of 2023-2024, but has not enacted a comprehensive ESR statute comparable to UAE or Bahrain. The applicable substantive test arises through general anti-avoidance principles in Law No. 91 of 2005, Law No. 82 of 2020 (Tax Evasion Combating Law), and ministerial tax inspections. The ETA can disregard an Egyptian LLC entirely if the company is a shell or lacks physical presence, employees, premises, or real business conduct, and recharacterize revenue as income of the foreign principal. Despite no formal ESR registration system in Egypt, multinational groups must demonstrate the Egyptian LLC has decision-making capacity, procurement and inventory management functions, engineering staff, and operational substance in EG/SCZONE; otherwise transfer pricing adjustments and deemed distributions can be made with double-tax consequences.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 91 of 2005 and Law No. 82 of 2020, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of not less than 6 months and a fine equal to 3 to 10 times the tax evaded; in severe repeated cases the fine reaches up to 10 times the avoided tax plus closure of the establishment. The ETA additionally levies an ex officio assessment with penalties of 2% per month (late payment penalty) and an additional 40% of the difference for misstatement.

Director personal liability: The resident manager and all directors who signed the return or who controlled the business are jointly and severally liable for the entirety of the tax difference and penalties. In criminal cases, the court may impose travel bans, asset freezing measures, and imprisonment for up to 5 years for executives with actual knowledge and intent. Civil restitution orders allow attachment of the personal assets of directors if the LLC is insolvent.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The ETA or SCZONE authority invokes anti-abuse rules and disregards the LLC for tax purposes. The invoice route is treated as a sham, and taxable revenue is reallocated to the foreign principal's Egyptian PE or to the local party, resulting in a 22.5% tax on the gross recharacterized margin plus an additional 40% penalty for deliberate misrepresentation and up to EGP 2 million administrative fine.

Director personal liability: Directors of the foreign parent who knowingly used the Egyptian shell as a fiction can be named in criminal prosecutions under the anti-money laundering framework, with penalties including 3 to 7 years' imprisonment and a fine of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000. The Egyptian nominee director is personally liable for the LLC's unpaid tax and may face a travel ban and passport seizure pending settlement.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The ETA performs a TP adjustment, disallows the excess royalties/fees, applies the general 22.5% tax rate on the upward adjustment, adds late payment interest of 1.5% per month, and imposes a misstatement penalty of 40% of the total additional tax due. If the intercompany loan is recharacterized as equity, the interest paid is treated as a constructive dividend and subject to 0% WHT, but the interest non-deduction is confirmed and the tax base is enlarged.

Director personal liability: The foreign-appointed director and the CFO are jointly liable for the additional tax if they prepared or approved the inaccurate return and documented false invoices. The directors may be subject to a personal fine in criminal prosecution equal to the difference between the tax assessed and the tax paid, plus imprisonment of up to 2 years if the ETA proves deliberate fraud.
AML and Economic Sanctions Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU) may impose a fine of EGP 50,000 to EGP 300,000 per violation on the LLC, and the ETA assesses tax on the non-declared cash inflow at 22.5% with a penalty of 2% per month. In aggravated cases, the account is frozen and the LLC is blacklisted from government procurement and SCZONE licences.

Director personal liability: Board directors and the compliance officer may be prosecuted for AML facilitation; imprisonment ranges from 2 to 7 years for knowing concealment of illicit funds. The court may freeze the personal accounts of directors, order confiscation of an equivalent value, and impose travel bans pending full recovery and settlement of the tax liability.
Customs Under-Invoicing and Import Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Egyptian Customs Law No. 207 of 2020, under-declaration of customs value is punishable by a fine of 20% to 40% of the customs duties evaded and forfeiture/conversion of the goods; the ETA recalculates taxable income using the arm's length price and unjustified depreciation adjustments, leading to an additional assessment of the tax difference plus 40% penalty.

Director personal liability: The company's import director and the legal representative are jointly and severally liable for customs debts. Customs authorities can issue an executive seizure order against personal assets, and administrative imprisonment (up to 90 days) may be imposed until the duties are paid. If the under-invoicing is linked to FX-related money laundering, imprisonment can extend to 5 years.
