Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; principal portals: ETA e-services (https://www.eta.gov.eg), unified tax services portal (https://taxservices.gov.eg), and mandatory e-invoicing system (https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) incorporate at the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) to obtain the Commercial Register extract and investment file; (2) submit the unified tax registration application under Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended by the Unified Tax Law, to the competent ETA tax office using the company incorporation deed, commercial registry extract, legal representative national ID, board resolution, and notarized power of attorney of a resident tax agent; (3) receive the Tax Card containing the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN), which serves simultaneously for Corporate Income Tax, VAT, and withholding tax; (4) complete the same registration file for VAT when projected annual turnover equals or exceeds EGP 500,000, or voluntarily for exporters expecting excess input VAT refunds; (5) open an Egyptian bank account and provide a bank account certificate for refund routing; and (6) activate the ETA-issued digital certificate and onboard the LLC on the e-invoicing and e-receipt platforms.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income at the standard CIT rate of 22.5% (25% for banking activities), with no pass-through or US-style check-the-box regime available; profits remitted to non-resident shareholders remain subject to dividend withholding tax, and the LLC itself is not eligible for tax-transparent classification unless operating through a GAFI-licensed free-zone project.

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (a) consolidated tax group filing for resident companies under common control with ETA pre-approval; (b) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 mandatory threshold; (c) bilateral tax treaty application through an ETA residency certificate and permanent establishment mapping, with limitation-on-benefits or principal-purpose-protection screening; (d) GAFI free-zone project election replacing standard CIT/VAT; and (e) BEPS MLI-informed positions governing hybrid mismatch outcomes for cross-border payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 per annum of taxable turnover from supplies of goods and services in Egypt, constituting the mandatory VAT registration threshold under Article 15 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016; exporters may voluntarily register below this threshold to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 6 and the Executive Regulations of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, the 0% rate applies to: (a) export of tangible manufactured goods, including cosplay prop apparel, licensed theatrical costume robes, and synthetic wigs, physically removed from Egypt and evidenced by a customs export declaration; (b) services rendered to non-residents for use wholly outside Egypt; (c) supplies to approved free zones for export; and (d) related international transport, handling, and logistics services. The manufacturer must retain full proof of physical export, foreign-currency consideration, and repatriation of export proceeds to the Egyptian banking system.

  • Customs Export Declaration as registered in the Egyptian Customs ASYCUDA/CargoX system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian bank demonstrating receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin issued by the competent chamber of commerce
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice/export invoice matching the e-invoicing schema

Egypt operates a mandatory clearance-based e-invoicing and e-receipt regime: every VAT-registered taxpayer must transmit each B2B invoice and B2C receipt in the ETA-prescribed XML/JSON schema to the Electronic Invoicing System (https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg) in near-real time using an API or web-portal channel, signed with an ETA-issued digital certificate; the ETA validates the document, embeds a unique UUID and QR code, and returns the cleared invoice, without which input VAT deduction and export zero-rating claims are denied; the mandate covers all VAT-registered entities, including manufacturers selling cosplay prop apparel through e-commerce gateways such as Stripe and Shopify Payments.

The ETA cross-matches customs export declarations, bank FX records, shipping data, and e-invoicing platform records; missing, inconsistent, or fictitious documentation triggers rejection of the 0% claim, retrospective VAT assessment on the full export consideration, delay penalties of 2% per month of the unpaid tax (or the statutory annual equivalent in unified assessments), fines of up to EGP 50,000, and imprisonment of up to five years for willful fraudulent zero-rating under Articles 72–74 of Law No. 67 of 2016 and Article 113 of Law No. 91 of 2005. The ETA also exercises retrospective clawback of refunded input VAT for up to five fiscal years where export documentation is found fraudulent or non-substantiated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains full current-account convertibility under a flexibly managed interbank float (post-March 2024). Current-account payments (raw-material imports, operational service fees) may be freely purchased at the market rate without pre-approval. Capital-account convertibility remains partially restricted: inward FDI (equity, loans) is permit-light and freely remittable via licensed banks after registration; outward repatriations of dividends, capital reductions, liquidation proceeds, and shareholder loans require audited financial statements, a valid Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax-clearance certificate (Form 41 or e-tax clearance), evidence of the original inward CBE-registered flow, and, above bank-delegated thresholds, explicit CBE approval. In practice, profit remittance timing of 5-20 business days is standard; prolonged clearance occurs only when tax filings or the FDI registration trail are incomplete. Non-trade unilateral transfers (e.g., corporate goodwill, management fees without contract) face de facto rejection. No FX surrender obligation exists post-float; residents and non-residents may hold FX accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Circular No. 129 of 2022 (as amended effective 2024), the EG LLC exporter must: (i) repatriate 100% of export proceeds into the Egyptian banking system within 5 months from the shipping date on the customs export declaration (despatch note); (ii) retain the proceeds in a foreign-currency account at a licensed Egyptian bank or convert them to EGP in the interbank market at the exporter's discretion (mandatory conversion ratios enacted during 2022-23 were repealed after the March 2024 float); (iii) support every credit with the original export declaration, supplier contract, and commercial invoice; (iv) obtain an export registration certificate from the Egyptian Customs Authority and stay current on annual renewal. Extensions are available only with CBE approval upon proof of end-customer credit terms. Failure to repatriate triggers penalties under Exchange Control Law arts. 15-22, suspension of import/export windows, and bank-facility freezes.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 (arts. 220-222, 246-247), Exchange Control Law No. 194 of 1980, CBE Circular No. 129 of 2022 (as amended), and CBE Board decisions of March 2024 establishing a flexible exchange-rate regime. The CBE exclusively licenses banks and currency exchange houses for FX dealing; all cross-border trade and capital flows must be channeled through a licensed Egyptian bank and reported via CBE's screening and statistical systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW (legally constrained). The CBE does not authorize offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) to maintain EGP clearing accounts or act as settlement agents for Egyptian resident exporters. Direct use of an offshore EMI account for business receipts or payments breaches Exchange Control Law No. 194 of 1980 and Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, exposing the LLC to fines, forfeiture of import/export privileges, and criminal AML referrals. The only compliant EMI structure is a SWIFT MT103 sweep from the EMI or payment processor into the EG LLC's USD account at a licensed Egyptian bank, supported by a transaction-level settlement ledger; the EMI must never be named as the underlying exporter or importer. For e-commerce card settlement, a CBE-licensed acquiring front-end (e.g., QNB Accept, Paymob, e-finance, Valu) should be used domestically for EGP, while international USD card flows from Stripe/Shopify must be reconciled end-to-end through the Egyptian bank channel. In practice, banks treat any recurring third-party-processor payout as an export transaction subject to CBE Circular 129/2022 repatriation timelines.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the initial FDI equity injection: (1) The non-resident shareholder remits USD from its own corporate or personal bank account via SWIFT MT103 to the EG LLC's designated USD capital account at an Egyptian Tier-1 bank; (2) the wire instruction must state 'Share Capital Subscription - EG_LLC', reference the GAFI file number, and identify the beneficiary tax card number; (3) the receiving bank issues an inward remittance advice and registers the flow with CBE's FDI reporting system (transfer purpose 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital'); E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW (legally constrained). The CBE does not authorize offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut) to maintain EGP clearing accounts or act as settlement agents for Egyptian resident exporters. Direct use of an offshore EMI account for business receipts or payments breaches Exchange Control Law No. 194 of 1980 and Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, exposing the LLC to fines, forfeiture of import/export privileges, and criminal AML referrals. The only compliant EMI structure is a SWIFT MT103 sweep from the EMI or payment processor into the EG LLC's USD account at a licensed Egyptian bank, supported by a transaction-level settlement ledger; the EMI must never be named as the underlying exporter or importer. For e-commerce card settlement, a CBE-licensed acquiring front-end (e.g., QNB Accept, Paymob, e-finance, Valu) should be used domestically for EGP, while international USD card flows from Stripe/Shopify must be reconciled end-to-end through the Egyptian bank channel. In practice, banks treat any recurring third-party-processor payout as an export transaction subject to CBE Circular 129/2022 repatriation timelines.
