Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Corporate Incorporation Division and One-Stop Shop (OSS), in coordination with the Commercial Registry offices of the Governorate where the registered office is located (Cairo/Giza).

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended by Law No. 4 of 2009, Articles 50-66 (Limited Liability Companies); Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations (GAFI One-Stop Shop incorporation procedure); Commercial Registry Law No. 34 of 1976, Articles 1-10; Industrial Licensing Law No. 15 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations (post-incorporation manufacturing license).

Notarized LLC Memorandum of Association (Incorporation Contract) executed in Arabic before the competent Notary Public (Shaher Al-Tawkeeq), enrolled with the Commercial Registry through the GAFI One-Stop Shop, and evidenced by the Commercial Registration Certificate and the GAFI incorporation certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — electronic portal: www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing platform: inv.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned LLC must first complete commercial registration at the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and obtain a commercial registry card. The LLC then registers with the ETA's competent tax office to obtain the Unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the 'Tax Card' by submitting the certificate of incorporation, commercial registry extract, statutory books, board resolution appointing a tax representative in Egypt, power of attorney, and lease/purchase proof of premises. VAT registration must be filed separately through the ETA's online VAT registration module (form VAT-1); for the e-invoicing system, the taxpayer must onboard via the ETA portal, generate user credentials, register technical API endpoints, and obtain a digital signature certificate for invoice submissions. Tax registration is mandatory before any import clearance or customs release. Non-resident sellers without an Egyptian establishment may be registered directly by the ETA for VAT on remote sales, subject to appointment of a resident fiscal agent if required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Egypt; the LLC is mandatorily treated as a separate taxable person for corporate and VAT purposes. Available statutory elections are limited to: (i) choice of VAT filing frequency (monthly or quarterly); (ii) voluntary VAT registration where turnover is below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (iii) consolidated e-invoicing and input VAT recovery treatment for affiliated persons operating an integrated fiscal supply chain; and (iv) transfer pricing advance pricing agreements with the ETA. No pass-through or fiscal transparency election is permitted under Egyptian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT registration; imports into Egypt are subject to VAT at customs irrespective of turnover, requiring an ETA VAT-ID prior to clearance

Egypt operates a centralized e-invoicing clearance mandate, phased in since 2020 and effective for most registered VAT taxpayers. Invoices must be issued in XML format with a cryptographic digital signature and submitted in real-time to the ETA via REST API, SFTP Plus bulk upload, or the ETA portal (inv.eta.gov.eg). The ETA servers validate and apply a digital stamp/QR code before an invoice is accepted; unvalidated invoices are not recognized for input VAT deduction. The parallel e-receipt system applies to business-to-consumer transactions. Mandatory integration requires obtaining ETA e-invoicing environment credentials, implementation of compliant invoicing software, and full API connectivity with the fiscal union (internet-based TTM) for non-resident suppliers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating regime in which current account trade transactions are freely convertible through authorized banks, whereas capital account flows require CBE-licensed bank intermediation and GAFI registration for foreign direct investment. Further, profit and dividend remittances are permitted without separate CBE prior approval once the relevant audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance, and prior year CBE/GAFI investment registration documentation are presented to the Egyptian bank, although repatriation timing can be materially delayed by banks' USD liquidity queueing and hard currency availability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CBE requires Egyptian exporting entities to repatriate 100% of export proceeds into an Egyptian bank account within five months from the shipment date and to convert such proceeds into EGP within 30 days of receipt, with the relevant USD/EGP conversion executed at the prevailing interbank rate; penalties, suspension of export financing facilities, and loss of the exporter's clean commercial status attach to non-compliance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the exclusive foreign exchange administrator and monetary authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 concerning the Central Bank and the Banking System.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs cannot hold an Egyptian banking license for EGP clearing services as such issuance lies exclusively within CBE's licensing powers under Law No. 194 of 2020 and the 2022 Payment Services Regulation; therefore, an offshore EMI may only act as a technical payment orchestration layer connected to a licensed Egyptian PSP bank and cannot serve as the settlement bank for CBE-controlled escrow or export surrender accounts. Consequently, the treasury blueprint should restrict EMI usage to lightweight API-driven payment solutions and must clear statutory trade and capital flows via tier-1 licensed banks.

Within four months after the end of the taxable fiscal year; for calendar-year taxpayers, the filing deadline is 30 April. Payment of the final balance is due on the same deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions trigger the mandatory contemporaneous Local File for the Egyptian entity. A Master File is required where the multinational group exceeds EGP 500,000,000 worldwide turnover and the Egyptian controlled transactions cross the aforementioned threshold. Annual transfer pricing disclosure must be appended to the CIT return for controlled transactions above the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a UAE-style separate Economic Substance Regulations code, but the ETA invokes GAAR, substance-over-form doctrine, and Article 38 of the Income Tax Law to disregard artificial interpositions. A medical device OEM must demonstrate genuine Egyptian operational substance: employees, leases, physical manufacturing/QA capacity, procurement decisions, and bank-account control. A regional procurement or IP-holding vehicle without real functions will be reattributed to the Egyptian manufacturer.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misstatement of Taxable Income

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to five years and a fine of not less than the tax evaded and not more than twice the tax evaded, in addition to the 2% monthly default surcharge (capped at 20%), immediate seizure of movable assets, and revocation of tax clearance.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorized signatories are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt when the misstatement results from their instruction; the Public Prosecutor may impose travel bans, order personal asset freezes, and criminal conviction disqualifies management from acting as company directors.
Shell Company / Facade without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA disregard of the intermediary, reattribution of full manufacturing profit to the Egyptian LLC, denial of intercompany expense deductions, reclassification of excessive payments as dividends/royalties subject to withholding tax, and assessment of back taxes, interest, and the 2% monthly penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution for taxes avoided by the sham arrangement, invalidation of contractual protections, personal attachment for unpaid liabilities if they signed false intercompany invoices, and criminal prosecution if fraudulent intent is established.
Failure to File or Pay CIT for an Egyptian Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: Five-year back assessment, tax paid with interest from the original due date, 2% monthly late-filing/ late-payment surcharge capped at 20%, denial of tax clearance for customs/export transactions, and suspension of the commercial registration for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be summoned to criminal court for pirating tax rules; personal attachment of bank accounts, travel restrictions, and liability for the total outstanding tax where the entity is left as an empty shell.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Incomplete Documentation

Statutory sanction: Compensating transfer pricing adjustments, disallowance of excessive deductions, primary tax plus interest and the 2% monthly penalty, and anti-abuse penalties of up to 100% of adjusted tax where gross negligence or perjury is found.

Director personal liability: The director signing false TP declarations is personally exposed to monetary fines and disqualification from government tenders and trade licenses; criminal evasion charges may be pursued where the TP misstatement deliberately masks taxable profit.
AML, Customs and Repatriation Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt AML Law No. 80 of 2002, imprisonment of up to seven years and fines of up to EGP 200,000 per violation; the ETA may freeze bank accounts, and the Central Bank of Egypt may deny foreign exchange allocation and suspend licensed payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for willful failure to apply AML controls and for unauthorised outward transfers; travel bans, asset seizure, removal from office, and repayment obligations are imposed on individuals who used corporate accounts for illicit fund movement.
