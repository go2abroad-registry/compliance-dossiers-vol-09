Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum registered share capital of an OÜ is EUR 2,500, per section 140 of the Companies Act. An e-Residency filing allows the founders to declare the subscribed capital without a proof of immediate payment at submission. There is no statutory escrow account requirement; contributions may be in cash or assets, and the board must procure full payment when a call is made. Minimum nominal value of one share is EUR 1.00.

Funding & unlock path: No dedicated civil-defense licence is required for a pure software office. Prior to physical occupancy, the leased premises must have a fire safety certificate issued by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) and, where applicable, a structural-load approval for any server rack installation. The employer must conduct a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, register employees with the MTA, and keep the working-environment documentation available for inspection.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); central portal: e-MTA (https://e-mta.ee) and e-Business Register (https://ariregister.rik.ee) for company registration

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of an OÜ, the MTA automatically registers the company for income tax and social tax; a non-resident applicant (or newly formed resident company) must file the registration within 10 days via e-MTA using the e-Business Register digital channel. A VAT identification number (Käibemaksukohustuslase number, format EE + 9-digit registry code) is applied for through e-MTA (application KRD) when taxable turnover exceeds €40,000 (or voluntarily). Foreign companies obtaining an Estonian VAT ID must designate a tax representative (maksuesindaja) unless the MTA grants an exemption; the registration application must include the founding documents, registry extract, and, where applicable, power of attorney. Statutory timeline: registration for VAT is effective from the start of the month following the application or from the beginning of the month in which the threshold was exceeded, whichever applies; income tax withholding registration for non-resident employer requires form TSD submissions monthly.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian OÜ is a resident legal person taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act [Tulumaksuseadus]; however, the charging event is deemed to occur only upon profit distribution — undistributed profits incur 0% corporate income tax (annual corporate income tax 0% on retained earnings). Territorial scope: Resident companies are taxed only on distributions (dividends, deemed profit distributions, non-business payments) and fringe benefits; capital gains on active participation are exempt if the subsidiary is engaged in actual business. No branch profit tax; permanent establishments abroad are effectively exempt through a participation exemption. Tax-exempt zone: No special economic free zone with automatic corporate tax exemption; however, the 'startup' status granted by the MTA provides 0% tax on profit distributions to private persons holding startup stock options, and the special 25% rate regime on credit institutions does not apply to ISIC 6201. — Upon incorporation of an OÜ, the MTA automatically registers the company for income tax and social tax; a non-resident applicant (or newly formed resident company) must file the registration within 10 days via e-MTA using the e-Business Register digital channel. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian OÜ is a resident legal person taxed at the entity level under the Income Tax Act [Tulumaksuseadus]; however, the charging event is deemed to occur only upon profit distribution — undistributed profits incur 0% corporate income tax (annual corporate income tax 0% on retained earnings). Territorial scope: Resident companies are taxed only on distributions (dividends, deemed profit distributions, non-business payments) and fringe benefits; capital gains on active participation are exempt if the subsidiary is engaged in actual business. No branch profit tax; permanent establishments abroad are effectively exempt through a participation exemption. Tax-exempt zone: No special economic free zone with automatic corporate tax exemption; however, the 'startup' status granted by the MTA provides 0% tax on profit distributions to private persons holding startup stock options, and the special 25% rate regime on credit institutions does not apply to ISIC 6201.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Estonia. Statutory elections available include: (a) election to levy the 14% lower income tax rate on regular distributions (when the parent is an individual/Estonian resident with 25%+ or qualifying recipient) instead of the 20% rate, in accordance with § 50-504 of the Income Tax Act; (b) election for the VAT small-business scheme (VKE) once turnover does not exceed €40,000; (c) election of cash-based VAT accounting (kassapõhine käibemaksuarvestus) by smaller taxable persons whose turnover is below €40,000, aligned to payment receipt; (d) optional OSS (One-Stop-Shop) mini-scheme registration to handle EU-wide B2C e-services and distance sales in the single VAT return; (e) deemed cost/expense election for remunerating board members' fringe benefits. Hybrid mismatch: Estonia applies the comprehensive EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) — for hybrid mismatches, the Tax Information and Tax Collection Act (MKS) § 22e onwards requires a taxpayer to reflect the actual treatment of a hybrid entity in the other jurisdiction and apply a corresponding fiscal treatment; no formal black-box election is available — instead, a taxpayer must file a statement to the MTA when claiming double non-taxation relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 (aggregate taxable turnover in a calendar year or from commencement of activities; for EU distance sales and cross-border B2C e-services supplied via OSS the threshold is €10,000; in the absence of intra-EU distance sales, a payer with annual turnover not exceeding €40,000 may apply the small-business regime but remains registration-eligible upon voluntary application)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available for: (a) export of goods physically departing the EU, evidenced by customs export declaration; (b) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer holding a valid VAT ID; (c) services directly connected with export of goods (e.g., logistics, port services, pre-export telemetry); (d) software maintenance and remote monitoring services supplied to a non-resident taxable person located outside Estonia (place-of-supply rule B2B — the service is outside the scope of Estonian VAT and, if the customer is in another EU state, subject to reverse charge; for B2C e-services to EU consumers, VAT is payable via the OSS scheme). For a remote desktop/IoT telemetry SaaS seller, zero-rating is substantiated by (i) a commercial contract identifying the customer as a taxable person, (ii) the customer's valid EU VAT ID (via VIES confirmation), (iii) IP/geolocation logs for non-EU B2C customers, and (iv) collection of export of digital services declaration (when no OSS is used).

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX) — electronic export declaration (AAD) accepted by MTA customs with assigned movement reference number (MRN) and the destination customs office clearance
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / CMR consignment note or equivalent third-carrier transport document confirming physical departure of goods from EU territory
  • Inward remittance certificate (bank document verifying receipt of foreign currency payment) — not mandatory for zero-rating, but required for customs value controls and foreign-exchange control checks; MTA may accept proof of payment as alternative
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract with delivery terms (Incoterms) demonstrating transfer of risk outside Estonia, and for digitally supplied services — a certificate of residence or extract of the customer's VAT registration

Estonia does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance stamping (no SDI-type certification) on standard B2B invoices. However, since 1 July 2019, an invoicer is required to issue an interoperable e-invoice (format XSD XML based on the e-invoice operator portal run under the MTA's Estonian e-invoice framework or PEPPOL-format) when the recipient has declared technical capability; since 1 January 2020, a VAT payer must receive, and a supplier must transmit, invoices in an electronic format where the recipient is an Estonian VAT payer with an e-address. The MTA receives transaction data through the periodic VAT returns (KMD), the aggregated e-declaration, SAF-T-type 'TURN' export data file (counterpart and invoice-level data at the request of the MTA), and direct API integration to the e-MTA service is available to accounting software providers but is not a mandatory real-time clearance. Foreign e-commerce vendors (including remote desktop/IoT sellers) making B2C supplies of electronically supplied services to Estonian consumers must either register in the special EU OSS scheme (quarterly return) or charge Estonian VAT and submit KMD monthly; the MTA uses the OSS data exchange portal (DIGIKOGU) for automated country-of-consumption clearance.

The MTA scrutinises export evidence retrospectively for up to 3 years (5 years for deliberate concealment). If documentation is deficient, the MTA reclassifies the supply as domestic and assesses 22% VAT with late-payment interest at the statutory rate (0.06% per day under the Tax Information and Tax Collection Act, currently 7% annually referenced to ECB rate) and a supplementary penalty of 5% of the unpaid tax when the return is submitted late. Where fraudulent zero-rating is established (false customs declarations, fictitious exports, circular e-money flows, fabricated invoices), the MTA applies aggravated fraud penalties of 50% to 100% of the under-declared tax plus criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code § 389-390 (tax fraud — up to 3 years' imprisonment up to €200,000 fine; large-scale avoidance up to 5 years). In cross-border OSS filings, an unjustified zero-rate claim triggers reopenings of all EU member-state filings and proportional fraud surcharges under the national law of the member-state of consumption; digital platform operators (Stripe/Paddle) are obliged to provide full transactional records to MTA upon request under MKS § 77-79, making retrospective clawback and reverse-charge re-issuance to self-invoicing highly probable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia, as a full EU member state, maintains unrestricted capital account convertibility under EU Regulation 2015/1535 and national implementation. Capital transactions including equity contributions and profit remittances face no quantitative or qualitative restrictions. Current account transactions such as trade settlements and dividend distributions are fully liberalized with only standard AML, tax, and beneficial ownership verification requirements applying; no material profit remittance hurdles exist for resident OÜ entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required under Estonian or EU law. Foreign currency receipts may be retained in company accounts or converted at prevailing market rates without statutory timelines or prescribed ratios. All transactions fall under current account liberalization rules with free usability or remittance subject solely to compliance with AML/KYC and transfer pricing documentation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility rating for cross-border electronic money issuance through EU-licensed fintech providers operating under PSD2 and e-Residency frameworks. Legal constraints include mandatory registration with the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon), adherence to strict AML/KYC obligations, and compliance with EU electronic money institution licensing requirements for any offshore digital wallet or remittance activities.
