Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik; RT I 1995, 26, 355), Chapter on Private Limited Company, Sections 135–177, in particular Sections 136 (formation), 140 (share capital), 142 (minimum capital), 161 (management board) and 170 (general meeting).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised Company Registration Application (Avaldus) together with the foundation deed and articles of association, submitted digitally through the e-Business Register.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; share capital may be set at EUR 0.00. If a share capital is fixed, it must be recorded in the articles of association. No escrow account or blocked-deposit certificate is required for registration; the registration application must declare whether cash contributions have been paid. In-kind contributions require a valuation statement. Non-resident ownership is 100% allowed.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises must have a valid use permit (kasutusluba) as an office, and the employer must carry out a documented occupational risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act. Fire safety measures, including extinguishers and clear escape routes, must be verified by the leaseholder or the lessor. No separate civil defence clearance is required for a low-risk SaaS operation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; share capital may be set at EUR 0.00. If a share capital is fixed, it must be recorded in the articles of association. No escrow account or blocked-deposit certificate is required for registration; the registration application must declare whether cash contributions have been paid. In-kind contributions require a valuation statement. Non-resident ownership is 100% allowed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, 'MTA'); digital portal: e-MTA (emta.ee / e-maksuamet), interoperable with the e-Business Register (Äriregister) and e-Residency services.

E-government filing portal

For an OÜ: statutory TIN = 8-digit company registry code (registrikood) granted automatically upon incorporation via the Äriregister and activated in the MTA commercial register data pool. VAT ID (format EE + 9 digits) procurement: (1) file VAT-registration application with MTA via e-MTA; (2) provide company particulars, legal address in Estonia (registered seat), and contact person; (3) obtain EORI number through the e-MTA customs module for export/import operations; (4) non-resident taxable persons may register directly under the reverse-charge/OSS rules without a fixed establishment, and no mandatory fiscal representative is required for EU-resident entities; (5) authenticate e-MTA access via ID-card, Mobile-ID or Smart-ID. Registration becomes compulsory when taxable supplies exceed the annual turnover threshold; voluntary registration is permitted from the first euro.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian distributed-profit model: 0% corporate income tax on retained and reinvested profits; taxable event arises only upon profit distribution, liquidation, hidden distributions and certain non-business expenditure; regular dividend distributions to resident individuals may qualify for the reduced 14/86 rate, other distributions are taxed at 20/80; territorial scope: Estonia does not tax undistributed worldwide business profits until distribution, but maintains CFC, exit-tax and hybrid-mismatch rules). — For an OÜ: statutory TIN = 8-digit company registry code (registrikood) granted automatically upon incorporation via the Äriregister and activated in the MTA commercial register data pool. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian distributed-profit model: 0% corporate income tax on retained and reinvested profits; taxable event arises only upon profit distribution, liquidation, hidden distributions and certain non-business expenditure; regular dividend distributions to resident individuals may qualify for the reduced 14/86 rate, other distributions are taxed at 20/80; territorial scope: Estonia does not tax undistributed worldwide business profits until distribution, but maintains CFC, exit-tax and hybrid-mismatch rules).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Estonia. Relevant statutory elections: (1) reduced CIT rate for regular dividends under §50(3) of the Income Tax Act (14/86 rate where dividends are paid within a recurring three-year cycle, subject to conditions); (2) VAT group election under §3(3) of the VAT Act for closely connected persons with joint and several liability and consolidated reporting; (3) opt-in to the VAT OSS/IOSS for intra-EU B2C digital services and distance sales in lieu of multi-state VAT registration; (4) election for with-holding style CIT settlement at the time of distribution aligned with the standard 20/80 rule; (5) hybrid-mismatch and ATAD/MLI adjustments remain limited because the system defers taxation to distribution, disallowing artificial mismatches under the domestic attribution rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year for domestic taxable supplies of goods and services (compulsory VAT registration; voluntary registration permitted below threshold). For intra-EU B2C digital/TBE services and distance sales, Estonia applies the EUR 10,000 Union-wide OSS threshold; non-resident suppliers of B2C digital services (LegalTech SaaS) have no domestic threshold and must charge Estonian VAT from the first euro unless using OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third countries qualify for 0% VAT (VAT Act §15) when the goods physically leave the EU, proof of dispatch within the statutory export period (normally 90 days under EU Article 269) is retained, and the supplier holds the export declaration and transport documents in its permanent records. Intra-EU B2B dispatches are zero-rated where the customer communicates a valid VAT registration number (VIES) and the supplier submits the EC Sales List (KMD-INF). Export-related ancillary services (carriage, handling, customs clearance) are zero-rated when performed for a non-EU purchaser or on exported goods. For LegalTech SaaS export supplies: B2B services to non-Estonian taxable persons are outside Estonian VAT (reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction); B2C digital services to non-EU consumers are exempt from Estonian VAT as the place of supply lies abroad; B2C consumers within Estonia and the EU remain subject to 22% VAT via OSS or domestic VAT, and no customs zero-rating applies to intangible cloud services.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD) with MRN released in the e-MTA Customs Information System and proof of physical exit from the EU
  • Transport documentation (Bill of Lading, sea waybill, CMR or Air Waybill) evidencing dispatch and destination
  • Commercial invoice, sale contract and terms of supply (Incoterms) with evidence of title and consideration transfer
  • Intra-EU: customer VAT ID confirmation via VIES plus EC Sales List (KMD-INF) and transport records for qualifying intra-community supplies
  • Foreign exchange or inward remittance confirmation (bank SWIFT or customer payment evidence) when proceeds are repatriated, consistent with the 180-day documentation benchmark

As of 2025 Estonia does not implement mandatory real-time transaction clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control (CTC) for B2B invoices; VAT returns (KMD and annexes KMD-INF) are filed periodically through e-MTA with EDI/API integration. Mandatory e-invoicing applies in B2G context: public-sector buyers must receive and process structured e-invoices (PEPPOL/e-arve XML standard); private B2B e-invoicing remains optional but is expected to become obligatory upon transposition of the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework. The MTA phases SAF-T-based structured XML annexes to corporate VAT returns for defined large taxpayers. No separate digital service tax applies; API connectivity reflects OAuth2-secured e-MTA machine-to-machine endpoints.

The MTA audits zero-rated export supplies on a risk-based basis; failure to prove export (or completion of the 90-day EU exit deadline) within the documentary retention period triggers reassessment of VAT at 22% plus penalty interest of 0.06% per day and late-filing fines. Where export zero-rating fraud or abusive arrangements are proven, the MTA may claw back the VAT refund with interest within the 7-year limitation period, impose tax misdemeanour fines (up to approximately EUR 8,000 for legal persons), apply general tax arrears enforcement, and refer intentional large-scale tax evasion to criminal prosecution (Penal Code §70) with imprisonment up to 5 years. Directors of the OÜ may face secondary liability for taxes withheld or knowingly unpaid. No separate FX repatriation statute applies in Estonia; the 180-day parameter is a prudential banking and cross-border contract benchmark used to demonstrate bona fide collection of export proceeds on audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under full capital account convertibility as an EU member state. Capital and current account transactions involving the Euro are unrestricted for both residents and non-residents. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders face no prior approval requirements, subject solely to standard AML/KYC verification, beneficial ownership disclosure, and corporate tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies to digital service revenues under Estonian law. SaaS and LegalTech revenues are recorded for VAT (20% standard rate) and corporate income tax purposes (0% on undistributed profits). All FX transactions remain subject to general banking regulations and AML requirements.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained and reinvested profits; 20/80 (20% of gross, 25% effective on net) on profit distributions and deemed distributions; reduced 14/86 (14% of gross) on recurrent dividends paid from profits previously taxed in Estonia

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20/80 (20% of gross dividend; 25% effective tax rate on net cash) under Income Tax Act section 50; reduced to 14/86 (14% of gross) for recurrent dividends paid from profits that have already borne 20/80 CIT; 0% for an EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted 2-year period under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 for a single controlled transaction with the same foreign related party per calendar year, or EUR 100,000 for the aggregate of all controlled transactions with foreign related parties, triggers the obligation to prepare and produce a Local File upon request; Master File applies where multinational group revenue is at least EUR 50 million; CbC Report applies at EUR 750 million group consolidated revenue. Documentation must be submitted within 60 days of the Estonian Tax and Customs Board request

Economic substance scope: No separate Economic Substance Regulation akin to Caribbean offshore centres is in force. The operative test is place of effective management: an OÜ whose board and senior management functions are exercised exclusively abroad is reclassified as a non-resident, losing the 0% retained-profit regime and the reduced 14/86 dividend rate for all foreign-source income. The general anti-abuse rule and substance-over-form doctrine allow the tax authority to disregard shell e-Residency structures and recharacterize round-tripped funds as taxable distributions

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion via distribution deferral abuse

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the benefit as a distribution, application of 20/80 CIT plus 33% social tax on employment-type benefits; late-payment interest of 0.06% per day; criminal tax fraud conviction under Penal Code section 399 with imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 8 million for the legal person

Director personal liability: The director is jointly and severally liable for unlawful distributions (Commercial Code section 167); criminal conviction may trigger disqualification, seizure of assets, travel bans and extradition under the Estonian Criminal Procedure Code
Shell company / e-Residency facade

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board applies the place-of-effective-management test retroactively, removes the company from the resident register, assesses 20/80 CIT on all historical distributions, and imposes daily interest plus fines up to EUR 8 million for deliberate evasion

Director personal liability: Directors face personal recovery of a tax debt after insolvency if assets were stripped through dividends; travel bans and arrest warrants may issue during the tax-crime investigation
AML / payment-flow integrity

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, a legal person may be fined up to EUR 8 million (or 10% of annual turnover) and the service may be suspended; the Financial Intelligence Unit may impose a binding precept to cease transactions

Director personal liability: The AML compliance officer and the management board face criminal liability, confiscation orders, professional bans and imprisonment up to 5 years; key-man travel restrictions apply pending proceedings
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Procedural fine for non-production within 60 days, adjustment of margins to arm's length under the transactional net margin method, recharacterization of the excessive royalty as a taxable dividend at 20/80 and daily interest on the revised assessment

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the mispriced controlled transactions can be made personally liable for the uncollectable corporate tax debt and prosecuted for tax fraud if the documentation gap is found to be intentional
