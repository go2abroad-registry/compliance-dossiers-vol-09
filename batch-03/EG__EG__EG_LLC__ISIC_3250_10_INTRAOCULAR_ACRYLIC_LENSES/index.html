Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax and Customs Authority (ETCA), Ministry of Finance; integrated e-services and e-invoicing platform of the Egyptian Tax Authority (unified ETCA digital portal)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC, the company must first complete incorporation through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) one-stop shop and obtain the Commercial Registry extract and tax card file. The LLC then applies to the competent ETCA tax office (Ma'mashya) for a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN), filing the certified articles of association, commercial registry certificate, a board resolution appointing an Egyptian tax representative or finance manager, proof of registered head office, and a turnover estimate. VAT registration under Law No. 67 of 2016 is made on the same ETCA file where taxable supplies exceed the threshold, after which the taxpayer obtains an ETCA e-invoicing profile and fiscal token. Non-resident suppliers without an Egyptian permanent establishment may register for VAT only through a locally appointed tax representative and are not entitled to a domestic profit-tax TIN absent a deemed PE.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime; Egypt applies entity-level taxation to LLCs as juridical persons. Available elections are limited to statutory choices such as fiscal-year designation (with ETCA approval), depreciation and amortization method elections under Egyptian Accounting Standards, deduction of certain provisions (e.g., bad debts) on prescribed conditions, and 5-year tax-loss carryforward elections. Investment incentive elections under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 (including free-zone licensing) are obtained via GAFI; hybrid mismatch election mechanisms are not available, and thin-capitalization/interest and related anti-abuse rules are applied administratively by ETCA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration is triggered where annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is permitted for persons with annual turnover below this threshold in accordance with ETCA procedures.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 23 of the VAT Law applies to goods physically exported outside Egypt pursuant to a formal customs export declaration and to services supplied to non-resident persons where the benefit and use occur wholly outside Egypt. For goods, the LLC must hold the ETCA-cleared export e-invoice, prove the physical movement of the intraocular acrylic lenses across the Egyptian border within the statutory export period, maintain the customs declaration and international transport documents, and evidence that title and risk passed to the non-resident buyer. For services, additional documentation must demonstrate that the recipient is a person resident abroad and that the service is not effectively connected with Egypt. Export proceeds must be received in a permitted foreign currency through an Egyptian licensed bank and repatriated within the Central Bank of Egypt deadline (180 days), while the refund is claimed in the periodic VAT return with full supporting files retained per the VAT Law and executive regulations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (C21/Single Administrative Document cleared through the NAFIZA single window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the intraocular lens consignment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • ETCA-cleared export e-invoice containing the fiscal validation UUID

Egypt operates a mandatory e-invoicing (E-Invoice) system on a fiscal clearance model: VAT-registered suppliers must issue B2B e-invoices in the ETCA XML/JSON data model, transmit them in real time through the ETCA API or web portal, and obtain a unique fiscal UUID/validation code from the authority before the invoice becomes valid and usable by the buyer. The parallel E-Receipt system covers B2C sales and uses QR-code stamping. Export and import declarations are cleared through the NAFIZA single window, which cross-matches the Customs Export Declaration with cleared e-invoices and bank forex receipts. Businesses not integrated via certified systems/APIs risk invoice rejection, denial of input VAT credit, and penalty exposure.

ETCA subjects export zero-rating and refund claims to document-based audits and risk-flagging of high-value or repeated intra-group exports. Where the statutory export, documentation, or forex-repatriation conditions are not met, the VAT at 14% is assessed on the underlying supply with statutory delay interest from the original due date; unlawfully obtained refunds are clawed back with interest computed from payment. Deliberate fraudulent claims are prosecuted as tax evasion, attracting an evasion penalty equal to double the tax due, fines up to EGP 50,000 per invoice, and imprisonment terms under Law No. 67 of 2016; assessments on fraud cases may be raised within an extended limitation period of up to 10 years versus the general 5-year rule.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a market-clearing flexible exchange-rate regime administered by the CBE since the March 2024 decontrol; the EGP float is recognized as an Article VIII compliant currency in practice. Current-account transactions—trade settlements, service payments, and distributable profit remittances—are freely transferable through Authorized CBE banks upon presentation of underlying commercial documentation. Capital-account flows (foreign-direct-investment injection, liquidation and divestment proceeds, intercompany loan repayments) require GAFI registration, submission of CBE Form 4 declaration for investment proceeds, and a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA). Profit remittance hurdles for a foreign-owned Egyptian medical-device LLC include: (i) audited financial statements evidencing legally distributable reserves; (ii) satisfactory proof of settled corporate and dividend withholding taxes (15% statutory dividend WHT or reduced rate under an applicable double-taxation treaty); (iii) bank-side FCY liquidity availability and internal CBE quota allocation; and (iv) a clean AML/KYC record of the ultimate beneficiary. FX availability has materially normalized in 2024-2025 following the IMF Extended Fund Facility disbursement and the removal of the parallel market premium, but banks continue to prioritize documentary-traced trade flows over non-operational transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Circular No. 438 of 20 August 2020, as amended, and Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, export proceeds arising from the sale of intraocular acrylic lenses must be repatriated to Egypt and credited to the exporter's licensed Egyptian bank account within a maximum statutory period of six months from the bill-of-lading or shipment date, subject to CBE discretionary tenor extension for letter-of-credit maturities. The EG LLC is required to maintain a dedicated foreign-currency Export Proceeds Account at its Authorized Bank; after the 2024 liberalization there is no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender obligation, meaning the receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD/EUR or sold at the prevailing interbank rate at the exporter's election. The bank must retain, for CBE inspection, the customs export declaration (Form 334), the SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice referencing the underlying sale, the commercial invoice, and the bill of lading/airway bill. Failure to repatriate outside the permitted timeline or the undocumented retention of export proceeds abroad exposes the entity to CBE monetary penalties up to the value of the undisclosed sum and suspension of the exporting license.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) in conjunction with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for foreign capital registration and liquidation proceeds certification

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. Egypt does not recognize unlicensed foreign EMI constructs: any payment institution that clears EGP, holds Egyptian client funds, or provides local payment accounts must be licensed by the CBE under Law No. 18 of 2021 on Payment Systems. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Currencycloud) cannot obtain an Egyptian IBAN, cannot join the CBE RTGS/FWIRE clearing infrastructure, and cannot execute the customs-linked Form 334 documentation required for regulated export receipts. Consequently, for a high-risk, specially licensed intraocular lens manufacturer, EMIs may serve only as ancillary non-resident disbursement wallets for low-risk foreign expenses; all regulated customer settlements and export proceeds must flow exclusively through a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank. The EMI balances should be disclosed in treasury audits as non-bank counterparty exposure and ring-fenced from all CBE-reporting obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must inject equity exclusively by wire transfer to the company's CBE-recognized 'Foreign Investment' escrow account at the Egyptian licensed bank; the originating wire must state the CBE purpose code corresponding to acquisition of participations/capital subscription (FDI injection) and reference the GAFI investment file number. The verification chain comprises: (i) GAFI certificate of investment approval; (ii) general-assembly resolution approving the capital increase; (iii) the receiving bank's SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice; (iv) source-of-funds evidentiary file; and (v) a completed CBE Form 4 declaration of investment proceeds. Upon matching, the Egyptian bank issues the official 'Certificate of Investment Registration' enabling unrestricted use of the funds for working capital, procurement of lens-polymer manufacturing inputs, or conversion to EGP at the interbank rate. For high-value USD inflows the typical rail is: originator bank (London/NY) to the Egyptian bank's Nostro account at a US correspondent, cleared via CHIPS/Fedwire, then booked onshore in the CBE interbank messaging environment; crediting occurs T+1 at the latest.For settlements to foreign suppliers of acrylic monomers, hydrophilic polymer pellets, lens-packaging materials, and precision-lathing consumables, the EG LLC should use SWIFT MT103 for payments up to USD 50,000 equivalent per transaction under the licensed bank's documentation-light window, and a confirmed commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707/MT710) for all higher-value and repeat vendor flows. The LC route is the only rail that assures FX allocation by the issuing Egyptian bank and satisfies CBE's external-trade supervision on medical-device inputs; an import declaration under the Egyptian customs harmonized system (HS 3920/3907 for acrylic polymers) must accompany each run. The bank matches each outbound MT103 against the on-board transport document, Form 334 import declaration, insurance certificate, and the vendors' signed pro-forma/commercial invoice. EUR-denominated outflows to EU suppliers settle via TARGET2 at the European correspondent; USD flows settle through New York clearing via the bank's Nostro.
