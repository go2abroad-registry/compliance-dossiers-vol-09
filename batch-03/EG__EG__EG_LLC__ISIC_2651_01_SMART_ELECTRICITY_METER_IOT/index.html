Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official portal: www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing platform: einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; customs trade single window (NAFEZA) operated by the Egyptian Customs Authority at www.nafeza.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA at the competent Tax Registration Office corresponding to its registered commercial address within 30 days of issuance of the Commercial Registry certificate. Submission file: notarized Arabic articles of association (with consular/apostilled translation), Commercial Registry certificate and tax card application, ETA registration form, power of attorney evidencing the authorized tax representative, passport copies of shareholders and managers, proof of Egyptian registered office (lease or title deed), and corporate bank account confirmation. The ETA issues a single unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) which is thereafter used for corporate income tax (CIT), VAT, payroll, and stamp tax compliance; this TIN must be obtained before customs clearance of imports, onboarding to the mandatory e-invoicing system, or participation in public tenders.

No pass-through classification is available to Egyptian LLCs. — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA at the competent Tax Registration Office corresponding to its registered commercial address within 30 days of issuance of the Commercial Registry certificate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The LLC is a resident juridical person subject to CIT at 22.5% on worldwide income pursuant to Law No. 91/2005 as amended (Suez Canal Economic Zone and Investment Law licensed entities may apply for statutory tax holidays and zone-specific exemptions). No pass-through classification is available to Egyptian LLCs.

Available election forms

Egypt does not adopt a check-the-box mechanism. Available statutory elections: (1) consolidated CIT filing for an Egyptian tax group where the parent holds at least 90% of the capital of each subsidiary; (2) election to apply Investment Law No. 72/2017 incentives, including the 'Golden License' unified approval and incident customs/VAT facilitation; (3) election for Special Economic Zone (SEZ) status within the Suez Canal Economic Zone, which substitutes zone-specific fiscal privileges for standard domestic CIT/VAT rules; (4) thin capitalization and transfer pricing documentation elections aligning debt allocation and arm's-length pricing with ETA regulations; (5) for VAT, no fiscal unity election exists, requiring separate registration for each legal entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT registration; importers and dispatch/warehouse operators are required to register irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies provided: (i) the goods physically leave Egypt within 90 days from the date of supply/invoicing; (ii) the export is supported by a Customs Export Declaration released by Egyptian Customs through the NAFEZA single window; (iii) hard-currency export proceeds are received in Egypt through an authorized bank within the applicable repatriation deadline; (iv) the zero-rated transaction is documented on an ETA-cleared e-invoice bearing the export zero-rating code. Services qualify where supplied to a non-resident customer and wholly performed or consumed outside Egypt, substantiated by a service contract, delivery acceptance, and offshore consumption evidence. Suez Canal Economic Zone licensed manufacturers may also zero-rate supplies delivered into non-Egyptian customs territories.

  • Customs Export Declaration (released via NAFEZA national single window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (negotiable transport document evidencing outbound shipment from an Egyptian port)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank certificate evidencing receipt of hard-currency export proceeds in Egypt)
  • Commercial Invoice (matching the ETA-cleared zero-rated e-invoice)
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin (supporting the customs export file)

Mandatory e-invoicing applies to all VAT-registered taxpayers: each invoice must be issued in ETA-approved XML/JSON format, signed with an ETA-issued digital certificate, carry a machine-readable QR code, and be submitted to the ETA e-invoice portal for centralized clearance within 24 hours of issuance; API integration enabling real-time validation, schema validation, business-rule checks, and duplicate control is required for registered enterprise systems. Subsequent e-receipt phases mandate fiscal cash-register integration for business-to-consumer sales. Invoices rejected at clearance are legally treated as unissued and cannot support input VAT deduction; export transactions require matching clearance on the NAFEZA customs single window.

The ETA audits all zero-rated VAT refund applications; a refund may be withheld, clawed back, and converted into a criminal tax assessment where the supporting customs/BL/remittance bundle is found false, incomplete, or ineligible. Penalties under Law No. 67/2016 include: (i) a monthly delay/collection penalty of 1% of the unpaid or wrongly refunded VAT; (ii) full refund clawback compounded by a fine of up to 100% of the tax amount involved; (iii) tax evasion fines of double the evaded tax, in addition to imprisonment of responsible management where fraudulent documents are used (Articles 71 et seq.); (iv) debarment from the VAT refund fast-track and loss of Authorized Economic Operator status. Parallel penalties may be imposed under Customs Law No. 207/2020 for false export declarations. All substantiating records must be retained for at least five years following the end of the relevant calendar year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-floating exchange-rate regime. Current-account transactions, particularly trade settlements and profit remittances related to banking services, manufacturing and contractual service obligations of the smart-meter/IoT sector, are generally convertible subject to CBE Form T for services, supported by underlying contracts, invoices and tax clearance. Capital-account transactions for FDI, loans and subsidiary financing require CBE registration, evidence of the original capital source, and bank confirmation of compliance with ministerial decrees; profit and dividend remittances by an EG LLC to non-resident members are assessed as current-account transactions when the entity maintains a valid audited balance sheet, CBE-approved foreign investment file, and paid-in capital certificate. Residual FX-availability pressure may arise during periods of balance-of-payments stress; banks may impose prioritisation queues and require documentary proof of a valid underlying transaction before granting EGP-to-USD or EGP-to-EUR clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Per CBE Foreign Exchange Instructions, export payments must be received in a permissible foreign currency into the exporter's bank account in Egypt within 180 days of shipment date. A mandatory conversion obligation applies to export proceeds: 50% of the incoming foreign exchange must be sold to the bank in exchange for EGP within five banking days of crediting, unless an approved exception (e.g. repayment of eligible trade finance) is granted by the CBE. The remaining 50% may be retained in a foreign-currency account and used, under CBE supervision, for permitted import or debt-service obligations. Exporters of smart electricity meters and IoT devices claiming mitigation of surrender requirements must present the standard export declaration, invoice, AWB/Bill of Lading, pre-shipment GR/PI inspection certificate where applicable, and the signed bank transaction order.

Central bank supervision: The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, together with the Egyptian Exchange and the Ministry of Investment for associated Capital Account and FX approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

The operational viability of offshore digital-Electronic-Money-Institution (EMI) platforms in Egypt is Low. Egypt does not recognise extraterritorial EMI licences for domestic account holding. A foreign EMI can only support the Egyptian treasury architecture through a licensed CBE-approved correspondent bank relationship, remittance-house approval under the Egyptian Postal Authority and CBE Law 18 of 2019, or by originating seamless SWIFT payment instructions into a commercial bank account maintained by the EG LLC. Direct virtual IBAN and settlement-account services from non-resident EMIs are blocked by CBE circulars because the entity holds an Egyptian commercial registration, tax card and local payroll presence; domestic payroll and creditor disbursements must clear through Egyptian banking channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound FDI must flow exclusively through an Egyptian licensed bank using SWIFT MT103/MT202 with the standard beneficiary field populated with the EG LLC commercial registration and tax card references. Before wiring, the company must register its inbound investment with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain the CBE foreign investment file, evidencing the internal/ external finance structure. The purpose code must accurately reflect 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital' (capital injection) or 'Direct Investment — Inter-Company Loan' (permissible shareholder advance), and the underlying board resolution, capital schedule and share allotment resolution must be presented. The recipient bank will generate the CBE 'FDI registration form' and 'M-form/E-form' (as instructed upon onboarding) to record the liability to the foreign investor for eventual profit repartriation. CBE verification of the genuine nature of the transfer will be based on the atomic dimensions contained in the SWIFT gpi envelope: Contract ID, commercial invoice number (if inter-company procurement), and reference to the Financial Regulatory Authority certification.Outbound trade settlements for imported smart-meter processors, wireless modules, plastic injection materials and test equipment are cleared via (1) CBE-approved documentary commercial letters of credit (LC) with sight or usance tenor, established at the local Egyptian bank against the supplier proforma invoice; (2) collection bills with documents against payment (D/P) for lower-risk supplies; and (3) direct SWIFT MT103 remittances for fully paid-up orders after receipt of a CBE Form T approved invoice and Egyptian customs/import code verification. Where the beneficiary supplier is based in a sanctioned or CBE-risk-list jurisdiction, Remittance must be routed through an intermediate clearing bank outside that country, and the SWIFT MT103 consequence fields must include the Egyptian tax activity code (ISIC 2651) and the country of final disbursement. All import applications must satisfy CBE's 'five hundred thousand EGP / one hundred thousand USD' sign-off level by an Authorised Dealer bank and must reference the pre-approved import registration in the Egyptian Customs Authority 'ACI' system.CBE-licensed deliverable Egyptian Pound / USD forward contracts with a documented underlying import/export transactionCBE-licensed foreign-exchange swaps (FX swap) between EGP and USD/EUR for cash-flow synchronisation of import LC tenorsCross-currency swaps (CBE-Supervised) for long-term inter-company liability and project finance hedgingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) when executed outside Egypt on a non-resident hedging desk, subject to CBE authorisation of the benefiting bank and the Egyptian treasury-account documentation evidencing hard currency exposure

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: The operational viability of offshore digital-Electronic-Money-Institution (EMI) platforms in Egypt is Low. Egypt does not recognise extraterritorial EMI licences for domestic account holding. A foreign EMI can only support the Egyptian treasury architecture through a licensed CBE-approved correspondent bank relationship, remittance-house approval under the Egyptian Postal Authority and CBE Law 18 of 2019, or by originating seamless SWIFT payment instructions into a commercial bank account maintained by the EG LLC. Direct virtual IBAN and settlement-account services from non-resident EMIs are blocked by CBE circulars because the entity holds an Egyptian commercial registration, tax card and local payroll presence; domestic payroll and creditor disbursements must clear through Egyptian banking channels.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed EGP 10,000,000. Master File is mandatory when worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 1,500,000,000 and the taxpayer engages in related-party transactions. EGP refers to the Egyptian Pound.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the ETA and Egyptian General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under Law 91 of 2005 apply substance-over-form and economic-substance principles. A shell LLC lacking real office, staff, or operational substance is vulnerable to re-characterization, denial of treaty benefits, and tax penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years and a fine equal to 100%-200% of evaded tax (Articles 78-79 of Law 91 of 2005). The ETA also imposes civil penalties of up to 100% of the evaded tax and may issue an ex parte tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false returns or participating in evasion are personally and jointly liable for the unpaid tax. Directors may face criminal imprisonment, travel bans, and attachment of personal assets if corporate assets are stripped.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the arrangement under GAAR, denial of treaty benefits, disgorgement of tax incentives, and a penalty up to 200% of the tax benefit. The company may also be de-registered and struck off.

Director personal liability: Directors and effective beneficial owners are jointly liable for the restored corporate tax. The ETA may impose travel bans and freeze personal bank accounts pending settlement.
Money Laundering / AML

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law 80 of 2002, as amended, sanctions include imprisonment for up to three years and fines up to EGP 20,000,000. The entity may be dissolved and transactions declared null and void.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face joint criminal and civil liability. Personal assets may be confiscated, and travel bans or passport seizures are routinely imposed by the Economic Criminal Court.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The ETA may make a transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income and impose a penalty of 100% of the resulting unreported tax. A non-deductible fine not exceeding EGP 2,000,000 may also be levied.

Director personal liability: The financial director and any designated tax executive who knowingly omitted related-party disclosure may be personally assessed for the penalty. Directors may face criminal liability for filing materially false transfer pricing declarations.
