Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the March 2024 exchange-rate liberalization (IMF Extended Fund Facility-supported reform), the CBE has operated a flexible exchange-rate regime for the Egyptian Pound (EGP). Current-account transactions for legitimate trade are fully convertible through CBE-licensed banks. Capital-account flows (inbound equity, loans, dividends, and liquidation proceeds) are transferable after bank-level compliance clearance, with no CBE pre-approval for inbound equity injections under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Outbound profit remittance requires: (i) audited financial statements of the Egyptian LLC, (ii) tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) pursuant to Law No. 91 of 2005, (iii) proof of the original inward remittance (CBE inward declaration), and (iv) CBE reporting of the transfer. Practical hurdles are documentary verification and bank-internal limits rather than currency rationing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE instructions current in the 2024 framework, export proceeds for goods must be repatriated to a licensed Egyptian bank within four (4) months from the date of shipment (bill of lading date). There is no mandatory surrender ratio: the exporter may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign-currency account at a licensed Egyptian bank or sell them into the interbank market at the prevailing rate. The settlement bank verifies full receipt against the export customs declaration (NAFEZA/CUF export file), commercial invoice, and proof of shipment; overdue or unrepatriated proceeds trigger CBE reporting obligations and may suspend new export finance or trade facilities for the entity.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), supported by the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for foreign direct investment administration; non-banking FX policy and remittance controls are exclusively administered by the CBE.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary settlement rail. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not licensed by the CBE; they cannot open domestic EGP corporate accounts, receive export proceeds for statutory books, or evidence capital deposits for LLC incorporation. Such platforms act only as auxiliary e-commerce collection gateways (Stripe/credit-card acquirers) and must be structured as sub-wallets with automated daily sweeps into the CBE-licensed primary account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on corporate profit distributions under Article 52 of Law No. 91/2005. The EG LLC, as distributing entity, must withhold the 10% tax at the time of the general assembly resolution on distributions, whether the shareholder is resident or non-resident, and remit it to the ETA within the statutory deadline.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Form No. 50 must be filed within four (4) months after the close of the fiscal year; for a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 30 April of the following year. Quarterly provisional tax installments based on the preceding year's tax are due by the last day of the fourth, seventh, tenth and twelfth months of the fiscal year (i.e., April, July, October and December).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Each advance installment is due on the statutory monthly dates irrespective of the annual return deadline; the base equals one-quarter of the prior year's final tax assessment, adjusted for the current year's expected profit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 250,000: aggregate related-party transactions in a tax period triggering the mandatory Related Party Transactions Disclosure Form No. 1. EGP 1,000,000: controlled transactions with a single related party per category triggering preparation of a Local File (within 30 days of ETA request). EGP 500,000,000: consolidated annual revenue of the multinational group triggering the Master File, under the ETA Transfer Pricing Guidelines (Decision No. 56 of 2020). Arm's-length documentation must be contemporaneous; absence of the Local File shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer and justifies the ETA's discretionary profit adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 36, Law No. 91/2005) empowers the ETA to disregard transactions, entities and structures that lack genuine commercial and economic substance. An EG LLC with no physical premises, no Egyptian-resident management, no local payroll, and all material decisions taken offshore may be treated as a shell; the ETA may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the entity as a permanent establishment of its foreign shareholder, or deem the entire profit taxable at 22.5%. SCZONE and Investment Law tax incentives are conditional upon the beneficiary maintaining substantial business activity, a committed capital base, and an Egyptian operational footprint.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 85 of Law No. 91/2005: imprisonment from two to five years and a fine of EGP 5,000-50,000, plus confiscation of undeclared profits; the unpaid tax carries a 2% monthly surcharge; e-invoice violations attract fines of EGP 20,000-100,000 per invoice under Law No. 206/2020.

Director personal liability: The general manager and the authorized Egyptian tax accountant who sign the false return face criminal prosecution; the court may issue a travel ban and freeze personal bank accounts; Article 89 of Law No. 91/2005 holds the directors jointly and severally liable for the tax debts if the company is dissolved or stripped of assets to avoid collection; the corporate veil is pierced in favor of the Egyptian tax authority.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Article 36 (GAAR) permits the ETA to disregard the structure, disallow intercompany deductions, reattribute profits to a deemed Egyptian permanent establishment, and impose the 22.5% tax on the full amount; the taxpayer also forfeits Investment Law and SCZONE incentives and may be denied treaty relief under limitation-on-benefits clauses.

Director personal liability: Egyptian courts pierce the corporate veil where the LLC is an instrument of fraud: directors and shadow directors are declared jointly liable for all corporate tax and civil damages; repeated GAAR findings draw criminal tax-evasion charges with travel bans, asset attachment and imprisonment up to five years.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Cross-border Payments

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is personally liable for the unwithheld amount plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge; the expense is disallowed in the payer's corporate tax return; the ETA may additionally impose a penalty equal to the evaded WHT and deny the deduction of the underlying payment for transfer-pricing non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The officer who authorized the cross-border wire transfer without WHT deduction is jointly and severally liable with the company; the ETA may directly execute collections against the director's personal assets and secure an arrest/travel-ban order in criminal proceedings.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Falsification

Statutory sanction: Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended) imposes fines of up to EGP 500,000 and imprisonment for false UBO declarations; downstream banking partners must freeze the LLC's Egyptian accounts pending verification; the company may be struck from the commercial register and denied e-invoicing privileges.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false UBO or corporate KYC declarations face personal imprisonment and fines; the public prosecutor may issue a preventive travel ban; directors are also personally liable for damages caused to creditors who relied on the concealed ownership structure.
Foreign Exchange Non-Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Violation of Central Bank of Egypt foreign-exchange regulations (Law No. 194/2020): administrative fines, blacklisting of the company's import/export license, forced conversion orders, and monetary penalties on the unrepatriated balance; the unrepatriated amount is treated as undisclosed income subject to 22.5% CIT plus the 2% monthly surcharge.

Director personal liability: The general manager who instructed the offshore retention is subject to criminal fiscal prosecution and personal garnishment; travel bans are routinely issued in foreign-exchange evasion cases, and the directors cannot liquidate the LLC without a Central Bank clearance certificate.
