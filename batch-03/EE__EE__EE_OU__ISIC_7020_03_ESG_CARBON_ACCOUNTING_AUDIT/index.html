Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Eesti Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) - national tax and customs authority. Official portal: e-MTA (www.emta.ee) for registration, filings, and fiscal data exchange.

E-government filing portal

For a resident Osaühing (OÜ): incorporation via the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) automatically creates an Estonian Commercial Register code, which functions as the universal tax identification number (TIN); no separate TIN application is required. For foreign legal entities making taxable supplies in Estonia: submit VAT registration application through e-MTA before commencing supplies; the MTA issues an Estonian VAT number (format EE + 9 digits). Foreign businesses without a fixed establishment may be asked to appoint a tax representative; e-Residency digital-ID users may use e-MTA remotely for the entire registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style entity classification election exists. Estonian commercial-law entity type is conclusive for tax classification; the OÜ is a juridical taxpayer under the deferred-profit corporate income tax model (Income Tax Act, §50). The only electivity relates to dividend distribution timing: retained profits are 0%-taxed, while distributions incur 20% gross CIT (with an eligibility-gated reduced 14/86 rate for regular dividends). Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are mandatory and not subject to taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover per calendar year (VAT Act §10); mandatory registration from the month following the threshold breach; voluntary registration available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Estonia zero-rates the supply and export of goods physically leaving the EU/EEA customs territory (VAT Act §19); intra-EU supplies are likewise treated as zero-rated when all conditions are met. For consulting services (ISIC 7020 ESG/carbon auditing and offset brokerage), the B2B place of supply is the customer's country under VAT Act §20; supplies to non-Estonian taxable persons are outside Estonian VAT and the domestic zero-rating/refund mechanism is not engaged. To rely on the export/outside-scope treatment the taxpayer must demonstrate the recipient's VAT status, place of establishment, and the genuine cross-border nature of the transaction.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD) accepted by Estonian MTA customs through the single window ETS
  • Transport document evidencing physical exit from EU: bill of lading, CMR, or air waybill
  • Commercial invoice, underlying contract/PO, and export ledger record
  • Bank payment/credit evidence, e.g. SWIFT wire statement or inward remittance certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance or central invoice stamping applies to ordinary B2B VAT supply. VAT returns and registrations are submitted electronically through e-MTA; taxpayers must transmit cash-register transaction records digitally under the MTA cash-register technical framework. E-invoicing is mandatory for supplies to Estonian public-sector bodies (Peppol/BIS/Estonian e-invoice format); structured invoice files should be retained for MTA digital audits.

MTA automatically matches customs export declarations, e-invoice data, and banking transactions. If export zero-rating is claimed without compliant proof, MTA retroactively burdens the supply with 22% VAT and may deny input VAT recovery, issuing a tax assessment plus daily interest (0.06% per day under the Taxation Act). Non-fraudulent defects attract an incremental penalty of up to 20% of the assessed VAT; deliberate misstatements or forged export papers constitute tax fraud under Penal Code §344, punishable with imprisonment of up to five years, forfeiture, and disqualification, in addition to full VAT clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender; Estonia has been a full Eurozone member since 1 January 2011. The Foreign Exchange Act (Välisvaluutaseadus) imposes no exchange-control, currency-licensing, prior-authorisation, or conversion-quota measures; Estonia is fully liberalised on both the current and the capital account, consistent with the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Article 63 free-movement-of-capital principle and the abolition of exchange restrictions upon EU accession in 2004. EUR receipts and foreign-currency balances (USD, GBP, SEK, NOK, CHF) may be retained, converted, and repatriated without central-bank approval or notification. Profit remittance from an OÜ faces no FX-related obstacle; the sole fiscal restraint is the Estonian distributed-profit corporate income tax under the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus): distributions trigger a company-level tax of 20% on the gross distribution (calculated by the statutory 20/80 method, i.e., 25% of the net dividend), with a preferential 14% rate (14/86 method) for regular dividends meeting the prior-year benchmark. No additional withholding tax is imposed on dividends paid to EU/EEA corporate shareholders qualifying under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, making cross-border profit pull-to-parent frictionless once the distribution tax is settled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender or conversion regime exists in Estonia. There is no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or service fees invoiced in foreign currency; the OÜ may hold USD, GBP, or other currency balances indefinitely in operational accounts, subject only to ordinary balance-of-payments (BPM6) statistical reporting captured by the banks. The only perimeter obligations are (i) the EU Cash Control Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2018/1672) declaration for cash or bearer instruments of EUR 10,000 or more crossing the EU external border, and (ii) standard AML monitoring of inbound trade settlements under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act. For treasury efficiency, the blueprint nonetheless recommends same-week conversion of hard-currency export earnings through a spot FX contract once balances exceed the working-capital buffer, because Estonia provides no rate advantage for delayed conversions.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the euro-area national central bank and member of the Eurosystem / European System of Central Banks. Prudential supervision of credit institutions is exercised jointly with the European Central Bank under the Single Supervisory Mechanism; the national authority competent for AML/KYC enforcement, payment systems, e-money, and investment-firm conduct is the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon), which operates in integrated statistical and supervisory coordination with Eesti Pank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High as an auxiliary rail; Medium as the sole treasury venue. Estonian and other EEA-authorised e-money institutions (EMIs) licensed under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and providing payment services under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer) can legally open accounts for an Estonian OÜ without a physical branch, because PSD2 passporting and the e-Residency digital identity enable fully remote onboarding. No statutory prohibition targets ESG carbon-audit or offset-brokerage merchants, and the low-risk tier of the business (ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541620) makes EMI acceptance probable. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, provide SWIFT-funded trade loans, hold client-money escrow settlement accounts in the same legal capacity as a bank, or offer deposits protected under the Estonian Deposit Guarantee Act. The blueprint therefore adopts a bank-led model with the Tier-1 bank as the core liquidity, SWIFT, and LC provider and a single PSD2 EMI as an overlay for instant SEPA transfers, small-amount multi-currency vendor payouts, and virtual-card disbursement, with prior clearance of EMI internal compliance appetite regarding carbon-offset payment references.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained profits; 20% on distributed profits (effective 20/80 on net distribution)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (no withholding tax on dividends; corporate income tax is levied at the company level on distributions)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 for cross-border controlled transactions per year (local file); Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50 million

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like offshore jurisdictions, but requires genuine economic substance to avoid being considered a tax resident elsewhere and to benefit from tax treaties. The company must have real business activity, employees, and premises in Estonia. Lack of substance may trigger anti-abuse rules and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €1.2 million; imprisonment up to 5 years; additional tax and interest on underpaid amounts

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company Without Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of income as taxable in Estonia; penalties up to 20% of the tax underpaid; potential criminal charges for tax fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties and taxes if they acted in bad faith; possible disqualification from serving as directors.
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1.6 million; imprisonment up to 3 years; suspension of business activities

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans and asset seizures may be applied.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalty of up to 20% of the additional tax assessed; interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they approved or knowingly allowed mispricing; potential criminal liability for tax evasion.
