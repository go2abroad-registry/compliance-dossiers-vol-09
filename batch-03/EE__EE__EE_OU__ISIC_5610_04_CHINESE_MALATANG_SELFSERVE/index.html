Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) with the e-MTA digital service portal (www.emta.ee).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains its Estonian registry code (registrikood) through the Commercial Register at company incorporation or branch registration; the VAT registration number (KMKR) is then applied for electronically through e-MTA. Non-resident incorporators or board members may use the e-Residency digital ID card to sign and submit the application; a fiscal representative is not mandatory for EU/EEA applicants but is recommended for non-EU residents. The MTA issues the VAT ID within five business days and registration becomes compulsory once taxable turnover reaches EUR 50,000 per calendar year or when an intra-Community acquisition threshold is breached.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — An OÜ is a tax-resident company subject to Estonia's deferred corporate income tax regime: undistributed profits are tax-exempt; 20% corporate income tax is levied on gross profit distributions and deemed distributions (fringe benefits, gifts, transfer-pricing adjustments, non-business expenses). Non-resident shareholders face no separate dividend withholding tax at the Estonian level, and foreign-source business income of a resident OÜ is generally taxable only upon distribution.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box classification exists for OÜs. Relevant elections include: (i) VAT group registration for related Estonian entities; (ii) the reduced 14% rate on regular profit distributions made in accordance with Estonian recurring-dividend rules; (iii) the participation exemption for qualifying dividends from subsidiaries (10% holding held for at least two years); (iv) taxation of a non-resident's permanent establishment as a resident company upon election; and (v) formal transfer-pricing documentation elections under the MTA guidelines. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory under ATAD, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 50,000 annual taxable turnover (increased from EUR 40,000 effective 1 January 2025; EUR 35,000 before 1 January 2024). Separate thresholds apply to distance sales and intra-Community acquisitions; restaurant and franchise-licensing services count toward domestic taxable turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies to exports physically dispatched or transported out of the EU and to intra-Community supplies made to a VAT-registered purchaser in another Member State. Services: B2B services supplied to a business customer established outside Estonia fall outside Estonian VAT (place of supply in the recipient's country, reverse charge), including franchise royalties, trademark licences and support services provided to foreign franchisees of the Chinese Malatang brand, provided the recipient's business status is evidenced. Restaurant self-service income generated domestically remains taxable at 22% VAT.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD EX A) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) confirming exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill or airway bill evidencing physical transport outside Estonia/EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or banking records proving receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and evidence of the recipient's business status (valid Estonian or foreign VAT ID)
  • Transport and warehousing documents supporting direct export journeys, including customs transit documentation where applicable

Estonia mandates real-time transmission of cash-register fiscal data to the MTA for B2C cash sales via the X-Road secure data-exchange platform (since 2017). Structured electronic invoicing (e-arve) is standard in B2B and B2G transactions, and phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing is being implemented under the EU ViDA Directive with national digital reporting integrated into the e-MTA interface. VAT returns (KMD) and counterparty-level annexes (KMD INF) are filed exclusively electronically; no physical fiscal stamp or pre-clearance approval of individual invoices is required.

MTA risk-based audits target export zero-rating and franchise-royalty structures; insufficient documentation leads to VAT clawback with default interest of 0.06% per day and penalty surcharges of up to 100% of the unpaid tax. Aggravated VAT fraud by a legal person may trigger fines up to approximately EUR 1.2 million, while responsible natural persons face up to five years' imprisonment under the Estonian Penal Code §403. Unrepatriated export proceeds beyond 180 days create a rebuttable presumption of non-export, shifting the burden of proof to the taxpayer in VAT and customs audit proceedings; fictitious circular exports are reconstructed as domestic taxable supplies with retrospective assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia maintains fully liberalised current- and capital-account convertibility as an EU member state under Article 63 TFEU. There is no exchange-control legislation, no approval or registration requirement for inbound or outbound dividends, royalties, loans, or equity flows, and no restriction on holding EUR or foreign-currency accounts. Profit remittance is structurally unrestricted but priced through Estonia's distributed-profit corporate income tax system: undistributed profits are taxed at 0%; upon distribution the OÜ itself pays the tax (not the non-resident shareholder) at a 20/80 gross-up on ordinary distributions (22/78 from 1 January 2025), with a reduced 14/86 rate (16/84 from 2025) available for dividends declared from profits that were already taxed in Estonia or a treaty jurisdiction. No additional dividend withholding tax is imposed on the non-resident beneficial owner under the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus) section 50.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia abolished all export-proceeds surrender and mandatory conversion obligations upon EU accession in 2004 and in full upon euro adoption on 1 January 2011. Export proceeds, franchise royalties, and master-licence fees may be retained in any currency indefinitely; there is no statutory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. The only residual compliance duty is the Bank of Estonia's balance-of-payments statistical return: a resident OÜ must report cross-border payments and receipts of EUR 12,500 or more per transaction to Eesti Pank on a monthly basis.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is the national central bank and a member of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank (ECB) sets monetary policy, while Eesti Pank is responsible for currency circulation, financial stability, payment-system oversight and balance-of-payments statistics. Prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions is carried out by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) in cooperation with the ECB's Single Supervisory Mechanism; suspicious-transaction analysis is handled by the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium as a liquidity overlay, but legally insufficient as the sole treasury rail. EEA-authorised electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Wise EU, Revolut Bank UAB passporting from Lithuania, Monese EU, Paysera LT) may operate in Estonia under the Payment Institutions and E-Money Institutions Act (Makseteenuste ja e-raha seadus, implementing PSD2) after a passport notification to Finantsinspektsioon. EMIs provide IBANs, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer, virtual cards and, in some cases, SWIFT MT103 routing, but they are legally barred from issuing or confirming commercial letters of credit, from providing committed credit facilities, and from accepting deposits protected by the Estonian Guarantee Fund (Tagatisfond); client funds are safeguarded and segregated rather than deposit-insured. For Stage 4, an EMI is permitted only as a low-value collection rail for franchisee royalty wires below EUR 5,000 per transaction; all capital, trade, FX, and clearing flows must be executed through Eesti Pank-supervised credit institutions.

A statutory review may be required at lower thresholds, generally where at least two tests are met: annual sales revenue of at least EUR 5 million, total assets of at least EUR 2.5 million, and average employees of at least 50.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained profits; 20/80 (20% effective) on profit distributions; preferential 14/86 (14% effective) for regular profit distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

There is no separate dividend withholding tax on the recipient under Estonian domestic law. The economic withholding-equivalent is the OÜ's profit distribution tax: 20/80 of net distributions (20% of gross profit distributed), reduced to 14/86 (14%) for qualifying regular distributions. Non-resident shareholders are not subject to additional Estonian dividend WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The TSD and the related corporate income tax payment are due on the 10th day of the month following the calendar month in which the taxable distribution or payment was made. Example: for a distribution made in March, both form and payment are due by 10 April.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR - A mandatory Local File/Master File is required when the Estonian OÜ belongs to an international group whose consolidated revenue in the preceding financial year equals or exceeds EUR 50 million, and the aggregate value of the Estonian entity's cross-border related-party transactions in the fiscal year equals or exceeds EUR 50,000 per controlled transaction category (goods, services, intangibles, financing). Below this de minimis threshold, simplified compliance applies; a CbC report is separately required for groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: No UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations codified in Estonian national law; however, the Estonian tax administration applies economic-substance principles through the place-of-management test, the anti-abuse provisions of tax treaties, and the EU ATAD/interest-royalties directive safeguards. An e-resident OÜ engaged in Royalty Wire flows must demonstrate bona fide substance (registered office, local management, bank account, personnel or outsourced agents, actual decision-making, and ownership/control of the licensed franchise IP) to avoid recharacterisation as a shell/conduit company and to preserve treaty and e-Residency benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporation Tax Evasion / Undisclosed Distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment of the disguised distribution as a deemed profit distribution, collection of the 20/80 tax (or 14/86 if deliberately mischaracterised), 0.06% daily late interest, and an economic offence fine under Penal Code (Estonian Penal Code §153) of up to 300 daily units or imprisonment for up to 3 years; legal-person fine up to EUR 1,600 for tax misdemeanour and repeated non-declaration penalty up to EUR 2,600.

Director personal liability: A board member who knowingly failed to declare and pay distribution tax can be jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax under the Estonian Tax Code veil-piercing rules, exposed to civil restitution of the undeclared dividend, a travel-ban pending criminal investigation, and disqualification from office in any commercial register of an EEA member state.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse in e-Residency

Statutory sanction: Strikethrough from the Estonian Commercial Register for failure to comply with annual reporting or for unlawful nominee arrangements; denial of treaty benefits and recharacterisation of royalties as income of a foreign permanent establishment; imposition of the full 20/80 distribution tax on the entire concealed base; administrative fines up to EUR 400,000 for misleading register statements; revocation of e-Resident digital ID access.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the OÜ's obligations where the company is a mere alter ego, subject to restitution orders, prohibition on starting or managing a legal entity, travel bans, and criminal liability for false declarations under the Estonian Penal Code if they certified false annual accounts.
