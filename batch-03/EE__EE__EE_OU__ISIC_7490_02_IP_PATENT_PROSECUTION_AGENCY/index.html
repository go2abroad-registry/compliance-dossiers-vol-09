Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Centre of Registers and Information Systems – Estonian e-Business Register (Äriregister)

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik) – §135(1) (legal persons), §137-142 (formation, registration and share capital of private limited company), §144-149 (management board and representation), §15(5) of the General Part of the Civil Code Act (registered seat); digital signing per §9(4) and e-Business Register filing rules under §36 of the Commercial Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Digital incorporation application with annexed Articles of Association (Asutamisotsus / Põhikiri) filed electronically through the e-Business Register portal; no notarisation required when 100% digital filing is used.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Osaühing (OÜ) as the minimum capital requirement has been abolished; if share capital is fixed in the Articles of Association, it must be subscribed at par value and paid either into a bank escrow account or through the e-Business Register digital capital verification service before or at the moment of registration; zero-capital formations are permissible.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the Estonian Rescue Board (Päästeamet) confirming compliance with the Fire Safety Act (Tuleohutuse seadus), including fire extinguishers, exit routes and building evacuation plans; structural load inspection of the premises carried out by a licensed surveyor to confirm safe occupancy; workplace clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus) recorded through a documented occupational health risk assessment and, where required, written work environment authorisation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Osaühing (OÜ) as the minimum capital requirement has been abolished; if share capital is fixed in the Articles of Association, it must be subscribed at par value and paid either into a bank escrow account or through the e-Business Register digital capital verification service before or at the moment of registration; zero-capital formations are permissible.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); primary portals: e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee) and the e-Business Register (ariregister.rik.ee); the 8-digit Commercial Register registry code functions as the corporate taxpayer identification number; the VAT registration number is assigned in the format EE100xxxxxx.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the OÜ, either with a notary or fully online through the e-Business Register using e-Residency/X-Road, to obtain the registry code that is automatically transmitted to MTA. 2. Use the registry code as the TIN for all Estonian tax filings; obtain an e-MTA authentication credential via Estonian ID-card/e-Residency digital signature, or appoint a management board member who holds such a credential. 3. File the VAT registration application in the e-MTA VAT module once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 per calendar year, or immediately where the foreign entity is not established in Estonia but commences taxable activities in Estonia and the reverse charge does not shift the obligation; MTA issues the EE-prefixed VAT number. 4. Register as an employer (income tax withholding, social tax, unemployment insurance, funded pension codes) before paying remuneration to board members or employees. The full procedure is paperless and does not require physical presence in Estonia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (deferred distribution-based CIT): the OÜ is a resident legal entity and is never fiscally transparent; undistributed and retained profits are taxed at 0%; the taxable event is profit distribution, including dividends, deemed distributions and non-business expenses, taxed at 20/80 of the net distributed amount (20% of the gross distribution); qualifying intra-group dividends and foreign-source dividends may be exempt where participation and holding conditions are met; Estonia applies territorial sourcing to distributions in the sense that only distributions are taxed, not accrued worldwide profits.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists; classification of the OÜ as an entity-level taxpayer is mandatory. Available statutory elections under the Estonian Income Tax Act and VAT Act: (i) the reduced 14/86 rate for regular profit distributions (regular-dividend regime, historically available under section 50(2), with conditions and potential phase-out to be verified with MTA for the current tax year); (ii) VAT group registration under section 8 of the VAT Act; (iii) elections as to the order and classification of in-kind distributions; (iv) hybrid mismatch treatment is mandatory under ATAD transposition, with automatic disallowance of deductions attributable to hybrid financial instruments, hybrid entities or permanent establishment mismatches; no elective opt-out is available for the OÜ.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable supplies for an Estonian-established taxable person; non-established foreign suppliers are required to register with no turnover threshold before making taxable supplies in Estonia unless reverse charge applies; intra-Community distance sales of goods and supplies of electronic services to final consumers may use the Union/Non-Union OSS, with the EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance-sales threshold applying across the EU-wide OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies where goods are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or on its behalf out of the EU customs territory, evidenced by a completed electronic customs export declaration and transport documentation. Services: patent prosecution, PCT filing, trademark registration and related IP agency services supplied to a B2B customer in another country, whether EU or third-country, fall outside the place of supply in Estonia under the customer-jurisdiction rule; services supplied to non-business customers outside the EU likewise have their place of supply outside Estonia because the services relate to intellectual property and are received abroad. Accordingly, no Estonian VAT accrues and no domestic zero-rating certificate is required, provided the supplier retains contemporaneous evidence of the customer's country of establishment or residence and of the offshore economic use of the service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-A form; electronic export declaration filed through ECS/EMCS with Movement Reference Number for EU-context movement)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (primary transport evidence of the physical removal of goods from EU customs territory; CMR accompanying road transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate/SWIFT payment evidence: bank statement or SWIFT MT103 showing receipt of payment from the third-country buyer or offshore client
  • Destination-country customs entry/electronic import record or delivery acceptance document corroborating arrival and third-country receipt of exported goods
  • Service engagement contract, patent mandate and invoice corpus evidencing that the place of benefit and economic use of the IP prosecution service is outside Estonia

Estonia has enacted a phased B2B e-invoicing mandate under the Accounting Act: structured e-invoices in EN 16931/Estonian evS format are required for domestic B2B transactions, with statutory implementation dates initially set from 1 July 2024 and subsequently postponed by amendment; the operative compliance date must be confirmed with MTA before software adoption. Estonia does not operate real-time government clearance, tax-authority API validation or digital stamping of invoices; MTA applies post-filing risk analytics and requires retention of structured invoice files for 7 years. Real-time transmission of cash-register transaction data through certified fiscal devices applies to retail, hospitality and taxi services only, and has no application to the patent-prosecution services model.

MTA systematically cross-matches zero-rated and outside-scope claims against customs export declarations, foreign-currency receipts and client-jurisdiction data; failure to substantiate the export documentation bundle triggers VAT reassessment at 22% plus surcharge interest of 0.06% per calendar day on the overdue amount; administrative fines of up to EUR 1,300 per violation apply to inaccurate VAT returns; deliberately fraudulent zero-rating or false export declarations constitute a criminal offence under section 394 of the Estonian Penal Code punishable by fine or up to 3 years' imprisonment, with forfeiture applicable to corporate offenders; retrospective clawback covers the general three-year tax-assessment limitation period, extended to seven years in cases of intentional tax evasion; all export documentation must be retained for at least seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility in both current and capital accounts. Estonia has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations and abolished exchange controls upon euro adoption in 2011. There are no central-bank permissions, no advance licensing, no export/import payment controls, and no restrictions on profit remittance. The only fiscal impingement on outbound profit is Estonia's unique distributed-profits tax regime: an OÜ pays corporate income tax only upon distribution (default 20/80 = 25%% effective on net distribution, reduced to 14/86 and 20% on regular dividends under the Estonian Income Tax Act), with treaty-based relief available to non-resident shareholders; this is a tax matter, not an FX or capital-control barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None in force. Estonia imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion quota, and no surrender ratio for export or service-proceeds receipts. Invoiced patent-prosecution and trademark-registration fee income may be retained in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency in accounts held at EU/EEA authorized credit institutions or EU-authorized e-money institutions passported into Estonia. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting to Eesti Pank may apply for regulatory sampling, with no transactional approval requirement.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank (ECB) is the monetary and foreign-exchange authority for the euro. The Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) supervises banks, e-money institutions, and payment institutions for AML/CFT. The free movement of capital is guaranteed under TFEU Articles 63-65 and implemented through EU regulations; Estonia maintains no separate foreign-exchange control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for outbound multicurrency disbursements to patent offices. Wise (EU e-money institution, passported into Estonia) is the preferred EMI for WIPO (CHF/EUR), USPTO (USD), and national-office fee payments because it offers local-currency clearing, mid-market FX conversion, and multi-currency balances. Legal constraints: under PSD2 and the Estonian E-Money Institutions Act, an EMI cannot accept deposits or grant credit, must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts at a credit institution, and remains subject to Estonian AML/CFT oversight; an EMI account is not a substitute for a domestic bank account where statutory escrow, multi-bank credit facilities, or a full account-holding relationship is contractually required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection from a foreign parent or shareholder: route via SWIFT MT103 in EUR, quoting the share-capital increase decision registered in the Estonian e-Business Register; use ISO 20022/ECB purpose code 'EQUI' or 'COMC' with the registry docket number as reference; the receiving bank verifies the capital increase and credits the account without Eesti Pank approval. Inbound client payments for patent prosecution services: use SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) or SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'GDDS' (Goods and Services) or 'INTC' (Intellectual Property fees); each payment must carry the invoice number and PCT/patent/trademark docket reference for automatic reconciliation in the firm's fee ledger. For foreign-currency client receipts (USD, CHF, GBP), the payee account must be a multicurrency account at an authorized credit institution or an EU/EEA EMI passported into Estonia; no mandatory conversion applies and no declaration is required.Clearing rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer for all EUR-denominated official fees to EU intellectual-property offices (EPO Munich, EUIPO Alicante, and national patent offices), 1-2 business days with no SWIFT charges; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR disbursements to WIPO (CHF or EUR), USPTO (USD), and other national offices, with the official docket number in the remittance information; (3) closed-loop institutional rails for high-frequency renewals via EPO deposit accounts and WIPO current accounts, enabling net-settled debits; (4) Wise Business multi-currency clearing for smaller non-EUR fee payments, executing local-currency settlement at mid-market rates and avoiding correspondent-bank double charges. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the company's distributed profit, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution, applicable to profit distributions made from 1 January 2025. Retained and reinvested profits are not subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the time of earning.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on ordinary dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ to a non-resident recipient under the current 22/78 distributed-profit regime. The Estonian company-level corporate income tax of 22/78 of the net distribution remains payable. Recipient-country taxation, treaty residence taxation, beneficial-ownership rules, anti-abuse provisions, and special transitional or reduced-rate rules must be separately reviewed.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The TSD and corresponding tax payments are generally due by the 10th day of the calendar month following the month of payment or other taxable event. The payment date, not merely the accounting recognition date, generally determines the reporting month for employment payments, benefits, dividends, and similar distributions. The annual report is separate from the TSD and is generally filed with the Commercial Register within six months after the end of the financial year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No universal transaction-value threshold automatically eliminates arm's-length obligations for a small Estonian OÜ. Estonia's full transfer-pricing documentation regime principally applies to specified large taxpayers and regulated entities, including resident companies meeting size criteria commonly involving at least 250 employees, annual turnover of at least EUR 50 million, or balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43 million, as well as certain financial institutions and listed entities. Local File and Master File requirements must be assessed under the applicable regulation and group circumstances. Even where formal documentation thresholds are not met, related-party transactions must be arm's length and sufficiently supported. The relevant local currency is EUR.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime. Nevertheless, Estonian and EU anti-abuse rules require genuine commercial substance and permit denial of tax benefits for arrangements lacking valid commercial reasons or economic reality. The OÜ should maintain Estonia-appropriate governance, decision-making, accounting, banking controls, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or documented working arrangements, business records, client contracts, patent-prosecution expertise, and risk control. The general anti-avoidance rule, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, hybrid mismatch rules, beneficial-ownership principles, treaty principal-purpose-test provisions, and transfer-pricing rules may apply. E-residency alone is not tax residence, management substance, or evidence of effective management in Estonia.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment for unpaid tax, statutory interest, compulsory collection, administrative fines, and potential criminal liability where the conduct and tax damage meet the criminal-law threshold. The legal entity may receive a substantial criminal fine; responsible individuals may face a fine or imprisonment depending on the offence and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual decision-makers may incur personal tax liability for deliberate or grossly negligent failure to pay tax where statutory conditions for management-board liability are met. Civil restitution, asset enforcement, disqualification from management, and criminal proceedings may follow. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty but may arise from judicial or enforcement measures in a criminal or collection proceeding.
Undeclared Permanent Establishment or Place of Effective Management

Statutory sanction: Foreign-country corporate income tax, payroll tax, VAT, interest, penalties, registration obligations, and possible dual-residence or treaty dispute. Estonia may also challenge the asserted management and residence position where records do not support it.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, tax recovery proceedings, management disqualification, and criminal exposure for intentional false statements or evasion. Personal liability is fact-dependent and is not created solely by ownership or directorship.
Transfer Pricing and Related-Party Abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to an arm's-length result, additional corporate income tax or withholding tax where applicable, interest, penalties, documentation sanctions, and possible denial of treaty or participation exemptions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for intentional or grossly negligent tax loss, breach of fiduciary duties, and insolvency-related creditor damage. Criminal exposure may arise if documentation is fabricated or the pricing is part of deliberate tax evasion.
AML, Sanctions, and Beneficial-Ownership Breaches

Statutory sanction: Supervisory measures, business restrictions, administrative fines, confiscation or freezing of funds, reporting obligations, and criminal sanctions for money laundering, sanctions evasion, or knowingly assisting unlawful transactions. Payment providers may suspend or terminate accounts.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and employees may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, imprisonment for serious intentional conduct, asset confiscation, and management restrictions. Travel bans may be imposed by a competent authority or court in an applicable criminal or enforcement proceeding, but are not automatic.
Unauthorized Patent or Legal Representation

Statutory sanction: Rejection or loss of procedural rights, professional or administrative sanctions, civil liability for client losses, contract and consumer claims, and potential criminal liability for fraud, forgery, or unauthorized professional activity where statutory elements are met.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible professionals may face personal civil damages, professional prohibition, administrative fines, and criminal liability for deliberate fraud or forged documents. The OÜ's limited liability does not protect intentional torts, fraud, criminal conduct, or personal professional negligence.
Improper Profit Repatriation and Corporate Asset Extraction

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawful distributions, corporate income tax and interest on deemed distributions, creditor claims, insolvency clawback, accounting penalties, and possible criminal liability for asset diversion or creditor prejudice.

Director personal liability: Board members may be jointly or personally liable for unlawful distributions, insolvency-related damage, breach of the duty of care, and failure to file for insolvency when required. Civil restitution, asset enforcement, management disqualification, and imprisonment may apply for intentional fraudulent conduct.
VAT and Client-Money Misclassification

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, interest, penalties, invoice correction requirements, denial of input VAT, repayment of improperly claimed refunds, and possible fraud or embezzlement proceedings for intentional misuse of client funds.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for unpaid VAT and client-fund losses where statutory management-board liability or personal wrongdoing is established. Criminal liability may arise from deliberate VAT fraud, accounting falsification, or misappropriation.
