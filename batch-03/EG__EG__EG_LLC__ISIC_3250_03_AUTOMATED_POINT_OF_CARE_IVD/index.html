Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; electronic services and filing through the unified national tax portal at https://www.incompta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

SOP for a foreign investor LLC: (1) complete incorporation at the Commercial Registry via the GAFI one-stop-shop and notarize the articles of association and statutory books; (2) open the corporate tax file at the competent ETA office and obtain the 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN), filing online through the national tax portal (incompta.gov.eg) or the GAFI investor window; (3) obtain the tax card and, if taxable turnover exceeds the VAT threshold, file simultaneously for VAT registration with the ETA; (4) enroll in the ETA e-invoice and e-receipt system with ERP/FA API integration and fiscal credentials; (5) maintain continuous updates of commercial registry, customs registration, and foreign-currency account data. A non-resident entity with no Egyptian permanent establishment may use the ETA non-resident registration route for VAT and e-invoicing purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: EGP 500,000 per annum of taxable supplies of goods; EGP 150,000 per annum of taxable supplies of services (per Executive Regulations). Voluntary registration is available below these amounts. Non-resident digital suppliers of services to Egyptian consumers may be required to register and charge VAT regardless of local turnover thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for 0% VAT only if the goods physically leave Egyptian customs territory within the contractual shipment period, the export is evidenced by customs declarations, and the foreign-currency sale proceeds are received and repatriated through an Egyptian licensed bank within the Central Bank of Egypt deadline. Offshore services, including after-sales support, calibration, servicing of point-of-care IVD analyzers, and remote medical-data services provided to a non-resident recipient whose benefit arises outside Egypt, are also zero-rated under Article 19 of the VAT Law and related Executive Regulations when the recipient is not connected to the exporter and payment is in foreign currency. The exporter must clearly code the e-invoice as zero-rated in the ETA e-invoicing system.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Egyptian Customs Authority single administrative data set) stamped and date-stamped for the export shipment
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the IVD goods from Egyptian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt issued by an Egyptian licensed bank confirming receipt of the foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and foreign sales contract (FOB/CIF) identical to the customs declaration and ETA-cleared e-invoice
  • VAT return export schedule and the ETA-cleared zero-rated e-invoice with QR code and fiscal stamp

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing (and e-receipts for B2C retail) integrated with the ETA fiscal clearance platform. Each invoice must be created in the ETA XML/JSON schema, digitally signed, submitted in real time through the ETA REST API, and cleared and validated by the ETA before the QR fiscal stamp is generated. Non-cleared invoices are treated as unissued. Penalties apply per unlawful invoice, and systematic failure may result in fines up to EGP 50,000 and operational restrictions. The phased mandate applies to taxpayers above turnover thresholds, including manufacturers and distributors of IVD analyzers and microfluidic cartridges.

The ETA audits export zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs export declarations, bank FX-receipt records, and cleared e-invoices. If zero-rating is not fully substantiated or is fraudulent, the ETA reassesses output VAT at 14% with late-payment interest, imposes administrative fines up to EGP 50,000 per irregular e-invoice, and, in cases of forged documents or collusion, applies criminal penalties under the VAT Law (imprisonment up to 5 years and fines of up to three times the tax evaded) and complementary Customs Law penalties. Any VAT refund already released is subject to full clawback plus interest and further sanctions, and the exporter may be suspended from future zero-rating clearance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt is an IMF Article VIII member state and maintains formal current-account convertibility: dividends, profit remittances, interest, royalties and trade payments may in principle be transferred freely through licensed banks without pre-approval, provided the bank's documentary underwriting is satisfied. The capital account, by contrast, remains bank-intermediated and subject to CBE prudential instruments. Since the 6 March 2025 CBE reform package (IMF Extended Fund Facility program), the Egyptian pound is managed under a flexible, market-driven regime: onshore and offshore USD/EGP rates have been unified (approximately EGP 49–50 per USD as of March 2025), all FX purchase caps and advance-import payment restrictions were abolished, and bank FX backlogs for dividends and supplier repayments were cleared. Practical profit-repatriation hurdles are nonetheless documentation-driven: the remitting bank requires (i) audited financial statements prepared under Egyptian Accounting Standards, (ii) a board resolution declaring distributable profits and complying with Law 159 of 1981 (LLC) reserve rules, (iii) proof of payment of the applicable 5–10% dividend withholding tax together with a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority, (iv) the CBE inward-transfer declaration evidencing the original capital entry, and (v) an AML source-of-funds statement. Foreign direct capital may be injected in USD, EUR or EGP and retained in multi-currency accounts; no pre-approved conversion license is needed for manufacturing within the ordinary regime, but the EG_LLC should still register every inward and outward transfer with GAFI within the statutory filing window to protect future exit transferability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE circulars and Law 194 of 2020, a resident manufacturing exporter must receive 100 percent of its export proceeds ('export proceeds realization') in an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the date of the export customs declaration / bill-of-lading date, through the same licensed bank handling the export documentation and GOEIC registration. There is no mandatory percentage conversion into EGP under the current liberalized regime: proceeds may remain in foreign currency accounts, be sold at the interbank rate, or be used for approved import and treasury payments; the historical percentage surrender requirement (50% of proceeds for the banking system) was abolished with the 2018–2019 CBE relaxation and the 2025 floating-rate package. CBE nevertheless retains emergency reserve powers to reinstate mandatory surrender in a balance-of-payments crisis. For EG_LLC, compliance architecture should include: matching each export shipment's CAD/LC/transfer to the 180-day receipt; maintaining the GOEIC exporter registration and the Egyptian Drug Authority (EDA) export license for IVD analyzers and cartridges; and documenting the EDA/ISO 13485 conformity certificates to avoid customs quarantines that delay proceeds. Failure to realize proceeds within 180 days exposes the exporter to fines, exclusion from the importer/exporter registry, and FX facility suspension.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 promulgating the Central Bank and Banking System Law and the Egyptian Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019. CBE holds exclusive authority over banking licensing, foreign-exchange (FX) licensing and regulation, foreign-transfer authorizations, payment and clearing system supervision, and export receipts monitoring; the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the General Organization for Export and Import Control (GOEIC) apply trade and investment overlay controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM (restricted). Egypt's banking architecture is institutionally closed to offshore non-bank flight rails: CBE licenses domestic Electronic Money Institutions under the Payment Systems Law and its bylaws, but those licenses do not permit cross-border banking, export-proceeds realization, or EGP/FX settlement for companies. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer or MultiPass cannot legally serve EG_LLC as the primary cash-management bank for export proceeds or import disbursements because CBE requires all foreign-currency transfers related to trade to pass through a licensed Egyptian bank with an economic nexus file. They are permitted only as (a) a sub-channel for petty-cash split payments, (b) a treasury overlay for vendor prepayments where the Egyptian licensed bank issues back-to-back payment instructions, or (c) a collection mechanism for international distributor card payments via a bank-issued gateway; in all cases, presence with a local licensed bank is compulsory, and the exporter's proceeds-realization test runs through the licensed bank. Foreign EMIs can therefore never be the compliant treasury spine for this entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For EG_LLC (ISIC 3250) capital injection from the foreign shareholder, routing must be executed solely via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the licensed Egyptian bank's correspondent or nostro account. The purpose-of-payment line must cite the CBE direct-investment purpose code for capital subscription, and the EG_LLC account must be provisioned as a capital deposit/escrow account until GAFI registration confirmation. Bank verification steps: (a) source-of-funds letter with supporting shareholder bank statements; (b) board resolution allotting shares to the non-resident; (c) the Egyptian bank will issue a certificate of inward transfer and a CBE record-of-investment entry, which is essential for future profit repatriation and capital exit; (d) capital may be retained in USD/EUR or converted to EGP at the 2025 free-market rate without CBE pre-authorization; (e) upon completion of commercial registry formation, the escrow account converts automatically into the operational account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM (restricted). Egypt's banking architecture is institutionally closed to offshore non-bank flight rails: CBE licenses domestic Electronic Money Institutions under the Payment Systems Law and its bylaws, but those licenses do not permit cross-border banking, export-proceeds realization, or EGP/FX settlement for companies. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer or MultiPass cannot legally serve EG_LLC as the primary cash-management bank for export proceeds or import disbursements because CBE requires all foreign-currency transfers related to trade to pass through a licensed Egyptian bank with an economic nexus file. They are permitted only as (a) a sub-channel for petty-cash split payments, (b) a treasury overlay for vendor prepayments where the Egyptian licensed bank issues back-to-back payment instructions, or (c) a collection mechanism for international distributor card payments via a bank-issued gateway; in all cases, presence with a local licensed bank is compulsory, and the exporter's proceeds-realization test runs through the licensed bank. Foreign EMIs can therefore never be the compliant treasury spine for this entity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions in a given fiscal year (applies to the Egyptian LLC). If the threshold is met, both a Local File and Master File must be prepared within one month of the tax filing deadline and submitted to the ETA upon request.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute equivalent to the UAE regime. Nevertheless, the general anti-avoidance provisions in Law 91 of 2005 and the Egyptian transfer pricing rules require that the Egyptian LLC demonstrates real operational substance—employees, premises, manufacturing capacity, and decision-making—in Egypt. A shell or letterbox company lacking economic substance will be denied treaty benefits and subject to tax recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of not less than 3 months and up to 5 years, a fine ranging from EGP 10,000 to 25% of the evaded tax obligation, and the doubling of the assessed tax under Article 133 of Law 91 of 2005. The Egyptian Tax Authority may also have the taxpayer's bank accounts frozen pending investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or fail to prevent the evasion may be personally jointly liable for the unpaid tax and fines, subject to a personal asset freeze, an international travel ban, and in aggravated cases imprisonment. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is found to be an instrument of fraud.
Shell / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA can recharacterize the entire revenue stream as taxable in Egypt, apply a 50% adjustment penalty over the assessed tax, and deny DTA benefits. The Commercial Registry may deregister the entity under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, and the anti-abuse provisions permit a retrospective tax assessment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the company's tax deficiencies where they stripped assets, diverted contracts, or operated the entity without genuine business purpose. Directors face disqualification from serving in any Egyptian company and possible criminal sanctions under the penalty provisions of the Investment Law.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80 of 2002 (AML) and its implementing regulations, the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Unit can impose fines up to EGP 5,000,000 and suspend the commercial license; repeat offenders may face permanent revocation of the IVD manufacturing license.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for AML compliance failures; where knowledge of a suspicious transaction is established, directors may be charged criminally, subject to asset seizure, and face imprisonment of up to 7 years. Travel bans may be imposed during the criminal investigation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust the taxable income using the arm's length standard, impose a 25% fixed penalty on the TP adjustment amount, and add late payment interest of 2% per month. The adjustment is not deductible and may also disqualify the entity from tax treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a flawed TP declaration or fail to respond to an ETA TP directive may be jointly liable for the additional corporate tax and fines. In egregious cases of TP-driven profit stripping, the Commercial Prosecutor may initiate criminal proceedings for tax evasion, leading to imprisonment and confiscation of the financial benefit.
