Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - AKA Al-Maslahah Al-Daribiyah Al-Misriyah (الهيئة المصرية العامة للضرائب); official portal www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipts platform www.einvoice-tax.gov.eg; customs single-window NAFIZA (www.nafiza.gov.eg). Large-scale EPC contractors are generally administered by the ETA Large Taxpayer Center (Cairo).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned EG LLC must obtain (1) a unified 9-digit Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) reflected on the 'Tax Card' (Kard Daribia) and (2) a separate VAT registration number. Procedure: (a) incorporate the LLC at the Commercial Registry via GAFI one-stop-shop and complete social insurance registration; (b) apply for the Tax Card at the competent ETA tax office attaching the certificate of incorporation, notarized by-laws and board resolutions, power of attorney of the local general manager, Egyptian premises lease/title, passports of directors/shareholders, and the company ledger; the statutory application window is up to 30 days; (c) upon annual taxable supplies exceeding EGP 500,000 (or voluntarily at formation, or immediately upon a taxable import), submit the VAT registration application to the ETA VAT desk within 30 days of the threshold; (d) activate the ETA e-invoice 'taxpayer wallet' and obtain the qualified e-invoice certificate/signature. A non-resident entity without a fixed establishment cannot hold an Egyptian VAT registration in its own name; the EG LLC is the local tax registrant for the EPC project.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the EG LLC is a fully taxable juridical person with no fiscal transparency and no pass-through eligibility; resident Egyptian companies are taxed on worldwide income (Law 91/2005 Art. 2) at the standard 22.5% CIT rate. A foreign parent maintaining only a branch/PE in Egypt is taxed on Egyptian-source income, with a deemed-profit regime of 15% of annual contract receipts for foreign contractors. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the EG LLC is a fully taxable juridical person with no fiscal transparency and no pass-through eligibility; resident Egyptian companies are taxed on worldwide income (Law 91/2005 Art. 2) at the standard 22.5% CIT rate. A foreign parent maintaining only a branch/PE in Egypt is taxed on Egyptian-source income, with a deemed-profit regime of 15% of annual contract receipts for foreign contractors. The entity does not benefit from the Tax_Exempt_Zone default; Free Zone / Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) exemptions under Law 83/2002 and Law 7/2015 are elective locational privileges, not ordinary LLC classifications.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box and no entity-classification election exists under Egyptian law; hybrid mismatch recharacterization of the LLC is rejected absent an express treaty provision. Statutory elections available in computing tax liability include: (i) depreciation method election (straight-line at ministerial rates or declining balance) per asset pool; (ii) fiscal year election only with Minister of Finance approval (statutory year is the calendar year); (iii) application of the 85% participation exemption for dividends/capital gains from qualifying shareholdings of at least 25% held for at least 2 years versus full inclusion; (iv) ordinary foreign tax credit (per-country credit limit) instead of deduction; (v) loss carryforward of up to 5 years (no carryback) subject to ownership continuity; and (vi) treaty relief via Egypt double-tax agreements. There is no group/consolidated filing election for related Egyptian LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods/services under Article 15 of Law 67/2016; registration is mandatory within 30 days of the threshold being exceeded. Importation of goods into Egypt for use in a taxable economic activity also triggers VAT registration irrespective of the threshold. EPC contractors performing taxable construction supplies in Egypt are registrants even if the principal is non-resident.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 6(1) of Law 67/2016, exported goods are zero-rated when they physically leave Egypt under a customs export declaration. Under Article 6(2), services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident party and used, exploited, and consumed entirely outside Egypt; this covers offshore design/engineering and foreign procurement components of a datacenter EPC. Services connected to Egyptian real estate - on-site civil, electro-mechanical, and cooling system installation - are treated as Egyptian-location supplies taxable at 14% even when the principal is foreign; zero-rating is restricted to the offshore-scope deliverables. Additional ETA conditions for 0% VAT: registered exporter status, receipt of the consideration in convertible foreign currency, repatriation of proceeds to Egypt through the banking system, issuance of an ETA-validated export e-invoice with the zero-rated code, and timely filing of the VAT return.

  • NAFIZA customs export declaration (Cargo Export Declaration) reflecting the zero-rated export entry and confirming the exporter TIN
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from Egyptian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by the Egyptian bank proving receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Foreign currency export proceeds statement/contract (CBE export proceeds form) with the bank's repatriation stamp
  • ETA-approved zero-rated export e-invoice carrying the validation UUID and QR stamp
  • EPC contract, purchase orders, work completion and acceptance certificates, and, for exported services, the foreign client's certificate of offshore use/consumption
  • Supporting input-VAT documentation (purchase ledger, supplier e-invoices, and customs import receipts) tracing inputs to the exported scope

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance for all VAT-registered entities through the ETA e-invoicing platform (national rollout phased from 15 November 2020 to 31 May 2023). The supplier submits each tax invoice in XML/JSON via REST/SOAP API or the web portal; the ETA validates against the registered counterparty master data, tariff/tax codes, and supply logic in near-real time, then returns a validation decision. Only cleared invoices bear the mandatory QR stamp/unique validation UUID and are legally recognized for input VAT deduction and customs clearance; buyers must perform electronic confirmation. E-receipts (POS digital receipts) are additionally mandatory for B2C transactions from January 2023. Non-compliance penalty is EGP 50,000 per violation under Decision 420/2020; the ETA may suspend the taxpayer's e-invoice wallet and disallow input credits.

The ETA may audit export zero-rating within the general 5-year assessment window (extended to 6 years in cases of fraud). Failure to substantiate export status or discovery of fictitious/circular exports results in: (i) retrospective reversal of the 0% rate to 14% VAT on the full export value, with clawback of all input VAT credits claimed and assessed interest at the Central Bank of Egypt credit rate plus 2% per month; (ii) under Article 46 of VAT Law 67/2016, intentional evasion carries imprisonment of 5 to 7 years and a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 in addition to full tax repayment; (iii) false customs declarations attract smuggling penalties under Customs Law 207/2020, including forfeiture and fines of not less than the evaded duties; and (iv) non-repatriation of export proceeds beyond 180 days violates Foreign Exchange Law 194/2020 and the CBE export-proceeds regulations, exposing the exporter to monetary sanctions, disqualification from export incentives, and potential criminal liability for exchange-law violations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction and maintains current-account convertibility for bona fide trade, services, dividends, and branch remittances once supported by underlying documentation and tax clearance. Capital-account transactions, including shareholder loans, liquidation proceeds, portfolio transfers, and repatriation of invested capital, remain subject to documentary review, CBE classification, GAFI registration, and sourcing checks. Profit remittance is generally lawful, but banks must verify audited financial statements, corporate tax receipts, beneficial ownership, and the absence of pending AML findings. There is no statutory guarantee of free convertibility for speculative or unregistered capital flows; resident and non-resident entities must settle through CBE-licensed banks and licensed exchange houses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE rules, export proceeds must be repatriated through an Egyptian authorized bank and credited to a foreign-currency or EGP account within the statutory period reflected in CBE instructions, generally not exceeding 180 days from the date of shipment or completion of the EPC installation certificate, unless an approved extension is obtained. The mandatory conversion ratio is applied at the CBE market rate and is currently 100% for general export receivables, unless the CBE grants a case-by-case exemption permitting the retention of foreign currency for documented import or debt-service obligations. For datacenter electromechanical EPC services, the engineer's progress certificate, customs export declaration, and the underlying contract must be lodged with the receiving bank so that the proceeds are tracked against the original invoiced amount.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking Sector Law No. 194 of 2020, together with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) for capital registration and the Foreign Trade Facilitation Authority for export/import trade documentation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Moderate for core treasury operations. Offshore fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized by the CBE as authorized banks for EGP clearing, RTGS participation, or capital-account FX conversion. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection tools for smaller trade advances or cross-border overhead payments, provided the substantive settlement, tax remittance, payroll, and profit repatriation are completed through a CBE-licensed tier-1 bank. Legal constraints include the prohibition on maintaining EGP nostro accounts offshore, CBE approval for any foreign-currency account, and heightened AML/CFT scrutiny over funds routed through non-bank payment providers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound direct investment funds from the foreign parent must be wired to the Egyptian LLC's foreign-currency capital account at a CBE-licensed bank using SWIFT MT103, with the remittance instruction clearly coded as 'Direct Investment – Share Subscription / Capital Contribution' under the CBE's designated purpose-code framework. The receiving bank will prepare the inward remittance certificate and GAFI investment registration file; the funds may then be converted into EGP through the interbank market at the prevailing CBE rate. The transaction must be supported by the LLC's certificate of incorporation, board/shareholder resolution authorizing the increase or payment of capital, a statement of the EPC project purpose, and the foreign parent's audited financial statements. Banks must verify that the funds do not represent third-party financing disguised as equity and that no portion is attributable to unnamed beneficial owners.Outbound payments for imported cooling systems, electrical equipment, and cross-border subcontractor services must be made through CBE-licensed banks using SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments, MT202 for bank-to-bank settlement, or commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) opened under an LC facility. LC issuance requires the underlying import contract, proforma invoice, payment terms, and, where applicable, prior CBE or trade authority approval. Large EGP disbursements to local subcontractors are routed through the CBE RTGS/EGP clearing system; USD liquidity for import LCs is allocated by the bank according to CBE priorities, and disbursements must be supported by customs releases or verified shipping documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Moderate for core treasury operations. Offshore fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized by the CBE as authorized banks for EGP clearing, RTGS participation, or capital-account FX conversion. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection tools for smaller trade advances or cross-border overhead payments, provided the substantive settlement, tax remittance, payroll, and profit repatriation are completed through a CBE-licensed tier-1 bank. Legal constraints include the prohibition on maintaining EGP nostro accounts offshore, CBE approval for any foreign-currency account, and heightened AML/CFT scrutiny over funds routed through non-bank payment providers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions in a fiscal year; or EGP 5,000,000 for controlled services and/or intangible-related transactions; where either threshold is crossed, contemporaneous Transfer Pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) must be prepared in Arabic and English and filed together with Form 100.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute (as in offshore Caribbean jurisdictions), but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 72 of Law No. 82/2016 denies tax benefits from arrangements with wholly artificial transactions lacking genuine economic purpose, personnel, premises, and local risk control. A shell Egyptian LLC used to pass EPC contracts through a low-tax affiliate without Egyptian office, staff, equipment, or procurement function will be disregarded for tax purposes; the ETA may re-characterize the income as the parent's business profit and levy 22.5% corporate tax plus 2.5% monthly delay surcharges. For DSAR / CRS transparency, the LLC's economic substance declaration is a condition for obtaining a resident-trust/corporate certificate and for applying DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for 3–5 years and a fine equal to 2 to 5 times the tax evaded (Art. 133, Tax Procedures Law 82/2016). In addition, the ETA applies a 2.5% monthly late-payment surcharge and interest, and may impose a civil penalty up to EGP 2 million for TP documentation infractions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations, approve fabricated invoices, or fail to exercise supervisory functions are held jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, in addition to penal sanctions; Egyptian courts may issue a travel ban and an asset freeze against directors, and a block of repatriation of funds until a final tax clearance is issued.
Shell Activity / Artificial Economic Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: The GAAR applies to re-characterize the LLC as a sham: corporate tax is recalculated on the full Egyptian-source profit at 22.5% plus late-payment monthly surcharges and a penalty of 3% of the assessed tax. The Ministry of Finance may also void the DTA residency certificate; criminal prosecution for tax evasion could follow if false declarations were used.

Director personal liability: Directors of the foreign parent and of the Egyptian LLC who caused the shell structure may be jointly liable for the restated tax; travel bans and entry bans to Egypt are available for foreign directors under the ETA's collection measures. For AML/CFT, a shell structure is also a red flag that may expose directors to asset confiscation proceedings under Law 78/2016 on Anti-Money Laundering.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Undocumented Controlled Transactions

Statutory sanction: ETA reassessment at arm's length methods (CUP, RPM, CPLM, TNMM, PSM) plus a monthly 2.5% delay surcharge; separate penalty up to EGP 2,000,000 for incomplete or non-contemporaneous TP documentation; a further 2% penalty for erroneous tax return accuracy. If the mis-pricing is held to be intentional evasion, the criminal penalties under Law 82/2016 apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the intercompany pricing or failed to ensure documentation requirements may be personally liable for the additional corporate tax, and may be charged with tax evasion if the TP manipulation was intentional and concealed; professional indemnity claims and disqualification from holding EPC public-sector contracts can also arise.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Terrorist Financing Exposure in EPC Payments

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering Law (Law No. 93/2018) imposes imprisonment from 1 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 200,000 to EGP 1,000,000; the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit (EFIU) can issue freezing orders without judicial review. The ETA may also treat laundered funds as deemed income and levy 22.5% tax plus surcharges; the contract with a government-related entity is automatically suspended on a red flag.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal prosecution for failure to establish AML compliance functions if the LLC is caught accepting unexplained third-party payments or laundering proceeds in construction contracts; travel bans, travel restrictions, and freezing of personal assets are routinely ordered. Under the Egyptian Criminal Code, directors who knowingly or negligently fail to prevent the money-laundering activity are liable as principals.
