Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance, operating through the central e-service portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing clearance is performed in real time through the ETA Mersal electronic invoicing platform.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian Limited Liability Company: (1) complete corporate incorporation before the Egyptian General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain the certificate of incorporation; (2) obtain the statistical card from the Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics (CAPMAS); (3) file the ETA tax registration application through the ETA online portal or designated office, submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, board resolution designating the local tax representative, specimen signatures, proof of registered address, and passport copies of shareholders and managers; (4) upon issuance, obtain the Tax Identification Number (TIN), the Tax Card, and, if required, the VAT registration certificate; (5) for a non-resident entity without a permanent establishment, complete simplified VAT registration to enable reverse-charge compliance on imported goods and digital services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not apply US-style check-the-box entity elections. The relevant statutory elections and special regimes available to an Egyptian LLC include: (i) VAT grouping election under Article 15 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, allowing related parties, subject to ETA approval, to register as a single VAT taxable person; (ii) election of a non-calendar fiscal year with ETA approval; (iii) small and medium enterprise income tax incentive elections under Law No. 152 of 2020 for qualifying taxpayers; (iv) investment incentives under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, including accelerated depreciation and investment increment deductions; (v) election for the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) special tax regime under Law No. 83 of 2015, which can provide a 0% corporate income tax rate for licensed zone projects; and (vi) treaty relief elections under Egypt's bilateral income tax treaties to reduce or exempt withholding tax on cross-border payments. No general hybrid mismatch election exists under Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services for mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration is available at EGP 100,000 of annual taxable turnover, with special de minimis treatment for small ventures.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by a registered Egyptian supplier to a non-resident recipient are zero-rated at 0% VAT under Article 24 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, provided the goods physically leave Egyptian customs territory, title and risk pass to the foreign buyer, and the supply is supported by a commercial invoice, export contract, customs export declaration, and bank documentation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency. For the self-heating instant hot water pot manufacturing activity, zero-rating also covers directly input raw materials and packaging used in the exported finished goods, with the exporter entitled to claim input VAT refund or credit through the ETA refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration through Egypt's NAFIZA customs system showing final removal of goods from Egyptian territory
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract/purchase order issued to the non-resident buyer
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing departure from an Egyptian port of shipment
  • Bank Foreign Currency Inward Remittance Certificate (NIRM) evidencing actual receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Packing list and certificate of origin issued by the competent Egyptian chamber or authority
  • Sanitary, health and exothermic-device product safety certificates required for self-heating food/beverage articles
  • TIN registration certificate and export license where the manufacturing license or food safety authority requires it

Egypt has introduced a phased mandatory e-invoicing regime. Every VAT-registered taxpayer must issue tax invoices in the ETA-approved XML format and transmit them in real time through the Mersal clearance platform using API integration. Each invoice must include a qualified electronic signature and a QR code, and receive a unique ETA invoice clearance approval before being considered valid and legally effective. The obligation covers B2B and B2G transactions; e-receipts for retail sales are also being phased in. Non-compliant invoices are treated as unissued invoices, input VAT is disallowed to the buyer, and statutory penalties and fiscal suspension measures apply.

The Egyptian Tax Authority audits export zero-rating claims and may require the exporter to present the original shipping and banking files. If the goods did not actually depart Egypt, the invoices are unsupported, or the foreign-currency export proceeds are not repatriated within the Central Bank of Egypt deadline, the 0% rate is reversed retroactively and the taxpayer is assessed 14% VAT on the invoice value, subject to the monthly 2% late-payment surcharge under the VAT Law, denial or clawback of input VAT credits, and repayment of any refund paid. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer and responsible officers to criminal prosecution, fines of not less than the tax evaded and up to double the tax amount, and possible imprisonment under the Egyptian tax evasion provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. Current-account transactions, including trade invoices, service payments, and dividends, are convertible through authorised banks upon completion of documentary due diligence and anti-money-laundering screening. Capital-account remittances, including shareholder capital antecedent to form an LLC, must follow CBE-approved channels and be registered with the investor registry to guarantee subsequent repatriation. The self-heating pot business is a non-strategic ordinary manufacturing activity, so no sector-specific convertibility restriction applies; the principal practical obstacle is intra-banking-system liquidity for US dollar settlements, not legal prohibition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current CBE instructions governing export receipts, export proceeds must be repatriated to an Egyptian authorised bank within 180 days from the date of the bill of lading / airway bill. Proceeds arecredited to a dedicated foreign-currency account in the exporter's name, and the mandatory conversion ratio to Egyptian pounds is nil (0%) under the post-2022 liberalised FX framework, meaning 100% of export receipts may be retained in USD and deployed for import settlements, hedging, or onward transfers. All proceeds must nevertheless be declared on the bank's electronic systems, and the exporter must retain the original customs export declaration and sales contract for verification by the CBE and the banking supervisory service. If the CBE reinstates a surrender ratio, the statutory deadline and conversion percentage would apply prospectively via official circular.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and its Foreign Exchange Reforms Department; the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) coordinates FDI registration and profit-transfer clearances, while the Ministry of Finance supervises tax-clearance conditions for dividend remittances.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% (Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 26/2020) for Egyptian Limited Liability Companies and other commercial/industrial corporations; banks, petroleum, and Suez Canal authorities remain subject to separate higher rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross cash dividends paid to non-resident shareholders under Law 91/2005 Article 56 as amended by Law 26/2020; Egyptian resident corporate shareholders are exempt from dividend WHT; deemed profit distributions (excess loans to shareholders, unrealistically priced buy-backs) are recharacterized as dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and TP Disclosure Form mandatory when the aggregate value of related-party transactions in the tax year exceeds EGP 5,000,000; Master File mandatory for members of multinational groups with consolidated group turnover above EGP 500,000,000 (or where the group has transactions exceeding EGP 20,000,000 as set by ETA guidance); Country-by-Country Report required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EGP 20,000,000,000 or the local equivalent of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to the UAE regime; however, Law 26/2020 introduced a statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) empowering the ETA to disregard transactions or entire structures lacking commercial justification, and every Egyptian DTA invokes the beneficial-ownership clause requiring substantive activity in the residence state. The EG LLC must therefore maintain, in Egypt, physical production facilities, qualified employees, payroll records, board minutes, operational bank accounts, and risk-management functions; a shell holding without Egyptian substance will be denied treaty benefits, subject to GAAR recharacterization, and assessed at the full statutory rates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of 100%–200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment of not less than 2 years and up to 5 years; the ETA recovers the full unpaid tax plus 2% monthly surcharge and reopens the entire limitation period (extended to 6 years, indefinite in fraud cases); the commercial register may be suspended

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly conceal sales are personally and jointly liable for the tax evaded, subject to criminal imprisonment, asset freezes, travel bans and disqualification from board positions; civil restitution is payable from personal estate where the company is insolvent
Non-Filing and Non-Payment of CIT

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000; monthly delay surcharge of 2% of unpaid tax; ETA may issue a default assessment with a 25% uplift, enforce collection through attachment of bank accounts, factory equipment, and export escrow receipts, and lodge a tax lien over the LLC's assets

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for unpaid corporate tax if the company is liquidated without tax clearance or if they distribute assets/profits while liabilities remain outstanding; Egyptian tax enforcement courts routinely impose travel bans and passport confiscation on directors for unpaid corporate debts exceeding regulatory thresholds
Shell Company, Lack of Economic Substance, and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: GAAR-disallowance of interposed entities, recharacterization of the arrangement, denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of distributions as taxable dividends at 10% and royalties/interest at 20%, plus back taxes, 2% monthly surcharge, and a 100% accuracy-related penalty on the under-assessed tax

Director personal liability: Directors certifying false substance declarations or signed beneficial-ownership forms face criminal perjury charges and personal civil liability for the avoided withholding tax; in aggravated cases the ETA obtains a court order freezing personal assets and imposing a travel ban pending restitution
AML and Unauthorized Foreign Exchange Handling

Statutory sanction: AML penalties of EGP 100,000 to EGP 5,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years for money-laundering offenses; the Central Bank of Egypt may revoke the company's bank accounts, the Ministry of Trade may suspend the export license, and the customs authority may block future shipments

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct informal fund transfers are criminally liable as principals, with imprisonment and fines equal to the value of the funds laundered; civil liability extends to restitution of all diverted proceeds and joint liability with the company
Transfer Pricing Under-Pricing of Export Sales

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer pricing adjustment raising taxable income to the median of the comparable range, additional 22.5% CIT, 2% monthly delay surcharge, and penalty up to 100% of the incremental tax for documentation failures; repeated non-compliance triggers a 3-year mandatory audit cycle

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or certify false transfer pricing documentation are disqualified under Companies Law 159/2020 and held personally liable for the resulting tax loss to the Egyptian treasury; foreign group directors may face enforcement under the DTA competent-authority and mutual-assistance provisions
