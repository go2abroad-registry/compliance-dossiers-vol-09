Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), operating the single national electronic portal e-MTA (https://e-mta.emta.ee) for VAT registration, tax returns, customs clearance and tax rulings.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Form the OÜ in the Estonian Commercial Register via a local notary or the e-Residency digital notary service; no minimum capital is required and non-resident ownership is unrestricted. Step 2: The Commercial Register automatically notifies the MTA, which issues the OÜ's unique 8-digit commercial registry code; that code is the statutory corporate TIN and, when prefixed with 'EE', the VAT identification number. Step 3: Open an Estonian or licensed EU banking/PSP relationship (Stripe, USDC settlement acceptable; client accounts must be held in the OÜ's name). Step 4: Apply through e-MTA for VAT registration when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 in 12 months, and immediately for intra-EU B2B supplies, distance sales or imports. Step 5: No separate corporate income tax registration is required; the OÜ is automatically a resident taxpayer subject to entity-level tax on distributions only.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style check-the-box regime; an OÜ's tax classification is fixed by the Commercial Code. Available elections and structuring mechanisms: (1) voluntary VAT registration below the statutory threshold; (2) VAT group registration for closely connected resident companies; (3) establishment of a non-resident branch instead of a domestic OÜ, preserving foreign tax residence while obtaining an Estonian tax number and, where required, an Estonian VAT ID; and (4) application for an MTA advance ruling to confirm the place-of-supply and zero-rating treatment for AI/LLM API token sales. Hybrid mismatch elections are not optional; ATAD anti-hybrid rules are applied by the MTA where inconsistent entity or instrument treatment produces double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months; mandatory earlier where intra-Community distance sales or intra-EU B2B supplies (including online electronical services) exceed EUR 10,000 per calendar year, or where goods are imported; voluntary registration permitted at any time

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% rate applies to: (i) supplies of electronically supplied services (AI/LLM API tokens, SaaS) to customers established or normally resident outside the EU, and to EU business customers holding a valid EU VAT ID (reverse charge); (ii) exports of goods (e.g., GPU servers or AI hardware) where dispatch outside the EU customs territory is completed within 90 days of supply and documentary evidence is obtained within 6 months; and (iii) services directly linked to such exports. For API sales, the OÜ must retain evidence of non-EU place of establishment (commercial register extract, IP address/geolocation data, payment rails and contract counterparty) and, for USDC/crypto settlements, the wallet addresses and transaction hashes.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital stamp requirement is mandated in Estonia. All VAT returns, EC Sales Lists and Intrastat declarations are filed exclusively through the e-MTA portal. Invoices to Estonian public-sector buyers must be structured e-invoices; B2B and B2C invoices must contain the statutory elements and, for intra-EU B2B supplies, include the statement 'reverse charge'. The MTA supports machine-to-machine interfaces via X-Road for automated declaration submission, but physical clearance of invoices is not required.

The MTA cross-checks export declarations, VAT returns, bank records and cryptocurrency/PSP remittance evidence. Failure to substantiate a 0% claim triggers retrospective clawback: VAT is reassessed at 22% on the full consideration, penalty interest accrues at 0.06% per calendar day, and an administrative fine of up to EUR 1,500 may be imposed on the OÜ. Deliberately false zero-rating (falsified customs documents, fabricated non-EU customer evidence, sham geolocation) constitutes criminal tax fraud punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, confiscation of proceeds and extended limitation periods; the management board may incur personal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is part of the euro area and applies full current and capital account convertibility under EU law, subject to horizontal restrictions from EU sanctions, AML/CFT screening, and the reserve-based monetary framework of the Eurosystem. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is permitted without withholding taxes when distributed from taxed corporate profits; neither exchange control approval nor central bank clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation exists in Estonia. Export receivables in non-euro currencies are freely retainable on accounts, with no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline imposed under normal economic regulation.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the national central bank within the Eurosystem, with the European Central Bank responsible for euro monetary policy; foreign exchange administration is harmonized under EU and Estonian law, with Finantsinspektsioon overseeing licensed financial institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legally constrained to licensed and passporting e-money institutions under Finantsinspektsioon or a recognized EU/EEA home-state license; offshore or unlicensed digital EMIs are generally rejected by Estonian banks and payment service providers. Feasibility rating: Low for non-EEA EMIs without local passport; Medium for EU/EEA-licensed EMI partners with robust correspondent banking arrangements and full transparency on underlying clients.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections follow standard cross-border wire via SEPA/EUR SWIFT MT103 or non-EUR SWIFT wire into the company's single euro payment area account. The transaction purpose code must be one of the ECB/European Coding system classifications reflecting direct investment or capital contribution; in Estonia, the standard is 'Capital transfer – equity contribution to a resident enterprise' (Code 9/12). Verification requires board resolution allocating capital, identification of the transferring beneficial owner, source-of-funds clearance, and, if capital is received as crypto/USDC, mandatory conversion to fiat through a licensed virtual asset service provider before booking on the operating cash account.Outbound trade disbursements use EU-standard clearing rails: SEPA Credit Transfer (XML scheme) for euro payments, SWIFT MT103 for non-euro or third-country wire, and Commercial Letters of Credit (UCB 600) for large unsettled procurement contracts.
