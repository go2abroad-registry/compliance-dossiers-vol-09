Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital for an Osaühing is EUR 2,500 under Commercial Code §136(1). Unless later payment is expressly permitted in the articles of association, the full share capital must be credited to a bank account in a credit institution operating in Estonia before the registration application is filed. A bank statement or credit certificate serves as evidence of payment. No formal escrow account is required; however, if later payment is authorized, the management board members are jointly liable until full contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance follows the Rescue Act; for a normal office premises used for computer-based art production, a fire hazard assessment by the operator is sufficient unless the Rescue Board declares a higher risk. Structural load inspections are not required for standard office use but are necessary if the studio installs heavy physical media equipment or 3D printers. The Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) requires a written workplace risk assessment, ergonomic measures for computer work, and first-aid readiness; no separate work permit is required for this business scope.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital for an Osaühing is EUR 2,500 under Commercial Code §136(1). Unless later payment is expressly permitted in the articles of association, the full share capital must be credited to a bank account in a credit institution operating in Estonia before the registration application is filed. A bank statement or credit certificate serves as evidence of payment. No formal escrow account is required; however, if later payment is authorized, the management board members are jointly liable until full contribution.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), e-MTA portal at https://emta.ee

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ): 1) Incorporate and register in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister). 2) Upon registration, the entity is automatically registered with the Tax and Customs Board for residence and VAT (if applicable). 3) Obtain the company's Estonian business registry code (registrikood), which functions as the TIN/VAT ID. 4) For non-resident companies without a permanent establishment, apply to the MTA for a non-resident VAT identification number if taxable supplies are made in Estonia. 5) Appoint a local contact person or tax representative if required for VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ): 1) Incorporate and register in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister). 2) Upon registration, the entity is automatically registered with the Tax and Customs Board for residence and VAT (if applicable). 3) Obtain the company's Estonian business registry code (registrikood), which functions as the TIN/VAT ID. 4) For non-resident companies without a permanent establishment, apply to the MTA for a non-resident VAT identification number if taxable supplies are made in Estonia. 5) Appoint a local contact person or tax representative if required for VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election under Estonian domestic law. Specific elections apply to the distribution-based corporate income tax regime (20% regular rate, 14% reduced rate on qualifying regular distributions), optional application of CFC rules under § 50–51 of the Income Tax Act, and hybrid mismatch elections under Controlled Foreign Company and EU Anti-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) implementations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for resident persons; non-residents with taxable supplies in Estonia are required to register if turnover exceeds this threshold or if they make certain distance sales exceeding EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods from Estonia to destinations outside the European Union qualifies for 0% VAT provided the supplier retains conclusive proof of export within 90 days of supply. The goods must physically leave Estonia; transport may be arranged by the supplier, customer, or a third party. Intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered persons are zero-rated subject to a valid VAT number and recapitulative statement (EC Sales List). Certain international services (e.g., B2B supplies to non-resident entrepreneurs, services related to immovable property outside the EU, and export-related transport services) are treated as outside the scope of Estonian VAT or zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX1 or electronic declaration, e.g., via Estonia's Single Administrative Document system)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill or other transport document evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Purchase order or contract with the foreign customer
  • Commercial invoice
  • Proof of payment (inward remittance certificate or SWIFT confirmation) demonstrating payment from the foreign buyer
  • Proof of customs exit (e.g., exit confirmation from the customs or carrier)

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate. Estonia uses an electronic tax accounting system (e-MTA) and accepts structured e-invoices (e.g., PEPPOL, XML, EDI). E-invoicing is mandatory for invoices issued to Estonian public sector entities. No digital stamping or live validation of invoices is required.

Estonian tax authorities (MTA) may assess retrospective VAT and penalties of up to 20% to 50% of the tax understated, plus interest at the statutory rate (currently 0.06% per day) and possible criminal liability for fraud. If export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient or timely proof, the supplier is liable for 20% VAT on the export supply, and VAT refunds may be clawed back with additional fines and interest. Knowingly submitting false export documentation attracts criminal penalties under the Penal Code, including fines or imprisonment up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. Estonia has no general exchange-control approval, compulsory repatriation, or foreign-currency holding restriction applicable to an Estonian OÜ. Inbound investment, intercompany funding, royalty payments, service fees, dividends, and loan repayments can generally be settled in euros or foreign currency through regulated payment channels. Profit distributions remain subject to Estonian corporate income tax on distributions, generally at the rate applicable for the relevant tax year, currently 22/78 of the net distribution from 2025, together with accounting, solvency, corporate-law, beneficial-ownership, transfer-pricing, and applicable treaty requirements. Banks may require evidence of distributable profits, board or shareholder approval, tax compliance, source of funds, and the commercial basis of related-party payments. Sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, payment-service controls, and bank risk policies may delay or reject transactions notwithstanding the absence of exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement applies in Estonia. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no compulsory conversion into euros, and no general statutory deadline for repatriating or converting export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through SEPA, SWIFT, card, payment-service-provider, or other lawful rails. The company must maintain accurate accounting, retain contracts and invoices, reconcile receipts to customers and services, report required statistical or tax information, and satisfy bank transaction-monitoring requests. VAT, customs, sanctions, and tax rules may impose separate documentation or reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank, the central bank of Estonia, operating within the Eurosystem. Estonia has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general currency controls. Banking and payment-service supervision is primarily conducted by the European Central Bank and the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon), according to the applicable institution and activity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and routine transfers; Low as a substitute for a fully functional Estonian corporate bank account where local account details, cash services, lending, escrow, or enhanced transaction support are required. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services in Estonia merely by marketing remotely. It must be licensed in an EEA state with valid passporting, operate through an appropriately authorized structure, or rely on a permitted agent arrangement. The OÜ must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, fund-segregation terms, SEPA access, chargeback exposure, foreign-exchange spread, account-closure policy, and access to SWIFT or correspondent services. PayPal is a payment service and not a bank deposit account; balances may be restricted under its risk controls and should not be the sole treasury rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity, share-capital funding, or shareholder loans from an account held in the funder's legal name through SEPA for euro transfers or SWIFT for non-SEPA currencies. Use the OÜ's exact registered name and account identifier, and state a precise purpose such as share-capital contribution, shareholder loan, or equity injection. Estonia does not use a universal statutory FX purpose-code regime for ordinary corporate transfers; the sending bank, receiving bank, and payment-service provider may nevertheless require an internal purpose code, invoice, loan agreement, capital contribution resolution, or source-of-funds declaration. Before remittance, provide the bank with the ownership chart, funding agreement, board or shareholder approval, source-of-funds evidence, expected amount and frequency, and accounting treatment. Reconcile the incoming payment to the ledger and retain payment messages and supporting documents. Do not route capital through unrelated third parties or personal accounts.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA or non-euro settlements. Include the legal beneficiary name, registered address where required, IBAN or account number, BIC/SWIFT code, invoice number, contract reference, and accurate payment purpose. For material or higher-risk cross-border purchases, use a documentary Commercial Letter of Credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or bank-guaranteed payment where commercially justified. Confirm the bank's trade-finance capability, applicable ICC rules such as UCP 600 or URC 522, sanctions screening, documentary requirements, discrepancy fees, correspondent-bank charges, and foreign-exchange conversion terms.
