Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) - registry departments of county courts, with e-filing processed through the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister) operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus, RIK), Tartu.

Governing statute

Commercial Code of the Republic of Estonia (Äriseadustik), Part III 'Private Limited Company': §§135-140 (foundation, articles and registration application), §§144-145 (share capital and contributions), §§147-149 (management board and representation), §§157-160 (shareholders' register); General Part of the Civil Code Act (legal acts and incorporation procedure); Digital Signature Act and e-Identity Act (qualified electronic filing); State Fees Act (online incorporation state fee of EUR 0).

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation application (kandeavaldus / asutamisavaldus) e-filed in the e-Business Register, together with the foundation resolution, the articles of association (põhikiri), the share subscription list, the board members' written consents and the beneficial owner declaration, all signed with a qualified Estonian digital signature; notarial alternative: notarized foundation deed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-MTA portal at emta.ee for registration, filing, and digital tax account management.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident shareholders and board members may complete registration remotely using an Estonian e-Residency digital ID card or national ID card. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the OÜ is taxed at the entity level only on distributed profits (statutory corporate income tax rate applied as 20/80 on dividend distributions from retained profits, and 14/86 reduced rate for regular distributions meeting the statutory regularity condition). Undistributed and reinvested profits are taxed at 0%; the entity is not a pass-through vehicle.

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT group registration (Käibemaksugrupp); (ii) reduced 14/86 corporate income tax rate election for dividends paid with at least the statutory regularity; (iii) participation exemption for dividends received from resident and EU subsidiary companies; (iv) ATAD-compliant hybrid-mismatch structural elections and corresponding disclosure notifications to the MTA; (v) special tax-deferral treatment for reinvested profits under the Estonian distributed-profit system.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover; mandatory registration above this threshold, voluntary registration permitted below it, with distance-selling and OSS rules applicable to foreign suppliers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment applies to goods physically transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU; intra-EU B2B supplies of goods are zero-rated when the customer is VAT-registered in another Member State and the goods leave Estonia; services directly connected to exported goods are zero-rated; software-as-a-subscription and financial portfolio tracking services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Estonian VAT by place-of-supply rules, while EU B2C digital services may be reported under the One-Stop Shop (OSS) scheme.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-1 or electronic export declaration lodged with Estonian Customs)

No real-time national fiscal clearance, digital stamp, or tax-stamp regime operates in Estonia. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory in PEPPOL BIS/EN 16931 format. B2B invoicing may be electronic or paper, but transaction-level sales data must be reported in the periodic VAT return annex submitted via e-MTA. E-Residency digital signature serves as the operational authentication mechanism for electronic filing. EU ViDA real-time digital reporting requirements are expected to apply on a phased basis.

Export and cross-border zero-rating claims are audited against goods-movement evidence, commercial supply chains, and payment-flow records. False, artificial, or sham export structures trigger retrospective VAT assessments at 22%, statutory late-payment interest, fines up to EUR 32,000 under the Taxation Act, clawback of input VAT credits with interest, joint and several liability of intermediaries, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area Member State; both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible under the EU free movement of capital and payment frameworks. The euro is freely transferable and exchangeable within all EU clearing systems. No prior exchange-control or central-bank approval is required for cross-border profit remittance, dividend distribution, or debt repayments. The sole practical constraints on conversions are bank-level anti-money-laundering, sanctions compliance, and beneficial-ownership verification; there is no Estonian statutory FX rationing or repatriation restriction. Distributed profits are treated as deemed dividends under Estonian income tax law (20% or 14% under prescribed conditions), but this is a tax rather than FX-remittance barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion of export receipts into euros. Export settlements may be retained in any freely convertible currency. Exporters remain subject to statistical balance-of-payments reporting to Eesti Pank under Regulation (EU) No 184/2016 when data thresholds are met, but this is passive statistical disclosure rather than an exchange surrender rule. If export receipts are received into a foreign currency account, banks may require a stated purpose code and underlying commercial documentation for the payment.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from shareholders should be routed as a SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR within the EEA (using the OÜ registry code as beneficiary reference) or as a SWIFT MT103 with the remitter identified as the registered shareholder. Eesti Pank imposes no prior approval on inward capital contributions; however, the remitter must use a purpose/statistical code indicating 'equity contribution' or 'intercompany financing' depending on the legal form of injection. For non-EUR remittances, the bank requires the MT103 field 23B to reflect no FX-control restriction, and field 70 should state 'Shareholder capital contribution to OÜ'. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the net amount of taxable profit distributions, calculated using the 22/78 gross-up mechanism. Estonia generally does not impose corporate income tax on retained or reinvested accounting profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Estonia generally does not impose a separate withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. The Estonian distributing company generally pays corporate income tax at 22/78 on the net taxable distribution. Statutory exceptions may apply, including certain distributions associated with previously taxed profits or other specified circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no single general transaction-value threshold that automatically exempts all smaller related-party transactions from the arm's-length principle. Documentation requirements depend on statutory criteria, including the parties and transaction circumstances. Prescribed detailed documentation may apply to a resident company meeting size tests—generally at least 250 employees, annual turnover of at least EUR 50 million, or total balance-sheet assets of at least EUR 43 million—or to specified cross-border and low-tax-territory transactions. A group-level Master File obligation can arise for a group meeting the applicable EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold. Confirm the current Estonian Income Tax Act and implementing rules for the entity's precise facts.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Estonian transfer-pricing, anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, and business-purpose rules remain applicable. An Estonian registered office or e-Residency status alone does not establish that management, personnel, functions, assets, or decision-making are located in Estonia for tax-treaty or foreign-tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unreported taxable distributions and profit transfers

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid corporate income tax, statutory interest, and applicable administrative penalties. Intentional tax fraud may be prosecuted under criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability where statutory conditions for liability are met, including intentional or grossly negligent breaches causing tax loss. Criminal conduct can result in individual prosecution and imprisonment. Civil recovery is not an automatic consequence of ordinary company tax debt, and travel restrictions require a separate legal basis.
Transfer-pricing adjustment and related-party mispricing

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board may adjust taxable income or transaction values and assess tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Documentation failures can weaken the taxpayer's evidentiary position and increase audit exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable under applicable law for intentional or culpable conduct that causes tax loss; routine transfer-pricing adjustments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Improper treaty or withholding-tax treatment

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, statutory interest, and applicable penalties. Treaty benefits may be denied under domestic anti-abuse rules or the applicable treaty's principal-purpose and entitlement provisions.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise where the statutory requirements for director or representative liability are met, including culpable failure to discharge tax obligations. It is not automatic solely because a withholding assessment is issued.
False accounting, concealment, or tax fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, administrative sanctions, and potential criminal prosecution under Estonian law; criminal penalties depend on the offense, intent, amount, and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Responsible individuals may face personal criminal liability, including fines or imprisonment where statutory elements are established. Civil liability and asset recovery require an applicable legal basis; veil piercing, travel bans, and imprisonment are not automatic tax-collection measures.
AML, sanctions, and financial-service perimeter

Statutory sanction: Regulatory, administrative, and potentially criminal consequences may apply under the relevant Estonian financial-services, AML, and sanctions legislation. A software-only portfolio-tracking service is not automatically a regulated financial service; functionality and actual conduct control.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face administrative or criminal liability where the applicable statutory elements are met. A software classification or low banking-risk label does not itself exempt the company or its directors from licensing, AML, or sanctions requirements.
