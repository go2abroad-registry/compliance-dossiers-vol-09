Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned EG LLC first obtains commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), then registers with the Egyptian Tax Authority district office to receive a Tax Card/TIN and, once taxable supplies meet the threshold, a VAT registration certificate. Non-resident suppliers without an Egyptian establishment must appoint an Egyptian tax representative and file the ETA VAT registration application with Arabic-notarised constitutional documents and a power of attorney.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies and imports (VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, as amended)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods qualify for 0% VAT when physically exported from Egypt under a valid customs export declaration and when title and risk have passed to a foreign buyer. Zero rating also applies to qualifying exported/offshore services actually consumed outside Egypt and to international transport services; evidence of export delivery and repatriation of sale proceeds in freely convertible currency is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Contract
  • Packing List

All registered businesses subject to Egypt's e-invoicing mandate must transmit invoices to the ETA-integrated fiscal clearance system in real time in XML/JSON format via the ETA IDC APIs. Each invoice receives an electronic reference code and QR code before delivery; failure to submit or obtain clearance can trigger fines and disallowed input VAT recovery.

ETA may audit export transactions for up to five years; a false or failed export zero-rating claim triggers immediate assessment of VAT at 14% plus late interest, a penalty between 30% and 100% of the tax evaded, forfeiture of input VAT credits, clawback of refunds, and criminal liability for fraud when documents are falsified.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII, so current-account transactions are freely convertible under a documented underlying; since 6 March 2024 the Egyptian pound trades unified under a market-clearing float with no CBE restrictions on legitimate trade settlements, dividend or profit remittances routed through an authorized bank. Capital-account items are only partially liberalized: inward FDI equity is permitted under GAFI Law No. 72 of 2017 subject to positive-list screening and initial deposit verification; portfolio flows, shareholder loans, hedging positions and change-of-control disposals require separate CBE registration. Profit remittance hurdles are largely documentary: audited annual financial statements, Egyptian corporate tax returns and tax clearance certificate, settlement of applicable Egyptian withholding obligations on profit distribution, and funding of the remitting foreign-currency account exclusively with realized export proceeds or registered capital; operational hurdles are EGP liquidity constraints and bank-level documentation queues.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender ratio applies after the 6 March 2024 liberalization: the industrial exporter may retain 100% of USD/EUR export receipts in a dedicated exporters' foreign-currency account and may use them for trade settlements, permitted hedging or voluntary sale to the bank at interbank rates. Statutory repatriation timeline: proceeds of each shipment must be remitted into Egypt within 90 days from the bill of lading/airway bill date (or the underlying letter-of-credit maturity), and a formal bank-approved extension up to 180 days is available for trade-finance-backed structures. The duty bank must report overdue cases to the CBE compliance unit, exposing the exporter to administrative fines, reduction of authorized-dealer limits and denial of future approval before a breach is cured.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE); legal bases: Foreign Exchange Law No. 80 of 1947, Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), CBE Board Decision No. 234 of 2011 (repatriation of export proceeds) as amended, and the CBE directives of 6 March 2024 establishing a unified free-float interbank foreign-exchange market.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium (narrow perimeter). Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut-type platforms) are not licensed Egyptian payment institutions under CBE E-Money regulations and Law No. 18 of 2019 (non-cash payments); they cannot serve as the treasury-of-record or receive EGP settlement for tax purposes. They may be used only as auxiliary outbound rails for low-value B2B supplier disbursements, software licensing and expatriate contractor payments, subject to (i) a signed contract identifying the EMI counterparty, (ii) a monthly sweep cap of 15% of operational disbursements, and (iii) an accounting policy treating EMI balances as foreign bank accounts attracting CBE reporting and Egyptian GAAP/TFRS note disclosure; inbound flows from EMIs to the Egyptian operating bank must always be supported by the underlying invoice, otherwise they are treated as unverified third-party credits and can be frozen under AML protocols.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (i) open a temporary capital-subscription escrow account at a CBE-licensed bank (NBE, Banque Misr or CIB) after GAFI name reservation; (ii) remit the FDI equity via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's own corporate account in a single value transfer with the CBE statutory foreign-exchange ticket purpose code for 'direct investment in Egypt - equity capital' and reference text 'capital contribution to EG_LLC under Companies Law No. 4 of 2018'; (iii) deliver to the bank the parent board resolution, constitutional documents, audited statements, UBO declaration, apostilled passports and the signed escrow agreement; (iv) after Commercial Registry issuance, the bank releases the escrowed funds into EG_LLC's operational USD account and submits the statutory inward-investment verification to CBE; (v) where capital is denominated in EGP, the USD conversion ticket is issued at the interbank rate on-site and the MT103 remains part of the bank's verification file; (vi) do not split the injection into sub-threshold tranches and do not route through third-party FX brokers, both of which trigger automated CBE monitoring and can block further treasury operations.The primary clearing rails are: SWIFT MT103 for operating disbursements and freight/insurance/e-licensing payments, and irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Egyptian bank and advised/confirmed by the supplier's bank for industrial component imports. Every outbound payment requires a CBE FX ticket matched to a signed underlying obligation, Egyptian tax card and, for physical goods, a GOEIC import declaration (Form 4) or customs release as attachment. Export proceeds must be earmarked forward: the USD export-proceeds account is the first source for outbound USD settlements, with any shortfall covered through the FX forward line; all rails must be cleared through CBE's interbank infrastructure rather than crypto networks or offshore E-money loops.Deliverable EGP/USD FX forward contract (tenor up to 12 months) with a CBE-licensed counterparty, executed against underlying trade invoices (permissible under the 2024 free-float framework).FX swap (spot against forward) for short-term EGP/USD liquidity matching within the same booking bank.USD/EGP cross-currency swap for matched debt-service hedging on registered shareholder loans, subject to CBE derivative exposure reporting.European-style FX option (USD call / EGP put) evidenced by a genuine export sales receivable and approved by the treasury committee.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF): expressly not permissible onshore; CBE rules prohibit EGP NDF settlement within the Egyptian banking market, so EG_LLC must prohibit NDFs in its treasury policy statement and source all hedging from licensed Egyptian banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 1,000,000 (annual related-party transactions) for TP Information Return (Form TT1); Local File required where related-party transactions exceed EGP 15 million; Master File required for multinational enterprise groups with consolidated turnover above EGP 600 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime; however, the general anti-avoidance rules under Law 91 of 2005 and the Agency and Investment Laws empower the tax administration to assess economic substance, disregard artificial or non-commercial transactions, and deny treaty/tax-incentive benefits where a mere shell arrangement is present.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 3 to 5 years and monetary penalty equal to the amount of evaded tax; tax authorities also impose double tax surcharge and bar the taxpayer from future tax settlements.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil can be pierced where directors actively conceal transactions; directors jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax plus civil penalties; criminal judges may impose travel bans and freeze personal assets.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of all intercompany deductions, assessment of deemed dividends/royalty income subject to withholding tax, and administrative penalties reaching 20% of the attributed profit; the entity may be struck off in case of sham status.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are personally liable for fraudulent creditor and tax misrepresentation; courts may order tracing of assets, enforce civil restitution, and issue travel bans against foreign shareholders who control the shell.
AML

Statutory sanction: Under Anti-Money Laundering Law 80 of 2002 and Executive Regulations, fines up to EGP 5 million for unpermitted transfers, plus imprisonment of 3–7 years for laundering offences; confiscation of unlawful proceeds and freezing of corporate bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers with supervisory responsibility face criminal imprisonment and personal fines; non-executive shareholders can be joined under joint tortious liability if they deliberately exploit the corporate structure to launder illegal industrial proceeds.
