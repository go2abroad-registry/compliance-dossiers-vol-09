Competent corporate registry

Estonian Business Register (Äriregister), administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus, RIK) via the e-Business Register portal (ettevotjaportaal.ee).

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik, RT I 1995, 26, 355), as amended; §§ 135–141 (formation of Osaühing), § 136 (share capital), §§ 148–160 (Articles of Association, shares, contributions); e-Business Register Act (äriregistri seadus); General Part of the Civil Code Act § 44 (registered office).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notice of Incorporation (asutamise avaldus) submitted electronically through the e-Business Register portal and digitally signed by all founders; postal or notary-authenticated paper filing is also permitted.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 10 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No mandatory minimum paid-up capital for incorporation: Commercial Code § 136 sets a default minimum of EUR 2,500 but expressly permits formation with subscribed share capital as low as EUR 1 via the online route. No escrow or blocked-deposit account is required at formation; contributions must be made by the date fixed in the Articles of Association (statutory maximum 10 years). For payment-institution licensing under the Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act, the Financial Supervision Authority requires minimum initial capital of EUR 20,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: prior civil-defense clearance by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) under the Fire Safety Act (Tuleohutuse seadus) and Emergency Preparedness Act (Hädaolukorra seadus) — mandatory fire alarm system, extinguisher availability, evacuation plan, and fire drill certification. Occupational clearance: Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) work environment clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus) — documented risk assessment, workplace fit-for-purpose certificate, and first-aid arrangements. Structural load inspection is required only for premises exceeding the design load of office floors; standard leasehold occupancy does not trigger separate structural inspection.

Paid-in capital rule: No mandatory minimum paid-up capital for incorporation: Commercial Code § 136 sets a default minimum of EUR 2,500 but expressly permits formation with subscribed share capital as low as EUR 1 via the online route. No escrow or blocked-deposit account is required at formation; contributions must be made by the date fixed in the Articles of Association (statutory maximum 10 years). For payment-institution licensing under the Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act, the Financial Supervision Authority requires minimum initial capital of EUR 20,000.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, 'MTA'); online services via e-MTA portal (e-mta.emta.ee) and the national eesti.ee business channel on X-Road.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an EE_OU (Osaühing) obtains a national registry code (Äriregistri kood) at incorporation through the e-Business Register (ariregister.rik.ee); this code serves as the statutory tax identification number (TIN). VAT registration is effected with MTA using electronic form KNA, filed on e-MTA; non-resident persons without an Estonian seat must register for VAT from their first Estonian taxable supply and are recommended to appoint a local fiscal representative. e-Residency authentication enables remote filing; corporate income tax self-assessment registration is automatic upon registry entry.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — OÜ is taxable on distributed profits at 20/80 (25% effective) or 14/86 (16.28% effective) for 'regular distributions'; undistributed profits are taxed at 0%; territorial scope limited to Estonian-source and deemed Estonian-source income; no pass-through status is available. — A foreign entity establishing an EE_OU (Osaühing) obtains a national registry code (Äriregistri kood) at incorporation through the e-Business Register (ariregister.rik.ee); this code serves as the statutory tax identification number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — OÜ is taxable on distributed profits at 20/80 (25% effective) or 14/86 (16.28% effective) for 'regular distributions'; undistributed profits are taxed at 0%; territorial scope limited to Estonian-source and deemed Estonian-source income; no pass-through status is available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box pass-through election exists. Available elections: VAT group registration (VAT Act section 13); regular-dividend basket election (14/86 rate) based on the previous three taxable years; small-business VAT exemption election for annual taxable turnover not exceeding EUR 40,000; OSS/IOSS registration for EU distance sales; hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch self-correction mechanisms are prescribed by the ATAD implementation in the Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable domestic turnover for Estonian-established persons (small business low-taxable-person threshold); EUR 10,000 annual distance-sale threshold for cross-border B2C supplies of goods and electronic services within the EU; no threshold for non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Estonia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) exports of goods physically dispatched from Estonia to a destination outside the EU, supported by transport and customs evidence; (ii) intra-Community supplies to a counterparty registered for VAT in another Member State, evidenced by a valid VAT identification number and recorded in the KMD-INF annex; and (iii) services directly related to export goods (transport, handling, insurance) and certain cross-border B2B services where the place of supply is outside Estonia. The supplier must obtain and retain documentary proof of dispatch and remittance within the periods required by the VAT Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX / electronic SAD lodged via the national customs ETS system)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR waybill / Air Waybill / postal or courier receipt evidencing physical departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 credit advice proving receipt of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice and export contract identifying the foreign consignee
  • VAT return KMD line-item export listing with underlying accounting records
  • Intra-Community recapitulative statement (KMD-INF) evidencing EU zero-rating where applicable

No continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time clearance or digital stamping is in force. VAT returns and recapitulative statements (form KMD with annex KMD-INF) are filed electronically through e-MTA by the 20th day of the month following each monthly or quarterly tax period; B2G e-invoicing is mandatory in PEPPOL-BIS format; B2B e-invoicing is voluntary under the national e-invoice (e-arv) standard; cross-border B2C electronic services are reportable via the Non-Union OSS scheme.

MTA may reassess VAT within a 3-year general limitation period, extended to 5 years where tax evasion or material misstatement is suspected; unsupported or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers reversal of the exemption, immediate VAT reassessment, clawback of input VAT credited on the export transaction, arrears interest of 0.06% per day, and administrative fines up to EUR 1,600 for filing violations; criminal liability arises under Penal Code section 399 for tax fraud, punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years, or up to 5 years for large-scale fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro as legal tender and has no general exchange controls, capital controls, currency allocation system, or mandatory approval for ordinary inbound or outbound corporate foreign-exchange transactions. The euro is freely convertible and Estonia participates in SEPA. Current-account payments, trade settlements, dividends, interest, service fees, and capital-account transactions are generally transferable through licensed banks, subject to sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, tax compliance, beneficial-ownership verification, and bank transaction-monitoring requirements. A shareholder may generally contribute equity or provide a shareholder loan without a foreign-exchange permit, but the transaction should be supported by corporate resolutions, an investment or loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, accounting entries, and correct payment references. Profit distributions require distributable equity, a valid shareholder resolution, applicable Estonian corporate income-tax treatment, and assessment of withholding-tax obligations in the recipient jurisdiction. Estonia generally taxes distributed profits rather than retained profits; payment-services revenue and other operating income remain subject to applicable accounting, payroll, VAT, transfer-pricing, and other tax rules. A licensed remittance business faces additional safeguarding, reconciliation, reporting, outsourcing, operational-resilience, and AML obligations. Banks may refuse or delay transactions involving high-risk corridors, cash-pickup networks, sanctioned countries, opaque intermediaries, unexplained third-party funding, or insufficient licensing evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no general statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general requirement to surrender export receipts to an Estonian bank or convert them into euros. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through correspondent banks, subject to contractual, accounting, tax, sanctions, and AML requirements. For a remittance provider, customer funds must be handled in accordance with the applicable payment-services safeguarding model and must not be confused with corporate operating funds. The company should maintain transaction-level audit trails, reconcile correspondent and safeguarding accounts, document payment purpose and beneficiary information, and comply with applicable statistical, tax, regulatory, and suspicious-transaction reporting obligations. No export-surrender exemption is required because no general surrender rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (the central bank of Estonia); Estonia applies the euro-area monetary and foreign-exchange framework of the European Central Bank. Prudential supervision of banks and licensing supervision of payment institutions and electronic money institutions are primarily conducted by the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon). The Financial Intelligence Unit supervises specified anti-money-laundering obligations outside Finantsinspektsioon-regulated sectors and exercises relevant AML functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for regulated account and payment infrastructure; low as a substitute for the company's own licensing obligations. An offshore digital bank, electronic money institution, payment institution, or virtual-IBAN provider may provide collection, payout, SEPA, SWIFT, card, or foreign-exchange services only within the provider's authorization, passporting, correspondent, and geographic limits. Use of an offshore EMI does not authorize an Estonian OÜ to conduct money remittance, payment initiation, account-information, cash-payout, or other regulated payment services in Estonia or the European Economic Area. The OÜ must obtain the appropriate Estonian or other EEA authorization, or operate as a properly notified agent or outsourced service provider of an authorized institution. Customer funds must be segregated or otherwise safeguarded under the applicable payment-services regime. Offshore onboarding is subject to beneficial-owner, source-of-funds, source-of-wealth, sanctions, transaction-volume, corridor, cash-payout, and licensing reviews. Virtual IBANs do not create a local bank account, local clearing entitlement, or exemption from AML, safeguarding, reporting, or consumer-protection requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions through a bank account held in the OÜ's legal name, preferably from an account owned by the shareholder or verified beneficial owner. Use a clear reference such as 'share capital contribution' or 'shareholder equity contribution' and align the payment with the articles, shareholder resolution, Commercial Register capital records, and accounting entries. Route shareholder loans under a signed loan agreement stating principal, currency, maturity, interest, repayment, subordination if applicable, and transfer-pricing rationale; use the reference 'shareholder loan.' Avoid third-party funding unless the relationship and economic purpose are fully documented. The receiving bank may request the investment agreement, corporate resolutions, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence, ownership chart, tax information, and explanation of the intended use of proceeds. For a licensed remittance provider, do not use customer funds or safeguarded funds to finance capitalization, working capital, shareholder distributions, or unrelated expenses. Apply sanctions screening, originator and beneficiary data requirements, reconciliation, and suspicious-transaction escalation to each transfer. SEPA Credit Transfer is suitable for euro-area and SEPA euro funding; SWIFT cross-border wires are suitable for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. Payment references and purpose coding should be accurate; Estonia does not operate a general statutory capital-import approval or foreign-exchange purpose-code regime.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within SEPA, subject to participant limits and sanctions screening. Use SWIFT cross-border payments for non-SEPA currencies and correspondent-bank corridors; MT103 is the traditional customer-credit-transfer format, while ISO 20022 payment messages are increasingly used and may be required by the relevant banking chain. Commercial letters of credit may be issued, advised, confirmed, or settled through a relationship bank under the bank's trade-finance rules and applicable ICC UCP 600, ISBP, and sanctions controls. Documentary collections may use URC 522 where appropriate. Each trade disbursement should be supported by a purchase or service contract, invoice, counterparty and beneficial-owner information, shipping or delivery evidence where relevant, sanctions screening, correct payment purpose, and approval under delegated treasury limits. Customer remittance principal should not be booked as a trade expense or paid from the operating account. Maintain separate ledgers for operating funds, safeguarded customer funds, settlement floats, fees, chargebacks, reserves, and liquidity buffers. Require dual authorization, beneficiary validation, callback controls for changes, daily bank reconciliation, and exception monitoring.Deliverable forward foreign-exchange contracts entered into with a bank or appropriately authorized investment firm to hedge documented operating, settlement, receivable, payable, or safeguarding-related currency exposures. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed/reinvested profits; 20/80 (20% gross) on distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20/80 domestic rate on profit distributions (25% of the net dividend; 20% of the grossed-up distribution); 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; reduced treaty rates (usually 15% portfolio, 5% or 0% direct investment) apply if domestic conditions and TRC are fulfilled.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 annual aggregate value of all cross-border related-party transactions conducted by the OÜ; Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations apply when the parent group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply West Indies-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Tax Act and judicial substance-over-form jurisprudence demand real entrepreneurial activity, actual day-to-day management, and business purpose for an OÜ. Shell companies, e-Residency-only letterbox entities, or remittance agents with no local compliance infrastructure can be disregarded, their transactions recharacterized, and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT Declaration

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment applying the full grossed-up amount; penalty interest 0.06% per day; criminal fine and imprisonment up to three years under Penal Code § 399; for aggravated cases involving international laundering, up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns can be prosecuted personally; courts may pierce the corporate veil and attach their personal assets for the company's unpaid tax; travel bans and prohibition from serving as director are common interim sanctions.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may disregard the corporate entity and tax the economic owner at personal level; administrative fines for misleading registration up to €50,000; company dissolution order may be filed by the Tax and Customs Board; cancellation of taxpayer identification is possible.

Director personal liability: The nominee director and actual beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for all tax debts and fines; travel restrictions and asset freezing may be imposed during the tax investigation.
AML / Special License Violation in Remittance Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, administrative fine up to €5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; revocation of payment license; seizure of remittance assets; referral to criminal prosecution for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Board members and compliance officers personally liable for AML failures; civil damages to affected customers; disqualification from management up to ten years; for willful money laundering, imprisonment up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may make a discretionary adjustment using CUP/TNMM proxies, assess additional distribution tax, impose penalty interest, and issue an enforcement penalty up to €2,500; repeated failure in a criminal context can trigger Penal Code § 399 liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify inaccurate TP documentation face personal fines; where the documentation was fabricated to support a tax evasion scheme, personal liability for the company's adjusted tax and joint seizure of assets applies.
