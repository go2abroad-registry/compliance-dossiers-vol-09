Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory minimum share capital for an LLC is EGP 50,000 (approx. USD 1,600), fully subscribed in equal-value shares and paid in cash into a provisional bank account at a licensed Egyptian bank before incorporation. The bank's capital deposit certificate is the statutory proof of payment; no escrow account or escrow agent is statutorily required. The deposited funds remain restricted until GAFI issues the Certificate of Incorporation and the First Board Resolution is presented to the bank.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and civil defense certification under Law No. 152 of 2001, issued by the Civil Defense Division of the Ministry of Interior, covering fire suppression systems, smoke detection, emergency exits, and evacuation plans. Structural load certification by a licensed engineering office is required for the floor bearing capacity of the SMT assembly lines. Occupational health and safety clearance by the Ministry of Manpower under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 is required, covering machine guarding, ESD grounding, fume extraction ventilation, personal protective equipment, and the occupational injury register.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), an agency of the Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing clearance portal: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must complete commercial registration with the Companies Registry (GAFI) to obtain the Commercial Registration Number; the LLC must then register with the ETA for the unified Electronic Tax ID (TIN/VAT ID) and tax card via the ETA electronic portal. The following notarized and legalized documents are required: certificate of incorporation and articles of association, board/shareholder resolutions authorizing establishment and tax registration, nationality/ownership proof, and audited financials or bank reference; non-resident shareholders must appoint an Egyptian tax representative or fiscal domiciliary agent with power of attorney. VAT registration is executed on Form No. 1 combined with the unified TIN application. The same TIN is used for income tax, VAT, stamp duty, customs, and e-invoicing, and must be activated on the ETA e-invoicing sandbox before issuing any cleared e-invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must complete commercial registration with the Companies Registry (GAFI) to obtain the Commercial Registration Number; the LLC must then register with the ETA for the unified Electronic Tax ID (TIN/VAT ID) and tax card via the ETA electronic portal. The following notarized and legalized documents are required: certificate of incorporation and articles of association, board/shareholder resolutions authorizing establishment and tax registration, nationality/ownership proof, and audited financials or bank reference; non-resident shareholders must appoint an Egyptian tax representative or fiscal domiciliary agent with power of attorney. VAT registration is executed on Form No. 1 combined with the unified TIN application. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Egyptian tax law. Statutory elections available include: (i) optional simplified presumptive tax regime for small taxpayers with annual gross revenue up to EGP 2 million under Articles 42-44 of the Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended; (ii) Free Zone investment regime elections affording customs/VAT exemptions and reclassified income treatment for export-oriented projects; and (iii) Special Economic Zone (SCZONE) incentives requiring an approval-based election at the GAFI/SCZONE Investor Services level. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized for domestic pass-through classification, but taxpayers may elect, subject to Egyptian transfer pricing documentation, the deemed profit method for export service income only where statutorily permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual turnover from taxable supplies during the preceding 12 months or reasonably expected in the coming 12 months, whichever threshold is first crossed; mandatory on the date immediately following breach of the EGP 500,000 threshold, with compulsory e-invoicing roll-out phased by the ETA according to taxpayer VAT registration cohort and annual revenue bands

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically leaving Egypt, direct supplies to recognized free zones, and qualifying international services supplied to non-resident recipients, are zero-rated (0% VAT) under law provided the taxpayer holds an authenticated export proof chain and, for services, can demonstrate the supply is effectively delivered and consumed outside Egypt. A PCBA contract manufacturer must identify each sale as directly destined to a foreign buyer or to an Egyptian licensed free zone customer and must not omit the designated zero-rating code in transactions within the ETA e-invoicing portal in order to trigger 0% instead of 14% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Cargo Manifest/Export Declaration Form) duly stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority evidencing exit of the goods from Egyptian territory
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued in the name of the exporter/consignee and evidencing physical departure within the declared period
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian commercial bank, or a certified bank statement showing receipt of the export proceeds in freely convertible foreign currency
  • Zero-rated VAT e-invoice cleared by the ETA with the statutory export zero-rating code and QR fiscal stamp
  • Commercial invoice/purchase order and shipment proof package retained for mandatory 5-year fiscal inspection and Foreign Trade Law compliance

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance: every VAT invoice issued by an e-invoicing-enrolled taxpayer must be transmitted to the ETA JSON-Schema/XML validation gateway via REST API, where it receives a unique invoice UUID (fiscal stamp) and an embedded QR code before being legally accepted. The e-invoicing mandate is compulsory for all VAT registrants in phases by turnover cohort; retail transactions are currently migrating to the parallel electronic receipt (e-receipt) clearing regime. An uncleared invoice is disregarded for input VAT deduction purposes, and the taxpayer receives a sealed electronic invoice record that must be preserved for five (5) fiscal years. ETA interoperability tests and digital signature certificates must be completed before live operation; the statutory mechanism prevents duplicate invoice numbering across the integrated system.

The ETA and Central Bank of Egypt jointly audit export transactions retrospectively; if the 180-day FX repatriation evidence or customs export exit documentation is missing, false, or the invoice was not cleared by the ETA e-invoicing system, the 0% rate is retroactively reversed to 14%, input VAT previously credited is clawed back, and the exporter is assessed a penalty equal to twice the VAT amount, delay fines, and suspension from export privileges. Material tax evasion, intentional issuance of forged export invoices, or misuse of free-zone zero-rating status triggers criminal prosecution, including imprisonment, and a statutory fine of up to EGP 50,000, in addition to revocation of refund claims with interest assessed at 2% per month, capped as provided by Law No. 67 of 2016, over a 5-year assessment window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the post-2022 flexible-exchange-rate reform, the Egyptian Pound (EGP) is convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods/services, profit remittance, and foreign-investment income) provided such transactions are routed through an authorised Egyptian bank with complete supporting trade documentation. The capital account remains administratively controlled: inward foreign direct investment (FDI) must be registered by the receiving bank for balance-of-payments purposes, and any subsequent dividend or capital remittance to non-resident shareholders requires traceability of the original inward capital transfer, a corporate tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, and settlement of the applicable dividend withholding tax (5%-10% depending on tax-treaty relief). Practical hurdles are primarily bank-level FX liquidity management, documentary scrutiny of large outward transfers, and the absence of a fully liberalised market for speculative or non-trade capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation timeline: export proceeds must be credited to the exporter's bank account in Egypt via an authorised bank within 90 days from the date of shipment for standard merchandise flows, with a maximum outer limit of 6 months (extendable by the managing bank or CBE where contract-manufacturing lead times justify an extension). Mandatory conversion ratio: under the current liberalised FX regime, the blanket conversion ratio has been removed; exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in domestic foreign-currency accounts and may earmark such proceeds against their own eligible import settlement obligations. However, all export receipts must physically enter Egypt through the banking system and be supported by the CBE export declaration filing; offshore collection or settlement through non-bank electronic channels is a regulatory violation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE; Arabic: البنك المركزي المصري), operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 concerning the Central Bank and Banking System, with additional FX and AML/CFT rulemaking authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Egyptian FX and banking law permits only CBE-licensed banks and CBE-registered payment-service providers to execute corporate trade settlements, FX, and capital movements with Egyptian counterparties. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) cannot act as the primary treasury bank for export-proceeds repatriation, capital injection, LC settlement, or hedging, and receiving export proceeds through an offshore EMI account would breach CBE repatriation rules. Such EMIs are usable only as ancillary collection wallets for non-core digital operating expenses, outside the regulated trade-payment flow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must transfer funds via SWIFT MT103 or TT into the LLC's pre-incorporation capital escrow account at a licensed Egyptian bank, with the sender's details and the purpose stated as 'foreign direct investment in Egypt' (balance-of-payments code G.INV for equity/invested capital). The receiving bank is legally obliged to report the inflow to the CBE through the balance-of-payments statistical system, to register the foreign investor's stake in the bank's investment register, and to issue an investment acknowledgement that later serves as the precondition for outbound profit or liquidation remittance. After commercial registration is completed and the capital-deposit certificate is issued, the escrowed funds are transferred into the company's operational EGP and foreign-currency current accounts.Approved clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 / TT for direct supplier payments covering components, tooling, and logistics, each supported by the CBE import-register filing, underlying invoices, and the authorised bank's import-transaction register; (2) SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit (sight, usance, or deferred payment) for high-value raw-material purchases; (3) SWIFT MT400 documentary collections for established trade counterparties; (4) MT202 cover payments for interbank settlement where applicable. All outbound FX payments are screened against CBE policy thresholds, the importer's registration status, and the customs import-monitoring system; a PCBA contractor should maintain an annual import/export budget lodged with the settlement bank to expedite transaction clearances.FX spot contracts (T+2 delivery) for converting export proceeds or funding supplier paymentsPlain-vanilla FX forwards (typically 1 to 12 months) available from authorised Egyptian banks against genuine underlying trade exposureFX swaps executed under a bilateral ISDA or similar master agreement with an authorised Egyptian bank for trade-related cash-flow matchingCross-currency interest-rate swaps for longer-term liability hedging on a request-for-quotation basis from top-tier banksNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executable offshore or, where the domestic deliverable market is impaired, with CBE-authorised banks under applicable documentation policy

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends distributed by an Egyptian LLC to resident and non-resident shareholders under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005. No additional Egyptian second-level taxation on the dividend recipient.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but Egyptian direct tax law and the ETA's anti-abuse enforcement apply a functional substance test. A PCBA contract-manufacturing LLC must demonstrate actual physical presence in Egypt: leased or owned factory floor, SMT lines and equipment, qualified operational staff, inventory warehousing and risk, and substantive board decision-making in Egypt. A letterbox entity that does not perform manufacturing activity will be construed as a sham or as a dependent agent P.E. of its parent, resulting in profit reattribution, denial of treaty relief, and imposition of CIT, late surcharge, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Statements

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Procedures Law No. 206 of 2020 and Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, willful evasion is punishable by imprisonment for a term of 3 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 20,000 to EGP 200,000 or twice the evaded tax, whichever is greater. Delivery of evaded tax and 2%-per-month surcharges is mandatory, plus confiscation of assets involved in the false accounting.

Director personal liability: Directors, managing directors, and signatories of the false returns can be prosecuted as principals. The corporate veil is pierced where the unlawful acts are attributable to an identifiable officer; travel bans, asset freezes, and passport confiscation are routinely imposed while the criminal audit is pending. Restitution orders may attach personal bank accounts.
Shell Company and Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The ETA is entitled to recharacterize the arrangement, disregard the Egyptian entity, or attribute profits to a deemed Egyptian P.E., assessing 22.5% CIT on the full PCBA manufacturing margin. Treaty benefits are denied and the parent is denied such profits in Egypt. The company may also be subject to fines for misleading the tax administration and customs declarations.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly uses the Egyptian entity as a shell or signs declarations that misrepresent the Egyptian operational presence can face civil recovery of the evaded tax plus interest. Where the façade is used to conceal foreign-controlled profits, the directors are jointly and severally liable and may be included in criminal tax evasion proceedings; personal travel bans are possible.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: ETA may impose a transfer-pricing adjustment that adds the difference between the actual margin and the arm's length median margin to Egyptian taxable income, subject to 22.5% CIT plus the 2% monthly late surcharge. Additional penalties for failing to submit the TP declaration and supporting documentation can reach EGP 500,000 per statutory demand.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax return and TP declaration containing an unsupportable margin may be considered participants in a tax fraud offense. They can be held personally liable to reimburse the additional CIT and penalties if the company is dissolved or the board members' insulation is pierced, and the responsible CFO may be subject to a travel ban during the audit.
Money Laundering / AML and Cross-Border Payment Irregularities

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 and subsequent amendments, the offense carries imprisonment of up to 7 years and a fine of up to EGP 1,000,000; additionally, the funds connected to money laundering are forfeited and the bank account is frozen. Failure to comply with AML obligations on corporate ownership and beneficial-ownership registration is separately enforceable by penalties and business license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who direct cross-border payment flows through LC and TT rails with the purpose of masking tax crimes are treated as money launderers; they face personal criminal liability and seizure of personal assets. The corporate veil does not protect the underlying natural persons when the company is used as the instrument of the offense, and the management face up to the same imprisonment as the corporate fine.
