Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation_With_Profit_Distribution_Trigger

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a check-the-box system. Statutory elections available to an Osaühing include: (i) election to apply the reduced 14% regular distribution withholding rate to qualifying profit distributions made within the first half of the year following taxable profit recognition (Income Tax Act § 50(3)); (ii) optional advance income tax payments; (iii) VAT group registration election under VAT Act § 7(7); and (iv) optional application to become a non-established taxable person liable for reverse-charge supplies in Estonia.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 — mandatory registration for resident taxable persons whose annual taxable supplies in Estonia exceed EUR 40,000; for non-resident distance sellers of goods the same EUR 40,000 threshold applies; non-resident providers of B2B services, including NVOCC freight forwarding, are generally not required to register because the reverse charge mechanism applies to their Estonian business customers, though voluntary normal registration remains available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under VAT Act § 16 applies to: (i) goods transported out of the EU by the supplier or a third party, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration (EXS); and (ii) cross-border international transport services of goods and directly related ancillary services, including ocean freight forwarding and NVOCC services that are provided in direct connection with the international carriage of goods to a destination outside the EU, or supplied to a principal freight carrier engaged in such carriage. The service must be clearly identifiable and separately itemised in the invoice; the exporter must retain objective evidence of the actual export movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EXS/SAD) in ICS bearing a valid export movement reference number
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading issued by the ocean common carrier or NVOCC
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and freight invoice
  • Packing list and container tracking / gate-in-out records evidencing actual departure from EU territory

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime exists in Estonia. Mandatory structured electronic invoicing applies to public-sector (B2G) transactions via the PEPPOL network and the Estonian national e-invoice operators. B2B e-invoicing is voluntary but must be machine-readable and authenticatable where used. All invoices must be archived for seven years and made available to MTA on request.

MTA conducts targeted risk-based audits of export zero-rating claims. Where the physical export cannot be documented or the payment is not repatriated, the zero rate is retrospectively reversed, 22% VAT is clawed back, statutory default interest accrues at 0.06% per day, and a tax evasion penalty of up to 30% of the assessed VAT may be imposed under the Tax Administration Act (MKS). Fraudulent claims with intent to evade VAT are criminalised under Penal Code § 384, carrying fines and imprisonment up to five years. Since 2021, MTA has also applied enhanced exchange of information with EU Member States to detect carousel and fictitious freight invoicing patterns in ocean freight forwarding.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro has been legal tender since 1 January 2011; Estonia maintains full capital and current account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU capital-movement legal framework. There are no exchange controls, prior-authorisation requirements, foreign-currency account restrictions, or limits on payments to non-residents. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted; the only fiscal friction is the Estonian distributed-profit tax: an OÜ is corporately tax-exempt on undistributed retained earnings, while distributions attract the 20/80 (20% effective) rate for ordinary dividends, the reduced 14/86 rate for regular periodic distributions, and 25% on certain deemed distributions or fringe benefit payments. Qualifying EU-parent EEA dividend flows can benefit from the Parent-Subsidiary Directive at 0% withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation: an NVOCC freight forwarder may retain export receivables indefinitely in any currency and may settle them directly into foreign-currency accounts. There is no statutory repatriation deadline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no central bank licence is required. The only compliance burden is statistical: the reporting Estonian credit institution submits transaction-level balance-of-payments data to Eesti Pank under the national reporting regulation and the ECB statistical framework, using the harmonised BPM6 purpose classification; the operator must therefore ensure all commercial invoices and SWIFT/SEPA narratives carry the counterparty country and the correct direct-investment or trade-services purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — the national central bank of the Republic of Estonia and member of the Eurosystem; monetary policy and euro issuance authority rests with the European Central Bank (ECB). Foreign exchange regulation is not separately administered: the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) supervises licensed credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions, while Eesti Pank operates balance-of-payments statistics collection and the TARGET2-Eesti settlement infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: High as a supplementary settlement rail for low-value operational and multi-currency virtual-IBAN payments (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut Business, Monese, Finom) authorised or passporting into Estonia under PSD2; Low as the sole or primary treasury platform for a licensed NVOCC operator. Legal constraints: the EMI must hold an Estonian e-money institution licence issued by Finantsinspektsioon or a valid EEA passport filed with the supervisory authority; safeguarded client funds are excluded from the Estonian Deposit Guarantee Fund; an EMI cannot open a statutory escrow account recognised by the commercial register, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, cannot provide bank guarantees to ocean carriers or port authorities, and generally applies transaction velocity and corridor sub-limits that are incompatible with high-value MT103 freight settlements. The enhanced AML risk overlay for NVOCC activities therefore restricts EMIs to low-threshold operating accounts rather than compliant institutional clearing rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder injects funds via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (in EUR) or SWIFT MT103/MT202 (in USD or other currency), with the payment narrative identifying the 'share capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan agreement' and the Estonian registry code of the OÜ. The receiving bank classifies the flow as direct investment under BPM6 category E1 (equity capital) or E2 (intercompany debt) for Eesti Pank balance-of-payments statistics. The beneficiary OÜ records the contribution in the e-Business Register within the statutory filing window; no minimum contribution is mandatory under the OÜ capital regime, but any paid quota capital is legally binding and evidenced by the bank credit summary. Source-of-funds verification requires the shareholder's audited financial statements or asset-sale certificate, and the bank may request a board resolution confirming the purpose and quantum. No prior Eesti Pank, Finantsinspektsioon, or exchange-control approval is required given Estonia's full capital-account liberalisation.Clearing infrastructure: (a) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR over TARGET2-Eesti and local clearing members for EU-based port handling, trucking, customs brokerage, pilotage, and feeder-vessel disbursements, using the ISO 20022 pain.001 message with structured remittance and booking references; (b) SWIFT MT103 over the correspondent network for non-EEA ocean carriers, foreign terminal operators, and overseas NVOCC counterparties, routed through the bank's EUR/USD nostro, with the commercial freight invoice and bill-of-lading supplemental documents submitted in the corporate-banking portal for all payments above the silent threshold of approximately EUR 25,000; (c) documentary trade instruments — MT700 (issuance of an irrevocable commercial letter of credit), MT707 (amendments), and MT760 (standby or shipping guarantee) — for high-value slot-charter and container-leasing contracts, conditioned upon presentation of a clean-on-board bill of lading, signed carrier receipts, and the delivered cargo manifest; (d) treasury sub-limits and standing-order liquidity sweeps to prevent overdraft incidents, with every payout reconciled to the NVOCC consolidation ledger and operating-cost account to preserve the clean 12-month distributed-profit horizon under Estonia's corporate tax framework.Outright FX Forward (EUR/USD 1M-12M) — concluded under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a bank master confirmation; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: High as a supplementary settlement rail for low-value operational and multi-currency virtual-IBAN payments (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut Business, Monese, Finom) authorised or passporting into Estonia under PSD2; Low as the sole or primary treasury platform for a licensed NVOCC operator. Legal constraints: the EMI must hold an Estonian e-money institution licence issued by Finantsinspektsioon or a valid EEA passport filed with the supervisory authority; safeguarded client funds are excluded from the Estonian Deposit Guarantee Fund; an EMI cannot open a statutory escrow account recognised by the commercial register, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, cannot provide bank guarantees to ocean carriers or port authorities, and generally applies transaction velocity and corridor sub-limits that are incompatible with high-value MT103 freight settlements. The enhanced AML risk overlay for NVOCC activities therefore restricts EMIs to low-threshold operating accounts rather than compliant institutional clearing rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the net amount of a taxable distribution, calculated by grossing up the net distribution at 22/78, effective from 1 January 2025. Estonia generally imposes corporate income tax when profits are distributed or otherwise deemed distributed, rather than on retained accounting profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by an Estonian resident company to a non-resident are generally not subject to separate Estonian dividend withholding tax under the rules applicable from 2025. The distributing OÜ generally bears corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net distribution. Recipient-country taxation and reporting remain separate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonian detailed transfer-pricing documentation requirements generally apply to specified categories of taxpayers and to entities meeting statutory size tests, including more than 250 employees, annual turnover exceeding EUR 50 million, or consolidated group turnover exceeding EUR 750 million; special rules apply to credit institutions, insurance undertakings, and listed companies. Master File applicability depends on the statutory conditions. Related-party transactions must be arm's length even where a documentation threshold is not met. Verify the current Income Tax Act and implementing regulation for the applicable Local File and Master File obligation.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have a general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Estonian transfer-pricing, anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, management-and-control, and EU anti-abuse rules remain applicable. An Estonian OÜ should maintain evidence that its freight-forwarding and NVOCC activities, personnel, decision-making, contracts, operational risks, and profits are consistent with its actual functions and control of risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax, statutory interest, and administrative penalties may apply. Intentional tax fraud may constitute a criminal offence under the Penal Code and can result in a fine or imprisonment; legal entities may face criminal monetary punishment. The sanction depends on intent, amount, and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability where statutory grounds are met, including culpable breach of tax obligations, and may be exposed to criminal liability for their own participation. Civil recovery, interim restrictions, and travel restrictions are not automatic and require an applicable legal basis and procedure.
Transfer-pricing misstatement and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust the taxable amount, assess corporate income tax on the deemed distribution or other taxable event, charge interest, and impose procedural penalties. Documentation failures may increase audit and adjustment risk.

Director personal liability: Directors must ensure appropriate controls and accurate reporting. Personal civil or criminal consequences require the relevant statutory basis and evidence of the director's conduct; they do not arise automatically from a company-level transfer-pricing adjustment.
Shell-company or sham-substance arrangements

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny claimed treatment under domestic or treaty anti-abuse rules, recharacterize transactions, assess tax and interest, and refer suspected fraud for investigation. Sanctions depend on the specific legal violation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own intentional misrepresentation, breach of statutory duties, or participation in an offence. Veil piercing, travel bans, and imprisonment are not automatic consequences of a low-substance structure.
AML, sanctions, and cargo or payment fraud

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include transaction blocking, regulatory reporting, administrative or criminal penalties, asset seizure, and restrictions under applicable Estonian, EU, and international sanctions and AML laws. The precise penalty depends on the applicable offence and facts.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible personnel may face personal administrative or criminal liability for their own knowing or culpable participation. Asset restraint or travel restrictions require legal authority and procedure; imprisonment is available only for offences carrying that penalty.
Failure to maintain tax, accounting, and transaction records

Statutory sanction: The company may face penalties for procedural breaches, loss of evidentiary support, denial of claimed treatment, tax reassessment, and interest. Retention and production duties depend on the applicable Estonian tax, accounting, and commercial-law requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for a culpable breach of recordkeeping or filing duties. Personal sanctions require an applicable statutory basis and are not automatic merely because company records are incomplete.
