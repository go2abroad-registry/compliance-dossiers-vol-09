Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Osaühing; share capital may be set at EUR 1.00 or higher in the foundation deed. The subscribed capital must be fully subscribed, and any cash contribution must be paid to a transaction account opened for the OÜ before registration unless the articles validly defer the contribution obligation. No statutory escrow account is required; if registration fails or is withdrawn, the temporary account funds are returned to the subscriber.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense-specific permit applies. If physical premises are occupied, the business must hold or be covered by the building's lawful use permit, fire safety certificate, and evacuation plan as required under the Fire Safety Act and Building Code; an occupational health inspection of the workstation and acoustic environment should be completed before employees commence work.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA, Maksu- ja Tolliamet). Digital portal: e-MTA at https://e-mta.mta.ee; e-Residency digital ID (Digi-ID) enables fully remote registration, filing, and payment without physical presence.

E-government filing portal

Form the Osaühing in the Commercial Register via e-Äriregister; the 8-digit registry code automatically becomes the corporate TIN and triggers automatic tax registration with MTA. Apply for VAT registration in e-MTA (KMKR number, format EE100000000) once taxable turnover reaches EUR 40,000, or earlier for B2C e-services; non-established foreign entities file the non-resident VAT application (Form V1/KATM) and may be required to appoint a local VAT representative. Foreign shareholders obtain no Estonian TIN; dividend withholding is handled by the resident payer. MTA issues the tax registration confirmation within 5 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Estonia applies a deferred/remittance CIT regime: an OÜ is taxed at 0% on undistributed profits; tax (20/80, i.e., 25% gross equivalent) arises only upon profit distribution (dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, certain non-business payments). Territorial aspect: tax is imposed on distributions from retained earnings, with CFC rules for captive offshore entities; no pass-through election exists for an OÜ. — Form the Osaühing in the Commercial Register via e-Äriregister; the 8-digit registry code automatically becomes the corporate TIN and triggers automatic tax registration with MTA. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. Estonia applies a deferred/remittance CIT regime: an OÜ is taxed at 0% on undistributed profits; tax (20/80, i.e., 25% gross equivalent) arises only upon profit distribution (dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, certain non-business payments). Territorial aspect: tax is imposed on distributions from retained earnings, with CFC rules for captive offshore entities; no pass-through election exists for an OÜ.

Available election forms

Election A (Section 50(3) Income Tax Act): a resident company whose shareholders are exclusively non-resident EU/EEA or treaty-state residents may elect regular corporate income taxation on worldwide income with foreign tax credits; election is binding for at least 5 tax years. Election B (Pillar Two): Estonian domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT) at 15% applies from 31 December 2023 to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million. Election C: ATAD2 hybrid mismatch rules disallow deductions for hybrid instruments/entities; no US-style check-the-box is available under Estonian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year for residents and non-residents (mandatory once exceeded; voluntary below). For intra-EU distance sales of B2C e-services, the Union-wide threshold is EUR 10,000 annually, above which the supplier must register via the OSS portal; non-EU suppliers of B2C e-services to Estonian consumers must register regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% applies to exports of goods to third countries and to intra-EU dispatches/supplies to VAT-registered customers (customer VAT number must be stated). For B2B services supplied to a customer established outside Estonia, the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction and the supply is outside Estonian VAT scope (0% with reverse charge), evidenced by the customer's valid VAT/GST number or business status. B2C digital e-services (automated subtitle transcription, AI voice cloning) supplied to EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's Member State via OSS; supplies to non-EU consumers are outside Estonian VAT. Substantiation: contracts, invoices, tax registration numbers, payment records, and IP-location evidence of the recipient.

  • Customs export declaration and MRN (EX/SAD) proving EU exit (required only for physical goods or film-media deliverables, not for purely remote services)
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, CMR/air waybill, or forwarder's arrival notice showing the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice referencing the zero-rated supply, signed service contract, and inward remittance certificate (SWIFT/SEPA credit advice) from the foreign customer

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping is implemented in Estonia; MTA audits VAT after filing. However, domestic B2B invoices must be issued as structure-based e-invoices (Estonian ATV format aligned with EN 16931) under Section 37(3) of the Accounting Act where the seller is technically capable; PDF/paper only allowed by exception. Invoice data must be retained digitally for 7 years. SAF-T or e-invoice reporting to the tax authority is not mandatory.

MTA selectively audits zero-rating claims, cross-checking customs exit records and banking/AML data. Missing or unreliable export evidence triggers VAT clawback at 22% plus statutory default interest and a misdeclaration surcharge under Section 154(2) of the Taxation Act of up to 10% of the understated tax. Reassessment window is 3 years, extended to 5 years for non-declared or fraudulent cases. Fabricated documentation is prosecuted as tax fraud under Penal Code Section 389 (imprisonment up to 5 years and/or fine) and triggers substance-over-form recharacterisation under Section 5 of the Income Tax Act. Estonia imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day period is a prudential export-file and bank lending benchmark.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area member state; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no foreign exchange controls, restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, or licensing requirements for cross-border payments within the EU. Profit remittance by an OÜ is subject only to Estonian distribution tax (20% on the gross distribution, 14/10% under certain conditions if a subsequent distribution is made to a resident individual), but no FX approval or conversion hurdle exists. For non-EU/EEA capital movements, Ireland-style notification thresholds under EU capital-movement free-movement rules may require statistical reporting to Eesti Pank, but no prior approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists under Estonian law to surrender export proceeds or to convert export receipts into euros. Residents may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign bank accounts. However, for EU balance-of-payments statistics, resident companies must report cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 10,000 per reporting period (quarterly) to Eesti Pank via the TING system (Turu- ja Infoteenuste Register). There is no compulsory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) and the Estonian Financial Services Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) under the aegis of the European System of Central Banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for payment services, Low for full banking functionality. Estonian law (Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act) allows licensed fintech EMIs (e.g., Stripe, Wise, PayPal) to open business accounts and process cross-border payments without a domestic bank account. However, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot hold escrow balances, issue guarantees, or provide lending. For an AI subtitle/dubbing studio with low AML risk and digital-first operations, Wise and Stripe are viable as primary settlement rails, subject to EMI-level KYC and sector-specific onboarding checks (e.g., proof of content licensing). Estonian e-Residency programme cooperates specifically with LHV, Swedbank, and Wise to enable remote account opening. Legal constraints include mandatory anti-money laundering due diligence under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (MLTFPA), and the requirement that EMIs provide full payment transparency (no anonymous accounts).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for inbound capital injections (share capital or shareholder loans) are: (i) remitter must use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code A0100 (or ISO 20022 `CTIHR` in SEPA/EU) for share capital; (ii) for shareholder loans, use purpose code A1000 (`SHLB`), and execute a loan agreement with an arm's-length interest rate if interest is charged; (iii) inbound transfers from non-EU countries must include a clear reference to the OÜ's registration number (registry code) and the beneficiary's bank account in Estonia; (iv) if the capi contribution exceeds EUR 10,000, report to Eesti Pank within the quarterly statistical return; (v) proof of source of funds (e.g., signed loan agreement, board resolution, share subscription form) must be retained and supplied to the Estonian bank upon first deposit. For e-Residency users, LHV and Wise offer automated 'SEPA instant' rails with business model validation requiring a digital product description and revenue examples.Outbound trade disbursements for subtitle/voice cloning services may be settled using: (i) SEPA CT (SEPA Credit Transfer) for EU/EEA payments—clearing in Europe within 4 hours (instant SEPA under 10 seconds); (ii) SWIFT MT103 for global payments (e.g., USD, GBP, JPY) with a stated BIC and IBAN; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) only for high-value non-EU production deals or if the counterparty requests documentary credit to mitigate non-delivery risk; (iv) open-account trade finance with invoice discounting if bank relationship is mature. For licensing-feet transactions (e.g., services to film distributors), the Estonian bank likely requires the underlying contract and an export invoice mentioning the revenue recognition method. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for payment services, Low for full banking functionality. Estonian law (Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act) allows licensed fintech EMIs (e.g., Stripe, Wise, PayPal) to open business accounts and process cross-border payments without a domestic bank account. However, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot hold escrow balances, issue guarantees, or provide lending. For an AI subtitle/dubbing studio with low AML risk and digital-first operations, Wise and Stripe are viable as primary settlement rails, subject to EMI-level KYC and sector-specific onboarding checks (e.g., proof of content licensing). Estonian e-Residency programme cooperates specifically with LHV, Swedbank, and Wise to enable remote account opening. Legal constraints include mandatory anti-money laundering due diligence under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (MLTFPA), and the requirement that EMIs provide full payment transparency (no anonymous accounts).
