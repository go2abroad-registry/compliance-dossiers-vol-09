Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, 'MTA'), headquartered in Tallinn; official administrative portal is 'e-MTA' (e-Maksuamet) at https://emta.ee/en/e-services, used for tax registration, VAT returns, OSS filings, customs clearance, and all direct tax compliance communications.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian Osaühing (OÜ) is incorporated online in the e-Business Register (e-äriregister / ariregister.rik.ee), ordinarily through an e-Residency digital identity. The issued registry code (äriregistri kood / registrikood) is the company's permanent statutory tax identification number and is transmitted ex officio to the MTA's tax register; no separate application for a TIN is required. Foreign founders may hold 100% of the share capital; there is no minimum capital requirement (a €0 minimum is permitted) and no physical presence is required. For VAT, the OÜ files an electronic VAT registration application in e-MTA upon exceeding the registration threshold or on a voluntary basis; for intra-EU B2B supplies the VAT number displayed is 'EE' plus the registry code for VIES validation. A non-EU seller without an Estonian fixed establishment may instead register under the non-Union OSS scheme or, where a local VAT registration is mandatory, appoint an Estonian tax representative; an EU-established seller uses the Union OSS scheme. All filings and taxpayer communications with MTA are completed through the e-MTA portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Estonia applies a distribution-based corporate income tax ('cash-flow CIT') rather than a classical accrual CIT: the OÜ is the taxpayer, but undistributed and reinvested profits are taxed at 0% (full deferral). Tax is triggered at the moment of profit distribution and on deemed distributions (dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, non-business costs, transfer-pricing and thin-capitalisation adjustments) at the 20/80 rate, i.e., 25% effective on the net distributed amount; a reduced 14/86 rate (16.3% effective) applies to regular recurring dividends within the three-year look-back mechanism. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Estonia applies a distribution-based corporate income tax ('cash-flow CIT') rather than a classical accrual CIT: the OÜ is the taxpayer, but undistributed and reinvested profits are taxed at 0% (full deferral). Tax is triggered at the moment of profit distribution and on deemed distributions (dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, non-business costs, transfer-pricing and thin-capitalisation adjustments) at the 20/80 rate, i.e., 25% effective on the net distributed amount; a reduced 14/86 rate (16.3% effective) applies to regular recurring dividends within the three-year look-back mechanism. Estonia is a unitary national jurisdiction with no sub-state tax distinction, no separate branch profits tax, no net wealth tax, and no local municipal corporate tax, so the regime is identical across the entire territory.

Available election forms

Estonian law contains no US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; all eligible commercial entities are taxed under the single distribution-based system. Available statutory elections relevant to an OÜ include: (1) 'regular dividend' relief — the taxpayer may elect to pay distributions on a periodic basis to access the reduced 14/86 withholding/CIT rate on the portion not exceeding the average taxable distributions of the preceding three tax years; (2) voluntary VAT registration before reaching the €40,000 turnover threshold where input VAT recovery is intended; (3) Union OSS scheme election for intra-EU B2C digital services, replacing individual member-state VAT registrations; (4) non-Union OSS election for a non-EU supplier; (5) VAT group registration under the Estonian VAT Act, permitting closely related Estonian entities to be treated as a single taxable person; and (6) for treaty benefits on outbound dividends, interest and royalties, an advance tax-clearance application to MTA is available to confirm reduced withholding rates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year (VAT Act § 3(3)); registration must be applied for in e-MTA within the statutory window once the threshold is exceeded, and voluntary early registration is permitted. For the OÜ's vertical micro-drama streaming operations the following nEXUS rules apply: (i) sales of digital content through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are treated as supplies made by the electronic interface (Apple/Google) under the deemed-supplier marketplace rules, so the OÜ is not required to Estonian VAT-register in respect of those channel proceeds; (ii) direct B2C sales completed via Stripe constitute supplies made by the OÜ — Estonian VAT registration is triggered at EUR 40,000 of domestic turnover, and Union OSS registration is required once intra-EU B2C distance sales of electronic services exceed EUR 10,000 in a calendar year (Union-wide distance-sales threshold); (iii) sales to non-EU B2C customers are outside the scope of Estonian VAT from the first euro and do not count toward the registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The OÜ may claim 0% export VAT treatment in the following archetypes: (1) physical export of goods to non-EU destinations under VAT Act § 33, evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration and exit confirmation; (2) services directly connected with exported goods (freight, processing, customs-related work on consignments under customs supervision); (3) intra-EU B2B supplies of digital drama-streaming services and related licences — the supply is reverse-charged to the customer and the Estonian supplier must obtain and validate the customer's EU VAT ID in VIES, resulting in a de facto 0% charge in Estonia; (4) sales through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing to EU and non-EU B2C consumers — the platform operator is the deemed VAT supplier, so the OÜ's channel proceeds fall outside Estonian VAT; (5) direct Stripe-based B2C sales to non-EU customers — place of supply is the customer's country of residence, outside the EU and outside the scope of Estonian VAT; the same sales to EU customers are taxable under Union OSS at the destination member state's rate. The OÜ must maintain a channel-level and geography-level revenue segregation to substantiate each zero-rating position.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export entry in the EU Export Control System / ECS) together with the exit confirmation from the competent customs office of export
  • Transport documentation evidencing physical departure from the EU: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order and Incoterms delivery terms linking the export declaration to the specific foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or licensed-bank foreign-exchange confirmation) evidencing actual receipt and repatriation of the export proceeds in convertible currency
  • For non-tangible digital micro-drama distribution via mobile apps and streaming: Apple App Store Financial Reports, Google Play payout statements, Stripe payout and transaction logs, IP-geolocation/billing-address evidence of customer residence, and VIES validation records for EU B2B customers, in lieu of customs documentation

Estonia does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) system and imposes no digital stamping or mandatory prior clearing of invoices; MTA uses risk-based audits, e-MTA VAT return data, OSS quarterly filings, customs ECS records, and payment-rail data for compliance analysis. Structured electronic invoicing (Estonian e-invoice format, PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / UBL) is legally recognised, and B2G supplies to Estonian public-sector entities require mandatory e-invoicing. For the OÜ: (i) Apple/Google store sales are documented via platform payout reports (Apple Financial Report, Google Play financial report/payout file), which serve as the primary fiscal records for deemed-supplier sales; (ii) direct Stripe sales require issuance of simplified invoices for B2C transactions (no e-invoicing obligation) and full invoices containing the customer's valid EU VAT ID for B2B reverse-charge sales; (iii) OSS returns are filed quarterly in e-MTA by the last day of the month following the calendar quarter, with no additional fiscal clearance or API-based real-time reporting.

MTA intensively cross-checks export zero-rating and OSS positions against EU customs transit data, VIES records, bank treasury inflows, and platform payout reports; digital-economy exporters without physical customs declarations face elevated scrutiny of their substantiation files. An unsupported zero-rating claim is reclassified as a domestic 22% VAT supply, giving rise to an additional VAT assessment, daily default interest (statutory arrears rate anchored to the ECB main refinancing rate plus 7 percentage points, accrued daily — approximately 0.06% per day in 2024), and, in cases of deliberate misdeclaration or fraudulent refund claims, criminal liability under Penal Code § 350 (tax fraud: fine and/or imprisonment up to 3 years; aggravated cases involving large-scale evasion, organised false documentation or substantial damage: imprisonment up to 5 years and confiscation orders; legal entities are additionally exposed to criminal fines). The MTA's retrospective assessment power runs three years from the calendar year of the supply, extended to seven years where tax was intentionally concealed. In the OSS context, persistent non-compliance can result in exclusion from the Union OSS scheme and revocation of the Estonian VAT number. Unrepatriated export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days without commercial justification may be recharacterised as a constructive dividend (subject to 20/80 CIT on deemed distribution) or as a shareholder loan attracting deemed-interest transfer-pricing adjustments.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a Eurozone member; the euro (EUR) is fully legal tender. Capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted under EU Treaty provisions (TFEU Articles 63–65) and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act. There are no exchange-control approvals, augmented surrender duties, or restrictions on dividend remittances, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds, or portfolio capital movements. The only structural friction for profit distribution is the Estonian corporate income tax (CIT) regime: undistributed profits are taxed at 0%, while distributions trigger a corporate-level income tax – generally 20% of the gross distribution (effective 20/80 on net dividend), or 14% for certain regular distributions from retained earnings. No dividend withholding tax is imposed on outbound payments, and no further profit-remittance license is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonian resident companies may hold export proceeds indefinitely in any currency, in Estonia or abroad. No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. However, for balance-of-payments statistics, cross-border flows must be reported to Eesti Pank with the correct transaction purpose code (e.g., 'T' for goods trade, 'TE' for services trade) if a threshold is crossed. No surrender to the central bank or commercial banks is permitted to be demanded.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) – e.g., Wise, Revolut, Payoneer – may passport their license into Estonia and provide virtual IBANs in the company's name. This is legally viable for merchant collection and disbursement, but not recommended as a primary treasury rail for an OÜ because: (a) EMIs are not full-license credit institutions under the Estonian Credit Institutions Act; (b) they do not provide overdraft, LC, or robust FX hedging; (c) the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA) may view EMI accounts as less transparent for VAT and CIT verification. An EMI can be used for low-value cross-border receipts from advertising platforms and micro-payment wallets, but settlement quality and KYC escalation should be proven. Key legal constraint: the EMIs must hold an Estonian or EEA license recognised by the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (FSA), and their service must not be for 'shell' or market-making purposes.

Defense evidence: Drafted copy-right and IP licences; production and delivery contracts referencing ISIC 5911; transfer-pricing documentation for related-party transactions; confirmation of no taxable presence in the destination; valuations from a creative-industries appraiser; and a written position from Estonian tax counsel confirming no royalty withholding mismatch.
Foreign directors appearing in public records but lacking Estonian/Tax presence

Root cause: Foreign directors appearing in public records but lacking Estonian/Tax presence

Defense evidence: Valid e-Residency digital ID scan; biometric passport; subsistence evidence for Estonia (if any); a bank reference for the individual; and a sworn statement disclosing all other directorships and beneficial ownership. If a director resides in a high-risk jurisdiction, a negative-news search and an independent sanctions report from a reputable screening provider become mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an Estonian OÜ, inbound equity injections from foreign shareholders are fully liberalized. Recommended routing: a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer to the company's Estonian IBAN with the payment narrative 'Shareholder equity injection – [OÜ name]'. The receiving bank should be instructed to classify the transaction as 'Foreign Direct Investment' (statistical purpose code 'D') and to include the Estonian Business Registry code in the unstructured reference. No central bank filing or permit is required. For verification, the company must provide a certified share-capital-resolution, an extract from the Estonian Business Registry, and a bank reference from the sending institution confirming the source of funds. If the injection is a shareholder loan, the loan agreement, an interest-rate schedule, and a board resolution authorising the borrowing must accompany the first disbursement; the transaction must be entered in the OÜ's accounting records with full accrual recognition.For global distribution of vertical micro-dramas, the primary clearing rails are: (i) SEPA Instant Credit and SWIFT MT103 for high-value royalty disbursements to EU/EEA licensors; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border license fees outside the EU (e.g., US, UK, Asia) with a reference to the underlying commercial invoice; and (iii) a documentary clean collection or a commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) for production financing or large content acquisitions where payment security is required. There is no exchange-control restriction on outbound disbursements from Estonia. All outbound service payments must reflect the VAT reverse-charge mechanism per the Estonian VAT Act, and a valid TIN/EORI identification must be recorded. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium. EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) – e.g., Wise, Revolut, Payoneer – may passport their license into Estonia and provide virtual IBANs in the company's name. This is legally viable for merchant collection and disbursement, but not recommended as a primary treasury rail for an OÜ because: (a) EMIs are not full-license credit institutions under the Estonian Credit Institutions Act; (b) they do not provide overdraft, LC, or robust FX hedging; (c) the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA) may view EMI accounts as less transparent for VAT and CIT verification. An EMI can be used for low-value cross-border receipts from advertising platforms and micro-payment wallets, but settlement quality and KYC escalation should be proven. Key legal constraint: the EMIs must hold an Estonian or EEA license recognised by the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (FSA), and their service must not be for 'shell' or market-making purposes.
