Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - Ministry of Finance; electronic services portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg and mandatory e-invoicing/e-receipt platform at https://einvoice.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited liability company must first complete commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), then register with the Egyptian Tax Authority before starting operations. Submission requirements include the certified incorporation/commercial registration certificate and memorandum of association; registered office lease; board resolution appointing a tax representative with notarized and consularized power of attorney; passport and national ID copies of shareholders and managers; and a completed ETA unified tax registration form. On approval, ETA issues the Tax Card (TIN), VAT registration certificate (VAT ID), and e-invoicing credentials on the ETA digital portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005; there is no pass-through classification. — A foreign-owned limited liability company must first complete commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), then register with the Egyptian Tax Authority before starting operations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005; there is no pass-through classification.

Available election forms

None. Egypt does not provide check-the-box or unilateral pass-through elections; classification follows the LLC legal form under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981. Anti-hybrid and related mismatches are limited by Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended, in line with BEPS Action 2; no elective entity classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is required when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; taxable persons with annual supplies above EGP 50,000 may voluntarily register. A taxpayer incorporated as an LLC is nevertheless expected to complete VAT registration as part of tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of ANC headphones and smart audio equipment qualifies for 0% VAT where the goods are physically exported to a destination outside Egypt under an Egyptian customs export declaration, the sale is made to an overseas customer, transport documents show departure from Egypt, and the foreign-currency proceeds are credited through an Egyptian bank. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated export supplies is recoverable by offset or refund subject to ETA documentary verification.

  • Customs export declaration approved by Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Original bill of lading or airway bill, or CMR/international transport document
  • Inward remittance certificate and bank credit advice evidencing foreign-currency receipt
  • Commercial invoice and corresponding ETA-cleared export VAT invoice with QR code
  • Packing list and sales contract or purchase order
  • Certificate of origin and any statutory export permit if required

Registered taxpayers must issue all B2B and B2G invoices (and, in covered phases, B2C receipts) through ETA-approved e-invoicing/e-receipt channels in real time. Files are transmitted in JSON/XML through the ETA API or seller portal using a registered digital certificate; ETA clears each document, returns a unique code/UUID, and requires a QR code and ETA control number to be printed on the invoice. Invoices are rejected or suspended if QR code, tax treatment, or XML schema validation fails.

ETA and customs audits may retroactively disallow export zero-rating when the goods do not leave Egypt, the customs declaration is false or incomplete, or export proceeds are not received and repatriated within 180 days. In that event, ETA assesses 14% VAT as if the supply were taxable domestically, adds late payment penalties and interest, and may impose statutory evasion fines and criminal prosecution under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016. ETA can also suspend the taxpayer's e-invoicing clearance credentials, freeze VAT credits and refunds, and coordinate with customs and the Central Bank of Egypt for cargo holds and entity blacklisting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the 6 March 2024 CBE policy shift, Egypt operates a flexible, market-determined exchange rate regime with the official interbank rate unified with the parallel market; the EGP is fully convertible for all current-account transactions (goods, services, royalties, licence fees, profit and dividend remittances) provided the underlying documentation is verifiable. Capital-account transactions are partially open: foreign capital injections, intercompany loans, and liquidation proceeds must be registered with GAFI and routed through a licensed bank with a designated capital (escrow) account; remittance of dividends is subject to audited financial statements, full settlement of corporate income tax and withholding taxes, and an ETA tax clearance certificate. The central remaining hurdle is documentation density and bank-level de-risking rather than a CBE quota or prior-approval requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion or surrender ratios exist under the current regime; the CBE requires exporters to repatriate export proceeds into an Egyptian licensed bank within 90 days of shipment (extendable to 180 days for approved non-traditional markets) and to deposit them into a foreign-currency retention account or convert to EGP at market rate at the exporter's discretion. Export proceeds held in foreign currency may be used to pay import obligations without separate CBE approval, but each receipt and usage is screened against the underlying customs export declaration, bill of lading, and ACI customs registration; retention beyond 180 days without a demonstrable trade need is flagged as a red flag by the EMLCU.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Banking Supervision Sector and Foreign Exchange Control Department; the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU) is the Financial Intelligence Unit under the CBE; the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) administers foreign investment registration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low - offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Rapyd) are not licensed by the CBE and cannot lawfully hold funds for an Egyptian treasury or intermediate export-proceeds collection without an in-country licensed bank partner; foreign EMIs are treated as high-risk non-bank counterparties (FATF Recommendation 16 Travel Rule and EMLCU supervision), and their use as a primary clearing rail would trigger cascading correspondent-bank de-risking. Egyptian e-payment licences are granted under National Payment Council Law 18/2019 only to entities with local presence, so a foreign EMI cannot replace a corporate bank account; the compliant architecture is a domestic licensed bank as the primary rail, with EMI rails confined to sub-threshold (under USD 5,000) marketplace platform settlements passed through the licensed bank's reconciliation layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where annual aggregate controlled transactions exceed EGP 5,000,000; Master File mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding EGP 5,000,000,000; Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding approximately EGP 12,000,000,000, in line with the ETA Transfer Pricing Guidelines effective 1 January 2020.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to the Gulf jurisdictions, but economic substance is enforced through (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Income Tax Law No. 91/2005, Article 85, which allows recharacterisation of transactions lacking business purpose; (ii) beneficial-ownership clauses in Egypt's DTAs, which deny treaty relief to shell conduits; (iii) free-zone and SCZone licence conditions requiring genuine physical premises, local payroll, manufacturing operations, and management in Egypt; and (iv) ETA transfer-pricing documentation that must evidence the functional, asset, and risk profile of the Egyptian LLC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Records and Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 3-5 years and a fine of EGP 50,000-5,000,000 under Articles 133-134 of Income Tax Law No. 91/2005; reassessment of undeclared profit for up to 5 years (extended in fraud cases); late-payment surcharge of 2% per month on the whole shortfall, capped at 100% of the tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, de facto managers, and signing shareholders who approved or concealed the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax, fines, and surcharges; the ETA may impose precautionary attachment, impose travel bans, and pursue criminal imprisonment against individuals.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The LLC as withholding agent is personally liable for the full uncollected WHT plus a monthly 2% surcharge (capped at 100%); the underlying expense is denied as a deduction; administrative fines under Law No. 206/2020 apply; hidden distributions may be recharacterised as taxable dividends attracting a second-layer 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the remittance instructions are personally liable to restore the unwithheld tax to the treasury; the ETA can obtain pre-judgment attachment of their personal assets and a travel ban pending settlement.
Shell Company / Abuse of Free-Zone or SCZone Incentives

Statutory sanction: Retroactive withdrawal of the 50%-80% investment deduction and/or the free-zone CIT exemption; reassessment at the full 22.5% CIT rate with 2% monthly late-payment surcharge; revocation of the SCZone/free-zone licence; GAAR recharacterisation under Article 85 of Law No. 91/2005; mandatory adjustment of any artificially shifted profit.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to repay the value of tax benefits improperly obtained, disqualified from holding board positions in Egypt under Companies Law No. 159/1981, and, if the shell structure enabled tax evasion, prosecuted criminally with imprisonment of up to 5 years and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Pricing

Statutory sanction: Burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer; the ETA may adjust the profit to an arm's-length level, deny deduction of the excess royalty, impose the statutory TP documentation penalty, and treat the excess payment as a constructive dividend subject to the 10% WHT; reassessment is backed by the 2% monthly surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length pricing as an indirect profit distribution are jointly liable for the additional CIT, WHT, and surcharges; aggravated cases are prosecuted as tax evasion, exposing individuals to asset attachment, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering / Informal Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended), fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years for laundering funds, freezing orders, confiscation of proceeds, and administrative licence revocation; simultaneous prosecution for tax evasion adds up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines of EGP 50,000-5,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are individually prosecutable for failure to maintain AML/KYC controls even in a low-risk consumer-audio manufacturing business; personal assets are exposed to forfeiture, freezing orders, and travel bans, and conviction may lead to disqualification from managing corporate entities in Egypt.
