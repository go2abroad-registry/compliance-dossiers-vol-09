Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Äriregister), operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (KIK) under the Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Estonia, Tartu; all filings transmitted digitally via the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister) portal.

Governing statute

Commercial Code of the Republic of Estonia (Äriseadustik, RT I 1995, 26, 355) Chapter 7, §§ 134–166 (Osaühing); § 136 (founding agreement), § 141 (share capital payment), § 142(2) (minimum capital EUR 0.01 derogation), § 176 (management board); read with the State Fees Act (Riigilõivuseadus) § 59 and the General Part of the Economic Activities Code Act (MTÜS, RT I 2011, 11, 25) §§ 1–3 (economic-activity freedom).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Entry in the Commercial Register (Äriregistri registreerimisavaldus), submitted as a structured, digitally signed e-form through the e-Business Register portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Estonian law imposes no fixed minimum share capital for an Osaühing; the articles may validly fix share capital at the statutory derogation of EUR 0.01, or at a higher subscribed amount such as EUR 2,500 for contractual and credit confidence. Cash contributions must be fully paid and credited, prior to or at the time of filing, to a formation/deposit account opened in the future company's name at an Estonian credit institution (Swedbank, SEB, Luminor, LHV, Coop Pank), or to a notary's escrow; non-cash contributions require a sworn valuator's report to be attached to the founding agreement. Distribution safeguard: if paid share capital is below EUR 2,500, the company's net assets after any dividend must not fall below EUR 2,500.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: before any office or warehouse is occupied, the operator must file a fire-safety declaration (tuleohutusdeklaratsioon) with Päästeamet, maintain evacuation plans, and hold current fire-extinguisher and alarm certificates; premises with more than ten persons require a staged fire-safety audit. Occupational clearance: the employer must perform a written workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, register with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon), certify personnel in load securing and rigging for out-of-gauge cargo, and verify the load-bearing capacity of any leased storage floor (minimum 2 t/m² safe working load) before receiving heavy machinery.

Paid-in capital rule: Estonian law imposes no fixed minimum share capital for an Osaühing; the articles may validly fix share capital at the statutory derogation of EUR 0.01, or at a higher subscribed amount such as EUR 2,500 for contractual and credit confidence. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

None at formation - Estonian law subjects freight-forwarding and logistics arrangement (ISIC 5229) to the general economic-activity freedom of the General Part of the Economic Activities Code Act and not to a special industrial licence. Conditional successor authorities: Estonian Transport Administration (Transpordiamet) if the company later operates road vehicles over 3.5 t; Environmental Board (Keskkonnaamet) if a physical OOG cargo yard or terminal is established; Estonian Rescue Board (Päästeamet) for fire-safety approvals of leased premises.

Category: No statutory operating licence is required for the incorporation-stage project-cargo logistics arrangement activity. If the company later operates vehicles in international road haulage, it must hold a Community Licence for the carriage of goods by road under Regulation (EC) No 1072/2009 and employ a certified professional transport manager under the Road Transport Act; for own-account out-of-gauge haulage between project sites, a national haulage activity licence is required, subject to financial standing, good repute, and professional competence checks.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA), administered electronically through the e-MTA portal. The Estonian Commercial Register, maintained by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems, issues the company registry code used as the principal domestic tax identifier.

E-government filing portal

An Osaühing obtains its statutory identity by incorporation in the Estonian Commercial Register. The assigned eight-digit registry code functions as the domestic taxpayer identifier. A non-resident entity must register with EMTA when it has an Estonian permanent establishment, taxable activity, employment-tax obligations, or another statutory Estonian tax nexus. VAT registration is requested from EMTA through e-MTA or by an authorized representative using the prescribed VAT registration application, supported by constitutional documents, registry extract, business activity description, management and ownership information, expected Estonian taxable turnover, contracts or invoices evidencing economic activity, and representative authorization where applicable. Upon approval, EMTA issues an Estonian VAT number in the format EE plus the company registry code. E-Residency facilitates digital administration but does not itself establish tax residence, permanent establishment, or VAT liability.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no U.S.-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no general election to convert an OÜ into a fiscally transparent entity for Estonian tax purposes. The OÜ is a separate legal and tax person. Estonian corporate income tax is generally imposed when profits are distributed or otherwise treated as distributed, rather than when profits are earned and retained. From 1 January 2025, the principal distributed-profit rate is 22/78 of the net distribution. Applicable EU and Estonian anti-hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions, impose inclusion, or otherwise neutralize hybrid arrangements; they are statutory anti-abuse rules and not elective entity-classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply arising in Estonia during a calendar year, generally measured under the statutory turnover rules applicable from 1 January 2025. The threshold does not universally protect a non-resident: a non-resident may be required to register from the first relevant Estonian taxable supply where the statutory conditions apply, subject to reverse-charge, special scheme, and other statutory exceptions. Voluntary registration may be available before the threshold is reached. The 24% standard rate applies from 1 July 2025; reduced rates and exemptions apply only where specifically provided by law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier or its agent transports or dispatches the goods out of the EU and the export is supported by customs evidence showing exit from the EU. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% where the customer is VAT-identified in another EU Member State, the goods are dispatched to that Member State, and the supplier holds the required customer-VAT and transport evidence and reports the transaction correctly. International transport of goods and services directly connected with qualifying export or import transactions may qualify for 0% under the statutory provisions. Many cross-border logistics services supplied to a business customer are instead outside the scope of Estonian VAT under place-of-supply rules, rather than being Estonian zero-rated supplies. Export treatment must therefore be determined separately for freight, handling, storage, customs brokerage, ancillary services, and bundled project-cargo services. Input VAT refunds require valid VAT invoices, a direct business connection, proper accounting, and evidence that the input tax is deductible.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and assigned MRN
  • Customs confirmation of release for export and evidence of exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and project-cargo or freight contract
  • Bill of lading, CMR consignment note, airway bill, or other transport document appropriate to the transport mode
  • Evidence identifying the goods, consignor, consignee, destination, and transport route
  • Proof of delivery or equivalent independent transport evidence where required to substantiate the export or intra-Community movement
  • Customer VAT identification evidence for an intra-Community supply, where applicable
  • Payment evidence and correspondence supporting the underlying transaction where requested by EMTA
  • Inward remittance certificate: not a general Estonian statutory requirement for export VAT zero-rating

Estonia does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance system for all domestic business-to-business transactions. No universal requirement exists for prior electronic stamping or continuous transmission of every invoice to EMTA. VAT invoices must satisfy the Estonian Value Added Tax Act and be retained in an auditable electronic or paper form. Structured electronic invoicing is supported through commercial service providers and is relevant to public-sector procurement and public-sector invoice-receipt requirements. The issuer must maintain invoice integrity, transaction evidence, VAT records, customs evidence, and accounting data for the statutory retention period and must provide them to EMTA on request. E-invoicing, API, and data-submission obligations may arise from the customer, public procurement contract, accounting platform, or future legislative changes, but do not currently constitute a blanket real-time clearance mandate.

Estonia has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. EMTA may deny or retrospectively withdraw 0% treatment where export, transport, customer VAT status, place of supply, or transaction authenticity is not substantiated. The resulting VAT is assessed at the applicable rate, together with statutory late-payment interest and potentially procedural fines. Incorrect VAT returns may require correction and repayment of improperly refunded input VAT. Fraudulent invoices, fictitious exports, concealed turnover, deliberate tax evasion, or document falsification may trigger administrative liability and, where the statutory criminal thresholds and intent requirements are met, criminal prosecution, recovery of tax loss, interest, and confiscatory or other court-imposed measures. Records should be retained and reconciled across customs declarations, transport documentation, invoices, VAT returns, general ledger entries, and bank receipts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full member of the European Economic and Monetary Union; the euro is legal tender. There are no exchange controls or restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Dividend distributions, loan repayments, intercompany transfers, and profit remittances to non-residents are freely permitted without regulatory approval, subject only to standard AML/CFT verification and tax distribution rules under the Estonian Income Tax Act (distributed profits taxed at 20%, or 14% where the exemption applies). Full currency convertibility exists within the euro area and for all foreign currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or forced domiciling of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances at licensed credit institutions. The Foreign Exchange Act and EU regulations provide complete free movement of capital. Banks apply transaction-monitoring thresholds and require adequate supporting documents (contracts, invoices, CMR/BL) for any payment above €10,000 under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, including statistical reporting to Eesti Pank when applicable.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the central bank of the Republic of Estonia and a member of the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-to-high for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating through EEA passporting rights. Such EMIs can provide euro IBANs, multi-currency accounts, and cross-border payment rails. However, for a heavy industrial project cargo logistics OÜ, most EMIs are not optimal for large-value milestone payments because of lower transaction limits, enhanced scrutiny on high-risk logistics activities, and lack of full corporate treasury services. Off-EEA offshore digital EMIs have very low viability and create legal and banking reputation risks; a regulated Estonian or EU credit institution remains the recommended anchor for treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan financing is completely unrestricted in Estonia. Recommended routing: SWIFT MT103 wire for non-EEA transfers or SEPA CT for euro transfers from EEA. Banks require the remitter's bank to include an appropriate purpose code: for direct investment or equity injection, use the EBICS/equivalent purpose category 'CF0/CF1' or 'Grants and operational subsidies' where the provider instructs; Eesti Pank does not require official prior approval but requires statistical classification. The receiving Estonian bank will request the board resolution approving the capital injection, the updated shareholding agreement, a shareholder register extract, and documentary confirmation that the amount matches the subscribed share capital or loan agreement. The transaction narrative must state 'Capital injection' or 'Shareholder loan' to avoid being flagged.For project cargo heavy-haul operations, outbound trade settlements shall be executed via: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign-currency supplier payments, with full remittance information; (2) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for large equipment purchases, especially from non-EEA suppliers; (3) SEPA CT for intra-EEA payments in EUR; (4) bank guarantees and standby letters of credit when required by vendors; and (5) standard bank payment undertakings or supply-chain finance tools where accepted. All disbursements must be paired with transport documents, insurance certificates, Incoterms declarations, and self-invoice matching to satisfy Estonian bank e-KYC and AML filters. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed profit; 20% on profit distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% (standard); 14% for regular dividends to Estonian resident individuals; final withholding at OÜ level with no further shareholder-level tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File/Master File threshold; Tax and Customs Board may demand documentation for any controlled transaction. Recommended to maintain Local File when annual cross-border related-party revenue exceeds EUR 150,000; CbCR applies to groups with consolidated group revenue >= EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Estonia applies the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) rather than offshore ESR. OÜ must demonstrate real management, personnel, assets, and project-risk control in Estonia; otherwise treaty relief is denied and distributions are recharacterized. For project cargo heavy haul, evidence of shipping coordination, invoicing, contract negotiation, and document control in Estonia is essential.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealed Dividend

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as profit distribution; additional 20% tax, 0.06% daily late interest, 25% penalty on underpaid tax; criminal penalty under Penal Code § 384 (tax fraud).

Director personal liability: Civil liability for the company's deficits via veil piercing; director travel ban in cross-border enforcement; imprisonment up to 5 years for intentional tax evasion.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Judicial strike-off under Commercial Code § 64-66; retrospective deemed distribution tax at 20%; fine up to €5,000; possible liquidation of company assets.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for liabilities if the corporate form is abused; director disqualification up to 3 years and a market-entry ban.
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €400,000 under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; freezing of accounts; revocation of e-MTA access and VAT registration.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability for money laundering under Penal Code § 394: imprisonment up to 8 years and mandatory asset confiscation; board-level AML oversight liability.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment by the Tax Board; additional CIT at 20% on the adjusted deemed distribution; interest and financial penalty up to 30% of the adjustment amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved unbenchmarked prices can be held personally liable for the refined tax and a ban from directorship; typical fine range €1,000–€10,000.
