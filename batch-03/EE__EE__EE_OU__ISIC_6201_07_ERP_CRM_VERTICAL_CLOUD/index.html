Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register Department of Tartu County Court, administered through the Estonian e-Business Register (e-Äriregister) maintained by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK).

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), primarily Sections 135-154 governing private limited companies (osaühing), including formation, articles, share capital, management board, and registration; Sections 62-64 govern commercial register filings and registered particulars.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for entry in the commercial register (private limited company registration application), accompanied by the articles of association, formation resolution or agreement, management board consents, and beneficial ownership information.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An OÜ may be formed with share capital of at least EUR 0.01 per shareholder, unless the articles prescribe a higher amount. Contributions must be fully paid in accordance with the formation documents. A statutory escrow account is not generally required; a bank account or payment procedure must be used where required by the selected electronic or notarial formation method. The contribution may be monetary or, subject to statutory valuation and documentation rules, non-monetary.

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial clearance is required for ordinary office operations. The premises must comply with the Building Code, applicable use-purpose and occupancy requirements, Rescue Act fire-safety obligations, emergency exits, fire detection and extinguishing arrangements, and any local building or use permit conditions. Structural load and fit-out compliance must be verified by the owner, designer, or competent professional where construction or alteration occurs. Employer obligations under the Occupational Health and Safety Act apply, with oversight by the Labour Inspectorate, including risk assessment, safe workstation design, emergency procedures, and employee training.

Paid-in capital rule: An OÜ may be formed with share capital of at least EUR 0.01 per shareholder, unless the articles prescribe a higher amount. Contributions must be fully paid in accordance with the formation documents. A statutory escrow account is not generally required; a bank account or payment procedure must be used where required by the selected electronic or notarial formation method. The contribution may be monetary or, subject to statutory valuation and documentation rules, non-monetary.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) — e-Tax/e-Customs portal (emta.ee / e-maksuamet)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains its statutory tax code by first registering with the Estonian Business Registry (Äriregister) and obtaining a registry code. Where taxable supplies in Estonia create a VAT registration obligation, the foreign entrepreneur must file an online VAT registration application (form VTA-E or equivalent) with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board through the e-Tax/e-Customs portal. A non-EU applicant is normally required to appoint a tax representative resident in Estonia or in an EU Member State with mutual assistance, while an EU entrepreneur may register directly but must provide evidence of economic activity. The assigned VAT number is the TIN for tax administration purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity obtains its statutory tax code by first registering with the Estonian Business Registry (Äriregister) and obtaining a registry code. Where taxable supplies in Estonia create a VAT registration obligation, the foreign entrepreneur must file an online VAT registration application (form VTA-E or equivalent) with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board through the e-Tax/e-Customs portal. A non-EU applicant is normally required to appoint a tax representative resident in Estonia or in an EU Member State with mutual assistance, while an EU entrepreneur may register directly but must provide evidence of economic activity. The assigned VAT number is the TIN for tax administration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election exists. An Osaühing is mandatorily subject to the Estonian corporate income tax deferral system, under which undistributed profits carry 0% current corporate tax and tax applies at the time of profit distribution, disguised distribution, or other deemed distribution at a rate of 20/80 of the gross amount. Taxable persons may elect to form a VAT group for VAT purposes where the enterprises are established in Estonia and have financial, economic, and organisational links; small businesses may optionally elect the small-business VAT scheme until the turnover threshold is exceeded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover in the preceding calendar year, or EUR 40,000 from the beginning of the calendar year; registration becomes obligatory from the day after the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate applies to: (i) export of goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier, a customer established outside Estonia, or on behalf of either, evidenced by customs export declaration and exit confirmation; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a taxable person in another EU Member State; and (iii) certain services related to exports, transfer of rights in immaterial property or services supplied to non-EU taxable persons where the recipient is registered outside Estonia, subject to the standard proof-of-departure rules.

  • Customs export declaration (EX) with customs office exit confirmation
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Estonian supplier
  • Bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill proving physical departure from the EU
  • Contract, purchase order, or written order confirmation
  • Bank credit advice or SWIFT payment confirmation evidencing settlement
  • Intra-Community sales listing (if applicable for EU-27 B2B goods supplies)

Estonia does not apply continuous transaction controls or real-time fiscal clearance through a central government stamping system. Electronically issued invoices must comply with the EU e-invoice standards (e.g. UBL XML or Finvoice-compatible formats) and be accepted when the customer consents. All VAT returns and VAT data declarations are filed electronically through the e-Tax/e-Customs portal, which provides API-based integration for accounting and ERP systems. Domestic B2B e-invoice exchange operates on market-based operator networks, but paper or PDF invoices remain legally permissible unless a private agreement requires e-invoicing.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may retroactively assess unpaid VAT if export documentation is incomplete or the actual exit of goods is not substantiated. The assessment period is generally three years from the statutory filing deadline, extending to six years where the infringement is concealed. Late VAT attracts statutory interest of 0.06% per day of delay; inaccurate declarations and use of false export documentation expose the taxpayer to fine of up to 5.5% of the concealed tax amount plus the tax, and serious violations may constitute a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and seizure of assets derived from the tax offence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender in Estonia, which adopted the euro on 1 January 2011 upon entry into the Third Stage of EMU. Capital and current account movements are fully liberalized pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; there are no exchange-control permits, quantitative limits, or central bank approvals applicable to inbound equity, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation, software license royalties, or trade payments. The only residual reporting duty is a EUR 10,000 cash-travel declaration threshold under EU Regulation 2018/1672; no profit-remittance moratorium or balance-of-payments approval gate applies to the cloud software vertical. Corporate taxation itself creates the only structural hurdle: retained and reinvested profits are taxed at 0% (Estonian accrual model), whereas distributed profits attract 20/80 corporate income tax (14/86 for regular dividends), with no withholding tax on shareholder distributions and no CFC penalty attached to timely dividend extraction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia abolished all export-proceeds surrender and repatriation requirements upon euro adoption; no mandatory conversion ratio, time limit for repatriation, or onshore collection obligation applies to foreign-currency invoices. USD and GBP receivables generated by the vertical CRM cloud subscription business may be held offshore, converted freely, or used directly for foreign supplier settlements without notifying Eesti Pank; the only compliance layer is proper statistical purpose coding on bank transfers for balance-of-payments reporting.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), a member of the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem; AML supervision is executed by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority), while the Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu andmebüroo) operates within the Police and Border Guard Board.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with a restricted mandate; SEPA-licensed electronic money institutions (Wise, Monese, Paysera, Revolut Business) provide fast EUR IBANs and multi-currency wallets but issue non-Estonian BIC-prefixed accounts (often Lithuanian, Belgian, or Dutch) and therefore cannot serve as the primary corporate treasury anchor for Estonian statutory filing, tax authority (EMTA) correspondence, or creditor registration. EMIs also lack full SWIFT correspondent depth for USD outbound wires and may impose velocity limits on SaaS subscription inflows; the recommended architecture is a tier-2 settlement sub-rail for merchant payouts with AS LHV Pank or AS Swedbank holding the definitive treasury relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans are routed via SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for USD/GBP, with statistical purpose code 'C.1.1.1 – Equity Capital' or 'E.2 – Intercompany Loans' per Eesti Pank balance-of-payments classification. Estonian law permits OÜ incorporation with zero minimum capital (2011 amendment), but banks require paid-in capital of at least EUR 2,500–10,000 for onboarding viability; the remittance must reference the shareholder's legal name and the company registry code (registry code 16xxxxxx) to avoid misclassification. For USD inbound, the transfer must include SWIFT field 70/72 tags such as 'EQUI' or 'LOAN' and the final beneficiary name matching the Äriregistri extract; banks verify source of funds through the remitter's bank statement and will freeze unclassified high-value wires pending purpose-code confirmation.EUR-denominated supplier payments use SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant on the TARGET2/Single Euro Payments Area rail; the OÜ must register an e-Faktura creditor identifier in the Estonian e-invoicing operator network for statutory B2B invoicing. Non-EUR trade settlements (e.g., AWS or Microsoft licensing in USD) execute via SWIFT MT103 with correspondent banks (typically Deutsche Bank, J.P. Morgan, or Bank of America) and explicit transaction-type codes; commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are available for hardware-integrated ERP modules but are unnecessary for pure cloud software licensing. Merchant subscription inflows from Stripe Billing settle through Stripe's SEPA Direct Debit and US ACH rails into the OÜ's EUR account with a T+1 to T+3 clearing cycle; dividend distributions to shareholders use SEPA CT with classification 'D.5.2.2 – Dividends' accompanied by a board minutes excerpt, while royalty payments for IP use classification 'D.4 – Royalties' with the licensing agreement annexed.Outright deliverable EUR/USD and EUR/GBP foreign-exchange forwards (tenor ≤ 12 months) transacted with AS LHV Pank, AS Swedbank, or AS SEB Pank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement; these are the standard hedge for USD-denominated Stripe Billing and AWS receivables against EUR functional-currency operating costs.Spot FX contracts for conversion of net USD settlement balances into EUR, executed at bank contract rates without derivative accounting treatment when settled within T+2.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for EUR/USD intercompany loans above EUR 1,000,000 with a parent guarantee, documented under IFRS 9 hedge accounting and cleared on the ISDA/CSA protocol.Non-deliverable forwards only for illiquid or non-convertible emerging-market pairs (e.g., KRW, INR) necessary for channel-partner pass-through payments; NDFs are not available or needed for EUR/USD because the euro is fully deliverable in the Estonian banking market.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with a restricted mandate; SEPA-licensed electronic money institutions (Wise, Monese, Paysera, Revolut Business) provide fast EUR IBANs and multi-currency wallets but issue non-Estonian BIC-prefixed accounts (often Lithuanian, Belgian, or Dutch) and therefore cannot serve as the primary corporate treasury anchor for Estonian statutory filing, tax authority (EMTA) correspondence, or creditor registration. EMIs also lack full SWIFT correspondent depth for USD outbound wires and may impose velocity limits on SaaS subscription inflows; the recommended architecture is a tier-2 settlement sub-rail for merchant payouts with AS LHV Pank or AS Swedbank holding the definitive treasury relationship.

0% on retained and undistributed profit; 20% corporate income tax on distributed dividends (20/80 of the net dividend, equivalent to 25% effective tax on the gross distribution); 14% reduced rate for regular cash dividends (14/86 of the net dividend, equivalent to 16.28% effective tax on the gross distribution).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% withholding tax on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders under Section 43 of the Income Tax Act; distributions to resident corporate shareholders are subject to the payer's domestic distribution tax of 20/80 on the net dividend (25% effective) and 14/86 on regular cash dividends; non-resident EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the capital for a continuous period of at least 12 months are exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly TSD is due by the 10th day of the month following the reporting month; annual corporate income tax declaration of a resident legal person is due by 1 October of the year following the fiscal year; annual report to the Commercial Register is due within six months of the fiscal year-end; VAT return deadlines are monthly or quarterly by the 20th day following the reporting period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonian domestic law sets no fixed euro-denominated threshold; the arm's length obligation under Section 49 of the Income Tax Act applies to every cross-border controlled transaction and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be produced upon audit demand. Groups within the EU Country-by-Country Reporting scope (consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million) must maintain OECD-aligned Master File and CbC reports; in audit practice the Estonian Tax and Customs Board expects a Local File where annual cross-border related-party revenue from services, royalties or interest exceeds approximately EUR 1,000,000, and expects simplified benchmarking documentation for lower-materiality transactions.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a standalone offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the Income Tax Act Section 6 taxes entities whose place of effective management (POEM) is in Estonia, the general anti-avoidance rule nullifies artificial arrangements, and the Commercial Code requires a registered seat and a real operating board. An e-Residency OÜ with zero local economic substance (no employees, no office, no Estonian bank account, board resident abroad) faces: (i) tax residency reclassification and CFC attribution of retained profit in the shareholder's home jurisdiction; (ii) denial of treaty benefits under limitation-on-benefits and principal-purpose tests; (iii) Estonian AML and beneficial-ownership enforcement action; and (iv) compulsory dissolution upon declaration of cessation of business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under Section 385 of the Penal Code: fine and/or imprisonment of up to five years; administrative enforcement penalty of up to EUR 1,300 per declaration under the Taxation Act; underpaid tax is recovered with 0.06% daily late-payment interest and forced execution against company assets; the court may order seizure of digital assets, real estate and bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Board members who intentionally or with gross negligence caused the evasion are jointly and severally liable to the company and its creditors for the resulting damage; directors face personal asset seizure, travel bans to prevent flight, disqualification from board service in any Estonian company, personal income tax recovery of unlawfully received benefits and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Nominee Director Misuse (e-Residency Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Compulsory dissolution by court order under the Commercial Code; administrative fines of up to EUR 200,000 under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (MLTFPA) for non-financial reporting entities, with higher percentage-of-turnover fines for supervised financial institutions; refusal or withdrawal of e-Residency digital ID status; criminal money laundering prosecution under Section 394 of the Penal Code with imprisonment of up to five years and forfeiture of laundered property.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are personally prosecuted for false declarations and false beneficial-ownership statements; they face personal fines, disqualification from all board positions, civil restitution to defrauded creditors and business partners, personal asset forfeiture and travel bans where the nominee controlled client funds.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the arm's length price under Section 49 of the Income Tax Act; the excess payment is deemed a dividend distribution taxed at 20/80 of the net amount (25% effective) plus 0.06% daily late-payment interest; administrative penalty of up to EUR 1,300 for non-filing of supporting evidence; sham arrangements attract criminal tax evasion charges with imprisonment of up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm's-length transactions are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax shortfall and financial damage; they face civil restitution claims from shareholders and creditors, cross-border asset freezing, travel bans and disqualification orders enforceable in the director's country of residence through mutual legal assistance.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the MLTFPA of up to EUR 200,000 for legal persons and separate fines for directors; FIU-ordered freezing of funds, transaction bans and temporary prohibition of business activities; denial or withdrawal of e-Residency status; criminal money laundering prosecution with imprisonment of up to five years; forced expulsion from the Commercial Register and compulsory dissolution of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as designated compliance officers are personally sanctioned for failure to implement AML controls; they face civil liability to third parties defrauded through the company, personal fines, disqualification from board membership, travel bans, personal asset seizure and criminal records that permanently impair cross-border banking and licensing.
