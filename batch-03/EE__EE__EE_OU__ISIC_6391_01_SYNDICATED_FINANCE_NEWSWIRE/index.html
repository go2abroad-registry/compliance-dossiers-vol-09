Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) — e-service portal at e-mta.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Osaühing (OÜ) must first register the company in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri infosüsteem) via a notary or e-Residency digital signing. Upon incorporation, the Commercial Register automatically transmits registration data to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA), which assigns a national tax identification number (TIN) and VAT number where applicable. Non-resident companies with no permanent establishment in Estonia but liable for Estonian-source withholding tax or VAT may separately apply for a simplified non-resident VAT registration and tax account through the MTA e-service portal by submitting Form KÄ 1 (VAT registration application) and supporting identity documents. Foreign entities that only perform B2B supplies of electronically supplied services to Estonian non-taxable persons may use the non-EU or EU VAT mini-one-stop-shop (OSS) regimes instead of full registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not apply a general check-the-box entity classification regime. Osaühing (OÜ) is per se an entity-level taxable corporation under the Income Tax Act. A non-resident company may elect to be taxed as a permanent establishment under the section 53 procedure if it carries on business through a fixed place of business in Estonia, but no hybrid mismatch entity elections are available. For VAT purposes, an election for exemption from registration may be available under the €40,000 annual turnover threshold, but a voluntary VAT registration election is available once the threshold criteria are not met or where the taxpayer intends to supply cross-border services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 (annual taxable turnover for resident taxable persons; non-resident suppliers without a fixed establishment are required to register from the first supply of taxable goods or services in Estonia unless the OSS scheme applies)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) exports of goods from Estonia to third countries or third territories where the goods are dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, and the supplier retains commercial evidence of dispatch/transport; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered person in another Member State, provided the customer’s valid VAT number is obtained and the recapitulative statement (Intrastat/VIES) is filed; (iii) certain internationally related services, including transport-related services and services directly connected with export of goods, under VAT Act § 16 and § 17; and (iv) supply of services to a taxable person established in another Member State where the customer is liable for reverse-charge VAT. For the financial newswire service under ISIC 6391, zero-rating applies only where the service qualifies as an electronically supplied service supplied to a business customer in another EU Member State (reverse charge applies) or as an export of a service to a third-country recipient, provided the supplier obtains documentary evidence of the customer’s business status and place of establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/EX summary declaration submitted to Estonian Tax and Customs Board customs system)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill or other transport document evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or contract, bank statement, and payment confirmation evidencing receipt of consideration in foreign currency
  • Customer’s VAT identification number (for intra-Community supplies) or other proof of business status for third-country recipients
  • Commercial invoice and contract specifying the nature of the service and the destination country
  • Proof of service performance (e.g., API usage logs, delivery receipts, or server access records for electronic services)

Estonia mandates e-invoicing for B2B transactions in the sense of structured electronic invoices under the European standard EN 16931 for public sector procurement, but does not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance regime. There is no general pre-clearance digital stamping obligation imposed on all invoices. VAT invoices must be issued within 7 calendar days of the supply, must contain the mandatory data fields under VAT Act § 37, and must be transmitted electronically to the MTA upon request. The MTA operates an e-service portal for VAT returns (Form KMD) submitted by the 20th day of the month following the tax period. For export and intra-Community supply substantiation, electronic customs declarations are submitted through the national customs system (STS) and the EU-wide Import Control System (ICS2); no separate digital fiscal stamping is required.

Under Estonian VAT Act § 31 and § 34, zero-rating is granted on the basis of documentary evidence retained by the supplier; if the supplier fails to prove the export or intra-Community supply, the MTA may reassess VAT at 22% plus default interest (0.06% per day). Knowingly false documentation supporting a zero-rated supply constitutes tax fraud subject to criminal penalties under Penal Code § 344, including imprisonment of up to 5 years, and administrative fine up to €80,000. The MTA may also apply a retrospective clawback for periods up to 5 years in cases of false declarations; VAT-registered persons are jointly liable for refunds claimed without valid evidence. Additionally, a penalty of up to 10% of the under-declared VAT may be assessed for failure to file the VIES recapitulative statement correctly. For currency exchange gains or losses on export proceeds, no specific clawback rule exists beyond general profit-repatriation taxation applicable to Estonian permanent establishments and foreign-owned OÜs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is the sole legal tender and is fully convertible in both current and capital account operations. Estonia has no exchange-control law, no capital authorisation regime, and no profit-remittance permit requirement. Cross-border payments within the EU/EEA and SEPA are unrestricted. Dividend distributions by an Osaühing are taxed at the entity level under the Estonian Income Tax Act: generally 20% on gross distributed profit (14% for regular cash distributions meeting statutory requirements), with taxation deferred until distribution. No withholding tax applies to dividends paid to EU/EEA corporate shareholders satisfying the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% minimum holding, 2-year holding period), and treaty relief or the same Directive applies to most non-EEA shareholders subject to beneficial-ownership certification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or custody requirement applies. All export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency may be freely retained in resident or non-resident accounts. Only statistical balance-of-payments reporting may be required for direct reporting of individual transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 or aggregate monthly positions, filed with Eesti Pank via its digital reporting portal; no approval or permit is associated with such filing.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as Eurosystem member; Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) as the AML/CFT and banking conduct supervisor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating under the PSD2 passport and registered with Finantsinspektsioon can offer cross-border IBAN and payment services to an Estonian OÜ with full SEPA and SWIFT connectivity. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU/EEA licence are not permitted to provide regulated payment services in Estonia and cannot access local RTGS or SEPA clearing directly; they are viable only as white-label rails or when holding a recognised EU licence. An e-Resident OÜ may use EU-licensed EMI banking partners for multi-currency operations, but the EMI must comply with the Estonian AML/CFT Act, MIFID/EMD rules, and EBA travel-rule requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections must be executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) or SWIFT MT103 (non-EUR). For share capital, the payment reference must declare ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity capital) or a bank-approved BOP code; for shareholder loans, use code GENY or GDDS as appropriate. The beneficiary account must be in the OÜ's name. Verification before booking: the bank performs a source-of-funds check, compares the sender with the shareholder register, and may require a notarised copy of the board resolution authorising the increased share capital. In EUR, no Finantsinspektsioon approval is required; for non-EUR, the bank may request a customs or currency declaration only if thresholds trigger Estonian balance-of-payments statistical reporting.Outbound trade settlements are cleared through SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated EEA payments, and through SWIFT MT103 for international non-EUR wires. Documentary trade settlements may use commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700), with direct confirmation through the issuing bank. EUR-denominated high-value payments are finally settled through TARGET2, operated by Eesti Pank as a TARGET2 participant. For utility and subscription disbursements to financial-information vendors, use IBAN-only payments with accompanying structured remittance advice (such as ISO 20022 pain.001) to avoid exemption or return.EUR/USD outright FX forwards (standard interbank or onshore forward contracts) to lock in USD/EUR when the OÜ invoices in USDEUR/USD vanilla foreign-exchange options (call or put) for optional hedge of subscription receipts or syndicated-finance settlement flowsEUR/GBP, EUR/SEK, and EUR/CHF spot and forward contracts for trade disbursements in Nordic and UK clearing corridorsCross-currency swaps for medium-term non-EUR working capital, subject to standard ISDA or local master agreement and Finantsinspektsioon EMIR reportingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on EUR are not generally necessary or permissible in the Estonian market because EUR is fully convertible and freely deliverable; NDF structures are accepted only in exceptional non-deliverable emerging-market currency pairings and would not qualify as regulatory hedges for EUR exposures

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, generally calculated as 22/78 of the net amount distributed. Retained and reinvested profits are not subject to corporate income tax at the time of retention or reinvestment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% additional withholding tax on ordinary dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ under the post-2024 regime. The OÜ generally pays corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net distribution. Special distributions, anti-abuse rules, payments to individuals and changes in legislation must be separately reviewed.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD must generally be filed and the related tax paid by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the payment or taxable event occurred. The annual statutory report of the OÜ is filed separately with the Commercial Register within six months after the end of the financial year, normally by 30 June for a calendar-year company.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The taxpayer must provide requested transfer-pricing documentation within the deadline specified by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. The authority commonly allows a defined response period, which must be followed exactly.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory detailed documentation is principally relevant to large enterprises and specified entities. The key large-company thresholds are generally at least two of the following: annual turnover of approximately EUR 50 million, balance-sheet total of approximately EUR 43 million, and average number of employees of at least 250. Additional obligations may apply to credit institutions, insurers, listed companies, permanent establishments and transactions with associated persons in low-tax jurisdictions. A Master File may also be required under applicable group and country-by-country reporting rules, including for groups meeting the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue CbC threshold.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a separate broad Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to the UAE ESR regime. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, treaty beneficial-ownership and principal-purpose tests, general anti-avoidance principles, permanent-establishment analysis, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules and the business-purpose requirements for deductible expenses and distributions. An OÜ operating a financial-news aggregation or API business should maintain genuine decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational control, technology ownership or access, customer contracts, accounting records, banking activity and commercially supportable functions in the jurisdictions claimed to perform them.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and concealed distributions

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid corporate income tax, interest and applicable fines. Deliberate tax fraud may constitute a criminal offence punishable by monetary penalties and, depending on severity and statutory conditions, imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal or misdemeanour liability, civil damages to the company or creditors, tax-related liability where statutory conditions are met, disqualification and enforcement measures. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are not automatic and require a specific legal basis and competent-authority or court action.
Shell-company and beneficial-ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Beneficial-ownership, tax-residence, anti-avoidance and reporting failures may result in tax reassessment, fines, account restrictions, suspicious-transaction reporting and termination of banking or payment services.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may incur civil, administrative or criminal liability for false registry information, concealment, fraud or aiding money laundering. Corporate separateness does not protect individuals from their own unlawful conduct.
Anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing

Statutory sanction: Administrative and criminal sanctions may include fines, confiscation, licence or service-provider restrictions, account termination and imprisonment for serious money-laundering conduct. Obligations depend on whether the OÜ conducts a regulated activity or falls within a designated obliged-entity category.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be personally liable for intentional or negligent AML failures where statutory elements are satisfied. Travel bans, asset restraints and imprisonment require competent-authority or court action and are not automatic consequences of a compliance breach.
Unlicensed financial activity

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist measures, administrative fines, forced termination of activity, confiscation and criminal liability may apply under financial-sector legislation. A financial-news aggregation service is not ordinarily licensed merely because its content concerns financial markets, but the actual revenue model and customer functionality control.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unauthorized regulated activity, consumer harm, restitution and criminal offences. Personal liability increases where directors knowingly evade licensing or misrepresent the business to banks, customers or regulators.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may adjust taxable results, deny unsupported deductions, impose corporate income tax, interest and fines, and require supporting documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for losses caused by deliberate or grossly negligent tax arrangements and criminal liability where false records or intentional tax evasion are involved.
Permanent establishment and tax-residence misclassification

Statutory sanction: Unreported corporate income tax, payroll taxes, VAT, employer contributions, interest and penalties may arise in the relevant jurisdiction. Treaty relief may be denied if facts do not support the claimed residence or allocation of profits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for knowingly false registrations, payroll concealment, accounting failures or fraudulent tax reporting. Civil restitution and enforcement measures may follow; imprisonment requires a qualifying offence and judicial process.
VAT and digital-services non-compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT registration, assessments, interest, penalties, invoice corrections and reporting obligations may arise. Incorrect VAT treatment can also create customer-country registration exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for intentional VAT fraud, false invoices or deliberate non-filing. Ordinary technical errors generally create company-level tax exposure unless aggravated by misconduct.
Sanctions, export controls and restricted counterparties

Statutory sanction: Asset freezes, payment blocking, administrative fines, criminal prosecution, confiscation and loss of banking or payment access may apply under Estonian, EU and other applicable sanctions regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally prosecuted for knowing or reckless sanctions breaches. Asset restraints, travel restrictions and imprisonment depend on the applicable offence and formal enforcement process.
Corporate-register and accounting failures

Statutory sanction: Warnings, fines, compulsory filing measures, publication of non-compliance and deletion proceedings may apply. Deletion can expose the company and directors to significant operational and creditor risks.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for creditor or shareholder losses caused by accounting or filing failures, and may face disqualification or sanctions for persistent statutory non-compliance.
