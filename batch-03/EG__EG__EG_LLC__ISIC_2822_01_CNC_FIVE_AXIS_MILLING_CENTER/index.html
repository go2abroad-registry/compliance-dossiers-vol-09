Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) – Companies Registration Department and Investor One-Stop Shop at the registered office jurisdiction; commercial registration notification is made through GAFI to the local Commercial Registry office.

Governing statute

Law No. 159 of 1981 (Egyptian Companies Law, as amended) Articles 1, 26, and 89; the executive regulation promulgated by Ministerial Decree No. 420 of 1982; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations (Prime Ministerial Decree No. 2310 of 2017) governing GAFI one-stop-shop incorporation of Limited Liability Companies.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Memorandum of Association (عقد التأسيس) and Articles of Association (النظام الأساسي) for a Limited Liability Company, submitted through the GAFI electronic incorporation portal, entered into the Commercial Registry (السجل التجاري), and published in the Egyptian Official Bulletin (الوقائع المصرية).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 60 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian LLC; the founding shareholders fix the share capital in the Memorandum of Association. All subscribed capital must be fully paid and placed in an escrow / blocked account opened in the company name with an Egyptian bank before incorporation, and the bank's capital deposit certificate (شهادة إيداع رأس المال) must accompany the GAFI filing. Inward remittances by foreign shareholders require the bank exchange certificate (Statement MI/19); locked funds are released only after the Commercial Registration certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory pre-operational clearance composed of: (1) Civil Defense Fire Safety Certificate issued by the General Administration of Civil Defense under the Ministry of Interior for the manufacturing hall and storage areas; (2) structural-load and site-construction inspection certificates issued by an accredited engineering authority verifying floor loading capacity for the five-axis milling centers; and (3) Occupational Safety and Health workplace clearance issued by the Ministry of Manpower approving illuminated evacuation plans, fire alarms, coolant-mist extraction, machine guarding, PPE programmes, and medical surveillance files before IDA will activate the industrial operating license.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian LLC; the founding shareholders fix the share capital in the Memorandum of Association. All subscribed capital must be fully paid and placed in an escrow / blocked account opened in the company name with an Egyptian bank before incorporation, and the bank's capital deposit certificate (شهادة إيداع رأس المال) must accompany the GAFI filing. Inward remittances by foreign shareholders require the bank exchange certificate (Statement MI/19); locked funds are released only after the Commercial Registration certificate is issued.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – ETA e-Tax Portal (https://www.inatax.gov.eg/etax-web/)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident LLC must submit a complete registration application to ETA through an authorized tax agent or a duly appointed fiscal representative in Egypt. The application includes the commercial registry certificate (or foreign registration proof), constituent documents (AOA/BOA), board resolution appointing the Egyptian representative, power of attorney, and a statement of intended activities in Egypt. ETA issues a single 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) for direct taxes, VAT, and payroll taxes. The same registration portal initiates VAT ID issuance simultaneously; failure to register within the prescribed period triggers penalty assessments and denial of input tax recovery.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident LLC must submit a complete registration application to ETA through an authorized tax agent or a duly appointed fiscal representative in Egypt. The application includes the commercial registry certificate (or foreign registration proof), constituent documents (AOA/BOA), board resolution appointing the Egyptian representative, power of attorney, and a statement of intended activities in Egypt. ETA issues a single 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) for direct taxes, VAT, and payroll taxes. The same registration portal initiates VAT ID issuance simultaneously; failure to register within the prescribed period triggers penalty assessments and denial of input tax recovery.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election mechanism. Eligible manufacturing entities may, upon ETA/GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) approval, elect special tax regimes, including the Free Zone regime, the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE) incentives, and the Investment Law 72/2017 incentive schemes. These elections are filings wholly within the standard entity-level taxation model and do not create flow-through status or hybrid mismatch positions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 (annual value of taxable supplies; foreign suppliers of services with Egyptian recipients may be subject to registration/reverse charge rules at the same threshold unless exempted by ETA directive)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported outside the customs territory of Egypt by a registered exporter, provided title in the goods passes to a non-resident buyer, the goods are removed from Egypt within the statutory period (normally 180 days from invoice/sale), and the export proceeds are credited to an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the export date. The exporter must maintain digital and physical records evidencing the chain of movement (factory to customs to discharge from the vessel/aircraft) and corresponding foreign currency inflow.

  • Customs Export Declaration (coded export entry released by Egyptian Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) evidencing on-board loading at an Egyptian port/airport for foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank-issued certificate verifying receipt of export proceeds in freely convertible foreign currency)
  • Sales contract and pro-forma/commercial invoice issued to the non-resident buyer showing the applicable zero-rated status
  • ETA-approved export tax invoice bearing the e-invoicing validation QR code and the word 'Export'
  • Certificate of export value/transport manifest issued by the export customs office

ETA’s e-invoicing system is mandatory for all registered taxpayers on a phased-basis. Taxpayers must transmit every B2B and B2G invoice to ETA’s central platform (Tax Electronic System) via an approved API or secure integration in near-real-time before issuance. ETA validates the invoice and returns a unique invoice identifier and QR code; no invoice may be legally issued without this digital clearance. The physical stamping regime is abolished, but non-integrated paper invoices are automatically treated as invalid for VAT deduction and subject the issuer to automatic blocking of the tax card.

ETA audit teams automatically cross-match monthly export sales listings with customs release data, Egyptian bank SWIFT credit advices, and inward remittance certificates. If goods are not exported within 180 days, proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, or documentation is false/incomplete, ETA retroactively assesses the standard 14% VAT on the full invoice value, adds late-payment interest (monthly delay fine), and denies input VAT refunds. Fraudulent claims constitute tax evasion under VAT Law 67/2016, leading to a fine of not less than EGP 20,000 and not more than EGP 100,000 or imprisonment (or both), confiscation of goods, and personal liability of directors and the appointed tax representative. Customs penalties and export privilege revocation also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the 6 March 2024 liberalization, Egypt maintains a market-determined flexible exchange rate regime consistent with IMF Extended Fund Facility commitments. Current-account transactions (trade in goods/services, royalties, management fees) are fully convertible and executable by CBE-licensed banks at prevailing interbank rates without pre-approval. Capital-account transactions, including foreign equity injection, intercompany loans, and profit/dividend repatriation, are permissible but require underlying-document verification under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 (Articles 71–73 granting free transfer guarantees) and CBE unified FX rules. Practical remittance hurdles: all FX flows must pass through an Egyptian CBE-licensed bank (offshore transfers directly between non-resident accounts of the LLC are not recognized); the bank may require a valid tax clearance certificate and Corporate Tax ID for profit-transfer classification; and interbank liquidity conditions may temporarily delay large USD outflows, though no legal convertibility restriction exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the consolidated 2023–2024 CBE circulars on export proceeds, there is no mandatory conversion ratio: exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at an Egyptian bank, effectively abolishing prior surrender obligations. Export proceeds must be credited to the exporter's designated FCY account within 90 calendar days from the bill-of-lading/airway-bill date for open-account sales, or within the LC/documentary collection maturity schedule (maximum 1 year with documented CBE-bank approval). The collecting bank must record proceeds in the CBE Export Proceeds Monitoring System linked to customs (GUST/Sofacts) and the Export Development Fund; overdue non-repatriation constitutes a violation of Law No. 194 of 2020 Article 100 and may trigger suspension of export-financing facilities and enhanced compliance review of the entity's FX allocations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — البنك المركزي المصري, operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and its implementing prudential and foreign-exchange (FX) regulations issued through the Banking Supervision and Exchange Control Departments.

Line 10 Taxable income and tax computation at the 22.5% statutory rate after deductions and exemptions. Non-resident shareholders are subject to a final 10% dividend WHT on distributions from after-tax retained earnings; the LLC files as withholding agent. Late filing: penalty of 10% of tax assessed (minimum EGP 5,000 for medium and large taxpayers) plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge on the unpaid tax; late payment: surcharge of 2% per month on the outstanding balance; non-filing: criminal fine of EGP 20,000-100,000 and imprisonment for persistent default; inaccurate filing: 20% administrative penalty on the tax under-declared.

Foreign tax credit is available for Egyptian LLC profits taxed abroad only under a DTA and upon submission of a notarized residence certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions in a fiscal year; above this threshold, the Egyptian LLC must prepare and submit on ETA demand a BEPS-aligned Local File and Master File (Egyptian TP Guidelines, Decree No. 652 of 2020); a CbCR is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 900 million where Egypt hosts a constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the ETA applies OECD BEPS substance-over-form and beneficial ownership doctrines. A non-resident-owned LLC must demonstrate real premises, technical staff, machining operations, and export activities to claim free-zone/SCZone exemptions or DTA relief. Shell holding vehicles and letterbox LLCs distributing profits without substance are recast as Egyptian tax residents with denial of treaty WHT benefits and risk of liquidation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraudulent Bookkeeping)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 3-5 years and fine of EGP 10,000-100,000 under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 (Articles 133-134); reassessment on deemed turnover at 22.5%; 2% monthly delay surcharge; 20% administrative penalty on under-declared tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; ETA may issue asset freezes, seize passports, impose travel bans, and refer the case to the Economic Criminal Court with direct criminal liability.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Specific penalty up to EGP 100,000 and disallowance of related-party deductions; the ETA may replace the transaction price via TNMM/CUP and impose 2% monthly surcharge on the adjustment, with a 20% penalty on the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's length intercompany contracts are jointly liable for the upward adjustment; personal bank accounts may be attached, and deliberate submission of doctored comparables triggers criminal sanction.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; re-characterization of the holding vehicle as a sham; full 22.5% CIT on undistributed profits, 10% final dividend WHT without treaty relief, administrative fines up to EGP 1,000,000 under anti-avoidance rules, and compulsory liquidation or strike-off of the Egyptian LLC.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign substance certificates or shell board minutes bear civil liability for recaptured taxes; travel bans and passport seizure; exclusion from holding directorships in Egypt; referral for penal sanction under Companies Law No. 159/1981.
AML / Export Control Red Flags in Trade-Based Flows

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80/2002: fines up to EGP 500,000 and imprisonment of 1-7 years; freezing of corporate and personal accounts; revocation of the statutory export license and cross-border trade license; forfeiture of shipments and denial of future End-User Certificate LC rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for failures to file Suspicious Transaction Reports with the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU); personal asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment for systematic money laundering or dual-use export violations.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The LLC remains liable for the unwithheld tax plus 2% monthly surcharge and a 20% penalty on the deficient WHT; ETA denial of operational expense deductions for unwithheld royalties; criminal fine of EGP 10,000-40,000 for repeated failure.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as withholding agents are personally and jointly liable for the unwithheld amounts; the ETA can enforce against personal bank accounts, issue a travel ban, and the Economic Court may impose imprisonment of up to one year for willful non-remittance.
