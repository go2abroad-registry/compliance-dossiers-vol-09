Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital; an OÜ may be formed with zero capital or with a stated share capital as low as €0.01. If share capital is declared, the contribution must be credited to a bank account opened in the OÜ's name and may be used freely for business purposes from registration; no escrow or blocked-account arrangement is mandated by Estonian law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: comply with the Fire Safety Act certificate and maintenance obligations (fire alarm inspection, extinguishers, evacuation plan, and annual fire safety audit for buildings over a specified occupancy threshold). Occupational health: notify the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) of employees, implement ergonomic workstation assessments, provide periodic health check-ups for screen-based work, and maintain workplace first-aid provisions per the Occupational Health and Safety Act.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital; an OÜ may be formed with zero capital or with a stated share capital as low as €0.01. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); digital portals: e-MTA at emta.ee and e-Estonia business portal at eesti.ee; commercial registry: Äriregister (ariregister.rik.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishes an OÜ (Osaühing) by filing a digitally signed (e-Residency, Smart-ID, Mobile-ID) or notarised application with the Estonian Commercial Register. Registration produces the 8-digit registry code (registrikood), which is simultaneously the corporate TIN and MTA tax account identifier. No separate TIN application is required for corporate income tax, social tax, or VAT. For VAT, the entity must self-assess turnover through e-MTA: registration is mandatory when taxable supplies exceed EUR 50,000 in a calendar year (or EUR 10,000 for intra-EU distance sales and electronically supplied services to consumers). The VAT application form ('Application for registration as a taxable person') is submitted via e-MTA and the MTA issues the KMKR number in the format EE + 9 digits, normally within 3–5 business days. A non-resident entity without an Estonian establishment uses the non-resident VAT registration procedure and may be required to appoint a local tax representative on a case-by-case basis. e-Residency allows a remote foreign board member to execute the entire registration electronically; no minimum capital is required for a standard OÜ.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the OÜ is a fully resident Estonian corporate taxpayer subject to the distribution-based CIT system: 0% tax on retained/undistributed profits; 20/80 CIT on non-regular profit distributions and deemed distributions; 22/78 CIT on non-regular distributions from 2025; reduced 14/86 CIT on regular profit distributions meeting the 3-year regular-payment test; capital gains are generally tax-neutral at entity level; qualifying dividends received from Estonian/EU subsidiaries are exempt. The entity is not a pass-through and there is no territorial scope election for Estonian-source income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box is available in Estonia. The statutory elections relevant to this BPO OÜ include: (1) voluntary VAT registration before exceeding the EUR 50,000 threshold; (2) VAT group election for affiliated Estonian entities; (3) election to apply the OSS Union or Non-Union special scheme for electronically supplied services and distance sales; (4) election to distribute against the 3-year regular distribution base so as to access the reduced 14/86 CIT rate; (5) the participation exemption and foreign tax credit mechanisms are applied automatically under law; anti-hybrid and ATAD rules operate by statute without elective check-the-box treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 50,000 per calendar year for taxable supplies by an Estonian-established business (general domestic registration threshold); EUR 10,000 per calendar year for intra-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons under the OSS special scheme; foreign suppliers have no minimum threshold — registration applies from the first taxable supply in Estonia unless using the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies when goods are exported to a non-EU destination or supplied as qualifying intra-EU consignments, evidenced by an export customs declaration and conveyance documents. Services, including AI data-labelling and RLHF BPO: no Estonian VAT arises when the place of supply is outside Estonia: (i) B2B supplies to customers established outside Estonia are subject to reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction; (ii) B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside the scope of Estonian VAT; (iii) B2C supplies to other EU consumers are taxed in the consumer's state via Union OSS where the EUR 10,000 threshold is exceeded. The provider must validate the foreign customer's VAT identification number through VIES (for EU B2B), retain contemporaneous service contracts, delivery/performance records, and bank or payment-rail statements proving receipt of consideration from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX/SAD) or export summary declaration for any physical dispatches; Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing departure from Estonia to a non-EU destination or onward member state
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or PayPal/Wise payout statement evidencing foreign consideration and supporting the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation benchmark
  • B2B service contract and itemised invoice identifying the foreign customer, the AI data-labelling scope, the customer's foreign taxable status, and the applicable 0% VAT or reverse-charge reference; VIES validation print for EU customers; service-use and delivery logs for digital supply substantiation

Estonia imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital stamping of B2B invoices. Structured e-invoicing (EN 16931 / PEPPOL BIS) is mandatory for B2G invoices since 1 July 2019. Cash-register sales data (POS/EFD) must be transmitted to the MTA in real time via certified cash-application APIs from 2023/2024. Standard VAT returns, European Sales Lists, and OSS returns are filed through e-MTA using X-Road-based APIs; documents may be archived digitally without MTA pre-clearance.

MTA applies a six-year statutory assessment window and automated risk scoring of zero-rating and OSS positions. If the documentation bundle is defective or the customer's place of supply is mischaracterised, the supply is reassessed at 24% VAT with late-payment interest of 0.06% per day (annualised approximately 21.9%) and a supplementary tax increase of 10%–20% for incorrect declarations, rising to 20%–50% for intentional evasion. Criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code (fraudulent tax evasion) can result in imprisonment of up to 5 years, seizure of corporate and payment-rail accounts, clawback of refunds, disqualification of directors, and suspension or revocation of OSS access and VAT registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a eurozone member; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. EU free movement of capital applies. Profit distribution is subject to corporate income tax at 20% upon distribution (or 14% for regular dividends), but no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents under domestic law and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No hurdles for profit remittance beyond standard corporate formalities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency, freely used, or converted. Estonia does not impose mandatory conversion or repatriation timelines. All receipts must be reported for statistical purposes to Eesti Pank under the Balance of Payments reporting framework, but no conversion obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) and the European Central Bank (ECB) for eurozone monetary policy; Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for banking supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

High feasibility for digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts such as Wise Business, PayPal Business, and Holvi. These are licensed under EU PSD2 and can provide IBANs in EUR. However, they are not full-service banks and may have limitations on high-volume B2B transactions, large capital injections, or complex trade finance. For AI data labeling BPO with mass PayPal payouts and Wise, these EMIs are viable for operational liquidity but should be supplemented with a tier-1 bank account for capital escrow and regulatory credibility. Legal constraints: EMIs are not subject to Estonian deposit insurance; they may have lower AML thresholds and require additional verification for high-risk jurisdictions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident ownership and remote director structure

Root cause: Non-resident ownership and remote director structure

Defense evidence: Provide notarized and apostilled certificate of incorporation, memorandum of association, register of shareholders, and board resolution authorizing account opening. Include passport copies of all UBOs and directors, proof of residential address (utility bill or bank statement), and a detailed business plan explaining the AI data labeling BPO model, client contracts, and expected transaction flows. For e-residents, provide e-Residency digital ID and certificate of e-residency.
High-volume outbound payments to freelancers or subcontractors (PayPal/Wise)

Root cause: High-volume outbound payments to freelancers or subcontractors (PayPal/Wise)

Defense evidence: Maintain a comprehensive AML/CFT policy, including customer due diligence (CDD) on all payees, transaction monitoring procedures, and a risk assessment. Provide sample contracts with freelancers, invoices, and proof of service delivery (e.g., time logs, quality reports). Ensure that all payouts are linked to verifiable business activities and that the bank's transaction monitoring system is satisfied. For mass payouts, use batch payment files with clear purpose codes (e.g., 'Payment for data annotation services').
Capital injection from non-EU shareholders

Root cause: Capital injection from non-EU shareholders

Defense evidence: Provide source of funds documentation: bank statements of the transferring entity, tax returns, audited financial statements, or proof of sale of assets. If the shareholder is a corporate entity, provide its corporate registry extract, UBO declaration, and a legal opinion on the legitimacy of funds. For amounts exceeding €10,000, be prepared to explain the economic rationale and provide a board resolution approving the capital contribution.
Use of e-Residency and virtual office address

Root cause: Use of e-Residency and virtual office address

Defense evidence: Provide a physical operating address (if any) or a registered agent address with proof of lease. If the company is fully remote, provide a detailed explanation of the operational model, including where employees are located, how they are contracted, and how the company maintains substance. Banks may require a local contact person or a professional service provider (e.g., accounting firm) to act as a liaison.
