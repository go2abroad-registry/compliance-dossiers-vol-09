Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), the unified national tax administration under the Ministry of Finance; official e-portals: eta.gov.eg for unified tax registration and filing, einvoicing.eta.gov.eg for e-invoicing integration, and nafiza.gov.eg for the NAFIZA customs single window. Customs duty and export declaration functions are executed by the Egyptian Customs Authority within the same Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Step-wise procedure for a foreign parent establishing an Egyptian LLC: (1) incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) One-Stop-Shop under Investment Law 72/2017, Arabic-translating and notarizing the foreign parent's constitutional documents, executing the LLC Articles of Association, and obtaining the Commercial Registry extract; (2) within 30 days of commercial registration, file with the territorially competent ETA office the commercial registry extract, certified Articles of Association, board of managers resolution appointing the statutory tax representative, proof of Egyptian head-office premises, opening bank certificate, and power of attorney; (3) obtain the ETA statutory Tax Card and the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN), which serves for corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax and e-invoicing; (4) register for VAT under Law 67/2016 if annual taxable turnover meets the statutory threshold or immediately if the LLC intends to claim export zero-rating; (5) activate the ETA e-invoicing account and obtain PKI credentials; and (6) complete payroll, social insurance and any sectoral registrations (Egyptian Drug Authority licensing for Class III medical device manufacture).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the Egyptian LLC is an opaque, Egypt-resident taxpayer (incorporated under Egyptian law) taxed under Income Tax Law 91/2005 at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5% on worldwide taxable profits, with no pass-through or transparent classification available. Territorial scope applies only to foreign-company branches or permanent establishments, which are taxed exclusively on Egypt-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Egyptian LLC is an opaque, Egypt-resident taxpayer (incorporated under Egyptian law) taxed under Income Tax Law 91/2005 at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5% on worldwide taxable profits, with no pass-through or transparent classification available. Territorial scope applies only to foreign-company branches or permanent establishments, which are taxed exclusively on Egypt-source income. The manufacturing project is not automatically within the Tax_Exempt_Zone category; free-zone status or Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) incentives are elective, project-specific regimes under Investment Law 72/2017 and must be secured at establishment stage.

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide a check-the-box or partnership-classification election for LLCs; entity-level taxation is mandatory. Available statutory elections include: (i) adoption of a non-calendar fiscal year with ETA approval; (ii) investment cost deduction and accelerated depreciation on qualifying new manufacturing assets under Law 91/2005 and Investment Law 72/2017; (iii) free-zone or SCZone election for qualifying export-oriented production projects; and (iv) tax dispute reconciliation and settlement incentives under Law 30/2023. For U.S. federal purposes only, the foreign parent may file a check-the-box election under U.S. Treas. Reg. Sec. 301.7701-3 treating the Egyptian LLC as disregarded, but such election has no effect on Egyptian taxation and hybrid mismatch exposure should be assessed under BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies where annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 on supplies of goods or EGP 150,000 on supplies of services (Article 26, VAT Law 67/2016); importers and exporters claiming zero-rating must register regardless of turnover, and voluntary registration is permitted for lower-turnover persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 18 of VAT Law 67/2016, exports of goods and services supplied to non-residents and wholly consumed abroad are chargeable at 0% VAT. To qualify for the coronary drug-eluting stent export: (i) the Egyptian LLC must effect a genuine sale to a foreign purchaser under a commercial contract financed by Letter of Credit or SWIFT wire; (ii) the goods must physically depart Egyptian customs territory, evidenced by the NAFIZA electronic export declaration; (iii) title and risk must pass to the foreign buyer outside Egypt under an export Incoterms rule; (iv) the shipment must leave within the applicable customs/VAT export deadline; (v) the Class III medical device shipment must carry a valid Egyptian Drug Authority export permit, free-sale certificate and ISO 13485/CE conformity documentation; and (vi) export proceeds must be collected through licensed Egyptian bank channels. Attributable input VAT on polymer tubing, laser micro-machining services, component raw materials, packaging and freight is creditable and refundable to the exporter through the ETA refund procedure.

ETA Electronic Invoicing (E-Invoice) and Electronic Receipts (E-Receipt) are mandatory under Ministerial Decrees 623/2020 and 364/2022. Taxpayers integrated by rollout wave must connect their ERP/accounting systems to the ETA integration API using OAuth2.0 authentication and the ETA XML/JSON schema with PKI digital signatures. Each invoice is submitted to the ETA clearance engine, which returns a cryptographic validation token (hash/UUID) and a Quick-Response code before the invoice is issued to the counterparty. The model operates as a pre-clearance and validation mechanism rather than a live fiscal cash-register stamp; invoices lacking ETA codes are fiscally non-compliant and input VAT deduction is denied. Export invoices must cross-reference the NAFIZA customs export declaration. Non-compliance attracts administrative fines of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000 per invoice under Article 36 of VAT Law 67/2016, in addition to disallowance of input VAT.

Export zero-rating is a priority ETA and customs audit focus. The ETA fuses NAFIZA departure records, e-invoice clearance codes, cargo manifests and bank inward-remittance data to verify that zero-rated exports are real, timely and fully repatriated. Failure to prove physical export or to repatriate export proceeds within the applicable Central Bank of Egypt deadline (generally 180 days) recharacterizes the supply as domestic taxable output VAT, assessed with statutory late-payment interest, refund clawback and per-invoice fines. Knowingly issuing forged, false or inflated export documentation constitutes tax evasion under Article 37 of VAT Law 67/2016, punishable by imprisonment of three to five years and/or fines of EGP 20,000 to EGP 50,000 in addition to the evaded tax, and may also trigger fraud penalties under Income Tax Law 91/2005. Consequences include retrospective clawback of refunded input VAT with interest, suspension or cancellation of VAT and e-invoicing registrations, revocation of NAFIZA and export privileges, criminal referral, and reopening of assessments within the statutory limitation period, ordinarily up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since March 2024, Egypt maintains a unified managed-float exchange rate. Current-account convertibility is broadly available for trade, permitted services, royalties, and current remittances through CBE-licensed authorized banks at the interbank market rate. Capital-account convertibility remains controlled: foreign direct investment in an Egyptian LLC must be registered with GAFI, and outward remittances of dividends, branch profits, shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds require audited financial statements, tax clearance, distributable-reserve evidence, and a documented source of the original EGP or foreign-currency funds. Profit-repatriation queues and dedicated FX allocation channels were removed in the 2024 unification; practical delays are liquidity-driven and can be mitigated by maintaining full commercial documentation and prompt tax filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE supervisory requirements, 100% of export proceeds for goods such as coronary drug-eluting stents and related medical-device components must be credited to an account maintained with an authorized bank in Egypt within the CBE-prescribed statutory period, generally 60 days from the date of export loading or bill of lading, unless a documented extended payment term or free-zone exemption is approved by the authorized bank. Under the current unified float there is no mandatory conversion ratio into EGP; export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account with an authorized bank, but all receipts must be repatriated through the formal banking channel and traceable to the underlying customs export declaration.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Egyptian Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 1993 as amended; ancillary authority is exercised by the General Authority for Foreign Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for inbound capital and by the Egyptian Tax Authority for profit remittance clearance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for treasury-critical clearing. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by the CBE, have no access to the Egyptian RTGS/Meeza clearing systems, cannot issue Egyptian EGP IBANs, and cannot hold or clear export proceeds requiring statutory repatriation. Their permissible use is limited to ancillary offshore cards and payment-wallet consolidation outside Egypt. A CBE-licensed local fintech may be used as a front-end payment interface only if it is backed by an authorized clearing bank and does not handle export-proceeds surrender or commercial L/C operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

After GAFI approval and Commercial Registry capital registration, the non-resident shareholder transfers equity through SWIFT MT103 to the LLC’s foreign-currency capital account at a CBE-authorized bank. The beneficiary payment reference must include the official company name, GAFI investment file number or no-objection certificate, Commercial Registry number, tax card number, and the Balance-of-Payments purpose code for equity injection / foreign direct investment. The authorized bank verifies the transaction through sanctions screening, source-of-funds confirmation, and underlying GAFI documents; cash deposits in Egypt require a customs/currency declaration if applicable. Funds from undisclosed third-party pooling accounts or unrelated group entities should be avoided; all upstream transfers should be supported by a chain-of-title document from the ultimate beneficial owner and a shareholder resolution approving the capitalization.Trade settlements are executed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for open-account supplier payments and through SWIFT MT700/MT707 for irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by an Egyptian authorized bank. For import of raw materials, machinery, and packaging, the LLC must submit the proforma invoice, purchase contract, customs import declaration, and any required conformity or quality certificates. Bank-to-bank settlement is cleared via correspondent banking rails: USD through Fedwire/CHIPS, EUR through TARGET2, and minor currencies through agent banks.
