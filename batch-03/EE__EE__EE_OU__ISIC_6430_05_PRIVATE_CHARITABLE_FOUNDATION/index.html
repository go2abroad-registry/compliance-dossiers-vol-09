Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital exists; share capital may be set as low as EUR 1. If the registered share capital is below EUR 2,500, it must be fully paid into the company's bank payment account before the application is submitted; a dedicated escrow account is not statutory, but payment must be evidenced by bank confirmation. If share capital is EUR 2,500 or above, installments may be agreed in the articles of association, yet full contribution is required before any distributions are made.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Eesti Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA); electronic tax administration portal: e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee/en/e-mta)

E-government filing portal

An Osaühing obtains its statutory Estonian registry code through the Commercial Register administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems. The registry code is used as the principal entity identifier for tax administration. The company must register with EMTA through e-MTA or a service point when commencing taxable activities. VAT registration is requested through e-MTA using the VAT registration application, supported by the registry extract, constitutional documents, evidence of business activity, management and contact details, expected turnover, contracts or invoices, and, where applicable, proof of economic substance. Non-resident entities without an Estonian permanent establishment may need an Estonian tax representative for specific VAT compliance circumstances, particularly where required under the VAT Act. A non-resident applying for Estonian tax registration generally provides its foreign incorporation documents, foreign tax identification number, power of attorney, representative details, and certified translations or apostilled documents where requested. Registration is effective from the date determined by EMTA under the Estonian Value Added Tax Act; VAT registration is not automatically created merely by incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no elective pass-through classification for ordinary corporate taxpayers. An OÜ is generally subject to Estonia's distributed-profit income tax system: retained and reinvested profits are generally not taxed at the time of earning, while profit distributions and certain deemed distributions are taxed when made. From 1 January 2025, the general corporate income tax on distributions is calculated at 22/78 of the net distribution, subject to applicable statutory rules. Estonian anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the relevant EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive provisions; these operate as mandatory anti-abuse rules and are not elective. A charitable or public-benefit status is not available merely because an OÜ uses a charitable purpose or grant-making business model. Public-benefit and nonprofit tax treatment generally requires an eligible nonprofit legal form and, where applicable, inclusion in the statutory list of income-tax-exempt nonprofit associations, foundations and religious associations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply arising in Estonia during a calendar year. The threshold applies under the statutory VAT rules and does not eliminate registration obligations for transactions or circumstances for which registration is required independently of turnover. The standard VAT registration threshold is not a general threshold for non-resident businesses making supplies subject to reverse charge or other special rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier or its representative dispatches or transports the goods outside the European Union and the statutory export conditions are satisfied. The supplier must retain reliable evidence that the goods left the European Union, normally including the electronic customs export record and exit confirmation. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% VAT where the customer is identified for VAT in another EU Member State, the goods are transported from Estonia to that Member State, the supplier reports the transaction correctly, and the required documentary evidence is retained. Services supplied to a business customer are generally taxed according to the place-of-supply rules; a service supplied to a non-Estonian business customer may be outside the scope of Estonian VAT rather than technically zero-rated. Specific services connected with goods, immovable property, passenger transport, events, financial activities or private customers require separate place-of-supply analysis. Charitable purpose, grant distribution or foundation-related status does not by itself create export zero-rating or VAT exemption.

  • Electronic customs export declaration with the Estonian Economic Operators Registration and Identification number where required
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic evidence that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, currency, delivery terms and VAT treatment
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier record or equivalent logistics evidence
  • Packing list and purchase order or sales contract where relevant
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence where available and appropriate
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number, transport evidence and required recapitulative statement data
  • Accounting records, VAT return records and reconciliation supporting the reported export or intra-Community supply
  • Bank payment or inward-remittance evidence where commercially available or requested as corroboration; Estonia does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a universal condition for export VAT treatment

Estonia does not generally impose universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or continuous transaction controls for all businesses. VAT invoices may be issued electronically if authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility are maintained. VAT-registered persons file VAT returns and related reporting electronically through e-MTA, generally by the 20th day of the following month. Customs export declarations are lodged electronically through the applicable Estonian customs systems and EU customs infrastructure. Public-sector procurement may require structured electronic invoices under applicable public procurement rules, but this is not a general private-sector real-time clearance mandate. No general statutory digital VAT stamp is required for ordinary invoices.

Estonia has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for export proceeds. Export evidence must be sufficiently reliable and retained for the applicable accounting and tax record-retention period. EMTA may deny the 0% rate and assess output VAT, late-payment interest and associated liabilities where export conditions or documentary substantiation are not met. Incorrect VAT returns may require correction and payment of the underpaid tax. Intentional or negligent tax violations may lead to administrative penalties under Estonian law; deliberate tax fraud or submission of materially false information may create criminal exposure under the Penal Code. Unlawfully claimed VAT refunds may be recovered with interest and may trigger audit, penalty and enforcement measures. A charitable entity does not receive protection from reassessment merely because transactions support philanthropic activities.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia applies EU rules on free movement of capital and payments. The euro is freely convertible, and there are no general exchange controls or prior central-bank approvals for ordinary cross-border capital transfers, current-account payments, or profit remittances. Transfers remain subject to sanctions, AML/CFT, tax, company-law, contractual, and bank due-diligence controls. Dividends may be paid only from legally distributable profits following the required corporate approvals and solvency safeguards; Estonian corporate income tax generally arises when profits are distributed, not when retained. Payments to non-residents may require tax withholding or reporting where applicable. A charitable purpose does not itself confer tax-exempt status on an OÜ.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no general export-proceeds repatriation deadline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or held in accounts in Estonia or abroad, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, AML/CFT, and contractual obligations. Banks may request invoices, customs or transport records, contracts, and explanations of payment purpose.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. The Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) supervises regulated financial institutions; the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) performs statutory AML/CFT functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ordinary EUR collections and payments through an EEA-authorised bank or electronic-money institution passported into Estonia; low as a substitute for a domestic bank where local account services, grant controls, cash services, or complex correspondent payments are needed. Verify the provider’s home-state authorisation, Estonian passporting, safeguarding arrangements, permitted services, payment limits, and account legal form. E-money or payment accounts are not necessarily bank deposits and may not have deposit-guarantee protection. An offshore provider without applicable EEA authorisation cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services in Estonia merely by offering an online account.

Reconcile the payment to the subscription or loan agreement, shareholder resolution, donor agreement where applicable, originating-account evidence, and accounting records. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution; retained and reinvested profits are not currently subject to corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For distributions made from 1 January 2025, an Estonian OÜ generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the net dividend. Estonia generally does not impose additional withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders. A 7% withholding tax may apply to dividends paid to resident or non-resident natural persons where the dividend is subject to the ordinary 22/78 regime, subject to applicable treaty relief. Recipient classification and the payment date must be verified.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no universal transaction-value threshold that automatically triggers a Local File for every Estonian OÜ. Mandatory transfer-pricing documentation generally applies to qualifying large enterprises meeting at least one prescribed size condition, including approximately 250 employees, annual turnover of at least EUR 50 million or balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43 million, and to specified categories of taxpayers and transactions, including certain transactions with low-tax jurisdictions. The relevant currency is EUR. A Local File and, where applicable, a Master File should nevertheless be maintained where material controlled transactions create arm's-length risk. Documentation must generally be provided to the tax authority within the statutory response period, commonly 60 days after request.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no standalone regime labelled as a general Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, permanent-establishment rules, anti-avoidance principles, EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive measures, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules and treaty anti-abuse standards. An OÜ should maintain genuine decision-making, local accounting and records, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, bank-account control, commercial rationale and evidence that charitable or grant activity is actually performed. e-Residency, an Estonian registered address or a nominee director alone does not establish tax residence, management substance or treaty entitlement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Misclassification of legal form and charitable status

Statutory sanction: Loss of charitable or donation-related relief, reassessment of corporate income tax, VAT or payroll taxes, statutory interest, administrative penalties and possible regulatory consequences. An Estonian foundation is ordinarily a separate sihtasutus, not an OÜ.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for tax loss, unlawful distributions and breach of fiduciary duties. Personal criminal liability requires individual participation, intent or statutory grounds; corporate status does not shield deliberate tax fraud. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are not automatic and require applicable enforcement or criminal proceedings.
Undeclared distributed profit and disguised dividend

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax at 22/78 of the taxable net distribution, possible 7% dividend withholding where applicable, statutory interest, reassessment, fines and potential criminal tax-evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution, liability for unlawful distribution and damages to the company or creditors may arise. Directors who knowingly approve concealment or false records may face individual fines or imprisonment under applicable criminal provisions.
Tax evasion and false accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment, interest, coercive penalties, administrative fines and criminal sanctions for intentional or aggravated tax evasion, including imprisonment for responsible individuals and criminal fines for legal persons where statutory thresholds are met.

Director personal liability: The managing director, board member, accountant or other participant may be prosecuted personally where knowledge and participation are established. The corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but intentional misconduct can generate direct civil and criminal exposure.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length income or expense, additional corporate income tax upon distribution or deemed distribution, interest, penalties and possible denial of deductions or treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for losses caused by knowingly approving non-commercial related-party transactions or inadequate records. Criminal liability requires proof of a qualifying intentional offence.
Beneficial ownership, AML and sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Suspension or termination of banking relationships, transaction blocking, reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit, administrative fines, confiscation and criminal sanctions under AML, sanctions and criminal law.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may face personal fines, professional restrictions and imprisonment for intentional serious offences. Asset freezing, travel restrictions or confiscation depend on the applicable enforcement or criminal process and are not automatic merely because a compliance breach occurred.
Unlawful distributions and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawful distributions, director damages, insolvency claims, reversal of transactions and possible civil or criminal liability for creditor prejudice or bankruptcy-related offences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore company assets and compensate creditors. Personal liability is assessed by conduct, causation and statutory insolvency duties; imprisonment is possible only where criminal elements are proven.
Permanent-establishment and management-location failure

Statutory sanction: Foreign-country corporate income tax, payroll, VAT and reporting exposure; Estonian reassessment where facts support Estonian tax liability; interest, penalties, dual-residence disputes and denial of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may face parallel foreign and Estonian compliance duties, civil liability for inaccurate filings and personal exposure for deliberate evasion. A registered office, e-Residency card or Estonian bank account does not prevent a foreign tax authority from asserting residence or a permanent establishment.
Unlicensed or regulated grant and investment activity

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders, activity prohibition, administrative fines, restitution, loss of licence or registration and potential criminal sanctions for unauthorised financial or fraudulent activity.

Director personal liability: Board members may face personal regulatory and civil liability for operating without authorisation. Criminal liability and imprisonment depend on the specific regulated activity, materiality and intent.
