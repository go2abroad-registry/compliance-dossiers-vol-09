Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); official portal www.emta.ee and e-filing environment e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee). Company-law registration and issuance of the Estonian registry code are handled by the e-Commercial Register (Äriregister).

E-government filing portal

A domestic OÜ obtains its 8-digit Estonian registry code (registrikood) automatically upon incorporation; the registry code serves as the operative TIN for both income tax and VAT and requires no separate procurement. A foreign entity without an Estonian seat that imports goods or carries out taxable supplies in Estonia must apply for VAT registration through the e-MTA portal, using a locally appointed tax representative where the MTA so requires and possibly providing a security deposit; such an entity receives an Estonian VAT number (KMKR). A non-resident establishing a branch or permanent establishment is additionally assigned a PE registry code from the Commercial Register. The e-Residency digital identity allows the entire registration and filing sequence to be completed remotely.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial, distribution-based system): resident OÜ and Estonian permanent establishments of non-residents are separate income tax taxpayers, with 0% tax on retained and undistributed profits and 20% gross / 25% effective tax on distributed net profits. No pass-through treatment or US-style check-the-box election is available, and there is no sub-national corporate income tax layer. — A domestic OÜ obtains its 8-digit Estonian registry code (registrikood) automatically upon incorporation; the registry code serves as the operative TIN for both income tax and VAT and requires no separate procurement. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (territorial, distribution-based system): resident OÜ and Estonian permanent establishments of non-residents are separate income tax taxpayers, with 0% tax on retained and undistributed profits and 20% gross / 25% effective tax on distributed net profits. No pass-through treatment or US-style check-the-box election is available, and there is no sub-national corporate income tax layer.

Available election forms

No entity classification elections exist beyond the choice of legal form at incorporation. Statutory mechanics are limited to: (i) the reduced 14/86 regular-distribution dividend rate, available up to the average distributable profits of the preceding three years and subject to legislative sunset provisions; (ii) ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch notifications covering dual-resident or hybrid payer treatment; and (iii) the stand-alone election to treat retained earnings as deferred rather than currently taxable (the default rule, requiring no opt-in).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 50,000 annual taxable turnover, effective 1 January 2025 (raised from EUR 40,000). Non-resident suppliers are registrable from the first euro of taxable sales in Estonia, and intra-EU distance sales follow the cross-border e-commerce thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to the supply of goods physically exported from the EU to a third-country consignee where the exporter holds conclusive proof of exit, and to ancillary reverse-logistics services (quality grading, return repacking, labelling, handling and freight) directly connected to exported or imported goods within the meaning of the VAT Act place-of-supply and export rules. Intra-EU dispatches are zero rated where the customer's valid EU VAT identification number is obtained and Intrastat requirements are timely satisfied. Documentary proof of export must exist at the time of the supply; where evidence is still outstanding, the MTA allows a 90-day proofing window, after which the reduced rate may be denied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX) or electronic customs exit record (ECS/ATLAS output) confirming the goods left the EU customs territory
  • Transport documentation: CMR consignment note, Bill of Lading or air waybill referencing the return batch, with carrier stamp or digital validation of the physical exit date
  • Commercial substantiation: invoice or credit-note package, return-merchandise authorisation (RMA), inward remittance certificate or proof of payment, and accounting records showing reconciliation of the return shipment

No real-time fiscal pre-clearance or stamping regime for standard B2B invoices. The MTA operates a mandatory real-time data channel for cash-register sales receipts, and the national e-invoicing roadmap requires EN 16931-compliant B2B e-invoicing from 1 January 2027; B2G e-invoicing is already mandatory. Authorized electronic exchange is accepted via the e-MTA portal or certified service providers, and inbound e-invoices must be stored in an auditable e-archive for the statutory retention period.

The MTA retroactively disallows zero rating and reassesses VAT at 22% where export proof is missing, forged, or known to have been issued in respect of diverted goods; the clawback is compounded by late-payment interest of 0.06% per day and, in aggravated cases, by a 5% penalty surcharge on the assessed VAT. Criminal tax-fraud liability under the Penal Code carries fines and up to five years' imprisonment; under the Taxation Act, administrative fines up to EUR 1,300 for natural persons and EUR 8,000 for legal persons apply, and repeat offenders may lose access to accelerated export refund channels.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty free movement of capital provisions. The euro is the domestic currency, eliminating within-euro-area exchange controls. Profit remittance from an Osaühing is subject to distribution taxation at the corporate level (20% income tax on net distributions or 25% on gross payments, as applicable) rather than FX withholding. There are no exchange control approvals, quotas, or repatriation hurdles; cross-border dividend payments may be subject to treaty-based or domestic withholding tax, but no convertibility restriction applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonian law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion timeline, or minimum exchange ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in nostro or correspondent accounts, convert freely in the interbank market, and may keep proceeds offshore, subject to general anti-money-laundering reporting and fiscal transparency requirements.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the national central bank within the Eurosystem; foreign exchange administration is largely EU-driven, with no separate Estonian FX control authority. The European Central Bank sets monetary policy, while the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit oversees AML/CFT aspects of cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for cross-border payment execution and multi-currency virtual IBANs, but subject to significant legal restrictions. Offshore digital EMIs can provide payment services in Estonia only if they hold a valid e-money or payment institution license in an EEA member state and passport the service under PSD2. Unregulated offshore EMIs cannot provide Estonian IBANs or access local clearing infrastructure. For a reverse-logistics entity with low-volume, low-risk flows, regulated EMIs such as Airwallex provide compliant alternatives, but they are not deposit-taking credit institutions and cannot substitute for a bank account for statutory capital escrow or commercial lending.

20% on gross corporate profit distributions; paid as 20/80 of any net cash or non-cash distribution (equivalent to 25% uplift on the declared net dividend). Retained and reinvested profits are subject to 0% corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory domestic rate is 20% of the gross dividend (remitted as 20/80 of the net cash paid). Qualifying recurring distributions enjoy a 14% effective rate (14/86 net uplift). Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, 0% applies where the corporate shareholder resident in an EU/EEA state holds at least 10% of the OÜ's capital for an uninterrupted 2-year period; the exemption is claimed before payment by filing the respective TSD annex with the ETCB.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before the 10th day of the month following the calendar month in which a taxable distribution, benefit or withholding event occurred. A passive OÜ with no employees and no taxable distributions is not obliged to submit a monthly TSD; the obligation is reactivated in any month in which a taxable event arises.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual TSD summary: 30 April of the year following the tax year for calendar-year taxpayers. Annual financial report: within 6 months after the financial year-end, i.e., 30 June for a calendar year. CbC notification, if applicable, must be filed by 31 December of the fiscal year to which it relates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Local File: mandatory where the aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions with related parties in a fiscal year exceeds EUR 250,000. Master File: mandatory at the top of the group where consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report notification: mandatory for constituent entities of MNEs with consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and produced within 60 days of an ETCB request; non-production entails a penalty of up to EUR 1,500 and permits the ETCB to estimate taxable income by applying an upward transfer pricing adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is enforced through (i) the General Anti-Abuse Rule in section 3 of the Taxation Act, under which transactions and structures whose dominant purpose is the avoidance of tax are disregarded; (ii) the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test applicable to all treaty claims; (iii) the ATAD CFC rules, which tax the passive income of a low-taxed foreign subsidiary of an Estonian OÜ; (iv) the domestic residence test of TuMS section 6, under which an OÜ registered in Estonia but managed in another jurisdiction is treated as non-resident for Estonian tax purposes, losing the 0% deferral; and (v) the OECD Pillar Two qualified domestic top-up tax in force from 2024 for groups above the EUR 750m threshold. A reverse-logistics OÜ is expected to demonstrate a physical registered office, a board with real decision-making authority in Estonia, local employment or substantive subcontracting, and financial accounts that reflect actual flows. An OÜ used as an invoice-splitting shell without any booking, grading or fulfilment function in Estonia will be stripped of treaty benefits and its undistributed profits will be taxed at 20/80 in the hands of the beneficial owner, with imprisonment of directors a possible criminal outcome.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion – undeclared deemed profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The unpaid amount is recharacterized as a taxable distribution with tax at 20/80, plus late-payment interest of 0.06% per day. ETCB imposes a coercive fine of up to EUR 1,200 per month of non-filing. Under Penal Code section 385, intentional evasion of an amount exceeding EUR 10,000 is a crime punishable by a fine or imprisonment up to 5 years; the OÜ is separately subject to a corporate fine under Penal Code section 14.

Director personal liability: Under the Tax Administration Act section 9, members of the management board are jointly and severally liable for tax arrears to the extent the company's assets are insufficient; a director who ordered or failed to prevent non-declaration is also exposed to a criminal conviction with disqualification from board positions, a travel ban and, regarding the aggravated form of evasion, imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer pricing manipulation – artificial intra-group margins

Statutory sanction: ETCB makes a primary adjustment, increasing taxable income to the arm's length profit; the adjustment is reclassified as a deemed dividend, applying 20/80 corporate income tax and daily interest from the original invoice date. A secondary adjustment may be made on the corresponding cash balance. Penalty for missing documentation: up to EUR 1,500; repeated or intentional mispricing constitutes tax evasion under Penal Code section 385 with penalties up to EUR 400,000 for the company or imprisonment for the responsible director.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the transfer pricing documentation or who approved the intercompany invoices are personally liable for the resulting tax, interest and penalties if they knew or should have known the prices were not arm's length. Criminal prosecution may include a confiscation order against assets of the director.
Shell company / letter-box OÜ lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: The ETCB may refuse treaty benefits under the MLI principal-purpose test, deny the 0% deferral, and recharacterize all accumulated retained profits as a deemed dividend to the foreign beneficial owner at 20/80, with interest. The OÜ is also exposed to compulsory deletion from the commercial register for failure to provide a registered office or to file annual accounts. E-residency services are revoked and the company name is placed on the public warning list.

Director personal liability: The nominal director (often a nominee) is personally liable for the company's tax debts by virtue of the Tax Administration Act section 9 when the entity is a sham; nominee directors face disqualification orders and a travel ban. A beneficial owner who directs the sham through a trustee can be prosecuted as a de facto director under the Commercial Code, with imprisonment up to 5 years.
