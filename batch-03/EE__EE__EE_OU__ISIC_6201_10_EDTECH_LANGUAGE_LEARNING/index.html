Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), an agency of the Ministry of Finance, with the e-Maksuamet (e-MTA) digital filing portal at https://emta.ee; company registration is handled by the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) via the e-Business Register at https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned OÜ: (1) register the company online in the Commercial Register using ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID, or an e-Residency digital identity — there is no minimum share capital requirement; (2) the Commercial Register automatically notifies the MTA, which issues the registry code, i.e. the statutory Estonian tax identification number; (3) separately apply for VAT registration through the e-MTA portal by filing the electronically signed VAT registration application; the MTA issues the VAT identification number (KMKR-kood) and confirms the date from which VAT liability arises; (4) a non-resident entity without an Estonian permanent establishment must apply directly to the MTA for VAT registration, or, for B2C digital services supplied to EU consumers, may instead register for the Union OSS scheme without obtaining an Estonian VAT number; supporting documents for a non-resident entity include board member data, articles of association, proof of a local place of business and beneficial ownership declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned OÜ: (1) register the company online in the Commercial Register using ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID, or an e-Residency digital identity — there is no minimum share capital requirement; (2) the Commercial Register automatically notifies the MTA, which issues the registry code, i.e. the statutory Estonian tax identification number; (3) separately apply for VAT registration through the e-MTA portal by filing the electronically signed VAT registration application; the MTA issues the VAT identification number (KMKR-kood) and confirms the date from which VAT liability arises; (4) a non-resident entity without an Estonian permanent establishment must apply directly to the MTA for VAT registration, or, for B2C digital services supplied to EU consumers, may instead register for the Union OSS scheme without obtaining an Estonian VAT number; supporting documents for a non-resident entity include board member data, articles of association, proof of a local place of business and beneficial ownership declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election exists under Estonian law; an OÜ is unconditionally a separate corporate taxable person. Available statutory elections are limited to: (i) voluntary VAT registration before the EUR 40,000 threshold is crossed; (ii) VAT group registration under VAT Act §9(3); (iii) election of the EU OSS (Union scheme) for B2C telecommunications, broadcasting and digital services instead of multiple EU VAT registrations; (iv) for CIT, qualification for the 14% preferential rate on regular dividend distributions under Income Tax Act §50; the participation exemption applies mandatorily by statute and cannot be elected. Hybrid mismatch rules are applied automatically under the ATAD transposition, with no taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period for Estonian resident businesses (VAT Act §19); voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold; non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Estonia are required to register without any threshold (VAT Act §20), except where the reverse charge or the OSS procedure applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU qualify for the 0% VAT rate under VAT Act §16 provided the exporter can prove the goods physically left EU territory; intra-Community supplies qualify for 0% under VAT Act §18 where the buyer holds a valid EU VAT number and the goods are transported to another Member State. For EdTech subscription services: B2B supplies to non-EU customers are outside the scope of Estonian VAT under the place-of-supply rules, so no VAT is chargeable and the supplier must retain evidence of the customer's business status and location; B2B supplies to EU customers qualify for 0% in Estonia only with the customer's valid EU VAT number; B2C supplies of digital services to EU consumers are taxed at the consumer's Member State VAT rate through the OSS procedure or local registration, not at the Estonian rate. Direct exports of services ancillary to exported goods may also be zero-rated with appropriate documentation.

  • Customs export declaration (e-EX) with the export movement reference number (MRN) evidencing departure from the EU
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note or equivalent transport document proving the goods left EU territory
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract or purchase order from the foreign customer
  • Bank SWIFT receipt record or inward remittance evidence confirming settlement of the export sales proceeds in EUR
  • For cross-border digital/EdTech services: evidence of customer location and business status (valid EU VAT number of the EU recipient, or corporate registry documentation of a non-EU recipient) and records of the digital supply channel (IP address, billing address, subscription records)

Estonia does not currently impose a real-time government pre-clearance or digital-stamping requirement for ordinary VAT e-invoices. Electronic invoicing is legally recognised under VAT Act §37 and follows the national ET-1 structured format and PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 where data are exchanged digitally. VAT returns (Form KMD) must be filed electronically via the e-MTA portal, normally on a monthly basis, using digital signature (ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID or e-Residency). The MTA applies automated risk-based cross-matching of VAT returns with customs declarations, intrastat data, OSS returns and payment-trail evidence; a digital control-stamp scheme is used for cash-register receipt verification in selected sectors, but it does not constitute an invoice-clearing mandate.

The MTA may disallow the 0% rate where the export documentation bundle is incomplete or inconsistent and reassess the supply at the standard 22% VAT, with default interest accruing at 0.06% per day under the Taxation Act and additional tax misdemeanour fines. A knowingly false zero-rated claim constitutes tax fraud under Penal Code §155, punishable by fine or imprisonment of up to five years for large-scale or repeated fraud; the standard VAT assessment limitation period is three years, extended where a criminal tax offence is asserted, and the MTA may immediately claw back refunded input VAT with interest and offset it against other tax liabilities following retrospective audit adjustments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area Member State with full current and capital account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and the eurozone legal framework. No exchange control approval is required for cross-border capital movements, shareholder loans, dividends, or profit remittance. The only material remittance hurdle is Estonian corporate income tax imposed on profit distributions at a rate of 20/80 of the net distribution (20% on the gross amount), reduced to 0% for reinvested and undistributed profits; this is a fiscal cost, not an FX convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Estonia imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion into euro, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no minimum retained percentage. Exporters may lawfully hold export receipts in any currency; only statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations to Eesti Pank may apply for regulatory data purposes.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia); no separate foreign exchange administration, with prudential supervision shared with the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate viability as a complementary treasury rail. EU/EEA licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Estonia, including Wise, Revolut, Monese, and Pockit, can provide rapid multi-currency IBAN onboarding for e-Residency companies and low-cost FX conversion. However, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot act as the primary sole banking relationship for statutory share capital escrow, supply of trade finance instruments, overdraft facilities, or deposit guarantee protection. Collected funds at EMIs are safeguarded client funds outside the Estonian Deposit Guarantee Fund. Recommended legal and treasury practice is to use an EMI only for operational disbursements, refunds, and FX conversion, while maintaining the main capital and settlement ledger at a fully licensed Estonian domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For non-EEA transfers, use SWIFT MT103 with field 50K containing the ordering customer legal name and address, field 33B specifying the currency and amount, and field 70 referencing 'INCREASE OF SHARE CAPITAL – EE_OU'. The EU Harmonised Transaction Code should generally be 'F31' (direct investment) or, where the bank accepts general operational purposes, 'OPER' based on the bank's internal classification.
