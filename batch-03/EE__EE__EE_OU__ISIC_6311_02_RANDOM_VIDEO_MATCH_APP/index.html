Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Äriregister), operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus, RIK) under the Ministry of Justice, via the e-Business Register portal (ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee); registry-court supervision is performed by the Tartu County Court Registry Department (Tartu Maakohtu registriosakond).

Governing statute

Commercial Code of Estonia (Äriseadustik), principally Sections 135 (minimum share capital), 136 (foundation deed), 137 (Articles of Association), 140 (payment of shares and deferred contribution), 178-186 (management board), and 187 (registration application); supplemented by the General Part of the Civil Code Act (Tsiviilseadustiku üldosa seadus) and the Commercial Register Regulation (Äriregistri pidamise kord).

Formation / constitutional filings

Digital Registration Application in the e-Business Register (ettevõtjaportaali äriregistri avaldus), annexed to the digitally signed Foundation Deed (asutamisotsus) and Articles of Association (põhikiri), executed with a qualified digital identity (e-Residency Digi-ID, Smart-ID or Mobile-ID).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum statutory share capital is EUR 2,500 (Section 135(1) of the Commercial Code). Since the 2011 reform, Estonian law permits the founders to defer payment of the entire share contribution for up to 10 years from registration (Section 140), so no paid-up capital is mandatory at the filing stage. If capital is pre-funded voluntarily, the amount must be placed in a temporary settlement account in the company's name held with an Estonian credit or payment institution and is released to the company upon registration; no state-mandated escrow account is otherwise required. Until contributions are fully paid, the company may not make distributions to shareholders.

Funding & unlock path: The employer must comply with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus), including a documented written workplace risk assessment (riskianalüüs) before commencement of operations and appropriate workplace measurements for office ergonomics and equipment safety. Fire safety obligations follow the Rescue Act (Päästeseadus) and building fire-design codes: provision of fire extinguishers, fire-alarm and evacuation measures, and a self-declared fire-safety notice to the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) where required by the lease; ordinary IT offices are not subject to structural load inspections, and no separate fire-operating certificate is issued for non-assembly commercial use. A workplace health commissioner and health committee must be appointed when more than 10 employees are employed, and civil-defence coordination documents are required only for high-risk or critical-infrastructure facilities, which is not the case for this digital service company.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum statutory share capital is EUR 2,500 (Section 135(1) of the Commercial Code). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-services portal: e-MTA, accessible at www.emta.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Estonia must apply for Estonian VAT registration via the e-MTA portal or in writing, using a local fiscal representative if not established in an EU member state. The application requires company incorporation details, Foreign E-Residency certificate (if used), and evidence of physical or e-residency. The Estonian Commercial Register (Registry Code, 'Äriregistri kood') serves as the statutory tax identification number for an Osaühing (OÜ); a separate VAT identification number in the format EE999999999 is issued upon VAT registration. Non-resident entities without establishment may receive a special VAT ID upon registration and are subject to Estonian VAT on taxable supplies made in Estonia, unless the reverse-charge or OSS scheme applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not provide US-style check-the-box elections. The standard corporate tax system taxes only distributed profits (20/80 method, i.e. 20% gross on distributions). An OÜ may elect the preferential 14/86 rate (14% gross on distributions) for regular dividend distributions to resident natural persons, subject to a seven-year holding requirement and a minimum threshold of prior-year distributed taxable profits. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are mandatory statutory provisions; no elective election exists for hybrid entities or instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident taxable persons: EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover per calendar year (mandatory VAT registration above this threshold; voluntary registration available below it). For non-resident suppliers of digital services (e.g. video chat app services) to Estonian non-taxable customers (B2C): no VAT registration threshold — the Non-Union OSS (One Stop Shop) scheme becomes mandatory; for intra-EU B2C distance sales of digital services, the Union OSS scheme is used. For B2B services subject to the general place-of-supply rule, Estonian VAT is generally reverse-charged to the established Estonian customer, and no local VAT registration of the foreign supplier is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies to physical exports from Estonia to destinations outside the EU, provided the exporter proves that the goods left the EU within 90 days of supply (extendable to 6 months for certain goods under contract). Export evidence must include a customs-approved export declaration, transport contract and proof of arrival in a third country. Services: cross-border B2B services supplied to a taxable person in another EU member state qualify for the Estonian 0% rate (reverse charge) if the supplier obtains the customer's valid EU VAT ID and the place of supply is determined to be the customer's country. Services to recipients outside the EU are generally outside the scope of Estonian VAT, not zero-rated as such; for B2C electronic, telecom and broadcasting services, the place of supply follows the customer's location, triggering VAT in the customer's country via the OSS scheme.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) stamped/accepted by customs
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical movement out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export payment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list

Estonia operates an e-invoicing framework aligned with EU Directive 2014/55/EU. E-invoicing is mandatory in B2G transactions (suppliers to Estonian public sector entities) using PEPPOL-compliant formats. No mandatory continuous transaction controls (CTC), no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping. VAT returns and the monthly sales and purchase ledgers (as structural code data) must be filed electronically via e-MTA by the 20th day of the month following each VAT period; the VAT return must include the full export evidence details.

Estonian export VAT zero-rating is subject to heightened audit scrutiny. If the exporter cannot furnish valid export evidence within the statutory period, the VAT authority retroactively claws back the zero-rating and assesses VAT at the standard rate (22%), plus statutory interest at 0.06% per day (Taxation Act § 116). In addition, a tax surcharge of up to 50% of the assessed VAT may be imposed for negligent incorrect declaration, and up to 100% for deliberate tax evasion. Fraudulent use of forged Inward Remittance Certificates or false customs declarations triggers criminal liability under the Penal Code, with fines up to €800,000 for corporate entities and imprisonment for up to 4 years for responsible persons (Penal Code § 348). The company may also be deregistered from the VAT payer register and lose entitlement to zero-rating for future transactions. As Estonia imposes no specific statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day period appended reflects the standard evidentiary window used by Estonian tax auditors when verifying that export proceeds have been received and the transaction is economically real.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full euro-area member and maintains complete current- and capital-account convertibility. No exchange-control permits or central-bank approvals are required for inward capital injection, operating cash flows, dividend distributions, or disinvestment proceeds. Free movement of capital is protected under TFEU Article 63, subject only to EU/UN sanctions, AML/CFT customer due diligence, and the Estonian deferred corporate income tax on profit distributions (20/80 tax basis on regular distributions). Profit remittance is unrestricted but requires commercially reasonable bank verification of the source of funds and the underlying legal capacity of the OÜ.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion of export receipts, and no fixed timeline for repatriation exists in Estonia. Export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency may be retained freely in resident or non-resident accounts. The only applicable obligation is statistical balance-of-payments reporting by the resident bank through Eesti Pank/ECB reporting systems; no monetisation or conversion ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia); monetary and currency affairs are delegated to the European Central Bank under euro-area membership. Financial-market supervision and AML/CFT oversight are shared with Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority) and the Rahapesu Andmebüroo (Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A licensed and passportable EEA Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution may be used as a supplementary operational/payment-rail provider in Estonia, but it cannot provide banking licences, term deposits, credit facilities, or statutory escrow under Estonian law. Offshore EMIs without full Finantsinspektsioon authorization or EEA passporting are legally unsuitable for the OÜ main settlement account. Paymentwall and Apple IAP remain viable as proprietary payment rails, but final liquidity must settle into a domestic Eesti euro-zone bank account to reduce legal and FX counterparty risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained and reinvested profits; 20% gross tax on profit distributions (20/80 of net amount); 14% reduced gross rate for qualifying regular distributions

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% shareholder-level withholding tax; Estonia taxes the distributing OÜ instead. The corporate income tax on a declared dividend is 20% of the gross distribution (net ×20/80), with a reduced 14% gross rate for qualifying regular distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR; Local File documentation is required when annual turnover of the Estonian entity exceeds EUR 3,000,000 and related-party transactions in the fiscal year exceed EUR 50,000. Master File documentation is relevant for groups with consolidated group revenue above EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting follows the EU/ATAD threshold of EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue. Documentation must be available with 60 days upon request by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime equivalent to offshore financial centres. However, economic substance remains central to tax residency, permanent establishment analysis, beneficial ownership under tax treaties, EU directive eligibility, and the General Anti-Abuse Rule. A Video Chat Social App OÜ operated through e-Residency with only a virtual office, no local management, and no substantive operational function in Estonia can be challenged as a letter-box entity; its income may be reallocated to the jurisdiction of effective management or subjected to deemed distribution treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Disguised Profit Extraction

Statutory sanction: Additional corporate income tax at 20/80 (20% gross) on the disguised distribution, plus late-payment interest of 0.06% per day and fines up to €1,300. Criminal tax fraud is punishable under the Estonian Penal Code by a fine or imprisonment of up to three years; aggravated large-scale tax fraud is punishable by up to five years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or conceal non-arm's-length extraction can be held personally liable for the company's tax arrears under the Tax Procedure Act. Intentional participation can trigger criminal liability, travel bans, asset seizures, and, after criminal conviction, imprisonment; the corporate veil may be pierced for gross negligence or intentional misconduct.
Shell Company / Nominee Director / False UBO Disclosure

Statutory sanction: The Commercial Register may compel rectification, impose fines, and refuse registration filings; the Estonian Tax and Customs Board may disregard the corporate form and assess tax on the beneficial owner. Administrative fines of up to €400,000 may be imposed for AML/UBO failures; false registry declarations attract criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who sign false annual reports or knowingly facilitate an undisclosed beneficial owner are personally exposed. Liability includes civil damages to creditors, disqualification orders, seizure of personal assets, travel restrictions, and criminal penalties for money laundering or false certification.
Transfer Pricing Understatement / Inflated Related-Party Payments

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust the transfer price and treat the excess as a deemed profit distribution taxable at 20/80, adding late interest at 0.06% per day. Failure to produce documentation upon request triggers fines and can shift the burden of proof to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Members of the management board who knowingly sign non-compliant related-party contracts or conceal TP documentation may be jointly liable for the additional tax and penalties. Criminal consequences apply where the TP mispricing is part of a tax-fraud scheme, including imprisonment and travel bans.
AML and Payment-Rails Abuse in Video Monetisation

Statutory sanction: Supervisory fines under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act can reach hundreds of thousands of euros; the OÜ may be stripped of its bank or payment acquire relationships. Criminal money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of one to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to implement AML controls or who knowingly process illicit funds are personally subject to criminal prosecution, confiscation of assets, entry bans, and travel restrictions. Civil liability to harmed parties and tax authorities may survive the dissolution of the OÜ.
