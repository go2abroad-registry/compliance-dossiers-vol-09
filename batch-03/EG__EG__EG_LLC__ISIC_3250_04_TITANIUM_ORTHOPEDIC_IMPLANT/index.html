Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Egyptian LLC law under Law 159/1981 as amended by Law 180/2018 imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital; the AOA fixes the capital and each founder's quota. Capital may be paid in cash or in kind and, where the AOA requires immediate funding, a bank certificate evidencing the deposit is submitted to GAFI. No escrow account is statutorily mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense clearance from the General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) covering fire suppression, emergency egress, and safe storage of inert gases and flammable solvents; structural floor-load and electrical installation inspection by a licensed engineering office; Ministry of Manpower occupational safety and health authorization; mandatory laser and electron-beam containment clearance and ISO 13485 design-control validation for medical devices.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), under the Ministry of Finance; electronic tax services are provided through the ETA portal at https://eta.gov.eg, including tax registration, VAT registration, electronic invoicing, and tax return services.

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian limited liability company must obtain an ETA tax registration certificate and tax registration number after incorporation and commencement of taxable activity. The standard process is: (1) complete commercial registration and obtain the company incorporation documents; (2) secure the registered office or factory lease and proof of premises; (3) submit the ETA tax registration application to the competent tax office or through the ETA electronic services platform; (4) provide the articles of association, commercial register extract, company establishment certificate, tax card application, lease or title documentation, authorized-signatory identification, and power of attorney where applicable; (5) provide legalized and Arabic-translated corporate documents for foreign shareholders or foreign parent entities where requested; (6) obtain the tax registration certificate and tax identification number; and (7) separately register for VAT where the statutory threshold, compulsory-registration category, import activity, or voluntary-registration conditions are met. A foreign entity conducting taxable business in Egypt through a branch, permanent establishment, local agent, or other registrable presence must obtain an Egyptian tax identification number through the ETA. A nonresident without an Egyptian establishment may require a local tax representative, withholding-tax compliance, or recipient-side reverse-charge treatment rather than ordinary domestic tax registration, depending on the supply and applicable treaty position. Importers must also complete the applicable customs, importer, and Advance Cargo Information registration requirements through the relevant customs platforms.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not generally provide a United States-style check-the-box election for an Egyptian limited liability company. The LLC is ordinarily treated as a separate Egyptian corporate taxpayer and is generally subject to corporate income tax at the applicable statutory rate, commonly 22.5% for ordinary companies, subject to special regimes and incentives. Tax treatment follows the legal form, residence, management, activity, and applicable special-zone status. There is no general elective hybrid-mismatch classification mechanism that permits an Egyptian LLC to choose pass-through treatment for Egyptian tax purposes. An entity may qualify for separately legislated investment, free-zone, or economic-zone incentives only if the statutory eligibility, licensing, substance, reporting, and approval requirements are satisfied; such status is not created by a check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover is the principal VAT registration threshold. Registration may be compulsory below the threshold for specified categories, including importers and other prescribed businesses, and voluntary registration may be available subject to statutory conditions. Manufacturers of medical or orthopedic implants must separately assess VAT treatment of domestic sales, imports, contract manufacturing, and any exempt or specially rated medical products.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Egypt are generally subject to VAT at 0% when the goods are actually exported outside Egypt and the exporter can substantiate the export through customs and commercial records. For titanium orthopedic implants, the exporter should retain the sales contract or purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, customs export declaration, transport document, proof of delivery or exit, and evidence of payment or export-proceeds collection. Services may qualify for zero rating where the statutory conditions for services supplied to a nonresident and used or enjoyed outside Egypt are satisfied; Egyptian performance, consumption, or benefit in Egypt may prevent zero rating. Zero rating does not eliminate customs, export-control, medical-device, quality, or destination-country regulatory requirements. Input VAT incurred in producing or exporting eligible zero-rated supplies may be recoverable or refundable, subject to valid tax invoices, accounting records, filing compliance, documentary substantiation, and ETA verification. Imported inputs and machinery remain subject to import VAT unless a specific exemption, suspension, customs regime, or investment-zone incentive applies.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the Egyptian exporter, foreign customer, product description, HS classification, quantity, price, currency, and zero-rated VAT treatment
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent multimodal transport document
  • Evidence of customs release and physical export, such as customs exit confirmation, port or airport records, or electronic customs-system confirmation

Registered taxpayers within the mandated classes must issue electronic tax invoices through the ETA electronic invoicing platform for business-to-business transactions and other covered supplies. The invoice must contain the prescribed taxpayer, customer, tax, product or service, value, and unique electronic document data, and must be digitally signed or authenticated using an approved electronic seal or signature mechanism. ETA integration generally requires portal onboarding or API integration, taxpayer authentication, electronic certificate credentials, and compliance with the applicable document schema. ETA electronic invoicing operates as a tax-authority validation and clearance framework for covered invoices, with a unique UUID or equivalent identifier required for legal recognition. Business-to-consumer transactions are generally subject to the separate electronic receipt regime where applicable. Failure to issue, transmit, retain, or correctly amend electronic documents can result in invoice rejection, denial of input-tax recovery, administrative penalties, and tax assessments.

The ETA and customs authorities may verify the physical export, product classification, customs exit, foreign-customer status, payment trail, foreign-exchange collection, invoice integrity, input VAT, and consistency between customs, banking, accounting, and VAT records. If zero-rating is unsupported or improperly claimed, the authority may recharacterize the supply as taxable at the applicable domestic rate, assess the underpaid VAT retrospectively, deny or recover input-VAT refunds, impose late-payment interest and statutory administrative penalties, and require amended returns. Fraudulent invoices, fictitious exports, concealment of taxable turnover, false customs declarations, or deliberate evasion may also trigger tax-evasion sanctions, including criminal prosecution, imprisonment, monetary fines, seizure or forfeiture measures, and liability for responsible officers under the applicable tax and customs laws. Export proceeds should be collected and repatriated within the applicable foreign-exchange period, generally 180 days from shipment unless an authorized extension or sector-specific rule applies. Failure to comply may cause banking or foreign-exchange penalties and may materially weaken the VAT zero-rating or refund file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions through licensed banks, including documented imports, service payments, dividends, royalties, loan servicing, and other approved commercial payments. Capital-account transactions, shareholder funding, intercompany loans, acquisitions, and repatriation of investment proceeds remain subject to bank verification, corporate-registration records, tax clearance or tax-payment evidence where applicable, underlying contracts, and CBE or other regulatory requirements. Egypt does not generally impose a blanket prohibition on repatriation of lawful profits or invested capital, but practical delays may arise from foreign-currency liquidity, enhanced compliance review, incomplete corporate records, sanctions screening, customs documentation, tax disputes, or restrictions applicable to the relevant transaction. All foreign-exchange dealings should be conducted through licensed Egyptian banks or other CBE-authorized institutions; informal market settlement is prohibited and presents material AML and regulatory risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be supported by the relevant customs and commercial documentation and routed through licensed Egyptian banks in accordance with current CBE, banking, customs, and foreign-trade requirements. Egypt does not generally operate a single universal statutory mandatory-conversion ratio requiring every exporter to surrender a fixed percentage of export receipts into Egyptian pounds. The exporter should nevertheless credit or repatriate proceeds through the Egyptian banking system within the period prescribed by the applicable CBE rules, bank procedures, export regime, and transaction documentation; a commonly applied operational benchmark is six months from shipment, but the current rule must be confirmed with the servicing bank for the specific export classification. Banks may require evidence of shipment, customs clearance, invoice, bill of lading, collection or LC records, and reconciliation of export proceeds. Retention of foreign currency, conversion timing, and permitted use of proceeds are subject to the bank's CBE-compliant procedures and any sector-specific or temporary controls in force at the time.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and applicable CBE foreign-exchange, banking, payment-system, and anti-money-laundering regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment support and Low as a substitute for an Egyptian corporate bank account. An offshore electronic-money institution generally cannot accept local Egyptian deposits, provide regulated Egyptian payment services, operate an Egyptian collection account, or bypass CBE foreign-exchange controls without the relevant Egyptian authorization or a licensed-bank structure. It may support supplier payments, card acquiring, or treasury technology only where its home-jurisdiction authorization, correspondent-bank permissions, Egyptian recipient-bank controls, sanctions screening, data requirements, and underlying-purpose documentation are satisfied. Offshore wallet balances should not be used for export-proceeds retention, undocumented capital injections, local payroll, tax payments, or settlement designed to avoid Egyptian banking channels.

Offshore wallet balances should not be used for export-proceeds retention, undocumented capital injections, local payroll, tax payments, or settlement designed to avoid Egyptian banking channels.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 (total aggregate annual value of related-party transactions); mandatory Local File obligation. Master File is required if the group's related-party transactions exceed EGP 20,000,000 and consolidated group revenue exceeds EGP 300,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egyptian tax practice applies BEPS-aligned economic substance principles; entities claiming treaty benefits, free-zone exemptions, or low-tax incentives must demonstrate a physical office in Egypt, an active workforce, substantive local management decisions, and business-relevant fixed assets; commercial manufacturing entities such as a titanium orthopedic implant producer must evidence local production, warehouse inventory, and technical personnel; failure to meet substance standards triggers denial of tax benefits, treaty relief, and potential recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Transfer Pricing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax amount (minimum EGP 500,000); monthly delayed payment surcharge of 3% plus 14% annual interest; compulsory unpaid tax collection; criminal imprisonment of individuals responsible for a term of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors found to have participated in or tolerated evasion are personally jointly liable for taxes and fines; the ETA may place a travel ban, seize personal assets, and directors may receive prison sentences.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of corporate income tax incentives, retroactive claw-back of tax holidays, recharacterization of payments as hidden dividend distributions subject to 10% WHT; imposition of penalties up to EGP 500,000 based on the value of unjustified benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives can be held liable for the back-due tax and can be disqualified from serving as officers in Egyptian companies; travel bans and seizure of personal bank accounts apply if the entity is deemed a sham.
Money Laundering / AML Breach in the Medical Devices Sector

Statutory sanction: Fine of EGP 200,000 to EGP 1,000,000 and imprisonment from 3 to 7 years under Egyptian Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002; businesses facilitating the healthcare sector are considered a Medium-Risk Enhanced category and must suspend any unusual transaction outside the banking rails.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers face financial penalties, confiscation of funds, and imprisonment; beneficial owners and directors are subjected to personal asset tracing, travel restriction, and dissolution of the company if the veil-piercing conditions are proven.
