Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum stated share capital of an Osaühing is EUR 1 (Companies Act § 135(1)); the full subscribed capital must be declared in the application and paid up within the statutory period. For a SPAC vehicle, 100% of the offering proceeds must be deposited into a segregated trust/escrow account maintained with a licensed Estonian credit institution and released only on a shareholder-approved qualifying acquisition; non-cash contributions require a licensed auditor's valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: At formation the SPAC has no physical premises, therefore fire safety, structural load, and workplace structural clearances are deferred. Before occupying any office space, the company must obtain a valid building use approval (kasutusluba), a fire safety certificate issued by the Rescue Board (Päästeamet), and electrical and structural inspection reports from a certified expert. Upon the first engagement of an employee, the employer must complete a workplace risk assessment, register with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon), and implement mandatory fire drills and evacuation procedures. A SPAC holding only financial assets and having no premises is not subject to physical inspections.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum stated share capital of an Osaühing is EUR 1 (Companies Act § 135(1)); the full subscribed capital must be declared in the application and paid up within the statutory period. For a SPAC vehicle, 100% of the offering proceeds must be deposited into a segregated trust/escrow account maintained with a licensed Estonian credit institution and released only on a shareholder-approved qualifying acquisition; non-cash contributions require a licensed auditor's valuation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet; MTA), operational digital portal e-MTA (www.emta.ee); company and VAT registration is integrated with the e-Business Register (www.rik.ee/arikontor) administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems.

E-government filing portal

A foreign founder establishes an Osaühing (OÜ) or Estonian branch through the e-Business Register, remotely via e-Residency digital identification where available; registration in the Commercial Register automatically triggers ordinary tax registration and issuance of the company's registry code (usable as the TIN). For VAT, the OÜ must submit a separate registration application in e-MTA; a non-resident entity without Estonian seat/permanent establishment must appoint a resident tax representative and may be required to post security before obtaining an Estonian VAT number (format EE + 9 digits). The SPAC vehicle must also complete AML/KYC verification for the trust escrow bank account; foreign directors or shareholders need an Estonian identification code or e-token to access e-MTA.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation with a unique deferred distribution-based corporate income tax (CIT) regime: undistributed and retained profits are taxed at 0%; CIT arises only on distributions and deemed profit distributions (dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, transfer-pricing adjustments, and non-business costs) at the standard rate of 20/80 of the net amount, producing a headline effective rate of 20% on pre-tax profit. Estonian-resident companies (registered in the Commercial Register or effectively managed in Estonia) are subject to Estonian CIT on distributions regardless of the source of underlying profits; foreign permanent establishments of Estonian companies may generate exempt distributive income in certain treaty cases. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with a unique deferred distribution-based corporate income tax (CIT) regime: undistributed and retained profits are taxed at 0%; CIT arises only on distributions and deemed profit distributions (dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, transfer-pricing adjustments, and non-business costs) at the standard rate of 20/80 of the net amount, producing a headline effective rate of 20% on pre-tax profit. Territorial scope: worldwide Solvency? Estonian-resident companies (registered in the Commercial Register or effectively managed in Estonia) are subject to Estonian CIT on distributions regardless of the source of underlying profits; foreign permanent establishments of Estonian companies may generate exempt distributive income in certain treaty cases. Tax-exempt zones: no free-zone CIT exemption is available; however, a bona fide SPAC holding IPO proceeds in escrow earns interest that remains untaxed until distributed.

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate US-style check-the-box classification; all Estonian legal persons are automatically opaque CIT subjects. Available statutory elections and regimes: (i) reduced CIT rate of 14/86 on regular qualifying profit distributions not exceeding the average of the three preceding years' regular distributions (with a corresponding 7% final income tax levied on resident individual shareholders, achieving an integrated 20% burden); (ii) ATAD hybrid mismatch elections via Income Tax Act assessments where deduction/non-inclusion mismatches are neutralised, including reverse hybrid and imported mismatch adjustments; (iii) VAT group election available for financial holding companies and affiliated entities (subject to MTA approval); (iv) option to apply the participation exemption or treaty relief for dividend distributions to qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; and (v) advance pricing agreements and binding preliminary rulings obtainable from MTA for SPAC merger and acquisition structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year for taxable supplies of goods and services in Estonia; a separate EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold applies to distance sales of goods and cross-border digital services (OSS regime). For a non-resident SPAC without Estonian fixed establishment, VAT registration may still be triggered by any Estonian taxable transactions, with a zero-turnover registration possible for the purpose of recovering input VAT only when an economic activity exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to a destination outside the EU are zero-rated when the supplier, the buyer, or a third party dispatches the goods from Estonia to a non-EU country within 3 months of the intra-community or domestic supply, and the exporter obtains and retains verifiable export evidence within 3 months of dispatch. Services directly linked to exported goods (freight, handling, customs clearance) and services supplied to non-resident business customers under B2B place-of-supply rules are zero-rated if the recipient is established outside Estonia and the reverse-charge or place-of-supply tests are satisfied. A SPAC's core financial services (escrow account interest, IPO underwriting-related fees, merger/consulting services to the acquisition target) are ordinarily outside the scope of VAT or exempt financial services, so export zero-rating is relevant only where the SPAC conducts physical goods trading or supplies qualifying offshore advisory services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX1/A with MRN via the EU Export Control System, including MTA customs release)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent binder bank credit confirmation (SWIFT MT103 / account statement) evidencing receipt of the consideration from the non-resident counterparty, as adopted in MTA administrative practice for export zero-rating evidence files

No nationwide real-time B2B fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous SAF-T transmission mandate exists. Estonia mandates B2G e-invoicing for invoices issued to Estonian public authorities via PEPPOL/UBL e-invoice formats (in force since 1 April 2019). All VAT returns and lists (including ECSL for intra-community supplies, Intrastat, OSS returns) are filed electronically through e-MTA in XML/structured digital format; an authorised tax representative signature via e-Residency/e-MTA token is required for foreign entities. Standard EC sales lists are due monthly/quarterly based on turnover.

The MTA scrutinises export zero-rating claims through cross-referencing customs export data with bank receipts; incomplete documentation results in VAT clawback with penalty interest at 0.06% per day, standard reassessment period of 3 years extended to 5 years in cases of fraud. Deliberate abuse, fictitious exports, or circular escrow/SPAC financing used to fabricate zero-rated turnover triggers criminal tax fraud charges under the Estonian Penal Code with custodial sentences up to 5 years, fines, and personal liability of directors and the appointed tax representative; the clawback recovers the unpaid VAT, interest, penalties, and potential reclassification of escrow income or IPO consideration as taxable deemed distributions, which in turn attracts 20/80 CIT and withholding consequences on the SPAC's shareholder redemptions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has no exchange-control legislation. The euro is fully convertible in respect of both current and capital account transactions pursuant to Article 63 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive. A foreign shareholder may freely remit dividends, loan repayments and disposal proceeds; no Central Bank permit or FX allocation is required. The only remittance hurdle is tax compliance: an OÜ must file a distribution return with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board and settle corporate income tax on distributions (standard 20/80 method, i.e. 20% on the gross dividend; the reduced 14/86 rate is available for regular distributions meeting statutory conditions). Sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification and AML source-of-fund checks remain contractual banking requirements, not FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory export-receipts repatriation timeline apply in Estonia. Exporters and SPAC treasury entities may retain export proceeds or foreign-currency capital in any EU/EEA credit institution. Eesti Pank only requires threshold-based balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical reporting, which the account-holding bank performs automatically; the corporate customer must correctly reference the underlying transaction type and counterparty. For treasury policy, export/contract proceeds should be tracked in SWIFT statements and reconciled to the customs and logistic evidence in order to pre-position for any AML inquiry.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), as the national central bank within the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank (ECB) has ultimate monetary policy and payment systems oversight for the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional Medium for operational treasury payments and Low for SPAC escrow trust. An EU/EEA licensed electronic money institution (a 'digital EMI') can open a euro account for an Estonian OÜ remotely under PSD2, but an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot legally provide a conventional escrow/trust account; client funds must be safeguarded in a partner credit institution. The Estonian SPAC IPO trust is therefore required to sit with a credit institution or qualified custodian. Furthermore, 'offshore' EMIs domiciled outside the EU/EEA have no single European passport into Estonia and would need a branch or authorised agent; their risk appetite in practice rejects blank-check/high-value structures. EMIs are useful for small operational disbursements, not for capital escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for sponsor equity and IPO trust deposits is via SWIFT MT103 / ISO 20022 pain.001 for euro credits and MT103 for non-euro; the Estonian bank will perform BOP reporting to Eesti Pank and may require the customer to state a transaction classification code in the payment purpose narrative (for example, 'direct investment / equity capital injection into EE_OU; SPAC trust deposit'). For SEPA euro payments, use the company's IBAN and a bank-approved payment purpose code for a capital contribution or equity subscription, together with the LEI, not a personal transfer description. Prior to executing, verify: (a) the sending account is in the same legal/beneficial ownership as the company or is covered by an investor agreement; (b) funds pass sanctions and AML screening at both sending and receiving banks; (c) SWIFT gpi end-to-end tracking (UETR) is retained; (d) the escrow account number matches the trust agreement; and (e) the bank receives legal confirmation that proceeds are contribution capital and are not characterised as a disguised distribution.High-value euro redemptions and business-combination payments should be settled over TARGET2/T2 (RTGS) or via SEPA Credit Transfer for sub-€100,000 transactions; non-euro payments require SWIFT MT103 through correspondent clearing (CHIPS, Fedwire or the relevant domestic system). Commercial documentary letters of credit are issued under UCP600 rules through the corporate bank if the SPAC acquires operating assets or makes trading advances; LC proceeds and claims are settled via SWIFT MT700/MT799 and reimbursement under MT202/MT202COV with full compliance.
