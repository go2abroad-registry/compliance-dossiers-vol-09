Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — unified tax administration via the official portal at www.eta.gov.eg and the 'inTax' / electronic invoice digital services platform (Egypt, Arab Republic; Member: Inclusive Framework on BEPS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or operating through an Egyptian office, permanent establishment or branch in Egypt must: (1) appoint a tax representative with a duly notarized and legalized power of attorney; (2) secure a registered Egyptian address supported by a lease or title document; (3) complete incorporation or branch registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) where a license is required (producing cryogenic pumps requires a statutory industrial license — Commercial Registry + Industrial Development Authority coordination); (4) file constitutional documents, board resolutions, passport copies and financial statements with the ETA under Law No. 91/2005 as amended by Law No. 30/2023 and recent 2025 amendments; (5) obtain the statutory Tax Card (TIN) and, where relevant, a separate VAT registration certificate issued by the ETA VAT department; (6) register in the ETA e-Invoicing/e-Receipts platform via web portal, API integration, or certified ERP solution; and (7) activate fiscal credentials for generating ETA-cleared invoices and QR stamping. Non-resident suppliers with a permanent establishment in Egypt are subject to the same TIN registration duty; entities acting solely at the free-zone/SCZone licensing regime follow special zone procedures, but zone entities still require a distinct tax identification code and must file periodic declarations to ETA even when using customs suspension.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian Limited Liability Company is a resident juridical person taxed on its worldwide income at the statutory corporate rate of 22.5% under Law 91/2005 (as amended, rate effective 2025; previously 20.5%). Non-resident entities are taxed exclusively on Egyptian-source income unless a Double Taxation Treaty provides otherwise. Industrial manufacturing profits derive from the head office/principal place of business allocation rules and the deeming provisions applicable to management and control; SCZone licensed companies within the Suez Canal Economic Zone may benefit from the special zone tax regime (Law No. 5/2022), including reduced or statutorily deferred profit taxation and streamlined customs/export treatment where a valid zone operating license is maintained.

Available election forms

None. Egyptian tax law applies a mandatory statutory entity-level classification; there is no elective check-the-box or pass-through mechanism. Partnerships (not applicable to the LLC structure) and joint ventures are fiscally opaque unless a specific decree provides otherwise. Egypt has enacted controlled-foreign-company and general anti-avoidance rules (including Article 33 bis of the Income Tax Law) that curtail hybrid mismatch outcomes and base-eroding structures; group/consolidated tax filing is not generally available, although amendments permit certain loss-offset or reorganization elections with ETA pre-approval (restructuring continuity-of-interest conditions).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies for compulsory VAT registration (Law No. 67 of 2016 and Executive Regulations), measured on a trailing twelve-month basis; producers of zero-rated goods may opt for voluntary registration regardless of turnover; suppliers of goods or services solely within designated exempt zones must verify whether the special-zone VAT suspension applies. Small suppliers with annual taxable turnover above EGP 250,000 may register under the simplified schedule at the ETA's discretion.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A manufacturer of cryogenic liquid pumps may apply VAT at 0% where: (1) tangible goods are sold to a non-resident buyer, ownership and risk pass to that buyer, and the goods physically depart the Egyptian customs territory within the statutory export window; (2) the export is evidenced by a validated Customs Export Declaration made through the NAFEZA single window; (3) an ETA-cleared electronic invoice carrying the statutory zero-rating reason code is issued in the exporter's name; and (4) the foreign currency consideration is collected through an authorized Egyptian bank in accordance with Central Bank of Egypt directives, including any CBE repatriation/offset rules. Services directly connected with exported goods (installation, off-shore technical commissioning, packing, international freight) may likewise qualify where rendered to a non-resident principal and substantiated by written contract plus bank documentation. Supplies into licensed SCZone beneficiaries enjoy a customs-duty suspension and, where statutorily applicable, zero-ratable treatment equivalent to export; the zone purchaser must hold a valid zone operator license and the goods must ultimately be exported or consumed in authorized zone operations.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued through the NAFEZA single window (Single Administrative Document) bearing the customs stamp and ED code
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill naming the Egyptian exporter as consignor/shipper
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from an authorized Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency funds
  • Commercial invoice accommodating HS/ISIC coding matching the ETA-cleared e-invoice serial number
  • Packing list, weight/certificate of origin, and any certificate required by the destination country (e.g., certificate of origin for GAFTA/COMESA preferential claims)

Mandatory e-invoice and e-receipt clearance under Law No. 67/2016, ETA Decision No. 208/2021, and subsequent amending decrees. Registered taxpayers must integrate with the central ETA clearance platform through the authorized API (or portal-based submission), transmit invoice data in the ETA JSON/XML schema before invoice issuance, and generate a machine-readable QR code and unique fiscal ID for every invoice. Clearance is real-time/automated; invoice validation includes TIN matching, commodity code cross-checks (ISIC/HS), and buyer/supplier rights verification, and any invoice rejected by the platform is treated as not issued for input-credit purposes, exposing the supplier to Article 20 late-payment and Article 69-70 civil penalties. The e-receipt mandate currently phases in by taxpayer turnover band; failure to integrate leads to suspension of electronic filing privileges and denial of input VAT offsets.

ETA performs systemic cross-matching of zero-rated export e-invoices against NAFEZA customs exit data and CBE foreign-currency reports; discrepancies generate desk audits and automated assessment notices. Goods not physically exported, shipments returned, or invoices bearing false exit numbers result in mandatory 0% reversal and assessment of 14% standard-rate VAT, plus delay penalties computed from the original due date under Articles 19-20 of the VAT Law and late-filing penalties (starting at 1% per day payable from the due date of the return). Fraudulent export zero-rating declarations — including forged bills of lading, fictitious customs declarations, sham circular transactions, or documentation reflecting repatriation through non-authorized accounts — constitute tax evasion under Article 72 of Law 67/2016, punishable by imprisonment for a term of not less than one month and a fine of not less than EGP 20,000 and not exceeding EGP 100,000, with confiscation and clawback of any refunded VAT plus fines equal to two to five times the evaded tax; for repeat offences or organized evasion, imprisonment up to five years may be imposed together with director/manager disqualification and exclusion from the e-invoicing platform. The VAT statute of limitations for assessment and collection is six years, running from the date the return falls due, and the ETA has discretion to publish final evasion rulings and to suspend the taxpayer's electronic fiscal credentials during the investigation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a liberalised current-account regime: imports and services payments, dividend remittances, and repatriation of profits are permitted through banks against ordinary supporting documentation and no separate CBE licence. Capital-account inflows such as equity injections, intra-group loans, and portfolio investments remain subject to CBE reporting/purpose codes, GAFI registration under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, and anti-money-laundering verification. Dividends/profit remittance do not require a pre-authorisation but can be delayed by temporary FX liquidity constraints; tax clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority in the form of a certificate of no outstanding tax liability is normally required for sums exceeding the historical paid-in capital. Transfer pricing/audited accounts must support the distributable reserve calculation. The EGP is convertible at a market rate determined by banks; official CBE rate and interbank rate converge after the March 2024 unification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current liberalised exchange regime, there is no mandatory conversion of full export proceeds into EGP and no fixed statutory surrender ratio. Exporters are required to repatriate export receipts into Egypt through a licensed local bank and file the receipt with CBE's electronic export proceeds form; the retained FX may be held in foreign-currency accounts, sold to the banking system at the interbank rate, or used for import/trade obligations. The practical statutory-completion timeline is within 90 days from the date of the customs export declaration or as extended by CBE circular in force at the time of remittance; entities in the SCZone benefiting from the 2023/2024 investment incentives should verify any applicable exporter registration and inward payment documentation rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – Foreign Exchange Administration, acting under the Exchange Control Act No. 97 of 1947, the Central Banking and Monetary System Law No. 88 of 2003, and Law No. 194 of 2020 delegating FX supervision to CBE.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low – as a standalone account provider for an Egyptian registered LLC. Offshore digital EMIs (Euronext / UK FCA e-money institutions, UAE payment firms) are not licensed to take deposits or execute EGP-denominated current-account transactions in Egypt and cannot satisfy CBE's 'beneficiary account in licensed Egyptian bank' requirement for import settlements. They may be used only as payment/correspondent rails within the European corridor after opening the primary treasury account at a CBE-licensed bank. Any EMI maintaining a segregated client account must document that it is not executing Egypt domestic clearing activity, otherwise it will trigger CBE unauthorised e-money business risk under Law 194/2020.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injection must be routed via SWIFT from the foreign parent's bank to the Egyptian operating LLC's account at a CBE-licensed bank using an MT103 with a clear beneficiary reference. The remittance purpose/description should use the CBE coding frame for 'direct investment – foreign company equity contribution', with the beneficiary name matching the commercial register and a unique CIB/Council-of-Ministers/GAFI investment file number if the facility is in the Suez Canal Economic Zone. The bank will require a copy of the GAFI import/export certificate or issuance from the General Authority for Investment, board resolution/proposed share capital increase minutes, a share subscription agreement, and an undertaking to file the CBE Form 441 (capital/debt operations) or the current equivalent e-reporting form; the receipt is then booked in the foreign-currency account and credit to the legal reserve is made according to the Egyptian accounting standards. No payment may be settled to a private offshore collection account before the Egyptian account is fully onboarded; the automated Clearing House ACH is only domestic EGP-denominated so cross-border funds must clear through SWIFT correspondent banking.For import of pump components and foreign technical services, settlement rails are SWIFT MT103 payment orders, MT202 covers interbank reimbursement, and commercial letters of credit (confirmed, irrevocable at sight or deferred payment) issued through an Egyptian bank with cable/SWIFT MT700/MT707 amendment routing. The trade disbursement must be marked with the Egypt import purpose code and supported by customs declaration, air/sea waybill, verified supplier invoice, and any necessary original certificate of origin for tariff preference. SCZone-operating LLCs may settle imports for the assembly of cryogenic pumps from a foreign-currency sub-account; an EGP-denominated settlement is executed at interbank rate; a bank payment order to a supplier in EGP is not permitted in only foreign-currency trade, except for local supplier payments under commercial-grade evidence. For LNG/cryogenic equipment that is classified under dual-use export/import regimes, the compliance pack additionally includes the importing carrier's export permit or destination exemption certificate and inland customs approval. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 annual aggregate for related-party transactions requiring a Local File; the Master File is required where the Egyptian constituent group revenue exceeds EGP 1,000,000,000; country-by-country reporting notification follows group consolidated revenue of EGP 25,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not adopted GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the ETA applies substance-over-form, beneficial-ownership tests, and general anti-abuse doctrines under Law 91/2005 and ministerial guidance. A cryogenic pump manufacturer must hold actual production premises, manufacturing equipment, qualified payroll, core operating decisions, and physical or digital inventory in Egypt. A shell invoice company with no operational substance will be re-characterized, and treaty benefits will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Accounts

Statutory sanction: Under Law 91/2005 as amended: restitution of underpaid tax plus monthly late-payment surcharge (2% monthly up to 60% max), administrative fine of EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000, and imprisonment up to five years for the responsible persons.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns may be personally liable for the full unpaid tax and penalties; veil piercing permitted; travel bans, asset freezes, and civil restitution orders are available.
Shell Company / Sham Factoring / Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: ETA re-characterizes the transactions under substance-over-form, disallows phantom deductions, applies the full 22.5% tax plus monthly surcharge, and may impose an additional penalty up to double the avoided tax; the Commercial Registry may deregister the shell.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in the sham arrangement can be held jointly liable for the entire tax deficiency, debarred from board positions, and subjected to provisional seizure of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalization / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increases taxable profit; penalty up to EGP 250,000 for documentation failure; the adjustment attracts 22.5% tax plus a 2% monthly surcharge; excess interest may be re-characterized as dividends and subject to 10% withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete or false transfer pricing disclosures face personal restitution; continued non-payment can lead to seizure of personal property and exclusion from the Egyptian banking system.
Money Laundering / AML & Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian AML Law 80/2002: imprisonment from at least 3 years up to 15 years for aggravated laundering, fines of EGP 100,000 to EGP 10,000,000, confiscation of laundered funds, and freezing orders issued by the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors with signature authority over affected accounts may face criminal liability, travel bans, personal bank account freezes, and extradition in coordinated cross-border financial crime investigations.
