Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) — Incorporation and Commercial Registry Sector, operating through the unified one-stop-shop and the electronic investor services portal, Ministry of Investment.

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 4 of 2023 (the Egyptian Companies Law), as amended, specifically the Chapter on Limited Liability Companies governing formation, quota capital, managers, and dissolution, read together with its Executive Regulations; supplemented by the Commercial Code (Law No. 17 of 1999) on Commercial Registry requirements and the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 on investor facilitation and guarantees.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Foundation Contract and Articles of Association (Aqd Al-Ta'sis wa Al-Nitham Al-Asasi) together with the Company Incorporation Application, filed electronically through the GAFI incorporation window, followed by automatic entry in the Commercial Registry and issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 39 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for Limited Liability Company incorporation under Law No. 4 of 2023; founders fix the capital freely. For the shipyard industrial license, however, the competent authority requires the LLC to demonstrate paid-up capital commensurate with the facility's construction capacity, and the subscribed capital must be deposited in an escrow/company bank account at a bank licensed in Egypt before incorporation is completed; foreign capital contributions are certified through the Central Bank of Egypt inward-transfer procedures.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Authority (Ministry of Interior) fire-safety certification covering a fire main ring around dry docks and quays, certified extinguishing systems for paint, thinner and flammable-liquid stores, welding-bay fire controls, and an approved emergency evacuation plan for the high-risk industrial site; structural load-inspection certificates for gantry cranes, slipways and quay walls issued by a licensed engineering consultant under Law No. 106 of 1976 and recorded with the municipal or SCZone building-control authority; Ministry of Manpower occupational-safety clearance confirming medical-examination protocols, PPE programs, confined-space entry procedures, hazardous-material storage compliance, and an active establishment file before shipyard labor forces are mobilized.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for Limited Liability Company incorporation under Law No. 4 of 2023; founders fix the capital freely. For the shipyard industrial license, however, the competent authority requires the LLC to demonstrate paid-up capital commensurate with the facility's construction capacity, and the subscribed capital must be deposited in an escrow/company bank account at a bank licensed in Egypt before incorporation is completed; foreign capital contributions are certified through the Central Bank of Egypt inward-transfer procedures.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Industrial Development Authority (IDA) under the Ministry of Trade and Industry, operating through the 'Investia' electronic industrial licensing platform; where the shipyard is located within the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone), the SCZone Industrial Licensing Center issues the same permit under identical technical standards.

Category: Industrial Operating License for the Shipbuilding and Ship-Repair Plant (Industrial Facility Operation License) issued under Law No. 15 of 2017 on Industrial Licensing, classified as an integrated Category (C) high-risk shipyard covering the construction of ocean-going container and dry-cargo vessels, together with the SCZone operating certificate where applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official e-portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg; e-filing portal: https://etax.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains the Unified Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID) by: (1) incorporating under Law No. 159 of 1981 and completing Commercial Registry registration; (2) submitting to the ETA the Tax Card application (Form 8) with notarised Articles of Association, Commercial Registry extract, tenancy/lease proof of the shipyard premises, manager/shareholder passports, and board resolution appointing the tax representative; (3) receiving the Tax Card (TIN) and mandatory VAT certificate; and (4) completing e-migration of the tax file on the ETA portal within the statutory registration period (30 days from commercial registration or from exceeding the VAT threshold). A foreign corporate shareholder may obtain a separate TIN for its Egyptian branch only if a permanent establishment arises; otherwise the Egyptian LLC registers in its own name.

A foreign corporate shareholder may obtain a separate TIN for its Egyptian branch only if a permanent establishment arises; otherwise the Egyptian LLC registers in its own name.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Egyptian LLC is a resident corporate taxpayer separate from its shareholders, subject to Corporate Income Tax (CIT) at 22.5% under Law No. 91 of 2005; capital gains and dividends from resident Egyptian companies are generally exempt for taxed corporate shareholders; manufacturing activities located in the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE) or public/private free zones may elect taxation on a territorial basis with an exemption or a gross-revenue tax regime.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box mechanism exists. Statutory elections available: (a) Free-Zone Project election under Law 91/2005 – tax at 1% of gross FOB revenue for goods or 5% of gross revenue for services instead of standard CIT; (b) SCZONE special regime under Law 83/2002 – election of 0% CIT for up to 5 years (extendable), 0% VAT on project inputs and special customs treatment, subject to SCZONE project licence and ETA approval; (c) election to exempt dividends received from other resident taxpaying companies; and (d) standard versus actual-margin stamp duty methods. Hybrid mismatch elections are not provided under Egyptian law; income tax conventions govern tie-breaker allocation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover (goods and services); VAT registration is mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the threshold. Voluntary registration is permitted and is standard practice for shipyard exporters to claim zero-rating and refunds; no minimum capital requirement for the LLC is imposed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exports of goods and services under Article 14 of Law 67/2016, including the sale of newly-built ocean-going container vessels and offshore shipbuilding/repair services supplied to a non-resident buyer. Substantive conditions: (1) valid VAT registration; (2) actual physical export of the vessel/materials and exit from Egyptian customs territory within the statutory period; (3) consideration received in freely convertible foreign currency remitted into Egypt through a CBE-accredited bank; (4) title and risk pass to a non-resident recipient; (5) the transaction is evidenced by an ETA-validated 0%-rate e-invoice; and (6) supporting contract, delivery certificate, and foreign-currency receipt are retained for audit. Shipyard projects in SCZONE may benefit from an exemption rather than zero-rating under the SCZONE law.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ECDL release certificate / export GCC statement)
  • Bill of Lading (or provisional delivery certificate for vessels under construction)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate of foreign-currency proceeds issued by a CBE-accredited bank
  • Notarised Sale Contract / Shipbuilding Agreement (with title, delivery, and instalment terms)
  • ETA-validated electronic export invoice reflecting 0% VAT
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin
  • Maritime vessel registration certificate / handover certificate evidencing transfer to the foreign buyer

Mandatory ETA e-invoicing for all registered taxpayers: invoices must be issued in ETA-prescribed XML/JSON format via ETA-certified API, ERP, or the e-invoice portal; the ETA performs near-real-time fiscal validation by assigning a unique UUID (fiscal stamp) and QR code to each invoice; e-receipts are mandatory for B2C and phased for B2B transactions; shipyards must integrate the e-invoicing API with the production ERP and issue the 0%-rate export e-invoice before goods leave the customs territory; paper invoices do not substantiate zero-rating.

ETA performs risk-based refund audits with a statutory 45-business-day refund issuance window; where the 0% claim is not substantiated by the full documentation bundle or the foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated within the mandatory 180-day window (with the vessel instalment schedule subject to CBE-approved escrow/LC terms), the ETA retroactively assesses 14% VAT on the transaction plus default interest on late payment and may claw back any refund already paid. Deliberate false zero-rating exposes the LLC and its managers to criminal liability under Law 67/2016: imprisonment up to 5 years, a fine of up to 100% of the evaded VAT, an additional penalty tax of up to double the evaded amount, joint and several liability of directors and tax representatives, and closure of the shipyard for repeated violations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-determined exchange rate system after the March 2024 floatation. Current-account transactions, including trade in goods, marine freight, port services, maritime insurance, and ordinary operating payments, are freely convertible through CBE-authorized banks. Capital-account transactions, notably direct foreign equity injection, intercompany loans, and foreign debt service, remain subject to CBE prior authorization, registration, and proper purpose-code filing. Profit and dividend remittances are permissible without a statutory cap, provided the LLC presents audited financial statements, an Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax clearance certificate, and satisfies bank-level AML verification. For a shipyard registered within the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone), repatriation of profits and foreign-currency proceeds may be accelerated by SCZone-specific regulations where the entity has secured the applicable SCZone incentives, but the legal entity in this pipeline is a domestic EG_LLC not explicitly identified as an SCZone invoice-issuing entity and therefore must rely on the general CBE framework. The Egyptian Pound is freely convertible for current account purposes but remains subject to liquidity management and bank matching requirements; onshore USD scarcity can delay wire execution unless the remitting bank holds strong correspondent limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from ocean-going container vessel construction and ship repair are statutory export receipts and must be repatriated into an Egypt-resident foreign-currency account within 90 days from the date of bill of lading, vessel delivery, or official completion certificate, unless the CBE grants an extended period. The CBE's administrative directives, as amended through 2024, require banks to convert up to 50% of repatriated export proceeds into Egyptian Pounds at the prevailing declared exchange rate, while the remainder may be held in foreign currency for lawful operating use or re-export obligations. The applicable conversion ratio is set by CBE circular and is dynamic; therefore the treasury blueprint must include a currency routing matrix that captures the circular in force at the date of each receipt. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window subjects the LLC to banking penalties, denial of future trade facilities, and potential anti-money laundering escalation. The shipyard must maintain a bank-approved Export Proceeds Repatriation Schedule supported by customs export declarations, vessel delivery protocols, and matching LC drawdown records.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Banking Supervision Sector and Foreign Exchange Control Department, acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, in coordination with the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (AMLU) and the Egyptian Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW and legally constrained. Egypt does not maintain an electronic money institution (EMI) passport or open-banking clearing license equivalent to the EU/UK EMI regime. Any digital wallet or e-money operation must be separately licensed by the CBE under Law No. 194 of 2020 and the CBE electronic money regulations; foreign offshore EMIs such as UK FCA-regulated or EU-licensed firms cannot open EGP-denominated corporate settlement accounts, issue escrow capacity, or provide domestic clearing rails for an Egyptian LLC without a locally licensed subsidiary or joint venture with a CBE-authorized bank. For an EG_LLC carrying a Medium_Risk_Enhanced AML tier, reliance on an unlicensed offshore EMI would constitute a banking law violation and a critical AML red flag. The recommended compliant substitute is the transaction-banking and host-to-host portal of a top-tier Egyptian bank, optionally layered with Swift GPI for cross-border tracking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All shareholder capital injections and non-resident installment payments must arrive through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to a CBE-authorized bank account of the EG_LLC. The remittance must carry a compliant purpose description, specifically 'Direct Investment - Equity Contribution to EG LLC' or 'Capital Increase under Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 as amended by Law No. 4 of 2021', and must be pre-notified to the receiving bank's compliance desk. The CBE reporting system requires the receiving authorized dealer to file the inward remittance with the exchange-control code for direct investment; the LLC must simultaneously file with GAFI if the investor is in the free-zone or outside the SCZone, and must maintain an updated Egyptian CR and tax card. For capital above the bank's internal threshold, the bank will request a Board Resolution authorizing the capital capture, a legalized incorporation certificate, and appointment of the compliance contact before releasing the funds to the operational escrow. Escrow release to the operating account is permitted only after the bank has matched the wire to the capital increase resolution and the GAFI filing, and after all AML source-of-funds questions have been answered to the bank's satisfaction.Primary clearing rails for trade disbursements are SWIFT MT103 for high-certainty supplier wires and irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit for major marine equipment, steel plate purchases, and sub-block construction contracts. The shipyard will issue a Maritime_Installment_LC series to upstream suppliers with payment against presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and an Egyptian-approved inspection certificate; installment disbursements under the related escrow account will be released on milestone certification by the shipyard's own naval architect and the buyer's surveyor. For operational dispatches, an Egypt-resident bank will require the LLC to submit the CBE import-form evidence or, where exempt, the matching purchase order; payments for foreign freight, insurance, and marine classification societies qualify as current-account transactions and are automatically permissible. Payments for foreign parent intercompany charges, technical royalties, or overseas debt service are capital-account events requiring CBE approval and a hard-coded underlying agreement, and must be separated from the trade flows. All outbound disbursements from the escrow ledger must be accompanied by delivery protocol certificates, builder's certificates, and a signed acceptance notice to satisfy the beneficiary bank's audit trail.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP forward contracts with CBE-authorized banks, executed with a term aligned to the shipyard's construction and installment collection cycle.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP) for long-term balance-sheet hedging of capital expenditure, LC payables, and foreign-currency revenue streams, subject to CBE treasury authorization and an underlying commercial exposure.Structured forward conversion programmes on export proceeds, allowing the LLC to fix the EGP conversion for the mandatory converted portion of export receipts.NDF (non-deliverable forward) instruments: not permissible for direct in-country corporate hedging; an Egyptian LLC must hedge exclusively through deliverable forwards booked at a CBE-authorized onshore bank, reflecting the unified-float policy and exchange-control prohibition on offshore synthetic hedging.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be filed when related-party transactions exceed EGP 8 million in aggregate during the tax year. A Master File is mandatory when such transactions exceed EGP 40 million. Country-by-country reporting (CbCR) is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EGP 3 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in some GCC states, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 109 of Law No. 91 of 2005 empowers the ETA to disregard transactions, structures, or arrangements lacking economic substance and whose sole purpose is tax avoidance. For DTA relief, the equitable approach of treaty entitlement also presupposes substantive economic activity, ownership, and substance in the residence state; asset-heavy shipbuilding operations are expected to maintain physical premises, personnel, and decision-making presence in Egypt to support treaty claims and avoid treaty abuse.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 200,000 and imprisonment for a term of 3 to 5 years under Law No. 91 of 2005, in addition to payment of the evaded tax plus a penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax and monthly late-payment interest. Repeat offenses can lead to license revocation and forced closure of the shipyard.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who participate in tax evasion are personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties if the company enters insolvency or when the separate legal personality is used to commit fraud. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be imposed by the tax authorities or court order, and a criminal conviction results in imprisonment of the responsible officers.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The ETA will disregard the entity for tax purposes under Article 109 GAAR, deny treaty benefits, and reassess tax at 22.5% on all Egyptian-source income. Additional penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded may apply, and the entity may be struck off the commercial register. In flagrant cases, prosecution for tax evasion and fraudulent commercial registration can lead to fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign declaration forms misrepresenting substance or who intentionally use a shell structure may be jointly and severally liable for all resulting corporate tax, interest, and penalties. Criminal liability for fraud can result in imprisonment, while exit bans and seizure of personal property are authorized pending payment of assessments.
AML / Money Laundering / Financial Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended), a corporate entity may incur fines up to EGP 1,000,000 and forfeiture of assets; senior officers face up to 10 years imprisonment. Non-compliance with beneficial ownership reporting and transaction monitoring rules results in additional CBE/Financial Regulatory Authority fines and revocation of the maritime industrial license.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners can be personally prosecuted for failure to file suspicious transaction reports, knowingly facilitating money laundering, or allowing the company to be used as a conduit for proceeds. Personal liability includes imprisonment, financial fines, confiscation of personal assets, and travel restrictions under prosecutor-approved travel bans.
