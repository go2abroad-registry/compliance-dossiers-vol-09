Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; a zero-capital OÜ is lawful since 01 January 2011. Standard commercial practice for aviation counterparties is a fully paid subscribed capital of EUR 2,500. Contributions may be in cash or non-cash and must be made to a bank account of the OÜ; no escrow account is mandatorily required by the Registrar. If subscribed capital is below EUR 2,500, restricted distribution and creditor-protection rules apply under Commercial Code Sections 140(7) and 145.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) in coordination with the Commercial Register; digital filing is mandatory through the e-MTA portal (e-maksuamet.ee).

E-government filing portal

An Osaühing (OÜ) obtains its statutory tax identification number automatically upon Commercial Register entry: the registry code is the corporate TIN. For VAT purposes, the taxable person must file a prior economic activity declaration electronically in e-MTA; the VAT registration number is formed as EE + 9 digits. A non-resident company with Estonian taxable transactions must register directly in e-MTA without a domestic VAT threshold; if established outside the EU/EEA, a tax representative may be required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An Osaühing (OÜ) obtains its statutory tax identification number automatically upon Commercial Register entry: the registry code is the corporate TIN. For VAT purposes, the taxable person must file a prior economic activity declaration electronically in e-MTA; the VAT registration number is formed as EE + 9 digits. A non-resident company with Estonian taxable transactions must register directly in e-MTA without a domestic VAT threshold; if established outside the EU/EEA, a tax representative may be required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Estonian law. Corporate income tax is deferred to profit distribution. The OÜ may elect to classify dividends as 'regular dividends' (paid at least once per previous three years) to benefit from the reduced 14/86 entity-level tax rate; the general distribution tax rate is 20/80. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Estonian Income Tax Act apply mandatorily and contain no elective mismatch mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover for Estonian-established taxable persons; €0 (no threshold) for non-resident persons making taxable supplies in Estonia; €10,000 for intra-Community distance sales of goods and certain digital services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically dispatched or transported by the seller or buyer to a destination outside the EU within 3 months of supply, evidenced by a customs export declaration, qualify for the 0% VAT rate. Supplies of provisions, stores, and in-flight catering to international airlines operating international passenger or cargo flights are similarly zero-rated as supplies for the operation of aircraft on international routes. Offshore services directly linked to exported goods may qualify for zero-rating. In-flight catering reserved for domestic flights remains taxable at standard rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/ECS record) confirming exit from the EU
  • Air Waybill (AWB) or Bill of Lading (B/L) evidencing transport destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank wire proof of settlement under the aviation contract
  • Delivery/acceptance proof for bonded in-flight meal stores loaded on a specific international flight

Estonia does not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance system, digital stamping mandate, or mandatory API-based e-invoicing for B2B VAT. E-invoicing in the universal E-arve format is used by public sector and accepted by the Tax and Customs Board; the Board conducts VAT return auditing through monthly/quarterly returns and documentary evidence on request.

The Tax and Customs Board may retrospectively deny zero-rating if documentation is incomplete, inaccurate, or the export movement is not proven; the VAT becomes due at 22%, plus late-payment interest of 0.06% per day (approximately 21.9% annualized) from the original due date, and penalties for undisclosed tax of up to 50% of the assessed amount in serious cases. Fraudulent export zero-rating or forged customs/AWB documentation can attract criminal liability for the board members, customs forfeiture, and VAT clawback under the general 3-year limitation period, extended to 5 years for aggravated tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area member since 1 January 2011; the euro is the sole legal tender and is fully convertible under Article 63 TFEU and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act. There is no distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility for resident or non-resident accounts, and neither exchange-control approvals nor central-bank permits are required for profit repatriation. Repatriation is effected by dividend distribution, which triggers Estonian corporate income tax at the distributing Osaühing (OÜ) level: 20% of the gross dividend under the standard rate, or 14% on regular, recurring distributions within the statutory three-year distribution-history regime; no additional withholding tax is imposed on outbound dividends to EU/EEA or non-resident shareholders. The practical remittance hurdles are corporate-law-based: the OÜ must have legally distributable net assets certified by the latest annual financial statements, a shareholder resolution must be recorded, and the paying bank will apply standard source-of-funds verification to any cross-border wire exceeding EUR 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Republic of Estonia imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios and no forced holding periods for export receipts. An Estonian OÜ may freely retain settlement amounts in EUR or any foreign currency in a licensed credit institution without any obligation to repatriate custody accounts. Compliance obligations are limited to EU customs declarations, Intrastat reporting for dispatches where thresholds are met, VAT zero-rating documentation for export catering sales, EU and UN sanctions screening of counterparties and payment messages, and the five-year retention of transport documents (air waybills, CMR, customs SAD forms) and commercial invoices under the Estonian accounting and AML/CFT framework.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting with Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority) and the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) for trade, customs and AML/CFT enforcement across cross-border payment flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary flows, Low as the primary banking relationship. A Finantsinspektsioon-licensed or EU-passported e-money institution (EMI) can supply multi-currency IBANs and SEPA/SWIFT-compatible collection and disbursement accounts for an airline-catering kitchen, but it is not a credit institution: it cannot extend working capital, issue documentary letters of credit, host structured FX-hedging master agreements, or provide the trade-finance evidence that aviation audit committees expect. Legal constraints under the Estonian E-Money Institutions and Payment Institutions Act and PSD2 require all client e-money funds to be safeguarded in a segregated account at a credit institution; the EMI may not commingle client funds, and it remains subject to the same Estonian FIU and Politsei- ja Piirivalveamet reporting obligations. Major airlines and ground handlers often apply enhanced supplier due diligence to payees presenting only digital-EMI bank details. The optimal blueprint uses a tier-1 domestic commercial bank for treasury and LC lines, with one or two reputable EMIs (operating from Estonia, Lithuania, the UK, or Germany) used only for fast low-value disbursements and emergency redundancy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an OÜ with zero minimum capital, the preferred funding structure is a tiered share-capital increase or a documented shareholder loan. Compliant routing: first, obtain a written shareholder resolution and register the capital increase with the e-Business Register; second, remit from the shareholder's personal or corporate account using SWIFT MT103, indicating purpose code 'S301 - Investment in equity capital' for equity contributions or, for shareholder loans, 'S302/S303 - Intercompany financing', with ISO 20022 purpose 'SUPP' when the wire also funds working capital; third, ensure the receiving bank issues a credit confirmation within five business days and that the beneficiary reference contains the OÜ registry code; fourth, note that no exchange-control declaration is required by Eesti Pank, but the bank will request recent source-of-funds evidence for any contribution exceeding EUR 10,000 (salary slips, capital-gains file, sale deed, or dividend resolution of the transferring entity); fifth, for fastest execution route the injection through the same Estonian bank where the OÜ has already passed KYC, with the shareholder pre-verified as a signatory on the account mandates.The primary clearing rail for all eurozone and EU-27 supplier settlements is SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) using ISO 20022 pain.001 with purpose code 'SUPP' for trade invoices. For non-EUR jurisdictions, use SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank reimbursement) stating the underlying invoice number in Field 70 and the standard purpose code '80 - Transfer for goods and services'. High-value kitchen-equipment and refrigeration-asset purchases are financed with UCP 600 compliant Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the Estonian bank via SWIFT MT700, with the beneficiary's documents presented through the advising bank. For receivables collection, the monthly ACH rail corresponds to the SEPA Direct Debit B2B (SDD B2B) scheme, pre-mandated by the airline or ground-handling client, with monthly due-date runs matching the aviation contract settlement calendar.
