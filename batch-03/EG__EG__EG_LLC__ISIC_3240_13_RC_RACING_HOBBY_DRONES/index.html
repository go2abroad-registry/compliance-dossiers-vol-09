Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Egyptian LLCs generally have no statutory minimum capital. The capital stated in the articles must be divided into equal-value quotas, fully paid by the quota holders at incorporation, and supported by the applicable bank certificate or other GAFI-approved evidence. Fees are payable in Egyptian pounds under the applicable tariff; no fixed official fee is published as a USD-denominated amount.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; e-services portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg and the integrated tax services portal of the Ministry of Finance

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA using the unified tax registration form. The application is submitted online via the ETA e-services portal or in person at the competent tax office, accompanied by the commercial registry extract, legal incorporation contract and bylaws, certificate of the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or form for the entity, certified ID/passport of the legal representative, power of attorney, registered office lease/proof of address, and board resolution appointing the tax representative. Upon approval, ETA issues a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) covering income tax, VAT, payroll tax, and stamp tax, followed by a separate VAT registration certificate where the threshold applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope; the LLC is a resident juridical person taxed at entity level on worldwide income under Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005. Profits distributed to non-resident members may attract withholding tax. This status is mandatory and no pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

Not applicable. Egyptian tax law does not provide US-style check-the-box elections or OECD hybrid mismatch elections. A limited liability company is mandatorily classified as a taxable corporate entity. VAT registration, quarterly payment election, and invoicing timing are administrative elections only and do not alter entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration applies when the annual value of taxable supplies of goods and services exceeds EGP 500,000. A taxpayer exceeding EGP 500,000 must file VAT registration within thirty days. Voluntary registration is permitted for persons who are not under mandatory registration, subject to ETA approval and security requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for the supply of tangible goods physically exported outside Egypt, provided the exporter is a registered VAT taxpayer, the sale contract is held, the goods leave Egypt within the statutory period, and the customs export declaration is accepted. The FPV racing drone components and assembled units, including carbon-fiber frames and brushless motor systems, qualify as exported goods when the sale transfers risk and possession to a non-resident customer and strict documentation is retained. Direct services rendered and consumed outside Egypt, or services that are ancillary to qualifying exported goods, may be zero-rated under ETA guidance where the recipient is outside Egypt and no use of the service occurs in Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (including the Egyptian Customs Authority export document and CargoX/platform validation),
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Egypt to the foreign destination,
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian bank or international bank transfer evidence demonstrating receipt of export proceeds,
  • Signed commercial invoice and export contract/order,
  • Packing list and itemized BOM supporting the FPV racing drone products and spare parts,
  • ETA-approved electronic invoice with QR code and digital signature,
  • Proof of customs exit and customs authorization/release documentation, and
  • Any export registration certificate or export declaration card required by the General Organization for Export and Import Control (GOEIC).

Egypt has a mandatory e-invoicing regime for B2B transactions. Registered taxable persons must issue electronic invoices in the ETA-approved XML format, digitally sign them using a valid ETA digital certificate, validate the data structure through the ETA e-invoicing REST API or portal, and obtain a unique invoice UUID and QR code before transmitting the invoice to the customer. The ETA performs real-time validation, hash authentication, and fraud checks through its centralized clearance platform. Retail B2C transactions are subject to the phased e-receipts regime. Failure to integrate with the ETA clearance system results in invoice rejection, suspension of tax clearance, and penalties. The Customs Declaration, ACI (Advance Cargo Information), and the national CargoX intelligent clearance system also interact digitally with export invoices.

ETA may scrutinize export transactions for physical exit, genuine sales to non-residents, realized currency conversion, and inward remittance. If zero-rating is claimed on fictitious or non-genuine exports, the ETA will claw back the entire zero-rated VAT, impose the standard VAT or the relevant schedule rate on such supply, charge late payment interest and default delay penalties, and impose administrative fines up to EGP 100,000 per incident. Fraudulent claims, forged customs documentation, or connected-party circular export schemes trigger criminal tax evasion proceedings, enhanced financial penalties up to twice the tax evaded, closure of the taxpayer's file, and potential imprisonment of the responsible directors under Egyptian VAT and income tax law. ETA also applies a mandatory cross-border audit check through the customs, foreign exchange, and digital invoice databases before releasing any refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime for the Egyptian pound (EGP). Current-account transactions, including trade in goods, trade in services, and profit remittances, are convertible through the interbank foreign-exchange market using CBE documentary forms A and B. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections and repatriation of invested capital, are subject to licensing by the CBE and registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI); remittance is allowed after verification of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and compliance with the corporate law. Dividends and branch profits are creditable through an Egyptian bank after deducting due taxes and after the confirming bank receives the necessary auditor and tax authority documentation. The practical constraint is foreign-currency liquidity rather than absolute statutory prohibition, but non-resident shareholders may hold and remit profits in USD or EUR once the EGP component is properly converted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the EG_LLC carbon-fiber FPV drone sales must be credited to an Egyptian-licensed commercial bank within 90 days from the date of the Customs Export Declaration (cargo release). Under current CBE circulars, 100% of foreign-currency export proceeds are subject to immediate interbank conversion into EGP unless the exporter is approved to retain the proceeds in a CBE-sanctioned foreign-currency retention account for current operational expenditures, such as imported component supplies and freight. Historical rates of 50% mandatory surrender can be waived for exporters with a valid retention account in good standing; however, in all cases the documentary trail must match an export customs declaration and the corresponding foreign invoice.

Central bank supervision: The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting through the Foreign Exchange Administration and the Regulatory and Supervision Control Department, under Law No. 194 of 2020 governing the CBE and banking system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed by the CBE and therefore cannot be used as the primary treasury account for statutory export surrender, payment settlement, or capital escrow. They may operate as payment initiation service providers only when partnering with a licensed local payment service provider such as Fawry or PaySky, and only for low-value, non-import trade receipts. Legal constraint: unlicensed payment processing for an Egyptian resident company is a violation of the CBE's payment-processing rules and the AML/CFT Law No. 80 of 2002; any foreign EMI must enter into a white-label arrangement with a CBE-licensed bank and may not hold immutable Egyptian customer accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound equity routing: the shareholders' capital injection must be sent by SWIFT MT103 from a bank outside Egypt to the EG_LLC's capital escrow account at a CBE-licensed bank. The wire instruction must include (i) the Ecuadorian/Egyptian recipient bank account number (0-5 country code is not used for Egypt; use local IBAN and BIC), (ii) the Corporate Banking Department code, and (iii) the purpose code corresponding to 'Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Capital' under the CBE's balance-of-payments classification. A foreign-currency deposit must be reported to CBE via the bank's Core Banking Transaction Portal and will be evidenced by a Foreign Currency Entry Certificate. For EGP credit, the bank will convert the USD at the applicable interbank rate and file CBE form A in the name of the EG_LLC. To avoid delays, the sending remitter must provide a legalized board resolution of the EG_LLC and the capital ownership structure to the receiving bank before the wire date.Trade disbursements for imported carbon-fiber, brushless motors, electronic speed controllers, and flight controllers should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier invoices and through an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (L/C) under UCP600 for high-value or cash-against-document transactions. For each disbursement, the bank will require a supply contract, the commercial invoice, the import customs declaration (Warehousing and Inspection Number), the shipping documentation (airway bill or bill of lading), and the cargo insurance certificate. Payments to several foreign component suppliers should be aggregated in a single monthly spread via the treasury workstation to optimize liquidity and minimize SWIFT amendment charges. For the retail e-commerce receivables collected through PayPal and Stripe, settlement can be swept into the EG_LLC's operational bank account only after the foreign payment aggregator has been onboarded as a licensed payment service provider; all such proceeds must be accompanied by the corresponding payment reconciliation file to satisfy CBE disclosure obligations.FX Forward Contracts – deliverable settlement in EGP/USD, only allowed when the hedge is supported by an underlying trade payment obligation or receivables exposure and requires prior CBE approval in the bank's treasury system.FX Swaps – two-leg spot/forward transactions for EGP-US-Dollar or EUR/USD liquidity management, permitted only between the EG_LLC and its CBE-licensed account bank and not for speculative purposes.Cross-Currency Swaps – floating-rate fixed- or variable-to-variable EGP/USD swaps, available from the CBE-approved commercial counterparties and subject to the CBE's prudential derivative exposure limits.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – not permissible for onshore resident entities as a general rule; in exceptional cases, a CBE-licensed bank may route an offshore NDF for a resident corporate only after receiving specific CBE transaction-level authorization and only for genuine import/cap-flow hedging. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividend distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to its shareholders. The 10% applies to dividends declared on profits accumulated after the effective date of the Egyptian Income Tax Law and may be reduced under an applicable double tax agreement. Branch profit remittances are not separately subject to branch remittance tax where the PE is already taxed on attributable profits, but any repatriation through interest, royalties, or management fees is subject to source withholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The final annual tax return must be filed and the final CIT paid within four months after the close of the fiscal year. For a company on a calendar-year basis, the deadline is 30 April of the following year. Quarterly advance payments fall due on the last day of the month following the end of each provisional quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5 million aggregate value of related-party transactions in a single tax period. The mandatory Local File and Master File thresholds are triggered when the aggregate controlled transactions exceed EGP 5 million. Multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above EGP 5.5 billion are additionally required to file Country-by-Counther reporting via the ETA.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted a Gulf-style standalone Economic Substance Regulation, but the ETA applies equivalent substance principles through the taxation of residents by place of effective management, the arm's-length attribution rules for PEs, the general anti-avoidance rule under Law No. 91 of 2005, and Egypt's MLI obligation to deny treaty benefits when a transaction lacks bona-fide economic substance. The racing drone LLC claiming Free Zone or investment incentives must prove real presence, local employees, warehousing, substantive board decisions, manufacturing or logistical activity, and commercial risk in Egypt; a shell entity with a registered address alone cannot sustain a reduced tax rate or treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Imposition of additional tax of up to 100% of the evaded tax differential plus a 2% monthly late-payment surcharge; criminal fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 30,000; and imprisonment for a period between three months and five years under Egypt's Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of a false return may be held jointly and severally liable with the LLC for the resulting tax deficiency. Egyptian tax enforcement allows civil attachment against personal assets, a travel ban against directors where tax collection is jeopardized, and imprisonment where fraud is proven.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Intercompany Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ETA may redetermine the LLC's taxable income under the CUP, TNMM, or profit-split method, assess the additional 22.5% CIT on the upward adjustment, and impose the monthly 2% delay surcharge. Non-production of TP documentation triggers separate statutory fines and evidence-adverse inferences, with penalties assessed at up to 30% of the difference between the declared and adjusted price in comparable Egyptian court practice.

Director personal liability: A director who approved a knowingly non-arm's-length contract may be required to reimburse the company for the extra taxes, penalties, and interest caused by that decision; personal liability is pierced when the director treated corporate assets or intercompany channels as a device to strip Egyptian taxable profit.
Shell Company and Treaty/Free-Zone Abuse

Statutory sanction: The ETA denies the claimed tax exemption or treaty benefit, recaptures the aggregate free-zone/incentive relief, taxes the full profit at the 22.5% CIT rate, and imposes late surcharges. Shell-company registrations may be struck from the commercial register, and the General Authority for Investment may impose administrative fines under Law No. 72 of 2017 and Companies Law No. 159 of 2020.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the LLC operates as an alter ego of the foreign shareholder/director. The directors become personally liable for civil restitution of the avoided tax, and the enforcement authorities may impose travel bans, freeze bank accounts, and apply asset-attachment orders.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Egypt's AML Law include imprisonment for not less than three years and a fine between EGP 100,000 and EGP 1,000,000 for money-laundering predicate acts. The ETA may additionally treat unbanked or improperly documented receipts as concealed revenue and assess tax plus penalties; the commercial registry may cancel the LLC's registration.

Director personal liability: Every director who signed bank account applications or payment-platform registration consents is personally accountable for truthful beneficial ownership disclosure. Knowingly allowing the LLC to be used as a laundering vehicle leads to criminal conviction, personal asset forfeiture, travel ban, and imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Avoidance and Falsified Tax Residency Certificates

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC is held liable for the full amount of withholding tax that was required to be deducted, plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge for each overdue month. Falsification of TRCs or treaty relief documents can trigger criminal penalties for tax evasion, including fines and imprisonment, and the ETA will disregard the reduced treaty rate and impose the full statutory withholding obligation.

Director personal liability: The managing director and the authorized signatories are jointly and severally liable with the LLC as withholding agents. If distributions were made without WHT while the director knew the shareholders were non-residents, the tax authority may execute against personal assets, prohibit international travel, and bar the director from serving on any Egyptian board.
