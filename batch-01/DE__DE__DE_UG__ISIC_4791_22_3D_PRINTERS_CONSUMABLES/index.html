Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification and EU-wide procedures; local Finanzamt (tax office) for income and trade tax registration and administration. Online portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online-Portal (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)), the legal representative must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the responsible local Finanzamt via ELSTER immediately after notarial incorporation and commercial register filing. The Finanzamt assigns both a Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT purposes. Separately, the UG must apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr (EU VAT ID) via form or ELSTER/BZSt portal. Foreign entities without German residence may need to appoint a German fiscal representative or tax advisor to complete registration, file tax returns, and obtain VAT ID. All registrations must be completed before commencing taxable activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A UG is a German limited-liability company and is treated as a separate taxpayer subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15%, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the municipal rate (typically 7.0%–17.5% applied to trade income, total effective corporation tax load approx. 30%). Profits are taxed at entity level regardless of distribution; no pass-through taxation is available. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A UG is a German limited-liability company and is treated as a separate taxpayer subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15%, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the municipal rate (typically 7.0%–17.5% applied to trade income, total effective corporation tax load approx. 30%). Profits are taxed at entity level regardless of distribution; no pass-through taxation is available. No German branch or permanent establishment is deemed formed solely by online sales unless a fixed place of business or dependent agent exists in Germany.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box election for entity classification. The UG is predetermined as taxable on entity level under the German Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG). However, certain elections affect tax grouping: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under § 14 KStG / § 2 UStG, allowing a German parent to consolidate a 75%-owned UG for income and VAT purposes; (ii) specific uniform balance-sheet (Einheitsbilanz) election under § 60a EStG for financial statement uniformity; (iii) small-enterprise (Kleingewerbetreibender) threshold elections for trade tax/ VAT simplification where applicable. No hybrid mismatch election exists domestically beyond the statutory anti-hybrid rules of § 4k EStG. For cross-border hybrid mismatches, Germany applies the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive rules, which override any elective classification under foreign law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic small businesses (Kleinunternehmer): €22,000 annual turnover and €50,000 prior-year threshold eligibility; for foreign entrepreneurs supplying goods or services in Germany, there is no registration threshold — registration is mandatory from the first transaction that establishes a German tax nexus (e.g., taxable distance sale or electronic supply). The OSS (One-Stop-Shop) non-EU scheme may apply for EU-wide distance sales above a €10,000 pan-EU threshold, but a German UG with German establishment is normally VAT-registered in Germany regardless.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies under § 6 UStG to: (1) exports of goods delivered to a third-country destination (i.e., outside EU), provided the goods are actually exported by the seller or by the foreign purchaser's carrier and the recipient is a third-country entrepreneur/end consumer; (2) intra-EU supplies of goods to business customers in another EU Member State (VAT ID validated) are not zero-rated but exempt subject to reverse charge; (3) certain cross-border services registered for VAT may be exempt with input tax recovery. For online retail of 3D printers and consumables, zero-rating requires proof of physical export outside the EU, transfer of power of disposal to the purchaser, and evidence that the goods left Germany within the statutory period (generally no later than the export date for documentary proof, with a maximum tolerance of 3 months for shipment/transport). The seller must record the nature, quantity, and value of exported goods and retain all export evidence in the accounting records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted and valid (electronic ECS / summary declaration)
  • International commercial invoice with export statement and incoterms
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Consignment Note / CMR (carrier-issued proof of transport)
  • Proof of arrival / import clearance in the third country (e.g., customs stamp, port load confirmation, delivery receipt by foreign carrier)
  • Bank transfer or inward remittance certificate evidencing final consideration received from foreign buyer
  • Contract or purchase order showing destination outside the EU and the purchasing party's identity
  • If sold via marketplace: fulfillment/warehouse export records from logistics provider (DHL, UPS, DAP carrier)

Germany mandates B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) in stages from 1 January 2025, requiring receipt-ability of structured electronic invoices for resident taxable persons. From 1 January 2027, all resident B2B taxable persons must issue, send, and transmit e-invoices that comply with the EN 16931 standard (e.g., XRechnung, ZUGFeRD). No real-time pre-clearance or fiscal stamping regime (such as Italian SDI) applies in Germany; electronic invoices must be transmitted to the recipient, but there is no national digital clearance portal for standard VAT invoices. However, for B2C distance sales, e-commerce operators must file EU OSS VAT returns electronically and pay VAT monthly/quarterly via the BZSt portal, without requiring per-invoice clearance.

The German tax administration (Finanzamt) and customs authorities may retrospectively verify export substantiation. If the exporter cannot prove actual export, the zero-rating is denied and VAT at 19% is assessed on the sales value, plus interest (0.5% per month or 6% per annum under § 233a AO). Further consequences include: late-filing surcharges (Verspätungszuschlag) of up to 10% of assessed VAT, administrative fines for incorrect tax returns under §§ 154–155 AO (up to €25,000 per violation), and criminal penalties for VAT fraud under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to 10 years for serious cases). The right to input VAT deduction on related purchases may also be clawed back if the underlying supply is reclassified as taxable. Export refund claims (e.g., through BZSt for non-German exporters) are similarly subject to a 6-month filing deadline and documentary audit, with fraudulent claims triggering state penalty proceedings.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility for the euro and all freely convertible currencies. EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63–65 TFEU) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act guarantee unrestricted capital movements, profit repatriation and dividend distribution to EU and third-country investors, subject only to statistical reporting and general anti-money-laundering/banking obligations. No exchange control approval, prior licence or surrender requirement applies to current account transactions or to profit remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion or repatriation obligation exists for export proceeds. Exporters may freely hold, convert or dispose of foreign currency receipts. A monthly/quarterly report to the Deutsche Bundesbank is required for outstanding receivables from non-residents exceeding EUR 5 million, and a threshold-based payment report must be filed electronically within five business days for any payment to or from a foreign country exceeding EUR 50,000 (AWV reporting).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) in cooperation with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry of Finance; the Bundesbank administers foreign trade and payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. An EU-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution operating under PSD2 passporting can provide ancillary IBANs and FX services and is legally viable for transaction settlement. However, offshore EMIs without a European passport are not permitted to acquire a German IBAN or act as a principal clearing institution for VAT/German bookkeeping purposes; they can only serve as auxiliary payment channels, and the UG must still maintain a tätiger (active) bank account with a German/European credit institution for statutory capital compliance, tax collection accounts and court/registry payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injections must be routed through a German resident bank via SEPA (EUR) or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR currencies. The wire instruction must include the Debtor Bank's BIC/IBAN of the UG and a purpose code under the German AWV statistics: for direct investment capital contribution use AWV purpose code V3 for participation/equity capital (or E1 for registered capital) depending on the transfer structure, together with the code 'KA' (Kapitaleinlage) in some bank formats. The transaction value must be reported by the domestic recipient bank to Deutsche Bundesbank; no prior approval is required. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT (0.825% effective) plus trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 340-450%), producing a combined effective burden of approximately 30-33% on retained profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% total (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge imposed upon the 25% withholding) on dividend distributions by the UG; reduced to 0% for Germany-resident corporate shareholders with a qualifying shareholding of at least 10%; for EU/EEA corporate shareholders applying the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 90/435/EEC, as amended) the rate is 0% when the shareholder holds 10% for at least 12 months and provides the BZSt relief certificate; non-EU corporate shareholders may invoke DTA relief at reduced treaty rates of 5-15%
