Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG must be formed with subscribed share capital of at least EUR 1 (sec. 5a(1) GmbHG). The full amount of the subscribed capital must be paid up in cash or in kind before registration and must be at the free disposal of the managing director (secs. 5a(2), 7(2) and 8(2) GmbHG). Cash is deposited into a business bank account opened in the company's name; no public escrow account is required, but a bank confirmation or a managing director's declaration of unrestricted disposal must accompany the registration application. In addition, sec. 5a(3) GmbHG requires retention of one quarter of annual net profits in a statutory reserve until the company's capital reaches EUR 25,000, at which point it is eligible to be converted into a full GmbH.

Funding & unlock path: The business premises must satisfy general building safety and structural load requirements under the Berlin building code; for warehouse and office use, no separate public-occupancy permit is required unless consumer-facing or event use changes the building classification. A fire safety certificate or emergency plan is required where the total usable floor area exceeds 2,000 m² or where goods are stored in public accessible areas. Occupational safety obligations under Arbeitsstättenverordnung (ArbStättV) and the Betriebssicherheitsverordnung apply, including first-aid provisions, fire extinguishers, lighting, emergency exits and workplace inspection; the managing director must register employees (after first hiring) with the commercial statutory accident insurance institution (Berufsgenossenschaft Handel und Warenlogistik – BGHW).

Paid-in capital rule: The UG must be formed with subscribed share capital of at least EUR 1 (sec. 5a(1) GmbHG). The full amount of the subscribed capital must be paid up in cash or in kind before registration and must be at the free disposal of the managing director (secs. 5a(2), 7(2) and 8(2) GmbHG). Cash is deposited into a business bank account opened in the company's name; no public escrow account is required, but a bank confirmation or a managing director's declaration of unrestricted disposal must accompany the registration application. In addition, sec. 5a(3) GmbHG requires retention of one quarter of annual net profits in a statutory reserve until the company's capital reaches EUR 25,000, at which point it is eligible to be converted into a full GmbH.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the Eurosystem framework. No restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders, subject to standard corporate law and tax compliance. The Euro is a freely convertible currency; no exchange control approval is required for cross-border payments. However, reporting obligations under AWV apply for certain transactions exceeding €12,500 (e.g., cross-border payments, holdings, and receivables) to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency earnings in domestic or foreign accounts. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation; however, all cross-border payments must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance of payments statistics if they exceed €12,500, but this is a reporting requirement, not a surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; FX administration is governed by the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German fintech EMIs (e.g., N26, Penta, Qonto, or solarisBank-based platforms) offer rapid onboarding and multi-currency IBANs, but they may impose restrictions on high-volume cross-border trade settlements and often lack full SWIFT correspondent banking capabilities. Legal constraints: EMIs are licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and must comply with AML/KYC regulations; they are not full banks and may not provide credit facilities or complex FX hedging. For a UG engaged in e-commerce, a hybrid approach (tier-1 bank for core treasury and EMI for operational agility) is recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 for EUR transfers. For capital injection from non-EU shareholders, the payment must be referenced as 'Capital Contribution' with purpose code 'P01' (Investment in equity) under the AWV reporting. The UG must open a capital escrow account (if required) at a German bank to receive the initial capital before registration. For ongoing capital injections, use standard SEPA or SWIFT transfers with proper reference to the UG's name and purpose. Verification: Provide the bank with the notarized articles of association, commercial register excerpt, and shareholder resolution. For amounts above €12,500, the bank will report to the Bundesbank automatically.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For supplier payments, use SWIFT MT103 (single payment) or MT202 (bank-to-bank) for high-value transactions. For documentary collections, use SWIFT MT400/MT700 for letters of credit (LC). For recurring payments to EU suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is efficient. For non-EU suppliers, SWIFT is the standard. Ensure all payments include proper purpose codes (e.g., 'P02' for trade in goods) and comply with AWV reporting thresholds. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) – combined effective rate approx. 30-33% depending on municipality

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed EUR 1,000,000 per year. Master File: required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but requires genuine economic substance for treaty benefits and to avoid anti-abuse rules (e.g., Section 42 AO, Section 7 AStG). A UG must have real business activity, premises, and personnel to justify tax residency and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine (Section 370 AO); additional penalty of up to 10% of evaded tax (Section 371 AO) if voluntary disclosure not made

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to file returns or pay taxes (Section 34 AO); criminal liability extends to managing directors; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, retroactive taxation, penalties up to EUR 25,000 (Section 42 AO); potential criminal charges for tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; travel bans and extradition possible in severe cases
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 per violation (Section 56 GwG); criminal penalties for money laundering (Section 261 StGB) up to 10 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML compliance; criminal liability for negligence; travel bans and asset freezes
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of income, additional tax, interest on underpayment (0.5% per month), penalty up to 5% of adjustment (Section 162 AO); documentation penalties up to EUR 1,000,000

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for additional taxes if they knowingly approved non-arm's length pricing; criminal charges for tax evasion if intentional
