Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG formation requires a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00 pursuant to § 5a(1) GmbHG. Contributions must be in cash only and fully paid before filing for registration; the funds must be credited to a German business bank account and remain at the free disposal of the managing director (§ 8(2) GmbHG). Until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, 25% of annual net profit must be allocated to a statutory reserve. Federal law does not mandate a formal escrow account, though an account designated as 'treuhänderisch gebunden' may be used to evidence availability.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection (Brandverhütungsschau) by the municipal fire authority; structural load certification of racking and cold-store systems; workplace safety clearance under the German Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV); DGUV occupational health screening for cold-storage personnel; emergency exit, fire alarm system and explosion-protection approval where applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has no general foreign-exchange controls or restrictions on conversion or cross-border transfer of euros. Capital and current-account payments are generally freely convertible and transferable, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, tax requirements, and statistical reporting. Dividends and other profit distributions by a UG are subject to German corporate-law distribution rules, including preservation of registered share capital, applicable UG statutory-reserve requirements, and a valid shareholder resolution. German withholding tax may apply to dividends, generally at 25% plus solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty relief, EU rules, and applicable procedural requirements. Banks may require evidence of distributable profits, tax residence, beneficial ownership, and withholding-tax treatment before remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no general statutory deadline for repatriating export proceeds and no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for export receipts. The UG may retain receipts in foreign-currency accounts or convert them voluntarily, subject to bank terms, sanctions, and accounting requirements. Separate AWV reporting obligations may apply to cross-border payments; the general reporting threshold is more than EUR 50,000 per payment, subject to statutory exemptions and transaction-specific rules. Payments for imports and exports of goods are generally exempt from this payments-statistics reporting obligation. The company must confirm the applicable classification, threshold, and any reporting duty with the Bundesbank; AWV reports are statistical and do not constitute exchange-control approval.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers German foreign-trade and payments statistics reporting under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV). The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. BaFin supervises banks and other regulated financial institutions; it is not Germany's foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR collections and payments where the provider accepts German UG entities and the craft-beer wholesale/export profile. An electronic money institution is not a bank unless separately licensed as one; safeguarding of payment funds is not equivalent to deposit protection. Verify the provider's EEA authorization, passporting status, safeguarding arrangements, permitted countries, transfer limits, and support for commercial alcohol transactions. Offshore providers without an appropriate EEA authorization or lawful passporting basis should not be used for regulated payment services. Maintain a German bank relationship for share-capital evidence, tax payments, cash-intensive or higher-value activity, and operational resilience.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Open a German business account in the UG's name and coordinate account opening with the notary and bank before formation. Transfer subscribed share capital from the shareholder's own verified account, preferably by SEPA credit transfer for euro payments or SWIFT for non-euro or non-SEPA payments. Use a truthful reference such as 'Share capital contribution – [UG name]'; do not use an invented regulatory purpose code. Retain the subscription documents, shareholder resolution, payer statement, bank credit advice, and account statement showing cleared funds for the notary and commercial-register filing. Identify third-party funding as such and document its legal basis before transfer. After registration, route further funding as a properly documented capital contribution or shareholder loan, with corporate approvals and tax/accounting treatment assessed in advance. Provide the bank with the commercial purpose, beneficial-owner information, source of funds, and underlying documents when requested. Assess AWV statistical reporting for cross-border payments separately; the reporting threshold does not constitute an approval requirement.Pay domestic and eligible EEA euro suppliers through SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Direct Debit, using mandate controls and invoice-level reconciliation for direct debits. Settle documented goods purchases against contracts, purchase orders, invoices, and transport or delivery evidence. Commercial documentary letters of credit may be used for material cross-border shipments where the bank supports the product; agree documentary terms, Incoterms, inspection requirements, presentation periods, and fees with the counterparty and issuing/advising banks before shipment. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Körperschaftsteuer 15%, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825 percentage points), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at Hebesatz multipliers; aggregate headline approximately 30%–33% depending on municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, aggregating to 26.375%. EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the UG's share capital may claim full exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; German double tax treaties typically reduce the rate to 5% for substantial shareholdings and 15% for portfolio dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by 31 July following the end of the calendar year in which the fiscal year ends; automatically extended to 28 February of the second following year when prepared by a professional tax advisor under Section 109 AO.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same administrative timetable as the corporate income tax return: 31 July following the tax year; extended to 28 February for professionally prepared returns.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

KSt 1 A, line 1 – Steuernummer / Identification Enter the tax office-assigned corporate tax number (Steuernummer) and the federal tax ID (Steuer-ID) of the UG. Non-resident managing shareholders must provide their foreign tax identification number and, if required, a German Steuer-ID. This field is mandatory; omission triggers automatic rejection. KSt 1 A, line 2 – Registered office (Sitz) State the legal seat as recorded in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The registered seat must be a verifiable physical German address; a foreign domicile or mail-forwarding address will invalidate German corporate tax residency. Late-filing surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag) generally EUR 25,000 cap and 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month; electronic filing default penalty up to EUR 2,500; late-payment interest at 0.5% per month (6% per annum).
KSt 1 A, line 3 – Place of effective management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) Identify the location where daily management decisions are taken, including cold-chain logistics, inventory control, and distributor contracting. If key management functions are performed from outside Germany, fully document the decision-making process; otherwise the tax office may treat the UG as a shell. Late-filing surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag) generally EUR 25,000 cap and 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month; electronic filing default penalty up to EUR 2,500; late-payment interest at 0.5% per month (6% per annum).
KSt 1 A, line 4 – Taxable corporate income (Einkommen) Report taxable income after book-tax adjustments, including non-deductible expenses, loss carryforwards, and recognized transfer pricing corrections. Reconcile German GAAP-based financial accounts with group accounting frameworks; attach a reconciliation note for foreign currency translation and intercompany adjustments. Late-filing surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag) generally EUR 25,000 cap and 0.25% of assessed tax per commenced month; electronic filing default penalty up to EUR 2,500; late-payment interest at 0.5% per month (6% per annum).
GewSt 1 A, line 5 – Gewerbeertrag (trade earnings) Declare the taxable trade income based on the corporate taxable income after addbacks, including financing expenses, royalties, and rental costs. Non-resident related-party financiers or IP holders must present withholding tax certificates; exemption depends on treaty and EU directive compliance. Identical late-filing and interest provisions as the corporate income tax return; underpaid trade tax prepayments trigger monthly 0.5% interest and, in audit situations, an additional penalty of 5% of the tax understatement.
GewSt 1 A, line 6 – Gewerbeverlust (trade loss carryforward) Show the amount of trade-tax loss carryforward utilized against current trade income. Loss recognition is denied if the business does not have an actual commercial establishment in Germany; shell structures cannot claim loss offsets. Identical late-filing and interest provisions as the corporate income tax return; underpaid trade tax prepayments trigger monthly 0.5% interest and, in audit situations, an additional penalty of 5% of the tax understatement.
GewSt 1 A, line 7 – Municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) Enter the applicable trade tax multiplier of the municipality where the UG's permanent establishment is located. Where distribution activities create multiple permanent establishments, provide a documented apportionment of trade income across those municipalities. Identical late-filing and interest provisions as the corporate income tax return; underpaid trade tax prepayments trigger monthly 0.5% interest and, in audit situations, an additional penalty of 5% of the tax understatement.
GewSt 1 A, line 8 – Trade tax assessment amount (Messbetrag) Net trade tax base, calculated as trade earnings multiplied by the statutory assessment rate of 3.5%. Confirm that the UG actually carries on commercial distribution activity; a dormant or asset-holding shell will not be subject to trade tax and cannot claim Messbetrag treatment. Identical late-filing and interest provisions as the corporate income tax return; underpaid trade tax prepayments trigger monthly 0.5% interest and, in audit situations, an additional penalty of 5% of the tax understatement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared for controlled cross-border transactions above EUR 100,000 per intangible or royalty-bearing arrangement and above EUR 500,000 for ordinary goods or service transactions; Master File required when consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (CbCR threshold). These documents must be produced upon tax audit request under Section 90 AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply separate Economic Substance Regulations as in zero-tax jurisdictions; however, the place of effective management principle (Sections 10, 11 AO) and Section 42 AO (abuse of legal transactions) impose a strict economic substance requirement. For a craft-beer distribution UG, verifiable substance includes a German registered office, physical premises, qualified staff, management meetings held in Germany, local bank accounts, and complete accounting records. A passive letterbox UG without genuine business activity risks being disregarded for tax purposes and losing all treaty and loss-offset benefits.
