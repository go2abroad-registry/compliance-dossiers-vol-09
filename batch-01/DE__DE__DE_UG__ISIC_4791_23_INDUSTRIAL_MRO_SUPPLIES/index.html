Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to the UG (haftungsbeschränkt): the stated capital may be fixed at EUR 1 or more in the articles; before registration, at least one quarter (25%) of the nominal amount of each cash share must be paid into the company's business bank account at the free disposal of the managing director (Section 7(2) GmbHG); contributions in kind are permitted subject to a notarial valuation report; no escrow or bank-blocking account is legally required; the company must retain 25% of annual net profit in a statutory reserve under Section 5a(3) GmbHG until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For VAT, export zero-rating and EU VAT identification: Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office, portal www.bzst.de. For corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessments: locally competent Finanzamt (German tax office) through the ELSTER electronic filing portal, portal www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

A resident German UG obtains its Steuernummer by filing the statutory Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung with the local Finanzamt after incorporation. Its EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is obtained by separate application to the BZSt under §27a UStG. A non-resident foreign entity supplying German-taxable goods or services must apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number, registering from the first taxable transaction where no general de-minimis threshold applies; if the foreign entity has a permanent establishment or otherwise becomes subject to German income/corporate/trade tax, it must also submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung to the competent Finanzamt and may be required to appoint a tax representative or authorised recipient in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The UG is automatically a separate corporate taxpayer subject to Körperschaftsteuer, Solidaritätszuschlag and Gewerbesteuer. Relevant statutory consolidation elections are available through Organschaft (corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT fiscal unity) where a controlling German parent has financial, economic and organisational integration over the UG. A small-business exemption under §19 UStG is available only below the statutory turnover thresholds and is unsuitable for a B2B MRO supplier. Hybrid mismatch treatment is not elective: §§4k, 4m EStG and related rules automatically apply the EU/ATAD anti-hybrid limitations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general economic-turnover registration threshold for a resident UG or for foreign suppliers whose supplies are taxable in Germany; VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply. The only statutory exemption threshold is the small-business regime under §19 UStG, which applies if total turnover in the previous calendar year did not exceed EUR 25,000 and is not expected to exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year; this exemption is not available or advisable for a B2B industrial MRO supplier because it prohibits input VAT deduction. For cross-border EU B2C distance sales, an OSS registration may be used once pan-EU distance sales exceed EUR 10,000, but B2B MRO supplies remain outside that special OSS threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of industrial MRO goods is zero-rated as an export supply under §4 No.1 lit.a UStG in conjunction with §6 UStG if the goods are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier, the customer, or a third-party carrier from Germany to a destination outside the European Union, and the supplier retains conclusive documentary proof that the goods left EU customs territory without German or EU-use before export. For supplies to customers in other EU member states, zero-rating as an intra-Community supply under §4 No.1 lit.b and §6a UStG requires a valid EU VAT identification number of the acquirer, transport to another member state, and the appropriate recapitulative statement. Services supplied to non-EU B2B customers are generally outside German VAT where the place of supply is in the customer's jurisdiction under §3a UStG, provided the conditions for B2B services are met.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) accepted electronically through the German ATLAS customs system, together with the resulting export accompanying document or electronic export declaration attestation
  • Bill of Lading or alternative transport document evidencing dispatch from Germany to the destination country
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank payment confirmation, or equivalent financial evidence showing receipt of the export consideration where the proof is required or contractual payment terms demand it
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and international trade documents specifying Incoterms, export classification and the non-EU destination
  • Proof of the non-EU acquirer's identity and export-relevant customs status, including any destination-country import declaration or arrival confirmation where available
  • Written export proof under §§10 and 13 UStDV, including export certificate or confirmation of actual physical exit from EU customs territory

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API stamping, or continuous transaction control regime. Since 1 January 2025, every German-resident business must be able to receive structured electronic invoices in an EN 16931-compliant format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X 2.3 or equivalent). Mandatory issuance for domestic B2B invoices applies from 1 January 2027 for suppliers whose prior-year turnover exceeded EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all resident suppliers. B2G invoices must be exchanged through the Peppol-network-compatible infrastructure. E-invoice data are not transmitted to the BZSt or Finanzamt as a clearance condition.

The Finanzamt scrutinises export zero-rating evidence continuously, including through targeted export VAT audits, and will retrospectively assess 19% VAT on the supply plus interest under §233a AO if the statutory export proof is missing, incomplete, or contradictory. Where an invoice erroneously shows VAT, the issuer becomes liable for the tax shown pursuant to §14c UStG. Fraudulent or grossly negligent export zero-rating claims give rise to criminal and administrative penalties under §§370, 378 AO, with imprisonment of up to 10 years for tax evasion, extended 10-year assessment limitation periods, retrospective VAT and trade-tax corrections, and joint liability for related entities where group structures are used to divert proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full convertibility jurisdiction for both current and capital account transactions within the euro area and vis-à-vis third countries, subject to EU capital movement liberalization under Articles 63–66 TFEU. Profit remittances by a German UG are unrestricted, provided that (i) annual financial statements are properly prepared and filed, (ii) any applicable corporate income tax and trade tax liabilities are settled, and (iii) the distribution does not violate balance-sheet-based capital maintenance rules under the German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG) as applied to an UG. No general exchange control approval is required for dividend repatriation, debt servicing, or liquidation proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Germany. Export proceeds are freely retainable and payable in any currency; however, reporting obligations under the AWV apply for payments received from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction or group of connected transactions, which must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank electronically without being a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (in coordination with the European Central Bank and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority, BaFin, for banking conduct matters); foreign exchange policy is governed under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-High feasibility for the UG entity. German-registered electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or passported under PSD2 can provide IBAN-based account infrastructure, multi-currency wallets, and cross-border payment gateways, and are suitable for low-risk B2B MRO e-commerce flows. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK or non-EU) are not viable as primary treasury accounts due to lack of German/EU passporting for resident entities, supervisory reach of BaFin, and ordinary-account benefits of a full German banking license. The key constraint is that EMIs do not provide credit facilities or foreign exchange derivatives without a credit institution license, so a tier-1 commercial bank account remains advisable for escrow and high-value SWIFT settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder loans or equity contributions to the DE_UG must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the German bank account using the appropriate purpose code — for capital contributions, use the German AWV statistical reporting code for capital participation (e.g., 'Kapitalbeteiligungen, Bildung von Reserven' under K1/K2 categories) or the ECB Balance of Payments reason code 'RE' for capital transfers. The wire must include the beneficiary's IBAN/BIC, full legal name, and the declared purpose 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' or 'Stammeinlage' as applicable. Verification includes the deposit of funds on a TARGET2 settlement basis, documentary proof of the underlying equity resolution or loan agreement (Gesellschafterbeschluss), and a certificate from the tax advisor that no restrictive measures apply. The Deutsche Bundesbank reporting obligation is triggered at EUR 12,500 or above; the bank or corporate treasury must submit the report within seven calendar days of the transaction.Trade settlements to suppliers and service providers are executed through regular European SEPA Instant/SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated transactions within EU/EEA, and through SWIFT MT103 on remote access systems (e.g. SWIFTNet, host-to-host banking portal) for non-euro or third-country payments. For high-value industrial MRO procurement, a documentary clean against acceptance model or a confirmed irrevocable Letter of Credit (UCP 600) is permissible; the issuing German bank verifies the transport documents and commercial invoices, with payment instruction via SWIFT MT700 series and settlement through correspondent banks. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK or non-EU) are not viable as primary treasury accounts due to lack of German/EU passporting for resident entities, supervisory reach of BaFin, and ordinary-account benefits of a full German banking license. The key constraint is that EMIs do not provide credit facilities or foreign exchange derivatives without a credit institution license, so a tier-1 commercial bank account remains advisable for escrow and high-value SWIFT settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax, plus a solidarity surcharge equal to 5.5% of the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax applies separately, generally producing a combined corporate burden of approximately 22.83% to 32.025%, depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital-income withholding tax, plus a solidarity surcharge equal to 5.5% of the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic deduction of 26.375% before any treaty or statutory exemption. For qualifying EU parent companies, the domestic participation exemption may apply where the parent satisfies the minimum participation, minimum holding-period and anti-abuse requirements. German anti-treaty-shopping rules must be considered.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax and trade tax returns are generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year under section 149 of the German Fiscal Code. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the statutory extended deadline is generally fourteen months after fiscal year-end, subject to statutory exceptions, administrative extensions and audit-related requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the annual value of cross-border related-party goods transactions exceeds EUR 6,000,000 or the annual value of cross-border related-party services and other transactions exceeds EUR 600,000. A Local File is generally required for qualifying transactions. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000, subject to the applicable German documentation rules. Documentation may also be required for extraordinary transactions regardless of ordinary thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain low-tax jurisdictions. Economic substance is nevertheless material under the German anti-abuse rule in section 42 of the Fiscal Code, treaty anti-abuse provisions, German withholding-tax relief rules, place-of-effective-management principles, permanent-establishment rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, interest limitation rules and general transfer-pricing requirements. A German UG should maintain real decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity under documented control, commercial premises or a defensible operating model, separate accounting, actual functions and risks, arm's-length contracts and evidence that distributions, royalties, services and financing reflect genuine transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax declarations

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest and penalties remain recoverable; voluntary disclosure relief is highly formalised and unavailable or restricted after discovery or investigation milestones.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for culpable breach of tax duties under section 69 of the Fiscal Code, civil claims, confiscation and director-disqualification consequences. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be imposed in criminal proceedings where legally justified; imprisonment is possible following conviction.
VAT fraud and carousel transactions

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion sanctions under section 370 of the Fiscal Code, repayment of improperly claimed VAT, interest, civil recovery and possible confiscation. Administrative VAT penalties may also apply for incorrect or late filings.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible employees may incur criminal liability and personal civil or tax liability where they knowingly participate or negligently fail to implement reasonable controls. Banks and tax authorities may freeze or scrutinise accounts during investigations.
Shell company, nominee ownership and beneficial-owner concealment

Statutory sanction: A shell structure is not unlawful solely because it is tax-efficient, but abusive arrangements may be denied under section 42 of the Fiscal Code and anti-treaty-shopping rules. False beneficial-owner information, tax evasion, money laundering or forged records may trigger criminal sanctions, tax reassessment, withholding-tax recovery and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability for intentional participation, personal tax liability for improper withholding or reporting, civil damages and disqualification. Travel restrictions or imprisonment require a separate statutory and procedural basis and are not automatic consequences of lacking substance.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust profits, deny deductions, impose additional corporate income tax and trade tax, recover withholding tax, apply interest and impose documentation-related penalties. Failure to produce required transfer-pricing records may lead to an estimated adjustment and a penalty generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to 1,000,000, subject to statutory limits and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for culpable tax losses, inaccurate filings and improper withholding. Criminal exposure arises where the adjustment is supported by intentional concealment or false records rather than an ordinary good-faith pricing dispute.
Withholding-tax failure on dividends, royalties or taxable interest

Statutory sanction: The UG may become liable for the unwithheld amount, solidarity surcharge, interest, late-payment surcharges and potentially tax-evasion penalties. Incorrect withholding certificates and unsupported treaty relief may be treated as serious compliance failures.

Director personal liability: The managing director may incur personal liability under section 69 of the Fiscal Code for culpable failure to withhold or remit tax. Criminal liability may arise if the failure is intentional or supported by fraudulent documentation.
Money laundering and AML non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under section 261 of the Criminal Code may result in imprisonment or a fine, with aggravated consequences for commercial, organised or professional conduct. Obligations under the German Money Laundering Act may produce administrative fines, supervisory measures and business restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML-responsible persons may be criminally liable for knowing participation or deliberate concealment and may face personal fines, imprisonment, confiscation and professional consequences. A travel ban is possible only where ordered under applicable criminal-procedure rules.
Insolvency and wrongful trading

Statutory sanction: The managing director must generally file without undue delay and no later than three weeks after insolvency, or six weeks after over-indebtedness, subject to the statutory conditions and restructuring limitations. Delayed filing and prohibited payments can result in criminal sanctions, insolvency administrator claims, director liability and creditor recovery actions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for payments made after insolvency, unpaid taxes and social-security contributions, creditor losses and restitution claims. Criminal prosecution may result in fines or imprisonment; disqualification from management may follow.
Accounting, record-retention and corporate-law breach

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include administrative fines, forced filing, tax estimation, loss of evidentiary position, tax reassessment, publication consequences and criminal sanctions for falsification or deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: The managing director may incur civil liability to the UG, shareholders, creditors and tax authorities, particularly where records are inadequate or assets are misapplied. Personal liability is not eliminated by the UG's limited-liability status.
