Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); competent local municipal tax office (Finanzamt) for the corporate income tax number (Steuernummer), trade tax, and payroll tax administration; sole official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1. Form the UG by notarial deed, register in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), and open a German business bank account. 2. Within one month of formation, file the Tax Registration Questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt to obtain the Steuernummer for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT prepayments. 3. Submit a separate application through ELSTER to BZSt for the USt-IdNr., citing ISIC 2790 (electrical equipment manufacturing) and proving intended intra-EU supplies. 4. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the UG is a corporation with legal personality; its profits are taxed at the entity level at 15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, plus municipal trade tax of approximately 14% to 17% depending on the branch rate; retained and distributed profits are never taxed at shareholder level as pass-through income, but distributions to shareholders suffer further 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge at the source, subject to treaty relief).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists under German law; tax classification is mandatory by legal form. Available statutory elections include: (a) small-business election under §19 UStG if annual gross turnover is below the statutory ceiling (€25,000) — generally inadvisable for B2B exporters because input VAT recovery is forfeited; (b) VAT grouping election (Organschaft) under §§2(2) No. 2 and 2a UStG for a UG fully integrated into a German controlling parent; (c) trade tax loss carry-back/carry-forward elections under §10a GewStG; (d) elective depreciations under §§7g, 7b EStG for movable assets and construction; and (e) hybrid-mismatch adjustment elections under §4k EStG (anti-avoidance against digital/non-resident group financing).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€25,000 annual turnover ceiling for the small-business exemption under §19 UStG; mandatory registration is triggered when prior-year turnover exceeds €25,000 or current-year turnover is expected to exceed €100,000; additionally, the €10,000 annual intra-Community distance-sales threshold applies to cross-border B2C e-commerce in the EU; notwithstanding these thresholds, the UG manufacturing and exporting solar inverters must register immediately because export zero-rating and input VAT recovery are available only to a properly registered taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under §6 UStG for export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferung) of solar inverters to recipients in non-EU countries where: (a) the goods physically leave the EU customs territory within the statutory transport period (generally within 3 months of supply commencement); (b) the departure is evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration (ETRN/ABN release number); and (c) the supplier, or a qualifying forwarding agent on the supplier's behalf, initiates the transport. Intra-EU dispatches are taxed at 0% under §6a UStG when the acquirer is an entrepreneur holding a valid EU VAT ID (confirmed via VIES), a consecutive intra-Community declaration is filed, and the goods arrive in the destination Member State. Related offshore services (remote monitoring, firmware support) qualify for 0% under §4(1)(b) UStG for B2B recipients outside the EU when the effective benefit lies abroad.

  • Electronic customs export declaration (ETRN/ABN with AE-code reference of the customs office)
  • Commercial invoice in English/German reflecting commodity code 8504.40.30/8504.40.90 with Incoterms clause
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note endorsed to the non-EU buyer
  • Packing list and weight/volume certificates supporting the customs export summary declaration
  • Inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103 confirmation) evidencing export proceeds repatriation to the UG's German account
  • VIES/USt-IdNr. confirmation from the EU acquirer for intra-EU zero-rating
  • Persistent 10-year retained GoBD audit file with export proof (Belegnachweis) records

Germany has a phased B2B e-invoicing mandate under §14 UStG: from 1 January 2025, every taxable person must be able to receive structured e-invoices; suppliers with previous-year turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue EN 16931-compliant structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.3.1) from 1 January 2026; all other suppliers face the same issuance duty from 1 January 2027, and from 1 January 2028 the full mandatory-field and transmission requirements apply. There is currently no central real-time fiscal clearance authority, stamping regime, or transaction-level API in Germany; the regime relies on GoBD/GDPdU archiving (10-year retention) and periodic VAT returns via ELSTER. EU ViDA real-time digital reporting is anticipated from 2030 for intra-EU transactions. For exports, the invoice must bear the USt-IdNr. of both parties, and the export customs declaration number must be recorded.

The Finanzamt may retrospectively deny the 0% rate if the aggregate export evidence is incomplete, inconclusive, or contradicted by transport data, and will then assess 19% VAT on the full gross invoice; interest is charged at 6% per annum (§233a AO) from the date the false exemption was granted. In cases of fraudulently fabricated exports (Scheinausfuhr), criminal penalties under §370 AO include imprisonment of 5 to 10 years for especially serious tax evasion, fines up to €500,000, a 10-year extended assessment period, seizure of assets, and loss of import-export license privileges under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). Administrative fines and denial of future zero-rating customs simplifications (AEO) apply for bookkeeping deficiencies, and the Tax Office can issue secondary liability notices against the managing director (Geschäftsführer) for unpaid VAT under §§34, 69 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account, in line with IMF Article VIII status and EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-66 on free movement of capital. There are no exchange controls, no licensing of currency purchases, no surrender obligations, and no prohibition on holding cross-border balances in foreign currency. Profit remittance by a non-resident shareholder of a UG is unrestricted as a matter of foreign-exchange law, subject only to German dividend withholding tax (25% plus solidarity surcharge, or 5%-15% effective under an applicable double taxation treaty) and to the UG's corporate law solvency and cash-flow test. Inward direct investment is unrestricted by default; however, investments by non-EU persons in enterprises active in critical infrastructure or critical technologies, which may include power-electronics manufacturing serving grid operators, are screened under Sections 55-60 AWV and may require notification to BMWK.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender or repatriation requirement on export proceeds. An exporting UG may retain, invoice, and invest in any convertible currency (EUR, USD, RMB/CNH, GBP) and hold the proceeds in domestic or foreign bank accounts. The only binding obligation is statistical: under Section 67 AWV, every payment received from a non-resident exceeding EUR 50,000 (per transaction, with cumulative treatment in the reporting cycle) must be reported to the Bundesbank through the bank's electronic reporting portal (or the entity's direct online reporting file) by the 7th day of the month following the payment month, including the statutory purpose code (e.g., goods trade, capital contribution, or loan). The underlying export shipment also enters the Bundesbank's external trade statistics but is not subject to any conversion or deposit rule.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, Frankfurt am Main, operating under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). BaFin supervises licensed FX and banking institutions; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) administers capital-movement screening; the Generalzolldirektion enforces financial sanctions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High for operational treasury flows and multi-currency FX conversion; NOT viable for documentary credit or import/export LC settlement. BaFin-licensed or EU-passporting e-money institutions under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Pliant, Qonto, Bunq, Solaris SE) provide multi-currency IBANs, pooled/omnibus safeguarding accounts, and SWIFT-SEPA connectivity suitable for marketplace collections, supplier payments on open account, and expense disbursements. Legal and operational constraints: (i) EMIs cannot issue, confirm, or negotiate letters of credit or bank guarantees - that constitutes deposit-taking and trade finance activity reserved to credit institutions under Section 1(1) of the German Banking Act (KWG); (ii) EMIs cannot provide overdrafts or pay interest on client funds under Section 1(2) of the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); (iii) the omnibus account structure used by EMIs may fail German tax-office expectations for a dedicated business IBAN strictly in the UG's name for VAT-credit and payment-traceability purposes; (iv) Bundesbank AWV payment-statistics reporting remains the UG's legal duty even when an EMI executes the transfer, but most EMIs do not generate the required reporting files; (v) Asian counterparties frequently refuse LC and high-value trade settlement against an EMI-based beneficiary account. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 German bank for LC and high-value trade rails; a licensed EMI for low-ticket e-commerce collections and foreign-exchange optimization.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from a non-resident shareholder must follow a three-layer compliance protocol. (1) Corporate law: execute a notarized shareholders' resolution for a capital increase (Kapitalerhöhung) or a written shareholder loan agreement (Darlehensvertrag) with an arm's-length interest rate, maturity, and subordination statement; update the Commercial Register and the Transparenzregister before or immediately after funding. (2) Wire protocol: instruct an SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) into the UG's dedicated German account in EUR; if the injection is denominated in USD or another currency, the receiving German bank converts at the standard on-board rate; the Remittance Information field must state the exact statutory purpose code and the text 'Gesellschaftereinlage' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' plus the reference number of the shareholder resolution and loan agreement. (3) Statutory reporting: under Section 67 AWV, the UG must report any inbound payment from a non-resident of EUR 50,000 or more to the Bundesbank within the prescribed monthly reporting cycle; the report identifies the counterparty, the transaction amount, the type of payment (capital contribution, loan drawdown, or goods trade), and the purpose code. Banks will additionally require the updated Gesellschafterliste, proof of identity of the funding party, and a dividends-and-loans-accounting policy; the German interest barrier (Zinsschranke under Section 4h EStG) applies to shareholder loans. Loan disbursements, repayments, and dividend remittances must be documented to preserve both tax deductibility and treaty protection of the non-resident parent.Trade settlements run through the established documentary credit and wire rails. For supplier payments on open account: an SWIFT MT103 with field 70 (Remittance Information) containing the purchase order, commercial invoice, and LC reference (if any), and field 71A indicating charges allocation (OUR/SHA/BEN); the German bank will allocate the Bundesbank purpose code for goods imports. For purchase under a Commercial Letter of Credit: the UG's German bank issues an MT700 to the advising bank in the supplier country, subject to UCP 600 and ISBP 745, typically on a 100% cash-covered basis for a first-time UG client or against an approved working-capital line (Kontokorrentkredit); the required document set for solar inverters and subassemblies comprises commercial invoice, packing list, full set of clean-on-board bills of lading or airway bills, certificate of origin, quality/conformity certificates (CE, VDE/AR-N 4105, TÜV), insurance policy, and, where applicable, a supplier's declaration of origin for the EU tariff regime. Settlement occurs by sight payment, deferred payment, acceptance, or negotiation, and the corresponding reimbursements between banks use MT202 COV. For Chinese suppliers, RMB settlement is available through the Frankfurt offshore RMB clearing hub (SWIFT MEPS+), and Cash Against Documents (CAD) is used where LC costs are disproportionate. SEPA transfers serve EU suppliers; PayPal/Stripe micro-rails cover small e-commerce disbursements.Outright deliverable FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD, EUR/CNH, and EUR/GBP, transacted under the German master agreement (DRV Rahmenvertrag) or ISDA 2002, fully permitted for underlying trade and loan exposures without regulatory authorization.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted-currency counterparty exposures (e.g., EUR/CNY where offshore deliverable liquidity or PRC regulatory constraints exist), permitted under German law for genuine hedging and documented under an ISDA/DRV agreement with standard collateral provisions.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) matching USD-denominated supplier credit or parent loan liabilities against EUR cash-flow, permitted for corporates and subject to EMIR trade reporting and, above clearing thresholds, mandatory central clearing.FX options and zero-premium collars (risk reversals), OTC and exchange-traded on Eurex, permitted for the UG to cap downside and retain upside on USD and CNH receivables, with hedge accounting documented under Section 254 HGB or IFRS 9 where the qualifying hedging criteria are met.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High for operational treasury flows and multi-currency FX conversion; NOT viable for documentary credit or import/export LC settlement. BaFin-licensed or EU-passporting e-money institutions under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Pliant, Qonto, Bunq, Solaris SE) provide multi-currency IBANs, pooled/omnibus safeguarding accounts, and SWIFT-SEPA connectivity suitable for marketplace collections, supplier payments on open account, and expense disbursements. Legal and operational constraints: (i) EMIs cannot issue, confirm, or negotiate letters of credit or bank guarantees - that constitutes deposit-taking and trade finance activity reserved to credit institutions under Section 1(1) of the German Banking Act (KWG); (ii) EMIs cannot provide overdrafts or pay interest on client funds under Section 1(2) of the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); (iii) the omnibus account structure used by EMIs may fail German tax-office expectations for a dedicated business IBAN strictly in the UG's name for VAT-credit and payment-traceability purposes; (iv) Bundesbank AWV payment-statistics reporting remains the UG's legal duty even when an EMI executes the transfer, but most EMIs do not generate the required reporting files; (v) Asian counterparties frequently refuse LC and high-value trade settlement against an EMI-based beneficiary account. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 German bank for LC and high-value trade rails; a licensed EMI for low-ticket e-commerce collections and foreign-exchange optimization.
