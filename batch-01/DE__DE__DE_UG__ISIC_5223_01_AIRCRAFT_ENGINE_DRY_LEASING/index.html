Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general statutory company-name reservation procedure or universal 30-day reservation period. The applicant should obtain a preliminary name assessment from the competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (IHK), instruct a German notary to submit the formation documents, and conduct a current search through the Unternehmensregister, Handelsregister and relevant trademark databases. If the commercial-register court rejects or objects to the name, obtain the written order, identify the statutory deficiency, submit a corrected name or explanatory evidence through the notary, and observe the court's stated deadline. Judicial advice should be obtained before filing because the competent regional court and available remedy depend on the procedural order.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Republic of Germany – decentralized administration: the local Finanzamt (tax office at the UG's registered seat) is competent for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT assessments and the Steuernummer (tax number); the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Bonn, www.bzst.de) is the federal central tax office competent for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) and EU central files. Primary e-filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de), the official German electronic tax gateway for the registration questionnaire, VAT preliminary returns, corporate/trade tax returns and wage tax filings.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Formation and corporate registration: the UG is formed by notarial deed and registered in the Handelsregister (commercial register). Step 2 – Tax onboarding: within one month after formation the UG files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER with the local Finanzamt; this single intake assigns the Steuernummer (statutory German TIN), opens corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT and payroll tax files, and is mandatory for any economic activity. Step 3 – VAT ID: the UG then applies to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr (VAT identification number), required for cross-border B2B lease invoices, EC sales lists and Intrastat declarations; non-established foreign entities use the BZSt application route (Form USt 1 TU) in parallel with registration at the Finanzamt where a German permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte) exists. Step 4 – Foreign-lessor alternative: a pure foreign aircraft-engine lessor without a German PE is not automatically required to register because the German airline lessee self-assesses VAT under the reverse-charge rule (§13b UStG); registration becomes mandatory only when the foreign supplier carries on taxable business in Germany outside the reverse-charge scope (e.g., B2C leasing).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a mini-GmbH and a full taxable corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft). It is subject to entity-level Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) at 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) of 5.5%, and municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at 3.5% applied to the municipal Hebesatz multiplier (commonly 200%–490%), yielding an aggregate effective profit tax of approximately 29.8%–33%. The UG is a German resident taxpayer (unbeschränkt steuerpflichtig); worldwide profits, including foreign aircraft-engine lease rentals, are taxable in Germany with foreign tax credit relief under DTT and §34c EStG (no territorial-scope exemption for leasing income). No German pass-through election exists for a UG; for U.S. federal income tax purposes the UG is a per se corporation under Treas. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account convertibility regime within the EU internal market and vis-à-vis third countries; the euro is freely convertible and there are no exchange control approvals, surrender requirements, or conversion mandates for lease receivables, equity injections, or profit repatriation. The only material frictions are (i) the mandatory balance-of-payments statistical reporting of cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank, and (ii) tax-side remittance hurdles: corporate income tax at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (effective rate usually 28-32%) levied on UG profits, a potential German withholding assessment under Section 50a(1)(3) EStG on cross-border movable-property rental streams (aircraft-engine leasing is typically assessed for domestic withholding at 20% plus solidarity surcharge unless treaty, EU/EEA, or statutory aircraft-exemption relief applies), and dividend withholding of 25% plus solidarity surcharge (26.375%) when profit is distributed to the Ugandan shareholder, with treaty relief available under the Germany-Uganda Double Taxation Agreement only upon formal verification of the beneficial-owner position and holding thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None: Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline. USD/EUR lease rentals earned by the German UG may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at licensed credit institutions. The sole compliance obligation is the cross-border statistical report (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) to the Deutsche Bundesbank for any payment received from or made to a non-resident of EUR 12,500 or more, due in aggregated monthly form by the seventh calendar day of the month following the reporting month; no conversion or central-bank licensing is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), jointly administered with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK); prudential regulation of payment institutions and credit institutions is exercised by the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions holding BaFin or equivalent EEA passports: such EMIs may provide IBANs, currency conversion, and payment initiation for operational rent streams, but they cannot lawfully hold the UG's capital escrow balance or perform credit-like functions beyond Safeguarding Rules, and many aviation-sector compliance teams will reject engine-leasing transactions as prohibited business. Low feasibility for non-EEA/offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-only, Caribbean, or unlicensed providers): German tier-1 correspondent banks will refuse nostro settlement, BaFin treats them as unregulated payment service providers, and the UG's Medium-Risk-Enhanced AML profile makes such rails a structural compliance blocker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and/or shareholder-loan capital from the Ugandan parent company must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from a licensed Ugandan commercial bank to the UG's tier-1 German account, with the payment narrative stating 'capital increase / shareholder loan' and the appropriate AWV statistical purpose code for participation acquisitions (Erwerb von Beteiligungen) or shareholder lending. The UG must report the inflow to the Deutsche Bundesbank as a cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 within the monthly aggregation window (by the seventh day of the following month), and banks will demand the notarised capital-increase resolution, a signed shareholder loan agreement, the parent's board approval, and proof of the source of funds before accepting the credit. Payments must not pass through third-party accounts or unlicensed intermediaries; routed via direct correspondent banking (Uganda -> New York/Frankfurt clearing -> German beneficiary account) using a confirmed beneficiary name and IBAN, with a transaction reference that exactly matches the loan or equity documentation to avoid freezing under German banking compliance.Operational outflows use SEPA-Credit-Transfer (ISO 20022) for EUR payments and SWIFT MT103 for USD lease disbursements, engine maintenance fees, insurance premiums, and redelivery-cost settlements. Larger engine acquisition or damage-repair exposures are collateralised through deposits held in dedicated escrow accounts at the German banking partner, complemented by commercial standby letters of credit (SBLCs) issued by the lessee's bank under UCP 600/ISP 98 and, where contractually required, documentary Commercial Letters of Credit for engine exchange or lease-security obligations. Actual clearing is executed via SWIFT/CHIPS/Fedwire for USD and TARGET2 for EUR through the bank's nostro network; German banks have no dedicated aviation clearing system, so all settlement instructions, payment calendars, and late-payment interest provisions must be pre-agreed in the lease agreement and mirrored in the account mandate.Outright EUR/USD forward contracts with a German tier-1 bank, transacted as a commercial-hedge exemption under MiFID II and reported under EMIR Refit for non-financial counterparties below the clearing thresholdNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing non-convertible or thinly traded African currencies (UGX, GHS, NGN, KES) to hedge lease receivables from regional airlines without creating deliverable local-currency settlement riskCross-currency swaps converting USD lease-revenue streams into EUR funding or vice versa, documented under ISDA 2002 with a Credit Support Annex providing two-way CSA collateralFX options on EUR/USD with a forward-overlay collar structure to cap hedging costs, permissible under the commercial-hedge exemption where the UG does not act as a systematic derivatives dealer, with mandatory EMIR trade reporting to a registered trade repository

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility for EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions holding BaFin or equivalent EEA passports: such EMIs may provide IBANs, currency conversion, and payment initiation for operational rent streams, but they cannot lawfully hold the UG's capital escrow balance or perform credit-like functions beyond Safeguarding Rules, and many aviation-sector compliance teams will reject engine-leasing transactions as prohibited business. Low feasibility for non-EEA/offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-only, Caribbean, or unlicensed providers): German tier-1 correspondent banks will refuse nostro settlement, BaFin treats them as unregulated payment service providers, and the UG's Medium-Risk-Enhanced AML profile makes such rails a structural compliance blocker.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 (cross-border controlled transactions per related party per year) triggers mandatory Local File; Master File required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 50 million; Germany applies OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and administrative principles.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but substance is a substantive legal requirement for treaty entitlement and anti-abuse purposes: a UG engaged in aircraft engine dry leasing must maintain local management, decision-making records, personnel (or outsourced managerial functions with control), and real economic substance; absence triggers §42 AO recharacterization and denial of DTA relief under §50d(3) EStG.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Abusive Loss Exploitation

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under §370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years (10 years in aggravated cases) and/or fines; mandatory tax re-assessment and interest at 0.15% per month; forfeiture of economic benefits.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §69 AO for unpaid corporate taxes, interest, and penalties caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach; criminal directors face travel bans, passport surrender, and disqualification.
Shell Company / Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may disregard the UG as an artificial arrangement under §42 AO, deny treaty benefits under §50d(3) EStG, retroactively impose dividend withholding tax and trade tax, and apply a 5% late payment surcharge plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for withheld taxes and unpaid contributions; civil damage claims by creditors under §43 GmbHG; criminal liability for bankruptcy and tax evasion if insolvency concealment occurs.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under §162 AO, tax authorities may presume increased income and impose penalty equal to 5% of the adjusted income, minimum EUR 5,000, increasing to 10% for repeated failure; plus assessment interest and possible criminal proceedings for falsely documenting transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false adequacy certificates or conceal transfer pricing documentation face fines, director disqualification, and personal enforcement for the penalty amount if the UG cannot pay.
Cross-Border Withholding Tax and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Imposition of withholding tax at 26.375% on constructive dividends plus non-compliance penalty; denial of treaty application; under §50a EStG, additional 15.825% royalty withholding with interest; fines for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for failure to withhold and remit tax under §69 AO and §73 AO; binding personal assessment orders; travel bans and seizure of assets in serious evasion cases.
AML / Economic Sanctions / Escrow Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under German AML Act (GwG), fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover for aggravated violations; supervision by BaFin/FIU; confiscation of illicit payments; criminal sanctions for money laundering under §261 StGB up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Board members face individual fines, imprisonment, and civil liability for failing to implement AML controls; a compliance officer can be personally prosecuted under §14 StGB; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation.
