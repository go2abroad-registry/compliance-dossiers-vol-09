Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt), responsible for issuing the German Value Added Tax identification number (USt-IdNr.), and the local tax office (Finanzamt) competent for the UG's registered seat, responsible for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), and VAT filing. Official electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

        
E-government filing portal
For a domestically incorporated UG: file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent Finanzamt within one month of commercial register registration; the Finanzamt assigns the business tax number (Steuernummer) and coordinates with BZSt for the USt-IdNr. For a foreign entity without a German establishment: submit VAT registration application using BZSt Form 'USt 1 TN' or an equivalent online application via ELSTER, together with certified copy of the foreign commercial register extract, proof of identity/directors, foreign tax identification number, intended German supplies, evidence of a fiscal representative where legally required under §22a UStG, and, if importing/exporting goods, a valid EU EORI number.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a domestically incorporated UG: file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent Finanzamt within one month of commercial register registration; the Finanzamt assigns the business tax number (Steuernummer) and coordinates with BZSt for the USt-IdNr. For a foreign entity without a German establishment: submit VAT registration application using BZSt Form 'USt 1 TN' or an equivalent online application via ELSTER, together with certified copy of the foreign commercial register extract, proof of identity/directors, foreign tax identification number, intended German supplies, evidence of a fiscal representative where legally required under §22a UStG, and, if importing/exporting goods, a valid EU EORI number.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Not applicable: German tax law provides no check-the-box or pass-through election for a UG; the entity is unconditionally subject to entity-level corporate income tax, trade tax, and solidarity surcharge. Available statutory mechanisms include tax group (Organschaft) with profit-and-loss pooling for corporate and trade tax purposes, VAT group treatment under §2(2) No. 2 UStG, and the small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG where turnover thresholds are met. Hybrid mismatch provisions under §4k EStG override any preferential treatment; no hybrid entity classification election exists.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
For resident small businesses under §19 UStG: exemption applies where total turnover was not more than EUR 22,000 in the previous calendar year and is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000 in the current calendar year. For foreign non-established businesses: no economic threshold; VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply made in Germany under the place-of-supply rules, subject to applicable simplification rules

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Supplies of movable goods physically exported from Germany to a third-country territory qualify for the German 0% export VAT exemption under §4(1)(1a) UStG and §6 UStG, provided the supplier is a German taxable person, the goods leave the EU under customs supervision, and the supplier retains documentary proof of export. Services may be outside the scope of German VAT where the place of supply under §3a UStG is outside Germany; however, construction services physically performed on real property located in Germany, including grain silo slip-forming, aeration-tower erection, and on-site steelwork, are mandatorily treated as supplied at the German property location and are therefore outside the scope of export zero-rating.

Germany mandates structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions under §14 UStG. Since 1 January 2025, every domestic B2B business must be able to receive e-invoices. Issuance obligations phase in: from 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all domestic B2B suppliers. E-invoices must conform to the EN 16931 semantic model, e.g., XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X 2.2.2. There is currently no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory API transmission of invoice data to the tax authority, and no general digital-stamping requirement; invoice reporting occurs through periodic VAT returns and recapitulative statements submitted via ELSTER.

      
German tax and customs authorities routinely audit export zero-rating claims. If the mandated export documentation is incomplete, inaccurate, or the goods are returned into the EU, diverted to domestic consumption, destroyed, or the consideration is not received within the generally accepted monetary-receipt period (customary six months / 180 days), the zero-rated VAT is retrospectively clawed back with default interest of 0.5% per month under §233a AO. Fraudulent or reckless export VAT claims constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, carrying imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary fine, and the limitation period for deliberate VAT evasion extends to ten years under §169(2) AO. In addition, penalties under §26a UStG for failure to issue proper invoices and §379/§384 AO for administrative customs violations apply, and EU post-clearance customs recovery may proceed under the Union Customs Code where goods export was irregular.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The euro is freely convertible. Germany has no general exchange controls, capital-account authorization regime, or restriction on foreign ownership of a German UG. Current-account and capital-account payments may generally be made through authorized payment providers, subject to sanctions, AML controls, tax rules, and statistical reporting. Dividends and other distributions may be remitted abroad after applicable corporate-law approvals, distributable-profit tests, withholding-tax compliance, and any treaty or EU relief procedures. Interest, royalties, service fees, and intercompany payments require genuine commercial support, arm's-length pricing where applicable, and tax documentation. Restrictions may arise from EU or German sanctions and targeted asset-freeze measures.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No mandatory conversion, surrender ratio, or general deadline applies to export proceeds. Receipts may be retained in euro or foreign currency. Cross-border payments and receipts may be reportable to the Bundesbank under AWV external-payments statistics rules where the applicable conditions are met; the general reporting threshold is EUR 50,000 per payment or equivalent, subject to statutory exceptions and current Bundesbank instructions. Reporting is statistical, not an approval or surrender requirement. The company should verify current thresholds, exceptions, reporting party, and filing method before each reporting period.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers external-payments statistics reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action administers the foreign-trade legal framework; BaFin supervises regulated banks and payment institutions. Germany applies EU law and is part of the euro area.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium for routine euro and foreign-currency payments where the provider is authorized in Germany or passported under EU law. Electronic money institutions and payment institutions are not equivalent to deposit-taking banks; balances may not qualify for statutory deposit-guarantee protection and may be subject to safeguarding arrangements. Confirm the provider's legal entity, authorization, safeguarding model, currency capabilities, SWIFT access, payment limits, and acceptance of a newly formed UG before relying on it for share-capital evidence, construction project receipts, or letters of credit. An offshore provider without applicable German or EU authorization should not be used for regulated payment services in Germany.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Unclear ownership, nominee arrangements, or beneficial owners that cannot be identified or verified under the German Money Laundering Act.
        
Root cause: Unclear ownership, nominee arrangements, or beneficial owners that cannot be identified or verified under the German Money Laundering Act.

        
Defense evidence: Current commercial-register extract or formation documents; articles of association; shareholder register or equivalent ownership records; group ownership chart to natural-person beneficial owners; identity and address evidence; beneficial-ownership register filing evidence where required; explanations and documents for any trusts, nominees, or intermediate entities.

      

      
      

        Cross-border share-capital funding or project receipts inconsistent with the UG's declared business, expected turnover, or sender profile.
        
Root cause: Cross-border share-capital funding or project receipts inconsistent with the UG's declared business, expected turnover, or sender profile.

        
Defense evidence: Shareholder resolutions and subscription records; bank statements showing source of funds; source-of-wealth evidence where requested; executed construction contracts, purchase orders, invoices, and milestone certificates; counterparties' legal and beneficial-ownership details; payment purpose and transaction-flow narrative.

      

      
      

        Payments involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or counterparties with adverse media.
        
Root cause: Payments involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or counterparties with adverse media.

        
Defense evidence: Documented sanctions screening of shareholders, directors, customers, suppliers, banks, vessels, and beneficial owners; screening records and disposition of potential matches; end-user and end-use statements where relevant; export-control classification and authorization evidence where applicable; contract, shipping, and delivery documentation.

      

      
      

        Large or rapid pass-through transactions, third-party payments, cash activity, or funds unrelated to grain-silo construction.
        
Root cause: Large or rapid pass-through transactions, third-party payments, cash activity, or funds unrelated to grain-silo construction.

        
Defense evidence: Board-approved treasury and payment-control policy; contracts and invoices tying each payment to a business purpose; explanation of third-party payer or payee relationships; reconciliation of receipts to milestones and accounting records; authorized-signatory matrix and dual-approval evidence.

      

      
      

        Unsubstantiated intercompany loans, dividends, management fees, or other payments to shareholders or affiliates.
        
Root cause: Unsubstantiated intercompany loans, dividends, management fees, or other payments to shareholders or affiliates.

        
Defense evidence: Executed intercompany agreements; board and shareholder approvals; transfer-pricing support where applicable; invoices and evidence of services or financing; tax and withholding-tax analysis; distributable-profit computation and statutory accounts for distributions.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Use a bank account in the UG's legal name or a bank-approved formation account. Send funds from the shareholder's verified account using a traceable SEPA Credit Transfer for euro or SWIFT transfer for other currencies. Identify the payer and beneficiary consistently with formation documents and instruct a clear reference such as 'share-capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan' only where that characterization is legally correct. Provide the bank with subscription documents, shareholder resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, and any requested capital-contribution confirmation. Do not route capital through an unrelated third party or mislabel a loan as equity. Reconcile receipt to the UG's books and notarial or commercial-register requirements. Assess AWV reporting separately; reporting is statistical and does not replace bank AML review.Settle domestic and euro-area invoices through SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer where available. Use SWIFT customer credit transfers, commonly evidenced by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments; agree fees, value date, intermediary-bank charges, beneficiary-bank details, and payment references in advance. For construction equipment, steel, engineering services, and other material imports, match each payment to the contract, purchase order, invoice, delivery terms, and milestone or acceptance evidence. A documentary commercial letter of credit may be arranged through an issuing bank where commercially justified, subject to bank credit approval, agreed UCP 600 terms, compliant presentation documents, and any applicable sanctions or export-control screening.
