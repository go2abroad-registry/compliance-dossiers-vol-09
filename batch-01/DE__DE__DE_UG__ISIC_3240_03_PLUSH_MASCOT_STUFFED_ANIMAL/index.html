19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 previous-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year turnover for the small business scheme (Section 19 UStG) as amended with effect from 1 January 2025; every entrepreneur is otherwise required to register for VAT with the Finanzamt irrespective of turnover volume immediately upon commencement of business activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of plush mascot goods to third-country (non-EU) customers are VAT-exempt with 0% output tax under Section 4(1)(1a) in conjunction with Section 6 UStG; intra-EU supplies to a customer holding a valid VAT ID are exempt under Section 6a UStG. Contract processing and other services performed by the UG on goods exported by a foreign principal may qualify as zero-rated ancillary or processing supplies. The UG must be the supplying entrepreneur, the goods must physically leave Germany/EU territory, and the export must be fully evidenced under the accounting records (Buch- und Belegnachweis) pursuant to Sections 8-14 UStDV. Input VAT is recovered through the German VAT return rather than a separate refund claim.

  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract (three-language compliant)
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, Airway Bill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical transport
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) or customs stamped confirmation of goods exit

  • Inward remittance certificate and bank payment proof for the export proceeds

B2G e-invoicing has been mandatory for all suppliers to German public authorities since 27 November 2020 in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats. Domestic B2B e-invoicing is introduced in phases under the Growth Opportunities Act: from 1 January 2025 every business must be able to receive e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 businesses with >EUR 800,000 taxable turnover must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B invoicing must be e-invoices. Invoices must be in an EN 16931-compliant structured XML format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or qualified EDI) and, once the Federal platform is operational, transmitted therein. A real-time fiscal clearance/stamping system as found in Italy or Latin America is not currently in force.

Finanzamt auditors verify export documentation in regular VAT audits; if export proof is missing or fraudulent, zero-rating is retroactively denied and output VAT is clawed back at 19% under Section 14c UStG with a one-month assessment period. Tax arrears bear interest of 6% per annum (Section 233a AO), plus a late-payment surcharge of 1% per commenced month (Section 240 AO). Intentional tax evasion under Section 370 AO carries imprisonment of up to 10 years and confiscation of proceeds; negligent understatements are fined up to EUR 50,000. Violations of the foreign trade reporting/repatriation obligations under Sections 70-80 AWV trigger administrative fines of up to EUR 30,000 per case, with criminal liability for repeat or serious breaches.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG), implementing the EU free movement of capital. The euro is fully convertible with no exchange-control approval, prior authorisation, or licence requirement for the ordinary treasury flows of a German UG engaged in the OEM manufacture and export of plush mascots, including inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, customer advance payments, outbound supplier disbursements, dividend distributions and profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders. Residual administrative controls are limited to (i) AWV statistical declaration duties: cross-border payments and receivables from/to non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 (including aggregated monthly flows above that threshold) must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank using the correct AWV reporting code (e.g., goods-payment codes, service codes, capital/loan codes), and (ii) EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening, which can block only those transactions involving restricted persons, goods or banks. No profit-remittance hurdle exists where the UG's annual financial statements are adopted and the shareholders' distribution resolution satisfies the German corporate-law test of distributable balance-sheet profit; dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) applies domestically but may be reduced or exempt under an applicable double tax treaty (including the Germany-China treaty) where the beneficial-ownership and substance conditions are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement on export proceeds. A German exporting UG may freely hold EUR, USD, CNY or any other currency export receipts in offshore accounts, convert them at market rates, offset them against import payments, or use them directly to pay foreign suppliers. The only statutory obligation is the AWV Section 67 balance-of-payments reporting duty for inbound export settlement exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month, normally filed electronically by the UG's German bank through the Bundesbank reporting interface by the 7th calendar day of the month following the receipt. Where export goods are covered by customs declarations containing the statutory trade codes, the reporting duty is discharged via the customs data, but the safe commercial practice is to instruct the house bank to code every incoming MT103/SEPA credit as an export-of-goods payment (AWV goods category) so that automatic Bundesbank notification occurs. There is no central-bank approval step, no repatriation quota, no export-retention regime, and no fixed conversion ratio into EUR.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, Frankfurt am Main), acting as the competent authority for German external-sector statistics and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) reporting; jointly within the Eurosystem headed by the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy, and in supervisory cooperation with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) for prudential and anti-money-laundering regulation of credit institutions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the assessed corporate income tax, yielding a combined federal rate of approximately 15.825%; add municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal multiplier (Hebesatz), commonly 400%, producing a total headline combined rate of approximately 29.825% to 32.975% depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in an effective rate of 26.375%. The withholding tax is levied on gross dividends distributed by the UG to its shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate income tax return and trade tax return must be filed by 31 July of the following year for the standard case. However, if the return is prepared by a tax advisor (Steuerberater), the deadline is automatically extended to the last day of February of the second following year (e.g., fiscal year 2024: filing deadline 28 February 2026 for advisor-prepared returns). The annual financial statements must be submitted to the Bundesanzeiger within 12 months of the fiscal year-end, but for small corporations (Kleinstkapitalgesellschaften) the deadline is extended to 6 months after the fiscal year-end for preparation and 12 months for disclosure. The fiscal year generally corresponds to the calendar year unless a divergent fiscal year is approved by the tax office.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is required if the taxpayer exceeds the following thresholds: (i) Local File: EUR 6,000,000 for goods transactions or EUR 600,000 for other transactions (e.g., services, royalties, interest) with related parties per year; (ii) Master File: EUR 100,000,000 group turnover; (iii) Country-by-Country Report (CbCR): EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. The UG, as a small entity, is unlikely to meet these thresholds unless part of a large multinational group. However, the tax office may request documentation for any related-party transaction if it suspects non-arm's length pricing, regardless of thresholds. Documentation must be prepared in a timely manner and submitted within 30 days upon request (60 days for extraordinary transactions).

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a standalone regime do not exist in Germany; however, the general anti-abuse doctrine and the Foreign Tax Act (Außensteuergesetz, AStG) apply. The UG must have genuine economic substance, including adequate office space, personnel, and decision-making in Germany. Shell companies without substance may be disregarded for tax treaty benefits under Section 50d(3) of the Income Tax Act (EStG) if the shareholder is not entitled to treaty relief due to lack of beneficial ownership. Additionally, the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) has been implemented, including the interest limitation rule (Zinsschranke), controlled foreign company (CFC) rules, and hybrid mismatch rules. For the UG as a plush toy OEM, substance is evidenced by actual manufacturing or procurement activities, employees, and local management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung, AO)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 50,000 for minor cases; in serious cases, imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; in particularly serious cases (e.g., large-scale evasion), imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years. Additionally, the tax liability is retroactively assessed with interest (6% per annum) and penalty surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors (Geschäftsführer) can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they breach their duty to ensure timely and accurate tax filings (Section 69 AO). This includes civil restitution for the unpaid tax, travel bans (Aufenthaltsverbot) to secure criminal proceedings, and imprisonment. Directors may also be disqualified from managing a company (Gewerbeuntersagung).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance under Section 8(3) AStG and Section 162 AO

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 1,000,000 for failure to submit documentation timely; additional fine of up to EUR 1,000,000 for inaccurate documentation. The tax office may estimate income under Section 162 AO, leading to higher taxable income and tax assessments. Adjusted income may be subject to corporate income tax, trade tax, and withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for the additional tax liabilities if they are responsible for the transfer pricing documentation. In cases of intentional or grossly negligent profit shifting, criminal prosecution for tax evasion may follow, with imprisonment up to 10 years. Directors may also face personal liability for any resulting withholding tax obligations.
Failure to Comply with VAT and Withholding Tax Obligations under Sections 48, 48a EStG and Section 18 UStG

Statutory sanction: Late payment surcharge of 1% per month on outstanding amounts; fine up to EUR 25,000 for late filing; in cases of evasion, imprisonment up to 5 years or fine. For VAT fraud, imprisonment up to 10 years and fines. The UG may be held liable for the unpaid tax if it failed to withhold.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the withheld tax amounts if they fail to remit them to the tax authorities (Section 69 AO and Section 44 EStG). This can result in personal bankruptcy, travel bans, and imprisonment. Directors may also be held liable for any tax liabilities incurred by the UG during their tenure.
Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 5% of annual turnover for serious violations; imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering. The UG may be subject to increased regulatory scrutiny and loss of banking relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML violations, including fines and imprisonment up to 5 years. They may also be subject to travel bans and asset freezing. Directors have a duty to report suspicious transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) and failure to do so can result in personal criminal liability.
Shell Company Abuse and Treaty Shopping under Section 50d(3) EStG and EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, including withholding tax reductions and exemptions; retroactive assessment of withholding taxes with interest and penalties; fines up to EUR 50,000 for non-compliance with documentation requirements. The tax office may also invoke the anti-abuse rule under Section 42 AO, which disregards the UG as a tax shelter.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for the additional tax liabilities if they knowingly participated in the abusive arrangement. In cases of tax evasion, imprisonment up to 10 years. Directors may also be subject to personal liability for any withheld tax not remitted. Travel bans and disqualification from directorships may apply.
