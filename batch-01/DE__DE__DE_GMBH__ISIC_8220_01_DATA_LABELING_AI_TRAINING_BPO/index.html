Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Amtsgericht – Registergericht (Local Court, Registry Court) at the company's registered office; electronic filing is submitted through a German notary via the Handelsregister portal and the federal electronic registration system.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG) §§ 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 35; German Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 33, 39-40a; Trade Regulation Act (GewO) § 14; Civil Code (BGB) notarial form requirements.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and formal application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) with the list of shareholders and specimen signatures of managing directors.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 46 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 of each cash contribution must be paid in and be freely at the disposal of the managing director before registration, evidenced by a bank confirmation from a blocked company account. A third-party escrow arrangement is not sufficient unless the account is opened in the company's name and the funds are irrevocably released to the company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety approval from the responsible fire prevention authority (Brandschutzdienststelle), structural load inspections under the applicable Landesbauordnung for office or server areas, and workplace safety clearance under ArbStättV, BetrSichV and DGUV regulations, including emergency exits, first-aid room adequacy and electrical safety testing.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital is EUR 25,000; at least EUR 12,500 of each cash contribution must be paid in and be freely at the disposal of the managing director before registration, evidenced by a bank confirmation from a blocked company account. A third-party escrow arrangement is not sufficient unless the account is opened in the company's name and the funds are irrevocably released to the company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – central federal tax office for VAT ID issuance (USt-IdNr.) and international/EU matters; locally competent Finanzamt (municipal tax office) for corporate income tax and VAT registration; electronic filing and registration portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity or newly formed GmbH submits the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) online via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (statutory TIN). Subsequently apply to BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.) for EU cross-border transactions. Required documentation: certified articles of association, Handelsregisterauszug (commercial register extract), managing director identity, German registered office address, and for non-EU parent entities: certificate of incorporation and economic-beneficial-owner declarations translated into German; typical processing time 2–6 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (GmbH is a resident corporation with unlimited tax liability: Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on corporate tax ≈ 15.825% combined, plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at 3.5% base rate × Hebesatz 200%–490% yielding approximately 7%–17.15% effective; it is not fiscally transparent and distributions to shareholders are subject to withholding/capital gains rules)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; available elections/mechanisms: (i) partnership Opt-in to corporate taxation under § 1a KStG; (ii) corporate VAT and income tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) election for ≥95% owned German subsidiaries; (iii) mandatory hybrid mismatch rules under § 4k EStG (ATAD implementation) with limited neutrality/disclosure elections; (iv) no pass-through election exists for an existing standalone GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident small businesses: €25,000 prior-year turnover and €100,000 projected current-year turnover ceiling (Kleinunternehmerregelung § 19 UStG as amended for 2025); non-established foreign suppliers: zero threshold (mandatory VAT registration on the first taxable domestic supply); an operational German GmbH with B2B export service income is registered without threshold under general rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: physical export to non-EU third countries qualifies for VAT-exempt zero-rating under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG upon verified customs exit from EU territory. For AI data labeling/annotation/RLHF services supplied B2B to non-EU customers, the place-of-supply rule under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG shifts taxation to the customer's jurisdiction (reverse charge), resulting in 0% German VAT; B2C electronically supplied services to non-EU customers are outside German VAT scope per customer-country destination principle. No German statute mandates a fixed 180-day FX repatriation deadline, but repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days is the conservative audit and transfer-pricing documentation benchmark.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed in ATLAS with confirmed Ausfuhrbegleitdokument (ABD) or electronic export manifest
  • Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung) stating seller, buyer, Incoterms, goods description, and zero-rated export VAT position
  • Bill of Lading / CMR (international consignment note) / Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/SEPA payment confirmation proving receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Delivery/proof-of-receipt documentation (Lieferschein and Gelangensbestätigung where required by § 17a UStDV)

Mandatory B2B structured e-Invoicing (E-Rechnung): obligation to receive applies from 1 January 2025; mandatory issuance from 1 January 2027 for businesses with >€800,000 turnover and from 1 January 2028 for all; required formats are XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X (XML FACTUR-X 2.x); invoices must be transmitted electronically via Peppol, authenticated exchange, or e-mail; no real-time governmental fiscal clearance code or digital stamping applies in Germany, but GoBD-compliant archiving (10-year retention) and machine-readable XML storage are mandatory for audit purposes.

The Finanzamt may retrospectively disallow the 0% export exemption and assess German VAT at 19% plus monthly interest (0.15% per month) where the export evidence chain is incomplete or inconsistent; fraudulent or reckless claims trigger criminal tax evasion proceedings under § 370 AO with imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases) or monetary fines, administrative fines under §§ 378–379 AO up to €50,000, late-payment surcharges (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per month, and retrospective clawback of input VAT refunds with an extended 10-year assessment limitation period in cases of evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is subject to unrestricted capital and current account convertibility under EU regulations. For a German GmbH, capital account transactions such as equity injections and profit distributions are permitted without prior approval, subject only to post-transaction reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders face no convertibility restrictions, though they are subject to corporate tax and potential withholding tax obligations under applicable double taxation treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German law does not impose mandatory surrender requirements on export proceeds. Service exports such as AI data labeling BPO must be documented for VAT and income tax purposes. Companies may receive and retain export receipts in their bank accounts without converting them to a specific currency via the central bank; however, all transactions require full compliance with AML/KYC standards and tax reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank
