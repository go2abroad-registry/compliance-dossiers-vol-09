Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For UG (haftungsbeschränkt), the minimum share capital is EUR 1. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), Elster portal (www.elster.de); local Finanzamt (tax office) for income/VAT assessments; Gewerbesteuer administered by the municipality (Hebesatz).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity registers in Germany by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the local Finanzamt after completing entry in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister); the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (tax number) and, upon separate application to the BZSt, the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). Digital filing via Elster or a tax representative/advisor is required; non-resident entities generally need a fiscal representative for VAT compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a separate legal person subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax), Gewerbesteuer (trade tax), solidarity surcharge, and VAT; no pass-through regime applies to limited-liability companies in Germany.

Available election forms

No German check-the-box election exists; a UG may, however, form an Organschaft (VAT and corporate income tax fiscal unity) with a German controlling parent. Hybrid mismatch treatments are governed by ATAD-implementing legislation (Section 4k EStG) and EU directives. Optional small-business VAT relief (Kleinunternehmer) applies only below EUR 22,000 domestic annual turnover.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small trader threshold EUR 22,000 annual turnover (Section 19 UStG); EU cross-border distance sales / OSS threshold EUR 10,000; non-resident persons making taxable supplies in Germany are generally required to register for VAT regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods to non-EU territories (Section 4 No. 1, Section 6 UStG), to EU cross-border dispatches when transport to another Member State is proven, and to certain services directly connected to exported goods (e.g. freight and logistics services). For bakery and pastry supplies, the goods must physically leave the customs territory of the EU and be documented as such; services rendered to EU customers are generally taxed at the customer's place of supply (B2B reverse charge) rather than zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / ATLAS customs declaration with Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill / transport document evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange repatriation confirmation (payment proof from the non-resident buyer)

E-Rechnung (electronic invoicing) is mandatory for domestic B2B invoices in Germany since 1 January 2025 (all businesses must be able to receive XML-based e-invoices); structured XML formats ZUGFeRD / XRechnung are required, with a sending obligation phased in from 1 January 2027 for prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all taxable persons. No real-time tax-authority clearance or digital stamping is currently mandated; compliance is ensured through GoBD-compliant electronic recordkeeping and periodic VAT audits.

German tax authorities require a complete export dossier (customs exit confirmation, transport documentation, and payment proof) to sustain zero-rating. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rated supplies trigger retrospective VAT assessment plus late-payment interest under Section 233a AO; deliberate false export claims constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment up to 5 years (aggravated cases up to 10 years) and monetary penalties. In addition, input VAT deductions on related purchases are clawed back with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies the euro as a fully convertible currency for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Articles 63 et seq. TFEU. There is no German exchange-control approval, licensing, allocation, or rationing procedure for inbound capital, outbound trade settlement, or profit/dividend remittance. Capital movement between Germany and non-EU states is unrestricted except where EU/UN sanctions or investment screening rules apply. A UG is bound by corporate law solvency and mandatory reserve rules, and by balance-sheet surplus requirements, before making shareholder distributions; these corporate-law constraints are not convertibility or FX constraints. Statutory AWV reporting may be required for cross-border payments above the applicable threshold, and is informational rather than permission-based. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders may be subject to withholding tax or treaty relief, but this does not limit convertibility or fund transferability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German law does not impose any mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds, any mandatory holding period, or any minimum conversion ratio for foreign-currency receivables. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency on accounts, convert it at freely negotiated market rates, or use it directly for payments. The only mandatory compliance dimension is the statistical reporting requirement under Section 67 AWV and related provisions: a resident who receives a payment from a foreign country or makes a payment to a foreign country in an amount equal to or greater than EUR 12,500 must ensure that the payment is reported or made reportable to the Deutsche Bundesbank, generally via the German credit institution effecting the payment or through the reporting channels made available by the Bundesbank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of Germany within the Eurosystem), with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as the competent German banking and anti-money laundering supervisor for licensing and conduct of banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions. For external-sector statistical purposes, cross-border payment and capital-movement reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV) is filed with the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary, non-primary clearing rail; Low as the sole principal banking infrastructure for a German UG. A licensed e-money institution (EMI) under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) can lawfully provide payment accounts, execute SEPA/SWIFT payments, issue e-money, and offer foreign-exchange conversion, but it may not grant loans, pay interest on e-money balances, or provide the stability and segregated account hierarchy expected of a tier-one corporate bank. Non-EEA fintech or EMI providers must have a German/EEA authorisation or an EU passport to market or operate payment services in Germany; unlicensed offshore digital banks cannot lawfully maintain a payment account for a German UG. Safe treasury practice is to use only BaFin-authorised EMIs for low-value cross-border payouts, niche currency-denominated disbursements, or auxiliary flows, while the primary liquidity hub remains a conventional German commercial bank. The statutory capital-formation account must remain with a prudentially supervised credit institution.

An audit becomes compulsory in two consecutive financial years when the entity exceeds two of three thresholds: total assets EUR 6,000,000, annual revenue EUR 12,000,000, average annual employees 50. Medium and large corporations exceeding these thresholds must appoint a certified auditor (Wirtschaftsprüfer).
