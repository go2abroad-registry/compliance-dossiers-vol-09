UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz—GwG), particularly §§ 3, 10–15, 18–26a and 23; Transparency Register Regulation (Transparenzregisterverordnung—TrGebV); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung—HRV). Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A German domestic business address is required for registration and service under GmbHG § 8(4) and HGB § 13. A virtual office may be acceptable as a registered business address if it is a genuine, deliverable domestic address with effective access to company communications and the provider permits the use. It does not itself establish tax residence, operational substance, or a permissible place of business. A fabless semiconductor design company should maintain evidence of actual management, personnel or contractors, development activity, IP control, and commercial records in the location represented to banks and tax authorities.

Competent corporate registry

Local Amtsgericht - Registergericht (commercial register department), acting through the mandatory electronic Handelsregister procedure; the notarial deed is filed in name of the company by the German notary.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG has no statutory minimum capital; share capital may be set at any amount between EUR 1 and EUR 24,999. The entire capital must be subscribed, may consist only of cash contributions, and must be deposited into a bank account in the company's name before registration. In a single-shareholder UG the cash contribution must be fully paid before filing; in multi-shareholder UGs at least one quarter of the par value per share is payable before registration. No court-mandated escrow account is required, but the bank must furnish a payment confirmation. The UG must retain 25% of annual surplus as a legal reserve until share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Municipal fire safety certification is required for the premises, including smoke detectors, emergency exits, and extinguishers; structural load inspection is mandatory if server racks or shielding rooms are installed; occupational clearance includes registration with the employers' liability insurance association, a DGUV-compliant workplace risk assessment, first-aid staffing, and any mandatory occupational medical examinations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) and the locally competent Finanzamt; the official electronic filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de), administered by the German Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Unternehmergesellschaft (UG) is incorporated before a German notary and entered in the Commercial Register, then must file the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER with the local Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns a corporate income tax and trade tax number (Steuernummer). The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) is issued separately by the BZSt after an application filed by the UG; foreign entrepreneurs without a German establishment apply directly to the BZSt or the Finanzamt responsible for VAT purposes before making taxable supplies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Unternehmergesellschaft (UG) is incorporated before a German notary and entered in the Commercial Register, then must file the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER with the local Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns a corporate income tax and trade tax number (Steuernummer). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not apply US-style check-the-box elections. The UG is mandatorily taxed as a domestic corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) at entity level for corporate income tax, solidary surcharge and municipal trade tax. Elections available are limited to specific regimes, such as VAT grouping (Organschaft) under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG when a controlling undertaking is financially, economically and organizationally integrated, and the corporate-income-tax group taxation election under Sections 14 and 17 KStG for a domestic profit-and-loss pooling arrangement requiring at least 95% shareholding. German anti-hybrid rules implement the EU ATAD and disallow deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements; no general hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 (prior calendar year) and EUR 50,000 (current calendar year) under the small-enterprise relief of Section 19 UStG, available only to domestic established entrepreneurs. Foreign entrepreneurs and UGs in regular business operations have no general de minimis exemption and must register for VAT from their first taxable supply in Germany; domestic intra-Community distance selling has separate EU thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Section 4 No. 1, Section 6 and Section 13b UStG, the supply of tangible goods is German export zero-rated at 0% where the goods are dispatched or transported from Germany to a non-EU third territory and corroborated by export evidence. For semiconductor design services and IP core licenses provided by a fabless UG to B2B customers established in a third country, German VAT is not chargeable if the place of supply is outside Germany under the recipient-location rule, provided the recipient is an entrepreneur and the relevant substantive and documentary conditions are met, including no abuse of the exported-service rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) as confirmed by German Customs (ATLAS clearance)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport documentation (Air Waybill, CMR consignment note) proving delivery to a third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT payment evidence matching the export invoice, used as documentary corroboration of consideration and to substantiate the commercial export transaction

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for domestic German B2B transactions entered into force on 1 January 2025, requiring EN 16931-compliant formats such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD; the obligation applies to issuers and recipients established in Germany, with phased transition rules for smaller-turnover suppliers until 2027/2028. Germany does not currently impose API-based real-time fiscal clearance, digital fiscal stamping or pre-clearance of invoices; the e-invoice must be transmitted digitally and retained in full XML semantic format.

German tax authorities may retrospectively disallow export zero-rating and assess 19% output VAT plus default interest at 0.9% per month (6% per annum) and, in trade tax and corporation tax, consequential adjustments. Fraudulent or negligent export proof triggers an intentional negligent or fraudulent tax evasion assessment under Section 369/370 AO, subject to fines and imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in severe cases; false export documents may concurrently constitute customs offences. The relevant VAT refund or zero-rating claim is denied, and clawback assessments are issued without limitation in comparable evasion periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is a fully convertible currency under EU law (TFEU Articles 63-66); Germany maintains no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions and no distinction between resident and non-resident accounts in respect of convertibility. Profit remittance by non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate law distribution tests (balance sheet net-asset cover under GmbH/UG law) and withholding tax obligations (KapESt 25% + SolZ 5.5% unless treaty-reduced). However, all cross-border payments or positions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics within five business days of receipt or payment (AWV Sections 62-68). For semiconductor IP core licensing, royalties qualify as current-account service payments and are remittable without administrative approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. A fabless UG holding EUR functional currency is free to retain, convert, or hedge foreign-currency receivables; the only statutory obligation is the statistical reporting of receipts from non-residents in excess of EUR 12,500 (AWV Section 67) using the Bundesbank's electronic reporting portal (Allgemeines Meldeportal). Inward direct investment (e.g., a US parent subscribing for UG shares) must be reported under AWV Section 64 when the non-resident holds 10% or more of the UG's equity or voting rights.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany) in conjunction with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) for prudential supervision; statutory basis: Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. BaFin-licensed or EU-passported EMIs (e.g., Penta/Solaris, Qonto, Revolut Business, Wise Business) can support day-to-day SEPA/instant-payment liquidity and low-value SWIFT disbursements. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs do not conduct credit or deposit-taking business and cannot issue or settle Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600); (ii) an offshore EMI holding only an e-money license passporting into Germany is permissible under CRD/EMD2, but high-value IP royalty flows and semiconductor export-control-linked payments will attract intensified KYC reviews; (iii) for the clearing of LC and large SWIFT MT103 transactions, a full German bank (Vollbank) relationship is mandatory. Recommendation: use EMI as a nearby operational layer only, never as the sole treasury rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed €5 million (goods) or €1 million (services) per year; Master File required if group turnover exceeds €100 million and related-party transactions exceed €5 million (per § 5-7 BMF TP Guidelines)

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the 'substance over form' doctrine under § 42 AO and the OECD BEPS Action 5. For a UG, economic substance is evidenced by actual management, employees, office premises, and decision-making in Germany. The UG must have a registered office and real business activity; otherwise, tax authorities may recharacterize transactions and deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (§ 370 AO); additional civil penalty of 10% of evaded tax per § 378 AO

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to file or pay (Geschäftsführerhaftung under § 69 AO); travel bans and asset freezes possible; criminal conviction leads to disqualification from directorship
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 25% WHT on deemed dividends; penalty up to €25,000 under § 42 AO

Director personal liability: Directors liable for back taxes and penalties; potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion; travel bans if substantial tax debt
AML / Money Laundering (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for individuals and €5 million for companies (§ 56 GwG); criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans and asset freezes; imprisonment for intentional violations
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of income, penalty of 5-10% of the adjustment amount (up to €1 million), and interest on underpayment at 6% per annum

Director personal liability: Directors liable for additional tax and penalties; criminal prosecution for intentional mispricing; travel bans for tax debt exceeding €100,000
