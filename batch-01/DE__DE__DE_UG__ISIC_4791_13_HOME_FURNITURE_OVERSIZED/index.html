Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and central withholding matters; locally competent Finanzamt (local tax office) for income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; official electronic filing portal is ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

A foreign or resident entity forming a UG (haftungsbeschränkt) must (1) register the company with the commercial register (Handelsregister) and obtain a registration certificate; (2) complete the statutory questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER or in paper form with the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the national tax identification number (Steuernummer) and corporate income tax/trade tax assessment basis; (3) apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr) using the official application form; (4) for intra-Community distance sales, register for the German One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme via BZSt if the EU-wide distance sales threshold of EUR 10,000 annually is exceeded or at the taxpayer's option; (5) a non-resident entrepreneur without a domestic establishment performing taxable supplies in Germany is subject to simplified VAT registration under Section 18(4a) UStG and must register directly with the competent Finanzamt; the UG is treated as a domestic corporation and will be issued a maintenance-free tax account number (Steuernummer) and separate VAT ID

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box classification exists in German tax law; the UG is conclusively treated as a resident corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) fully subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available elections include the formation of a corporate income tax group (Organschaft) under Section 14 KStG and a VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG with a controlling domestic parent, subject to profit-and-loss pooling and financial integration requirements; anti-hybrid election and documentation requirements under the ATAD Implementation Act (Section 4k EStG) apply to hybrid financial instruments and payments to hybrid entities, requiring the taxpayer to align treatment with the classification in the counterparty jurisdiction; trade tax Minderungen/Erhöhungen elections are available for financing expenses; the UG may elect the small business scheme (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Section 19 UStG only where turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 in the prior calendar year and EUR 50,000 in the current year, but this election is unavailable to non-resident entities and is generally inadvisable for B2C e-commerce exposure

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident small entrepreneurs, the EU distance sales and domestic small business threshold is EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year turnover for the Section 19 UStG exemption; non-resident suppliers without a domestic establishment have no registration threshold and must register from the first euro of taxable supplies in Germany; intra-Community distance sales of goods exceed a EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual turnover threshold before OSS registration becomes mandatory; the primary nexus rule under Section 3(6) UStG for e-commerce is the place where transport of goods to the customer ends (delivery destination), and for B2C distance sales the destination principle applies from the first item regardless of small consignment relief

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (Steuerbefreiung mit Vorsteuerabzug) for exports is governed by Section 4 No. 1(a) and Section 6 UStG: a supply is zero-rated as an export delivery if the entrepreneur transports or dispatches the goods from the German domestic territory to a third-country territory (non-EU) or to a free zone, whereby the goods must physically leave the EU and be transported by the supplier, the customer, or a commissioned third party; for online retail of oversized furniture, the supply must be unequivocally linked to an export requirement in the sales contract, the destination in a third country must be verifiable, and the goods must not be processed or used in Germany before dispatch; zero-rating also applies to services, including remote digital and logistics services, under Section 3a(2) UStG where the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside the EU; a dealer may alternatively rely on the export of goods in a single supply chain under Section 6(2) UStG, but the burden of proof lies with the supplier; the UG must demonstrate that the purchaser is the exporter or that the goods were directly transported to a third country, with care required for consumer-direct shipments where B2C export proof rules under Article 13 of Implementing Regulation (EU) No. 282/2011 require transport evidence retained by the supplier; where furniture is delivered to an intermediate EU fulfillment center before final onward shipment to a non-EU customer, each leg must be separately characterized and only the final intra-Community export leg qualifies for zero rating

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted and electronically processed in ATLAS — for exports valued above EUR 1,000 or with controlled/prohibited goods, including MRN (Movement Reference Number) allocation and export customs declaration confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note — evidencing physical transport of the oversized furniture from Germany to the third-country destination, with loading port, destination port, weight, and consignor/consignee details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Banking payment evidence) — showing the full purchase price in convertible foreign currency has been received from the non-resident buyer on the UG's German bank account within the statutory repatriation window

Germany imposes a statutory invoice issuance obligation under Sections 14, 14a UStG with mandatory e-invoice (E-Rechnung) obligations for B2B and B2G: as of 1 January 2025, domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats) and, from 1 January 2027, all domestic B2B taxpayers with turnover above EUR 800,000 in the preceding calendar year must issue structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 the mandate extends to all domestic B2B suppliers; there is no real-time fiscal clearance, transaction stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) system analogous to Italy or France currently; German tax law does require compliance with the GoBD (Principles for the Proper Management and Keeping of Books, Records and Documents in Electronic Form) including machine-readable export of invoice data in a standardized format; B2C invoices to private consumers may be simplified invoices under Section 33 UStDV; the VAT return (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung) is due electronically via ELSTER on a monthly basis (or quarterly for smaller taxpayers) by the 10th day after the close of the reporting period, with a permanent extension available for monthly filers via a special prepayment regime, and the annual VAT return (Umsatzsteuererklärung) is due by 31 July of the following year with extension available upon filing; OSS returns are filed quarterly via BZSt electronic portal

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict ex-post audit scrutiny by the Finanzamt and the Hauptzollamt (main customs office). If the supplier cannot substantiate the physical export with the required documentation bundle (at least the export declaration and transport documents, plus payment evidence upon audit), the tax office retroactively assesses 19% German VAT on the full sales price, with separate assessment of interest at 0.5% per month from the original VAT due date (eventually capped) under Section 233a AO; fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Section 370 Abgabenordnung (AO), punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary fine, and in aggravated commercial or gang-related cases under Section 370(3) AO imprisonment can extend to ten years; administrative fines of up to EUR 25,000 may be imposed under Section 379 AO for customs violations, and under Section 392 AO enforcement of the full VAT clawback plus monetary surcharge of 10% of the assessed amount may be levied under Section 240 AO for late payment; German anti-fraud statutes further allow retrospective denial of input VAT credits for the same transactions, seizure of goods in transit pursuant to Section 6(5) UStG, joint and several liability of the freight forwarder for exported goods later reimported into the EU, and mandatory disclosure of the exporter in the customs declaration; bank records, warehouse movement logs, GPS transport data, and export-refusal documentation from the receiving customs authority must be retained for ten years under Section 147 AO, and any fabric-proof deficiency presumptively collapses the zero-rating claim

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full euro convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions; the EU Treaty free-movement-of-capital provisions and the German AWV impose no exchange-control licensing or prior-authorization requirement for repatriation of profits, settlement of imports, servicing of shareholder loans, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. A qualifying UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) may remit dividends abroad subject only to non-exchange-control statutes: (i) the UG's mandatory statutory reserve of 25% of annual net profit under §5a GmbHG; (ii) dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (statutory combined rate 26.375%) under §43a EStG, possibly reduced to 0% for a qualifying EU parent under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or to 5%/10%/15% under a double-tax treaty (e.g., Germany–US or Germany–China applicable rate depending on shareholding); and (iii) anti-money-laundering verification of the ultimate beneficial owner under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) where remittances cross EUR 12,500. Therefore, profit remittance is a tax/compliance exercise, not a currency-convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None: Germany imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio; export receipts may remain indefinitely in foreign currency in German or foreign bank accounts. Mandatory formalities instead concern statistics: under AWV §§67/68, any cross-border payment (including export proceeds) between a resident and a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 7 calendar days using the Z1 (inbound) or Z2 (outbound) electronic report, save for exempt routine transactions. In addition, exports of goods to non-EU countries require the electronic customs export declaration (ATLAS), and intra-EU dispatches of furniture create monthly Intrastat declaration obligations where the applicable dispatch value threshold (currently EUR 500,000 for goods dispatches) is exceeded.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), Frankfurt am Main, in its capacity as the German foreign-exchange statistics and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) reporting authority; the European Central Bank (ECB) supplies euro-area monetary policy; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) licenses and supervises German banks and payment/e-money institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz, KWG). For an e-commerce exporter/importer, Deutsche Bundesbank is the operative FX-reporting counterparty, while BaFin prescribes the KYC/AML conduct expected of all German banking counterparts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for routing daily Stripe and Affirm BNPL settlements into a digital ledger, but LOW for statutory UG capital reserve custody, high-value freight disbursement, and cross-border FX clearing integrity. Legal constraints: only an EU/EEA-licensed e-money institution (EMI) passporting into Germany under PSD2 may lawfully hold balances for a German UG; a non-EEA EMI is not recognized as a 'bank' by BaFin, cannot assign a German IBAN, and operates outside the statutory deposit-protection scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz). EMI balances are 'safeguarded', not deposit-insured up to EUR 100,000, and the EMI is not a settlement-system participant with the same finality under the German KWG. Recommended blueprint: use a tier-1 German licensed bank as the sole treasury concentration and capital-escrow bank; use a PSD2 EMI strictly as an inbound collection ledger with automated daily total-zero sweeps to the licensed bank; keep Anglo-US EMIs (e.g., US-licensed PSPs) outside the account structure to avoid AWV reporting mismatches and sanctions-screening gaps.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for routing daily Stripe and Affirm BNPL settlements into a digital ledger, but LOW for statutory UG capital reserve custody, high-value freight disbursement, and cross-border FX clearing integrity. Legal constraints: only an EU/EEA-licensed e-money institution (EMI) passporting into Germany under PSD2 may lawfully hold balances for a German UG; a non-EEA EMI is not recognized as a 'bank' by BaFin, cannot assign a German IBAN, and operates outside the statutory deposit-protection scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz). EMI balances are 'safeguarded', not deposit-insured up to EUR 100,000, and the EMI is not a settlement-system participant with the same finality under the German KWG. Recommended blueprint: use a tier-1 German licensed bank as the sole treasury concentration and capital-escrow bank; use a PSD2 EMI strictly as an inbound collection ledger with automated daily total-zero sweeps to the licensed bank; keep Anglo-US EMIs (e.g., US-licensed PSPs) outside the account structure to avoid AWV reporting mismatches and sanctions-screening gaps.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 15.825%); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is then added at rates averaging 14%–17% depending on the municipality.

25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld amount, producing an aggregate 26.375%; reduced to 0% for qualifying corporate dividends under Section 8b KStG if the recipient is subject to German corporate tax; foreign shareholders may claim a lower DTA rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and supporting transfer-pricing documentation are mandatory for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services in a fiscal year; a Master File is required for corporate groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 100,000,000 and Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but Section 42 AO and anti-abuse case law require real economic substance to prevent treaty shopping and artificial constructions. A UG claiming DTA benefits must show actual business premises, personnel with decision-making authority, accounting substance, and a genuine commercial purpose in Germany; a letterbox UG with all material functions abroad will be disregarded and profits reallocated to Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Section 370 AO

Statutory sanction: Up to 5 years' imprisonment for basic tax evasion and up to 10 years in especially serious cases; monetary criminal fine; automatic assessment of unpaid tax plus 6% default interest; denial of loss carryforwards and treaty benefits; disqualification from corporate office for repeated violations.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally criminally liable and may be imprisoned; travel bans and asset seizures may be imposed; directors who breach their duty to file accurate returns are jointly and severally liable for the UG's tax shortfall under Section 69 AO; insolvency clawbacks may reach payments made to non-resident shareholders.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement under Section 42 AO

Statutory sanction: The tax office disregards the artificial arrangement, reallocates the full taxable income to Germany, applies the ordinary 15% (plus trade tax) rate, denies treaty protection and loss carryforwards, and may impose late-payment interest and penalties; an accuracy fine of up to EUR 5,000 may additionally be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign substance declarations or transfer-pricing documentation known to be false are personally liable for the resulting tax undercharge and may be prosecuted for tax evasion or falsification of business records; civil damages, travel restrictions, and management disqualification are common.
AML/KYC Deficiency under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Section 56 GwG up to EUR 200,000 per violation, or up to 5% of annual global turnover for entities subject to the full money-laundering regime; criminal conviction under Section 261 StGB carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and seizure of furniture stock, payment accounts, and equipment.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML officers are subject to individual criminal prosecution, personal fines, and possible travel bans pending trial; they may be ordered to make restitution to the state and can be barred from management roles in financial or e-commerce businesses.
