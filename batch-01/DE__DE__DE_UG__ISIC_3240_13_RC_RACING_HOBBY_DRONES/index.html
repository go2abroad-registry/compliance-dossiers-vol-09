Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with share capital below EUR 25,000, including EUR 1 legally, but the subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash before registration. Contributions in kind are unavailable for a UG at formation. The capital must be freely available to the managing director after registration; no statutory escrow account is required, although a dedicated bank account is customary. The UG must retain 25% of annual surplus, reduced by losses carried forward, until capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain premises-specific building-use and fire-safety approvals where required by the municipality or building authority. Maintain fire protection, emergency plans, electrical safety, machinery guarding, ventilation, dust control, chemical and battery storage, occupational risk assessments, employee instruction, and statutory workplace inspections. Structural load and machinery installation checks are required where relevant. Products must satisfy applicable EU product, EMC, RoHS, REACH, battery, radio-equipment, consumer-safety, and drone-market requirements; EASA operational rules apply separately to drone operators and users.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German corporation and is subject to entity-level corporation tax and trade tax; it is not fiscally transparent by default. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a UG. A UG may participate in a German tax consolidation group (Organschaft) if the statutory financial, control, profit-transfer, and operational-integration requirements are satisfied. German partnerships may have a statutory election under Section 1a of the Corporation Tax Act to be treated as corporations for corporation-tax purposes, but that election is not available to a UG. There is no general hybrid-mismatch election that changes the UG's default classification; anti-hybrid rules under Sections 4k of the Income Tax Act and related provisions can deny deductions or impose corresponding restrictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a business from VAT registration where it carries out taxable supplies in Germany. The German small-business regime under Section 19 UStG may apply where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to the statutory conditions and effective rules. The small-business regime generally removes output-VAT charging and input-tax deduction and does not eliminate all registration, invoicing, recordkeeping, or reporting obligations. Non-established suppliers may require registration from the first taxable German supply unless a specific reverse-charge, marketplace, import, distance-selling, or other statutory rule applies. EU-wide distance sales of goods and certain electronically supplied services are subject to the EUR 10,000 EU threshold, after which the One Stop Shop regime may be relevant.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may be VAT-exempt with input-tax credit under Sections 4 no. 1(a) and 6 UStG if the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the supplier can substantiate both the export transaction and the export procedure. The supplier must generally be the exporter or act under a qualifying export arrangement, and the goods must physically leave the EU. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-identified business customer in another EU Member State may be VAT-exempt under the applicable German and EU rules if the goods are transported to the other Member State, the customer provides a valid VAT identification number, the supplier satisfies reporting requirements, and transport and transaction records are complete. Services are not automatically treated as exports. Business-to-business services are generally taxed at the customer's location under Section 3a(2) UStG and may be invoiced without German VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism, while separate place-of-supply, use-and-enjoyment, and exemption rules apply to particular services. Export zero-rating or exemption must be supported by objective commercial, transport, customs, customer-status, and accounting evidence.

  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the applicable German customs system, with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, quantities, values, delivery terms, and export destination
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, air waybill, road consignment note, or equivalent evidence
  • Customs export accompanying document and exit-results message where applicable
  • Proof of delivery or import clearance in the destination country where available
  • Evidence linking the exported goods to the German sales invoice, inventory records, and accounting records
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, contractual documents, and correct recapitulative statement reporting
  • For export-related services: contract, customer-location evidence, service-performance evidence, and documentation demonstrating the applicable place-of-supply rule
  • Bank settlement or payment evidence where relevant; Germany does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption

Germany does not operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system comparable to Italy's SDI. From 1 January 2025, domestic German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. The receipt mandate generally covers invoices meeting the statutory definition of an electronic invoice and excludes certain exempt or specially regulated cases. The issuance obligation for domestic B2B electronic invoices is being phased in: businesses may generally continue issuing other invoices during the transition period through 31 December 2026; businesses with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally continue through 31 December 2027. From 1 January 2028, the structured electronic-invoicing requirements apply broadly subject to statutory exceptions. Qualifying formats include standards such as XRechnung and compatible ZUGFeRD versions. The invoice must contain mandatory VAT information and be retained in an auditable electronic form. There is no general government pre-clearance or digital VAT stamp requirement. VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and related filings are generally submitted electronically through ELSTER or other approved electronic interfaces.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement to obtain an inward-remittance certificate for VAT export exemption. The tax authority may deny or reverse the VAT exemption, assess output VAT retrospectively, disallow related input-tax credits, impose interest under the German Fiscal Code, and issue amended assessments where export, transport, customer-status, or reporting evidence is incomplete or unreliable. Intentional or reckless false VAT declarations may constitute tax evasion or negligent tax reduction under the German Fiscal Code and may trigger criminal or administrative sanctions, including fines and imprisonment in serious intentional cases. Incorrect VAT invoicing may create separate liability for tax shown on an invoice under Section 14c UStG. Intra-Community reporting failures may cause loss of exemption until corrected and may result in administrative penalties. Fraudulent refund claims can require repayment of the refunded amount, statutory interest, enforcement costs, and additional criminal or administrative penalties. Customs violations may also trigger customs-duty reassessment, import VAT claims, seizure, and sanctions under applicable Union Customs Code and German customs law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the euro applies under EU free movement of capital principles (TFEU Article 63). Germany imposes no central-bank approval or licensing for inbound share capital, trade payments, dividends, or profit repatriation. The only mandatory obligation is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV Section 68 for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500. Profit remittances by a UG to non-resident shareholders are subject to the statutory German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (including solidarity surcharge), with treaty relief available upon prior filing of an exemption/relief application with the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern; no FX conversion, surrender, or separate repatriation approval barrier exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation timeline for export receipts. Export revenues may be held in EUR or foreign currency in any German or EEA account without central-bank permission. Administrative compliance obligations: (i) every cross-border payment received from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) by the clearing bank not later than 30 days after the end of the reporting month; (ii) physical exports require a customs export declaration (ATLAS) and statistical value reporting; (iii) the UG must maintain accurate Verwendungszweck references identifying the underlying commercial transaction for each inbound payment.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank; monetary policy competences shared with the European Central Bank within the Eurosystem). Currency and capital movement supervision is administered jointly by the Bundesbank, the Federal Ministry of Finance, the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-high ancillary viability, low primary-bank viability for a German UG. Foreign EMIs (Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, Qonto, Holvi, Penta via Solaris) offer rapid onboarding and efficient cross-border payout routing; however, German tax authorities and payroll processors generally expect a German IBAN (DE) for VAT refunds, payroll, and probate/court deposits; deposit protection applies only through the EMI's European licence partner and not through the statutory German deposit-insurance scheme when funds sit in a safeguarding account. PayPal and Stripe payout settlement into EMIs is technically possible but operationally suboptimal; the prudent treasury structure is a domestic German corporate account as the primary concentration account with foreign EMIs used solely for inbound collections from non-EEA marketplaces. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing of the shareholder capital contribution (Einlage) requires an inbound SWIFT MT103 message originating from the bank account of the legal shareholder, not from a third party. The payment reference (Tag 70) must clearly state 'Kapitaleinlage gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag' and cite the notarial deed number and share register entry. The German receiving bank will assign the transaction to the Bundesbank balance-of-payments code for direct-investment equity inflows (AWV Z5/Zahlungsbilanzstatistik); no prior regulatory approval is required. Verification bundle: notarial deed of formation, shareholder resolution, the shareholder-account credit advice, and confirmation from the management board (Geschäftsführung) that the contribution is unconditionally at the disposal of the UG. For inward remittances exceeding EUR 12,500, the bank will pre-clear the purpose code and may require the underlying share-subscription document before final value dating.Trade settlements for carbon-fibre materials, motors, and drone subassemblies are executed through: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for routine supplier invoices under EUR 50,000, referencing the import contract and invoice number and carrying the standard AWV payments-reporting obligation; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via MT700 for larger purchases, with presentation under UCP 600 through the supplier's advising bank in China, financed on 90-day usance terms, with EUR or USD billing and FX cover matched through a forward; (iii) PayPal and Stripe payout rails: platform settlement is batched in EUR and credited via SEPA to the German operational account; treasury policy requires a daily automated sweep of platform balances exceeding EUR 10,000 into the primary bank to minimise safeguarding-balance credit risk (funds held by PayPal/Stripe are not German statutory deposits).Outright EUR/CNY and EUR/USD forward contracts executed under a standard German Devisen-Rahmenvertrag (DRV 1995) or ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, limited to the UG's documented commercial exposure.FX swaps (Devisenswap) for rolling near-dated import payments without balance-sheet FX risk.Cross-currency swaps (Zinsswaps in fremder Währung) matched to long-term supplier financing or LC usance exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing EUR/CNY and EUR/USD when offshore interbank liquidity is constrained, subject to the underlying trade invoice and classification of the UG as a non-financial counterparty (NFC-) under EMIR with exemption from mandatory clearing below the clearing threshold.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-high ancillary viability, low primary-bank viability for a German UG. Foreign EMIs (Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, Qonto, Holvi, Penta via Solaris) offer rapid onboarding and efficient cross-border payout routing; however, German tax authorities and payroll processors generally expect a German IBAN (DE) for VAT refunds, payroll, and probate/court deposits; deposit protection applies only through the EMI's European licence partner and not through the statutory German deposit-insurance scheme when funds sit in a safeguarding account. PayPal and Stripe payout settlement into EMIs is technically possible but operationally suboptimal; the prudent treasury structure is a domestic German corporate account as the primary concentration account with foreign EMIs used solely for inbound collections from non-EEA marketplaces. The UG must maintain one account in its own name in Germany to evidence the statutory contribution of the Stammkapital and to satisfy notarial certification requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Koerperschaftsteuer corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT liability, yielding 15.825% before municipal trade tax; adding Gewerbesteuer at typical municipal rates of 14% to 17% produces a combined effective income tax burden of approximately 29.8% to 32.8%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, giving an aggregate statutory dividend withholding burden of 26.375%. This withholding is imposed on dividends distributed to non-resident individual and corporate shareholders unless a tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive provides for a lower withholding rate or exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German local file and master file documentation duties arise from sections 90(3) and 90(4) AO and the Gewinnabgrenzungsaufzeichnungsverordnung. Administrative de-minimis materiality thresholds under the German Verrechnungspreisdokumentation rules apply approximately at EUR 1.85 million for cross-border related-party goods transactions and EUR 500,000 for related-party services transactions; transactions involving hard-to-value intangibles must be documented regardless of volume. Upon a tax audit, the UG must produce local file documentation within 30 days and master file documentation within 60 days of request.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations as found in zero-tax jurisdictions; instead, German law enforces substance through the general anti-abuse rule under section 42 AO, the controlled-foreign-company rules under sections 7 to 13 AStG, the beneficial-ownership requirements of German tax treaties, and the OECD/G20 BEPS minimum standard on treaty shopping. The UG must maintain real premises, a bank account, a registered office, employees, local management decisions, and actual operational functions in Germany. A letter-box UG interposed mainly for treaty benefits is a fatal substance risk and may be disregarded, with income reattributed to the foreign owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years under section 370 AO, raised to between 6 months and 10 years in particularly severe cases; fines under sections 38 to 43 StGB; mandatory repayment of evaded taxes; late payment interest of 0.5% per month under section 240 AO; late filing surcharges under section 152 AO; confiscation of proceeds; publication of criminal judgments in serious cases.

Director personal liability: The managing director personally commits the offence when signing the false return; personal criminal conviction may be combined with travel bans, EU arrest warrant exposure, professional disqualification, disgorgement of all private benefits, and personal liability under sections 34 and 69 AO for unpaid corporate taxes caused by intentional misconduct.
Withholding Tax Default on Direct or Constructive Dividends

Statutory sanction: The UG is liable for the unwithheld Kapitalertragsteuer; the tax office assesses the full 26.375% withholding against the company even though the funds were paid to the shareholder. Interest accrues at 0.5% per month under section 240 AO; administrative late payment penalties may be imposed; deliberate non-collection is prosecutable under section 370 AO and the company may be subject to criminal fines under section 30 OWiG.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under sections 34 and 69 AO for taxes that should have been withheld and remitted, regardless of the UG's limited liability; private assets are exposed to attachment if payroll, dividend withholding tax, or royalty withholding tax is intentionally not transferred; the director may additionally face criminal prosecution and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Artificial Profit Migration

Statutory sanction: The tax office may estimate taxable income and recharacterize transactions under section 162 AO; TP documentation penalties range from 5% to 10% of the adjusted income increase, with a minimum penalty of EUR 5,000; additional interest under section 240 AO; if the arrangement is regarded as abusive under section 42 AO, treaty benefits may be denied and all hidden profit distributions are subject to withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge.

Director personal liability: A managing director who signs knowingly false TP documentation or who directs payments to entities without economic substance risks personal criminal liability for accessory tax evasion and, where tax has been withheld but not paid, personal liability under section 69 AO; repeated offending may lead to managing-director disqualification proceedings and cross-border enforcement measures.
Money Laundering and Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under sections 56 and 57 GwG up to EUR 5 million or, for legal persons, up to 10% of annual worldwide turnover; criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years under section 261 StGB for intentional money laundering; confiscation of funds and drones; freezing orders; permanent termination by payment providers; and public enforcement notices.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are criminally liable if they suspected the unlawful origin of funds and continued processing; civil liability may arise under section 823(2) BGB in conjunction with section 261 StGB; personal travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed during criminal proceedings; if the UG becomes insolvent due to non-payment of taxes or engaging in illicit transfers, the director may face personal liability for failing to file for insolvency under section 15a InsO.
Shell Company / Inadequate Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may disregard the entity under sections 42 and 41 AO, reattribute income to the actual beneficial owner, deny treaty relief, and impose late interest and withholding taxes; commercial register and insolvency authorities may institute de-registration proceedings; fines under OWiG for breaches of disclosure duties are possible.

Director personal liability: If the corporate veil is pierced, the director can be held personally liable for all taxes and debts caused by intentionally undercapitalized USB structure; personal liability also arises for payments made to shareholders after the company was insolvent if those payments are treated as repayment of capital; the director may be banned from acting as a managing director in Germany and may be subject to extradition or travel restrictions in related criminal proceedings.
