Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal: Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for the EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.); operational: locally competent Finanzamt (Tax Office) at the registered seat of the UG; national electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1) After notarial incorporation, the managing director (Geschäftsführer) submits the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER within 4 weeks of formation, disclosing shareholders, business scope and forecast turnover. 2) The Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) assessments and sets quarterly prepayments (Vorauszahlungen) based on forecast taxable income. 3) For VAT, the UG is registered within the same Finanzamt and receives a local VAT reference (Umsatzsteuernummer); in parallel, an application is made to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. under § 27a UStG for EU invoice and reverse-charge purposes; BZSt verifies economic activity before VIES activation. 4) The UG must notify the Finanzamt of any changes to the questionnaire within one month; incorrect or incomplete statements create administrative fine risk under § 378 AO and can trigger personal liability of the managing director under §§ 34, 69 AO.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in German tax law; a UG is inherently opaque. Available elections: 1) Organschaft (tax group): with a controlling shareholder and a written control-and-profit-transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), the UG's profits can be consolidated into a parent for CIT and trade tax (§ 14 KStG, § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG), and the parent can become the VAT taxable person (§ 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG). 2) A single-member UG may validly conclude an Organschaft with an individual entrepreneur as parent for VAT and trade tax; for CIT the parent must be a corporation. 3) Under § 1a KStG, an eligible non-resident corporation subject to non-crediting foreign tax may opt for fictional German tax residence in inbound scenarios (not applicable to the domestic UG). 4) On a corporate restructuring, the book-value rollover election under § 20 UmwStG permits tax-neutral conversion of an interest in a partnership into a UG; the reverse is not available. 5) Hybrid mismatch treatment of intra-group financing follows the mandatory ATAD rules (§ 4k EStG); no elective re-characterization is possible beyond accurate reporting in the annual tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies to domestic entrepreneurs; any business activity triggers VAT registration. The small-business exemption under § 19 UStG is optional and available only if prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and forecast current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000 (thresholds as amended with effect from 1 January 2025). Foreign businesses performing taxable supplies in Germany must register without threshold, whereas the EU distance-sales/OSS small consignment threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU are VAT-exempt (0%) under §§ 4 Nr. 1a, 6 UStG if the goods leave the EU through a customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and the exporter holds documentary proof of the transport event. Ancillary services to such an export (Ausfuhrnebenleistungen) under § 6 Abs. 1 Satz 2 UStG qualify for 0% only if they are in direct connection with and demonstrably necessary for an identified export transaction and the recipient participates in that transaction. For a marine insurance surveyor: (i) if the survey of export cargo is a contractual condition of the export insurance/transport and is covered by the exporter's export documents, 0% is available; (ii) if the recipient is a foreign insurer exercising an independent establishment abroad, the B2B place-of-supply rule under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG displaces German VAT entirely (reverse charge for EU recipients, non-taxation for third-country recipients) and no export zero-rating documentation is needed, only proof of business status; (iii) surveys of goods located abroad performed for a German-established insurer are taxable at 19% in Germany and cannot be zero-rated. In all cases, the UG must hold written evidence, and a false zero-rating claim is treated as non-payment of VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / Abgangserklärung via ATLAS) with Movement Reference Number and official export confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung)
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) or survey service contract specifying the direct link to the export transaction
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) / Air Waybill (air) / CMR international consignment note (road) with third-country customs crossing evidence
  • Copy of the exporter's instructions, freight contract, and inland/on-carriage documentation evidencing the actual movement out of the EU
  • Payment receipt / inward remittance certificate (Zahlungsbeleg) evidencing settlement by the foreign counterparty from a foreign bank account
  • VIES confirmation or foreign tax registration certificate proving the recipient's business status for B2B non-taxable services
  • Signed survey report linking the inspection to the marine cargo insurance policy and the export shipment

Germany operates no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, and no digital stamping regime. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing under the E-Rechnungsverordnung: from 1 January 2025, every domestic entrepreneur must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X); from 1 January 2027, suppliers with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue such e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, the issue obligation extends to all domestic B2B supplies. Transmission is via EDI or secure email with embedded XML; there is no central clearance API or fiscal stamp. VAT returns and the EU recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) are filed electronically via ELSTER respecting the 25th day deadline; intra-community reverse-charge items must be declared under § 18b UStG.

German tax authorities scrutinize export zero-rating through ATLAS data matching, Eurofisc alerts, and on-site examination of transport and payment files. If the UG cannot produce the mandatory documentation within the statutory six-year retention period under § 147 AO, or the documents are forged, the 0% treatment is reversed and VAT is assessed at 19% retroactively, plus interest of 0.5% per month under § 233a AO (6% p.a.) running from the statutory assessment start (normally 15 months after the calendar year of supply); the limitation period for assessment in fraud cases extends to 10 years under § 169 Abs. 2 Satz 2 AO. Deliberately or negligently causing a VAT shortfall triggers criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO (up to 5 years' imprisonment, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases), confiscation orders, and managerial joint liability under §§ 34, 69 AO where the director failed to secure payment of the unassessed VAT despite insolvency of the UG; additionally, § 25f UStG imposes liability on any person exploiting a misused VAT identification number for fraud. Germany imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export VAT purposes; the 180-day benchmark reflects market tolerance before the Finanzamt will question the reality and arm's-length character of the cross-border supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a full-convertibility regime. Article 63 TFEU and Section 1(1) AWV guarantee unrestricted free movement of capital and payments across both current and capital accounts. No central bank approval, exchange control permit or conversion mandate applies to inbound investment, dividend remittance, profit repatriation, loan repayment or transfer of liquidation proceeds. The only frictions are statistical and documentary: (i) every cross-border payment by a German resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank; (ii) month-end foreign receivables and payables of a German non-bank exceeding EUR 5 million must be reported; (iii) dividend and interest remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject to German withholding tax (26.375% including solidarity surcharge), reducible under the Germany-UAE DTT or applicable double-tax treaties by filing a withholding tax relief application; no foreign-exchange clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory repatriation deadline and no forced conversion ratio for export proceeds or receipts from marine survey, inspection and claims services. Invoices may be denominated in EUR, USD, GBP or any freely convertible currency and may be retained in foreign-currency accounts at German or foreign banks. The sole statutory requirement is statistical reporting: any single payment received from a non-resident underwriter exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank no later than the 7th working day after the end of the reporting month, citing the debtor and the reference number of the underlying invoice (Section 67 AWV). Underlying trade and payment documents must be retained for 6 years.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) jointly administer the German foreign exchange and cross-border payment statistics regime under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung/AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banks and licensed financial institutions executing FX, trade finance and payment services but does not issue foreign exchange licenses or approvals.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the capital injection as a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the UG's German account, with the Remittance Information field containing 'Gesellschaftereinlage' (shareholder contribution) or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' (shareholder loan) and quoting the UG's HRB registration number. No German exchange control approval is required, but the UG must: (i) deliver the signed shareholder resolution or loan agreement and source-of-funds evidence to the German bank before the transfer; (ii) ensure the inbound payment carries a UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference); and (iii) file the Deutsche Bundesbank payment statistics report if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, classifying the flow as direct investment or shareholder loan. A formal share capital increase additionally requires notarization and commercial-register registration; otherwise the injection should be documented as a shareholder loan to prevent hidden-profit-distribution reclassification.For cross-border non-EUR disbursements to overseas surveyors, salvage yards, expert witnesses and legal recovery agents, use SWIFT MT103 with structured invoice references and UETR; for EUR payments inside the EU/EEA use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT). For underwriter-funded claim settlements where the UG presents survey evidence, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) may be advised in favour of the UG with the survey or certified loss report as a required document, eliminating collection risk; for the UG's own onward settlements, use SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for third-party beneficiary transfers) against signed loss-adjustment reports. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are acceptable for high-value disbursements without open-account terms.
