Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany grants full and unrestricted convertibility for current account (trade in goods and services, royalties, software licenses) and capital account (equity injections, intragroup loans, dividends, liquidation proceeds) transactions, consistent with IMF Article VIII status. Non-resident shareholders may remit profits and repay shareholder loans freely without exchange-control approval, clearance certificates, or profit-repatriation quotas. The only fiscal friction is the automatic withholding of 26.375% Kapitalertragsteuer (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) on dividend distributions, reducible to 0-15% under an applicable double taxation treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or a refund claim filed with the German Federal Central Tax Office. All cross-border payments with a countervalue exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV by the 7th day of the following month; this is a statistical duty, not a permission gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany abolished export-proceeds surrender, compulsory conversion and repatriation time limits in 1958. The UG may retain 100% of export proceeds in any invoiced currency, hold foreign currency accounts without restriction, convert into EUR at any time and transfer funds abroad at free disposal. Purely statistical duties apply: (i) every export receipt from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 requires an AWV payment report to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the 7th day of the following month; (ii) annual or quarterly external-position reports are required when foreign receivables/liabilities exceed EUR 5,000,000 per quarter. No mandatory conversion ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank; operates the German T2 RTGS settlement component, compiles external-sector statistics and receives all AWV payment reports) in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), which licenses and supervises credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions. Exchange-control powers are abolished for Germany under Art. 63 TFEU and section 4 of the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG); the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) steers foreign trade policy. The regime is statistical and reporting-based only, not approval-based.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment rail; Low as the sole or primary banking relationship. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, Penta, Qonto and N26 Business may passport into Germany under PSD2 and the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG), providing virtual IBANs, SEPA/SWIFT settlement and commercial cards. Binding constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits, issue letters of credit or bank guarantees, grant material credit, or offer derivative products; client funds are safeguarded under ZAG section 16 through segregation or insurance, but are not protected by the statutory deposit-guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz). Non-EEA offshore EMIs (UK, Hong Kong or Singapore entities without a German or EU license) have no passporting right and may route only through a licensed partner bank, creating settlement risk and likely BaFin objections. Recommended architecture: maintain a Tier-1 German relationship bank as the cash-management core; use an EMI solely for low-value e-commerce marketplace payouts (Stripe, Amazon, PayPal balance concentration) and platform receivables containment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol for equity and debt injections: (1) Payment instruction - the non-resident shareholder executes an SWIFT MT103 (USD/CNH) or, if remitting in EUR, a SEPA Credit Transfer to the UG's account at its German house bank; field 70 (Remittance Information) must cite the notarized resolution text, e.g., 'Einlage gemaess Gesellschafterbeschluss vom [date]' or, for loans, 'Darlehen gemaess Darlehensvertrag vom [date]'; the ISO 20022 Category Purpose for company capital flows is 'CTCA'. (2) Bank-side verification - the bank collects the notarized resolution, updated shareholder list, the executed loan agreement where applicable, and a current Handelsregister excerpt; a capital increase exceeding the registered share capital triggers notarial re-registration with the commercial court. (3) AWV statistical filing - every inbound payment above EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the 7th day of the following month under the capital-account code; no prior authorization or central-bank clearance is ever required. (4) Tax consequences - equity and loan principal inflows are exempt from German withholding tax; interest on the shareholder loan remains deductible at arm's length; dividends distributed later are subject to the 26.375% withholding tax mitigated by treaty or EU directive relief.Two procurement corridors are supported. Corridor 1 - EEA component subcontractors: SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR, settled via T2 RTGS with intraday finality, referencing the purchase order number; no FX conversion and no withholding friction. Corridor 2 - PRC and Hong Kong production suppliers: (i) SWIFT MT103 via SWIFT GPI for USD/CNH tooling and work-order payments, with the UG's EORI number and commercial invoice reference in field 70; or (ii) irrevocable Documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 by Commerzbank or HypoVereinsbank in favor of the Chinese manufacturer, with a confirming Chinese correspondent bank; typical tenor 30-90 days after bill-of-lading date, covering 80-90% of the contract value, while the balance is released against inspection certificates. For urgent repeat orders, documents-against-payment (D/P) collection is acceptable only when combined with export credit insurance. Every outbound payment above EUR 12,500 is subject to the AWV monthly report to the Deutsche Bundesbank; documentation must reconcile the LC number or invoice number with customs declarations and goods-receipt records.EUR/USD Deliverable FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschaefte) - OTC with the German house bank, maturities up to 12 months; used to fix the EUR cost of quarterly USD component imports; no mandatory central clearing because the UG, as a non-financial counterparty, remains below the EMIR clearing threshold for FX (FX is generally excluded from the mandatory clearing obligation).EUR/CNH Deliverable FX Forwards - standard hedge for CNY-denominated letters of credit and tooling invoices; quoted against offshore CNH; restricted contractually to underlying import exposure to satisfy the bank's suitability review.FX Swaps (Devisenswaps) - simultaneous spot and forward legs used to roll over temporary USD working-capital balances ahead of LC maturities, reducing funding cost without creating an open currency position.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CIRS) - EUR/USD or EUR/CNH fixed-to-floating swaps for long-term USD shareholder loans or sales finance receivables; executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex.Purchased Currency Options (EUR Call/USD Put and EUR Call/CNH Put) - premium-only instruments that cap procurement-side currency appreciation without margin calls; recommended as a secondary hedge for competitively tendered imports.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - permissible only for non-deliverable emerging-market currencies in the UG's inbound-sourcing corridor (INR, KRW, TWD, BRL); EUR/USD and EUR/CNH are deliverable pairs and are therefore not eligible for NDF-only treatment in Germany.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment rail; Low as the sole or primary banking relationship. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, Penta, Qonto and N26 Business may passport into Germany under PSD2 and the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG), providing virtual IBANs, SEPA/SWIFT settlement and commercial cards. Binding constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits, issue letters of credit or bank guarantees, grant material credit, or offer derivative products; client funds are safeguarded under ZAG section 16 through segregation or insurance, but are not protected by the statutory deposit-guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz). Non-EEA offshore EMIs (UK, Hong Kong or Singapore entities without a German or EU license) have no passporting right and may route only through a licensed partner bank, creating settlement risk and likely BaFin objections. Recommended architecture: maintain a Tier-1 German relationship bank as the cash-management core; use an EMI solely for low-value e-commerce marketplace payouts (Stripe, Amazon, PayPal balance concentration) and platform receivables containment.
