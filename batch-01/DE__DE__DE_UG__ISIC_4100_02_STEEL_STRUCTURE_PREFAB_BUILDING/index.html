Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister Abteilung B) kept by the Registry Court (Registergericht) at the Local Court (Amtsgericht) in the district of the registered office; electronic filing is transmitted exclusively by a notary via the secure electronic notarial mailbox (beA) through the Federal Chamber of Notaries portal (handelsregister.de); the beneficial-owner filing in the Transparency Register (Transparenzregister) follows the registration.

Governing statute

GmbH-Gesetz (GmbHG) as amended - core formation provisions: §§ 1-8 (formation, Articles of Association, registration application), § 5a (UG (haftungsbeschränkt), no legal minimum capital, mandatory 25% statutory reserve), §§ 5, 7, 8(1), 8(3) (share capital, cash payment, statutory declaration), § 35 (managing director's representation); Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 1-8, 29, 33-34, 39b (merchant status, Commercial Register, e-register notices under the MoPeG framework effective 2024); Notarization Act (BeurkG) § 8; Trade Regulation Act (GewO) §§ 14, 14a.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified incorporation deed (Gründungsprotokoll) containing the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), together with the electronic application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister) signed by the managing directors; for an elementary UG, the statutory model record (Musterprotokoll) under § 2(1a) GmbHG may be used.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG (haftungsbeschränkt) has no statutory minimum capital; share capital of EUR 1 and above is admissible, and every share participation (Geschäftsanteil) must be at least EUR 1. In-kind contributions (Sacheinlagen) are prohibited for a UG (§ 5a(2) GmbHG); therefore the full subscribed nominal capital must be contributed in cash and be at the free disposal of the managing directors prior to the registration filing (§§ 7(2), 8(3) GmbHG). Payment is made into the German business bank account or the notary's escrow account (Notar-Anderkonto), and the managing director confirms this to the registry court by statutory declaration. The company must retain 25% of its annual net profit in a statutory reserve until the share capital plus the reserve reaches EUR 25,000 (the threshold of a full GmbH).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – central VAT ID office; local Finanzamt for income tax registration; ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) for electronic filings.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first obtain a local tax number (Steuernummer) from the responsible Finanzamt by filing the registration form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Ermittlungsbogen) via ELSTER or paper. Then, for cross-border intra-Community or international transactions, apply for a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) from BZSt via Form USt 1 TU, providing proof of the foreign entity's identity, trade register extract, and evidence of intended taxable supplies in Germany.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must first obtain a local tax number (Steuernummer) from the responsible Finanzamt by filing the registration form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Ermittlungsbogen) via ELSTER or paper. Then, for cross-border intra-Community or international transactions, apply for a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) from BZSt via Form USt 1 TU, providing proof of the foreign entity's identity, trade register extract, and evidence of intended taxable supplies in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not apply IRS-style check-the-box elections. A UG is treated as a corporation with full entity-level taxation (Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer). Available elections include formation as UG with deferred minimum capital, corporate group fiscal unity (Organschaft) for VAT and income tax purposes, and thin-capitalization or hybrid mismatch elections limited by the German Interest Barrier (Zinsschranke) and ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 annual turnover for small business exemption (Kleinunternehmer); for foreign supplies and distance sales, registration is mandatory without a turnover threshold when taxable supplies are made in Germany (case-by-case determination of nexus).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export supply under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG where goods are physically dispatched or transported from Germany to a third-country destination, or to a free zone, and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export, including customs export confirmation, transport documents, and proof of receipt in the destination country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhr- und Abmeldeanmeldung - ATLAS) with export confirmation (Ausfuhrbegleitschein/Ausgangsvermerk)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR Waybill evidencing physical transport to a non-EU country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving receipt of export proceeds in EUR or convertible currency

Germany enforces a mandatory B2B e-invoicing obligation (XInvoice/ZUGFeRD) from 2025 for domestic and cross-border transactions; a real-time fiscal clearance or digital tax stamp system is not applicable to ordinary prefabricated steel structure exports. VAT returns must be transmitted electronically through ELSTER.

Fraudulent or unjustified zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT clawback, interest at 6% per annum (§ 233a AO), penalties of up to EUR 25,000 or imprisonment for tax evasion (§ 370 AO), and damage to the UG's standing with the BZSt and customs authorities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a member of the euro area, maintains full current and capital account convertibility under EU primary and secondary law. There is no general exchange control, no prior licence requirement for cross-border payments, and no differential treatment between resident and non-resident investors. Profit remittance is subject only to standard banking verification, corporate treasury documentation, and tax procedures: the German paying agent is obliged to apply dividend withholding tax at 25% plus solidarity surcharge where applicable, reduced or eliminated under the parent-subsidiary directive or an applicable double-tax treaty only if a valid exemption certificate and beneficial ownership declaration are presented before payment. Practical hurdles are therefore not regulatory convertibility barriers but rather banking compliance, anti-money-laundering source-of-funds evidence, tax residency documentation, and, where relevant, transfer-pricing and substance requirements under the German stewardship regulation (HGB accounting and CFC rules).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not apply any export proceeds surrender obligation. Exporters have free disposal over all foreign currency receipts and are not required to convert them into euro at the central bank or any domestic bank. There is no statutory conversion ratio or timeline for repatriation. However, under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV), residents must report to the Deutsche Bundesbank any payment transaction with non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500, including both receipts and payments, within seven days in electronic form via the Bundesbank's AWV reporting portal. Export invoices and underlying contracts must be retained to support the reported transaction.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank), operating in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem, and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and anti-money laundering supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Feasibility rating: LIMITED. Non-German offshore digital EMIs generally do not have authorization to provide their own German IBANs or operate as principal account providers inside Germany without BaFin authorization or a valid passport under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). EEA-authorized EMIs may lawfully passport into Germany only after submitting a required regulatory notification to BaFin, after which limited payment account and FX services may be offered; post-Brexit UK EMIs cannot passport into the EU and must establish a substantive presence or cooperate with an EU-authorized partner. Offshore EMIs not licensed in EEA or not holding a German license cannot legally open a German corporate account with a German IBAN, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and are not accepted as a main treasury clearing rail for a capital-paying GmbH/UG. They may be used only as a secondary receivables collection tool after contract-specific legal review, with all key liquidity and LC flows reserved for a German tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loan contributions must be executed via the UG's German bank account in EUR or permitted foreign currencies. For EEA-origin EUR funds, use the SEPA Credit Transfer with the beneficiary's prevailing IBAN and include a purpose reference using the following convention: 'VWZ: CAPITAL INCREASE UG / RECHNUNG [Invoice #] / KAPITALERHOEHUNG'. For cross-border non-SEPA wires, use SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary bank's BIC, the contractual reference in field 70, and the Deutsche Bundesbank-compliant purpose code for direct investment equity (e.g., 'EQUI') where supported by the sending bank. Intermediary bank routing must state no suspense account and quote the German beneficiary bank's correspondent bank to avoid delay. Mandatory verification pack: share subscription agreement, notarized resolution on capital increase, Transparenzregister filing proof, updated commercial register extract, UBO declaration, confirmation of final beneficial owner, passport copies and source-of-funds documents. For shareholder loans, an executed loan agreement and intercompany loan terms are mandatory; the bank will normally require a commitment that loans be subordinated to senior credit if trade finance facilities are later requested.Trade settlements for EPC steel structure contracts should flow through SWIFT MT103 for simple supplier and subcontractor payments; for documentary trades, the German bank should issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit using SWIFT MT700, which then settles through the SWIFT messaging and reimbursement pipeline (MT754 advice and MT202/MT202COV cover payments). The UG can additionally use documentary collections as a low-cost clearing rail for established buyer relationships. Every outbound disbursement must carry the underlying commercial invoice and purchase order, customs declaration where goods cross borders, and the AWV reporting threshold must be observed for every payment over EUR 12,500. For amounts above EUR 50,000, German banks will expect immediate identification of the payee and confirmation of any mandatory withholding tax, particularly for interest, royalties, or management fees paid to non-resident related parties.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forward contracts traded with the German account bank or an onshore EU credit institution, matched to a specific steel structure procurement or receivables exposure and documented in a mirroring ISO 20022 confirmation.FX swaps (spot-forward and forward-forward) used to manage temporary currency gaps in the UG's underlying trade settlement cycles.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) permissible only if matched to a committed bank loan or project finance liability and documented under EMIR margining rules.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in currencies with settlement restrictions, such as CNH or INR, permissible solely when backed by a binding underlying construction or supply contract and only with a German or EU licensed counterparty subject to BaFin supervision.Depthless vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars where offered by the bank under a written trade authorization, provided the underlying steel-structure contract amount and term are expressly linked in the hedging documentation and no speculative intent is declared.
