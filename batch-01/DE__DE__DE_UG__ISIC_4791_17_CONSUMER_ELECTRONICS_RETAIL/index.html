Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

German Commercial Register (Handelsregister), accessible through the joint register portal of the German federal states (Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder); the Unternehmensregister provides centralized company information. Name suitability is assessed by the competent local register court, commonly after consultation with the local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (Industrie- und Handelskammer—IHK).

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no uniform nationwide statutory name-reservation period for a UG. Before notarisation, request a company-name assessment from the competent local IHK and confirm availability with the local register court through the notary; an IHK assessment is advisory and does not guarantee registration or protect trademark rights. If the register court rejects the name, obtain the written decision and reasons, revise the name or submit supporting evidence, and pursue the applicable register-court remedy (Beschwerde) under the FamFG within the period stated in the decision, commonly one month where a time limit applies. The notary should confirm the correct remedy, competent court, and deadline in the particular case.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Local Amtsgericht (Registergericht) in the district of the company's registered seat; Commercial Register (Handelsregister) is maintained by the registry court.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG) §§ 1, 5(2), 7(2), 8, 10, 35, 39, 46; Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 8-10, 39-40 for commercial register filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized incorporation deed (Gesellschaftsvertrag/Satzung) together with the application for registration (Anmeldung) submitted to the Commercial Register (Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG may be formed with minimum issued share capital of EUR 1.00; the subscribed capital must be paid in full in cash before registration and irrevocably placed at the free disposal of the managing directors. The UG is subject to the mandatory reserve rule: 25% of annual net profit must be retained until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000. No escrow account is required for cash formations under § 7(2) GmbHG.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for commercial office and storage premises in accordance with state building regulations (Bauordnung); workplace compliance under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung), including fire extinguishers, first-aid equipment, and structural load inspections for storage areas; occupational health clearance for employees under the Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office – and the locally competent Finanzamt; electronic filing via ELSTER (www.elster.de) and the BZSt online VAT registration portal.

E-government filing portal

A German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with its competent Finanzamt within one month after notarial incorporation to obtain the tax registration number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax and trade tax. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately through ELSTER/BZSt; it is mandatory before taxable intra-EU supplies. A non-resident seller without a German seat or permanent establishment must register for German VAT under § 27a UStG via BZSt and obtains a corporate/national TIN for each German registration; such registration is triggered from the first euro of taxable activity in Germany unless the seller elects the EU OSS/IOSS scheme for qualifying distance sales or imports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; a UG is automatically a taxable corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, and Solidaritätszuschlag on retained and distributed profits. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) VAT small-business treatment and the waiver of the exemption under § 19 UStG; (ii) formation of a tax fiscal unity ('Organschaft') for VAT and corporate income tax where a control and profit-and-loss transfer agreement is established and approved; (iii) staggered revaluation/step-up treatments under UmwStG upon qualifying restructuring; (iv) hybrid mismatch and dual-inclusion filings under § 4k EStG and §§ 12-13 AStG to ensure consistency with ATAD, which do not confer pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic German entrepreneurs are VAT-registered for all taxable activities, subject to the small-business exemption in § 19 UStG when prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed €50,000. Foreign/non-established sellers have no de minimis threshold and are registrable from the first euro of German taxable turnover, unless the distance sale of goods is covered by the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS); EU-wide distance sales exceeding the €10,000 threshold are taxed in the destination Member State. The e-commerce electronic interface rules also impose VAT liability on marketplaces for qualifying sales by non-established sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under § 6 UStG applies to tangible personal property (including consumer electronics) physically exported by the vendor or an unrelated purchaser/forwarder from the EU customs territory to a third country, provided export proof is maintained. Intra-Community supplies qualify for exemption where the goods are moved to another EU state in a B2B transaction and the customer's valid VAT ID is evidenced; the supplier may also rely on the business-to-consumer EU distance sales regime via OSS. Digitally supplied B2B services to non-EU customers are outside German VAT territory by reverse charge; B2C digitally supplied services to non-EU consumers are generally not subject to German VAT, requiring adequate records of customer location and usage/consumption.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export completion confirmation
  • Commercial export invoice (Handelsrechnung) identifying seller and consignee, item details, Incoterms, and export value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (Konnossement/Luftfrachtbrief) verifying the goods have left the EU customs territory, for direct or indirect exports
  • Proof of inward remittance or consideration received from the foreign customer, evidencing actual payment inflow; for e-commerce, settlement records of Stripe/PayPal linked to the sale
  • Export book-entry evidence (Buchungsnachweis/Ausfuhrnachweis) and, for intra-Community movements, the purchaser's VAT ID extraction, compliant delivery documents, and periodic EC Sales List reconciliations

Domestic B2B e-invoicing is mandatory in Germany from 1 January 2025: all domestic B2B invoices must be issued and transmitted in a structured electronic XML format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD-compliant), with an issuing obligation fully phased in for turnover above €800,000 by 2027 and for other businesses by 2028, including EDI systems. Germany does not currently require real-time fiscal pre-clearance, a clearance API, or government digital stamping; compliance relies on electronic delivery, GoBD retention of e-records, certified cash registers, and semi-automated audit access to invoice data.

If export documentation is incomplete, non-reconcilable with payment records, or the goods do not demonstrably leave the EU within the statutory period, the Finanzamt will reassess German VAT at 19% and apply clawback interest of 0.5% per month (6% p.a.); administrative penalties range up to the culpability-adjusted tax amount, with a maximum 10-year retroactive assessment in evasion cases. Intentional or reckless false export claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years) or misdemeanors under § 26b UStG; fines and personal liability of the managing director under § 69 AO are imposed; customs-related violations trigger further administrative sanctions under the Customs Administration Act and AWG foreign-trade penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU law (Article 63 TFEU), the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV). There is no exchange-control approval for opening foreign-currency accounts, taking shareholder loans, paying suppliers abroad, or repatriating dividends. Profit remittance hurdles are not exchange controls but corporate-law and tax-law requirements: a German UG may distribute only distributable balance-sheet profit; shareholder resolutions must be notarised where required; and dividends paid to non-residents are subject to 26.375% German withholding burden (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reduced only through an applicable double-taxation treaty after an exemption/refund application. Funds may be freely converted into EUR or foreign currency through any licensed bank or electronic money institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory surrender, no minimum conversion ratio, and no official repatriation timeline for export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in EUR or a foreign-currency account. Under AWV sections 66-67, cross-border payments, including export receipts, must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes once they exceed the statutory threshold (currently EUR 12,500 or the equivalent in another currency, with certain exemptions and direct-bank reporting channels). The notification is normally filed by the receiving bank within five working days following the value date, and the exporting UG must retain underlying invoices, customs declarations and transport documents for at least five years. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank, exercising prudential regulatory reporting and balance-of-payments statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV))

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional Medium. A German UG may use an EEA-licensed electronic money institution or payment institution (e.g., an EMI established in Germany or another EEA member state passported and notified to BaFin) as a secondary settlement or disbursement rail. Such EMIs may hold funds in safeguarded accounts and offer faster cross-border payments or FX conversion. However, they are not credit institutions and cannot serve as the sole treasury account for statutory and tax purposes; statutory share-capital contributions and official filings should remain with a German bank. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are legally prohibited from providing payment accounts or e-money services to German entities without a licence or active passport under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); use of unlicensed non-EEA EMIs triggers unavoidable AML/KYC red flags and should be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be settled through a German IBAN using SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR remittances, or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR or third-country remittances. The remittance-information field (Field 70) must unambiguously identify the transaction as 'KAPITALEINLAGE', 'STAMMKAPITAL', 'GESELLSCHAFTERDARLEHEN' or 'DARLEHEN' together with the loan/contract reference. The UG must provide to the bank the notarised capital increase/shareholder resolution, an updated shareholder list, source-of-funds documentation, UBO declarations, and, where relevant, the signed shareholder loan agreement. No prior foreign-exchange approval is required; however, all inbound credits exceeding the AWV statistical threshold must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank (normally by the receiving bank). Funds are released after the UG is registered in the commercial register and the bank completes the client-due-diligence file.For supplier payments inside the EEA, the UG should use SEPA Credit Transfer (SWIFT MT103 in EUR). The UG may also use open-account settlement under supply contracts supported by cargo insurance and import/customs documentation.
