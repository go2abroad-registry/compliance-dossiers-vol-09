Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Handelsregister (Commercial Register), accessed through the official joint Länder register portal at handelsregister.de; incorporation filings are submitted electronically by a German notary. The Unternehmensregister (unternehmensregister.de) provides company information but is not the incorporation filing portal.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general nationwide statutory company-name reservation period or extension procedure. Obtain an optional local name assessment from the competent IHK and confirm availability with the register court through the notary; any informal reservation practice or duration is local and must be confirmed with that authority. If the register court refuses registration, request the reasoned decision and correct or amend the name, or file a complaint (Beschwerde) under FamFG §§ 58 et seq.; the ordinary complaint period is one month under § 63, subject to the decision's appeal notice and applicable procedural rules. A separate administrative appeal is not the standard remedy.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) may be formed with a nominal share capital between EUR 1 and EUR 24,999. The full subscribed capital must be paid in cash or evidenced as available to the management before registration; no escrow account is statutorily mandated. Until share capital reaches EUR 25,000, one quarter of annual net profits must be retained.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance according to the Land Fire Code and local fire brigade inspection; structural static/load certificate from a licensed structural engineer; workplace safety clearance under the Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG) and Betriebssicherheitsverordnung; electrical installation check by TÜV or an accredited inspection body.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) for VAT IDs and Federal Tax Certificate matters; locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for income taxes, trade tax, payroll tax, and VAT returns; joint electronic portal: Elster (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign and resident entities must file a Registration Questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt at the registered seat or management address. The Finanzamt assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax and trade tax. For VAT identification, the entity must separately file an application under § 27a UStG with BZSt through the Elster portal; BZSt will issue the German VAT ID (USt-IdNr) after identity verification, including the acceptance of non-resident ownership documentation. The UG, once registered with the commercial register, must complete this process within one month of business commencement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign and resident entities must file a Registration Questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt at the registered seat or management address. The Finanzamt assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax and trade tax. For VAT identification, the entity must separately file an application under § 27a UStG with BZSt through the Elster portal; BZSt will issue the German VAT ID (USt-IdNr) after identity verification, including the acceptance of non-resident ownership documentation. The UG, once registered with the commercial register, must complete this process within one month of business commencement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists under German law. The UG is an incorporated legal entity subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax), Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge), and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax). Optional tax elections include Organschaft (fiscal unity) with a controlling parent, lump-sum deduction of business expenses for certain non-resident entities, and thin-capitalization/interest-barrier elections. German anti-hybrid and section 4k EStG rules impose mandatory recharacterization rather than elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General registration requirement applies to any entrepreneur with taxable supplies in Germany. Small-business exemption applies when previous-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and projected turnover in the current year does not exceed EUR 50,000; a German UG is generally excluded from this exemption if its articles or economic substance indicate an organized commercial enterprise, and it may waive the exemption under § 9 UStG. For EU distance sales into Germany, the cross-border OSS threshold of EUR 10,000 triggers German/domestic VAT treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border exports to third countries (outside the EU) qualify for tax-free treatment as an exempt supply with input VAT deduction (equivalent to zero-rating) under § 4(1)(1) UStG if the goods are physically exported by the supplier or a commissioned carrier to a third-country destination within the statutory time limits. Intra-Community supplies (deliveries to VAT-registered customers in another EU Member State) are likewise tax-free under § 4(1)(1)(b) UStG subject to proof that the goods left German territory and the purchaser's valid VAT ID. For services connected with temporary export processing and certain offshore industrial services, separate export-related exemptions may apply; all exemptions require documentary proof in accordance with §§ 8-17 UStDV.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung, confirmed by customs)
  • Transport documentation evidencing the exit of goods from Germany/from the EU (Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, air waybill, or rail consignment note)
  • Commercial sales invoice and underlying sales/purchase contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/payment documentation evidencing the receipt of payment from the foreign customer
  • Buyer's VAT identification number declaration (for intra-EU supplies) and recapitulative statement confirmation

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API-based stamping, or national digital-stamping regime. Since 1 January 2025, all B2B domestic German supplies and domestic non-business supplies generally require structured electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) using standards such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD, transmitted in a machine-readable format; paper or PDF-only invoices remain permissible for certain limited B2C and exempt scenarios. B2G invoices must comply with EN 16931 and be received via the Federal government's e-invoicing infrastructure. Records must be retained for 10 years and exportable to the tax authority upon VAT audit.

The tax authority may retrospectively cancel zero-rating if documentary proof is incomplete, post-export audit deficiencies are found, or the payment and transport evidence contradict the declared export. This triggers reassessment of VAT at 19%, plus interest at 0.5% per month under § 233a AO. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary fine; administrative penalties of up to EUR 50,000 may be imposed under §§ 378, 382 AO. The responsible accountant, managing director, or UG shareholder can be held personally liable for the unpaid VAT and penalties. Customs audit records must be stored according to the 12-year retention requirement under § 147 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible. Germany generally permits current-account and capital-account transactions, including foreign investment, capital contributions, loans, dividends, and repatriation of sale proceeds, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax rules, corporate-law procedures, and applicable foreign-investment or export-control restrictions. Dividend distributions require distributable profits, proper shareholder approval, and compliance with German corporate law; withholding tax and treaty or EU relief procedures may apply. A UG must allocate one quarter of its annual net profit, after loss carryforwards, to its statutory reserve until the reserve and share capital reach the statutory threshold for conversion to a GmbH. Cross-border payments may be reportable to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes under the AWV; reporting is not a general payment-approval requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory deadline requires exporters to repatriate or surrender foreign-currency proceeds, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign currency or offshore, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, accounting, and applicable reporting requirements. Under current AWV statistical reporting rules, qualifying cross-border payments generally become reportable when the relevant transaction exceeds EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exclusions and current Bundesbank instructions. The German resident required to report must observe the applicable monthly reporting deadline and verify the current classification, threshold, exemptions, and submission procedure with the Bundesbank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's national central bank and administers foreign-trade payment statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The European Central Bank sets monetary policy for the euro area. Germany has no general exchange-control authority requiring prior approval for ordinary cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine payments, subject to the provider's German and EU permissions, risk appetite, and onboarding requirements. An authorized electronic-money institution may issue and redeem e-money and provide permitted payment services, but e-money is not a bank deposit and does not receive statutory deposit-guarantee protection. Confirm the provider's home-state authorization, passporting or German establishment status, safeguarding arrangements, IBAN and payment capabilities, transaction limits, and acceptance for share-capital payment by the notary and commercial register. Use a German credit institution where the formation process or counterparties require a conventional bank account.
