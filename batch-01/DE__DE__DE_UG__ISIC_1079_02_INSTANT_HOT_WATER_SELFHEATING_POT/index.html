Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (UG is an opaque, German-resident Kapitalgesellschaft with unlimited German tax liability on worldwide income; subject to Körperschaftsteuer 15%, Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% of CIT (0.825%) and Gewerbesteuer at the municipal Hebesatz – effective combined burden approx. 29–31%; no pass-through eligibility; distributions to shareholders are subject to 25% capital gains tax/Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge and, for corporate shareholders, 95% exempt under §8b KStG; non-resident shareholders may apply treaty relief under the applicable double tax treaty; no tax-exempt zone applies in Germany).

Available election forms

German tax law contains no 'check-the-box' pass-through election; the UG is a mandatory corporate taxpayer. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT small-business exemption (§19 UStG) if prior-year turnover ≤ €25,000 and current-year turnover ≤ €100,000 – not recommended for exporters because input VAT deduction and export zero-rating are then unavailable; (ii) VAT Organschaft and Corporation/Trade Tax Organschaft (tax group) by executing a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and satisfying financial, economic and organisational integration; (iii) loss carry-back election under §10d EStG (2-year carry-back); (iv) depreciation and fiscal-year elections; (v) binding advance tax ruling ('verbindliche Auskunft', §89 AO) to secure the tax classification of the self-heating meal packaging and supply chain; (vi) hybrid-mismatch and anti-avoidance rules under §4k EStG automatically neutralize double deductions and prevent arbitrage; no hybrid entity election is available for a UG.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital- and current-account convertibility under EU law (Art. 63 TFEU) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG); there are no foreign exchange surrender controls, no currency rationing, and no Central Bank approval for inward capital injections, profit remittances, trade settlements, or dividend distributions; profit remittance is unrestricted subject to standard pre-clearance documentation, corporate income tax on German-source income, and the 26.375% withholding tax on dividends (reduced or eliminated under treaty double-tax agreements, DBA, or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive with refund procedure), while bank compliance must ensure correct AWV statistical reporting rather than exchange control authorization

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Germany; export proceeds in foreign currency may be held freely on account; the sole obligation is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for any inbound/outbound payment or transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 per counterparty per transaction cycle, with the report (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik, form Z4/Z5) due by the 7th day of the month following the receipt; no beneficiary conversion requirement applies to the self-heating meal exporter because the product is a standard manufactured good outside goods-control list annexes

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); German foreign exchange policy is executed through the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) under the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as supplementary treasury rail, Low as sole settlement backbone; German and EU Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) such as Solaris, PPRO, and Airwallex (EU-licensed branch) can offer onboarding speed and cheaper inbound FX conversion, but face legal constraints including no money-creation ability, no statutory German deposit protection (Einlagensicherung) on e-money balances, restricted credit/overdraft capacity, and higher perceived compliance risk for cross-border outbound trade disbursements by dual-use-adjacent food-manufacturing exporters; therefore EMIs must be used only for low-value working-capital flows while Tier-1 commercial banks remain mandatory for LC opening, supplier guarantees, and high-value SWIFT wire settlement

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% aggregated federal rate (Körperschaftsteuer 15.0% + Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% of the corporate income tax = 0.825%). A separate municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) applies at 3.5% of the trade assessment amount multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typical 200%–490%; e.g., Berlin Hebesatz 410% yields 14.35%, Munich 490% yields 17.15%). Trade tax is not deductible for corporate income tax purposes since 2008. Aggregate effective statutory burden for a Berlin UG ≈ 30.175%; for a Munich UG ≈ 32.975%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag), withheld by the paying entity or its paying agent upon dividend distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 7 months after the end of the fiscal year (31 July for calendar-year taxpayers) under § 149(2) AO; where the return is professionally prepared by a tax advisor (Steuerberater), the statutory deadline is automatically extended to the last day of the 14th month after fiscal year-end (i.e., 28/29 February of the second following year) under § 149(3) AO. Administrative relief extensions may apply for specific assessment years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Landesdokumentation) is contemporaneously required if cross-border transactions with any single related party exceed EUR 5,000,000 per annum, or if cross-border intangibles/licensing transactions exceed EUR 500,000 per annum, per BMF Verwaltungsgrundsätze 2019; Master File (Stammdokumentation) is required where the group's consolidated annual turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) filing to BZSt applies where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but imposes strict economic substance requirements through: (i) § 42 AO general anti-abuse rule recharacterizing artificial arrangements; (ii) § 50d(3) EStG denying treaty relief to low-substance letterbox companies; (iii) § 1 AStG transfer-of-functions rules requiring documentation and arm's-length valuation when functions, risks, or assets are shifted; (iv) CFC rules under §§ 7–13 AStG; (v) ATAD implementation (interest limitation § 4h EStG, exit taxation § 4g/6 AStG). The UG must demonstrate real substance: local management decision-making, payroll, premises, operational contracts for the self-heating food production, regulatory licenses, bank accounts in Germany, and adequate accounting substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false disclosure

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, or to 10 years in aggravated cases where the evasion is especially severe or committed with a shell company (§ 370 AO); order of forfeiture/confiscation of the evaded amount; monetary fines concurrent with imprisonment; statutory late payment surcharges at 1% per month; interest at 6% p.a.; exclusion from public tenders.

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable via Haftungsbescheid under §§ 34, 69 AO for unpaid corporate taxes, wage tax, and VAT up to 100% of the claim, plus surcharges; criminal prosecution of the Geschäftsführer; travel bans under § 326 AO, attachment of private assets, and up to 10 years' imprisonment for the individual.
Transfer pricing documentation default

Statutory sanction: Reversal of the taxpayer's transfer prices with upward income adjustments; presumption of a higher taxable income under § 162(3) AO; penalty surcharge of 10% of the upward adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000) where documentation is completely absent, and 5% (minimum EUR 5,000) where documentation is unsuitable; additional 6% interest; possible criminal tax evasion if intentional understatement follows.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign defective tax returns or obstruct TP audits become joint debtors; personal liability for the penalty surcharge and adjusted tax where funds dissipate; professional disqualification as Geschäftsführer and potential fraud charges.
Shell company and economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty withholding relief under § 50d(3) EStG; recharacterization of the UG's structure under § 42 AO with all income reallocated to the beneficial owner; 15.825% royalty withholding and 26.375% dividend withholding imposed at source; excess assessments with 6% interest; potential CFC attribution to German shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot rely on limited liability if the UG is a sham; courts may pierce the corporate veil (Durchgriffshaftung) for restitution of distributions made contrary to the statutory reserve requirement; insolvency of a sham UG triggers § 15b InsO liability for third-party payments; entry in the professional register and prohibition from managing companies.
AML and cross-border payment compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under GwG up to EUR 5,000,000 (or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities); freezing orders; mandatory filing of suspicious transaction reports; forfeiture of assets; loss of the food production license; reputational exclusion from e-commerce payment rails (SWIFT/TT restrictions).

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is the designated AML compliance officer; personal fines up to EUR 1,000,000; criminal liability for money laundering under § 261 StGB (imprisonment up to 10 years); travel bans and seizure of personal assets; in cases of gross negligence, joint liability with the UG for regulatory fines.
