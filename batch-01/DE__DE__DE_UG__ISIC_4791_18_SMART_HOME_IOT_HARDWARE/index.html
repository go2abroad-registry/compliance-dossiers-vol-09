Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG is exempt from the €25,000 minimum capital. The nominal share capital may be as low as €1, but the full agreed cash contribution must be paid in cash into a freely available German business bank account in the company's name before registration. No escrow or locked account is permitted. Until the share capital reaches €25,000, one quarter of the annual net profit must be retained in a statutory reserve.

Funding & unlock path: For any physical premises, obtain the building-occupancy permit or use-change clearance (Nutzungsänderungsgenehmigung) from the municipal Bauamt before operation; carry out fire-safety certification with fire alarm/extinguisher systems, emergency-route signage and structural-load inspection for storage racks; comply with the Arbeitsschutzgesetz (Occupational Safety Act) workplace risk assessment, BetrSichV equipment safety verification, DGUV incident-prevention regulations, and register the employees with the Berufsgenossenschaft Handel und Warenlogistik for statutory accident insurance. Fire and structural load inspections are documented and retained for the local authority.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT administration. Main portal: ELSTER / Mein ELSTER via www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt within 4–6 weeks of incorporation or commencement of business. Step 2: The Finanzamt issues the statutory tax number (Steuernummer) and forwards an application for the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) to BZSt via Form UST 1 or electronic ELSTER submission. Step 3: For foreign-owned UGs, the application may be filed directly with BZSt's VAT-ID portal; the foreign parent or managing director must evidence legal entity formation, German registered office, and business purpose. Step 4: Register for VAT prepayment filing (Voranmeldung) and annual VAT return (Umsatzsteuerjahreserklärung); also register for corporate income tax advance payments, trade tax, and solidarity surcharge; all filings and returns are due electronically through ELSTER.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; UG is a domestic capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to corporate income tax at 15% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, effective 15.825%) and municipal trade tax, with a typical combined aggregate income tax burden of 30–33%; resident UGs are subject to German taxation on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available; the UG is eligible for German fiscal unity (Organschaft) only under strict profit-and-loss transfer agreements.

Available election forms

Germany does not maintain a check-the-box regime; statutory elections include Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income and VAT, small-entrepreneur exemption under Section 19 UStG, and the option to apply input VAT deduction. For hybrid mismatches, German tax law provides elections and adjustments under Section 4k EStG and ATAD implementation rules, including transparent entity election consequences, withholding tax adjustments, and tie-breaker clauses; these mechanisms are limited to controlled hybrid arrangements and must be documented in tax filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The domestic small-entrepreneur exemption applies where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 (2024) / EUR 25,000 (2025) and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000; mandatory VAT registration arises above these thresholds. Non-established entrepreneurs are required to register for VAT irrespective of threshold, while intra-Community distance sales remain covered by the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold for simplification purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferung) of tangible goods to third-country destinations are exempt from German VAT at 0% under Section 6 UStG if the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Germany to a non-EU destination, either directly by the seller or through an export agent. The exemption also applies to certain cross-border supplies to non-EU business customers where the place of supply is outside Germany; offshore services supplied in the B2B sphere are generally treated as supplied at the customer's location under the reverse-charge principle. To claim the 0% rate, the substantive conditions of export and reliable documentary proof of export must be fully satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Ausfuhrbegleitschein / EX A generated through ATLAS/ECS customs clearance)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or CMR transport document evidencing departure from the EU
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis), including the Abnehmer- und Liefernachweis (customer and delivery evidence) where required
  • Evidence of payment, including inward remittance certificate or bank transfer confirmation, if required by the tax office or BZSt for evaluation of export credibility

Electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) is mandatory for B2B transactions from 1 January 2025; all taxable entrepreneurs may receive structured electronic invoices in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x formats; issuance is obligatory from 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all domestic taxable persons. Transmission must follow Peppol infrastructure or other compliant channels; there is no government-operated real-time clearance API, no digital stamping, and no pre-approval requirement; VAT returns are submitted via ELSTER annually or quarterly/monthly as advance returns.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict documentary verification by the Finanzamt and BZSt; missing, false, or unverifiable export evidence results in retrospective VAT assessment under Section 173 AO, interest of 0.5% per month under Section 233a AO, and clawback of input VAT deducted. Intentional or fraudulent presentation of export documentation triggers criminal tax evasion under Section 370 AO with penalties up to EUR 25,000, administrative fines, and imprisonment of up to five years; negligence is punishable under Section 378 AO. The scheme is designed to penalize field-to-crazy recovery; further sanctions include suspension of the exporter's VAT number and liability under the German customs liability rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as part of the eurozone, maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account. There are no exchange controls on profit remittance, dividends, interest, royalties, or intra-group loans. The euro is fully convertible, and settlement in any foreign currency is unrestricted. The only real constraint is the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) statistical reporting obligation to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500, inclusive of direct investment and repat-riation flows; this is a reporting duty, not an authorisation requirement. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to domestic German withholding taxes (e.g., dividend withholding tax at 25% plus solidarity surcharge, reduced by tax treaties; interest withholding tax under certain cases) and the obligation to evidence the underlying commercial or corporate transaction in parallel with the bank remittance order.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding period applies in Germany. Exporters may hold foreign currency freely. However, any inbound payment for exports must be statistically reported to Deutsche Bundesbank via the AWV electronic portal within the statutory deadline (generally by the fifth working day of the month following receipt for paper reports, or via online reporting within the same day if submitted through the bank) when the payment amount exceeds €12,500. The payment code must align with the goods classification; a bank confirmation or keyboard-designed AWV code (e.g., '0010' for export payments for goods) is required on the SWIFT instruction.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) in coordination with BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) for prudential banking regulation; Deutsche Bundesbank is the responsible monetary and balance-of-payments statistics authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and neobanks with valid passporting rights into Germany; LOW for offshore or third-country EMIs. EEA EMI accounts (e.g., entities licensed in Lithuania, the Netherlands, or Ireland and passporting into Germany) can serve as supplementary settlement channels for merchant payouts and payment operations, but they are not accepted as primary treasury clearing rails by most conservative German deposit guarantee schemes. Offshore digital EMIs without a German branch, an ECB/BaFin passport, or a dedicated agent in Germany face a de facto prohibition because they cannot perform deposit-taking or payment services without a German licence. Any digital-balance holding must comply with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), the EU PSD2 and the German Money Laundering Act (GwG); custodial accounts at unlicensed offshore EMIs are legally non-viable and operationally risky.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funding must be routed via regulated bank channels using SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR currencies or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR. The purpose code for equity or shareholder loan should be aligned with the AWV reporting catalogue; common codes are 'Kapitalbeteiligung/Direktinvestition' for equity contributions and 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' for shareholder loans. The bank must receive a copy of the corporate resolution, the shareholder register entry, and the tax confirmation (if any) before the credit is applied. Any direct investment payment above €12,500 must be reported by the UG to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV, using the relevant payment statistics code. Immediate supplier payment via online banking should match exactly the currency and amount of the underlying purchase order; any divergence triggers Articles 575–580 of the German Banking Act (KWG) risk classification. The UG must keep a transaction ledger that links each SWIFT MT103 to the purchase order, customs declaration, and payment confirmation for a statutory period of six years. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany applies the OECD documentation framework under §90 Abs. 3 AO. A Local File is required if the aggregate annual consideration of controlled transactions with non-resident related parties exceeds EUR 5,000,000; a Master File is required where the global consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Even below these thresholds, the UG must create contemporaneous TP documentation sense data for any material cross-border controlled transaction.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to UAE ESR. However, economic substance is a mandatory precondition for treaty eligibility, anti-abuse protection, VAT registration, and the right to deduct foreign taxes under §50d Abs. 3 EStG and the Principal Purpose Test (OECD BEPS). A UG retailing smart-home IoT hardware must maintain a physical office, concluded contracts, inventory decision processes, bookkeeping in Germany, an actual managing director resident in Germany (or a competent non-resident manager unless treaty interferes), and employment or service records for any outsourced activity. Shell companies (Briefkastenfirmen) without such substance are disregarded or treated as transparent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unterdeckung of turnover

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under §370 AO: up to 5 years imprisonment (or 10 years in aggravated cases) plus fine at the court's discretion; assessment of additional tax, late payment interest (6% per annum), and a 5% reduction in tax for late payment; criminal forfeiture of the tax savings under §73 StGB.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who are responsible for tax evasion are personally liable under §§69, 71 AO for the UG's unpaid taxes, including VAT, trade tax, and corporate income tax; civil restitution may reach the director's private assets; criminal courts can impose travel bans, seizure of bank accounts, and, in repeated offences, imprisonment.
Missing VAT Compliance and OSS abuse

Statutory sanction: Late payment penalties under §240 AO of 1% per month on unpaid VAT, late filing penalties under §152 AO up to EUR 25,000, and VAT evasion penalties under §370 AO with imprisonment up to 5 years; or, if negligent, EUR 30,000 fine under §378 AO; customs and tax authority may issue a correction notice with increased tax base.

Director personal liability: Directors are personal liability debtors for withheld VAT from sales supplies under §§34, 69 AO; a director who knowingly permits VAT omission is liable jointly and severally for restitution of the unpaid VAT plus interest, and can face criminal confiscation of personal goods.
Shell Company / Artificial Letterbox Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under German anti-abuse rules and the Steueroasen-Abwehrgesetz, sanction includes denial of DTA relief, denial of interest and royalty deductions, recharacterisation of profit allocations, addition of hidden reserves, VAT cancellation, and a penalty of up to EUR 25,000 under §379 AO for missing substance documentation; tax office may ignore the legal form and tax the German beneficial owner directly.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to refund hidden profit distributions under §31 GmbHG analog and for the unpaid tax that could not be collected from the sham entity; personal travel restrictions are enforceable where the director sponsors the abusive letterbox structure, and criminal liability for aiding tax evasion under §369 AO arises.
AML and Transparency Register Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the German Geldwäschegesetz (GWG), fines of up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons and up to EUR 100,000 for individuals can be imposed for non-reporting; plus administrative penalties up to EUR 50,000 for Transparenzregister non-compliance; criminal prosecution for money laundering under §261 StGB with imprisonment up to 5 years for severe breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the UG to serve as a conduit for forged trade invoices face personal criminal liability for money laundering; a court may impose asset freezes, travel bans, and a ban on serving as a director of any German commercial entity under §70 StGB; the UG's corporate veil is pierced and directors' personal assets are liable for AML fines and civil damages.
