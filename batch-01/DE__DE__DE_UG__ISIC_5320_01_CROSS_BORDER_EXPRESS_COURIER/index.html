Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Finanzamt (local tax office at the registered seat of the UG) for corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) for the USt-IdNr. (EU VAT ID) and OSS/IOSS administration; Generalzolldirektion (GZD) and local Zollamt for customs export clearance via ATLAS. Electronic portals: ELSTER (https://www.elster.de) and BZSt Online-Portal (https://www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

Domestic UG: (1) complete commercial register entry at the local Amtsgericht and Gewerbeanmeldung with the municipality; (2) obtain the express-courier operating license under GüKG (Erlaubnis nach § 2 GüKG) or, if licensed postal services are rendered, the Post-Dienstleistung license of BNetzA; (3) within one month of business start, file the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the competent Finanzamt; (4) receive the Steuernummer (national tax number) used for corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT; (5) apply separately for the USt-IdNr. via BZSt (Form USt 1 TU for non-established persons; online ELSTER inquiry for resident UGs). Non-resident entrepreneurs from third countries without a domestic branch must appoint a fiscal representative where mandated by § 22a UStG. All tax records and correspondence must be kept in German for at least 10 years under GoBD principles.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime; the UG is indelibly a corporation taxed at entity level. Available elections are: (a) Organschaft (VAT and corporate/trade-tax fiscal unity) with a financially and organizationally integrated controlling parent, supported by a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (§ 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG; § 14 KStG); (b) Kleinunternehmer-Regelung under § 19 UStG, exempting turnover up to EUR 25,000 (previous-year) with a EUR 100,000 prospective cap, at the cost of losing input-VAT deduction; (c) option to waive VAT exemption under § 9 UStG for real-estate transactions; (d) election of OSS/IOSS and reverse-charge treatment in cross-border shipments; (e) no elective hybrid classification is permitted — hybrid-mismatch consequences are non-elective and follow § 4k EStG (ATAD II). A UG cannot elect to be treated as a deemed partnership or disregarded entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for a commercially active UG: VAT registration and compliance are mandatory from the first taxable supply unless the optional Kleinunternehmer exemption (§ 19 UStG) applies (previous-year net turnover ≤ EUR 25,000 and current-year projection ≤ EUR 100,000; 2025 figures). For Union distance sales of goods and OSS supplies by EU-established suppliers, the EU-wide registration threshold is EUR 10,000 net turnover; for non-established third-country suppliers there is no de-minimis — a B2B customer owes the tax under reverse charge (§ 13b UStG), and direct registration or OSS Non-Union election is required for B2C supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods to third countries is exempt with input-VAT credit at 0% under § 4 Nr. 1a UStG and § 6 UStG where the UG, as exporter, proves the goods physically exited the EU customs territory. For international express courier services: (i) cross-border transport of small parcels is exempt under § 4 Nr. 3 UStG if the transport directly relates to export goods; (ii) services ancillary and directly connected to the export transport likewise qualify; (iii) transit across the EU is exempt pro-rata based on documented route distance and relevant tonne-kilometres; (iv) when the UG acts as principal on DDP/DAP or consolidated export consignments, it must hold its own export declaration and exit confirmation; (v) B2C courier services physically performed in Germany to non-EU recipients are subject to 19% German VAT unless the statutory place-of-supply rules (§ 3b/§ 3e UStG) or the transport-services exemption apply; (vi) zero-rating fails if the recipient rather than the UG is the exporter and no valid AEO/fiscal representation or power-of-attorney is evidenced.

  • Ausfuhranmeldung (export declaration) released in ATLAS with Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and where required the export value certificate in the consignor's name
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air, master and house) — for parcels, the carrier's electronic manifest and track-and-trace event log proving actual ex-EU exit
  • CMR waybill or, for EU transit legs, the NCTS/ECS transit declaration
  • Customs exit confirmation (CZN) or ATLAS AVR export acknowledgment
  • Proof of payment from the foreign buyer: SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advice, cash-against-documents bank receipt, or inward remittance certificate evidencing settlement within 180 days

No real-time tax clearance, fiscal stamping, or government API clearance requirements exist in Germany. Mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing applies from 1 January 2025 for the ability to receive e-invoices in EN 16931 format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.3). Mandatory issuance is phased: from 1 January 2027 for enterprises whose prior-year B2B turnover exceeds EUR 800,000, and for all B2B suppliers from 1 January 2028. E-files must be exchanged via EDI/PEPPOL or interoperable channels and archived in audit-proof GoBD form. B2C invoices remain paper/PDF eligible. Export movements require customs departure declarations in ATLAS (EXS) but no separate fiscal authorization code.

Exports are subject to Außenprüfung (§ 193 Abs. 1 AO), targeted customs controls by Zollfahndung, and automated data matching between inbound FX credit advice and ATLAS export declarations. Missing or deficient export evidence retroactively invalidates the 0% exemption: the UG must reassess 19% VAT on the transaction, repay any input VAT wrongly credited, and pay interest at 6% per annum (§ 233a AO). The assessment limitation period is five years, extended to ten years where tax evasion is established. Intentional false statements constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO (up to five years' imprisonment or fine); negligent false statements are punishable under § 378 AO with a fine of up to EUR 50,000. Failure to substantiate export settlement within 180 days rebuts the presumption of an export-for-consideration and triggers retrospective clawback of zero-rated turnover, invoicing corrections, and potential personal liability under §§ 69 and 71 AO. A valid Selbstanzeige (§ 371 AO) grants relief only if the tax shortfall is below EUR 25,000, is filed before the authority has opened an investigation, and the facts are previously unknown.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and OECD capital-movement obligations. The UG may invoice, hold, and settle in EUR, USD, GBP, CNY, or any freely usable currency without prior central-bank approval, conversion quotas, or profit-remittance licensing. The binding compliance constraint is statistical transparency: every cross-border payment or receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV via the BPI online gateway, no later than the fifth working day of the month following the transaction. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely payable without FX authorization but attract German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer at 25% plus applicable solidarity surcharge) unless reduced under a double-tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory minimum conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline on foreign-currency earnings. The UG may retain courier revenues in foreign currency, hold them indefinitely in multicurrency accounts, convert them at negotiated commercial bank rates, or net them against payables. The sole binding obligation is AWV Section 67 statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for transactions exceeding EUR 12,500, using the BPI activity code corresponding to international courier and parcel service revenues (Post- und Kurierdienste).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), acting as administrator of balance-of-payments statistics and AWV reporting collection; BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) as prudential supervisor of banks and payment institutions; Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as policy owner of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF) for fiscal and sanctions coordination. No dedicated exchange-control licensing agency exists because Germany operates a fully liberalized capital-account regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for the Credit_Card_POS and ACH-Direct payment rails; Low as sole primary treasury relationship. German and EU/EEA-licensed EMIs (e.g., Solaris SE and passporting digital banks) may lawfully hold funds and operate payment accounts for a German UG under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG), transposing PSD2. The intermediary EMI layer accelerates onboarding, enables card acquiring, and supplies SEPA Direct Debit settlement. Legal constraint: an unlicensed offshore EMI is statutorily barred from holding funds for German residents without BaFin/EBA authorization; moreover, the UG's Medium_Risk_Enhanced courier profile will trigger the EMI's sponsoring-bank enhanced due diligence. The architecturally safe design therefore uses a Tier-1 commercial bank as settlement base and overlays the EMI strictly for card acquiring, FX conversion, and push-payment disbursement, never as the custodian of the UG's core treasury.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

(1) Equity subscription or shareholder loan: route via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 with intermediary layering) using the AWV balance-of-payments classification for direct investment in Germany (Gesellschaftereinzahlung in eine UG) or for inbound financial loans (Finanzkreditaufnahme von Gebietsfremden); each transaction above EUR 12,500 requires Section 67 AWV BPI reporting. (2) The UG must pre-open its Capital Escrow Account at the selected Tier-1 bank; the notarized shareholder resolution and subscription confirmation are presented at onboarding so the bank records the economic purpose, and funds are released to the operational account only after compliance sign-off. (3) For operational customer receipts from cross-border courier invoicing, the rails are: card-acquirer settlement (Credit_Card_POS) through the licensed EMI, and direct SWIFT customer credit transfers from non-EU clients; all inbound messages must contain the UG's full corporate name, IBAN/BIC, and structured beneficiary details in fields 50K/56A/59 to satisfy travel-rule verification under the EU Wire Transfer Regulation (EU) 2023/1113. (4) Every inbound credit above EUR 12,500 is logged in the treasury ledger with its AWV reporting reference and BPI subdivision code before funds are swept into the operating account.(1) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR payments to international freight captains, fuel suppliers, customs agents, and subcontracted last-mile carriers, always carrying UETR and the purpose code for Post- und Kurierdienste for AWV statistical mapping. (2) TARGET2 / SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EUR disbursements below the reporting threshold, and SEPA Direct Debit (B2B/CORE) as the ACH-Direct rail for recurring terminal handling charges, airway-bill processing, and forwarder settlement levies. (3) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 and Standby Letters of Credit under ISP98 for high-value consolidation contracts requiring bank-intermediated settlement against presented documents; instrument issuance or confirmation accessed through the Tier-1 Trade Finance desk and collaterized by working capital or customs-debt guarantee frameworks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for the Credit_Card_POS and ACH-Direct payment rails; Low as sole primary treasury relationship. German and EU/EEA-licensed EMIs (e.g., Solaris SE and passporting digital banks) may lawfully hold funds and operate payment accounts for a German UG under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG), transposing PSD2. The intermediary EMI layer accelerates onboarding, enables card acquiring, and supplies SEPA Direct Debit settlement. Legal constraint: an unlicensed offshore EMI is statutorily barred from holding funds for German residents without BaFin/EBA authorization; moreover, the UG's Medium_Risk_Enhanced courier profile will trigger the EMI's sponsoring-bank enhanced due diligence. The architecturally safe design therefore uses a Tier-1 commercial bank as settlement base and overlays the EMI strictly for card acquiring, FX conversion, and push-payment disbursement, never as the custodian of the UG's core treasury.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the corporate income tax, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the local Hebesatz; typical combined rate approx. 29% to 31% depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) withheld at source on dividend distributions by a German UG, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in an aggregate statutory rate of 26.375%. The withholding obligation lies on the UG as the distributing entity; if the UG fails to withhold and remit, the managing director assumes personal liability under § 69 AO.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) for cross-border transactions with related parties. For a UG engaged in express courier services, the relevant thresholds are: annual gross compensation for intra-group supply of goods exceeding EUR 5,000,000; annual gross compensation for intra-group provision of services (including management fees, transshipment income, network participation fees, financing, and intangible royalties) exceeding EUR 500,000. A Master File must be prepared if the group's consolidated revenue is at least EUR 100,000,000 and the German UG is a constituent entity with cross-border related-party transactions exceeding the thresholds. In addition, taxpayers must produce on request valuations for cross-border business transfers and intra-group licences; failure to maintain contemporaneous records triggers estimation and penalty surcharges.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) in the format of the UAE or Bahrain regimes. However, the principle of economic substance is embedded in § 42 AO (General Anti-Abuse Provision) and the ATAD-implementing rules. A UG used for cross-border express courier services must demonstrate real operational substance: lease or ownership of a German hub, employed or contracted logistics staff, physical vehicle fleet, active management decisions in Germany, and correctly charged intercompany fees. A mere letterbox UG with no employees and no assets, used exclusively for routing invoices and utilising treaty benefits, is considered an abusive tax arrangement. Under § 42 AO, the German tax authorities may disregard the UG and re-attribute taxable income to the shareholder or to a permanent establishment where actual economic activity takes place. The CFC rules (Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung, ATAD) also require that passive income of a German UG held by a non-resident entity can be reallocated if the UG is not engaged in genuine substantive business operations for six months or more.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary fine. In particularly severe cases, based on the amount of taxes evaded and the scheme's sophistication, imprisonment of up to ten years may be imposed. Monetary penalties are assessed according to the deterrence principle and may exceed the evaded amount; in addition, the assessed tax becomes immediately enforceable with late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for the unpaid tax amount under § 69 AO if the tax was not withheld or remitted, or if negligent failure caused the loss of revenue. Criminal prosecution attaches personally to the director; upon conviction the court may order forfeiture of the evaded amounts, impose travel bans and issue a disqualification from serving as a director for up to five years. The corporate veil is pierced for fraud and intentional wrongful acts, making the director's personal estate reachable.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Defaults

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 (4) AO, a transfer pricing adjustment in the course of an audit triggers a surcharge of 5% of the resulting upward adjustment, with a minimum of EUR 5,000 and a maximum of EUR 50,000 per adjusted transaction. In the absence of acceptable documentation, the Finanzamt is entitled to estimate the taxable profit to the detriment of the taxpayer, often by using minimum margins for express courier services.

Director personal liability: The managing director who signs the tax return and declares completeness is a 'responsible tax representative' under § 90 (3) AO. If his or her failure to ensure documentation results in an underestimation of taxable profits, the director can be held personally liable for the resulting corporate tax deficiency. In cases where the misstatement is found to be intentional, criminal proceedings for tax evasion may be opened against the director.
Shell Company / Abusive Legal Form (Letterbox UG)

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may disregard the UG's legal existence for tax purposes pursuant to § 42 AO and assess taxes on the recipient of the economic substance, typically the foreign parent or beneficial owner. The UG's cross-border expense allocations are disallowed, the income is reattributed, and a permanent establishment tax assessment is issued in Germany for the activities actually carried out there. Sanctions can include substantial additional assessments, interest, and a criminal referral for fiscal fraud.

Director personal liability: Where the UG is found to be an abusive shell, the director is treated as the person responsible for creating the abusive arrangement and may be personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, trade tax and wage tax. German civil courts may pierce the veil for claims by tax creditors if the director intentionally departed from the statutory minimum capital requirements or operated for fraudulent purposes. Directors can face travel bans and, in the case of fraud or misappropriation of third-party payments, imprisonment under § 263 StGB.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financing of Terrorism (GwG Violations)

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) carry fines for the entity of up to EUR 1,000,000 or, in serious commercial cases, up to 10% of annual turnover. Non-compliance with the cash-control and record-keeping obligations can lead to confiscation of the seized assets, exclusion from procurement, and administrative orders prohibiting the exercise of the courier licence. If the activity is judged to constitute money laundering under § 261 StGB, imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years is imposed on responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: The managing director, as the statutory AML compliance officer for the UG, may be fined personally for failures in the design and execution of the AML internal control system. In the event of intentional money laundering through the courier network, the director is subject to personal criminal prosecution, property seizure, cross-border arrest and extradition treaties, and a prohibition on holding management positions in financial or licensed commercial entities for a period of up to five years. The corporate veil is pierced for the intentional criminal act, exposing the director's personal assets.
