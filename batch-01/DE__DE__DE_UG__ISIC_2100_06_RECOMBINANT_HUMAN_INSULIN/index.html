Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For a UG no minimum share capital exists; the nominal capital can be EUR 1 unless a higher amount is set. Paid-in cash contributions must be fully available to the managing director before registration (GmbHG Sec. 8(2)). A separate escrow account is not statutorily required for cash-only UG foundations, but if contributions are made in kind or capital exceeds small-ticket amounts, the notary will require an escrow scheme to guarantee availability to the company before commercial register entry.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance) as policy authority; operative competence vested in the local Finanzamt (local tax office) of the seat of management; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) issuance, central withholding matters, and EU administrative cooperation. Portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for filing/registration; BZSt Online-Portal (www.bzst.de) for VAT ID applications.

E-government filing portal

A UG must register within one month of commencing business by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the competent local Finanzamt, either via ELSTER or in writing; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT account number). For cross-border transactions, the UG separately applies to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under UStG §27a; foreign owners may file through a German tax agent (steuerlicher Berater), and no statutory minimum capital is required for UG formation, though 25% of annual profit must be reserved until minimum capital of EUR 25,000 is reached.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists; the UG is per se opaque and subject to unlimited Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) and solidarity surcharge. Available statutory elections include: (i) ertragsteuerliche Organschaft (fiscal unity) under KStG §§14–17, requiring a profit-and-loss pooling agreement and majority voting rights, and (ii) VAT Organschaft under UStG §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 for group VAT consolidation. Hybrid mismatch rules under EStG §4k apply by default to deny deductions on hybrid instruments, bring non-inclusion amounts into account, and impose withholding penalties; no elective opt-out is permitted for arrangements within the anti-hybrid scope.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany imposes VAT on every 'Unternehmer' (entrepreneur) under UStG §2 from the first taxable supply; there is no general registration threshold for a UG engaged in manufacturing. The Kleinunternehmerregelung (small business exemption) under UStG §19 with an annual turnover ceiling of EUR 22,000 is available only to natural-person/small partnerships and is inapplicable to a UG operating as an export-oriented manufacturer; cross-border trading triggers mandatory VAT ID issuance and Intrastat/non-EU export declarations without turnover limitations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export delivery (Ausfuhrlieferung) under UStG §6 is zero-rated when the recombinant human insulin is physically dispatched or transported by the UG or the recipient to a destination outside the European Union; the recipient must be a foreign entrepreneur or a body governed by public law in a third country, and the supply must be convincingly evidenced by commercial, customs, and transport documentation. Services closely related to exports—including quality-control testing, contract toll-manufacturing, and supply-chain logistics for third-country principals—qualify as zero-rated under UStG §7 or are treated as supplied at the recipient's place of business under UStG §3a; a statutory licence under the German Medicinal Products Act (AMG) and pharma GMP certification must coexist with the VAT zero-rating claim.

  • ATLAS Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with MRN (Movement Reference Number) and exit summary declaration
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list evidencing the third-country recipient and goods description (HS/pharma code)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing despatch from German customs territory and delivery to non-EU port/airport
  • CMR International Consignment Note (for land transport) with receiver's signature and customs stamp
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 proof of proceeds repatriated from the non-resident buyer
  • EU Certificate of Export / verification of exit confirmation from German customs (Ausfuhrnachweis) retained for at least 10 years

From 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers in Germany must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; sellers with turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.2, CIUS-EN 16931) from 1 January 2027, and all domestic B2B invoices must be issued electronically from 1 January 2028. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance/stamping API comparable to Italy's SDI; instead, the mandate is receipt- and issuance-based through certified EDI platforms, and cash-register/ERP systems must use a certified TSE (Technical Security Device) for DAV transactions. Reporting platforms and marketplaces must transmit EU cross-border data under UStG §22f, and all transactions remain subject to retrospective tax-audit data extraction (GoBD).

A wrongful zero-rating claim triggers retrospective VAT assessment under UStG §17, input VAT clawback, and interest of 0.5% per month under AO §233a, plus a penalty surcharge on under-declared VAT under AO §240; deliberate or reckless false export substantiation constitutes tax evasion under AO §370, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years (aggravated cases up to ten years), monetary fines, and extension of the assessment period to ten years under AO §169. Additionally, the UG faces invoice-liability for incorrect VAT statements under UStG §25b, loss of the zero-rating protection for subsequent buyers, disgorgement of refunds in the ELSTER audit system, and German customs enforcement actions including export-suspension and debarment from simplified export procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany ensures full current and capital account convertibility under EU free movement of capital rules (Art. 63 TFEU) and OECD liberalisation codes. No exchange approval is required for dividend, royalty, interest or service-fee remittances by a UG. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to the mandatory annual profit retention under GmbHG Sec. 5a(3) until the UG's capital plus statutory reserves reach EUR 25,000, and endogenous German withholding tax at 26.375% on distributions, reducible under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable DTT upon submission of relief forms and proof of beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No lawful requirement exists in Germany for the surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts may be maintained in any bank account in any currency. The only mandatory compliance operation is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank: each cross-border payment (export receipt or supplier payment) of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported by the 5th working day of the month following the transaction. The conversion ratio is therefore 0%.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) as the competent foreign-exchange and cross-border payments statistics authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), coordinated with BaFin and the Federal Ministry of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed by BaFin (or an equivalent EU authority) cannot act as the UG's principal bank, cannot hold statutory capital under a Sperrvermerk, cannot open commercial letters of credit or accept sovereign health LC payments, and present unacceptable AML/settlement risk for a high-risk biopharma manufacturing entity. An EEA-authorized EMI passporting into Germany may be used only as a supplementary payment service provider for incidental operational payments, never as the exclusive clearing or cash concentration rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (corporate income tax); plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the assessed corporate income tax and municipal trade tax, producing an aggregate effective burden of approximately 30%–33% in most German municipalities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory dividend withholding tax, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that withholding tax, producing an effective German dividend WHT rate of 26.375%. Municipal church surcharge may add between 0.75% and 1.05% at shareholder level where applicable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within seven months after the end of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year UG, generally no later than 31 July following the tax year. A seven-month extension is available in most federal states when the return is prepared by a tax adviser; further extension may be granted on application to the Finanzamt.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same annual statutory deadline as the corporate income tax return: no later than 31 July following the tax year; tax-adviser extension to the end of the following February or March is customary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 in controlled cross-border transactions in a fiscal year (administrative de minimis threshold for the preparation of a full German Local File; no statutory fixed monetary threshold is codified). A German Master File is required when the consolidated worldwide group revenue exceeds EUR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute, but economic substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rule under §42 AO, the functional and risk analysis requirements of §1 AStG, and beneficial-ownership clauses in every German DTA. The UG must have real production or development operations, qualified employees in Germany, genuine decision-making, sufficient working capital, and documented business purpose for retained management fees or IP royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) and deliberate misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years or criminal fine under §370 AO; in aggravated cases involving large-scale concealment or foreign elements, imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years. Additionally, the tax office levies penalty interest of 0.5% per month und §233a AO and may impose an estimated assessment under §162 AO. Fines may be up to EUR 50,000 per offence, plus full recovery of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §69 AO and §43 GmbHG for intentionally or negligently unremitted tax liabilities. In a criminal finding, directors face confiscation of assets, visa restrictions, and a potential travel ban. Where the director is a flight risk, remand in custody is available; civil restitution claims may be brought by the other shareholders and the remaining assets of the UG are exposed.
Transfer pricing documentation deficiency and avoidance of arm's length principle

Statutory sanction: Under §162 AO, the tax authorities may presume that the taxable income is higher than declared and impose an upward adjustment; a surcharge of up to 6% of the additional assessed tax, with a minimum EUR 5,000 per adjustment, is applied. Late payment interest of 6% per year follows, and the taxpayer may lose the right to offset losses or use DTA crediting. Total tax and surcharges can reach more than 40% of the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused the documentation failure or signed false declarations may be personally fined under §378 AO (administrative fine up to EUR 10,000 for aiding a tax misdemeanor) or prosecuted as principals under §370 AO. They are also personally liable to the UG for any transfer-pricing penalty if they failed to exercise the care of a prudent business manager under §43 GmbHG.
Shell company / treaty shopping / abusive beneficial ownership arrangement

Statutory sanction: DTA relief is denied under the beneficial ownership clause; withholding tax is retroactively imposed at the statutory 26.375% dividend rate; the transaction is recharacterized under §42 AO as a direct payment to the ultimate shareholder. In addition, the UG may lose input VAT recovery, R&D tax credits, and trade tax reliefs, and administrative fines under §379 AO of up to EUR 10,000 may be imposed. The tax authority may revoke tax rulings and refuse future tax registration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the restored withholding tax where they knowingly participated in abusive structuring; they can be prosecuted as principals under §§369, 370 AO and face imprisonment up to 5 years. In cross-border cases, courts may impose provisional travel bans, confiscate passports, and issue European Arrest Warrants. Civil liability for lost taxes and shareholder damage claims is borne by the directors personally.
Unauthorized recombinant insulin manufacture / supply chain and AML violation

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 3 years or a criminal fine under §95 AMG; administrative closure of the production facility, seizure and destruction of products, revocation of marketing authorization, and inclusion in EDQM and German regulatory non-compliance records. Under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) and §§370, 398a AO, failure to establish AML controls can lead to administrative fines of up to EUR 1 million, loss of export licensing, and suspension of bank payment rails.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for the consequences of unlawful manufacturing and for any damage suffered by the UG's creditors or patients under §43 GmbHG and general tort law. In aggravated public-health danger cases, custodial remand and travel bans are possible. Directors may also be ordered to make personal restitution for conversion costs, product recalls, and criminal fines when the company cannot pay, and can be barred from serving as managing directors under the commercial law for repeated violations.
