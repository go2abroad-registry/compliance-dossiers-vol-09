Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – central services and VAT ID allocation; local Finanzamt (district tax office) – substantive tax registration and assessments; official filing portal: ELSTER – https://www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

After commercial register entry, the UG files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt through ELSTER or by paper, declaring corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT and payroll tax. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (permanent corporate tax number). The UG then applies separately to BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr) via the BZSt online portal. A non-resident managing director or foreign shareholder may be required to appoint a German tax advisor to obtain and operate an ELSTER authentication certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG is treated as a German resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income, subject to Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer and Solidaritätszuschlag. — After commercial register entry, the UG files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt through ELSTER or by paper, declaring corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT and payroll tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG is treated as a German resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income, subject to Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer and Solidaritätszuschlag.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under German law. For an UG, the principal statutory election is the formation of an Organschaft (corporate/trade-tax and VAT fiscal unity) where interdependence, financial integration and organizational integration exist. Other elections include the §19 UStG small-business exemption where turnover thresholds are satisfied and, for cross-border structures, compliance with §4k EStG anti-hybrid rules that override mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold applies to a UG; VAT liability arises from the first taxable supply under §§2 and 18 UStG. The only small-business relief under §19 UStG, available to qualifying entrepreneurs, requires prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 25,000 and current-year prospective turnover ≤ EUR 100,000 (2025 threshold onward); this is an elective relief and not a registration barrier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT under §4 Nr. 1a and §6 UStG when the goods are physically transported to a non-EU destination and the UG retains conclusive documentary proof (Ausfuhrnachweis). Intra-EU dispatches to a valid EU VAT ID may be zero-rated under §6a UStG. Offshore restaurant-consultancy or condiment-supply services to non-EU entrepreneurs may be zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Germany under §§3a ff. UStG; dining and catering services physically performed in Germany remain standard-rated. Export refund substantiation requires evidence that the service is not connected to German real property and that the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside Germany.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) with the Ausfuhrbegleitdokument (ABD) and customs release/export confirmation
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or CMR combined transport document showing final non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, foreign exchange receipt record, or bank payment confirmation proving receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping or transaction-level API reporting to the tax authority is in force. Businesses must, however, maintain compliant electronic cash registers with a certified technical security element (TSE) under §146a AO and GoBD for POS records. Since 1 January 2025, all B2B taxpayers must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant e-invoices (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD); full mandatory e-invoice issuance applies to all German domestic B2B invoices from 1 January 2028, with an earlier 2027 requirement for taxpayers exceeding EUR 800,000 prior-year turnover. VAT returns are transmitted to the tax authority through ELSTER API, which does not constitute per-invoice government clearance.

If the export-proof bundle is incomplete, contradictory, or fails the 180-day proceeds benchmark, the Finanzamt may deny zero-rating retroactively and assess 19% VAT on the original invoice, plus interest at 6% per annum under §233a AO; assessments remain open for up to 7 years, or 10 years in cases of evasion. Intentional misuse incurs criminal liability under §370 AO for tax evasion and §§26a/26b UStG for unlawful zero-rating or invalid invoices; fines and imprisonment up to 10 years may be imposed in aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full de jure convertibility and an open capital account under the EU free movement of capital and the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG). There is no general prior-approval regime for equity injections, profit remittances, repayment of shareholder loans, royalties, or trade payments, provided that applicable sanctions and reporting duties are met. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) may hold EUR and foreign-currency balances without limitation. Profit remittance frictions are fiscal rather than exchange-control: distributions to a non-resident Chinese shareholder attract German dividend withholding at 26.375% including solidarity surcharge, reducible under the Germany-China double taxation agreement to a treaty rate (generally 5% or 10% depending on qualifying shareholding and beneficial-ownership substantiation). The receiving German bank will require proof of tax filings and corporate formalities, but no central-bank conversion licence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies to export proceeds under German law. The UG may retain export receipts in EUR or any foreign currency and may freely use them for operating costs, supplier settlements, or debt service. The statutory compliance duty is statistical: every individual payment from a non-resident counterparty with a countervalue of EUR 12,500 or more, including export receipts, must be reported by the German resident beneficiary to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days of the value date through the AWV electronic reporting portal. In addition, monthly status reports (Zahlungsbilanz-Bestandsmeldungen) must be filed when aggregate cross-border receivables or payables exceed the equivalent of EUR 5,000,000. No other retention, repatriation, or conversion obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) as the German central bank within the Eurosystem, supported by the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for banking and payment-institution supervision and by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action for foreign-trade and payment restrictions. The Bundesbank operates the external trade and payments statistics registry (Außenwirtschaftsstatistik) and the electronic reporting system for cross-border payments under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a supplementary treasury rail and LOW as a primary account. A non-German offshore electronic-money institution (EMI) without a BaFin licence or an EEA passport under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) cannot lawfully provide payment accounts, acquire POS transactions, or issue German IBANs to the UG. Such platforms may be used only for ancillary cross-border vendor payments, CNH/USD conversion, and trade settlement through a licensed EU partner bank and must maintain client-fund safeguarding. The UG should maintain its primary operating account and merchant acquiring relationship at a tier-1 German bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for inbound FDI from China is a high-value SWIFT MT103 payment (or MT202/MT205 cover) from the Chinese shareholder's commercial bank to the UG's German operational account, carrying a UETR and a consistent payment purpose code classified as equity contribution, e.g. 'Kapitalerhöhung' or 'Stammeinlage UG' rather than 'loan'. The remitting Chinese bank must have a valid SAFE ODI/FDI registration and the German receiving bank must complete enhanced due diligence before value date. The complete capital-injection verification bundle includes the notarized foundation document, a current Handelsregister excerpt, German tax identification numbers (local tax office notice of USt-IdNr and W-IdNr), shareholder passport, Chinese SAFE approval certificate, and a bank reference. For an EU-based shareholder, a SEPA Credit Transfer (PACS.008) is permissible for the same purpose, subject to the normal AWV reporting threshold. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 0.825%; trade tax Gewerbesteuer of approximately 14–17% is additionally levied on business income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that 25%). Church tax is generally not imposed on non-resident and corporate shareholders. Treaty rates may reduce withholding: 0-15% depending on recipient's jurisdiction; EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive grants 0% withholding for an EU/EEA parent holding at least 10% for 12 months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due 31 July of the calendar year following the tax year. If prepared and signed by a professional tax adviser, automatic extension to 28/29 February of the second following year (e.g., for fiscal year 2023, 31 July 2024, extended to 28 February 2025). Discretionary further extensions may be requested under § 109 AO.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when annual cross-border intercompany transactions with related parties exceed EUR 500,000 for goods/supplies or EUR 50,000 for services. Master File: mandatory when consolidated group annual revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Country-by-Country Report: filing required for groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million. All documentation must be contemporaneous; submission on demand within 60 days (30 days for extraordinary transactions).

Economic substance scope: Germany does not enact an Anglo-Caribbean Economic Substance Regulation as a standalone statute; nevertheless, economic substance is decisive for corporate tax residence, permanent establishment attribution, treaty benefits and transfer-pricing functional analysis under § 42 AO and the base-company jurisprudence of the Bundesfinanzhof. A German UG/restaurant entity used solely as a letterbox with effective management carried out from abroad, no bona fide local decisions, no local staff or no operating risk capacity is recharacterized as a shell company, its income reallocated to the foreign principals, and treaty benefits denied under § 50d Abs. 3 EStG.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine (§ 370 AO); particularly serious cases, including evaded tax amounts exceeding EUR 50,000, carry 6 months to 10 years' imprisonment. Penalty of 1.5% per annum interest on the evaded tax demand is assessed from the moment underpayment arose, and late-payment surcharges apply until settlement. For unregistered cash registers, additional turnover tax assessments of 10%-25% per § 162 AO may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Managing directors (Geschäftsführer) are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under §§ 34, 69 AO even for simple negligence. In cases of intentional evasion, the director's personal assets can be seized, a travel ban may be issued pending criminal proceedings, and imprisonment is a direct personal sanction. Non-resident directors can be stripped of travel rights, extradited and prosecuted in Germany without limited liability protection.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 Abs. 4 AO the Finanzamt may estimate taxable income with adverse TP inference and impose a nontaxable surcharge of at least 5% of the tax increase, minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 1,000,000; for intentional violations the surcharge may be doubled. Interest, royalty and license deductions may be denied under § 4j EStG, and the underlying tax assessment remains payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve deficient documentation or non-arm's-length pricing are jointly and severally liable for uncovered corporate tax, interest and late-payment surcharges. The corporate veil does not protect directors when they signed the declarations; criminal prosecution for deliberate misdeclaration is possible and can lead to personal fine, imprisonment and travel restriction.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Subject to § 42 AO anti-abuse recharacterization: the UG's income is treated as directly attributable to the foreign principals, distributions are deemed disguised profit distributions, treaty benefits are denied under § 50d Abs. 3 EStG, and the tax authorities may disregard all artificial agreements. Reassessed corporate income tax, trade tax and withholding tax are levied at statutory rates, together with interest and surcharges up to 25% of assessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or tolerate circular shell arrangements are personally liable for tax deficiencies caused by treaty denial. Non-resident beneficial owners risk extended 10-15 year limitation periods, forced transparency-register disclosure, travel bans, and confiscation of repatriated profits where personal guilt exists.
Anti-Money Laundering and UBO Registration Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 per violation under § 56 GwG or up to double the economic benefit; serious/repeat violations may trigger the withdrawal of the restaurant license, blocking of merchant-acquiring contracts, and criminal liability for money laundering (§ 261 StGB) with imprisonment up to 5 years. For failure to file UBO reports, Finanzamt and Registergerichte impose daily penalty payments until compliance.

Director personal liability: Managing directors, including non-resident appointees, incur criminal and regulatory fines for non-compliant AML structures; personal disgorgement of profits, prohibition on holding management office in Germany, travel bans and seizure of personal assets are enforceable. The absence of a German director does not limit liability because the UG has a statutory managing director who must ensure UBO and AML compliance.
