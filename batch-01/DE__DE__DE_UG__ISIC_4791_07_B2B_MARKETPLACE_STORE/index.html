Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Local Amtsgericht — Registergericht (Commercial Register) at the registered office; filings are submitted electronically by a German notary through the secure electronic court and administrative mailbox (besonderes elektronisches Anwaltspostfach, beA).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Company Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), primarily Sections 1, 2, 5a, 7, 8, 10, 35, 39 and 78; supplemented by the German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB) Sections 6, 106 to 108 and commercial register procedural rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation containing the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory notarial model protocol (Musterprotokoll), together with the electronic application for entry into the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 33 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) may be formed with a nominal share capital of EUR 1 or more; cash contributions must be fully paid up before registration. Contributions in kind are prohibited under GmbHG Section 5a(2). The capital must be credited to a German bank account held in the name of the company or company-in-formation and must be at the free disposal of the managing director(s), evidenced by a bank confirmation to the Commercial Register. A statutory reserve of 25% of annual net profit must be accumulated until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000. No public escrow account is generally required for this standard cash-based UG formation.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial fire-safety permit is required for an office-only online marketplace; the registered workplace must nevertheless comply with the German Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) regarding emergency exits, ventilation, first aid equipment, and electrical safety inspections. Occupational clearance includes completion of a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (ArbSchG), DGUV V3 electrical equipment checks, and ensuring structural load capacity of the leased office premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and national taxes; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income, trade tax and VAT filing; ELSTER is the mandatory online tax portal.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the UG, the managing director must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) to the local Finanzamt within one month, which assigns a Steuernummer (tax number). A separate USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) is applied for online through BZSt; foreign entities must appoint a German resident representative and must use the UG's registered office in Germany as service and tax correspondence address. The UG, being a German corporate resident, is registered without requiring a minimum capital proof beyond the constitutive UG rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a German corporation (Körperschaft) with legal personality; it is subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability on worldwide income, trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge. No income tax pass-through to shareholders is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in German tax law. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) forming a corporate tax group (Organschaft) for corporate income tax and VAT purposes, requiring an operational and financial integration under §§ 14 ff. KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG; (ii) deferring trade tax on retained profits (§ 34a EStG does not apply to corporations); (iii) applying the small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG only when annual turnover is below EUR 25,000, which is generally unavailable for an active B2B marketplace operator.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration for every entrepreneur performing taxable supplies in Germany; no turnover threshold for established businesses. The small-business exemption ceases at EUR 25,000 annual turnover (EUR 100,000 estimated threshold from 2025), but B2B marketplace operators are excluded from this exemption. For cross-border distance sales utilising the One-Stop-Shop (OSS), the EU Member State registration threshold is EUR 10,000 of pan-EU distance sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The UG can claim a 0% VAT rate on export supplies of goods to non-EU destinations under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG, provided the goods are physically removed from the German customs territory and the seller retains conclusive documentary evidence (Beleg- and Buchnachweis). Services rendered to foreign entrepreneurs are normally zero-rated as cross-Border B2B supplies under the reverse-charge mechanism when the recipient is in another country. The UG must demonstrate that the recipient is not resident in Germany and that the service is not attributable to a permanent establishment in Germany.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument with ATLAS electronic confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent CMR/airway bill indicating destination outside the European Union)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing settlement of the foreign payment

B2B electronic invoicing mandate became applicable from 1 January 2025: every German VAT payer must receive structured electronic invoices (XInvoice or ZUGFeRD) in a machine-readable XML format and must transmit them using approved Peppol or EDI-based channels. Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not yet introduced in Germany; however, all B2G invoices require the XRechnung standard through the national e-invoicing portal.

Export zero-rating is a high-risk area for VAT fraud. If documentary proof fails, VAT is retroactively charged at 19% and assessed with interest (0.15% per month) and late-payment penalties. Administrative fines up to EUR 30,000 may be imposed; intentional evasion constitutes a criminal offence under § 370 AO, punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment. German customs and tax authorities closely scrutinise marketplace transaction trails, missing trader patterns, and requests for ACP/PDI certificates, and will claw back erroneously claimed zero-ratings plus statutory surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU law (TFEU Art. 63) and the AWV. No central bank approval or administrative permit is required to convert EUR or to remit dividends, loan repayments, or trade settlement amounts in any foreign currency. The Euro is freely floating; the capital account is fully liberalised, and profit remittances by a UG are not subject to FX surrender or exchange-control restrictions. The sole legal burden is statistical reporting to the Bundesbank and compliance with German anti-money-laundering documentation for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 in a calendar month.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender requirement or conversion ratio for export proceeds in Germany. Exporters may retain EUR or any foreign currency and may disburse funds freely. The statutory obligation of a German resident company, including a UG, is to notify the Deutsche Bundesbank of all payments received from non-residents and payments made to non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 in a calendar month (AWV Section 67), using the Bundesbank balance-of-payments purpose codes (Zahlungszweck). The reporting deadline is the fifth banking day after the end of the month in which the payment was executed; the UG's domestic commercial bank usually files the report on the customer's behalf.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), with banking and payment supervision by BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority); foreign exchange and cross-border payment reporting is administered under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut Business, Qonto, Penta): Medium for the operational/treasury account and Low for the statutory capital escrow account. A UG engaged in cross-border B2B marketplace settlement may lawfully hold funds in a BaFin-licensed EMI (ZAG licence) or a PSD2-passported foreign EMI, and such accounts facilitate multi-currency FX and SWIFT/SEPA rails. However, EMI deposits are not covered by German statutory deposit insurance, and the notary and the Commercial Register generally require initial capital contributions to be evidenced by a German credit institution, not an EMI. For escrow-style marketplace proceeds, the UG should use client-money safeguarding sub-accounts at the acquiring bank or a licensed payment institution. The Alibaba Pay target rail is not a German-licensed bank; connectivity must be arranged through a licensed acquiring/PSP partner settling to the UG's bank account via SEPA or SWIFT.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on CIT plus municipal Gewerbesteuer; combined statutory burden typically 29.8% - 32.1% depending on the local municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag, producing an aggregate 26.375% withholding tax on dividend distributions. The rate is reduced to 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if the German UG is at least 10% owned for 12 continuous months. Treaty rates generally range from 0% to 15% for portfolio holdiunder applicable DTAs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation when cross-border related-party transactions are material. Administrative safe thresholds: annual goods transactions with related parties greater than EUR 5 million, annual services greater than EUR 500,000; a Local File is generally expected when the German entity exceeds these materiality levels. A Master File is required when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. The threshold applies in EUR; if the UG acts solely as a commissionaire/middleman in a B2B marketplace, the relevant commissions must be benchmarked separately from flow-through purchase and sale values.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the Cayman/UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, § 42 AO (Abgabenordnung) disallows abusive structures lacking economic reality, and German case law requires the UG to possess organisational substance: a functioning registered office, actual directors, staff, bank account, contracts with marketplace vendors, and decision-making authority in Germany. A letterbox UG or a platform company whose employees and management are exclusively outside Germany will be recharacterised as a sham entity, income will be reattributed to the actual entrepreneur, and treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test of the Multilateral Instrument.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Steuerhinterziehung

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years under § 370 AO, or from six months to ten years in aggravated cases. Financial penalties reach up to 1.5 times the evaded tax amount (often the entire assessed back tax plus a punitive 6% per annum on the unpaid amount).

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for unpaid corporate tax. Travel bans and passport seizure can be imposed. Deliberate concealment gives rise to imprisonment and, upon insolvency, civil restitution claims by tax administrators and third-party creditors who relied on the UG balance sheet.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax auditor may estimate income under § 162 AO, and a surcharge (Schätzungszuschlag) of at least EUR 5,000, generally up to 10% of the adjusted profit improvement, is added when documentation is absent or materially unusable. Additional negligence fines under § 378 AO apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to preserve records are personally exposed to the penalty and to restitution claims if they knowingly acquiesce in fictional intercompany prices. Courts may lift the liability shield for the UG; a travel ban can be ordered to secure tax collection.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Abusive Substance

Statutory sanction: Under § 42 AO, the abusive transaction is recast; the entire taxable income may be imputed to the non-resident enterprise, with a German permanent establishment presumed. Withholding tax of up to 26.375% is imposed on deemed dividends; DTA relief is refused because the UG is not the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced; directors are personally liable for the withholding tax and corporate tax debts. Criminal investigations for commercial fraud and tax evasion may include travel bans, electronic monitoring, and imprisonment up to five years.
Withholding Tax / Dividend Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The UG is liable to the tax office for the withheld tax; late payment triggers default interest of 0.15% per month, and a penalty may reach up to 10% of the unpaid amount. The tax authority can enforce against the UG's bank accounts or against any Moveable assets of the directors if liability was incurred through willful neglect.

Director personal liability: Directors as withholding agents are personally liable under § 44 EStG and §§ 69, 70 AO. They may be required to pay civil restitution, are barred from leaving German jurisdiction by travel ban, and can be convicted of criminal tax evasion for up to five years if the dividend was accompanied by false declarations to the Finanzamt.
AML/KYC and Cross-Border Payment Integrity

Statutory sanction: Fines under the GwG may be up to EUR 1 million or an administrative penalty calculated as 5% of annual turnover for serious, systematic violations. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) can also prohibit payment operations and appoint a monitor.

Director personal liability: Directors and Money Laundering Officers face personal fines, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment up to five years for intentional violation of reporting and freezing duties. Travel bans may be issued as a precaution, and civil restitution claims arise from fraudulent payments laundered through the marketplace.
