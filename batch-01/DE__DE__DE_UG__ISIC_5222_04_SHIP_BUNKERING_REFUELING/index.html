Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT-ID and central tax matters; local municipal tax office (Finanzamt) for TIN issuance and tax assessment; digital portal ELSTER (https://www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity obtains the statutory Steuernummer (economic TIN) by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the local Finanzamt after registration of a branch or UG in the Commercial Register; the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for via BZSt/ELSTER using the electronic form and requires identification of legal representative, place of business, business purpose, and intended taxable activities. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment must separately register for VAT when performing taxable supplies in Germany.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a resident UG is taxed as a German corporate entity on its worldwide income under corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); no pass-through eligibility for the entity itself. — A foreign entity obtains the statutory Steuernummer (economic TIN) by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the local Finanzamt after registration of a branch or UG in the Commercial Register; the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for via BZSt/ELSTER using the electronic form and requires identification of legal representative, place of business, business purpose, and intended taxable activities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a resident UG is taxed as a German corporate entity on its worldwide income under corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); no pass-through eligibility for the entity itself.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in German tax law. Statutory elections are limited to fiscal unity (Organschaft) for VAT and corporate income/trade tax purposes, the small-business VAT exemption (§ 19 UStG), and certain depreciation/provision adjustments. Hybrid mismatch elections are prohibited by the ATAD-implementing German rules, which apply mandatory re-characterization and deny mismatched deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and forecast not exceeding EUR 50,000 for the domestic small-business exemption; foreign non-established suppliers have no general threshold and must register for VAT if any taxable supply is deemed to take place in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies to physically exported goods under § 4 Nr. 1 UStG and to supplies of marine fuel, bunkering and services for vessels engaged in international navigation under § 4 Nr. 2 UStG in alignment with Article 148 of the VAT Directive. Eligibility requires the bunker fuel or lubricant to be delivered into a commercial vessel engaged in international traffic, the supplier to obtain proof of the vessel's destination and voyage, and the recipient/foreign customer to be verifiable; ancillary services for such shipping also qualify for 0%.

  • Customs Export Declaration – ATLAS / detailed export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading (Konossement) or equivalent multimodal shipping document evidencing vessel identity and seaway movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-exchange payment settlement evidence confirming receipt of consideration (used in conjunction with the export declaration and transport documents)

Germany mandates structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD v2.3) for B2G supplies and, as of 1 January 2025, requires all domestic businesses to be able to receive e-invoices in a structured XML format; from 2027, EU ViDA real-time digital reporting will require transaction transmission through the BZSt interface. No national fiscal stamping or real-time API clearance is currently in force under the UStG; invoices must contain a valid German/EU VAT ID, VAT rate, and, for zero-rated supplies, the statutory evidence reference.

The German tax authorities may scrutinize export transactions during an external audit (Außenprüfung) and require contemporaneous proof of physical departure, commercial authenticity, and payment matching. If zero-rating is not duly substantiated, the supply is re-taxed at the standard 19% VAT rate, input VAT previously deducted can be clawed back retroactively, and late-payment interest accrues at approximately 6% annually from the calendar quarter following the supply. Fraudulent or abusive claims can trigger penalties under § 26a UStG, additional surcharges under § 371 AO, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, with fines calculated on the tax amount evaded and potential imprisonment for serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU capital movement liberalization (Article 63 TFEU). Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and repatriation of capital are unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding and documentation requirements. No exchange control approval is required for cross-border transfers, but reporting obligations apply for statistical purposes (e.g., Bundesbank's External Transactions Reporting).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. However, all cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical balance-of-payments purposes, with no conversion ratio imposed.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and FX regulatory oversight; the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and eurozone payment systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) licensed by BaFin can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services. However, for ship bunkering (ISIC 5222), which is classified as Medium-Risk Enhanced under AML, many EMIs impose restrictive onboarding or decline due to perceived sanctions exposure and cash-intensive nature. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK, Lithuania) are generally not viable for a German UG due to regulatory arbitrage concerns and limited access to SEPA/domestic clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital contributions (share capital or shareholder loans) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code '1100' (Direct Investment) or '1101' (Investment in a German company). The bank will require a copy of the capital contribution resolution and proof of the UG's share capital deposit (at least EUR 1, but typically EUR 1,000 for credibility). For shareholder loans, a loan agreement and interest rate documentation are needed. Verification: The receiving bank will perform a source-of-funds check and may request the sender's bank to confirm the beneficial owner.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For bunkering fuel purchases and port service payments, use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers (e.g., to suppliers) and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for large or recurring transactions. Standby LCs are used as performance guarantees. For EUR-denominated payments within SEPA, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for low-value transactions, but for cross-border non-EUR (e.g., USD), SWIFT is mandatory. All outbound payments must include appropriate purpose codes (e.g., '1300' for trade in goods, '1301' for trade in services) and be reported to the Bundesbank if exceeding EUR 12,500.FX Forwards (up to 12 months) with German banks for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP hedgingFX Swaps (short-term) for managing liquidity across currenciesCurrency Options (OTC) for flexible hedging of bunker fuel price and FX exposureNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not commonly available for EUR as it is a fully convertible currency; instead, deliverable forwards are standard.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Return Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to 5% of tax liability per month plus interest; possible criminal referral

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability for penalties; imprisonment up to 5 years for willful tax evasion under StGB
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income reassessment, back taxes, penalties up to 5% of understated tax, and criminal sanctions

Director personal liability: Personal fines and imprisonment for directors authorizing or benefiting from abusive transfer prices
Abusive Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as constructive dividends, back taxes and penalties

Director personal liability: Personal restitution liability and criminal charges for fraudulent tax evasion
Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, income recharacterization and ESR penalties

Director personal liability: Imprisonment and fines for directors responsible for compliance failures
