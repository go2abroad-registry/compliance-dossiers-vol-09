Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A UG must have a German domestic business address capable of receiving official service and correspondence under the GmbHG and commercial-register rules. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern — BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; local Finanzamt for income and trade tax registration; online portal ELSTER (elektronisches Steuerverfahrensportal).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must submit the statutory registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt prior to commencing economic activity. A fiscal representative or permanent German branch signatory is accepted. The Finanzamt issues a local Tax Number (Steuernummer) for corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT returns; the BZSt separately issues a German VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) after confirmation of the application. Non-resident entrepreneurs without a domestic seat must register through the responsible Finanzamt for their German establishment or, in default, through the Finanzamt of their fiscal agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (unbeschränkt steuerpflichtig resident corporation: Corporation Tax + Solidarity Surcharge + Trade Tax; worldwide income subject to tax treaty relief; no pass-through eligibility outside of a corporate fiscal unity).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. Election for corporate tax group group taxation under KStG §§ 14-19 through an enforceable profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under UStG § 2(2) for consolidation into a single taxable person. Small-business election under UStG § 19 permitted only for domestic startups/reduced-turnover enterprises, not for export-oriented contract manufacturers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not a turnover-exemption threshold for regular cross-border/export manufacturers: any entrepreneur carrying out taxable supplies in Germany must register. No general de minimis. The small-business exemption in UStG § 19 applies only to German-resident businesses with prior year turnover ≤ EUR 25,000 and current year expected turnover ≤ EUR 100,000. For non-resident businesses selling into Germany, the distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide, and any activity exceeding this triggers full German VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under UStG § 4 Nr. 1 and § 6 for export deliveries: goods must physically leave German customs territory and reach a third country (non-EU); the seller must supply the buyer through an export transaction and provide conclusive documentary evidence in two parts — bookkeeping records (Belegnachweis) and proof of delivery (Ausfuhrnachweis under § 9 and § 10 UStDV). For offshore B2B services (§ 15 VO) and contract manufacturing of PCBA components destined for non-EU customers, eligibility additionally requires a valid customer VAT/GST registration outside Germany, a commercial invoice demonstrating the direct sourcing/re-export flow, and no local economic presence in the import destination unless specifically treated as a supply there.

  • Electronic Export Declaration (Elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung, registered in ATLAS/ITMS with movement reference number)
  • Customs Exit Confirmation (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument or actual exit certificate from customs office)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or through-transport document evidencing physical cross-border shipment
  • Commercial Invoice describing goods under ISIC 2610 PCBA assembly, country of origin, Incoterms and declared customs value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or verified SWIFT-based proof of final payment/foreign exchange received on a German bank account or a trust account of the foreign contracting entity
  • If transportation via non-customs procedure: supplementary multi-modal transport log and foreign customs entry stamp / treatment code

No national real-time fiscal clearance or AI-based digital stamping. Since 1 January 2025, German taxable persons must accept an electronic invoice in a structured format (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD 2.x, EN 16931). Mandatory structured e-invoicing issuance for domestic B2B supplies will be phased in from 1 January 2027 for large taxpayers and 1 January 2028 for all taxpayers, with the obligation to issue e-invoices for all domestic B2B supplies by 2030. Export invoices and pro-forma documents may be issued via regular EDI or PDF; no stamping is required, but full audit traceability through the official ITMS customs clearance service (e.g., ATLAS/ITMS) must be demonstrable.

The tax inspectorate conducts export-proof audits (Ausfuhrnachweisprüfung) and cross-border third-country audit reviews. Any missing, false, or forged export documentation reverses zero-rating retroactively. Penalties include: recovery of German VAT at 19% plus late-payment surcharges (1% per month per § 233a AO), interest at 6% annualized, tax misdemeanor fines up to EUR 25,000 under § 378 AO, and criminal tax evasion liability under § 370 AO carrying up to 10 years' imprisonment for fraudulent export schemes; additionally, breaches of customs/export surveillance laws may trigger commodity-surveillance fines and exclusion from the ATLAS/ITMS electronic clearance system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current-account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no prior approval for equity or loan inflows, no restrictions on holding or disbursing foreign currency, and no repatriation licensing. Profit remittance is legally unhindered; the only frictions are fiscal and statutory: (i) German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus solidarity surcharge), reducible under applicable double tax treaties, and (ii) the UG's mandatory retention of 25% of annual net profits in a statutory reserve under § 5a(3) GmbHG until share capital plus reserve reaches EUR 25,000. Individual cross-border receipts or payments exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) must be statistically reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV §§ 68 and 70, with the bank effecting the report on the customer's declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation obligation for export proceeds. Exporters may freely invoice in EUR or any foreign currency and retain proceeds in domestic or offshore accounts. Reporting obligations remain: incoming wire receipts from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 require a Bundesbank balance-of-payments payment report, and monthly aggregate reports of claims on non-residents and liabilities to non-residents apply where the statutory threshold (EUR 5,000,000) is exceeded.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV); BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) supervises banking and anti-money-laundering conduct; the European Central Bank (ECB) holds monetary authority within the Eurosystem/TARGET2.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viable as a supplementary multi-currency operating rail, not as the primary treasury bank. BaFin-authorised and EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, PPRO, Solaris SE) offer rapid onboarding, free SEPA processing, and cost-effective FX for routine supplier payments. Legal constraints under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and the German KWG/GwG: EMIs cannot accept deposits, cannot grant credit, and cannot issue documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, or documentary collections. EMIs also routinely refuse to receive share-capital injection into a newly incorporated UG before commercial-register registration. Viability rating: High as a secondary rail, Low as a sole institutional relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity subscription: the shareholder wires EUR from a bank account in the shareholder's own name to the UG's blocked Gründungskonto at the chosen German bank, referencing the subscription list and stating the purpose 'Bareinlage Stammkapital' (cash contribution to share capital). The receiving bank verifies the remitter, records the statistical purpose code for capital contribution, and automatically files the AWV § 70 balance-of-payments report for amounts exceeding EUR 12,500. The notary obtains the bank's confirmation (Voreintragungsprüfung) and files the registration application with the commercial register; after registration, the blocked account is released and merged into the UG's operational account. Shareholder loans must be executed under a written loan agreement with arm's-length interest, marked 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' in the SWIFT instruction, disclosed as a liability in the annual financial statements, and reported to the Bundesbank as a liability to a non-resident. Payments from unrelated third-party accounts are rejected as non-compliant; repayment of the subscribed equity is strictly prohibited to avoid repayment-of-capital exposure under GmbHG §§ 30/31.SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for supplier advances, deposits, and open-account settlements: instruction must carry full beneficiary name, IBAN/BIC, and invoice reference; EUR 12,500-plus is automatically captured in AWV § 68 payment statistics. Irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit (subject to UCP 600) for high-value component imports denominated in USD or CNY, typically 90-day usance to match the PCBA assembly-to-delivery cycle; the first LCs will require 100% cash cover until a bank relationship is established. Documentary collections (documents against payment/acceptance) as a cost-effective alternative for repeat low-default-risk suppliers. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viable as a supplementary multi-currency operating rail, not as the primary treasury bank. BaFin-authorised and EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, PPRO, Solaris SE) offer rapid onboarding, free SEPA processing, and cost-effective FX for routine supplier payments. Legal constraints under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and the German KWG/GwG: EMIs cannot accept deposits, cannot grant credit, and cannot issue documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, or documentary collections. EMIs also routinely refuse to receive share-capital injection into a newly incorporated UG before commercial-register registration. Viability rating: High as a secondary rail, Low as a sole institutional relationship.
