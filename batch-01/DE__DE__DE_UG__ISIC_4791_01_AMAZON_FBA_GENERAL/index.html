Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt - Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) for foreign entrepreneurs and centralized e-commerce VAT matters; local tax office (Finanzamt) competent for the UG's registered seat for Steuernummer issuance and ongoing assessments (Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, Umsatzsteuer, Lohnsteuer). Official portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for all electronic filings, and BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de) for VAT ID applications for non-resident sellers.

E-government filing portal

A German UG is incorporated with notarial deed and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht; no minimum capital is required (nominal at least EUR 1) but 25% of annual net profit must be retained until the statutory capital of EUR 25,000 is reached. Within one month of registration, the managing director files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt; the Finanzamt then issues the Steuernummer (tax number, format e.g. 12/345/67890) and the BZSt allocates the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID). Foreign entrepreneurs without a German registered seat apply directly to BZSt for a VAT ID (Form USt 1 TG via BZSt portal) once any taxable German supply, distance sale, or FBA warehousing activity exists; a German fixed establishment via Amazon FBA stock triggers mandatory local VAT registration regardless of turnover. Compliance obligations commence at the earliest of incorporation, commencement of business, or first VAT-relevant activity; monthly VAT advance returns are due by the 10th day after each calendar month.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) and a separate taxable person; no pass-through regime applies; it is resident in Germany if registered seat or place of effective management is in Germany, creating unlimited tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) on worldwide income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) and a separate taxable person; no pass-through regime applies; it is resident in Germany if registered seat or place of effective management is in Germany, creating unlimited tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) on worldwide income. Corporate tax rate is 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825%) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of typically 7% to 17%, yielding an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 29.8% to 33.0%. Non-resident ownership is permitted; dividend distributions to shareholders trigger 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (total 26.375%), subject to treaty relief and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reductions.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box pass-through election for a UG. Statutorily available elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) creating a single taxpayer for corporate and trade tax and a separate VAT umbrella for a German parent and the UG for a fixed five-year term; (ii) VAT real-estate waiver option under §9 UStG to treat land supplies as taxable; (iii) small-business exemption under §19 UStG is not available to a UG as a corporation; (iv) anti-hybrid alignment elections under §4k EStG implementing ATAD II to match entity and financial instrument characterization; (v) the UG's mandatory profit-retention rule (§5a GmbHG) as part of its equity maintenance classification; and (vi) intra-EEA and third-country cross-border loss-election alternatives in treaty and trade-tax context limited to narrow fact patterns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-resident sellers: any taxable supply, distance sale, or physical warehousing in Germany (including Amazon FBA stock) triggers immediate VAT registration. Domestic small-business exemption (§19 UStG) at EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year ceiling applies only to individuals and partnerships, not to a UG. EU OSS distance-sale threshold is EUR 10,000 per year for the entire EU; upon exceedance, B2C distance sales are taxable in the destination member state, with German FBA sales remaining taxable in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies to third countries (non-EU) qualify for zero-rating under §6 UStG if the UG or its fulfillment agent (Amazon FBA) transports or dispatches the goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU; the goods must physically leave EU territory and the entire chain must be evidenced contemporaneously. Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers are exempt under §6a UStG where the customer's valid VAT ID is declared in the periodic recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) and the goods reach another member state. Domestic FBA sales to German or EU consumers are standard-rated at 19% (7% reduced rate for qualifying goods); only provable dispatches to third-country addresses, direct exports, or removal of goods from the German warehouse to a non-EU destination qualify for the 0% rate.

  • Electronically lodged customs export declaration in ATLAS with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and corresponding Export Accompanying Document
  • Commercial invoice compliant with §14 UStG and customs valuation rules
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or postal/parcel carrier dispatch record evidencing departure from the EU
  • Proof of dispatch and arrival (Versendungs- und Belegnachweis) per §§8-13 UStDV, including third-party transport agent receipts
  • Payment evidence: bank SWIFT/SEPA transfer statements, Amazon payout/settlement reports, and inward remittance records of the export proceeds
  • Amazon FBA inventory records showing stock movement from German fulfillment centers to non-EU destinations

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing for all domestic B2B supplies: receiving obligation since 1 January 2025; dispatch obligation based on prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 from 1 January 2027 and for all taxpayers from 1 January 2028. Accepted formats are XStandard, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or EDI. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control regime comparable to Italy's Sistema di Interscambio; tax control is exercised periodically via monthly/quarterly VAT advance returns and annual VAT returns submitted through ELSTER. Invoices must be issued within 6 months of supplies (§14 UStG) and stored for 10 years; GOBD record-keeping principles apply to the UG's bookkeeping and Amazon sales documentation.

Germany imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is a program-monitoring default and not a German legal requirement. Export zero-rating is a high-fraud audit focus. Incorrectly claimed zero-ratings are clawed back with additional VAT assessments at 19%, late-payment interest of 0.15% per month (§233a AO), late-filing penalties (Verspätungszuschlag up to EUR 25,000), and refusal of input VAT recovery for counter-parties. Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine, increasing to 6 months minimum and up to 10 years in aggravated cases involving false customs documents or large-scale operations. Retrospective assessment periods extend to 10 years for intentional evasion, and managing directors face personal and criminal liability. Incorrect VAT shown on invoices creates liability under §14c UStG for the full amount stated. Persistent evasion triggers revocation of VAT IDs, Amazon marketplace disclosure obligations, anti-money-laundering reporting under the GwG, and potential forced liquidation or director disqualification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has no general exchange controls or mandatory conversion of foreign currency. Current-account and capital-account transactions are generally freely convertible and transferable, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, tax obligations, and applicable EU and German restrictions. UG profits may generally be distributed after approval of annual accounts and satisfaction of statutory reserve and capital-maintenance rules. Dividends paid to nonresidents are generally subject to German withholding tax of 25% plus the solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty relief, EU-law relief where applicable, and procedural requirements. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profit, after loss carryforwards, in its statutory reserve until the conditions for conversion to a GmbH are met. Cross-border payments may trigger AWV reporting; reporting is statistical and is not an approval or remittance restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory deadline or mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio applies to export proceeds received by a German UG. Proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's discretion. AWV reporting may apply to cross-border payments between German residents and nonresidents exceeding EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exceptions and reporting rules; the company should verify the current threshold, classification, deadline, and exemptions with the Bundesbank before filing. Separate Intrastat or customs declarations may apply to goods movements, depending on transaction and reporting thresholds; these are not export-proceeds surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The European Central Bank also conducts euro-area monetary policy. BaFin supervises banks and relevant financial services providers; it is not Germany's foreign-exchange control authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Remit subscribed share capital from the shareholder's own verified account to the UG account designated for formation or capital funding, using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within SEPA or SWIFT for cross-border/non-SEPA transfers. Use a truthful reference such as 'share capital contribution' and identify the shareholder and UG; do not describe equity as a loan or sales revenue. Retain the subscription documents, bank credit advice, remitter-account evidence, and ledger entry. Additional shareholder funding must be documented as equity or a properly authorized and documented loan. There is no general German FX purpose-code regime for ordinary commercial remittances; provide the bank's requested purpose and supporting documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for payment operations, subject to provider eligibility, German UG onboarding, supported currencies, and transaction limits. An EU-authorized electronic money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts and cross-border services under its authorization and passporting rights, but an EMI account is not necessarily a bank deposit and may not be accepted for every notarial, registry, tax, lending, or capital-confirmation purpose. Confirm that the provider can receive and evidence the UG's subscribed share-capital contribution before relying on it for formation. Amazon Pay, Stripe, and Airwallex are payment-service relationships, not substitutes for a corporate bank account where a bank account is required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if annual revenue exceeds EUR 10 million; Master File required if group revenue exceeds EUR 100 million; additionally, a contemporaneous TP documentation (transfer pricing study) must be prepared for transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 with related parties

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but requires genuine economic substance to benefit from treaty provisions and to avoid anti-abuse rules under Section 42 AO (General Anti-Abuse Rule). The UG must have real business activities, including physical presence, employees, management decisions, and operational substance in Germany. A shell company without substance may be denied treaty benefits and subject to recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; if committed on a large scale or with intent, imprisonment up to 10 years; additional penalty of up to 10% of the evaded tax (Section 370 AO)

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers can be held personally liable for evaded taxes if they acted with intent or gross negligence; civil restitution, travel bans, and asset freezes may be imposed; in severe cases, extradition may be sought
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of additional taxes; penalties under Section 42 AO; potential criminal charges for abuse of legal forms

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for additional taxes and penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham structure; travel bans and asset seizures may be ordered
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 or 10% of annual turnover (for serious breaches); criminal penalties for money laundering under Section 261 StGB (imprisonment up to 5 years)

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML violations if they failed to appoint a compliance officer or implement required controls; travel bans and restrictions on business activities may be imposed
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 5% of the adjusted amount (minimum EUR 5,000) for missing documentation; additional late payment interest of 0.5% per month

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they failed to ensure proper documentation; in cases of gross negligence, criminal liability for tax evasion may arise
