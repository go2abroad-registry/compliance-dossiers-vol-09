Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the locally competent district court (Amtsgericht) for the GmbH’s registered office; filing is transmitted electronically by a German notary.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), particularly sections 1–3 (formation and articles), 5 (share capital), 7–8 (registration and filing documents), 10 (registration particulars), and 35 (management representation); Commercial Register filings are governed by the German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB), including section 12.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for initial registration of a GmbH in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister), filed electronically in publicly certified form by a notary, together with the notarized articles of association or formation deed and statutory supporting documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 44 business days (SOP sum)

For registration, cash contributions must be paid so that at least one quarter of each subscribed share and at least EUR 12,500 in aggregate have been paid; contributions must be available to the managing director without restriction. No statutory escrow account is required. The bank account is commonly opened for the company in formation (GmbH i.G.) to receive contributions. Sacheinlagen are subject to additional statutory documentation and valuation requirements.

Funding & unlock path: No sector-specific civil-defense or manufacturing clearance ordinarily applies. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (federal policy body); Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for USt-IdNr. and OSS administration; locally competent Finanzamt for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll withholding, and VAT assessment. Official e-portal: Mein ELSTER via www.elster.de; EU OSS portal administered by BZSt.

E-government filing portal

The GmbH is incorporated by notarial deed and registered in the Handelsregister at the Amtsgericht. Within 4 weeks of formation, the Geschäftsführer files the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER with the local Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer and registers the GmbH for Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, payroll withholding, and VAT. A separate application to BZSt yields the cross-border Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.). Non-resident owners may act without a domestic fiscal agent for income tax, but a permanent establishment of a foreign entity requires registration under Sec. 139b AO.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the GmbH is a per-se Körperschaft without tax transparency; it is a resident taxpayer subject to unlimited worldwide taxation with foreign tax credit (Anrechnungsmethode) and PE carve-outs. Profits bear corporation tax at 15% plus solidarity surcharge of 0.825% and municipal trade tax (effective 14-17% depending on the municipal Hebesatz). Dividends to shareholders are separately taxed at shareholder level (Teileinkünfteverfahren / 26.375% Abgeltungsteuer for individuals, 95% exemption for corporate shareholders).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; the GmbH is mandatorily an opaque corporation. Available elections: (a) Organschaft / VAT fiscal unity under UStG Sec. 2(2) requiring financial, economic, and organisational integration, plus a corporate/trade tax profit-and-loss pooling agreement (KStG Sec. 14) with a minimum 5-year term; (b) small-enterprise VAT exemption under Sec. 19 UStG for gross turnover up to EUR 25,000 from 2025 where no reverse-charge exposure exists; (c) trade tax option for rental income under Sec. 9 GewStG; (d) hybrid mismatch adjustments under Sec. 4k EStG require elective documentation and structuring responses where foreign check-the-box entities or transparent partnerships are involved.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For inbound B2B executive-search services by a non-resident provider, no German VAT registration is required because the German business recipient accounts for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism (Sec. 13b UStG). A foreign supplier must register only if it makes B2C supplies or other local non-recipient-accounted supplies. Internal small-business threshold: EUR 25,000 gross annual turnover (Sec. 19 UStG, effective 2025). Intra-EU distance-selling and OSS non-union thresholds: EUR 10,000 total cross-border turnover; no threshold applies to B2C digital/telecom/broadcasting services via the Non-Union OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Executive-search and headhunting fees invoiced to a foreign business customer are B2B supplies under Sec. 3a(2) UStG; the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction, so the German supply is outside the scope of German VAT and invoiced at 0% (reverse-charge in the customer's state). Eligibility requires: (i) valid EU VAT ID or equivalent foreign business tax ID of the customer; (ii) written engagement contract confirming business-client status and establishment abroad; (iii) invoice statement of the reason for non-chargeability under Sec. 14(4) UStG; (iv) actual repatriation of the fee to the GmbH's German bank account. Ancillary export of physical materials is zero-rated only with customs export evidence (MRN via ATLAS/AES).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument, AES/ATLAS MRN) where goods are exported as ancillary supplies
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document for any physical export; for services: signed engagement agreement, candidate search deliverables, placement reports, and chronological time/work evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Germany issues no statutory IRC; the operative equivalent is the bank credit note/Kontoauszug evidencing receipt of the foreign payment in EUR or FX converted to EUR
  • Valid foreign customer VAT ID or foreign business-identification certificate (with Recapitulative Statement confirmation for EU customers)
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) referencing the contract and the statutory reason for 0% VAT under Sec. 14(4) UStG

Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions entered into force on 1 January 2025: all German businesses must be able to receive e-invoices, and issuers must transmit structured invoices conforming to EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.3). Phased issuer obligation: from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000; from 1 January 2028 for all remaining B2B issuers. B2G invoicing requires XRechnung/ZUGFeRD via federal portals. Germany operates no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control; enforcement is achieved through denial of input VAT deduction and administrative fines up to EUR 2,500 per non-compliant invoice (Sec. 26a UStG).

Where the 0% regime is claimed without substantiation, the Finanzamt retrospectively assesses 19% VAT on the gross fee, plus interest of 0.5% per month (Sec. 233a AO, i.e., 6% p.a.). Fraudulent or abusive schemes (sham foreign customer, concealed German beneficiary, unrepatriated proceeds without commercial justification beyond approximately 180 days) are prosecuted as tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO with imprisonment of up to 5 years, and up to 10 years in aggravated cases, along with fines up to the full evaded amount, restitution, revocation of input VAT deductions, joint liability of the Geschäftsführer, loss of the USt-IdNr., and adverse Negativattest consequences for the supply-chain contract portfolio.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates under full currency convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions by virtue of EU Treaty obligations (Article 63 TFEU) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). Profit remittance, dividend distribution, repatriation of capital, and shareholder-loan repayment are permitted without central-bank approval or quantitative limits. The practical review by a German commercial bank focuses on the underlying commercial purpose, money-laundering registries, and tax compliance, not on exchange-control authorization. For a GmbH conducting executive-search and headhunting services under ISIC 7830, inward retainers and outward success-fee payments to candidates or freelance sub-contractors are treated as ordinary service-transaction flows; there is no restriction on receiving or disbursing foreign currencies. Sanction screening and embargo compliance constitute the only material non-tariff monetary barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose export-proceeds surrender obligations or mandatory conversion ratios. There is no statutory timeline requiring conversion of export proceeds into EUR. Under Section 59 et seq. of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), all cross-border payments received by a resident from non-residents must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical balance-of-payments purposes if the aggregate amount of such payments in a calendar month exceeds EUR 12,500. The reporting obligation is fulfilled by the receiving bank when the transaction contains the necessary purpose code; for payments outside SEPA, the resident entity remains responsible for a timely declaration. For export of professional services (headhunting retainer income), the corporate treasury function must ensure that each inbound remittance carries a precise purpose code (e.g., SWIFT MT103 field 70 designation identifying executive-search services) so that Bundesbank statistics correctly classify the proceeds. No part of the proceeds requires surrender to the central bank; the company may maintain a EUR operating account or multi-currency accounts at its discretion.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main), in cooperation with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for prudential banking supervision and the Federal Office of Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) for foreign-trade control matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — permissible for transactional flow only. An EEA-licensed electronic-money institution (EMI) or payment institution operating in Germany under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may provide cross-border payment receipt, currency conversion, and pay-out services; however, it may not perform deposit-taking, issue commercial loans, or serve as the sole banking relationship for a GmbH's capital escrow account. Legal constraints are substantial: non-EEA EMIs cannot maintain a German-licensed account without a passported license; the corporate account must ultimately be held with a credit institution authorised under the German Banking Act (KWG). For this headhunting entity, Wise B2B and similar e-money rails can support expedited small-amount retainer receipts and candidate pay-outs, but all large-ticket international mandates must be cleared through a tier-1 German commercial bank or a legally recognised escrow agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound non-resident capital injection into a German GmbH must be executed via SWIFT MT103 using the receiving bank's corporate-banking interface. The payment narrative must clearly contain the statutory purpose code and a free-text 'Verwendungszweck' consistent with the German balance-of-payments classification, e.g., 'Gesellschaftereinlage' (shareholder contribution) or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' (shareholder loan). For capital increase in the commercial register, the instruction should reference the HRB number, the notarial deed number, and the tax number (Steuernummer). Verification additionally requires proof of the corporate resolution, a register extract showing the new share capital, and the UBO identification in accordance with the GwG. The receiving bank performs sanctions screening and negative-news checks; any flagged source jurisdiction requires the payer to provide a source of funds certificate, the corporate charter, and the economic rationale of the investment. A Deutsche Bundesbank statistics report on the capital transaction is generated automatically through the SWIFT payment data, but the treasury function must reconcile the bank's reference number with the notarial closing documents for audit purposes.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade and service disbursements are SWIFT MT103 messages in qualifies of immediate transfers; for EUR-denominated payments, the SEPA infrastructure is used. Settlement to third-country vendors, candidate contractors, and service partners must include a structured invoice reference, a payment reason code, and contractual documents attached only if requested by the counterparty's bank. For high-value fees and candidate placement bonuses where secure documentary separation is required, the parties may adopt a documentary letter of credit or escrow arrangement under a common bank guarantee. An irrevocable commercial LC issued by a German tier-1 bank and confirmed by a distinct advising bank is accepted in recruitment service supply contracts where part of the consideration is conditioned on successful hiring after a protection period. All outbound disbursements from the GmbH treasury shall undergo automated AEOI classification, tax withholding checks (e.g., German trade tax and dividend withholding where relevant), and strict audit-trail preservation at the level of the payment instruction and supporting invoice.Plain vanilla FX forwards on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and EUR/CHF executed under an ISDA 2002 or German framework agreement with a licensed commercial bank, limited to underlying retainer receivables or contractual liabilities.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNH, INR, IDR, BRL) where physical delivery is legally or practically impossible, designated specifically for negotiated candidate-placement or cross-border client fees.Cross-currency swaps (FX swap or currency swap) to align shareholder-loan repayments and foreign-currency capital inflows with EUR liquidity, subject to HGB hedge documentary requirements.Zero premium FX options and collars used as insurance against adverse moves in retainer collections from clients invoiced in non-EEA currencies, in each case without recourse to speculative trading motives, and each transaction requiring initial and periodic board approvals.
