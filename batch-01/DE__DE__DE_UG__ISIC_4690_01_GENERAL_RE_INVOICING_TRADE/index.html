Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) as supreme policy body; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) as central federal tax office responsible for the EU VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), recapitulative statements, refunds to foreign entities, and certain central data procedures; routine tax administration is delegated to the tax authorities of the Länder and exercised by the locally competent Finanzamt; official e-filing portal is ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at elster.de.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the UG formation agreement and file for registration with the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the competent Amtsgericht; Step 2: submit the electronic questionnaire for statutory tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER, including registered seat, shareholders, forecast turnover, and beneficial ownership data; the local Finanzamt then assigns the statutory tax number (Steuernummer) and sets the advance VAT return cycle; Step 3: separately apply to BZSt for the cross-border VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the Steuernummer and commercial register extract; BZSt verifies the data and issues the VAT ID, generally within two to eight weeks; Step 4: for a non-resident shareholder structure, provide identification of the ultimate beneficial owner, proof of registered office, and evidence of appointment of a person authorized to accept service in Germany; the UG must maintain a compliant bank account for inward FX receipts and outward payments; no statutory fiscal representative is mandatory for income tax purposes, but ELSTER authorizations for a tax adviser are recommended as a practical requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a UG is a separate taxable legal person resident in Germany if its statutory seat and place of management are located in Germany; it bears unlimited German tax liability on worldwide income; pass-through or fiscal transparency is not available; profits are subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15%, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) producing a combined effective burden of approximately 30%; no territorial exemption zone or preferential special regime applies; foreign income is exempt or credited under double taxation treaties and domestic unilateral rules.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box election for resident UGs; the available statutory elections include: (i) corporate tax grouping and VAT grouping (Organschaft) under sections 14-18 KStG and section 2(2) No. 2 UStG, conditional on majority shareholding and a profit and loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (ii) option to tax real estate supplies and certain leases under section 9 UStG; (iii) small-business VAT regulation under section 19 UStG when annual turnover is at or below EUR 22,000 (EUR 25,000 from fiscal year 2025), combined with the right to waive the exemption and remain VAT registered to preserve input VAT recovery; (iv) restructuring neutrality elections under the Umwandlungssteuergesetz for mergers, demergers, asset transfers, and legal-form conversions; (v) unilateral classification of foreign hybrid entities under section 1(1) AStG for German tax purposes; and (vi) hybrid mismatch neutralization mechanisms under sections 14-15 AStG relevant to re-invoicing structures involving interposed opaque or transparent entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary turnover threshold for VAT registration: every person performing an entrepreneurial activity under section 2 UStG must register with the Finanzamt; the Kleinunternehmer (small-business) exemption applies below EUR 22,000 annual taxable turnover (raised to EUR 25,000 for fiscal year 2025), exempting output VAT but precluding input VAT deduction; intra-EU distance-sales and OSS registration applies at EUR 10,000 cumulative pan-EU turnover for B2C supplies; for a B2B re-invoicing hub, the UG is compulsorily VAT-registered irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods to a third-country destination qualify for the 0% rate under sections 4 No. 1 and 6 UStG provided the UG is the supplier, the goods are delivered or transported out of the European Union, and the exporter can prove the export through documentary evidence (Belegnachweis) and accounting evidence (Buchnachweis); intra-Community deliveries to EU business customers qualify for 0% under section 6a UStG where the buyer is an entrepreneur holding a valid VAT ID, the goods physically move to another Member State, and the supplier files the recapitulative statement; cross-border services supplied to foreign entrepreneurs are placed in the customer's country under section 3a UStG and are therefore not subject to German VAT, so no German zero-rating declaration is required; commissionaires and Streckengeschäfte (drop-shipment re-invoicing chains) must evidence the actual contractual chain and physical movement of goods to avoid domestic VAT; export zero-rating fails if the goods do not physically depart the EU or if the documentary proof is incomplete.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed via ATLAS, evidenced by the Export Accompanying Document with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and, where used, the NCTS transit document
  • International transport document: signed bill of lading (B/L), airway bill (AWB), or CMR consignment note showing physical movement out of the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement demonstrating receipt of the foreign currency sales price by the UG
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and weight note, supplemented where relevant by the destination-country import customs evidence and proof of preferential origin (EUR.1 or ATR)
  • Proof of the customer's status for intra-Community supplies, comprising a valid EU VAT identification number of the buyer and evidence that the goods were dispatched to another Member State

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing under the E-Rechnungsverordnung: from 1 January 2025 every liable person must be able to receive e-invoices in the EN 16931 data model (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices, and from 1 January 2028 all businesses must issue e-invoices; Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API stamping, or digital invoice sealing for B2B invoices; VAT returns and advance VAT returns are filed electronically via ELSTER in XML by the 10th day following the end of each filing period; the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) for intra-community supplies must be transmitted to BZSt by the 25th day of the following reporting period; where cash-register hardware is used in retail operations, the KassenSichV mandates certified fiscal devices and TSE modules, although this does not apply to the re-invoicing business itself.

The Finanzamt audits export zero-rating by verifying the documentary and accounting evidence; missing or inaccurate Belegnachweis or Buchnachweis reclassifies the supply as domestic and demands VAT at 19% retrospectively; the assessment period is four years in standard cases and ten years in cases of tax evasion under section 169(2) AO; intentional tax evasion under section 370 AO is punishable by imprisonment up to five years, raising to ten years for particularly serious cases, together with monetary fines; negligent or reckless endangerment of VAT revenues constitutes an administrative offence with fines up to EUR 30,000; issuing invoices carrying a non-existent tax amount or enabling an unjustified input VAT deduction exposes the UG to administrative fines under section 26a UStG of up to EUR 5,000 per case; retrospective clawbacks attract late-payment surcharges of 1% per commenced month under section 240 AO and statutory tax interest of 0.5% per month under section 233a AO; managing directors, beneficial owners and arranged parties may be held jointly liable under sections 69 and 71 AO; in cross-border fraud patterns, customs authorities and public prosecutor offices apply seizure measures and forfeiture of goods, and the VAT recovery chain may be denied on abuse grounds under section 42 AO.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions under EU law; there are no statutory exchange controls, no profit remittance licensing, and EUR-denominated shareholder distributions may be made freely to foreign investors, subject only to corporate law formalities, adequate proof of tax residence, potential withholding tax documentation such as German tax residence certificates or AEA records, and anti-money laundering screening. Re-invoicing trade payments into and out of German bank accounts are not subject to Central Bank approval, but cross-border payments must satisfy the Bundesbank's External Economic Ordinance reporting, with statistical reporting required on a monthly basis for transactions exceeding EUR 12,500, aggregating debit and credit payments per country; no substantive restriction or balancing requirement applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds in Germany. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted freely at commercial bank rates; the primary compliance obligation is statistical declaration to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung and the Reporting Regulation, requiring the submission of cross-border payment reports via bank channels or Department Z4 forms once cumulative monthly payments with a non-resident counterparty exceed EUR 12,500. Re-invoicing trading companies must retain source documents for five years to substantiate the purpose, value, and country allocation of all export-related money movements.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank), with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) as the supervisory authority for banking, payment and AML compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Risk. German and other EEA electronic money institutions and payment institutions holding an EEA authorization may passport services into Germany and offer SEPA and SWIFT clearing to commercial clients without a physical German branch; however, German treasury counterparties and tax authorities may treat such accounts with lower substance weight, FSC controls may not include treasury-grade counterparty exposure, and many EMI acceptors impose concentration limits, restrict high-risk ISIC 4690 re-invoicing flows, and require detailed purpose and beneficiary disclosures. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA face high legal constraints because they cannot validly provide payment services to German residents without a German passporting license or BaFin license; any such arrangement is unlawful unless the EMI is licensed in a third country with a specific German or EEA passported authorization.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial equity or shareholder loan funding to the German UG re-invoicing entity should be routed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for interbank intermediary legs and flagged with the ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI for equity capital or INTC for intercompany financing if required by the sending bank; actual German bank beneficiaries will map the transaction to the Bundesbank reporting nomenclature. For equity injections, documentary verification consists of the notarized capital contribution agreement, the commercial register entry or pending registration certificate, and the depository bank's confirmation of contributions received; for shareholder loans, a signed loan agreement, interest-rate calculation, maturity schedule, and registration in the tax balance sheet are mandatory. The inbound remittance must be made in a transparent, unbroken wire chain from the shareholder of record to a dedicated project account, with no third-party repayment layers. The receiving German bank will execute standard sanctions screening and may request a completed Verwendungszweck or purpose-of-payment declaration; the Bundesbank reporting obligation is triggered by the monthly cross-border threshold, but the UG treasury should nevertheless notify the Bundesbank department Z4 for all inbound capital receipts.Trade settlements may be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for T/T wires, SWIFT MT700 or MT710 for issuance of commercial documentary letters of credit, and documentary collections using MT400, MT412, or MT752 for LC payments; the German entity should instruct the bank with a precise MT103 field 70 narrative identifying the underlying invoice and contract and use the trade purpose code for goods trade with reference to the German Außenwirtschaftsprüfung classification. Payments denominated in EUR to non-resident suppliers in the EEA can use SEPA wire with minimal fees, but cross-currency disbursements in USD, GBP, or CHF require correspondent banking via one of the large clearers; German banks will internally allocate an IBAN or ABA number and ultimate beneficiary, subject to end-to-end UBO name checks and transaction monitoring. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a 15.825% combined federal rate. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional: the federal base rate is 3.5% multiplied by the municipality’s Hebesatz. The combined corporate and trade tax burden commonly approximates 30%, but depends on the municipality and tax base.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, for an effective domestic rate of 26.375%, before any church tax or relief. Treaty rates and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may reduce or eliminate withholding where their ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements are met.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual corporate income tax, trade tax, and ordinarily VAT returns are due by 31 July of the year following the tax year for a calendar-year taxpayer filing without a tax adviser. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the general statutory deadline is the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory extensions and special rules. The tax office may require earlier filing. Corporate income tax and trade tax advance payments are generally due quarterly on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, a Local File is generally required where annual transaction volume exceeds EUR 6 million for deliveries of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services and financing. A Master File is generally required where the taxpayer belongs to a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 100 million. Separate documentation may be required for extraordinary business transactions regardless of these thresholds. German rules and applicable documentation deadlines must be checked for the relevant tax year.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore regimes. German tax authorities may nevertheless scrutinize actual functions, assets, risks, decision-making, personnel, premises, beneficial ownership, and business purpose under transfer-pricing, anti-abuse, treaty, controlled-foreign-company, and general tax rules. A re-invoicing hub with limited personnel or decision-making must substantiate its commercial functions, control of risks, pricing, logistics, and contractual allocation of responsibilities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion or fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code may be punished by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax arrears, interest, and related assessments remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability and civil tax-liability exposure for culpable breach of duties. Imprisonment is possible after conviction. Travel bans are not an automatic tax penalty; restrictions require a separate legal basis.
Reckless tax understatement or negligent tax compliance

Statutory sanction: Reckless tax understatement under section 378 of the Fiscal Code may result in a fine of up to EUR 50,000, in addition to tax, interest, and applicable surcharges.

Director personal liability: A director may be fined or held liable where statutory duties are breached. Criminal imprisonment is not the ordinary sanction for the administrative offence itself, but separate offences may apply.
Late or missing tax returns and unpaid tax

Statutory sanction: Late-filing and late-payment surcharges, interest, estimated assessments, enforcement measures, and, for culpable withholding failures, potential criminal or administrative proceedings may apply. A director’s intentional failure to remit taxes withheld for another may constitute a criminal offence under applicable law.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable under section 69 of the Fiscal Code for tax claims caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties, including certain failures to pay taxes when due.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Taxable income may be adjusted, with additional tax and interest. Documentation failures can trigger statutory surcharges, including a percentage-based surcharge subject to statutory minimums and caps, and the authority may estimate income where records are inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for culpable breach of duties and, where conduct is intentional or fraudulent, separate tax or criminal exposure. A transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create criminal liability.
Abuse of treaty or EU withholding-tax relief

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied or withdrawn; withholding tax, interest, surcharges, and penalties may be assessed. False submissions can lead to tax-evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize false claims may face personal criminal and civil exposure. Shareholder status alone does not automatically pierce the UG’s limited-liability protection.
Money laundering, sanctions, or trade-based financial crime

Statutory sanction: Consequences depend on the offence and may include criminal fines or imprisonment, asset seizure, confiscation of proceeds, sanctions-related penalties, and restrictions on business activity. AML and sanctions duties depend on the company’s regulated status and transaction facts.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible employees may be personally prosecuted for participation, authorization, or culpable compliance failures. Travel restrictions require a separate legal basis and are not an automatic consequence.
Fraudulent or sham re-invoicing and VAT abuse

Statutory sanction: VAT may be denied or assessed, with interest and surcharges; intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion. Invoicing and recordkeeping violations may attract additional statutory penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may face tax, civil, and criminal liability where they knowingly participate in, authorize, or negligently fail to prevent the conduct. Limited liability does not protect individuals from their own misconduct.
Insolvency and creditor-prejudice offences

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include repayment claims, director liability, insolvency-related fines, and criminal penalties under applicable insolvency and company-law provisions. The applicable filing and payment duties depend on the UG’s financial condition and timing.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal civil liability for prohibited payments and losses, and criminal liability for specified insolvency offences. Personal exposure is fact-specific and is not an automatic piercing of the corporate veil.
