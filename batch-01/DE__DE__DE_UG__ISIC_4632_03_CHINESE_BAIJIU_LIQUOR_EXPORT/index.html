Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) and Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt); operational assessment, Steuernummer issuance, and VAT enforcement handled by the local tax office (Finanzamt) having jurisdiction over the UG's registered seat. Official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at elster.de; VAT ID applications via BZStOnline-Portal.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated UG must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of commencing business. The Finanzamt issues the national tax number (Steuernummer), which is the primary TIN for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment. Separately, an electronic application must be submitted to the BZSt (BZStOnline-Portal) to obtain the statutory VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under § 27a UStG. Foreign non-established entities that are not yet registered may apply directly to the BZSt for a VAT ID or, where required, appoint a fiscal representative. A UG cannot use the founder's personal TIN; each legal entity must procure its own Steuernummer and USt-IdNr.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not provide US-style check-the-box elections; a UG is mandatorily treated as a corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), with no pass-through eligibility. Available elections include: (i) Organschaft tax grouping election to consolidate a financially and operationally integrated subsidiary into a single VAT and corporate income/trade tax taxpayer; (ii) the small-enterprise exemption under § 19 UStG (optional for turnover up to €22,000, raised to €25,000 from 1 January 2025, with a forward ceiling of €100,000); (iii) the option to waive VAT exemption on real estate lettings; and (iv) certain anti-hybrid ordering elections, though hybrid mismatch outcomes are generally disallowed by § 4k EStG and ATAD-implementing provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic registration threshold for German-resident entrepreneurs; any UG making taxable supplies must register and file VAT returns. A reduced-compliance exemption applies to small entrepreneurs with turnover not exceeding €22,000 (€25,000 from 2025) and expected turnover below €100,000 in the following year. For intra-EU distance selling of goods, including spirits, an OSS registration is required once cross-border distance sales exceed €10,000 per calendar year and the customer is a private individual. Non-established foreign suppliers are required to register (or use the reverse-charge mechanism) when making taxable supplies in Germany; since 1 July 2021, marketplace operators may be deemed the supplier in certain B2C scenarios.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods to third countries (outside the EU) are zero-rated under § 6 UStG provided the goods are physically transported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf to a destination outside the EU and the supplier obtains conclusive documentary and bookkeeping proof of the export (Ausfuhrnachweis). Intra-Community supplies to another EU member state are zero-rated under § 6a UStG where the customer is an entrepreneur acting as such, holds a valid VAT ID, and the goods are dispatched or transported to another member state; an Intrastat declaration may be required. For Chinese Baijiu sold to non-EU markets, zero-rating applies when customs export formalities are completed; for EU-bound bulk distribution, the UG must verify the customer's VAT ID and retain transport evidence. Export-related services (e.g., international freight, logistics, and selling commission) may be zero-rated where the B2B recipient is a third-country entrepreneur under the § 3a UStG place-of-supply rules, subject to the reverse-charge documentation.

  • Handelsrechnung (commercial invoice) meeting the German export invoice content requirements

Germany does not yet mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for B2B VAT. Domestic B2B e-invoicing is being phased in: from 1 January 2025, all domestic suppliers must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XInvoice/ZUGFeRD 2.x or EDI equivalent); from 1 January 2027, taxpayers with annual turnover above €800,000 must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions; from 1 January 2028, all taxpayers must issue such e-invoices. Export invoices are exempt from the e-invoicing obligation but must be issued within six months of supply. B2C retail receipts in Germany must be protected by BSI-certified technical security equipment (TSE) to record every transaction digitally, with the data transmitted to the tax authority only upon audit request.

The Finanzamt rigorously audits export zero-rating under §§ 8-13 UStDV and requires the exporter to substantiate the physical exit of goods within the statutory documentation window; failure to produce valid export confirmation triggers a retrospective VAT assessment at 19% on the invoice value, plus late-payment interest under § 233a AO and a possible surcharge under § 26c UStG of up to €25,000 or 10% of the tax understated. Fraudulent or forged export evidence constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years or fines; the authorities may retrospectively claw back input VAT credits, deny zero-rating for the entire consignment, impose liability on managing directors (Geschäftsführer) for corporate tax debts, and, in serious cross-border cases, coordinate with foreign customs and tax intelligence via the EU Mutual Assistance and anti-fraud network.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a fully convertible euro-area market economy. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; there are no exchange controls, no prior central-bank approval for outward or inward remittances, and no profit-remittance licensing. The UG may freely convert EUR to CNY/CNH, repatriate dividends, repay shareholder loans, and settle trade payables to the Chinese supplier. The practical constraints are limited to (i) the AWV statistical reporting obligation for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500, (ii) the German withholding-tax regime on dividends paid to the Chinese shareholder, which is a tax-stage matter rather than a treasury convertibility barrier, and (iii) normal bank KYC/AML documentation for the underlying commercial transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no country-specific repatriation timeline for receipts. The applicable discipline is the AWV Zahlungsbilanzstatistik reporting regime: the resident UG must report every payment received from or made to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank on the Z1 statistical form, normally by the fifth working day of the month following the payment month. Because the German UG acts as the import and wholesale-distribution vehicle for Chinese baijiu rather than as the exporter-seller, an 'export-proceeds surrender' rule is not triggered; the operative duties are the Z1 reporting of the inbound Chinese equity or shareholder-loan capital injection and of all outbound CNY/EUR trade settlements to the Chinese supplier.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, Frankfurt am Main) acting together with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for banking supervision and with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action / Federal Ministry of Finance for the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV) foreign-trade and payments-control framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Chinese shareholder should route the equity contribution or subordinated shareholder loan by SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) in EUR to the UG's operational account at the German bank. The MT103 Remittance Information field must be exclusively coded to identify the transaction as a capital contribution ('Kapitalzuführung Stammeinlage') or shareholder loan ('Gesellschafterdarlehen'), with reference to the executed contribution or loan agreement, and must never be mixed with commercial invoice payments. The UG must file the Z1 statistical report with the Deutsche Bundesbank by the fifth working day of the month following receipt and must maintain the underlying Einlagevertrag or Darlehensvertrag, the Chinese bank's outward-remittance confirmation evidencing SAFE registration, and the German bank's credit-side documentary file. If the outstanding cross-border receivable/liability balance exceeds EUR 5,000,000, the UG must also submit the quarterly AWV balance-of-payments report.Two clearing rails are used for baijiu trade settlements. First, for standard restocking, a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer is executed in EUR or CNH to the Chinese exporter's account at a Chinese bank; the UG instructs its German bank to convert EUR into CNH/CNY in the bank's interbank book, with the payment purpose declared under the AWV goods-import statistical category. Import customs duties and spirits excise tax are settled separately by SEPA credit transfer to the Generalzollamt / Generaldirektion Zoll, which is an EU-domestic clearing rail rather than SWIFT.
