Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

UG minimum nominal capital is EUR 1.00, fully payable in cash before filing; the contribution must be transferred to an account opened in the company's name and confirmed as freely disposable to the managing director (Freiverfügungsbestätigung). No statutory escrow account is required, but a blocked-account or pledge confirmation may be issued by the bank. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5a(3)). Notary fees are additional and tariff-regulated.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office for issuance of the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.); local Finanzamt (competent tax office) for the institutional tax number (Steuernummer) covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment; public electronic filing and registration portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident founders of a German UG procure the Steuernummer by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt, either electronically through ELSTER with a qualified electronic signature or in writing via a domestic authorized representative (tax advisor or fiscal agent). Simultaneously, the UG applies to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (Form USt 1 TN or ELSTER application); registration is retroactively effective from the first taxable German supply once statutory requirements are met; subsequent accounts are filed via ELSTER in accordance with the tax assessment period cycle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de minimis registration threshold applies to a German-established UG or to foreign entrepreneurs once any taxable supply is made in Germany; the EUR 25,000 small-business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG is limited to resident small enterprises and is subject to the EUR 100,000 expected-turnover ceiling in the current tax year (post-2025 legislative state); intra-Community acquisitions and distance sales trigger mandatory VAT registration regardless of turnover level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated treatment applies under Sec. 6 UStG to dispatches of electric cargo e-bikes to third-country territories leaving the EU customs territory and under Sec. 4 No. 1(a) together with Sec. 6a UStG to intra-Community B2B supplies where the customer holds a valid foreign VAT ID; eligibility conditions: the UG acts as an entrepreneur, the physical dispatch is completed within the statutory period (export proof within three months after the end of the VAT pre-registration period under Sec. 10 Export Proof Regulation / NachweisVO), and documentary evidence of dispatch or export transport is maintained in accordance with Sec. 6(4) UStG and Sec. 17a UStDV; offshore services and processing work physically performed on goods destined for third countries additionally qualify for zero rating under Sec. 6(1) No. 2-3 UStG.

  • Bill of Lading / CMR international consignment note / Air Waybill evidencing the physical cross-border dispatch from Germany to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (INR) or documentary bank credit evidence substantiating the foreign buyer's payment flow and supporting the export bookkeeping and currency account reconciliation

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general exchange controls, capital-repatriation approval requirement, mandatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts, or general restriction on transfers of dividends, interest, royalties, loan principal, or liquidation proceeds. Transfers remain subject to bank anti-money-laundering, sanctions, counterparty, beneficial-ownership, tax, and source-of-funds controls. A UG may distribute profits only from distributable after-tax annual profit, subject to German statutory accounting rules, preservation of registered capital, the mandatory UG reserve allocation under Section 5a of the German Limited Liability Companies Act, prior-loss absorption, and shareholder-resolution requirements. Dividend payments to non-residents may generally require German withholding tax at 25 percent plus solidarity surcharge, subject to domestic exemptions, applicable tax treaties, and qualifying EU parent-subsidiary relief. Interest and royalty payments may have separate withholding-tax, treaty, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse implications. Cross-border related-party financing must comply with arm's-length transfer-pricing, corporate-benefit, thin-capitalization or interest-limitation, and documentation requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring an exporter to convert or repatriate export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and contractual bank requirements. German residents must provide statistical reports to Deutsche Bundesbank where applicable under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act framework and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance. Reporting obligations depend on transaction type, residency, amount thresholds, and applicable Bundesbank reporting procedures; they are not a currency-conversion obligation. Payment-service providers and banks may separately request commercial documentation and transaction-purpose information.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) supervises relevant banking, payment-services, investment-services, and anti-money-laundering activities. Germany applies European Union capital-movement, payment-services, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary payment accounts and international collections; Low to Medium as the sole treasury bank for a newly formed German UG. A licensed European Economic Area payment institution or electronic-money institution may provide IBANs, payment accounts, foreign-exchange services, and certain cross-border payment services under its licence and passporting regime, but it is not a German deposit-taking bank unless separately licensed. Customer funds are generally safeguarded rather than covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme. Offshore or non-European digital EMIs may face correspondent-bank, licensing, German market-access, sanctions, safeguarding, and onboarding restrictions and should not be used for regulated payment services in Germany without an appropriate legal basis. EMI accounts may not support statutory capital deposit evidence, cash-intensive operations, commercial letters of credit, guarantee facilities, borrowing, or complex treasury requirements. Maintain a German or European bank account as the primary operating and reserve account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital and shareholder funding from an account held in the identified shareholder's or lender's legal name to the UG's German bank account. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. State the legal purpose accurately, such as paid-in share capital, shareholder contribution, shareholder loan, or intercompany loan; do not label a loan as equity or use generic descriptions such as 'investment' where the legal characterization differs. Provide the bank with the shareholder or loan agreement, corporate approval, ownership evidence, source-of-funds documentation, and expected-use statement before or immediately after receipt. For a formation capital deposit, use the bank's restricted incorporation or capital-deposit process where available, obtain the bank confirmation required by the notary or commercial-register process, and release funds only after the account and corporate-registration procedure permits it. Maintain remitter consistency, retain SWIFT UETR or SEPA transaction references, reconcile the receipt to the general ledger, and document any foreign-exchange conversion. Check Deutsche Bundesbank external-statistics reporting obligations and thresholds for cross-border loans, claims, liabilities, and payments; statistical reporting is distinct from tax reporting and does not replace transfer-pricing documentation.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer where eligible for euro supplier payments; use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly represented operationally by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 successor, for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments. Match every payment to an approved purchase order, invoice, supplier master record, goods or services receipt, and accounting entry. For imports and larger machinery or component purchases, use documentary commercial letters of credit issued or advised through a relationship bank under Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600), with documentary requirements aligned to Incoterms and transport documents. Consider standby letters of credit or bank guarantees only after credit assessment and collateral review. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 0.825% Solidaritätszuschlag (5.5% surcharge on corporate income tax), yielding a federal headline of 15.825%; municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) is then added at 3.5% base rate multiplied by the local Hebesatz. Berlin's Hebesatz of 410% yields 14.35% trade tax, giving a combined headline effective rate of 30.175%. Across other municipalities, the combined rate generally ranges from approximately 29% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% = 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 0.825% Solidaritätszuschlag (5.5% surcharge on the 25% withholding). This rate applies to all dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders unless a DTA or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief is granted. DTA rates commonly range between 0% and 15%, depending on the residence state and shareholding thresholds.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The statutory general deadline is 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end. If the return is prepared by a certified tax advisor, an automatic extension applies until 28 February (29 February in leap years) of the second calendar year following the fiscal year-end. Further extension may be granted by the tax office upon application.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 10 million of annual cross-border controlled transactions per fiscal year triggers the mandatory Local File obligation under § 90(3) AO and the BMF documentation decrees. A Master File is required when the worldwide group of which the UG is a member generates consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 100 million. Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (§ 138a AO). Documentation must be filed within 60 days of a formal tax audit request; ex-post documentation within 30 days of the transaction is insufficient and attracts statutory penalties.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to UAE or Barbados, but equivalent substance requirements operate through: (i) § 50d(3) EStG beneficial ownership test for treaty and EU directive relief; (ii) § 42 AO abuse-of-law doctrine which disregards arrangements lacking economic substance; (iii) § 7 ff. AStG CFC rules attributing passive German and foreign income to resident owners when the UG lacks entrepreneurial activity; (iv) BEPS Action 6 Limitation-on-Benefits and Principle-Purpose-Test clauses in the German DTA network; (v) the German Transparency Register and AML/KYC obligations requiring full beneficial-owner identification. A manufacturing UG must demonstrate a physical factory or contractual manufacturing arrangement in Germany, payroll with social security filings, management decisions taken by its Geschäftsführer in Germany, German bank accounts used for third-party payments, inventory and warranty risk, and commercial substance commensurate with the production of urban electric cargo bicycles. Failure of the substance test results in denial of DTA benefits, recharacterisation of transactions by the BZSt, disallowance of expense deductions, retroactive WHT liabilities and bank de-risking.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) – Intentional Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: § 370 AO: imprisonment from six months to ten years; monetary fines calculated by the court up to confiscation of all evaded amounts; the full back-tax remains payable with 6% per annum interest under § 233a AO; automatic entry in the criminal record; loss of the right to hold a Geschäftsführer position under § 6(2) GmbHG; possible extradition and asset freezing in international cases.

Director personal liability: The managing director is the statutory fiscal representative (§ 34 AO) and is personally liable under § 69 AO for all tax amounts the UG could not pay because of the director's culpable breach; criminal conviction leads to personal restitution of the evaded tax plus penalties; a travel ban/passport withdrawal may be ordered to secure collection; if the UG becomes insolvent due to unpaid taxes, the director faces insolvency compensation claims and potential § 82 GmbHG criminal charges for depletion of assets.
Shell Company / Treaty-Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: § 42 AO recharacterisation: tax effects are denied and income is attributed to the foreign beneficial owner; § 50d(3) EStG denial of withholding tax relief; retroactive applicability of the 26.375% withholding; trade tax and corporate income tax re-assessed; penalties for false statutory filings up to EUR 1 million under the Transparency Register violation provisions; the UG may be dissolved as an abusive entity.

Director personal liability: The nominee Geschäftsführer and the foreign principal are jointly and severally liable for the back-tax, penalty interest and withholding taxes; the court can impose a travel ban and passport seizure for tax debts exceeding EUR 5,000; in aggravated cases, imprisonment for aiding tax evasion of up to five years and permanent disqualification from managing any German limited liability company.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: § 162(3) AO penalty of 5% of the adjusted income (minimum EUR 1,000, maximum EUR 1,000,000 per audited assessment) for failure to submit transfer pricing documentation; arbitrary tax estimation with reversal of the burden of proof; 6% per annum interest on the resulting tax under § 233a AO; if the adjustment creates deemed dividend distributions, additional 26.375% WHT penalties apply; criminal proceedings follow where intentional mispricing is proven.

Director personal liability: The managing director who signed the tax return and documentation (or failed to prepare it) is personally liable under § 69 AO for the penalty and additional tax caused by the TP adjustment; personal insolvency risk if the UG cannot settle; criminal liability under § 370 AO for aiding tax evasion assists a prison sentence of up to five years; travel restrictions issued in the course of the criminal investigation.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Banking Integrity

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million for severe violations of § 20 GwG and the Transparency Register duty; daily fines for persistent non-compliance; banking relationships terminated by German banks resulting in frozen assets; separate prosecution for money laundering under § 261 StGB, with imprisonment from three months to five years and criminal forfeiture of the corresponding e-bike cargo values; BaFin administrative sanctions.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is the officer responsible for AML/KYC and Transparency Register compliance; personal fines can be imposed directly on the director; conviction under § 261 StGB leads to imprisonment, personal confiscation of assets and disqualification under § 6 GmbHG; civil liability to the UG for consequential loss of bank facilities and trade credit.
Disguised Profit Distribution / Illegal Capital Withdrawal

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a hidden profit distribution under § 8(3) KStG: add-back to corporate income, trade-tax add-back under § 8 GewStG, retroactive Kapitalertragsteuer of 26.375% on the deemed dividend, 6% per annum interest and late-payment surcharges; the shareholding transaction may be taxed as a capital gain to the recipient; civil sanctions under § 82 GmbHG for unlawful withdrawal of assets, with fines and confiscation.

Director personal liability: The managing director who authorised the disguised distribution without withholding Kapitalertragsteuer is personally liable as collection agent under §§ 44, 50 EStG and § 69 AO; joint and several liability with the shareholder for the German tax; criminal restitution and imprisonment up to three years under § 82 GmbHG for asset stripping; denial of discharge in any subsequent personal insolvency proceeding; the foreign shareholder may also face CFC and reallocation attacks in the residence state.
