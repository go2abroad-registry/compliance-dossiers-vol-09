Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under EU free movement of capital (Treaty on the Functioning of the EU, Art. 63). No prior exchange control approvals are required for profit repatriation or capital injections. Profit remittance by a GmbH to foreign shareholders faces a 25% corporate income tax withholding (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding), effectively 26.375%, unless reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty. For trade transaction settlement, no conversion restrictions exist; however, cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes under AWV §67-70.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. German resident companies may hold foreign-currency receipts in non-EU foreign currency accounts abroad or domestically, provided that all cross-border payments and account balances are reported under the AWV statistical thresholds. The Deutsche Bundesbank does not impose any forced conversion to EUR; repatriation of export earnings is free subject to anti-money-laundering and sanctions compliance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (monetary policy delegated to the European Central Bank, with the Bundesbank acting as national registry and statistics supervisor under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation, AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Under PSD2, an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) such as Wise (formerly TransferWise) or N26 can provide cross-border payment services in Germany, but is not a credit institution and therefore cannot offer overdraft or lending facilities. For a patent prosecution agency handling official fee disbursements, an EMI may serve as a cost-effective secondary clearing rail for low-value international transfers, but the primary corporate treasury must maintain a SEPA-capable account with a regulated German bank to satisfy local tax payment mandates (e.g., direct debit to the Finanzamt) and the fiduciary segregation requirements of the German Lawyers' and Patent Attorneys' Compensation Act. Legal constraints include BaFin licensing of the EMI, the need for the GmbH to hold all client funds in protected accounts, and the practical limitation that EMIs do not provide the documentary format or purpose-code infrastructure required for AWV statistical reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection of €25,000 minimum share capital (or higher if agreed in the articles of association) and any subsequent capital increase must be made via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer to the GmbH's locked bank account, quoting the specific purpose code 'X1 — Direct investment in German enterprises' under the AWV reporting matrix. The transfer reference must include the date and number of the notarized capital increase resolution, the shareholder identity, and the EUR amount. For non-EU shareholders, the sending bank must perform a corresponding FX control check and keep a record of the applicable Bundesbank balance-sheet item. Prior to opening the escrow account, the bank will require the completed money laundering declaration (Geldwäschegesetz §10) and a copy of the German trade license (Gewerbeanmeldung). After the deposit, the managing director must obtain a written confirmation from the bank that the funds are freely available (Freigabe), which is then filed with the local registry court (Amtsgericht).The standard clearing rail for official patent office fees, annuity payments, and international trademark renewal fees is SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR disbursements and SEPA Credit Transfer for withdrawals within the Eurozone. For transactions with patent offices in the EEA, SEPA direct debit is permitted only for fee collection by the authorities, not for outbound commercial disbursements. A Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) is not typically used for intangible service disbursements; it is reserved for physical goods trade. However, in exceptional cases where the patent prosecution agency purchases specialized international database rights or pays service providers under a large-volume subscription, an MT103 with documentary evidence (invoices, contracts) is accepted. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT, plus trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal multiplier (Hebesatz), typically resulting in an effective aggregate rate of approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%); reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% holding for 12 months) or to 5% under most DTAs for qualifying shareholdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 (ordinary transactions) or EUR 250,000 (extraordinary transactions); Master File required when group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but economic substance is examined under § 42 AO, § 50d EStG anti-abuse provisions, EU ATAD, and treaty entitlement tests. IP and patent-related income must be supported by qualified personnel and actual business activity to qualify for treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under § 370 AO: imprisonment up to 10 years for severe cases, or monetary fine; plus repayment of evaded taxes with late payment interest of 0.5% per month and civil enforcement measures.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for unpaid corporate tax; criminal conviction can lead to travel bans, extradition orders, seizure of assets, and imprisonment.
Failure to File / Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 10% of assessed tax (min €100, max €25,000), daily surcharges, enforced tax assessment by estimation (Schätzung) with discretionary surcharges, and late payment interest of 0.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Non-payment of taxes can trigger personal liability for company tax debts, enforcement against personal assets, and potential arrest for obstruction of tax authorities.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate entity under § 42 AO, denial of treaty benefits under § 50d EStG, recharacterization of income, and imposition of hidden dividend withholding tax; additional penalty interest and late payment charges.
