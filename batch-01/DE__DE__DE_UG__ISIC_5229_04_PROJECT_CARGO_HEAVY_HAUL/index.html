Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office (VAT ID issuance, EU OSS administration) and the locally competent Finanzamt (Tax Office) for Steuernummer, corporate and trade tax assessments. Electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1. Substantive incorporation of the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) by notarial deed, followed by entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). 2. The managing director files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt, disclosing legal form, shareholders, business purpose (ISIC 5229 OOG project cargo logistics), expected turnover, and banking details. 3. Within 2-6 weeks, the Finanzamt issues the corporate income tax/trade tax Steuernummer; the BZSt simultaneously issues the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) where EU cross-border or export activity is declared. 4. Non-resident (foreign) entities without a German seat or domicile register directly with the special Finanzamt jurisdiction allocated to non-resident entrepreneurs (e.g., Finanzamt Neukölln in Berlin) or via the ELSTER non-resident registration module, submitting the same questionnaire plus evidence of the foreign statutory seat (certificate of good standing, apostilled). 5. The entity is then placed in monthly VAT advance-return filing cycles (Vorabanmeldungen) and annual corporate tax (Körperschaftsteuererklärung) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuererklärung) filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a fully opaque, separately taxable corporation under Sec. 1 (1) No. 1 KStG. It pays corporation tax at the statutory head-line rate of 15% plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of the KSt) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective approx. 7-17% depending on municipal Hebesatz), producing an aggregate effective income tax burden of approx. 30% on distributed profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a fully opaque, separately taxable corporation under Sec. 1 (1) No. 1 KStG. It pays corporation tax at the statutory head-line rate of 15% plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of the KSt) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective approx. 7-17% depending on municipal Hebesatz), producing an aggregate effective income tax burden of approx. 30% on distributed profits. Territorial scope: resident UG — unlimited worldwide tax liability (Sec. 1 KStG); non-resident shareholders' foreign status only limits personal taxation, not the entity's classification. For VAT, the UG is an entrepreneur (Unternehmer) under Sec. 2 (1) UStG.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German statute; the UG is irrefutably opaque. Available elections and unity regimes: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity/corporate tax group) election under Sec. 14 KStG — permissible only with a controlling shareholding exceeding 50-75% and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (ii) VAT/fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) under Sec. 2 (2) No. 2 UStG — requires financial, economic and organisational integration; (iii) multi-site allocation of trade tax between business premises under Sec. 28 GewStG; (iv) anti-hybrid alignment: German ATAD I/II implementation (Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 4j EStG) automatically overrides foreign pass-through classification mismatches — a UG cannot be recharacterised as a transparent entity by foreign shareholders, and any hybrid mismatch arising in a cross-border structure is neutralised by the German primary/defensive rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general zero-threshold relief: entrepreneurs established in Germany must register for VAT from the first euro of taxable turnover (Sec. 2 (1) UStG). Small-business (Kleinunternehmer) exemption under Sec. 19 (1) UStG applies up to EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year turnover (from 1 January 2025: EUR 25,000 / EUR 100,000) — a UG may avail itself of this only while operating below those ceilings. Non-resident foreign suppliers performing taxable supplies in Germany, intra-Community distance sales, or electronically supplied services to final consumers are registrable from the first euro; the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual distance-sales threshold (Sec. 3c UStG) may route liability to the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) instead of full German registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under Sec. 4 Nr. 1 letter a UStG read with Sec. 6 UStG where: (i) the UG is the supplier, (ii) the heavy-lift/OOG goods physically leave the EU customs territory within the statutory 90-day period from the supply, and (iii) the complete book-and-voucher proof (Beleg- und Buchnachweis) per Sec. 8-13 UStDV is contemporaneously maintained. For project cargo logistics (ISIC 5229), ancillary services — freight forwarding, sea/air freight, loading, lashing, securing, heavy-lift craneage, dunnage, stevedoring, and inland pre-carriage connected to an export consignment — qualify for zero-rating under Sec. 4 Nr. 3 letter a UStG when the transport has an out-of-the-EU destination and is evidenced by transport documents. Intra-Community supply zero-rating (Sec. 6a UStG) applies when the OOG cargo is dispatched to a VAT-registered customer in another EU member state and the customer's VAT ID is valid, the goods physically arrive, and the EC Sales List is submitted. Missing export evidence reclassifies the transaction as a domestic taxable supply at 19%.

  • ATLAS/AES electronic customs export declaration — Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / MRN-based exit declaration (with EORI number of the UG)
  • Commercial invoice (final or pro-forma) stating the export price and Incoterms (EXW/FOB/CIF/CPT/DAP) for the OOG consignment
  • Bill of Lading (BoL) or Sea Waybill / Air Waybill (AWB) / CMR consignment note evidencing outward transport from the EU
  • Packing list, cargo manifest and, for OOG/Heavy-Lift, the stowage plan, lashing/securation certificate and heavy-lift survey report
  • Certificate of origin (EUR.1/CN EUR.1 movement certificate or supplier's declaration) where preferential origin claims are made
  • Customs exit confirmation (Ausgangsbestätigung) from the export/transit declaration or AMS/ENS filing confirmation
  • Inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice) evidencing receipt of foreign currency and FX repatriation to the UG's German bank account
  • Insurance certificate and, where applicable, the freight contract or forwarding (Speditions) instruction dossier

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping at invoice level. A phased mandatory e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) framework applies: (i) from 1 January 2025, all German resident entrepreneurs must technically receive electronic invoices; (ii) from 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue structured e-invoices (EN 16931-compatible: XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.2) for domestic B2B sales; (iii) from 1 January 2028 the issuance duty extends to all entrepreneurs. E-invoices require XML-based structured data and must be exchanged via interoperable channels (PEPPOL, secure hosting mailboxes, upload portals); public-sector invoices additionally require the Leitweg-ID. VAT returns, advance returns, and recapitulative statements (EC Sales Lists) are mandatorily transmitted electronically via ELSTER. The BZSt and local Finanzämter perform risk-based audits on e-invoice datasets; no API-based transaction clearance is required for general export/transport logistics.

Where zero-rated export treatment is claimed without a valid Beleg- und Buchnachweis, or the 90-day EU exit condition is not met, the Finanzamt retrospectively assesses VAT at the 19% standard rate on the invoiced consideration, adjusted under Sec. 162 AO where records are incomplete. Late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% p.a.) accrues on the VAT clawback under Sec. 233a AO. Falsified or sham export documentation triggers criminal tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine; in especially severe cases (commercial scale, falsification of customs documents, gang involvement) imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years applies under Sec. 370 (3) AO. Additional consequences: fines under Sec. 26a UStG and Sec. 380 AO for violation of invoicing and record-keeping duties, exclusion of the UG from future zero-rating claims until a clean compliance certification is established, and a heightened audit cycle imposed by the Veranlagungsbezirk; civil fraud liability to carriers, insurers and letter-of-credit banks under Sec. 263 StGB may also be pursued in parallel.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro and permits full current- and capital-account convertibility, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, export controls, and statistical reporting. A German UG may generally receive, hold, convert, and remit funds without exchange-control approval or a general profit-remittance restriction. Dividends remain subject to corporate-law distributable-profit and capital-maintenance rules, shareholder resolutions, withholding-tax requirements, and any applicable treaty or EU relief procedure. Banks may require source-of-funds, transaction-purpose, tax, and beneficial-owner evidence. Cross-border payments may trigger AWV reporting; reporting is statistical and does not itself constitute prior approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or statutory deadline for converting or repatriating export receipts applies. Receipts may be retained in euros or another permitted currency. For reportable cross-border payments, German residents generally file the applicable AWV report with the Bundesbank when the amount exceeds EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exemptions and transaction-specific rules. Reporting deadlines and methods depend on the report category; the company should verify the current Bundesbank reporting instructions for each transaction type and maintain supporting records.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank. Germany has no general foreign-exchange licensing authority or exchange-control regime. The Bundesbank administers balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle, BAFA) administers relevant export-control requirements, not routine currency conversion.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro collections and payments where the provider is authorized in Germany or another EEA state and passported as required. An electronic-money institution account is not a bank deposit and is not covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme; safeguarding rules apply. Provider terms may restrict cash handling, high-value project transactions, trade finance, or complex ownership structures. Confirm that the provider accepts a German UG, supports the required currencies and payment corridors, and can supply account evidence acceptable to counterparties. Fintech accounts do not replace a bank relationship required for lending, commercial letters of credit, or certain capital-deposit procedures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Fund the UG through an account in its registered legal name after completing bank onboarding and account-purpose checks. For initial share capital, follow the notary's and bank's formation instructions, identify the transfer as subscribed share capital or share-capital contribution, and retain the subscription, shareholder, and bank-credit evidence for registration. For later equity, use properly documented capital increases or contributions; for shareholder loans, execute written loan terms and assess transfer-pricing, interest-withholding, and corporate-law implications. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT cross-border transfers for other currencies; include accurate originator, beneficiary, bank, and payment-purpose information. German banks do not use a universal statutory FX purpose-code system for ordinary transfers. Provide the transaction purpose and supporting contracts or invoices when requested. Assess AWV reporting separately for reportable cross-border payments above the applicable threshold and retain the filing confirmation and supporting records.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA scheme and SWIFT cross-border payments for other currencies or corridors. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax (effective 0.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Trade tax is calculated using a 3.5% federal base rate multiplied by the municipality’s Hebesatz; the combined burden is municipality-dependent and is commonly approximately 30%, not a uniform statutory rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital-yield withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic rate of 26.375%, before any applicable church tax. Treaty relief or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may reduce or eliminate the rate if all substantive and procedural requirements are met.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The tax office may require earlier filing. A non-calendar fiscal year follows the same relative rule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German documentation thresholds generally require transaction-specific records for cross-border related-party dealings where annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other services, subject to the applicable statutory rules. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100 million in the preceding fiscal year. Country-by-country reporting thresholds and documentation rules are separate. Thresholds, scope and applicable timing must be checked for the relevant fiscal year, including the German transfer-pricing documentation amendments effective from 2025.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a standalone regime generally known as Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). German tax law nevertheless applies substance-sensitive rules, including the general anti-abuse rule in section 42 AO, treaty and EU anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership requirements, transfer-pricing rules, controlled-foreign-company rules and limitations on interest deductions. A German UG should maintain genuine decision-making, personnel and operational capacity appropriate to its functions and risks. For project-cargo logistics, retain contracts, quotations, routing and freight records, customs and transport documentation, insurance, project-management evidence, subcontractor records, pricing support and evidence of who controls operational and financial risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Intentional tax evasion or fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Section 370 AO provides for imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may be punishable by imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest and possible asset-recovery obligations remain separately payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability where they intentionally participate or act as perpetrators or accomplices. They may also face tax-liability claims under section 69 AO for culpable failure to discharge company tax obligations. Imprisonment is possible following criminal proceedings. Travel restrictions are not an automatic sanction.
Reckless tax understatement and negligent compliance failures

Statutory sanction: Reckless tax reduction under section 378 AO may result in an administrative fine of up to EUR 50,000, in addition to tax, interest and applicable filing surcharges. Other procedural breaches may attract separate statutory fines.

Director personal liability: A director may be personally exposed where the statutory culpability and duty requirements are met, including liability for company tax debts under section 69 AO. Liability is not automatic solely because of office.
Late or non-payment of payroll and withholding taxes

Statutory sanction: The UG may owe the unpaid tax, late-payment interest and applicable surcharges; intentional withholding-tax offences may also fall within tax-evasion provisions. Failure to pay withheld employee contributions may separately constitute a criminal offence under social-security law.

Director personal liability: Managing directors responsible for tax administration may face personal liability under section 69 AO for culpable non-payment and potential criminal exposure. Liability depends on the facts, duties and degree of fault.
Transfer-pricing misstatement or inadequate records

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, impose a surcharge for missing or inadequate transfer-pricing documentation under section 162 AO and applicable statutory provisions, estimate the tax base, and assess additional tax and interest. Penalty amounts depend on the breach, period and statutory calculation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax-liability or criminal exposure only where the applicable statutory duties, culpability and participation requirements are met. A transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically establish personal criminal liability.
Fictitious operations, sham company or abuse of legal form

Statutory sanction: Tax consequences may include denial of deductions or treaty benefits, recharacterisation, tax assessment, interest, penalties and criminal prosecution where statutory elements are met. Corporate-law and insolvency consequences may also arise.

Director personal liability: The UG’s limited liability is not automatically disregarded. Directors may incur liability for personal wrongdoing, breaches of statutory duties, tax liabilities, unlawful distributions or insolvency-related violations. There is no automatic travel ban; any restraint requires a separate legal basis and process.
Money laundering, sanctions evasion or concealed beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties, fines, confiscation and regulatory measures may apply under the relevant German criminal, anti-money-laundering and sanctions laws. The specific sanction depends on the conduct and applicable statute; no single fixed penalty applies to all cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible personnel may be prosecuted or fined where they personally commit, direct, assist or culpably fail to meet applicable duties. Imprisonment and asset confiscation may be available under the relevant laws; travel restrictions are not automatic.
Insolvency offences and unlawful payments

Statutory sanction: The German Insolvency Code and related statutes may impose civil repayment or damages liability and, for specified offences, criminal penalties. The applicable filing period and payment restrictions depend on the company’s financial condition and statutory facts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments and may face criminal prosecution where the statutory elements are satisfied. The UG’s limited liability does not protect a director from liability for personal breaches of duty.
