Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), the minimum nominal share capital is EUR 1, but in practice a stated capital of EUR 1,000 or more is recommended. The subscribed share capital must be fully paid up in cash to a German bank escrow or corporate account before registration; if contributions are made in cash, at least 50% of the subscription amount must be paid in prior to filing, in accordance with Sect. 7(2) GmbHG. The company must retain 25% of annual surplus in a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: For the storage premises used for matcha powder: (1) fire safety certificate from the local fire protection authority (Bauaufsicht) based on the building's fire load classification and alarm systems; (2) structural floor-load capacity inspection for the warehouse; (3) occupational safety clearance under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) and the Health and Safety at Work Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz), including first aid equipment, emergency exits, and hygiene welfare facilities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT registration number (USt-IdNr.) and the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for income tax/trade tax/VAT registration; official e-filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt has its own online application portal (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with a taxable presence in Germany must submit an application for tax registration via the official ‘Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung’ form. This is filed electronically through ELSTER or at the competent local Finanzamt. The application must include name, legal form, registered office, German permanent establishment address if any, business purpose, and expected turnover. Once the local tax office issues a German tax number (Steuernummer), the entity may separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using the appropriate online form (Form USt 1/2) to conduct EU-wide supplies. A non-EU entrepreneur may be required to appoint a fiscal representative in Germany if no permanent establishment is maintained and if no mutual assistance treaty exists. German resident entities, including a UG, are generally registered automatically by the local authorities upon commercial registration; the UG must subsequently complete tax registration through ELSTER.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not have check-the-box elections. A UG (Unternehmergesellschaft) is automatically treated as an entity-level taxed corporation subject to corporate income tax and trade tax. Available statutory elections relevant to the entity structure include: (1) VAT Organschaft under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG allowing a controlling German parent and a financially, economically, and organisationally integrated subsidiary UG to be treated as a single taxable person for VAT; (2) Corporate income tax and trade tax Organschaft requiring the parent to hold a majority of voting rights and to execute a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) valid for at least five years; (3) no hybrid mismatch elections are provided under German law; anti-hybrid measures under ATAD are implemented mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 (small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG for resident entrepreneurs with prior-year turnover below EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover below EUR 50,000; non-resident entrepreneurs have no de minimis threshold and must register for VAT when they make any taxable supply in Germany unless the reverse-charge mechanism applies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% VAT on exports of matcha powder to non-EU destinations, the goods must be transported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf directly from Germany to a third-country territory. The exporter must prove that the goods have physically left the EU within the month following delivery or within a reasonable extension, and must retain all documentary evidence required under Section 6 UStG. Intra-Community supplies to EU customers are also zero-rated under Section 6a UStG, requiring both parties to have valid VAT IDs and mandatory recapitulative statement filing. Services related to exported culinary matcha goods may be zero-rated only if they are ancillary to the supply and performed in direct connection with the export. The supplier must issue a statutory VAT invoice and record the export correctly in the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted in the ATLAS customs clearance system
  • Commercial invoice with full export designation and organic certification references
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill depending on mode of transport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT payment proof evidencing actual receipt of export consideration
  • Transport documents such as CMR road consignment note or packing list

Germany has phased B2B e-invoicing: from 1 January 2025 all taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices compliant with EN16931 (formats such as XRechnung and PEPPOL BIS); from 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all businesses must issue them. There is no real-time tax clearance, fiscal stamping, or mandatory API pre-authorisation for invoices in Germany. However, electronic invoices must be transmitted in a structured, machine-readable format using an approved e-invoicing channel; cash register revenues are subject to technical security measures (TSE) and digital record-keeping obligations.

Fraudulent or substantiation-deficient export zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code (AO). The consequences include retrospective VAT assessment at 19%, interest of 0.5% per month on the assessed VAT (6% per annum) after the statutory 15-month grace period, fines up to EUR 50,000 for administrative violations, and imprisonment up to 10 years for aggravated tax evasion. Customs authorities (Hauptzollamt) and the local tax office (Finanzamt) perform systematic audits of export evidence, including cross-checks through mutual assistance and international exchange of information. Records must be maintained for 10 years; retrospective clawbacks are permitted without limitation in cases of intentional evasion and within a shortened limitation period for negligent or unlawful claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full liberalisation of capital movements under the EU Treaty and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. No exchange control or prior licensing requirement exists for current account transactions, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation, provided tax compliance obligations are met. The sole binding constraints are (i) reporting duties to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV, (ii) EU/UN/peacabwe sanctions screening, and (iii) statutory anti-money-laundering verifications. The euro is freely convertible; no effective de facto convertibility restriction is in force for the cross-border matcha procurement and distribution operating model.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory conversion or surrender of export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency abroad or sell it in the open market. Statutory obligation reduced to prudential statistical reporting under Section 67 AWV: residents must report cross-border monetary claims and payment receivables from non-residents by the 7th day of the following month if the aggregate value exceeds EUR 12,500, reporting the transaction reference, amount, currency, purpose code, and counterparty. No mandatory conversion ratio or minimum repatriation ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank) acting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) oversees AML compliance for banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Direct access to offshore or non-EEA digital electronic money institutions is legally constrained: an EEA/UK EMI operating through a German branch or cross-border passport under PSD2 can provide principal payment services and IBAN provision via intermediary partners, but cannot replace the full corporate banking remit of a licensed credit institution (credit, trade finance, extensive FX settlement lines, and documentary credits). Viability assessment: Medium, conditional on the EMI holding a valid German/EEA passport or a BaFin license, and on the UG retaining a tier-1 bank account as the treasury anchor and FX clearing hub.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer): 15%, plus solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%. A UG conducting business in Germany is also generally subject to municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), at a municipality-dependent rate; the combined corporate and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 30%, but is not a single national rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, for an effective domestic rate of 26.375%. Treaty relief may reduce the rate, commonly to 15% or 5% depending on the treaty, shareholding, beneficial ownership, and other conditions. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or German domestic relief may provide exemption or reduction for qualifying corporate recipients, subject to ownership, holding-period, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For German transfer-pricing documentation, transaction-level documentation obligations generally apply where the aggregate consideration with a related person exceeds €6 million for deliveries of goods or €600,000 for other transactions in a fiscal year. A master file is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least €100 million. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750 million, subject to German rules and applicable filing arrangements. The thresholds, documentation contents, deadlines, and applicability should be checked for the relevant year.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. German tax law nevertheless applies arm's-length requirements, beneficial-ownership and treaty anti-abuse tests, general anti-avoidance rules under AO section 42, controlled foreign company rules, and substance-related rules affecting deductions, treaty relief, and attribution of income. The UG should maintain evidence of actual decision-making, personnel, premises or operational arrangements appropriate to its functions, assets, and risks. Matcha sourcing, quality control, inventory, logistics, and distribution functions should be documented consistently with the entity's claimed profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and materially false tax returns

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under AO section 370 may be punished by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Back taxes, interest, and administrative or criminal proceedings may also follow.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability where they intentionally participate or are responsible for the offense. A director may also be personally liable for tax debts under AO section 69 where culpable breach of tax duties causes the debt to be unpaid. Corporate limited liability does not protect a director from personal criminal or statutory liability.
Failure to remit payroll or withholding taxes

Statutory sanction: Potential tax assessment, interest, surcharges, enforcement, and criminal liability under applicable AO provisions, including tax evasion where intent is established.

Director personal liability: Managing directors responsible for tax compliance may be personally liable under AO section 69 for culpable failure to meet payment obligations and may face criminal liability for intentional conduct.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, additional corporate and trade tax, interest, and documentation-related surcharges under AO section 162 and related provisions. German law provides for surcharges for missing or materially unusable transfer-pricing records, subject to statutory calculation rules and limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability where culpable failure to discharge tax duties causes unpaid taxes, and criminal exposure may arise if deliberate tax evasion is established.
VAT fraud, carousel fraud, or fictitious trading

Statutory sanction: Input-tax recovery may be denied, VAT and interest assessed, and fines or criminal penalties imposed under tax and criminal law; intentional tax evasion may carry imprisonment under AO section 370.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate or negligently breach relevant duties may face criminal, civil, or statutory tax liability. Limited liability does not shield personal misconduct.
Shell-company or sham-substance arrangements

Statutory sanction: Treaty or statutory relief may be denied, income reallocated, taxes and interest assessed, and penalties imposed where reporting or tax obligations are breached. AO section 42 and applicable treaty anti-abuse provisions may apply.

Director personal liability: A director may face personal tax or criminal exposure for knowing participation in false filings or deliberate evasion. No automatic piercing of the corporate veil or automatic travel ban arises solely from an adverse tax assessment.
AML, sanctions, and suspicious-payment violations

Statutory sanction: Potential administrative fines, asset freezes, payment restrictions, confiscation, and criminal penalties under the applicable AML, sanctions, and criminal statutes. The specific sanction depends on the conduct and the applicable legal provision.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible individuals may incur personal administrative or criminal liability for their own acts or culpable breaches of applicable duties. There is no automatic director imprisonment or travel ban merely because an AML concern is raised.
Insolvency-related tax and capital-maintenance violations

Statutory sanction: Potential repayment claims, director liability for prohibited payments, insolvency-law sanctions, and criminal penalties where statutory elements are met. A UG must also comply with statutory reserve rules applicable to its legal form.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments or culpable failure to file for insolvency within the statutory period. Liability depends on the facts and applicable insolvency and corporate law.
