Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the Amtsgericht (Local Court) of the district in which the registered office is situated, with electronic filing via the notary's secure transmission channel (XJustiz / Handelsregisterportal der Länder).

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), particularly §§ 1 (purpose), 2 (incorporation deed), 3 (articles of association), 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), 7 (share capital), 8 (application contents), 10 (commercial register entry), 35 (managing director), 39 (change of managing directors), and 54 (amendments); complemented by the Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) §§ 8–16 on Commercial Register requirements and the Gesetz über das Aufsichtsrecht der Wirtschaftsprüfer for notarial certifications.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and the Notarized Application for Registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung), filed jointly under § 8 GmbHG, together with the certified shareholders' list (Gesellschafterliste) and the managing director's specimen signature sheet.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG requires a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00, fully paid-up in cash at the time of incorporation; the contribution must be at the free disposal of the managing director as evidenced by a bank credit confirmation. No statutory escrow lockbox account (Sperrkonto) is mandated for UG formation, but the bank's pecuniary credit confirmation must identify the UG as account holder. The company must retain 25% of annual net profits in a statutory reserve until the subscribed share capital reaches EUR 25,000, pursuant to § 5a(3) GmbHG.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: the registered office and any warehouse must comply with the state building code fire protection requirements, including the submission of a fire safety concept if the storage area exceeds the prescribed thresholds; fire extinguishing equipment and emergency routes must be inspected by the local fire protection authority. Occupational clearance: the company must register with the competent Berufsgenossenschaft (employers' liability insurance association), conduct a workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (ArbSchG), comply with the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) for ventilation, lighting, and emergency exits, and, if warehouse personnel handle heavy cathode slabs, implement lifting-equipment inspections and manual-handling safety procedures under DGUV regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG requires a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00, fully paid-up in cash at the time of incorporation; the contribution must be at the free disposal of the managing director as evidenced by a bank credit confirmation. No statutory escrow lockbox account (Sperrkonto) is mandated for UG formation, but the bank's pecuniary credit confirmation must identify the UG as account holder. The company must retain 25% of annual net profits in a statutory reserve until the subscribed share capital reaches EUR 25,000, pursuant to § 5a(3) GmbHG.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sector-specific industrial license is mandatory for wholesale trading of refined copper cathodes under German industrial law; the pertinent general authority is the municipal Gewerbeamt (Trade Office) for issuance of the Gewerbeschein, complemented by the Chamber of Commerce and Industry (IHK) for mandatory membership, and, for any warehousing site, the local building authority for structural use permits.

Category: Gewerbeschein (Trade License) issued under § 14 Gewerbeordnung (GewO) for the activity of wholesale trade in non-ferrous metals, copper cathodes, and associated base-metal products, without processing or manufacturing operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), local Finanzamter, Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF); official portal ELSTER at www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders establish a German UG and register in the Handelsregister (commercial register). The managing director submits the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. After registration, the BZSt issues the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). A separate EORI number must be obtained from the German customs administration (Generalzolldirektion/Zoll) for export clearance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign shareholders establish a German UG and register in the Handelsregister (commercial register). The managing director submits the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. After registration, the BZSt issues the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). A separate EORI number must be obtained from the German customs administration (Generalzolldirektion/Zoll) for export clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. The UG may elect fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate income tax and trade tax and may elect VAT fiscal unity under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are mandatory under § 4k EStG; no elective hybrid mismatch arrangement is available. The small-business exemption under § 19 UStG may be elected only below statutory turnover limits, but copper cathode wholesale normally elects general VAT taxation to preserve input VAT recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year turnover are the small-business relief limits; a UG engaged in physical copper cathode wholesale has mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable transaction, irrespective of a general exemption threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports to third territories are zero-rated under § 4 Nr. 1a UStG and § 6 UStG if the copper cathodes are physically transported from Germany or another EU Member State to a destination outside the EU. The UG must evidence all export particulars and prove receipt at the foreign destination in accordance with §§ 8-13 UStDV. Intra-EU supply to a registered business may also be zero-rated as an intra-Community supply, but only with valid VAT IDs, EC sales list reporting, and dispatch documentation. Transfers of LME warrants or deliveries from a German bonded/free-zone warehouse without physical EU departure do not qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration via ATLAS (Ausfuhranmeldung and Export Accompanying Document)
  • Commercial invoice and final zero-rated export invoice
  • Packing list and exact weight lot list for Grade A refined copper cathodes
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or documentary LC settlement evidence showing payment by the foreign buyer
  • Customer receipt/declaration confirming arrival at the third-country destination
  • Warehouse and LME-warrant release documentation showing the physical movement from German storage to export

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing under § 14 UStG using XInvoice or ZUGFeRD: from 1 January 2025 every entrepreneur must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 an entrepreneur with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B issuers must issue structured e-invoices. Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API clearance, or digital stamping. Export transactions remain subject to ATLAS customs clearance and GoBD-compliant record-keeping.

The Finanzamt subjects export zero-rating to documentary audit. If export proof is incomplete, false, or foreign-currency proceeds are not received within 180 days, zero-rating is reversed and statutory VAT of 19% is assessed on the net supply value. Late payment interest applies under § 233a AO at 0.5% per month, together with late payment penalties under §§ 229 and 240 AO. Intentional or negligent misstatement in export documentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, carrying fines and imprisonment up to 10 years for intentional evasion or up to 5 years for negligence. The UG managing director may be personally liable under §§ 34, 69, 71 and 73 AO, and customs offences may be punished under § 382 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general German approval requirement for foreign ownership, shareholder funding, dividend payments, interest payments, or repatriation of liquidation proceeds. Practical restrictions arise from bank anti-money-laundering, sanctions, correspondent-bank, beneficial-ownership, source-of-funds, transfer-pricing, thin-capitalization, withholding-tax, and documentary requirements. Dividends from a German UG may require German withholding tax, generally 25 percent plus solidarity surcharge, subject to domestic relief, an applicable tax treaty, or qualifying European Union parent-subsidiary relief. Interest and service payments require separate withholding-tax and transfer-pricing analysis. Capital contributions should be documented as paid-in capital, shareholder contribution to capital reserves, or shareholder loan, with consistent accounting and tax treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or compulsory sale of foreign-currency export receipts to a German bank. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts in permitted accounts, subject to bank controls, sanctions, accounting, tax, and customs obligations. Cross-border payments and receipts may be reportable to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung and applicable Bundesbank procedures. For reportable payment transactions, the reporting deadline is generally the seventh calendar day of the following month, subject to current thresholds, exemptions, payment-channel rules, and reporting-party allocation. The company must verify the current threshold and applicable reporting form with its bank or Bundesbank before implementation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem and subject to applicable European Union and German foreign-trade, payments, anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and statistical-reporting rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro collections, payments, expense management, and treasury sub-accounts; Low as the sole banking solution for physical copper trading, documentary letters of credit, large shareholder funding, LME-related settlements, or high-value third-country flows. An electronic-money institution or payment institution is not equivalent to a German deposit-taking bank, may not provide deposit protection or full lending and trade-finance services, and may impose transaction, country, commodity, or counterparty restrictions. Offshore digital EMIs may be used only after confirming their authorization, passporting or local service basis, safeguarding model, SEPA access, correspondent arrangements, sanctions controls, German tax and accounting evidence requirements, and acceptance by counterparties. The company should retain a German or European Economic Area bank as the primary settlement and contingency institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital through a bank account held in the UG's legal name, preferably at a German or European Economic Area bank. Use a documented payment narrative identifying the contributor, legal nature of the payment, currency, and reference to the shareholder resolution or contribution agreement. Classify the payment consistently as paid-in share capital, contribution to capital reserves, or shareholder loan; do not mix these categories in payment instructions or accounting. Obtain source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence before remittance. For non-euro receipts, use a bank conversion at a documented rate or retain the currency where commercially justified. Reconcile the incoming wire against the funding agreement, shareholder ledger, accounting entry, and bank statement. Confirm whether the transaction is subject to Deutsche Bundesbank statistical reporting, German tax reporting, beneficial-ownership updates, or notarial and commercial-register filings. Avoid third-party funding, cash, cryptoasset proceeds, unexplained overfunding, and split transfers designed to avoid controls.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border wires for non-SEPA or non-euro settlements. A SWIFT MT103 is a payment instruction and should be supported by the underlying contract, commercial invoice, purchase order, shipping or warehouse evidence, and sanctions screening. For documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit transmitted through the SWIFT trade-finance network, commonly involving an MT700 issuance message and related amendment, document, and settlement messages; require an authorized issuing, advising, confirming, or nominated bank and define governing rules such as UCP 600 where contractually appropriate. Match payer, beneficiary, account holder, invoice, goods description, origin, destination, Incoterms, currency, and shipment documents.
