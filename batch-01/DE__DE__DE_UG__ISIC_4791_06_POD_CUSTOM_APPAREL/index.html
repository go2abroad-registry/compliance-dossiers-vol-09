Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general statutory nationwide name-reservation period for a UG. Before notarization, request a name assessment from the competent local IHK and confirm the proposed name with the notary and register court. If the register court rejects the filing, obtain the written order and reasons, correct the name or supporting documents, and resubmit. A court decision may generally be challenged by a complaint under the applicable FamFG procedure and deadline stated in the order; obtain German counsel promptly because the remedy and deadline depend on the decision. An IHK assessment is advisory and is not a binding reservation or an appealable registration decision.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authorities are the responsible local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate tax and domestic VAT registration, the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), and the Generalzolldirektion through its customs administration for customs and EORI registration. Tax registrations and electronic tax filings are generally administered through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung).

E-government filing portal

A German UG must submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER after incorporation and commencement of business. The responsible Finanzamt then issues the German tax number (Steuernummer), normally covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. The entity may separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using the BZSt online application process; the USt-IdNr. is required for many intra-EU transactions and EU VAT reporting. For imports and exports, the entity must obtain an Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI) from German Customs through the customs EORI application process. A foreign entity without a German establishment generally applies to the competent German tax office for non-established taxable-person registration, submits constitutional documents, evidence of business activity, representative and bank details, and appoints a German tax representative where legally required or commercially appropriate. VAT registration is required where the entity makes taxable German supplies and does not qualify for an applicable small-business exemption or another special regime.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A UG is a German capital company and is generally subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax on its taxable income. It is not fiscally transparent by default. Shareholder distributions are generally taxed separately at shareholder level. The UG remains subject to German tax on worldwide income, subject to applicable tax treaties and foreign tax relief rules. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A UG is a German capital company and is generally subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax on its taxable income. It is not fiscally transparent by default. Shareholder distributions are generally taxed separately at shareholder level. The UG remains subject to German tax on worldwide income, subject to applicable tax treaties and foreign tax relief rules. It may qualify for the German small-business VAT exemption only if the statutory turnover conditions are satisfied; that exemption does not alter its corporate income tax or trade tax classification.

Available election forms

Germany has no United States-style check-the-box election for a UG. A UG cannot elect to be treated as a transparent partnership for German corporate tax purposes. The statutory corporate group regime (Organschaft) may apply if its legal, financial, and operational requirements are satisfied, but it is not a general entity-classification election. The corporate income tax option under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act applies to qualifying partnerships and certain other partnership forms, not to a UG. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 4k and related provisions of the German Income Tax Act operate by law and do not provide a voluntary hybrid classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business making taxable German supplies is automatically exempt from VAT registration. The German small-business exemption under Section 19 of the VAT Act may apply where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and the applicable tax year. A business using the exemption generally does not charge German VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. For qualifying intra-EU consumer distance sales and certain electronically supplied services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply, with VAT generally due in the customer's Member State above that threshold unless OSS is used. Imports, domestic taxable supplies, and other specific transactions may create VAT obligations independently of turnover thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt export supplies with input VAT recovery under the German VAT Act if the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination, the supplier or customer effects the transport as legally required, and the supplier retains prescribed documentary and accounting evidence proving export and the identity of the transaction. The export exemption is not available merely because the customer is outside the EU; the goods must leave the EU customs territory and the export must be properly evidenced. Intra-EU supplies may qualify for exemption where the customer is a taxable person identified for VAT in another Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies the documentary, invoice, recapitulative statement, and VAT identification requirements. Services supplied to business customers established outside Germany may be outside the scope of German VAT under the customer-location rule, rather than zero-rated, if the customer status and place-of-supply conditions are substantiated. Consumer services and electronically supplied services require separate place-of-supply analysis. Input VAT refunds require valid supplier invoices, proper accounting, and a direct connection with taxable or exempt-with-credit transactions.

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system or mandatory government pre-clearance for ordinary domestic invoices. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must generally be capable of receiving structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices is subject to transitional rules: generally permitted transition periods extend through 31 December 2026, and businesses with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally use transitional arrangements through 31 December 2027. From 1 January 2028, structured e-invoicing is generally required for in-scope domestic B2B transactions. Compliant formats include XRechnung and other EN 16931-compliant structured formats, including qualifying hybrid ZUGFeRD invoices. The mandate generally does not apply to ordinary B2C invoices, exempt transactions, small-value invoices, or specified foreign and special transactions. Invoices must contain the statutory invoice particulars, and electronic records must preserve authenticity, integrity, readability, and audit accessibility. VAT returns and related filings are generally submitted electronically through ELSTER; this is reporting, not real-time invoice clearance.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption may be denied or clawed back if the supplier cannot substantiate export, the goods did not leave the European Union, the recipient or transport chain is misidentified, invoices or customs records are inconsistent, or documentary and accounting requirements are not met. The tax office may assess the unpaid VAT retroactively, together with late-payment interest under the German Fiscal Code, and may deny related input VAT recovery where conditions are not satisfied. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code, with imprisonment or monetary penalties; negligent tax reduction may result in an administrative or criminal penalty under applicable provisions. Incorrect VAT returns, false customs declarations, or export-document manipulation may also trigger customs duties, import VAT, civil recovery, fines, criminal liability, audit assessments, and loss of VAT refunds. Statutory assessment periods are generally four years, extended to five years for reckless conduct and ten years for tax evasion, subject to applicable suspension and special rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full currency convertibility for both capital and current account transactions under EU law, particularly Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no exchange-control restrictions on dividend distributions, profit repatriation, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is however subject to German corporate income tax (15%), trade tax (average municipal rate varies, typically 14-17%), and, where distributions are made to shareholders, withholding tax at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate), unless reduced under double tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. The UG must reliably document beneficial ownership to avail of treaty/withholding relief.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or compulsory conversion of export proceeds in Germany. Export receipts are freely disposable. Under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), cross-border payments, including export proceeds, must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes if the individual payment exceeds EUR 12,500 or the aggregate of related payments exceeds this threshold in a calendar month. Reporting is due by the 7th calendar day following the month in which the payment is effected. This is a statistical reporting obligation, not an exchange-control approval or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (in coordination with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority - BaFin - for banking and capital-market supervision and with the Federal Ministry of Economics and Climate Action for foreign-trade matters)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High for operational liquidity and e-commerce settlement, subject to the fintech holding a valid e-money institution (EMI) licence in the EEA and being passported into Germany under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Such EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, airwallex) may legally provide business current accounts, payment initiation, and FX conversion without a German banking licence. Critical constraints: (i) EMIs cannot accept deposits or provide credit; (ii) funds are safeguarded, not covered by the German statutory deposit guarantee scheme; (iii) EMIs generally cannot issue documentary letters of credit or act as confirming banks; (iv) the UG should obtain confirmation that the EMI can settle payouts to Stripe and PayPal and generate compliant SWIFT/SEPA MT103-equivalent records. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from a foreign shareholder must be remitted by bank wire (SWIFT MT103) to a dedicated bank account of the UG in Germany. The payment must reference the notarised capital contribution resolution and the share subscription (Übernahmevertrag). In accordance with the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), the German receiving bank reports capital inflows exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the external economic and payments statistics. The remitter must use a commercial payment purpose description (e.g., 'final cash contribution to UG share capital' or 'contribution to capital reserve') and should include the UG's Commercial Register number whenever possible. German law imposes no exchange-control approval requirement, but funds must be received free of any anti-money-laundering adverse findings. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory monetary threshold for the Local File under Sec. 90(3) AO and the GAufzV; German tax administration requires contemporaneous documentation for material cross-border controlled transactions — administrative practice treats transactions above EUR 100,000 per category as material for POD/procurement and administrative services. Master File and CbCR are obligatory only for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be produced within 60 days of a tax audit request for ordinary transactions and within 30 days for exceptional significant transactions; deficiency penalty per Sec. 162(4) AO: 5% of adjusted income, minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 250,000, capped at 10% / EUR 1,000,000 for documentation withheld beyond six months.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation exists in Germany, but substance is a binding legal condition for treaty relief and anti-abuse protection. Sec. 42 AO voids transactions entered into solely for tax advantage; Sec. 50d(3) EStG denies DTA benefits to companies without business purpose, substantive staff, adequate premises, and real management functions in the residence country. The draft EU Unshell Directive codifies the same substance gates: in Germany, a UG that keeps all management decisions abroad, lacks local employees, office space, and active bank operations, and channels licensing or payment flows through German dress without local activity is recharacterized as a letterbox company. The POD business model requires genuine local substance: registered office, complying records, local signatory powers, and demonstrable decision-making in Germany to avoid treaty override and retroactive taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal charge under Sec. 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years; aggravated cases (commercial tax fraud, large scale, use of strawmen) carry imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years; monetary fine; back tax assessment plus evasion interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) from the due date; civil penalty assessments per Sec. 162 AO.

Director personal liability: Managing director personally liable under Sec. 69 and Sec. 71 AO for unpaid taxes caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties; criminal conviction triggers director disqualification up to 5 years, asset freezing, passport surrender, and international travel ban.
Withholding Tax and VAT Collection Failure

Statutory sanction: Acting as a tax collector, the UG is liable for the full withheld amount; criminal exposure under Sec. 266a StGB and Sec. 370 AO for failure to allocate employee contributions; administrative late-payment penalties of 1% per month; VAT shortfall is assessed with interest at 6% per annum.

Director personal liability: Automatic personal liability of the Geschäftsführer under Sec. 71 AO for VAT and wage tax withheld from customers/employees but not remitted; liability pierces the corporate veil; non-payment triggers enforcement against personal assets and a travel ban in cases of systematic diversion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Sec. 42 AO voidance of the structure: retroactive reattribution of income to the non-resident shareholder, denial of DTA benefits under Sec. 50d(3) EStG, 5.5% solidarity surcharge penalties, reassessment of six years (extended to 10 years in cases of tax evasion); compulsory deletion from the commercial register if no genuine business activity is maintained.

Director personal liability: Directors of a substance-less UG face personal financial liability for unpaid taxes at the corporate level; criminal penalties for aiding tax evasion; exit bans and passport forfeiture during tax-criminal investigations; loss of limited-liability protection in insolvency if the UG was factually dormant or a sham.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership (AML / GwG)

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fine under the GwG of up to EUR 100,000 for legal entities (higher for serious/repeat violations up to 5% of annual global turnover); criminal money-laundering liability under Sec. 261 StGB with imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years; confiscation of profits; the UG may be excluded from e-commerce receiving platforms (Stripe, PayPal) after compliance suspicion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly conceal ownership or allow the UG to act as an instrument of money laundering are subject to criminal imprisonment and confiscation; civil joint and several liability for damages; travel ban, passport seizure, and prohibitions on acting as director of any German company for up to 5 years.
