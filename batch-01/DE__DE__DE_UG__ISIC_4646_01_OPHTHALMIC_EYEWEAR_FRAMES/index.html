Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and local Finanzamt; official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) as policy authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German UG must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the local Finanzamt through ELSTER, indicating corporate existence, shareholders, business purpose, and projected turnover. The Finanzamt assigns a local tax number (Steuernummer); a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is then issued by the BZSt for cross-border transactions. Foreign directors require a German tax representative for non-established registration if applicable.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections include corporate income tax and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft), which requires control and profit-and-loss pooling, and hybrid mismatch elections available under German implementation of the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for standard UG VAT registration; German entrepreneurs are obliged to register for VAT upon commencing commercial activity. The small-business exemption under § 19 UStG applies only for turnover not exceeding €22,000 in the preceding calendar year and expected turnover not exceeding €50,000 in the current year, but it is optional and not available for export activities seeking input VAT deduction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to exports of goods to third countries under § 4 No. 1 UStG in conjunction with § 6 UStG, provided the goods leave the Community customs territory within the statutory deadlines and exporter retains the required documentary evidence. Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers are zero-rated under § 6a UStG, subject to valid VAT IDs and delivery evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. A phased mandatory e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) regime applies to domestic B2B transactions, requiring structured machine-readable formats (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, PEPPOL BIS) by 2025 for receipt and by 2027/2028 for full issuance; compliance is integrated with the standard VAT audit and not a live fiscal authorisation.

Zero-rating is subject to full documentary audit by the Customs authorities and Finanzamt; missing or inconsistent export proof triggers retroactive VAT assessment, statutory interest under § 233a AO, and denial of input VAT deduction. Fraudulent claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, carrying imprisonment of up to five years or fines and retrospective clawback of all VAT understated.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU free movement of capital provisions. Profit remittance is unrestricted but requires compliance with AWV reporting obligations for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500. No prior approval is needed for remittances, but documentation (e.g., invoices, tax certificates) must be retained for verification. Capital account transactions (e.g., equity injections) are also freely convertible, subject to statistical reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments (receipts and disbursements) exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes within 5 working days of the transaction. No conversion ratio is imposed; exporters may freely choose to convert or hold foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; FX control and statistical reporting are administered by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. German BaFin-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Payer, Qonto, or N26 Business can open accounts for a UG, offering streamlined onboarding and multi-currency capabilities. However, these EMIs may impose restrictions on high-volume cross-border trade settlements and FX hedging. Offshore EMIs (non-EU) are not viable due to lack of local licensing and potential AML/CFT concerns. For a wholesale eyewear distribution business, a traditional bank is recommended for primary treasury operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the CIT (aggregate 15.825%), plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at the federal base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (commonly 200% to 490%); combined effective statutory burden typically 29.8% to 33.0% for a Berlin, Hamburg or Munich UG.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the 25%), assuming no church tax. Dividends to a German-resident corporate shareholder are exempt from withholding. Dividends to a qualifying non-resident corporate shareholder holding more than 10% are fully exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (§ 43b EStG). Under German double tax treaties (e.g., Germany-US, Germany-Switzerland, Germany-UAE), the withholding rate is reduced to 5% for qualifying holdings of at least 10% and 15% in all other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For calendar-year UGs the tax return is due on 31 July of the following year for self-filers (§ 149 AO). Where the return is prepared by a tax advisor (Steuerberater), the statutory deadline is automatically extended to the 15th month after fiscal year-end, i.e. 31 March of the second following calendar year for calendar-year taxpayers, and is further administratively extended to 31 May by BMF decree.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the UG's cross-border related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceed EUR 5,000,000 for merchandise (e.g., wholesale acetate eyewear frames) or EUR 1,000,000 for services. Master File: mandatory where the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 60 days of a formal tax-office request (30 days for extraordinary transactions); failure triggers § 162(4) AO penalty of 5% of the income adjustment, minimum EUR 5,000.

Economic substance scope: No formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) registration exists in Germany. Substance requirements are enforced through: (i) § 10 AO, under which tax residence is determined by the place of effective management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung); (ii) § 42 AO, which disregards artificial arrangements lacking economic substance; (iii) § 50d(3) EStG, which denies treaty or EU-directive relief to foreign shareholders that are letterbox entities without their own business operations, personnel and premises; and (iv) § 1 AStG, which allocates profits on the basis of the functions performed, assets used and risks borne. A UG acting purely as an invoicing conduit for a foreign principal without local decision-making, inventory, employees or commercial risk risks loss of German tax residence, denial of VAT recovery, and recharacterization of profits as hidden distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, and from 6 months to 10 years in especially serious cases under § 370(3) AO; full recovery of evaded taxes; evasion interest (Hinterziehungszinsen) of 0.5% per month on the assessed shortfall (§ 235 AO); extension of the statute of limitations to up to 10 years; confiscation of the proceeds of crime; publication of the criminal conviction.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable for the evaded corporate tax, trade tax, wage tax and VAT under §§ 34, 69 AO; civil restitution is enforceable against private assets; courts may impose a travel ban, seize the passport and, where the director obstructs enforcement, impose Erzwingungshaft (compulsory imprisonment) of up to 2 years; a conviction triggers disqualification from holding a directorship in Germany.
Managing Director Liability for Unpaid Taxes and Insolvency Violations

Statutory sanction: The tax office issues a liability order (Haftungsbescheid) under § 69 AO against the managing director; the director must reimburse the UG for payments made after insolvency under § 64 GmbHG; criminal charges for breach of trust (Untreue, § 266 StGB) or bankruptcy offenses (§ 283 StGB) may follow.

Director personal liability: Personal, unlimited and joint-and-several liability; enforced through seizure and garnishment of the director's assets and bank accounts; travel restrictions and refusal of exit permits where the director is a flight risk; imprisonment of up to 5 years for fraudulent bankruptcy; the same regime applies to de facto directors and shadow managers controlling the UG from abroad.
