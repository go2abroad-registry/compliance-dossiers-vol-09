Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers, VIES recapitulative statements and the federal electronic-invoice platform; locally competent district tax office (Finanzamt) for corporation tax, trade tax, VAT and payroll tax assessment; statutory electronic filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A German GmbH obtains its statutory tax identity as follows: (1) notarised incorporation and entry into the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht; (2) within four weeks of formation, submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER, declaring shareholders, ownership chain, business purpose, banking details and expected turnover; (3) the competent Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (tax assessment number) for corporation tax, trade tax and VAT; (4) separately apply to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr., format DE + 9 digits) through the BZSt online form, which is mandatory for EU cross-border B2B invoicing and VIES listing; (5) obtain an EORI customs registration number from the German Customs authority (Generalzolldirektion) for export declarations; (6) register for the OSS scheme via ELSTER where EU B2C digital services are supplied. Foreign shareholders require no German permit; no statutory tax representative is mandatory for EU-resident shareholders, but BZSt and the Finanzamt may demand proof of identity and economic substance for non-EU principals.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a resident, opaque capital company (Körperschaft) with unlimited German tax liability on worldwide income, subject to treaty relief. Profits bear corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on the corporation tax (effective 0.825%) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (effective rate approximately 7% to 17%). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a resident, opaque capital company (Körperschaft) with unlimited German tax liability on worldwide income, subject to treaty relief. Profits bear corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on the corporation tax (effective 0.825%) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (effective rate approximately 7% to 17%). German tax law does not permit pass-through treatment or fiscal transparency for a standard GmbH.

Available election forms

Germany applies a categorical, non-elective entity classification (no US-style check-the-box). Available elections affecting a GmbH: (i) Organschaft fiscal-unity election — VAT group under Section 2(2) no. 2 UStG and corporation tax group under Section 14 KStG, conditional on financial, economic and organisational integration plus a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (ii) reverse corporate classification election under Section 1a KStG permitting certain partnerships and sole traders to opt into corporate taxation (not applicable to a GmbH as its default status); (iii) annual tax-return elections for loss carry-back utilisation, asset valuation and depreciation; (iv) German hybrid-mismatch rules (Section 4k EStG, implementing ATAD 2) disallow deduction/non-inclusion mismatches, and no elective hybrid-mismatch instrument is recognised. The GmbH is treated as an opaque entity for all EU and third-country tax treaty and classification purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany imposes no general turnover threshold for VAT registration: every entrepreneur making taxable supplies in German territory must file VAT returns. The only statutory exclusion is the small-business scheme (Section 19 UStG) for turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 in the prior year and EUR 100,000 projected in the current year — inapplicable to a professional design-subscription GmbH. For EU distance sales and cross-border B2C digital services, an annual EUR 10,000 union threshold triggers OSS registration; foreign suppliers supplying services in Germany must register for VAT regardless of turnover. The operative registration trigger for this entity is therefore the first taxable supply, not any monetary floor.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the digital-design subscription model, German VAT is displaced or zero-rated as follows: (1) EU B2B customers — reverse-charge displacement under Section 13b UStG where the customer's valid VAT ID is quoted and the invoice states 'Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers'; (2) non-EU business customers — 0% German VAT under Section 4(1) no. 1(b)(bb) UStG where the place of supply is the third-country recipient's seat under Section 3a(2) UStG; (3) EU B2C consumers — VAT of the consumer's member state through the OSS regime, not zero-rated; (4) physical UI/UX print or tangible prototype exports — 0% under Section 6 UStG with customs proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) within the statutory evidence period. Continuous proof of the recipient's business seat, delivery logs, IP handover records and bank payment origin is required to sustain 0% treatment or reverse charge.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically via ATLAS/e-customs with the exporter's EORI reference (DE format)
  • International transport evidence: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer statement substantiating consideration received
  • Commercial invoice in EN 16931/UN-EDIFACT format with VAT-ID declarations and reverse-charge annotation
  • Ausfuhrnachweis (German customs proof-of-export acknowledgement) satisfying Section 6(2) UStG
  • Service delivery log, UI/UX handover records and recipient's business registration or VAT-ID evidence for services

Germany applies a phased B2B structured e-invoicing mandate. From 1 January 2025, every resident entrepreneur must be capable of receiving structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x under EN 16931/Peppol); from 1 January 2027, issuers with annual turnover above EUR 800,000 must transmit e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all resident B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. Germany does not operate an Italian-style real-time clearance or continuous transaction control at issuance; instead, the BZSt operates the federal e-invoice reception platform (EESF) through which foreign suppliers and non-resident issuers lodge EN 16931-compliant invoice files, interoperable with Peppol networks. A subscription billing stack using Stripe or Wise must therefore emit ZUGFeRD 2.x or CII-XML files, quote the customer's VAT ID for EU B2B reverse-charge supplies, and reconcile monthly to the VAT prepayment return (UStVA), the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and any OSS declaration; no physical stamping or prior fiscal approval applies to digital design services.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or sham reverse-charge is prosecuted as tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Section 370 AO: imprisonment from six months up to ten years in aggravated cases, criminal and administrative fines, automatic reassessment at 19% VAT and an extended ten-year assessment period under Section 169(2) AO. Failure to produce the export proof within 60 days of dispatch triggers retroactive reclassification of the supply as taxable in Germany under Section 6(2) UStG. Interest of 0.5% per month applies to under-declared VAT under Section 233a AO; managing directors are jointly and severally liable under Sections 69 and 71 AO. Non-reporting of cross-border receipts of EUR 12,500 or above to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV attracts administrative fines of up to EUR 30,000 per case. BZSt, German Customs and the tax auditor additionally perform real-time forensic matching of VAT-ID confirmations, Stripe/Wise payment flows, IP addresses and service logs; recurring or organised abuse triggers union-level VAT administrative cooperation under Regulation (EU) No 904/2010 and potential removal of the taxpayer from the VIES and OSS schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital-account and current-account convertibility for the euro under the EU Treaties, the OECD Codes of Liberalisation, and Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital). There are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval for cross-border payments, and no restrictions on holding, converting, or repatriating funds in any freely convertible currency. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to taxation and documentation: dividends distributed by a German GmbH to non-resident shareholders are subject to statutory withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate) and, where applicable, church surcharge; treaty relief (including the 0% withholding rate under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying 10% holdings held for at least 12 months) may be obtained through the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) refund procedure. For cross-border design-subscription service revenue, German domestic withholding tax is generally not imposed on pure service fees; however, royalties or payments with a German source and no permanent establishment may be treaty-affected. The only substantive constraint on free convertibility is the EU/CFSP sanctions regime: funds of designated persons or entities must be frozen, and transactions with blocked jurisdictions are prohibited under the AWG/AWV Abwicklungsverbot, irrespective of currency or account location.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender regime: there is no statutory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no requirement to sell export receipts into euros. Exporters may freely retain, invoice, and disburse proceeds in any currency, in German or foreign bank accounts. The residual obligations are statistical and documentary: cross-border payments received from non-residents, including service-export proceeds, must be reported electronically to Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV within five business days where the individual payment exceeds EUR 12,500; the report is filed through the Bundesbank's statistical reporting portal using the official balance-of-payments purpose classification. For high-volume service receivables, the relevant reporting threshold for aggregate annual receivables from non-residents also triggers the Bundesbank's direct and indirect investment statistics. No official license is required to credit export proceeds to a domestic account, and the General Permission (Allgemeine Genehmigung) under the AWV permits unrestricted crediting and use of export receipts; however, the paying bank must screen each SWIFT/SEPA message against EU, OFAC and UN sanctions lists and transaction-typology filters.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Germany's central bank and balance-of-payments statistics authority) acting jointly with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) as prudential supervisor, and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) as the competent authority for investment screening. The legal bases are the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz - AWG), the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG), and EU/CFSP sanctions regulations directly applicable in Germany.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, and strictly conditional on legal authorization. An EEA-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution (e.g., Wise Europe, N26, PPS) holding a valid PSD2 passport may lawfully open and operate a payment or e-money account for a German GmbH without a separate German license, provided the passport has been notified to BaFin. Such a fintech account is operationally viable for collecting design-subscription fees, executing supplier disbursements, and effecting FX conversion at mid-market rates. However, an offshore digital EMI without an EEA passport and without a German correspondent relationship cannot lawfully provide payment services in Germany; engaging an unregulated third-country EMI may trigger administrative and criminal liability of the managing director under the ZAG and jeopardize the account relationship at tier-1 German banks. Furthermore, the statutory GmbH capital deposit (minimum share capital of EUR 25,000) cannot be validly verified for the Handelsregister registration through a pure offshore EMI: German notaries, commercial-register courts, and tier-1 banks require a capital confirmation (Kapitalbestätigung) or bank receipt from a German credit institution or a German branch of an EEA bank. Inbound subscription revenue may nevertheless be initially collected by Stripe Billing into an EEA settlement account (e.g., Stripe's own IDBV/EMI partners) and then swept to the German operational account; this two-rail structure is legally sound but must be disclosed to the German account officer to avoid suspicious-transaction reporting or account termination.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for inbound equity capital or shareholder loans is a SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the German beneficiary account held by the GmbH; for very large transfers the correspondent clearing may require a pre-notification through the receiving bank's capital-increase desk. The payment message must include a complete remittance-information field setting out the shareholder's legal name, the GmbH's commercial-register number, the notarial deed reference for the foundation or capital increase, and an explicit purpose text such as 'Kapitaleinlage gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag / Kapitalerhöhung' so that the receiving bank can map the funds to the statutory direct-investment purpose code in the Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics. Where both parties hold SEPA/IBAN accounts and the currency is EUR, SEPA Instant or TARGET2 is an acceptable clearing rail, but the same statutory reference must be preserved. The bank will demand a notarized copy of the capital-increase resolution or the foundation deed, the updated Gesellschafterliste, the current Handelsregister extract, the UBO confirmation, and a completed capital-measures form ('Kapitalmaßnahmen-Fragebogen') prior to releasing any funds. Statutorily, the GmbH must report the inbound cross-border payment to Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV within five business days if the payment exceeds EUR 12,500; the electronic report is filed through the Bundesbank statistical reporting portal. If the inbound transfer is executed in USD or another third-country currency, the corresponding bank will additionally screen the transaction against EU/OFAC/UN sanctions lists and may ask for a statement of the underlying capital transaction; a failure to provide the purpose code or the notarial packet will cause the funds to be placed in suspense and, ultimately, repatriated. Inbound capital for a GmbH is not subject to exchange-control approval, FDI clearance does not apply for the EU/EEA parent, and, for the design-subscription sector, no sectoral license triggers the investment-screening review threshold.For a German design-as-a-service GmbH, outbound disbursements comprise payments to foreign contractors, cloud and SaaS licensors, freelance designers, marketing agencies, and tax or legal advisers. Within the euro zone, disbursements are cleared over SEPA Credit Transfer and TARGET2, and the payment order must identify the beneficiary IBAN, the VAT treatment (reverse-charge B2B or intra-community supply), and, where applicable, the beneficiary's valid EU VAT-identification number for the invoice file. For non-EEA disbursements, the governing rails are SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for supplier payments, SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for interbank clearing where a correspondent credit is required, and, for goods-related or high-value settlements, a Commercial Letter of Credit pursuant to UCP600 or a standby LC — although LC-based settlement is atypical for recurring design-subscription services and tends to be reserved for hardware-integrated projects. Every outbound disbursement must include a precise purpose text ('Zahlungsgrund: Vergütung für Design-Dienstleistungen' and the applicable Bundesbank balance-of-payments service-purpose code), and the paying bank will screen each beneficiary against EU/OFAC/UN sanctions; payments to high-risk jurisdictions or to personal accounts of foreign freelancers require additional underlying-documentation (contract, invoice, deliverable acceptance) to avoid the false-invoicing money-laundering typology. Cross-currency disbursements are executed via the bank's FX desk or the corporate FX platform (e.g., Wise Business), with the FX confirmation retained as the treasury record. Outbound payments to non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be captured in the GmbH's Bundesbank balance-of-payments report within five business days, although in practice the bank forwards the statistics on payments made through its accounts; for payments made through fintech rails, the GmbH retains primary reporting responsibility unless the fintech provides statistical reporting on its behalf.FX Forward (Devisenterminkontrakt) with a German-regulated bank or an EEA bank passporting into Germany, used to fix the EUR/USD conversion rate on forecast subscription receivables settled via Stripe and Wise; permissible without prior authorization, but subject to EMIR margin and reporting rules if the GmbH exceeds the non-financial-counterparty clearing threshold.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) on restricted or emerging-market currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL) strictly limited to hedging genuine and documented design-subscription receivables in those currencies; NDFs require economic-substance justification, an ISDA or local master agreement, and are subject to EMIR reporting and BaFin-compliant counterparty due diligence.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS / Währungsswap) for multi-year USD, GBP or CHF revenue streams converted into EUR operating liquidity, provided the swap is documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a CSA collateral annex and qualifies for the hedging exemption from mandatory clearing for the non-financial counterparty.Vanilla European FX Options (call and put) purchased for downside protection on disputed or variable client payments, particularly where the design subscription includes milestone-based pricing; OTC options must be executed with a prudentially supervised counterparty, marked-to-market monthly, and reported under EMIR where applicable.Spot FX and short-dated FX swaps (Tom/Next and value-date rollovers) used for operational liquidity of incoming multi-currency subscription receipts and for funding the German EUR payroll and supplier ledger; these instruments are executable directly with the corporate bank's FX e-trading platform without prior regulatory approval and are treated as treasury best-execution trades.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, and strictly conditional on legal authorization. An EEA-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) or payment institution (e.g., Wise Europe, N26, PPS) holding a valid PSD2 passport may lawfully open and operate a payment or e-money account for a German GmbH without a separate German license, provided the passport has been notified to BaFin. Such a fintech account is operationally viable for collecting design-subscription fees, executing supplier disbursements, and effecting FX conversion at mid-market rates. However, an offshore digital EMI without an EEA passport and without a German correspondent relationship cannot lawfully provide payment services in Germany; engaging an unregulated third-country EMI may trigger administrative and criminal liability of the managing director under the ZAG and jeopardize the account relationship at tier-1 German banks. Furthermore, the statutory GmbH capital deposit (minimum share capital of EUR 25,000) cannot be validly verified for the Handelsregister registration through a pure offshore EMI: German notaries, commercial-register courts, and tier-1 banks require a capital confirmation (Kapitalbestätigung) or bank receipt from a German credit institution or a German branch of an EEA bank. Inbound subscription revenue may nevertheless be initially collected by Stripe Billing into an EEA settlement account (e.g., Stripe's own IDBV/EMI partners) and then swept to the German operational account; this two-rail structure is legally sound but must be disclosed to the German account officer to avoid suspicious-transaction reporting or account termination.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer on taxable income plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (aggregate 15.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at rates between 7% and 17%, yielding a typical combined burden of about 30-33%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital-yield tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, giving an aggregate 26.375%; the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces the rate to 0% for an EU parent company holding at least 10% for 12 months; comprehensive German DTAs commonly reduce the rate to 5% for portfolio corporate shareholders or 15% for other non-treaty recipients.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 in aggregate per annum; extraordinary individual controlled transactions exceeding EUR 500,000; Master File and CbC Report: group consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany is not an offshore ESR jurisdiction; it imposes general anti-abuse (§ 42 AO), substance-based beneficial-ownership tests (§ 50d(3) EStG), ATAD/GAAR, and CFC rules. A German GmbH providing UI/UX digital subscriptions to foreign principals must maintain real office, employees, contracts, board decisions and bank accounts; otherwise treaty privileges may be denied and hidden profit distribution adjustments imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or monetary fine (§ 370 AO); especially serious evasion cases (large amount, abuse of authority) carry 6 months to 10 years; plus arrears interest at 0.15% per month, late surcharges, and court-ordered confiscation of tax savings.

Director personal liability: Managing directors incur direct criminal liability for their own false declarations; corporate veil does not protect them. They may be personally sued for unpaid taxes under §§ 69, 73 AO and § 34 AO, and imposition of travel bans and asset freezes is possible under § 132a AO during the criminal investigation.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Dividend/Royalty Tax

Statutory sanction: The GmbH is treated as debtor and becomes liable for the full unwithheld amount; payment is enforced under § 44(1) and § 50a EStG with late payment surcharges (§ 240 AO, 1% per month) and interest under § 233a AO; administrative fines can be imposed on the legal entity.

Director personal liability: The managing director personally becomes Haftungsschuldner for the withheld but unremitted tax under § 73 AO, jointly and severally with the GmbH; the liability is not dischargeable in insolvency and may lead to enforcement against personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under § 162(4) AO, the tax office may presume adjusted business income and impose a surcharge of 5% of the adjusted amount, minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 100,000, if documentation is not provided within 30 or 60 days; late documentation carries the same penalties under GAufzV.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed insufficient records or misrepresented transactions are personally liable for the tax adjustment if they acted with intent or gross negligence; the liability is enforced through § 69 AO and may be combined with criminal tax evasion charges.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse and AML Negligence

Statutory sanction: Denial of trade tax allowance and treaty benefits; recharacterization of transactions as hidden profit distributions; loss forfeiture under § 8c KStG; deregistration and liquidation; administrative fines under § 56 GwG (up to EUR 400,000 for natural persons and up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons) for failure to implement AML controls.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for wrongful trading (§ 64 GmbHG), insolvency-preference payments, and punitive criminal sanctions. Where a foreign tax authority assesses the true economic owner, the director may be subject to cross-border tax warrants, travel bans, and extradition proceedings.
