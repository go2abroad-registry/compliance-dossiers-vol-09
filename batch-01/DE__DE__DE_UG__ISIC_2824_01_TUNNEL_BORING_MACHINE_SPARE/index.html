Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Local Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the competent Local Court (Amtsgericht) – typically the district court where the company's registered office is located.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG) as amended, particularly §§1, 2a, 5a (UG), 7-8 (formation), 35-38 (management), and the Commercial Code (HGB) §§8-16 for registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and Application for Registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) in the Commercial Register.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG requires a minimum share capital of EUR 1.00 per shareholder, but the company must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000. The capital must be fully paid up in cash or in kind before registration; no escrow account is required for cash contributions, but the contribution must be at the free disposal of the managing director.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire department (Feuerwehr) and structural load inspections from the building authority (Bauamt). Occupational safety clearance from the Employer's Liability Insurance Association (Berufsgenossenschaft) and compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG) and the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung).

Paid-in capital rule: UG requires a minimum share capital of EUR 1.00 per shareholder, but the company must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000. The capital must be fully paid up in cash or in kind before registration; no escrow account is required for cash contributions, but the contribution must be at the free disposal of the managing director.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen); operative administration by the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt, www.bzst.de) and the Federal Customs Service (Generalzolldirektion), with local municipal tax offices (Finanzämter) as primary tax administration and the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) for electronic filing.

E-government filing portal

After the UG is entered in the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht, the managing director must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent Finanzamt, in paper form or via ELSTER, whereupon the Finanzamt assigns the local Steuernummer; the UG must then apply separately to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, format DE + 9 digits). Foreign entities without a German seat or permanent establishment register directly with the competent Finanzamt and may in practice require a tax representative, although EU-based suppliers are not statutorily obliged to appoint one.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — an Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with unlimited resident tax liability on worldwide income subject to 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), producing an effective combined rate of approximately 23% to 33% depending on the municipal Hebesatz (local assessment rate of 200%–490%). — After the UG is entered in the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht, the managing director must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent Finanzamt, in paper form or via ELSTER, whereupon the Finanzamt assigns the local Steuernummer; the UG must then apply separately to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, format DE + 9 digits). Foreign entities without a German seat or permanent establishment register directly with the competent Finanzamt and may in practice require a tax representative, although EU-based suppliers are not statutorily obliged to appoint one.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with unlimited resident tax liability on worldwide income subject to 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), producing an effective combined rate of approximately 23% to 33% depending on the municipal Hebesatz (local assessment rate of 200%–490%).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in German law; the UG is mandatorily opaque for corporate income tax. Relevant elections include: VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG; income/corporate tax group (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) under §§14–17 KStG requiring a domination and profit-and-loss transfer agreement with a corporate shareholder holding at least 75% of shares; the small-business regime under §19 UStG (practically unavailable to an exporting manufacturer); and GLoBE Pillar Two top-up elections under the Mindestbesteuerungsgesetz (MinStG), with hybrid-mismatch adjustments contained in §4k EStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to a resident UG: any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany must register with the Finanzamt. The small-business exemption of §19 UStG (previous-year turnover below EUR 25,000 and current-year expected turnover below EUR 100,000, effective from 2025) is in practice unavailable to a manufacturing exporter, and non-EU foreign suppliers are subject to registration whenever they perform German-taxable supplies or remote digital services, without any de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Third-country export supplies are zero-rated under §§4 Nr. 1 lit. a and 6 UStG where the UG dispatches the TBM spare parts and machinery into a third-country customs territory and obtains documentary proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) within the statutory evidence periods. Intra-EU dispatches are zero-rated under §§4 Nr. 1 lit. b and 6a UStG against the customer's valid EU VAT ID, with mandatory filing in the European Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung). Services ancillary to exported heavy equipment are generally treated under §3a Abs. 2 UStG as supplied where the B2B recipient is located and therefore fall outside German VAT if the customer is a non-resident entrepreneur.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged in the ATLAS/AES export system with a valid Movement Reference Number (MRN) and printed Ausfuhrbegleitdokument
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, Air Waybill, or CMR waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, Incoterms statement, and certificate of origin (Ursprungszeugnis)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/telex payment confirmation evidencing payment of the foreign-currency purchase price
  • Intra-EU dispatch documents where applicable: customer VAT ID validation, delivery/call-off notes, and any export licence for dual-use or mining-controlled equipment

German law mandates structured electronic invoicing (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD with UBL-XML) for domestic B2B supplies: from 1 January 2025 every entrepreneur must be able to receive e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 issuers with prior-year taxable turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all remaining entrepreneurs must issue them, with legacy EDI formats accepted until 31 December 2027. Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance, API authorisation, or digital stamping system (unlike Italy, Hungary, or Poland), but the Federal Government has announced transaction-based electronic reporting to BZSt aligned with the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) programme, expected for 2028.

False or insufficient export substantiation retroactively converts the zero-rated supply into a 19% VAT-taxable supply with assessments under §§169–171 AO (seven-year limitation period in evasion cases), monthly interest of 0.5% under §233a AO, late-payment surcharges of 1% per month, and penalties of up to EUR 50,000 plus imprisonment under §370 AO for tax evasion, or up to EUR 30,000 for negligence under §378 AO. BZSt and the Generalzolldirektion cross-match MRN and VAT records, and customs post-clearance recovery follows UZK Articles 103/105 (three-year standard period, five years for punishable acts). Germany imposes no separate statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects the standard arm's-length credit period for intercompany export receivables, with later-settled balances exposed to transfer-price adjustments and constructive dividend treatment under §1 AStG.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account regime under the Eurozone framework. Both current account transactions and capital movements are unrestricted for residents and non-residents. Profit remittances, dividends, and interest payments face no prior approval requirements or convertibility hurdles when compliant with standard tax, AML, and beneficial ownership reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds required under German law. Exporters must submit statistical reports to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the Bundesbank reporting portal within statutory timelines (typically monthly for aggregated transactions). Conversion of export receipts into EUR occurs at prevailing market rates with no forced ratios or surrender percentages. Reporting thresholds apply for individual shipments exceeding €12,500.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability for cross-border electronic money services under BaFin licensing and PSD2/MiCA frameworks. Legal constraints include mandatory BaFin registration, minimum capital requirements, strict investor protection rules, and restrictions on e-money issuance for non-EU activities. Traditional banking rails preferred for large-volume treasury operations due to settlement finality and regulatory capital mandates.
