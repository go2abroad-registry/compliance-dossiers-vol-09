Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) is the competent authority for issuing the national VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and for administering the EU recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and the OSS/IOSS one-stop-shop portals. The locally competent municipal tax office (Finanzamt) at the UG's registered seat (Sitz) and place of central administration (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) administers corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), payroll tax, and the domestic VAT registration file (Steuerakte). The principal digital interfaces are the ELSTER online tax portal (www.elster.de) and the BZSt VAT ID portal (www.bzst.de), supplemented by customs clearance through the ATLAS system operated by the Generalzolldirektion (www.zoll.de).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt/Ordnungsamt) and complete the commercial register entry (Handelsregister) for the UG, including shareholder resolutions, managing director appointment, and the articles of association. Step 2: Within one month of business commencement, submit the official statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent Finanzamt exclusively via ELSTER, declaring the UG legal form, registered address, estimated turnover and profit, bank account, shareholders, and the intended VAT method; failure to do so is an administrative offense and may trigger an estimated tax assessment. Step 3: Upon opening the tax file, the Finanzamt issues the national personal tax number (Steuernummer) in the format 10/123/45678, while the BZSt, following a separate application within the questionnaire, issues the cross-border USt-IdNr. (VAT ID). Step 4: Register in the customs IT system ATLAS and obtain an EORI number (DE + 9 digits) through the Generalzolldirektion for physical export activities. Step 5: Appoint a domestic authorized agent (Empfangsbevollmächtigter) for tax service of process if the sole managing director resides outside Germany. Step 6: Activate the ELSTER mailbox for mandatory electronic filing of monthly VAT prepayment returns, annual VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and corporate tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)) is a German micro-capital corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) and a fully separate legal and tax subject with no pass-through or transparent alternative at federal level, irrespective of the residency of its shareholders. It is subject to unlimited tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) in Germany because both its statutory seat and its place of central administration are domestic, meaning its worldwide commercial income is within the corporate income tax net unless relieved by a double tax treaty. The UG bears corporate income tax at 15% plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate typically between 7% and 17% depending on the municipal multiplier. For indirect tax purposes the UG is an entrepreneur (Unternehmer) under Section 2 UStG, entitled to input VAT deduction (Vorsteuerabzug) and obliged to charge German Umsatzsteuer on its domestic and communal supplies. No special tax-exempt zone or freeport applies to the UG even where export-oriented bonded warehousing for Kickstarter/tabletop-dice inventory is used under customs warehousing procedures.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime; no election can make the UG fiscally transparent. The electable mechanisms available are: (a) Organschaft (VAT and income-tax grouping) under Sections 14 and 17 KStG, Section 2(2) GewStG, and Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG, requiring financial, economic, and organizational integration and allowing profits, losses, and VAT outputs to be pooled intra-group; (b) election of monthly VAT filing with extended deadline (Dauerfristverlängerung under Section 18(6) UStG) against payment of one-eleventh of the prior-year VAT liability; (c) small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG, available only where prior-year turnover was not more than EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000 — generally disadvantageous for a dice manufacturer purchasing resins, molds, and packaging because input VAT recovery is foreclosed; (d) option to tax on a cash-received basis (Ist-Versteuerung under Section 20 UStG) if the prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000; (e) election to apply the OSS non-Union/Union scheme to designate VAT on cross-border business-to-consumer distance sales at the destination country rate; and (f) hybrid mismatch outcome-shifting is precluded by Section 4k EStG, which neutralizes deduction/non-deduction mismatches involving entities or instruments treated as transparent in one state and opaque in the other.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold relieving a domestic UG from VAT registration: registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply, and the Finanzamt will open the VAT file upon receipt of the registration questionnaire regardless of revenue volume. The only statutory low-turnover threshold is the Section 19 UStG small-business cap of EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year turnover, which is optional and disqualifies the UG from input VAT recovery if elected. For cross-border nexus: non-established foreign businesses trigger German VAT registration from their first taxable transaction with no de-minimis, while EU distance sales of goods are subject to the uniform Union-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold below which the supply may remain taxed under German domestic rules unless the seller elects OSS; for intra-Community acquisitions by a non-eligible acquirer, the registration threshold is EUR 12,500. As an export-active manufacturing entity with Kickstarter, Stripe, and PayPal receipts, the UG will in practice be mandatorily, fully VAT-registered and will file via the OSS for all its EU private-consumer distance sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The UG may claim effective zero-rating (steuerfrei with full input VAT deduction) only in three statutory patterns: (i) export supplies to third-country territories under Section 4 No. 1(a) and Section 6 UStG, provided the tabletop dice and accessories physically leave the German customs territory within 90 days of the supply and the export documentation exists before the VAT return for that month is filed; if the goods fail to leave within 90 days, the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply at 19%; (ii) intra-Community B2B dispatches under Section 4 No. 1(b) and Section 6a UStG, requiring the receiving entrepreneur's valid EU VAT ID and actual transport to another Member State, with the Gelangensbestätigung (arrival confirmation) obtained within 45 days and retained cumulatively; and (iii) closely related ancillary services supplied in connection with exported goods under Section 18(9) UStG. Supplies to private EU consumers are not zero-rated: they are distance sales taxable at the destination Member State's rate via OSS (or at 19% German rate while the EUR 10,000 Union threshold is not exceeded), while supplies to non-EU private backers (US, UK, Canada, Japan) are genuine exports and zero-rated if the proof bundle is intact. The UG must therefore maintain a segmented sales ledger distinguishing domestic supplies, OSS/EU distance sales, intra-EU B2B dispatches, and third-country exports before each VAT return is submitted.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and the customs office of export (Ausfuhrzollstelle)
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, HS tariff codes under Chapter 95 (e.g., 9504.90 or 9503.00), country of origin DE/EU, item values in EUR, and the UG's EORI number
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing the physical departure of the goods from Germany to the non-EU destination
  • Carrier consignment note, proof of delivery (POD), and the customs-stamped export surrender copy (Ausfertigung der Ausfuhrsendung) or the electronic fallback record
  • Inward remittance certificate from the UG's German bank account, or an FX conversion receipt, evidencing repatriation of the export proceeds to Germany
  • For intra-EU B2B dispatches: the customer's valid EU VAT ID, the Gelangensbestätigung (confirmation of arrival) signed by both parties, and transport documents tracking the goods to the Member State of destination
  • For Kickstarter, Stripe, and PayPal merchant-of-record sales: platform payout statements, order identifiers, and shipment tracking proof mapped to each export parcel

Since 1 January 2025, Germany has a mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing framework (E-Rechnung): every taxable person must be able to receive structured electronic invoices in an EN 16931-conformant format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, XML syntax UBL or CII) through interoperable transmission channels such as Peppol, email, or the recipient's ERP interface; the issuer-side mandate is phased in, with full structured e-invoicing required by 1 January 2027 for enterprises with more than EUR 800,000 annual turnover and by 1 January 2028 for all other issuers, subject to grandfathering of existing EDI agreements. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal invoice clearance or digital stamping regime comparable to the Italian SDI or French PPF, but any point-of-sale cash or card terminal used at conventions or trade fairs must be connected to a certified Technical Security Device (TSE) under Section 146a AO to prevent software manipulation. All VAT filings — monthly prepayment returns, annual returns, the Zusammenfassende Meldung, and OSS declarations — are mandatory electronic submissions through ELSTER or the OSS portal. Intra-EU B2B invoices must show the recipient's valid EU VAT ID and the notation that the supply is an intra-Community supply; export invoices to third countries require the export declaration number and EORI references but no digital stamp is required.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to targeted audits by the Finanzamt and the Generalzolldirektion's audit service, including electronic cross-checks of ATLAS data, carrier waybills, and German banking records. If the UG cannot present the full export documentation bundle, the VAT exemption is retroactively denied and output VAT at 19% is assessed, with late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) under Section 233a AO running from the original statutory due date; payment of the assessed amount does not extinguish a penalty, because Section 379 AO separately imposes fines of up to EUR 5,000 for the false, incomplete, or untimely preparation of the Gelangensbestätigung or export declarations. A deliberately falsified export paper trail or systematic use of the UG as an invoice intermediary in a missing-trader scheme constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to 10 years and asset seizure, with joint and several liability extending to the managing director and, in established trust cases, to the beneficial shareholder. The Generalzolldirektion additionally monitors the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation standard for export receivables: proceeds not repatriated within 180 days of invoice, absent documented bank guarantees or MG/equity offsets, will be recharacterized as hidden profit distribution or shifted income, generating a transfer-pricing upward adjustment under Section 1 AStG, additive trade tax, and potential criminal exposure under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act. The statutory limitation period for tax clawbacks is four years in ordinary cases and ten years in evasion cases, while the criminal prosecution period for VAT fraud is five years generally and ten years where aggravating circumstances exist; in a Kickstarter or payment-service-provider structure, the UG remains the person liable for the export VAT and customs debt wherever the UG's EORI number or VAT ID appears on the export declaration, and the platform's merchant-of-record status does not relieve the UG of its own documentation and substantiation obligations.

Germany maintains full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions pursuant to EU liberalization rules. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted provided tax clearance and beneficial ownership documentation are satisfied.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective single-entity rate 15.825%); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 14–17% yields an aggregate effective rate of approximately 30–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding (effective rate 26.375%); church tax (8–9%) may be added if the shareholder is an individual subject to church tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation is triggered for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per tax year (per GAufzV and German Administrative Principles 2020). Low-value intercompany services up to EUR 100,000 may qualify for simplified documentation. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but economic substance is crucial to establish tax residence (place of effective management under § 10 AO) and to preserve DTA benefits under the MLI principal purpose test. A UG lacking personnel, premises, business decisions, or a German bank account is presumed a shell; the tax authorities may reattribute its income to the shareholder and disallow treaty relief under § 42 AO (abuse of legal forms).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years for commercial or particularly serious cases) and/or an unlimited criminal fine. In addition to imprisonment, the evaded tax is due plus late-payment interest of 0.15% per month; a final criminal conviction can trigger forfeiture of profits.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate tax when the non-payment results from intentional or grossly negligent breach of duties (§ 69 AO). Directors face criminal prosecution, travel bans, seizure of private assets, and up to 7 years’ disqualification from acting as a director.
Failure to File / Incomplete Record-Keeping

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of EUR 25 per month (or 0.25% of assessed tax, minimum EUR 25 per month), capped at EUR 25,000; coercive fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000 by the tax office; if no tax is due, penalty still up to EUR 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors become jointly and severally liable for all monetary consequences arising from late or inaccurate filing (§ 69 AO), including the monthly penalties and interest. Persistent non-filing can lead to tax office estimation and criminal proceedings for tax evasion against the director.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: A surcharge of 5% of the resulting tax adjustment is imposed, with a minimum penalty of EUR 5,000; if tax authorities cannot determine the arm's-length price, they may estimate income at maximum levels. Intentional TP misstatements constitute tax evasion and attract criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the surcharge and the additional tax if the documentation failures are attributable to their wilful misconduct or negligence (§ 69 AO). In severe cases, personal liability for the entire adjusted tax shortfall may be asserted.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax benefits are denied and income is reattributed to the actual shareholder under § 42 AO; fines up to EUR 150,000 for non-compliance with Transparency Register obligations (§ 56 GwG); the commercial register may strike off the UG upon insolvency or non-payment.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for all taxes, interest, and surcharges that result from the disregard of the shell structure. Additional AML penalties of up to EUR 150,000 are imposed personally; for intentional evasion or money laundering, directors face imprisonment, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets.
