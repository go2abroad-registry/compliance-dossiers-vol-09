Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The entire cash contribution must be paid before the managing director applies for registration. Contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. German law does not require a statutory escrow account; funds must be demonstrably available to the company, with a corporate bank account normally opened before or immediately after registration. The UG must retain one-quarter of annual profit, after loss carryforwards, as a legal reserve until conversion conditions are met.

Funding & unlock path: For physical storage or handling, obtain building approval where required, fire-protection approval or review from the competent fire authority, structural and load-capacity verification for storage areas and racks, workplace-risk assessment under German occupational-safety law, hazardous-substance assessment, emergency planning, suitable ventilation and dust controls, and ADR compliance for transport. No separate clearance is normally required for a pure broker with no premises, inventory or handling operations.

Paid-in capital rule: A UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The entire cash contribution must be paid before the managing director applies for registration. Contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. German law does not require a statutory escrow account; funds must be demonstrably available to the company, with a corporate bank account normally opened before or immediately after registration. The UG must retain one-quarter of annual profit, after loss carryforwards, as a legal reserve until conversion conditions are met.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No general industrial licensing board is required for ordinary sulfur mineral trading or brokerage. The competent local Gewerbeamt handles trade notification; site-specific permitting is handled by the relevant Land or municipal environmental, building, fire and occupational-safety authorities.

Category: Gewerbeanmeldung (trade notification). A separate Betriebsgenehmigung may be required for physical storage, processing or emissions-generating installations, including permits under the Federal Immission Control Act (Bundes-Immissionsschutzgesetz, BImSchG), depending on the facility and quantities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt; portal: www.bzst.de) issues the German VAT ID (USt-IdNr) and administers OSS/IOSS and foreign entrepreneur VAT registrations; municipal tax offices of the federal states (Finanzämter) assess corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT, and issue the Steuernummer; all electronic filing is mandatory via ELSTER (www.elster.de). Export/import customs declarations are processed electronically through the ATLAS system of the Generalzolldirektion and Hauptzollämter.

E-government filing portal

Domestic UG: (1) notarial incorporation deed and registration with the commercial register (Handelsregister, Amtsgericht) to obtain the HRB number; (2) file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung under §138 AO) via ELSTER within one month of commencing business; (3) the local Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer and registers the entity for VAT and wage tax; (4) separately apply to BZSt for the USt-IdNr (VAT ID) using Form USt 1 A for intra-EU and export transactions. Non-resident sellers without a German establishment register directly with BZSt via Form USt 1 TG and, if established outside the EU, must appoint a German fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter); there is no de minimis VAT registration threshold for non-established suppliers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a German-resident UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is fully tax resident with unlimited worldwide tax liability under §1 KStG; corporate income tax 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge equals 15.825%, and municipal trade tax (base rate 3.5% x local Hebesatz) yields a combined effective burden of approximately 29-33%. No pass-through election exists. Foreign business income is subject to German tax with foreign tax credit relief under DTTs; dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings are 95% exempt under §8b KStG. Non-resident sellers without a German establishment register directly with BZSt via Form USt 1 TG and, if established outside the EU, must appoint a German fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter); there is no de minimis VAT registration threshold for non-established suppliers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a German-resident UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is fully tax resident with unlimited worldwide tax liability under §1 KStG; corporate income tax 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge equals 15.825%, and municipal trade tax (base rate 3.5% x local Hebesatz) yields a combined effective burden of approximately 29-33%. No pass-through election exists. Foreign business income is subject to German tax with foreign tax credit relief under DTTs; dividends and capital gains from qualifying shareholdings are 95% exempt under §8b KStG. No special German tax-exempt zone applies to trading profit, but goods may be staged in free ports (Freihäfen) and customs warehouses that are outside the German VAT territory and defer import VAT.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is mandatory and determined by statutory type comparison (Typenvergleich) under §§1-2 KStG. Elections relevant to a UG: (i) waiver of the VAT small-business exemption under §19 Abs. 2 UStG; (ii) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft, §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG) and corporate tax grouping via a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (§14 KStG); (iii) for partnerships only, the option to be taxed as a corporation under §1a KStG (not applicable to a UG); (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under §4k EStG (ATAD 2) are mandatory, with documentation duties and no elective opt-out; (v) selection of the input VAT apportionment key under §15 Abs. 4 UStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Resident entrepreneurs are registrable from the first taxable transaction; the §19 UStG small-business exemption applies only if turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the prior year and is not expected to exceed EUR 100,000 in the current year (thresholds raised effective 1 January 2025), so an industrial-mineral trading UG will be mandatorily registered. Non-established foreign suppliers must register from the first euro of German-taxable supplies. For intra-EU distance sales to consumers, the EUR 10,000 pan-Union threshold triggers OSS taxation in the customer's member state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under §4 Nr. 1a and §6 UStG to export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferung) of elemental sulfur (HS 2503) when: (i) the goods physically leave the EU customs territory to a third-country destination; (ii) the goods are transported or dispatched by the UG or by the non-resident buyer; (iii) the export is evidenced by the Ausfuhrnachweis (export proof under §§8-13 UStDV) which must be in the entrepreneur's possession when the VAT return is filed and retained for 10 years. No EU export duty applies to sulfur. When the UG acts as a disclosed broker or commissionaire for non-resident principals, the commission is outside German VAT scope under §3a Abs. 2 UStG B2B place-of-supply rules; related freight, insurance and ancillary services qualify for exemption under §4 Nr. 3 UStG. Input VAT on German and EU purchases of the sulfur, warehousing, inland freight and handling is fully deductible, generating a cash refund through the monthly Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung or annual VAT return in structurally export-heavy periods.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) released in the ATLAS electronic customs system by the Hauptzollamt
  • Customs export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument/ABD) or the electronic export declaration confirmation
  • Bill of lading (Konnossiment) or sea waybill (Seefrachtbrief) issued by the shipping line, or forwarder's cargo receipt for multimodal movements
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung), packing list and delivery notes aligned with the letter of credit terms
  • Bank and LC payment records evidencing receipt of funds; Germany does not operate an inward remittance certificate regime, but such evidence is commercially maintained as supporting export documentation

From 1 January 2025, every entrepreneur established in Germany must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) conforming to EN 16931 (ZUGFeRD 2.x / XRechnung) and must retain records per GoBD. Issuance of e-invoices is phased: mandatory from 1 January 2027 for entrepreneurs with taxable turnover above EUR 800,000 in 2026, and from 1 January 2028 for all others; bilateral EDI agreements under Art. 230 of the VAT Directive remain valid through 31 December 2027. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance model and does not use digital stamping; tax authorities rely on GoBD-compliant storage, ELSTER filing, ATLAS customs clearances, and unannounced cash register audits (Kassen-Nachschau) with certified technical security modules for B2C receipting systems.

German law does not impose a statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard LC and export credit insurance practice, and under accrual accounting the receivable is taxable even before collection. Tax audits focus on the existence and plausibility of the Ausfuhrnachweis, the physical movement of goods to third countries, anti-avoidance in circular export schemes, and missing trader chains. If zero-rating is disallowed, the Finanzamt retrospectively assesses VAT at 19% plus monthly interest of 0.5% (§233a AO) and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month (§240 AO). Penalties include tax evasion under §370 AO (imprisonment from six months to five years, up to ten years in aggravated cases, plus fines) and fines of up to EUR 50,000 under §378 AO for grossly negligent under-declaration; managing directors face personal liability under §§34, 69 AO and §64 GmbHG. Assessment limitation periods are 4 years standard, 5 years for gross negligence, and 10 years for tax evasion; commercial and VAT records must be retained for 10 years (§147 AO).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility for both capital and current account transactions, consistent with EU free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU and OECD liberalisation codes. There are no exchange controls, no central-bank approval for inbound or outbound transfers, and no prior screening of profit or dividend remittances. Dividend distributions by a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft) to non-resident shareholders are legally unrestricted but are subject to 26.375% German withholding tax (25% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) before applicable double-tax-treaty reduction or exemption. Practical friction exists mainly in correspondent bank compliance, sanctions screening, and the mandatory statistical declarations to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, mandatory repatriation, or forced conversion regime for export proceeds in Germany. Exporters may hold receivables in EUR or any foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, two reporting duties apply: (i) exports of sulfur mineral ore are captured by customs/Intrastat declarations (ATLAS) for EU external-trade statistics; (ii) under Section 67 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), each receipt of an export payment from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within seven calendar days, generally executed by the receiving German bank through its statistical reporting interface. No conversion ratio is prescribed, and assignment of export proceeds to a trade-finance bank is unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank and monetary/statistical foreign-exchange reporting authority); prudential banking and AML supervision is exercised jointly by BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) and the European Central Bank within the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited viability. German-licensed and EU-passported EMI/PSP fintechs (e.g., entities authorised under the German Payment Services Supervision Act, ZAG) are feasible only as operational auxiliary accounts for SEPA/EUR clearing, vendor payments, and expedient account-velocity onboarding; they are not viable primary rails for dry-bulk commodity trade. Legal constraints: an electronic-money institution may not grant credit, issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide bank guarantees, or maintain a full-service correspondent account network. Trade settlements for sulfur cargoes (MT103 USD wires, MT700 LC) require a KWG-licensed credit institution with correspondent and trade-finance capacity. Feasibility rating for this UG: Medium for operational collections, Low for the core LC-based trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 (EUR or USD) to the German account of the DE_UG entity, with the payment narrative referencing the capital contribution or shareholder loan and including the statutory purpose code for the German balance-of-payments statistics (AWV code for direct-investment equity/debt) as declared by the beneficiary bank at the time of credit. All inward payments exceeding EUR 12,500 are automatically reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the receiving German bank within seven calendar days. No Bafin authorization or conversion mandate applies to inbound non-resident investment; however, the UG must be able to evidence, upon bank request, the shareholder resolution, the capital-credit entry in its German GAAP accounting, and the corresponding Transparenzregister update for the UBO.The primary clearing rails for sulfur mineral-ore trade settlements are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct outbound payments and disbursements against invoices; (ii) SWIFT MT700 irrevocable documentary letters of credit (L/C) governed by UCP600, typically payable at sight against presentation of B/L, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, weight certificate and insurance document; (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) as a lower-cost fallback; (iv) SWIFT MT760 standby undertakings where performance bonding is required; and (v) ISO 20022/T2 payments for EUR clearing inside the eurozone. All outbound payments in EUR or foreign currency exceeding EUR 12,500 are reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the paying German bank under the AWV; USD payments routed through US correspondent banks are additionally subject to OFAC sanctions screening and must be structured with full originator/beneficiary transparency to avoid delays.FX spot transactions (delivery T+2) for immediate conversion of trade proceeds – unrestricted.FX outright forwards (deliverable) for hedging firm USD/EUR receivables or payables in the bulk-sulfur trade; subject to bank credit-line approval and documented underlying exposure.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps (CCS) for matching longer-term trade and borrowing flows, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the German Master Agreement for Financial Futures Transactions (DGV).Plain-vanilla FX options (calls and puts) for asymmetric hedge of cargo tender risk; permitted with the bank's derivative-capacity assessment under German MiFID rules.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for hedging exotic/non-convertible currency pairs where no onshore deliverable market exists; for EUR/USD or EUR/GBP the deliverable forward is standard and NDF pricing is unnecessary.Commodity and freight hedging (e.g., FFA on Baltic Exchange or sulfur-linked swaps) is legally permissible but is treated as speculative under German commercial law unless a direct hedge nexus to the physical cargo documentation is maintained.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited viability. German-licensed and EU-passported EMI/PSP fintechs (e.g., entities authorised under the German Payment Services Supervision Act, ZAG) are feasible only as operational auxiliary accounts for SEPA/EUR clearing, vendor payments, and expedient account-velocity onboarding; they are not viable primary rails for dry-bulk commodity trade. Legal constraints: an electronic-money institution may not grant credit, issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide bank guarantees, or maintain a full-service correspondent account network. Trade settlements for sulfur cargoes (MT103 USD wires, MT700 LC) require a KWG-licensed credit institution with correspondent and trade-finance capacity. Feasibility rating for this UG: Medium for operational collections, Low for the core LC-based trade settlement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of 7–17%; aggregate effective rate approximately 29–31%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing an aggregate statutory rate of 26.375%. Reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period; treaty rates may further reduce the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 2,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (administrative threshold under the German Verwaltungsgrundsätze; local file expected above this level). Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue above EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting threshold EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not impose the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but applies substance-over-form under §42 AO and the anti-avoidance jurisprudence. A UG must maintain a real German registered office, a physically located management, employees, own capital, and substantive commercial records. A letterbox company is disregarded for tax purposes, treaty benefits may be denied under the beneficial ownership clause, and CFC rules under the AStG re-attribute passive income to ultimate foreign shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Off-Book Schemes

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years under §370 AO, unlimited criminal fine, reassessment of evaded tax, interest of 6% p.a. under §233a AO, and estimated assessment under §162 AO where records are absent.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable under §69 AO for taxes evaded through gross negligence or intent, may be subjected to travel bans and asset freezes, and face disqualification from serving as directors under §6 GmbHG.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity under §42 AO; re-attribution of trading income to the actual beneficial owner; denial of DTA relief; reassessment with 6% interest and late payment surcharges; foreign entity may be treated as tax transparent.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal restitution for asset stripping or undercapitalization of the UG, liability under §64 GmbHG for payments made after insolvency, and criminal charges for breach of trust under §266 StGB.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Defect

Statutory sanction: Reversal of burden of proof under §162(4) AO; tax authorities may estimate income upward and impose a mandatory surcharge for missing documentation of up to 10% of the adjusted income (capped at €25,000); additional penalties under §378 AO for negligent underdeclaration.

Director personal liability: Directors who wilfully or grossly negligently fail to ensure documentation are personally liable for the surcharge and penalties under §69 AO; repeated or wilful conduct triggers criminal liability for aiding tax evasion under §370 AO.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The UG is liable as withholding agent for the full uncollected tax under §44(1) EStG, plus 1% monthly late payment surcharge and §233a interest; intentional failure constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO with imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve distributions or cross-border payments without verifying WHT compliance are personally liable for the unwithheld tax under §69 AO in conjunction with §44 EStG, and may face criminal conviction, travel bans, and seizure of assets.
