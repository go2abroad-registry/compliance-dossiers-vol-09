Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – the Federal Central Tax Office, and local Finanzämter (district tax offices). Primary portals: ELSTER (elster.de) for filing and Steuernummer management; 'Mein BZSt' (bzst.de) for VAT ID application and certificates; ATLAS/AES customs export declaration portal for export zero-rating evidence.

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation of the UG (haftungsbeschränkt), the notary registers the company with the Handelsregister; the competent local Finanzamt automatically issues the Steuernummer after receiving the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire). For the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), the UG files an application with BZSt via the 'Mein BZSt' portal (Form USt 1 TN). A non-resident foreign entity with no German establishment applies directly to BZSt or the responsible Finanzamt for a VAT ID and provisional tax number; if no fixed German place of business exists, a fiscal representative or direct electronic registration through ELSTER/BZSt may be required, and the entity must obtain a German EORI number for customs export clearances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for a UG under German law; the UG is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of KSt), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, municipal rate approximately 7–17%). Available elections: VAT treatment option for real-estate supplies (Sec. 9 UStG); small-business VAT exemption (Sec. 19 UStG, €25,000 threshold); corporate and VAT Organschaft (tax group/fiscal unity) for at least 95% participations; loss carry-back and carry-forward elections (Sec. 10d EStG, Sec. 8c KStG); investment subsidy deduction (Sec. 7g EStG); and hybrid-mismatch consequences under Sec. 4k EStG (ATAD) — there is no elective neutralization for entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany does not impose a general economic turnover registration threshold for resident taxable persons; every entrepreneur is required to register under Sec. 18 UStG. A small-business exemption under Sec. 19(1) UStG applies up to €25,000 annual turnover (effective 2023); for annual turnover above €100,000, the exemption is unavailable, and it is generally economically adverse for an exporting manufacturer claiming input VAT offsets. Non-resident and foreign distance suppliers must register in Germany from the first taxable transaction, with no minimum threshold; OSS/IOSS may replace domestic registration for B2C cross-border supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of canned boba tapioca pearls to a non-EU third country are zero-rated under Sec. 6 UStG provided the goods physically leave the EU by the supplier, the customer, or a third party acting on the supplier's behalf, and the export is evidenced by customs declarations and transport documents. Intra-Community (EU) dispatches are exempt as 'steuerfreie innergemeinschaftliche Lieferungen' under Sec. 4 No. 1(b) and 6a UStG if the customer holds a valid EU VAT ID and the goods demonstrably arrive in the destination Member State. The goods must be exported in an unaltered commercial state, without post-export processing in the EU, transported under the documented commercial Incoterms, and the supplier must obtain the prescribed documentary proof within the applicable tax period; invoicing must clearly reference the exemption and include the customer's VAT ID for intra-EU sales.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged via ATLAS/AES with the exporter's German EORI number and exit confirmation (Ausfuhrbescheinigung)
  • Bill of lading (B/L), air waybill, or CMR/railway consignment note showing the EU exit frontier and third-country consignee
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung) containing Incoterms, HS code, full description 'canned tapioca pearls' or 'aseptically tubed quick-cook tapioca pearls', and VAT zero-rating or intra-EU exemption reference
  • Packing list evidencing commercial quantities, net weights, batch numbers, and shipping marks corresponding to the customs declaration
  • Gelangensbestätigung (delivery confirmation) for intra-EU supplies; for non-EU exports, the customs verification together with transport contract, insurance certificate, and proof of payment remittance

No real-time fiscal clearance, revenue stamping, or API-based transaction transmission applies in Germany. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for business-to-government (B2G) supplies in XRechnung/Factur-X format. From 1 January 2025, domestic B2B e-invoicing becomes the default legal regime: structured electronic invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X) must be capable of being received by all domestic businesses; enforcement is phased by prior-year turnover (issuers with more than €800,000 turnover must issue e-invoices from 1 January 2026; all suppliers from 1 January 2027). VAT returns are transmitted via ELSTER (monthly/quarterly Voranmeldungen), while the German tax administration applies automated risk profiling and documentary audit obligations; e-invoices and supporting records must be preserved for 7 years (10 years for accounting records).

Falsified or inadequate export evidence triggers retroactive VAT assessment at 19% on the invoice consideration, disallowance of input VAT credits, late-payment interest (6% per annum under Sec. 233a AO), default surcharges under Sec. 240 AO (1% per commenced month, maximum 10%), and administrative fines up to €50,000 pursuant to Sec. 26a UStG. Criminal tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO applies to intentional misdeclaration of exports, with imprisonment up to 5 years (10 years in aggravated cases); managing directors of the UG are personally liable, and Sec. 26c UStG imposes joint liability on participants. Tax audits (Betriebsprüfung) may claw back zero-rated output for up to 10 backward assessment years where export documents are not contemporaneous, the goods cannot be traced to the declared border crossing, the customer lacks a valid VAT ID for intra-EU claims, or the payment/FX proceeds fail economic plausibility checks; a minimum 10-year retention of matched customs, transport, and payment evidence is mandatory to defend zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unconditional currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Articles 63-66 TFEU and IMF Article VIII obligations (accepted 1961). There is no legal distinction or restriction between capital and current account payments; no authorization is required for cross-border dividends, loan repayments, royalties, or equity inflows. Profit remittance from a German UG to foreign shareholders is freely executable through the banking system; the only treasury hurdle is the statutory withholding tax on dividends of 26.375% (25% tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), which can be reduced to zero via the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% shareholding, 12-month holding period) or to a treaty-reduced rate under an applicable double-tax treaty. Sanctions, EU embargoes, and anti-money-laundering freezes are the only substantive blocking conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements on export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain foreign currency receipts in domestic or offshore accounts, convert at will, and use them for working capital. The sole statutory duty is statistical: an export payment received from a non-EU state which, in aggregate with other payments from the same state in a calendar month, exceeds EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven calendar days after the end of the month (AWV Section 67). No export proceeds conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) is the competent monetary authority and administrator of external-sector statistical reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV); BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) supervises banks and payment institutions; BAFA (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle) administers export-control licensing and embargo enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for low-value operational collections, FX conversion and expense management; Low for any documentary trade operation.
