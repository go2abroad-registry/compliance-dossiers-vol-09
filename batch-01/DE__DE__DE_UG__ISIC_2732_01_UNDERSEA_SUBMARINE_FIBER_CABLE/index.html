Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG (haftungsbeschränkt) has a statutory minimum share capital of EUR 1.00 (one euro), which must be fully paid-up in cash before registration; no escrow account or capital blocking account is required by law, but the Managing Director must sign a notarial capital-confirmation declaration under § 8(2) GmbHG proving the contributions are at the company's free disposal. A paid-up capital of EUR 1,000–3,000 is commercially advisable; 25% of annual net profits must be allocated to a statutory reserve until the issued capital reaches EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG).

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety acceptance certificate (Brandschutzabnahme) from the local fire authority before commissioning, covering the cable extrusion lines and cable oil storage; structural load clearance (Standsicherheitsnachweis) from the building authority for heavy winches, cable towers and lead-processing equipment pursuant to the state building code; occupational workplace clearance in accordance with the Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG) and DGUV regulations, including machinery risk assessments, VOC workplace-exposure monitoring, cable-drum handling safeguards, and clearance by the government-supervised occupational physician.

Paid-in capital rule: UG (haftungsbeschränkt) has a statutory minimum share capital of EUR 1.00 (one euro), which must be fully paid-up in cash before registration; no escrow account or capital blocking account is required by law, but the Managing Director must sign a notarial capital-confirmation declaration under § 8(2) GmbHG proving the contributions are at the company's free disposal. A paid-up capital of EUR 1,000–3,000 is commercially advisable; 25% of annual net profits must be allocated to a statutory reserve until the issued capital reaches EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) together with the local tax office (Finanzamt) under the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF). Official e-portal: ELSTER (ElsterOnline, www.elster.de); BZSt VAT portal at www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Following commercial register entry, the UG must file the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') electronically via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt within four weeks of formation. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (income tax/trade tax number) and coordinates with BZSt to issue the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) under § 27a UStG. Non-resident shareholders do not require a separate TIN for entity registration. A foreign managing director with German tax liability must obtain a personal Steuernummer. Any subsequent cross-border activity requires an updated questionnaire; foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic establishment must register for VAT via BZSt using Form USt 1 TG. An EORI number is required for all customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the UG is a corporation taxed at entity level: corporation income tax at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge thereon and municipal trade tax at 14-17% depending on the Hebesatz; aggregate effective rate approximately 30-33%; no pass-through eligibility).

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§ 14, 17 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG for CIT, trade tax and VAT consolidation, requiring financial, economic and organizational integration plus a profit-and-loss transfer agreement; (2) small-business VAT relief under § 19 UStG for turnover up to €25,000 in 2025 with option to tax; (3) hybrid mismatch disallowance and ATAD-aligned rules under § 4k EStG and § 8b KStG restricting deduction/non-taxation outcomes; (4) no unitary or combined reporting election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration; any entrepreneurial activity triggers registration. The Kleinunternehmer relief (§ 19 UStG) exempts turnover up to €25,000 in 2025 with option to tax, but undersea cable export manufacturing constitutes entrepreneurial activity requiring VAT registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of subsea fiber cables to third-country destinations qualify for 0% VAT under § 4 Nr. 1 and § 6 UStG where the goods physically leave EU customs territory. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered EU customer qualify under §§ 4 Nr. 1b, 6a UStG subject to valid VAT ID and EC Sales List filing. Offshore services connected to cable installation are taxed at the recipient's jurisdiction for B2B supplies under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG; auxiliary export services (freight, insurance, handling) to non-EU recipients are zero-rated under § 4 Nr. 3 UStG. Claiming zero-rating requires strict documentary proof of export (Belegnachweis) and transport evidence (Beförderungsnachweis); absence of such proof triggers reverse assessment of 19% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed electronically via ATLAS with MRN certificate
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or CMR/AWB transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank confirmation) evidencing payment receipt from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed packing list
  • Proof of origin (EUR.1 movement certificate or Statement on Origin for preferential trade agreements)
  • Beförderungsnachweis (transport proof: carriage contract, port loading order, customs office of export confirmation)

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for federal contracts in XRechnung/ZUGFeRD format under the E-Rechnungsverordnung. A phased B2B e-invoice mandate (EN 16931 format) applies from 1 January 2025 for receipt of e-invoices; issuance becomes mandatory for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2027 for suppliers with turnover above €800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all suppliers. Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; however, electronic cash registers require a BSI-approved Technical Security Element (TSE). Customs exports are declared electronically via ATLAS; the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) will add digital reporting requirements for intra-EEA supplies from 2030.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict scrutiny by the Finanzamt (field audit / Betriebsprüfung) and the Zoll (customs) and German export control authorities. Non-compliant or inadequately documented claims result in retrospective VAT assessment at 19% plus default interest under § 233a AO (0.5% per month, generally up to six years for VAT). Fraudulent zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to five years, with targeted enforcement against offshore VAT fraud schemes. Invoice liability under § 25d UStG and § 71 AO extends to the invoice issuer for uncollected VAT. Customs declaration violations under § 382 AO attract administrative fines up to €5,000 per act; repeated non-compliance triggers revocation of zero-rating for subsequent periods, denial of input VAT recovery, and disqualification from the official exporter status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account under the EU free movement of capital regime (TFEU Articles 63-65). The euro is a freely deliverable, deeply liquid currency; there are no exchange controls, no surrender obligations, no FX licensing, no prior-approval procedures, and no capital repatriation permits for foreign shareholders. The sole statutory overlay is statistical: every cross-border payment or receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days under Section 67 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), typically discharged automatically by the bank's reporting feed on behalf of the client. Profit remittance by a UG is structurally unrestricted after adoption of the annual financial statements and completion of commercial register disclosure; the only operative clearance hurdle is punctual filing of annual accounts and maintenance of a clean KYC profile with the relationship bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in Germany. Receipts from exporting armored submarine telecommunication cables may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any foreign currency, and may be held in domestic or overseas accounts without restriction. The only mandatory compliance duty is the AWV Section 67 reporting threshold: receipts from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be captured in the Bundesbank Z1/Z2 balance-of-payments statistics, and open export receivables or supplier trade credits exceeding EUR 12,500 must appear in the monthly external-position statement. No conversion ratio, no forced sale to the central bank, and no minimum retention period applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank, foreign exchange statistics, balance-of-payments reporting) and Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin, banking supervision and AML/KYC enforcement); export-control licensing authority is the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury use, Medium as a secondary operational overlay. BaFin-licensed German electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., PPRO, Airwallex Germany, Banking Circle) can legally process EUR-denominated sub-threshold disbursements and provide virtual IBANs, but the mandated clearing rails in this treasury blueprint are Government Letter of Credit and SWIFT wire, both of which require a full credit-institution relationship to issue, confirm, negotiate, or advise documentary credits under UCP600. Offshore and non-German EMIs are lawfully usable only for peripheral flows; they cannot act as confirming banks for sovereign LCs, generally refuse to accept Bundesbank AWV reporting responsibility, and may trigger BaFin KYC scrutiny if material outflows are routed through them. Recommendation: maintain one BaFin-regulated EMI account for minor supplier and utility payments below EUR 12,500; route all LC proceeds, above-threshold wires, and hedging transactions through Tier-1 German banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed as a SWIFT MT103 credit to the UG's German bank account IBAN with the payment reference 'Kapitaleinlage [UG legal name] Handelsregister HRB [number]' and the Bundesbank AWV purpose classification 'ERWERB VON ANTEILSRECHTEN / KAPITALANLAGE' (acquisition of equity interests). The transfer is lawful without prior ECB or Bundesbank authorization under TFEU free movement of capital; it is not a VAT, corporate-tax, or customs event at entry, and the receiving bank will require the notarized contribution confirmation (Einzahlungsbeleg) to classify the credit as equity rather than shareholder debt. If the non-resident shareholder instead funds via shareholder loan, the wire must carry 'GESELLSCHAFTERDARLEHEN' as the purpose text, and the bank will apply thin-capitalization, interest-transfer-pricing, and source-of-funds KYC checks; an equity injection is administratively preferred. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury use, Medium as a secondary operational overlay. BaFin-licensed German electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., PPRO, Airwallex Germany, Banking Circle) can legally process EUR-denominated sub-threshold disbursements and provide virtual IBANs, but the mandated clearing rails in this treasury blueprint are Government Letter of Credit and SWIFT wire, both of which require a full credit-institution relationship to issue, confirm, negotiate, or advise documentary credits under UCP600. Offshore and non-German EMIs are lawfully usable only for peripheral flows; they cannot act as confirming banks for sovereign LCs, generally refuse to accept Bundesbank AWV reporting responsibility, and may trigger BaFin KYC scrutiny if material outflows are routed through them. Recommendation: maintain one BaFin-regulated EMI account for minor supplier and utility payments below EUR 12,500; route all LC proceeds, above-threshold wires, and hedging transactions through Tier-1 German banks.
