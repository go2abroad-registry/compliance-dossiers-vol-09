Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted current- and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and Sections 1 and 4 AWG. There are no exchange controls, no capital-transaction licensing requirements, and no repatriation limits applicable to a German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), either inbound or outbound. Profit and dividend remittance is free of exchange-control hurdles; the only structural costs are the statutory 25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% combined), which is eliminated for qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for 12 months under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) and may be reduced under applicable double-taxation treaties. Practical hurdles are limited to (i) mandatory AWV statistical reporting of cross-border payments above the reporting threshold, (ii) publication of annual financial statements in the Bundesanzeiger, and (iii) documenting shareholder loans on an arm's-length basis to avoid hidden-equity or thin-capitalisation recharacterisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation period, or mandatory conversion ratio for export, freight, or service proceeds. A UG may freely retain charter-hire and transport earnings in any convertible currency in domestic or foreign accounts without Deutsche Bundesbank approval. The sole compulsory obligation is statistical reporting under the AWV: every payment to or from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) within the statutory deadline (currently five working days after value date), classified under the applicable purpose/statistical code. These reports are informational only and do not constitute authorisation, permission, or conversion instructions.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), Frankfurt am Main, exercising monetary and external-payments statistics authority under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV); prudential and AML/CFT supervision of banks, payment institutions, and EMIs is vested in the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin), Bonn/Frankfurt, with ongoing operational AML supervision of banks performed jointly by BaFin and the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

CONDITIONAL MEDIUM. BaFin-licensed or EEA-passported EMIs (e.g., Pliant, Kontist, Qonto, Airwallex) are legally permitted under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) and PSD2 to provide multi-currency IBANs, SEPA Instant, and SWIFT-capable outgoing wires, with faster onboarding and lower servicing costs. The hard legal limitation is that an EMI is not a credit institution under the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG): it cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide trade advances, or underwrite vessel finance, and its correspondent banking network may restrict high-value USD clearing. Offshore EMIs operating without a German licence or EU passport violate Sect. 73 ZAG and Sect. 54 KWG, constituting unlawful payment services and banking business, which triggers immediate account freezing and BaFin enforcement. Therefore, the fintech EMI is viable only as a secondary wallet for sub-threshold operational payments; the primary clearing relationship must be a full German commercial bank (Vollbank) to support the LC-heavy and high-value USD treasury profile of a RoRo car-carrier operation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder funds are remitted through SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR within SEPA) or SWIFT MT103 (any currency outside SEPA) into a dedicated founder contribution account (Gründungskonto) of the UG in formation; the bank blocks the funds until Handelsregister registration is completed and then releases them into the operational Girokonto. Each remittance must be tagged with the AWV purpose/statistical code for direct investment (Direktinvestition — Kapitalzuführung) and reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank via ITRS if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Shareholder loans require a written arm's-length loan agreement and must be coded as external loan liabilities to prevent recharacterisation as hidden equity or constructive dividend. The beneficiary line must carry the UG's tax number and VAT ID, and the remitter must state a clear reason for payment (e.g., /EQUITY/SUBSCRIBED-CAPITAL) to avoid false-positive sanctions filtering.High-value trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 for USD and other non-EUR disbursements via correspondent banking, with USD clearing through CHIPS/Fedwire and high-value EUR through T2 (TARGET2). EUR payments below EUR 100,000 settle through SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant. Documentary commercial letters of credit are issued under ICC UCP 600 / eUCP 2.0 with SWIFT MT700/MT710 message types, supported by the full shipping-document set (negotiable bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, P&I cover note). Bank-to-bank cover is executed with SWIFT MT202 COV. All outbound disbursements exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV Sect. 62 within the statutory deadline, and the underlying charterparty, port disbursement account, bunker delivery notes, and invoices must be archived with each payment instruction for audit and AML defence.Outright FX Forward (Devisentermingeschäft) — locked-rate hedge for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP freight and charter-hire flows, documented under the German Master Agreement for Financial Derivative Transactions (DRV) or an ISDA; exempt from MiFID II appropriateness assessment when used strictly for hedging.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) — cash-settled hedge (in EUR or USD) for revenues or voyage costs receivable in restricted, non-deliverable emerging-market currencies where onshore conversion is prohibited; no physical exchange of principal.Cross-Currency Swap (CCS) and FX Swap — to convert USD-denominated vessel-finance liabilities and USD charter revenue into the UG's functional currency base; the UG as a non-financial counterparty under EMIR below the clearing threshold is exempt from mandatory central clearing but must report every derivative to an authorised trade repository.Vanilla FX Options (plain-vanilla call and put) — permissible for hedging freight-rate and currency-uncertainty within shipping contracts, provided that the premium, strike, and underlying exposure are documented to evidence the hedging purpose (transaktionsbezogene Absicherung) and to avoid speculative recharacterisation under German commercial and tax law.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: CONDITIONAL MEDIUM. BaFin-licensed or EEA-passported EMIs (e.g., Pliant, Kontist, Qonto, Airwallex) are legally permitted under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) and PSD2 to provide multi-currency IBANs, SEPA Instant, and SWIFT-capable outgoing wires, with faster onboarding and lower servicing costs. The hard legal limitation is that an EMI is not a credit institution under the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG): it cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide trade advances, or underwrite vessel finance, and its correspondent banking network may restrict high-value USD clearing. Offshore EMIs operating without a German licence or EU passport violate Sect. 73 ZAG and Sect. 54 KWG, constituting unlawful payment services and banking business, which triggers immediate account freezing and BaFin enforcement. Therefore, the fintech EMI is viable only as a secondary wallet for sub-threshold operational payments; the primary clearing relationship must be a full German commercial bank (Vollbank) to support the LC-heavy and high-value USD treasury profile of a RoRo car-carrier operation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality; the statutory base rate is 3.5% multiplied by the municipal assessment factor. The resulting combined corporate and trade tax burden commonly falls around 30%, but is municipality-dependent. Qualifying international maritime shipping profits may, subject to election and statutory conditions, be taxed under the tonnage-tax regime in section 5a of the Income Tax Act rather than on actual profit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic rate of 26.375%, before any church tax or relief. Treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief may reduce or eliminate the charge if statutory conditions are met. A refund or exemption is not automatic.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German transfer-pricing documentation obligations apply to cross-border related-party transactions under sections 90(3) and 90(4) of the Fiscal Code and the related regulations. The Local File documentation threshold is generally annual transaction volumes exceeding EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or exceeding EUR 600,000 for other transactions, subject to the applicable statutory rules and documentation categories. A Master File is generally required where the German entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 100 million. These thresholds do not remove the obligation to price all related-party transactions at arm's length or to provide records when requested.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. German tax consequences nevertheless depend on actual facts, including management and control, business functions, personnel, assets, risk assumption, beneficial ownership, and the location of effective management. Anti-abuse rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, controlled foreign company rules, transfer-pricing rules, and permanent-establishment principles may deny benefits or reallocate income where arrangements lack commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion or reckless tax understatement

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code may be punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, and in particularly serious cases by imprisonment from six months to ten years. Reckless tax understatement under section 378 may result in a fine of up to EUR 50,000. Tax, interest, and potentially other criminal or administrative consequences remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal criminal liability and personal tax-payment liability under the Fiscal Code where statutory duties are breached. Civil piercing of the corporate veil, travel bans, or imprisonment are not automatic; criminal travel restrictions and detention require the applicable procedural and substantive legal grounds.
Late or non-filing and unpaid assessed tax

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, late-payment surcharges, interest, estimated assessments, compulsory collection, and potential administrative fines may apply. Continued non-compliance may lead to enforcement against the company and, where statutory conditions are satisfied, liability of responsible managers.

Director personal liability: A managing director may be liable for tax debts under section 69 of the Fiscal Code where intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties causes non-payment. Personal criminal liability requires the elements of a criminal offence; imprisonment and travel restrictions are not automatic.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Income may be adjusted under section 1 of the Foreign Tax Act. Documentation failures can trigger surcharges under section 162 of the Fiscal Code, including a base surcharge generally starting at EUR 5,000 and potentially percentage-based surcharges, subject to statutory minimums, maximums, and exceptions. Additional tax, interest, and penalties may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors may face company-law, tax, and potentially criminal consequences if they knowingly cause false reporting or intentionally or grossly negligently breach tax duties. No automatic veil piercing or travel ban applies.
Treaty abuse, beneficial-ownership failure, or improper withholding relief

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied or revoked; the withholding agent may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and applicable surcharges. Intentional or reckless conduct may also trigger tax-offence or administrative proceedings.

Director personal liability: The responsible managing director may be personally liable for withholding tax under statutory rules where the required withholding and remittance duties are culpably breached. Criminal sanctions require proof of the relevant offence.
Unlicensed or non-compliant commercial shipping operations

Statutory sanction: Consequences depend on the specific maritime, flag-state, port-state, environmental, and licensing provisions breached and may include administrative fines, detention of vessels, suspension or withdrawal of approvals, and civil liability. These consequences are separate from corporate income tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal administrative or criminal liability where the applicable law imposes duties on them or their conduct satisfies the offence elements. Liability is not automatic merely because the company operates a vessel.
Insolvency-related payment or accounting misconduct

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include civil repayment claims, managing-director liability, insolvency-related criminal offences, fines, and imprisonment under the applicable insolvency and criminal provisions. The precise sanction depends on the conduct and statutory elements.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments and may face criminal prosecution where the legal requirements are met. Imprisonment and travel restrictions require individual legal grounds and are not automatic.
