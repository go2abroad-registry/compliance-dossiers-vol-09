Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregisterabteilung (Commercial Register Division) of the Amtsgericht (Local Court), Munich, Germany; electronic filing through the secured XJustiz government portal (Elektronisches Handelsregister), with publication via the Unternehmensregister (www.unternehmensregister.de).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung - GmbHG), in particular §§ 1, 2, 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), 7(2), 8 and 10; Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch - HGB) §§ 8 ff. on the commercial register; trade notification governed by § 14 Gewerbeordnung (GewO).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notariell beglaubigte Anmeldung zum Handelsregister (notarially certified commercial register application) on the official Bavarian standard form for the incorporation of a UG (haftungsbeschränkt), accompanied by the notarial Musterprotokoll deed, the shareholder list, the managing director's acceptance and specimen signature, and the bank capital release confirmation under GmbHG § 8.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG permits a minimum nominal share capital of EUR 1 (GmbHG § 5a); the statutory notarial template commonly fixes EUR 1,000. Under GmbHG § 7(2), at least 25% of each cash contribution must be paid in before registration, and under § 5a(2) the subscribed capital must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director. The payment must be verified by a bank release confirmation (Bankbescheinigung) issued against a blocked paying-in account or notarial escrow account (Treuhand-/Anderkonto).

Funding & unlock path: No fire safety certificate for public occupancy is required because the registered office functions as a non-public business office or warehouse; landlord consent for non-residential use suffices for registration; structural load verification under the Bavarian Building Code is necessary only if high-rack storage systems are erected; occupational safety obligations, including workstation layout, lighting, electrical safety and fire extinguisher provision, follow the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) and the DGUV accident-prevention rules.

E-government filing portal

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account purposes. Germany has no general exchange controls, mandatory domestic-currency conversion rule, or prior approval requirement for ordinary inbound or outbound commercial payments, dividends, shareholder loans, capital contributions, or trade settlements. An UG may generally hold foreign-currency accounts and remit distributable profits to non-resident shareholders, subject to German corporate-law distributable-profit rules, annual financial statements, preservation of registered capital, tax clearance, withholding-tax requirements, treaty relief procedures, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner identification, and bank anti-money-laundering controls. Cross-border payment and asset-transfer reporting under the AWV may apply, subject to statutory exemptions, thresholds, filing deadlines, transaction classifications, and current Bundesbank reporting instructions. Dividend distributions also require sufficient distributable retained earnings and cannot impair the UG's statutory capital-reserve obligations, including the requirement to allocate one-quarter of annual surplus, reduced by loss carryforwards, to the legal reserve until the statutory capital threshold for a GmbH is reached.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or repatriated through a German bank. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or with foreign payment institutions, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, AML, payment-services, and foreign-trade reporting obligations. The UG must retain commercially adequate evidence for exports and marketplace settlements, including invoices, platform statements, shipment or delivery evidence, customs records where applicable, payment-provider reports, and bank reconciliations. AWV payment and asset reporting obligations, if triggered, are separate from any currency-conversion obligation and must be assessed against the current Bundesbank rules and applicable exemptions.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank. Banking supervision is primarily conducted by the European Central Bank and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin). Foreign-exchange reporting is principally administered by Deutsche Bundesbank under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, foreign-currency balances, marketplace settlement, and supplier payments, provided the provider is an EU-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution operating in Germany under passporting or a German authorization. A fintech EMI account is not a substitute for a German bank account for every corporate purpose and may not provide statutory deposit protection equivalent to a credit institution. It may be unsuitable as the sole account for UG share-capital funding, notarial incorporation evidence, tax payments, payroll, cash deposits, regulated payment activities, or transactions requiring a German IBAN accepted by a particular counterparty. Wise Europe SA and comparable providers must be verified for current authorization, permitted services, safeguarding model, country availability, account-holder name matching, transaction limits, and marketplace compatibility. Offshore digital EMIs without EU authorization, passporting, or an appropriately licensed German service provider present high account-freeze, licensing, safeguarding, and payment-finality risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (Körperschaftsteuer 15.0% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT, equivalent to 0.825 percentage points; municipal trade tax/Gewerbesteuer is additional and commonly brings the aggregate effective charge to approximately 30%–33%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% statutory German capital gains tax on dividends, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld tax, yielding a domestic combined rate of 26.375%. Treaty and EU participation relief may reduce or eliminate this rate for the non-resident corporate shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany does not impose an annual automatic filing threshold for a transfer pricing local file. Mandatory contemporaneous documentation arises on revenue-audit request under §90(3) AO, and administrative practice applies materiality relevance of approximately EUR 1,000,000 for cross-border goods transactions and EUR 100,000 for services transactions. A master file is typical for a multinational group with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million (CbCR threshold); below that level, a UG engaged with TikTok group affiliates should still prepare intercompany agreements, remuneration documentation, and arm's-length benchmark files.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply Economic Substance Regulations modeled on UAE or Caribbean regimes, but it applies substantive anti-abuse doctrines under §42 AO and the effective-management-seat rules under §§10–11 AO. The UG must have actual German substance: registered office, management decisions, employees, bank account, own contracts, and operating control over the TikTok Shop social commerce activity. A letterbox UG with no real economic activity will be re-characterized, denied treaty benefits, and may be vulnerable to the foreign shareholder being treated as having a permanent establishment in Germany.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unreported E-Commerce Income

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under §370 AO for tax evasion: fine or imprisonment up to five years; aggravated cases up to ten years. Civil tax assessment is recalculated with back-tax interest at 6% p.a. and late filing penalties up to EUR 25,000; late payment surcharges continue at 1% per month.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under §69 AO if corporate tax obligations are not met due to willful or grossly negligent behavior. In criminal proceedings, the director may face asset seizure, travel restrictions, arrest pending investigation, and, upon conviction, restitution and imprisonment. The corporate veil is insufficient protection where the director knowingly caused underreporting.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may disregard the UG as an abusive structure under §42 AO, attribute the e-commerce income to the foreign beneficial owner, impose corporate income tax and trade tax reassessment plus 6% interest, impose late-filing penalties, and deny treaty benefits. The entity may also be treated as having its place of management outside Germany, triggering investor penalties and mandatory deregistration orders.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability for the full amount of distributions made while the UG was insolvent or after the loss of capital, through GmbH law principles of Existenzvernichtungshaftung. Delayed insolvency filing by management is criminal under §15a InsO and can lead to imprisonment. Directors may also be named in tax liability assessments under §§34, 69 AO and ordered to pay unpaid corporate taxes directly.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax office may estimate income upward and apply a transfer pricing penalty under §162(3) AO: surcharge between 5% and 10% of the positive income adjustment. The adjustment may also be treated as a constructive dividend, triggering withholding tax at 26.375% plus solidarity surcharge, interest, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return and control the transfer pricing arrangements are personally liable for the resulting under-withheld tax and unpaid company liabilities if they failed to keep proper books and to disclose material facts to the tax adviser. Directors may be exposed to criminal liability for aiding tax evasion if false documentation is intentionally submitted.
AML / Payout Structuring

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German AML Act (GwG) of up to EUR 100,000 for negligent violations and up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious corporate violations. Intentional structuring to conceal taxable profits additionally constitutes money laundering or tax evasion under §§261 StGB and §370 AO, with imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: The AML compliance officer and managing director can be imposed fines or imprisonment; bank accounts and payment payouts may be seized; travel bans are commonly imposed during investigation. The director is also liable for damage caused to tax creditors and other creditors where funds are moved for evasion purposes.
VAT / Customs and Registration Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion is treated as tax evasion under §370 AO and carries up to five years' imprisonment, ten years in aggravated cases. In addition, the UG will be assessed for unpaid VAT plus interest (6% p.a.), late payment surcharges, and penalties up to EUR 25,000. Input VAT recovery on TikTok platform fees may be denied where invoices are missing.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under §§34, 69 AO for unpaid VAT when the failure to register and pay is due to intentional or grossly negligent conduct. Directors of an insolvent company are personally responsible for applying collected VAT to corporate operating expenses and may be prosecuted for preferential payment or breach of insolvency priority rules.
