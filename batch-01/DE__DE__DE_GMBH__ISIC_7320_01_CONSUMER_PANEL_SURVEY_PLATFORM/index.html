Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registergericht (Commercial Register Court) at the local Amtsgericht of the registered office; electronic filings are submitted exclusively by a certified notary through the centralized Handelsregisterportal (XJustiz/Unternehmensregister back-office).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung - GmbHG): §§ 1 (formation), 5 (minimum capital of EUR 25,000), 7 (registration application; at least half of cash capital deposited), 8 (application contents and bank confirmation), 10 (register entry and publication), 35 (managing directors), 39 (list of managing directors); supplemented by the Commercial Register Regulation (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRV) and by German Civil Code provisions applicable to contributions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized formation deed containing the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and the application for registration to the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister), executed as an Errichtungsprotokoll (establishment protocol) before a German notary.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 41 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum nominal share capital is EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5). Prior to registration, each cash contribution must be paid up to at least 50% (aggregate minimum EUR 12,500) into an escrow account (Sperrkonto) opened in the name of the company 'in formation' (GmbH i.G.) and held at the free disposal of the managing directors; in-kind contributions must be fully provided and substantiated in the application (GmbHG §§ 7(2), 7(3), 8(1)).

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy and fire-safety clearance for the registered office: use of an officially approved or grandfathered office building with valid fire-safety inspection (Brandverhütungsschau) under the applicable Landesbauordnung; compliance with the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) covering ventilation, escape routes, and first-aid equipment; mandatory registration with the employers' liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft, DGUV); and DGUV Vorschrift 3 electrical-equipment inspections completed before operating the consumer-panel data centers and office infrastructure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt, Saarlouis/Leipzig) for the EU VAT Identification Number (USt-IdNr.) and cross-border VAT refunds; the locally competent Finanzamt (e.g., the Finanzamt assigned to the registered seat or, for foreign non-resident applicants, the case-handling Finanzamt designated via the BZSt central intake) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and local VAT (Umsatzsteuer) assessments; all filings are mandated through the ELSTER online portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Within one month of commercial commencement, the GmbH must register with the responsible Finanzamt using the official 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) filed electronically via ELSTER; this simultaneously grants the Steuernummer (local tax number). Subsequently, the BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. upon application; for a foreign-owned GmbH the shareholders must additionally submit certified POS/register excerpts, FEIN/TIN equivalents, and proof of beneficial ownership. Foreign non-resident companies without a German establishment obtain VAT registration directly via the BZSt central gateway, which coordinates the substantive assessment with the competent Finanzamt (typically Finanzamt Neubrandenburg). A registered place of business in Germany (Geschäftsanschrift) and a German statutory managing director or authorized agent (Empfangsbevollmächtigter) are required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

GmbH is a separate corporate tax subject under Körperschaftsteuergesetz (KStG) and Gewerbesteuergesetz (GewStG), taxable at 15% corporation tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (effective 23–32% combined; ~30% typical); Germany offers no US-style check-the-box classification, but a GmbH may elect into an ertragsteuerliche and umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft (fiscal unity) with a German parent whereby the subsidiary's profits, trade tax base, and VAT liabilities are consolidated onto the parent; hybrid mismatch elections under the Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) implementation (§§ 4k EStG, 1 KStG-like rules) are permitted only to the extent they align the German result with the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive; a GmbH may also voluntarily waive VAT exemptions and elect regular taxation; thin-capitalization (Zinsschranke) restricts interest deductions to 30% of EBITDA unless the escape clauses apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for foreign suppliers and for the GmbH upon first taxable supply in Germany (no de-minimis threshold; registration is mandatory prior to commencing activity). For very small domestic sole proprietorships the §19 UStG small-business threshold applies (prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 25,000 from 2025 and projected current-year turnover ≤ EUR 100,000), but this exemption is generally unavailable or economically irrelevant for a GmbH. Intra-EU distance-sales and goods-acquisition registration triggers: EUR 10,000 remote-sales threshold (union-wide) and EUR 12,500 intra-Community acquisition threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export zero-rating is claimable under §6 UStG for goods physically exported to third-country territories (Ausfuhrlieferung) and under §6a UStG for intra-Community supplies, provided the supplier retains evidence of transport/removal from the EU and the acquirer's valid EU VAT ID (for IC supplies). For cross-border B2B services, market research platform subscriptions and data-services are taxable at the customer's place of establishment under §3a(2) UStG, thus excluded from German VAT entirely (reverse-charged abroad) once the customer's business status and jurisdiction are evidenced; no German export VAT formality is then required. If the survey platform sells digital reports or panel access to German private consumers, German VAT at 19% applies. Claiming zero-rating requires the supplier to act in good faith with complete delivery documentation; export of promotional goods (vouchers, samples) embedded in the survey operation must be separately evidenced by customs declarations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/ATLAS with MRN) and/or Export Accompanying Document for physical exports; for services, signed contract and service completion certificate specifying the foreign recipient and place of supply.
  • Bill of Lading / CMR waybill / IATA air waybill / courier pickup record evidencing physical removal from the EU customs territory.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank-account statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the non-resident customer (FX repatriation traceability).
  • Written declaration of the foreign customer's business status (Unternehmereigenschaft) and VAT ID, plus the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) filing confirmation for intra-Community supplies.
  • Commercial invoice and order documentation with Incoterms and payment terms.
  • For panel-incentive shipments: customs value declaration and CPC 10 00 00 evidence that goods leave the EU.

Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies is phased in: from 1 January 2025, all German businesses must be able to receive e-invoices in a machine-readable format (EN 16931, XRechnung, or ZUGFeRD); issuers may continue to issue paper/PDF invoices until 31 December 2026 (and until 31 December 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 800,000), after which electronic issuance becomes compulsory. Germany has obtained a Council Implementing Decision derogation to introduce mandatory transaction-based VAT digital reporting (VDA – Verkehrsdatenerfassung) for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2027, requiring near-real-time clearance submission to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern via an API; no national fiscal stamping or continuous cryptographic clearance (akin to Italy's SDI) is currently mandated, but the 2027 reporting will require automated transmission of invoice data fields. Foreign e-invoices received by the GmbH must meet the same EN 16931 requirements to preserve input VAT deduction.

German customs and tax authorities (Hauptzollamt, Finanzamt) audit export zero-rating rigorously; missing or inconsistent transport documents trigger retrospective VAT clawback plus default interest at 0.5% per month (§233a AO) from the original due date, revocation of input VAT recovery, and 6% annual late-payment surcharge. Fraudulent zero-rating or false evidence constitutes tax evasion (§370 AO) and customs VAT fraud, punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 500,000 per count; corporate directors are personally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO and GmbH law for wrongful VAT refunds; repeat or organized violations attract criminal asset seizure and publication of penalties. Additionally, failure to remit reverse-charge VAT on services imported into Germany is treated as a personal liability of the managing director (Geschäftsführerhaftung) with enforcement against the corporate and personal assets.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies broad current-account and capital-account freedom of movement within the European Union and the euro area. The euro is fully convertible for ordinary commercial, investment, financing, dividend, royalty, and service-payment purposes, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, payment-transparency requirements, tax rules, and reporting obligations. A German GmbH may generally receive foreign capital, hold foreign-currency accounts, convert currencies, make cross-border payments, and remit distributable profits to non-resident shareholders without a general exchange-control approval. Profit distributions remain subject to German corporate-law distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax treatment, applicable double-tax treaty relief, beneficial-owner requirements, and evidence supporting treaty entitlement. Banks may require transaction-purpose evidence, shareholder and beneficial-owner documentation, tax forms, and sanctions screening before executing material payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds, surrender foreign-currency receipts, convert export proceeds into euros, or complete conversion within a prescribed period. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through payment institutions and electronic-money institutions, subject to contractual, AML, sanctions, accounting, and tax controls. Cross-border payment reporting may apply under Deutsche Bundesbank foreign-trade statistics rules, including reporting of qualifying cross-border payments or claims and liabilities when applicable thresholds, exemptions, and filing conditions are met. The company should confirm current reporting thresholds, filing channels, and exemptions with Deutsche Bundesbank or its banking provider before implementation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Relevant foreign-exchange reporting rules are principally administered under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung), and applicable European Union law.
