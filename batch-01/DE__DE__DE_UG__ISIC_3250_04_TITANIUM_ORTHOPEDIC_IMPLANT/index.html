Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; local tax office (Finanzamt) responsible for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment. Electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of a UG (haftungsbeschränkt) in the German commercial register, the entity must submit a registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically to the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) for income tax and VAT assessment. For cross-border transactions, the UG must apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using the official application form. The application must state the business purpose, expected turnover, and place of establishment. The UG, as an entity-level taxpayer, is obliged to register from commencement of business activity regardless of turnover where it is established domestically.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After incorporation of a UG (haftungsbeschränkt) in the German commercial register, the entity must submit a registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically to the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) for income tax and VAT assessment. For cross-border transactions, the UG must apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using the official application form. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German tax law. A UG is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) including solidarity surcharge and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available elections relevant to indirect tax and tax grouping: (i) option to waive VAT exemption under the small-entrepreneur rule (Kleinunternehmer) per Section 19 UStG; (ii) formation of a VAT and corporate income tax fiscal unit (Organschaft) with a domestic controlling entity; (iii) elective annual accounting basis for VAT; (iv) no pass-through classification is permitted for a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident small businesses: exemption threshold of €22,000 prior-year turnover and €50,000 projected current-year turnover (Section 19 UStG). For non-resident and non-established entrepreneurs making taxable supplies in Germany, there is no turnover threshold: VAT registration is compulsory regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Section 4 No. 1 and Section 6 UStG to supplies of goods dispatched or transported from Germany to a destination outside the European Union (export supplies). For intra-EU supplies to businesses with a valid VAT identification number, the tax-free treatment as an intra-community supply requires (i) the acquirer to be an entrepreneur acting as such, (ii) the goods to physically leave Germany to another EU Member State, and (iii) the supplier to hold the acquirer's valid VAT ID and submit a recapitulative statement (EC Sales List). Offshore services directly connected to the orthopedic implant goods may also qualify for zero-rating if performed for export. For supplies of services to non-business customers outside the EU, the place of supply rule generally moves the supply outside German VAT, substituting zero-rating for a non-taxable transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS / Export Control System confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from Germany
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of payment
  • Intra-Community Supply evidence: customer VAT identification number, EC Sales List, and transport documentation

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. However, since 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers, including UGs engaged in orthopedic implant manufacturing and sales, must be able to receive electronic invoices (E-Rechnungen) conforming to the European standard EN 16931. Structured e-invoice formats such as XRechnung and ZUGFeRD are recognized. Issuance of e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover above €800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all other businesses. Export invoices may still be issued in other formats, but the data submitted via ELSTER must reconcile with the VAT return.

The tax authority applies strict documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. If the export evidence is missing, incomplete, or inconsistent, the zero-rating is retrospectively denied and VAT is assessed at 19% plus statutory late-payment interest of 0.5% per full month of underpayment. Knowingly false export documentation constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary fine. Repeated violations can lead to imprisonment up to ten years in aggravated cases, confiscation of goods, and denial of future export zero-rating privileges. Additionally, clawback penalties may include a penalty of 5% to 10% of the evaded tax amount under Section 378 AO and full invalidation of VAT refund claims.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible currency regime under the Foreign Exchange Act (AuWGe). Both capital and current accounts are liberalized with no prior approval requirements for most transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, and royalty payments face no convertibility restrictions; however, all FX flows are subject to standard tax withholding (e.g., 25% on dividends) and German tax reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender to the central bank or fixed conversion ratio applies. Exporters must furnish proof of export (commercial invoice, packing list, transport documents) to their bank prior to FX conversion. Foreign exchange must be surrendered to a German bank within 10 business days for commercial transactions. Banks report FX transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, producing a combined federal burden of 15.825%. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and depends on the municipality; its effective rate is generally calculated using the 3.5% federal base rate multiplied by the municipal assessment factor.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate rate of 26.375%, before any applicable church tax. A reduced rate or refund may be available under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if its conditions, beneficial-ownership requirements, and anti-abuse rules are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally, returns are due seven months after the end of the tax year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the general statutory extended deadline is 14 months after year-end, subject to statutory exceptions and any applicable administrative extensions. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year; the adviser deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, adjusted where it falls on a weekend or public holiday.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany has no general monetary threshold below which cross-border related-party transactions are exempt from the arm's-length principle or all documentation duties. Under the German transfer-pricing documentation rules, a Local File is generally required where annual cross-border related-party transaction volume exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group whose consolidated revenue is at least EUR 100 million. Special documentation and record-keeping requirements may apply irrespective of these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a general regime formally called Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). German tax law nevertheless requires genuine commercial arrangements to be respected, permits recharacterization or denial of arrangements lacking economic substance in applicable circumstances, and applies transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, controlled foreign company, and treaty anti-abuse rules. The UG should maintain evidence of decision-making, personnel, premises, functions, assets, risks, and commercial rationale proportionate to its activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code may be punished by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Back taxes, interest, and tax surcharges may also be assessed.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability and personal liability for tax losses caused by culpable breach of their tax duties under applicable law. Imprisonment and fines are possible; travel restrictions are not an automatic statutory tax penalty.
Failure to remit payroll or withholding taxes

Statutory sanction: Tax arrears, late-payment surcharges, interest, enforcement, and potential tax-evasion or tax-misdemeanour proceedings may apply, depending on intent and circumstances.

Director personal liability: A managing director may be personally liable for tax claims where the statutory conditions for culpable breach of tax duties are met and may face criminal or administrative proceedings.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Taxable income may be adjusted. Failure to submit required documentation can trigger statutory surcharges, generally subject to a minimum of EUR 5,000 and, in specified cases, percentage-based amounts linked to the income adjustment, with statutory caps and minimums depending on the breach.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability or sanctions where their own culpable conduct satisfies the relevant statutory requirements. A transfer-pricing adjustment does not by itself pierce the corporate veil.
Insolvency delay and prohibited payments

Statutory sanction: Under the Insolvency Code, an insolvency application must generally be filed without undue delay and no later than three weeks after illiquidity or six weeks after over-indebtedness. Intentional delay may be punishable by imprisonment of up to three years or a fine; negligent delay may be punishable by imprisonment of up to one year or a fine. Civil repayment and damages claims may also arise.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal civil liability for prohibited payments and may face criminal liability for insolvency delay. A travel ban is not an automatic consequence.
Money laundering, sanctions, or prohibited trade

Statutory sanction: Sanctions depend on the offense and governing statute and may include substantial fines, confiscation, business restrictions, and imprisonment. German money-laundering offenses may carry imprisonment or fines; sanctions and export-control violations can attract severe criminal and administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible personnel may face personal criminal or administrative liability for their own conduct, consent, or culpable supervisory failures. Corporate liability and confiscation may also apply; travel restrictions are case-specific and not automatic.
Medical-device regulatory breach

Statutory sanction: Under the EU Medical Device Regulation and German implementing law, authorities may order corrective action, restrict or prohibit marketing, withdraw or recall products, and impose applicable administrative fines or criminal penalties for specified violations.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability where statutory elements, personal participation, or culpable supervisory failures are established. Product liability, regulatory enforcement, and corporate liability are distinct from veil piercing.
