Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office – and the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office). Statutory portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for registration, return filing and tax-number management; BZSt Online-Portal (www.bzst.de) for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and EU exchange requests; ATLAS/Zoll-Portal for EORI registration and customs declarations. The Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) is the superior fiscal authority.

E-government filing portal

A freshly incorporated UG must complete the following statutory sequence: (1) notarial formation and entry into the Handelsregister (commercial register); (2) electronic filing of the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the local Finanzamt for Steuernummer (corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment number); (3) separate application through BZSt Online for the USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) using the 'Grenzüberschreitender Leistungsbezug' procedure; (4) EORI number registration in the German customs system (ATLAS) for exports. Foreign shareholders/entities may own 100% of the UG; the registration must be lodged before commencement of business (typically 1–8 weeks for Steuernummer, 4–6 weeks for USt-IdNr.). Failure to apply within the statutory notice period can trigger penalty surcharges and a forced assessment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a legal entity under German law taxed as a Kapitalgesellschaft irrespective of the number or residence of shareholders: subject to 15% Körperschaftsteuer, 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate tax (0.825% effective) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, base rate 3.5% multiplied by a municipal Hebesatz of typically 200%–490%, effective circa 7%–17%), producing an aggregate profit-tax burden of about 30%–33%. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a legal entity under German law taxed as a Kapitalgesellschaft irrespective of the number or residence of shareholders: subject to 15% Körperschaftsteuer, 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate tax (0.825% effective) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, base rate 3.5% multiplied by a municipal Hebesatz of typically 200%–490%, effective circa 7%–17%), producing an aggregate profit-tax burden of about 30%–33%. It has unlimited (worldwide) tax liability as a German resident entity; the low minimum-capital UG status does not alter the entity-level classification and there is no territorial- or tax-exempt-zone treatment.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box or elective transparent treatment. Available statutory elections affecting tax identity: (a) Organschaft (corporate/trade-tax fiscal unity) under §§ 14–19 KStG/GewStG and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG – binding joint application by parent and subsidiary; (b) option to waive VAT exemption for real-estate supplies under § 9 UStG; (c) small-undertaking (Kleinunternehmer) election under § 19 UStG up to EUR 25,000 turnover – practically irrelevant for a high-voltage contracting UG; (d) cash-basis VAT accounting (Ist-Versteuerung) under § 20 UStG for specified low-turnover or contractual situations; (e) reverse-charge allocation for § 13b UStG supplies. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are statutory (ATAD implementation via §§ 4k EStG, §§ 8b, 14 KStG) and not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small entrepreneurs: EUR 25,000 annual gross turnover (Kleinunternehmer threshold under § 19 UStG, effective 1 January 2025; previously EUR 22,000). Non-resident/foreign entrepreneurs: EUR 0 economic threshold – any taxable supply performed or deemed performed in Germany, including installation services on German immovable property, and any receipt of § 13b reverse-charge services from abroad, requires Finanzamt registration and a German VAT number immediately regardless of turnover. EU cross-border distance-selling/OSS threshold: EUR 10,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Physical export supplies to third countries: exempt with credit under § 4 Nr. 1a and § 6 UStG where the supplier proves, by Beleg- und Buchnachweis (per §§ 8–10 UStDV), that the goods physically left the EU within the statutory evidence window and the Incoterms (EXW/FOB/CIF/CPT) are consistent with export. (2) Intra-Community supplies: exempt under § 4 Nr. 1b and § 6a UStG if the customer holds a valid EU VAT ID, the VAT-ID is validated, goods move to another Member State, EC Sales List and Intrastat are filed. (3) Services: B2B services are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Germany. For a UG installing 500kV gas-insulated switchgear: installation services on real property (fixed substations) follow the situs of the land under § 3a Abs. 2 Nr. 1 UStG – German-located substations are always charged at 19% German VAT (even to foreign project owners, subject to reverse-charge only if supplier is non-resident); substations constructed outside Germany are outside German VAT scope and generally reverse-charged in the project country. Turnkey contracts combining export equipment and installation may qualify as a Werklieferung (goods supply with installation) under § 3 Abs. 4 UStG if the materials/components dominate, or as a construction service under § 3 Abs. 11 UStG; the tax character must be documented project-by-project. Germany does not impose a statutory 180-day export-related FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects commercial milestone-billing and letter-of-credit practice, not a German VAT substantive rule – export evidence and consideration receipts must be retained for 10 years.

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, national electronic invoicing clearinghouse, API stamping or digital withholding mechanism. Mandatory structured e-invoicing is phased under the E-Rechnung-Verordnung and Wachstumschancengesetz: (i) B2G supplies to federal authorities: XRechnung (EN 16931 / UBL / Zugferd 2.x) mandatory since 27 November 2020; (ii) B2B: every taxable person must be able to receive a structured e-invoice from 1 January 2025; (iii) B2B issuance of structured e-invoices becomes mandatory from 1 January 2026 for providers with pre-year taxable turnover above EUR 800,000 and for all taxable persons from 1 January 2027. Invoices issued by non-established suppliers to German recipients do not require German clearance; export supplies are cleared electronically via the EU customs ATLAS system.

German tax audit (Betriebsprüfung) and criminal tax investigation (Steuerfahndung) rigorously test export evidence; the burden of proof lies on the UG. Findings of false, forged or incomplete export documentation trigger: (i) retroactive VAT assessment at 19% on the full net consideration plus statutory arrear interest under § 233a AO at 0.15% per month (1.8% p.a.) and late-payment surcharges; (ii) administrative understatement surcharge under § 26a UStG up to EUR 25,000 and fines for negligent understatement up to EUR 50,000; (iii) criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO with imprisonment from 6 months up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases), confiscation/forfeiture, and denial of the zero-rating benefit for all comparable transactions in the same audit period; (iv) joint and several liability under § 25d UStG for incorrect invoicing; (v) extended assessment periods – 4 years for regular, 5 years for minor negligence, and up to 10 years for fraudulent concealment – with 10-year retention of books, records and export evidence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full current- and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and Sections 1-4 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). No central bank approval, prior exchange-control license, or remittance tax clearance applies to dividends, loan repayments, shareholder loans, or inbound equity. The EUR is freely floating and globally settled in CLS. Residual obligations: (1) mandatory balance-of-payments reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for every payment or transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 (or FX equivalent) within five business days; (2) standard bank-level sanctions and UBO screening; (3) BMWK investment screening only when a non-EU investor acquires control of critical infrastructure, which treasury flows do not trigger; and (4) standard German withholding tax of 26.375% on dividends (reduced to 0-15% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or double-tax treaties) and, for profit-participating loans (partiarisches Darlehen), withholding at 25% plus solidarity surcharge. None of these block capital injections or trade settlements for an ISIC 4321 electrical contractor.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation timeline exist in Germany. Export revenues may remain in EUR or in any foreign currency indefinitely. The only statutory duty is statistical: any cross-border payment or value transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV within five business days using the institution's electronic reporting interface (BAKIS or the bank's integrated statistical service). The UG may hold, invest, or convert export receipts at market rates without approval.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank in coordination with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK); foreign payment statistics are administered by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational treasury (FX conversion, supplier disbursements, secondary transaction sub-accounts) and Low/Not-permitted for regulated capital escrow or documentary letter-of-credit issuance. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and shareholder loans are routed via SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's external account into the UG's designated EUR account at a German Tier-1 bank. The payment reference must identify the nature: 'EINLAGE STAMMKAPITAL' for paid-in capital, 'GESELLSCHAFTERDARLEHEN' for shareholder debt, or 'EPC-MILESTONE-01 [project name]' for contract progress payments. For euro-denominated flows, settlement occurs same-day through the T2/TARGET2 real-time gross settlement system; for third-country transfers, MT103 must include field 33B (currency and amount), field 59 (UG as beneficiary with full name, address, and IBAN/BIC), and field 70 (purpose reference), with charges instruction 'OUR' to preserve net receipts. Before crediting, the receiving bank performs sanctions and embargo screening against EU, UN, and OFAC lists. Every inbound payment from abroad exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days via the AWV statistical reporting portal, either directly by the UG or through the bank's integrated reporting system; underlying invoices and SWIFT confirmation records must be retained for audit. No exchange-control approval is required, although BAFA authorization may be triggered separately if the installation project simultaneously involves export of dual-use or controlled switchgear components.The UG's clearing rails comprise: (1) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (EBA CLEARING RT1) for sub-EUR 100,000 operational payments within the eurozone; (2) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for all non-SEPA supplier and subcontractor payments, with the corresponding AWV report where the amount exceeds EUR 12,500; (3) documentary Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the UG's German bank via SWIFT MT700 (issue), MT707 (amendment), and MT720/MT740 (transfer), covering transformers, gas-insulated switchgear, and high-voltage busbar equipment; (4) documentary collections under URC 522 using MT400/MT412 where the counterparty does not require a confirmation L/C; (5) T2/TARGET2 settlement for large EUR disbursements that must settle in central bank money; and (6) CLS payment-versus-payment settlement for eligible FX legs such as EUR/USD to eliminate settlement risk. Third-country outbound transfers require an ATLAS export declaration when goods cross the EU border, a captured invoice, and the same EUR 12,500 AWV statistical obligation. The UG should embed project references in every MT103 to preserve an immaculate audit trail across milestone collections and supplier disbursements.EUR/USD Deliverable FX Forwards and FX Swaps under an ISDA Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex in place with Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, or HypoVereinsbank; EUR/USD is fully convertible, so settlement is deliverable.EUR/UGX Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) where project margins are denominated in Ugandan Shillings; because UGX is not freely convertible, NDF contracts settling in EUR in Frankfurt are the standard, compliant hedging modality.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) for converting long-dated supplier financing or advance-payment obligations into EUR at a fixed rate.Zero-Cost FX Collars and European-style FX Options (on a premium-paid or collared basis) for tender-phase cash-flow protection, documented against identified milestone exposure and classified as cash-flow hedges under IFRS 9.FX Forward combinations in EUR/CHF, EUR/GBP, and EUR/USD only for genuine trade payables or receivables; uncovered speculative trading is not permitted for a non-financial UG under German commercial banking practice and MiFID II client categorization rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for operational treasury (FX conversion, supplier disbursements, secondary transaction sub-accounts) and Low/Not-permitted for regulated capital escrow or documentary letter-of-credit issuance. Legal constraints: EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Pliant) operate under PSD2 with a BaFin or passported EEA license and may legally hold the UG's funds in segregated e-money accounts; however, they are not 'credit institutions' under Section 32 of the German Banking Act (KWG), cannot issue or confirm onshore letters of credit or bank guarantees, cannot hold the UG's statutory capital for segregation purposes, and their pooled-account clearing through partner banks complicates the UG's AWV reportability thresholds. Offshore/non-EEA EMIs without a German branch license or EU passport cannot provide payment accounts to a German UG; they are restricted to ancillary, clearly labelled flows and must never be used for L/C-covered settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

German corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) is 15%, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporation tax, producing a 15.825% federal corporate-tax burden. A UG carrying on commercial business is also generally subject to municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer): the federal base rate is 3.5% multiplied by the municipality's assessment factor (Hebesatz), so the combined burden varies by municipality. For an incorporated company, the trade-tax allowance applicable to individuals and partnerships does not apply.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate on dividends is generally 25% under section 43a EStG, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing a combined rate of 26.375%, before any church tax or relief. Treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief may reduce or eliminate withholding if statutory conditions, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse rules, and procedural requirements are met. A refund or exemption is not automatic.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, an unadvised return is generally due by 31 July of the following year. If prepared by a qualifying tax adviser, the ordinary extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions and any applicable extension or special deadline. For a non-calendar fiscal year, the deadline runs from fiscal year-end under the same statutory framework. Assessed corporation-tax and trade-tax prepayments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under section 90(3) AO and the Foreign Tax Audit Ordinance (GAufzV), contemporaneous transaction documentation is generally required for cross-border related-party transactions where the taxpayer's annual consideration exceeds EUR 6,000,000 for deliveries of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions. The Master File obligation generally applies to a qualifying multinational group where consolidated revenue reaches at least EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year. Documentation must be produced within the statutory period following a request; special rules apply during tax audits and for extraordinary business transactions.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. German tax consequences nevertheless depend on actual functions, assets, risks, management, and control; transfer-pricing rules require arm's-length outcomes, and treaty and EU anti-abuse rules can deny benefits in artificial or non-genuine arrangements. A German UG must maintain actual corporate governance, accounting, and operational records consistent with its claimed activities and tax position.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion or intentional tax understatement

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Reckless tax understatement under section 378 AO is an administrative offense punishable by a fine. Unpaid tax, interest, and late-payment surcharges may also be assessed.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal or administrative liability based on their own conduct, and civil liability for tax obligations may arise under sections 34 and 69 AO where statutory duties are culpably breached. Imprisonment is available for criminal offenses. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty; enforcement measures require a separate legal basis.
Failure to remit payroll, dividend, royalty, or construction withholding tax

Statutory sanction: The UG may be assessed the unwithheld tax, interest, and applicable surcharges. Intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion; negligent or reckless conduct may trigger administrative penalties under the applicable tax provisions.

Director personal liability: A managing director responsible for withholding and remittance may incur personal liability under sections 34 and 69 AO if the statutory conditions, including culpable breach, are met. Criminal liability depends on personal intent or other applicable offense elements.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income and impose documentation-related surcharges under section 162(4) AO, including statutory minimums and maximums; an estimation of the tax base may be made where records are deficient. Additional tax, interest, and late-payment charges may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal tax or criminal liability only where the applicable statutory requirements are met, including culpable duty breach or personal participation in an offense; a transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create personal liability.
Insolvency-related payments and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: The managing director must file for insolvency without undue delay and, under section 15a InsO, no later than three weeks after illiquidity or six weeks after over-indebtedness, where applicable. Breach may result in criminal penalties, civil damages, repayment claims, and insolvency avoidance. Section 15b InsO restricts payments after insolvency maturity and may impose reimbursement liability.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal civil liability for prohibited payments, criminal liability for insolvency offenses, and repayment or avoidance claims. A travel ban is not an automatic consequence; any restriction requires separate legal authority.
Shell, nominee, or sham arrangements and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Benefits may be denied under applicable treaty principal-purpose tests, domestic anti-abuse rules including section 42 AO, or section 50d(3) EStG where applicable. Reassessment, withholding-tax recovery, interest, and penalties may result; criminal sanctions require proof of a relevant offense.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own false statements, culpable tax-duty breaches, or participation in an offense. There is no automatic veil piercing, imprisonment, or travel ban solely because an entity lacks substance.
Money laundering and suspicious transaction controls

Statutory sanction: Criminal money laundering under section 261 StGB may be punishable by a fine or imprisonment, with increased penalties in specified cases. Geldwäschegesetz administrative breaches may attract statutory fines. Applicable obligations depend on whether the UG falls within a regulated or otherwise covered category.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal or administrative liability depends on the director's own conduct, role, and applicable statutory duties. Corporate liability or regulatory fines may also apply where statutory attribution requirements are satisfied; travel restrictions are not an automatic sanction.
Unlicensed electrical installation or regulated craft activity

Statutory sanction: Electrical installation activity may fall within regulated craft requirements under the Handwerksordnung, including registration in the relevant register and qualifying technical management. Unauthorized practice may lead to prohibition orders and statutory administrative fines; project, safety, and contractual liabilities may apply independently.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability where they knowingly direct unlawful activity or breach applicable safety or regulatory duties. Tax filing does not cure licensing non-compliance; imprisonment or travel bans require a separate statutory basis.
