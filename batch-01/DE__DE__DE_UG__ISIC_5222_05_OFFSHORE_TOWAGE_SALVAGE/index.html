Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG is formed with no statutory minimum capital; nominal share capital must be at least EUR 1 and each cash contribution must be fully paid up before filing into a German bank account opened in the future company name. A state escrow account is not mandatory; an enforceable receivable is not sufficient unless all contributions are fully covered and payable, and the notary verifies the payment proof. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profit in a statutory reserve until the reserve reaches 25% of the registered share capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-protection acceptance by the local Feuerwehr for offices, workshops, and mooring facilities; structural load and building clearance by the municipal Bauaufsicht for any fixed salvage platform or shore shed; crew and managers must hold STCW certificates, seafarer medical fitness certificates, and BG Verkehr occupational-safety clearances; vessels require fire safety and damage-stability plans certified by the recognised classification society; a civil-defense/emergency communication plan must be filed with the Havariekommando and the competent Arbeitsmedizin/Gewerbeaufsicht office.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern - BZSt) and local Finanzamt; official portals are ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online (www.bzst.de); Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance) is the supervisory authority.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity or a German UG, the procedure begins with filing the statutory economic questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt, which assigns the domestic economic tax number (Wirtschaftssteuernummer); for EU VAT identification purposes, the BZSt issues the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-IdNr.) after the Finanzamt's recommendation. A non-resident supplier with no German establishment must file the VAT registration form before the first taxable transaction, providing its foreign tax identifier, place of business, and projected German turnover; processing is via ELSTER or a qualified tax representative; the USt-IdNr. is mandatory for issuing valid invoices and for intra-Community statements; timing typically ranges from two to six weeks, and non-compliance exposes the entity to registration back-charges.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German capital company automatically subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge; it cannot elect partnership or pass-through classification. Available elections include forming an Organschaft (fiscal unity) with a controlling corporate shareholder to consolidate VAT, corporate income tax, and trade tax for the controlled UG; election under Section 19 UStG for small-business VAT exemption where qualitatively thresholds are met; and no hybrid-mismatch elective planning is permitted because the German ATAD anti-hybrid rules in Section 4k EStG and Section 12a KStG are mandatory and non-elective, requiring definitive income adjustments to be evidenced contemporaneously.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 taxable turnover in the preceding calendar year and EUR 50,000 projected in the current year (small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG); above this threshold registration is mandatory. A foreign business without a German establishment has no de minimis threshold for supplies made in or into Germany that are fiscally attributed to German territory; for such suppliers VAT registration is required before the first transaction irrespective of turnover amount, with the monthly prepayment regime and annual return obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Section 4 No. 2 UStG applies to services supplied for oceangoing seagoing vessels engaged in international traffic, including deep-sea towage and salvage operations, provided the service is directly connected to the vessel or its cargo and performed for an entrepreneur as defined by German VAT rules; if the tug or salvage vessel itself is an oceangoing commercial vessel, its supply, repair, charter, and auxiliary services qualify for zero rating. For general exports, the zero rate applies under Section 6 UStG where goods are actually transported to a non-EU territory; with respect to offshore towage/salvage, the zero-rate must be supported by voyage documentation demonstrating departure from German tax territory and arrival at a third-country vessel, an offshore installation outside the German customs territory, or a high-seas destination. Services to fixed continental-shelf installations are not zero-rated and remain subject to German VAT at the standard rate unless explicitly exempt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhr-/summary declaration processed through ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing the voyage from the EU customs territory to a third country or an oceangoing vessel in international traffic
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation proving receipt of the export/salvage proceeds within 180 days

Germany imposes a mandatory B2B e-invoicing requirement from 1 January 2025, with specific sanctions and acceptance duties phased from 1 January 2026 and mandatory structured transmission from 1 January 2028. The e-invoice must comply with EN 16931, with formats such as XInvoice or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X accepted; there is no real-time fiscal clearance procedure, no prior-authorisation API, and no digital stamping in Germany, but all tax-relevant invoice data must be output in a structured electronic format, transmitted through a declared channel (e.g., PEPPOL, email EDI, or supplier portal), and archived unaltered for ten years. Customs declarations are processed via the ATLAS electronic system for import and export, and the invoice should separately state the German VAT ID and the customer's VAT ID for intra-Community supplies.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO, carrying imprisonment up to ten years and fines up to EUR 500,000 for deliberate fraud; in aggravated commercial cases the imprisonment minimum is two years. The German tax authorities may retrospectively reverse the zero-rating and issue a VAT assessment for the statutory limitation period of up to ten years in case of concealment; statutory late-payment interest accrues at 0.5% per month (Section 233a AO). Section 14c UStG exposes the supplier to liability for VAT shown on an invoice where zero-rating was incorrectly claimed, and Section 26a UStG provides administrative penalties up to EUR 5,000 per documentary violation. The BZSt, Finanzamt, and customs authorities may conduct joint audits, and the burden of proof is on the exporter to maintain a full, uninterrupted, and authentic evidence chain including contracts, bills of lading, customs declarations, and bank settlement records; non-compliance leads to immediate clawback of unpaid VAT plus accrued interest and criminal referral in fraud cases.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility within the euro area and generally permits unrestricted foreign-exchange transactions, inbound investment, dividend distributions, loan repayments, and repatriation of sale proceeds. The euro is freely convertible and no general German foreign-exchange approval is required for ordinary commercial payments. Practical hurdles include bank anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening, beneficial-owner verification, source-of-funds evidence, German foreign-trade restrictions, EU sanctions, transfer-pricing support, corporate-law restrictions on unlawful return of capital, and tax compliance. Dividends paid by a German UG are generally subject to German withholding tax, normally 25% plus solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, exemption procedures, and anti-abuse rules. Distributions also require distributable profits, proper financial statements, preservation of the UG statutory reserve, and compliance with insolvency and capital-maintenance rules. Cross-border payments may trigger Bundesbank International Transactions Reporting requirements under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, including statistical reporting thresholds and exemptions applicable to payment-service providers or banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal deadline requiring a German company to convert export receipts into euros or repatriate them to Germany. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, customs, anti-money-laundering, and transaction-reporting obligations. Banks may impose contractual settlement, documentation, or account-crediting requirements. Payment statistics reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank may apply to cross-border receipts or payments above the applicable reporting threshold and must be submitted through the prescribed electronic reporting process by the relevant reporting deadline. Export documentation, customs declarations, transport evidence, invoices, contracts, and proof of service completion should be retained for tax and audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, subject to applicable supervision and reporting requirements administered by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), the Federal Central Tax Office, customs authorities, and the German Financial Intelligence Unit where relevant.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for an EU or EEA-licensed electronic money institution or payment institution with valid German passporting rights and demonstrable SEPA, SWIFT-access, safeguarding, and foreign-currency capabilities. Low for a non-EEA offshore digital EMI without an EU-authorised entity, German branch, passported services, correspondent-bank support, or acceptable regulatory transparency. An EMI account is not a German bank deposit and normally cannot be used as a substitute for a bank account required for UG share-capital verification. Safeguarded customer funds, account segregation, payment limits, restricted correspondent access, delayed compliance reviews, non-SEPA currencies, sanctions screening, and termination risk must be addressed. The provider must verify the UG, directors, beneficial owners, business purpose, maritime counterparties, source of funds, expected transaction volumes, countries, vessels, and payment flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share capital and shareholder funding through an account held in the UG's legal name at a regulated German bank or compliant EU payment institution, using the bank's prescribed capital-contribution or shareholder-loan payment reference. Before transfer, provide the bank with the notarised formation documents, provisional commercial-register information, shareholder list, beneficial-owner evidence, subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth documents, and expected transaction profile. For share capital, obtain the bank confirmation required by the notary and commercial-register filing process; the bank should not treat an unverified third-party transfer as the UG's capital contribution. For shareholder loans, identify the lender, principal, interest, maturity, arm's-length terms, transfer-pricing position, and repayment restrictions. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments where available and SWIFT MT103 or equivalent cross-border payment messaging for non-SEPA currencies. Include complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, ultimate-beneficiary, purpose, invoice or agreement reference, and LEI or registration details where requested. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for an EU or EEA-licensed electronic money institution or payment institution with valid German passporting rights and demonstrable SEPA, SWIFT-access, safeguarding, and foreign-currency capabilities. Low for a non-EEA offshore digital EMI without an EU-authorised entity, German branch, passported services, correspondent-bank support, or acceptable regulatory transparency. An EMI account is not a German bank deposit and normally cannot be used as a substitute for a bank account required for UG share-capital verification. Safeguarded customer funds, account segregation, payment limits, restricted correspondent access, delayed compliance reviews, non-SEPA currencies, sanctions screening, and termination risk must be addressed. The provider must verify the UG, directors, beneficial owners, business purpose, maritime counterparties, source of funds, expected transaction volumes, countries, vessels, and payment flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €500,000 annual turnover threshold for mandatory local file transfer pricing documentation (Master File required for groups exceeding €20 million in consolidated revenue)

Economic substance scope: Germany enforces economic substance requirements pursuant to the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD); qualifying entities must demonstrate sufficient employees, premises, and core income-generating activities in Germany to justify tax residency and avoid recharacterization

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: fines up to 10 times the evaded tax or imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil restitution of evaded amounts

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment and disqualification from managing companies
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Potential loss of German tax residency status; penalties up to €1 million for non-compliance with substance requirements

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil; directors personally liable for tax liabilities and may face travel bans or asset freezes
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million; potential adjustments to taxable income by tax authorities

Director personal liability: Personal responsibility for ensuring compliance; failure may result in personal fines or imprisonment for willful misconduct
AML and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €500,000 or more; criminal prosecution under money laundering laws

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for compliance failures; potential imprisonment and professional bans
