Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be incorporated with stated share capital below EUR 25,000, including EUR 1, provided the capital is fully paid in cash before Commercial Register registration. Contributions in kind are excluded at formation. At least 25% of annual surplus, after loss carry-forwards, must be retained as statutory reserve until capital reaches EUR 25,000. German law does not require a statutory escrow account; the managing director must verify payment into the company's business account or otherwise document availability. The amount shown is zero because Germany has no fixed statutory filing fee denominated in USD; court and notarial fees are charged in EUR under the German Court and Notarial Costs Act (GNotKG).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent tax administration is the relevant local Finanzamt, coordinated federally by the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt). Tax registration and electronic tax filings are generally processed through the ELSTER portal. The BZSt issues German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.).

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated UG must complete commercial registration, obtain a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, and submit it to the Finanzamt responsible for the registered office. The Finanzamt then issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), generally covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. The company may separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using the BZSt electronic application process or the relevant tax registration data. A foreign entity conducting taxable business in Germany must register with the competent Finanzamt, provide constitutional documents, ownership and management information, business activities, German premises or tax representative details, expected turnover, bank information, and evidence of taxable transactions. A German fiscal representative is not generally mandatory for non-EU businesses, but may be appointed where legally appropriate. The UG must notify changes in business activity, registered office, bank details, or VAT status and must use ELSTER for applicable electronic returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German corporation and has no US-style check-the-box election to become tax transparent. It is generally subject to German corporate income tax and solidarity surcharge, as well as municipal trade tax. The corporate income tax option under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act is available to specified partnerships and not to a UG. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the ATAD framework and can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not provide an elective pass-through classification. A UG may participate in German tax consolidation arrangements where statutory requirements, including an eligible controlling company, profit-and-loss transfer agreement, and minimum duration conditions, are satisfied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital-account and current-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU (Capital Movements Directive). There is no exchange-control licensing regime, no forced conversion, and no administrative profit-remittance clearance for a German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt). Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely executable via SEPA/SWIFT; the sole fiscal burden is the statutory German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag), which can be reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty via a withholding-tax refund or exemption certificate. Foreign direct investment in deep foundation and diaphragm wall contracting does not require exchange-control approval; only sectoral trade licensing, EU/UN sanctions screening, and AWV statistical reporting apply. FX settlement may be denominated in EUR, USD, CNY or any freely convertible currency without central bank consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. Export receipts may be held indefinitely in the original currency in a German foreign-currency transit account. The only regulatory obligation is the Deutsche Bundesbank cross-border payment reporting regime: individual payments and receipts in excess of the statutory threshold (currently EUR 12,500 per transaction) must be reported; for goods-related export traffic, the exporter may alternatively file a monthly aggregate report when monthly cross-border receipts exceed EUR 50,000. For diaphargm-wall subcontracting, the UG's EUR/USD/CNY settlement is unrestricted, but every inbound receipt and outbound payment above the threshold must be traceable to the SWIFT/SEPA message and retained for at least six years for Bundesbank and Finanzamt inspection.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank), acting jointly with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) as the financial supervisory authority; the statutory administrator of cross-border payment statistics and capital movement reporting is the Deutsche Bundesbank pursuant to the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) and the Bundesbank reporting regulations. BaFin exercises KWG licensing supervision over banks and payment institutions, while the Bundesbank executes foreign-exchange statistics collection, balance-of-payments reporting, and sanctions-screening verification for all cross-border wire traffic.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium – secondary execution rail only. A German full-licensed fintech bank (e.g., Solaris SE) or an EEA EMI passporting into Germany under PSD2 (e.g., Revolut UAB, Wise Europe) can legally open an EUR account for the UG with almost instant video-KYC. However, the EMI is structurally unsuitable as the primary treasury rail for a deep-foundation contractor: (i) it cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit (MT700) or standby LCs (MT760); (ii) it cannot provide an overdraft, ISDA hedging line, or cross-border correspondent clearing in USD/CNY; (iii) its IBAN is held by the payment institution itself, which weakens the SWIFT evidentiary chain required for AWV trade reporting and German customs/VAT audits; and (iv) it may not accept deposits from the public under KWG §1(1a) – client funds can only be safeguarded/pass-through. An offshore non-EEA EMI (e.g., a Singapore or UAE payment company) is non-viable for main settlement because a German construction client and the German bank will require a regulated German correspondent BIC on SWIFT MT103 and a transparent account-holder name. Recommended use: employee travel cards, payroll facilitation, and small local expense accounts only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital-injection protocol: (1) execute a notarised capital increase resolution (Kapitalerhöhung) and file the UG's updated shareholder list with the Commercial Register; (2) order the inbound transfer as a SWIFT GPI MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR) with ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPC' (Capital Injection) and the German balance-of-payments statistics code 'K100' (Beteiligungskapital / Einlage) in the remittance information; (3) route only to the notified EUR IBAN of the tier-1 German bank account; (4) present the notarised resolution and the updated Handelsregisterausweis to the receiving bank before the credit is categorised as contributed equity; (5) where the injection exceeds EUR 12,500, file the statutory AWV/K1 report with the Deutsche Bundesbank in the prescribed reporting cycle and retain the MT103 confirmation, FX contract note (if USD/CNY converted), and account statement for six years. No offshore intermediary account or crypto rail is permissible for registered capital; the funding chain must be fully transparent to the German clearing bank.Clearing rails for deep-foundation project disbursements: (1) within Germany/EU – SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) or ordinary SEPA Credit Transfer on the UG's German IBAN for steel, bentonite slurry, rebar, and subcontractor invoices; (2) EU/UK suppliers invoicing in GBP or USD – SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer with purpose code 'TRAD', FX conversion executed through the German bank's dealing desk; (3) overseas heavy-equipment procurement (hydraulic trench cutters, slurry treatment plants, diaphragm-wall grabs) – irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit at sight (SWIFT MT700) issued by the German bank under ICC UCP600, with commercial invoice, packing list, full set of ocean bills of lading, marine insurance certificate, and pre-shipment inspection certificate as required; usance 30-90 days if a trade line is available; (4) performance guarantees to clients (Vertragserfüllungsbürgschaften) and advance-payment guarantees – standby Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT760) to avoid cash collateral lockup; (5) supplier rebates and warranty settlements – SWIFT MT103 credited to the UG's foreign-currency transit account with the trade-disbursement reference to the relevant LC and B/L. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium – secondary execution rail only. A German full-licensed fintech bank (e.g., Solaris SE) or an EEA EMI passporting into Germany under PSD2 (e.g., Revolut UAB, Wise Europe) can legally open an EUR account for the UG with almost instant video-KYC. However, the EMI is structurally unsuitable as the primary treasury rail for a deep-foundation contractor: (i) it cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit (MT700) or standby LCs (MT760); (ii) it cannot provide an overdraft, ISDA hedging line, or cross-border correspondent clearing in USD/CNY; (iii) its IBAN is held by the payment institution itself, which weakens the SWIFT evidentiary chain required for AWV trade reporting and German customs/VAT audits; and (iv) it may not accept deposits from the public under KWG §1(1a) – client funds can only be safeguarded/pass-through. An offshore non-EEA EMI (e.g., a Singapore or UAE payment company) is non-viable for main settlement because a German construction client and the German bank will require a regulated German correspondent BIC on SWIFT MT103 and a transparent account-holder name. Recommended use: employee travel cards, payroll facilitation, and small local expense accounts only.
