Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) of the Amtsgericht (Local Court) of the district of the registered office, with mandatory electronic filing by a German notary (Justizportal / handelsregister.de).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), particularly § 1 (formation), § 2 (notarial form of Articles of Association), § 3 (contents of Articles), § 4a (registered office), § 5 (minimum share capital), § 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft – UG), § 6 (managing directors), § 8 (application for registration); German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch – HGB) §§ 6, 26, 106 (Commercial Register); Act on Electronic Commercial Register (EHUG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG requires nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00 and below EUR 25,000.00 (GmbHG § 5a); capital must be fully paid in cash into a German SEPA corporate bank account before the registration application is filed, with a bank confirmation (Depotbescheinigung/Vorbescheinigung) attached; cash contributions only, no contribution in kind permitted; the UG must annually retain 25% of net profit as a statutory reserve until its share capital reaches EUR 25,000.00, enabling automatic conversion into a full GmbH.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutzabnahme) from the local fire authority confirming compliance with Musterbauordnung and VKF standards: fire alarm system, certified extinguishers, escape route signage and kitchen hood suppression; structural load inspection (Standsicherheitsnachweis) by the building supervisory authority for extraction hoods, gas lines and equipment installation; occupational clearance by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt covering the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) — ventilation rates, noise exposure ≤ 80 dB(A) personal rating level, thermal comfort, electrical safety, and first-aid and hygiene requirements for cooking staff.

Paid-in capital rule: UG requires nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00 and below EUR 25,000.00 (GmbHG § 5a); capital must be fully paid in cash into a German SEPA corporate bank account before the registration application is filed, with a bank confirmation (Depotbescheinigung/Vorbescheinigung) attached; cash contributions only, no contribution in kind permitted; the UG must annually retain 25% of net profit as a statutory reserve until its share capital reaches EUR 25,000.00, enabling automatic conversion into a full GmbH.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and withholding tax; local district tax office (Finanzamt) with jurisdiction over the UG's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the UG's Articles of Association and register the company with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Step 2: File the trade application (Gewerbeanmeldung) with the local Trade Office (Gewerbeamt). Step 3: Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the local Finanzamt via ELSTER, disclosing the ghost-kitchen activity, estimated turnover, shareholder details, and payout rails (UberEats/DoorDash); the Finanzamt assigns the local Steuernummer. Step 4: Apply to BZSt for the cross-border VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) via Formular USt 1 TU or ELSTER. Step 5: Activate the e-invoicing receiver capability and register the TSE-certified cash register for point-of-sale delivery transactions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law provides no US-style check-the-box election and no fiscal-transparency election for an Unternehmergesellschaft (UG); the UG is mandatorily taxable as a corporation under the Corporate Income Tax Act (Körperschaftsteuergesetz) and Trade Tax Act (Gewerbesteuergesetz), with dividends subject to 26.375% withholding (Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge). Available statutory elections: (i) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG where financial, economic, and organisational integration exists; (ii) corporate income-tax group (körperschaftsteuerliche Organschaft) under Secs. 14–17 KStG executed through a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (iii) cash-basis VAT accounting (Ist-Besteuerung) under Sec. 20 UStG where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 800,000; (iv) small-entrepreneur relief (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Sec. 19 UStG where prior-year turnover is EUR 22,000 or less and current-year turnover is EUR 50,000 or less; (v) no elective hybrid treatment — anti-hybrid rules under Sec. 4k EStG apply to deny deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold: mandatory VAT registration upon commencement of commercial activity. The only threshold-based relief is the optional small-entrepreneur scheme (Sec. 19 UStG) applicable where prior-year turnover is EUR 22,000 or less and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000; once exceeded, standard registration and VAT collection apply. For the delivery-only ghost kitchen, the food component of meals is taxed at the reduced rate of 7%, while delivery/ancillary fees are taxed at 19%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to territories outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Sec. 4 No. 1(a) and Sec. 6 UStG provided the goods physically leave Germany and the exporter retains both the book entry and documentary evidence of export. Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers qualify for 0% VAT under Sec. 4 No. 1(b) and Sec. 6a UStG where the recipient holds a valid VAT ID, the goods are dispatched to another Member State, the supply is reported in the recapitulative statement and the e-export list, and transport evidence is retained. For a delivery-only ghost kitchen, food delivered to consumers in other EU Member States is generally treated as distance selling subject to destination-country VAT rather than German zero-rating; cross-border B2B catering and digital services fall under the reverse-charge mechanism of Sec. 3a(2) UStG and must be invoiced with the reference 'reverse charge', with no German VAT levied.

From 1 January 2025, all German businesses must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0+). Issuance obligations are phased: for 2025–2026 paper/PDF invoices remain valid if the recipient consents; from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all businesses, including the UG ghost kitchen, must issue structured e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions. No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous-control system (no Italian SDI equivalent) exists; however, POS and delivery-billing software must integrate a certified TSE (Technische Sicherheitseinrichtung), records must comply with GoBD retention rules, and B2G invoices require XRechnung.

German tax authorities apply strict documentary evidence rules (Beleg- und Buchnachweis) to all zero-rating claims. Missing or defective evidence triggers retrospective VAT assessment under Sec. 14c UStG, estimated assessment (Schätzung) under Sec. 162 AO, and late-payment interest of 0.5% per month under Sec. 233a AO. Fraudulent or reckless zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years, and may cause loss of the zero-rating benefit with VAT clawback for up to 10 years. Repatriation of export proceeds after the 180-day deadline does not automatically invalidate zero-rating but raises rebuttal risk of fictitious export and may trigger anti-money-laundering filings by the paying bank.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU free movement of capital framework. The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible; no exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance for foreign investors. However, reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 (AWV reporting) and for direct investments, but these are statistical and not approval-based. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (5% under most DTTs) and proof of tax residence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad, subject to AWV reporting requirements for receivables/payables exceeding €5,000. There is no statutory timeline for conversion; proceeds may be retained in any currency. However, for tax purposes, unrealized FX gains/losses are recognized at year-end based on the official Bundesbank reference rates.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and financial market supervision; the Bundesbank administers foreign exchange statistics and monitoring under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. German-licensed EMIs (e.g., PPRO, Solarisbank, N26 Business) can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment rails. However, for a UG (GmbH) with ghost kitchen operations, traditional banks are preferred for credibility and acceptance by delivery platforms (UberEats, DoorDash). EMIs may have restrictions on cash deposits, higher fees, and limited credit facilities. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed under PSD2 and are subject to BaFin supervision; they cannot offer lending or interest-bearing accounts. For high-volume settlement, a full-service bank is recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections (shareholder loans or equity contributions) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with proper purpose codes. For equity contributions, use purpose code 'P01' (Direct Investment) or 'P02' (Capital Contribution) under the AWV. For shareholder loans, use 'P03' (Loans). The receiving bank will require a copy of the commercial register entry and the resolution of the shareholders. Verification: The bank will perform a plausibility check against the company's registered capital and the shareholder's identity. For amounts above €12,500, the Bundesbank must be notified via the AWV reporting system (Allgemeine Meldeportale). The funds must be credited to the company's operational account; if a capital escrow account is used, it must be converted to a regular account after registration.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For ghost kitchen operations, outbound payments include supplier invoices, platform fees, and operational expenses. Standard rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers (e.g., to overseas ingredient suppliers); (2) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EU payments; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for large, high-value imports (e.g., kitchen equipment) – typically via MT700/MT707; (4) Direct Debit (SEPA DD) for recurring payments. For delivery platform payouts (UberEats, DoorDash), these are inbound, but outbound to the platforms for fees may be via card or direct debit. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) + 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 15% (aggregate 15.825%) + trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates (approx. 14–17% depending on local multiplier), yielding a combined effective rate of approximately 30–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25%, i.e. an additional 1.375 percentage points). Reduced to zero for German corporate shareholders meeting the participation exemption and providing an exemption certificate; treaty rates may apply for non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 — §2 GAufzV requires mandatory Local File documentation for German entities with cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5 million in a fiscal year; a Master File is additionally required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (EU Transfer Pricing Documentation Directive, implemented under §90(3b) AO).

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but enforces the substance-over-form doctrine, effective place-of-management rule (§10 AO), and anti-abuse provisions (§42 AO). A ghost-kitchen UG must maintain actual business substance: local operational premises, kitchen staff, delivery-payment contracts with UberEats/DoorDash, and local strategic decision-making. Lack of substance can trigger treaty denial under §50d(3) EStG, permanent-establishment recharacterization, or CFC attribution to non-resident shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Systematic Underreporting of Digital Platform Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to €10.8 million or imprisonment up to 5 years under §370 AO; aggravated cases carry imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years; orders for disgorgement of the evaded amount under §§73 ff. StGB.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal prosecution as individuals; under §69 AO personal liability attaches if corporate taxes remain unpaid due to intentional or grossly negligent breach of duties, permitting seizure of personal assets, travel bans under §132 AO, and disqualification from directorship.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Income re-characterization and upward adjustment; 25% penalty surcharge on the resulting tax increase under §1(3) AStG; denial of DTA benefits under §50d(3) EStG; administrative fines for abusive structuring under §42 AO up to €25,000; commercial-register deregistration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for evaded taxes if the shell structure is found fraudulent; creditors may pierce the UG veil; court-ordered prohibition from managing companies; travel bans and imprisonment up to 3 years for assisting tax evasion.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance in Digital Commerce

Statutory sanction: Under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG): administrative fines up to €100,000 and up to 10% of annual turnover for legal entities; criminal money-laundering penalties under §261 StGB of up to 5 years imprisonment; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who negligently supervise compliance face civil liability for losses; criminal accountability under §261 StGB or §285a GwG leads to imprisonment, forfeiture of illicit assets, and bans from management positions.
International Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting to Foreign IP Shells

Statutory sanction: Upward income adjustment by the tax office plus 25% penalty surcharge on the tax increase under §1(3) AStG; 6% annual default interest; denial of royalty deduction under §4j EStG and §1(1) AStG.

Director personal liability: Directors become secondarily liable for adjusted corporate taxes under §69 AO if they signed defective returns; intentional falsification triggers personal criminal liability for tax evasion up to 5 years; D&O insurance policies typically exclude such recovery.
