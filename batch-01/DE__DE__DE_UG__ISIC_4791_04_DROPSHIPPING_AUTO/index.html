Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and competent local Finanzamt; online filing via ELSTER (ElsterOnline-Portal)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)) must first be registered in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The Managing Director(s) then submit the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) for income tax, corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. The VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is issued separately by BZSt upon application using official form USt 1 TW. The entire process can be initiated online via ELSTER, with foreign entities also using BZSt's special registration portal for cross-border cases.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime. The UG is a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer at entity level. Available statutory elections include the formation of an Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Sec. 14 KStG whereby the UG can be treated as a financially integrated subsidiary for income tax, corporate income tax, and VAT purposes; and the small business VAT exemption election under Sec. 19 UStG, subject to turnover thresholds. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD are implemented in German law but are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic UG: €22,000 prior-year turnover and €50,000 current-year projected turnover for the small business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG; non-resident e-commerce suppliers have mandatory VAT registration in Germany without any economic threshold if they make taxable supplies in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies to third-country destinations are zero-rated under Sec. 4 No. 1 and Sec. 6 UStG provided the UG is the entrepreneurial supplier, the goods physically leave Germany and the EU territory, and the exporter possesses conclusive transport and security evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis). For automated dropshipping, if the UG acts as seller of record and the goods are delivered directly from a supplier located in the third country to the final third-country customer without entering the EU, such supplies generally do not fall within German VAT scope; if goods are physically dispatched from Germany to a third country, zero-rating applies only with the required export documentation. Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers may alternatively be zero-rated with the customer's valid German or EU VAT number and appropriate summary declarations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) / ATLAS export confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (depending on transport mode)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing payment from the overseas customer
  • Delivery proofs and carrier tracking data

From 1 January 2025, domestic B2B supplies require structured electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) in formats compliant with the European standard EN 16931, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD; receipt via accessible electronic channels is mandatory. There is currently no continuous real-time transaction clearance or mandatory government digital stamping requirement in Germany. However, automated online sales are subject to GoBD data-retention requirements, and any electronic cash-register system must use a certified technical security device (TSE) under Sec. 146a AO if physical POS devices are used, though pure online payment processing via Stripe or PayPal does not itself trigger TSE installation.

German tax authorities require full documentary evidence of export shipment; failure to provide the export proof leads to denial of the zero rate, retroactive assessment at 19% VAT, late-payment interest of 0.5% per month, and penalties of up to €50,000 under VAT procedural rules. Fraudulent or negligent obtaining of export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, subject to criminal fines, imprisonment up to 10 years, and a 10-year limitation period for black-money tax evasion cases. The BZSt and customs authorities increasingly cross-check export declarations with payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) and customs transaction data in automated dropshipping audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates under the euro (EUR). The euro is fully convertible on both capital and current account transactions, subject to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). There are no exchange controls restricting the conversion of EUR into foreign currency or the remittance of profits, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. Non-EU resident investors enjoy unrestricted profit repatriation subject to domestic corporate income tax, trade tax, and possible withholding taxes reduced or exempted under double taxation treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts may be held in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency, banked domestically or abroad. All cross-border payments, including export receipts, are subject to the German cross-border statistical reporting regime under AWV Section 67–68, requiring resident enterprises to report individual transactions exceeding €12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days of the value date. Non-reporting can trigger fines, but there is no mandated conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) with prudential supervision by the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (additional risk overlay). Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, or similar are not authorized under the German Banking Act (KWG) to conduct full banking business. They provide payment services under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and are classified as e-money institutions. For a German UG active in automated dropshipping, such EMIs may be used for cross-border collections, but they are not acceptable as a sole treasury platform. Legal constraints include: (i) EMIs generally cannot provide credit facilities; (ii) funds received are not bank deposits but safeguarded customer assets; (iii) many EMIs block transactions to high-risk payment industries or require extensive provenance documentation; (iv) German tax authorities and audit principles often require a domestic or EU/EEA bank account with standard IBAN for commercial substance verification. Thus, an EMI can supplement, not replace, a tier-1 corporate bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a capital contribution (Stammeinlage) into a German UG from a non‑resident shareholder, the compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the UG's account at a German bank. The remittance information field (Field 70) must clearly reference 'Einlage in die Kapitalrücklage' or 'Stammeinlage auf Gesellschaftsanteile' (capital deposit). The payer's bank must transmit the full purpose code per AWV; commonly Germany uses the statistical code 'N110' (Capital outflow for investments) for the payer/foreign country and the receiving German bank will book it as a capital inflow to the subscribed capital accounts. The UG must report the transaction to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days under AWV §67 (cross-border reporting threshold €12,500), providing the creditor/debtor reference and the legal transaction type. All documentation—including the notarised GmbH/UG deed or shareholders' resolution, capital contribution proof, and bank statement copy—must be retained for BaFin/registration court (Registergericht) inspection. For inbound payments exceeding €50,000, the bank will request an exemptions certificate (Nichtveranlagungsbescheinigung) or, if not available, proof of tax compliance to ensure no vehicle is being used for tax avoidance.Primary clearing rails for trade settlements from the German UG to foreign suppliers: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers, allowing full remittance info and allocation of purpose codes; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) for high-value initial transactions, with discharge via compliant presentation at the IG referral bank; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro-denominated payments to suppliers within the EU/EEA countries; (iv) Institution-to-Institution Cross-border Payments via the SEPA ISO 20022 XML format, when applicable. For each outbound payment, the UG must maintain a trail comprising a purchase order, supplier invoice, logistics proof, and a payment instruction referencing the commercial contract. Any trade settlement exceeding €12,500 requires Bundesbank statistical reporting (if cross-border). Foreign exchange conversion costs and clearing channel choice are optimized by holding multi-currency accounts (EUR, USD, GBP) and by electing the netting of AR/AP where both customers and suppliers are domiciled in third countries.Outright FX forwards (devisentermingeschäft) with a licensed German or EU/EEA credit institution, documented under an ISDA or DRV master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies subject to offshore convertibility restrictions; permitted only if the counterparty is a reputable EU bank and the transaction is hedged to physical trade exposure (under EMIR trade reporting thresholds).Cross-currency swaps (CCS/Cross Currency Interest Rate Swaps) for matching long-term cash flow mismatches between EUR and foreign-currency revenues; requires derivative authorisation and post-trade transparency under EMIR.USD/EUR rolling spot forwards and 'window forwards' to accommodate monthly payout cycles from PSP platforms.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory for cross-border related-party transactions with aggregate value exceeding EUR 5,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services (de minimis for penalty imposition); Master File required where consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000; CbC Report threshold EUR 750,000,000 group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations statute, but economic substance is an implicit prerequisite: Treaty benefits and domestic withholding tax relief require actual business activity in the residence state (section 50d(3) EStG); anti-abuse section 42 AO allows disregarding shell structures; CFC rules (sections 7–13 AStG) pierce passive low-taxed income; a UG used solely as an automated dropshipping conduit without employees, premises or business decisions is exposed to these substance and beneficial-ownership rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years for intentionally underpaid tax in significant amounts (section 370 AO); financial restitution and interest at 0.5% per month; fines by daily-rate system, not capped at a fixed amount.

Director personal liability: Managing directors (Geschäftsführer) are personally liable for withheld and unpaid VAT/tax amounts under section 69 AO if obligations are not met; civil liability for intentional omission (section 823 BGB) and violation of § 64 GmbHG; in cases of severe evasion a travel ban and seizure of passport may be ordered; imprisonment for tax evasion is a criminal offence joined to the civil fine.
Missing Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Presumption of income adjustment (section 162(3) AO) with penalty surcharge of 5%–10% of the resulting increase in taxable income, minimum EUR 5,000 (section 162(4) AO); documentation penalty up to EUR 25,000 for non-submission; refusal to disclose facts may lead to the tax authority using best judgments.

Director personal liability: Directors who decide to disregard TP documentation obligations are jointly liable for the additional assessment and the penalty; criminal liability under section 378 AO (negligent tax evasion) can arise for directors who do not ensure proper documentation.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Treaty Abuse, MLI)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test and section 50d(3) EStG, meaning no reduction of withholding tax; additional tax plus interest; administrative fines under § 42 AO (recalculation of income); disqualification for reduced trade tax multiplier in some municipalities.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for unpaid withholding taxes due to the denial of treaty relief, including retroactive payments; a D&O tort claim for acting against the company's interest; travel bans under section 326 AO if a director leaves Germany without paying taxes.
AML / Compliance Violations (Payment Rails Stripe/PayPal)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million or twice the economic benefit (section 56 GwG); account freezes and termination by payment service providers; criminal fines for facilitating tax evasion; possible seizure of goods and internet store closure.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for sanctions not discharged by the company; AML responsibilities cannot be delegated without compliance; criminal liability for money laundering (section 261 StGB) if the director knows the funds are tax-relevant; a travel ban may be issued as ancillary penalty.
