Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash before registration; contributions in kind are excluded at formation. There is no statutory escrow account requirement, but the capital should be paid into a company bank account and made available to the company. The company must allocate 25% of annual net profit, reduced by carried-forward losses, to a statutory reserve until the capital reaches EUR 25,000. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates under a fully liberalized Euro FX regime aligned with EU free movement of capital and current account rules. Capital account transactions, including profit remittances, capital repatriations, and equity injections, are permitted without prior approval or restrictions for standard corporate activities. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are allowed under EU directives, subject to German withholding tax (Abgeltungsteuer) and beneficial ownership reporting. All FX transactions exceeding €12,500 must be reported electronically to the Bundesbank via the eFREP platform. Convertibility hurdles remain minimal for low-risk entities but may require enhanced due diligence, source-of-funds verification, or Bundesbank notification for large or unusual transactions involving politically sensitive jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under German or EU law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in domestic or offshore bank accounts and convert them at market rates as needed. Repatriation to a German account is recommended for liquidity management and tax optimization, with no statutory timeline mandated. Banks require supporting documentation including commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and customs declarations for FX conversions above reporting thresholds. Conversion ratios are market-driven with no statutory mandates or fixed ratios.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

High feasibility for digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) including Stripe, PayPal, and Wise for cross-border treasury and payment services.
