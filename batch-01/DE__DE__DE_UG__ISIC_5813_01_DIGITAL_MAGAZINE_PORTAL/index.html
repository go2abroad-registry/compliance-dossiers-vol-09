Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt; Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT identification number and federal tax administration; locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity establishing German tax presence: (1) file the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt within four weeks of commencing business; (2) the Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer) for income/corporate/trade tax and VAT; (3) separately apply to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) via the application portal or Form USt 1, required for intra-EU supplies and OSS registration; (4) a domestic fiscal representative may be required if the foreign entity has no German residence or fixed establishment and derives German-source VAT-liable supplies; (5) registration is mandatory irrespective of turnover where a domestic fixed establishment or registered branch (Zweigniederlassung) exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the UG is a German-resident corporation subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), with no pass-through to shareholders; taxation is unlimited (worldwide income) subject to double-tax-treaty relief; no tax-exempt-zone status applies to the UG in the federal territory.

Available election forms

Germany does not have a general check-the-box regime. A UG is mandatorily taxed as a corporation. Available elections: (i) corporate income tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§ 14–19 KStG and VAT fiscal unity under § 2(2) Nr. 2 UStG with a financially and organizationally integrated controlling domestic parent; (ii) trade tax consolidation alongside Organschaft; (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustments and elections under ATAD implementation (§ 4k EStG, § 8b KStG) limiting deduction/non-recognition benefits; (iv) treaty tie-breaker and permanent-establishment allocation elections under applicable German double-tax treaties; (v) option under § 1a KStG for partnership-style transparency is not available to a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold for a UG with domestic seat: VAT registration is mandatory from inception. For small-business exemption: EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 expected current-year turnover (§ 19 UStG). For EU cross-border distance sales of digital magazine subscriptions: EUR 10,000 annual threshold before OSS-mandatory registration in the destination member state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports to third-country destinations qualify for 0% VAT under § 6 UStG where the goods are transported or dispatched to a place outside the EU and the exporter maintains conclusive proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis). Intra-EU B2B supplies of goods qualify under § 6a UStG subject to recipient VAT-ID validation, EC sales list filing, and commercial documentation. Electronically supplied services (digital magazine subscriptions, sponsored media placements) supplied to a recipient outside the EU fall outside German VAT by virtue of the place-of-supply rule; supply to EU consumers is taxed in the member state of the consumer through the OSS scheme rather than the German export zero-rating regime. Zero-rating requires the supplier to obtain and preserve customer business/residence evidence (e.g., VAT ID, IP address data, bank remittance details).

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS electronic customs declaration or equivalent EU export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing transport to a third country)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank statement proving receipt of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer)

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) stamping, or mandatory API reporting as of the 2024/2025 reference period. The e-invoicing mandate under the Growth Opportunities Act applies: from 1 January 2025 all domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 mandatory issuance of e-invoices applies to businesses with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000; from 1 January 2028 mandatory for all domestic B2B supplies. Mandated formats: XRechnung (EN 16931 XML) and hybrid ZUGFeRD (PDF+XML); EDI partial exemption applies. VAT returns must be transmitted electronically via ELSTER/ERiC; VAT payment deadlines align with the 10th day of the following month (extended to the 10th day of the second month where a VAT advance return is filed electronically).

German tax audits (Betriebsprüfung) strictly test export evidence. Where the export proof is incomplete or invalid, the Finanzamt reverses the 0% rating and assesses output VAT at 19% on the gross consideration, adding interest of 0.5% per month on the assessed tax (§ 233a AO) and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month (§ 240 AO). Fraudulent export zero-rating or falsification of export documentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to five years (ten years in aggravated cases) and/or monetary fines; managing directors are liable under §§ 69, 71 AO for unpaid or withheld VAT. The assessment limitation period is extended from four to ten years for tax evasion cases, enabling long-tail retrospective clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU free-movement rules and the German Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG). Profit and dividend remittance from a UG is unrestricted, subject only to standard German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge (where applicable), and trade tax. Outbound profit distributions to foreign shareholders are generally subject to German withholding tax of 26.375% on dividends unless a lower domestic or treaty rate applies, e.g. 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the beneficial owner is an EU/EEA corporation with a qualifying shareholding. No exchange-control approval is required for capital injections, service fees, licensing royalties, or dividend outflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters are entitled to retain foreign-currency balances and to decide the timing and counterparty for conversion. The only binding obligation is the statistical reporting duty under §59 of the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV): each cross-border receipt or payment with a non-resident in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 30 days following the relevant calendar month, via the Bank's electronic processing system (EPEX). The report must include transaction amount, currency, external transaction code, and the counterparty's name and country.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank and integrated foreign-exchange statistics authority), acting within the Eurosystem and in coordination with the Federal Ministry of Finance and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for payment services supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border offshore EMI viability is low unless the digital EMI holds a valid EU/EEA banking license or a BaFin-authorised payment institution licence, or is registered in Germany under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). Offshore fintech EMIs not passportable into Germany will have no BaFin cross-border passport and therefore cannot lawfully open transaction accounts for a UG. Feasibility for fully licensed EU EMIs (e.g., Solaris SE, N26 Bank GmbH, or PPRO-licensed platforms) is medium-to-high for day-to-day operations, but they are generally not acceptable as principal treasury/clearing accounts for capital events or international vendor payments where standard SWIFT MT103 channels are required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Das Kapital for a UG (initial registered capital or subsequent contributions) from non-resident shareholders is transferred via SWIFT wire or SEPA credit transfer using the account held in Germany. The payment instruction must include an explicit purpose statement in the unstructured reference, e.g. 'Einzahlung auf das Stammkapital – Digital Magazine Publishing UG' or 'Capital Increase – UG', together with the relevant judicial or notarial basis. Under AWV reporting rules, the German recipient must report the inbound cross-border payment to the Deutsche Bundesbank if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, using the monthly statistical report with external transaction code (FZ) and counterparty country. Verification for corporate treasury purposes includes the bank confirmation, share-register entry, Commercial Register filing of the capital increase, and the notarial challenge of the capital contribution rules for a UG. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT (0.825%), resulting in a base CIT burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is superimposed at a 3.5% assessment rate multiplied by the local Hebesatz (commonly 200%–490%), adding approximately 7%–17.15%. The aggregate combined effective rate for a German-resident UG with digital publishing income is approximately 27.6%–31.3% depending on the municipality, because trade tax is deductible for corporate income tax purposes.
