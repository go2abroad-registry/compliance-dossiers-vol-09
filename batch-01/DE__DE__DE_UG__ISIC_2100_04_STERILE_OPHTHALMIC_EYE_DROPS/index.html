Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG may adopt a nominal share capital of EUR 1.00, but because this is below the normal GmbH minimum of EUR 25,000, the full subscribed capital must be paid in cash or be fully covered by in-kind contributions before the registration application is filed. Payment must be proven by a bank statement or confirmation addressed to the notary; the funds must be credited to a dedicated company capital account and remain at the company's free disposal. No state escrow account is mandatory, but a blocked account or notarial escrow instruction is customary until registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety approval based on a project-specific Brandschutzkonzept; structural-load and works-certificate inspection for cleanrooms, blow-fill-seal machines, autoclaves and HVAC installations; occupational workplace clearance under the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) and German Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG); employer's liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft) registration; exposure-limit assessments for volatile organic compounds and pharmaceutical active substances.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), the Federal Central Tax Office, is the competent national authority for the issuance of the VAT identification number and international tax matters (portal: www.bzst.de and 'Mein BZSt'); the locally competent Finanzamt (e.g., Finanzamt Berlin-Charlottenburg) is responsible for the Steuernummer (tax number) and ongoing tax assessments; all tax filings and registration forms are submitted electronically through the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German UG (including foreign-owned): (1) notarize the articles of association, register the UG in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), appoint a managing director, and open a German bank account; (2) within four weeks of commencing business, submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt; (3) receive the Steuernummer (11-digit corporate income tax and trade tax number) which is also used for regular VAT returns; (4) apply separately to BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) through 'Mein BZSt' or by opting in on the registration questionnaire; the VAT ID is mandatory for intra-EU B2B supplies and must be shown on invoices; (5) if a German-registered UG is dormant, registration is still required once a notarial deed has been entered into the Commercial Register; (6) typical processing times: Steuernummer 2–6 weeks, VAT ID 4–8 weeks; an expedited VAT ID procedure is available for urgent transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; a UG is mandatorily an opaque corporation subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of the corporate income tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate 7–17% depending on the municipality). Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT and corporate income tax/trade tax Organschaft (fiscal unity) upon conclusion of a domination and profit transfer agreement; (ii) the 95% dividend exemption under §8b(1) Körperschaftsteuergesetz for qualifying shareholdings; (iii) the intercorporate dividend exemption from trade tax under §9 No. 2a Gewerbesteuergesetz; (iv) compliance and documentation elections under §4k EStG addressing hybrid mismatch arrangements; and (v) conversion of the UG into a German limited partnership (GmbH & Co. KG) to adopt transparent partnership taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for VAT registration applies: a German-resident UG is a taxable person from its first taxable transaction. The small-business exemption under §19 UStG applies only where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 100,000 (thresholds as amended effective 1 January 2025); a UG engaged in pharmaceutical manufacturing (ISIC 2100) ordinarily cannot rely on this exemption. Intra-EU distance sales and electronically supplied services are subject to the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold under §3c UStG.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods to third-country territories are zero-rated under §4 No. 1(a) UStG and §6 UStG where: (i) the supplier or a commissioned carrier ships or transports the goods from Germany into a third-country territory; (ii) the goods are properly declared in an ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and released for export; (iii) the supplier retains the export evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis) required by §§8–10 Umsatzsteuer-Durchführungsverordnung, comprising customs exit confirmation, transport documents, and the commercial invoice; (iv) the export consideration is demonstrably credited to the German entity's account (per §10 UStDV); (v) for intra-EU dispatches instead, zero-rating under §4 No. 1(b) and §6a UStG requires a valid customer VAT ID and documentary proof of intra-Community transport. Regulatory export licenses required by the German Medicines Act (AMG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) for sterile ophthalmic pharmaceutical products do not by themselves affect the VAT zero-rating analysis.

  • Customs export declaration (ATLAS/AES Ausfuhranmeldung) with the export accompanying document and customs exit confirmation
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung) issued under §14 UStG containing export-specific particulars and the zero-rating reference
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing carriage from Germany to the third-country destination
  • Proof of payment or SWIFT credit advice demonstrating crediting of the export consideration to the UG's German bank account
  • Complete export evidence file (Ausfuhrnachweis) retained in original digital form for at least ten years under GoBD

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung): from 1 January 2025, every German-resident business must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1) for domestic B2B supplies; from 1 January 2027, taxpayers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, e-invoice issuance is mandatory for all enterprises (existing EDI agreements qualify until 2028). Germany does not currently operate real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority pre-clearance APIs, or digital stamping; it applies a post-audit model with mandatory structured data and full data retention under GoBD. Customs export declarations are transmitted in real time through the ATLAS system, and compliance with the EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) digital reporting requirements is expected to be phased in from 2030.

German tax authorities verify export zero-rating through documentary field audits (Außenprüfung) and reconcile export proceeds against the consideration evidence under §10 UStDV. German VAT law does not prescribe a fixed statutory 180-day repatriation deadline; however, 180 days is the customary due-diligence benchmark, and unrepatriated proceeds create a rebuttable inference that the export transaction was not commercially genuine. Where zero-rating is denied or evidence is deficient, the tax office retrospectively assesses 19% VAT on the full consideration, plus monthly interest of 0.5% under §233a AO and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month under §240 AO. Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years where customs duties are also evaded, plus unlimited administrative fines; §378 AO penalty proceedings, input VAT adjustment claims under §15a UStG, trade tax reassessments, AEO certification revocation, and GoBD record-keeping sanctions also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full euro convertibility under EU free movement of capital. The euro is a fully convertible currency for both current and capital account transactions; profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be repatriated freely, subject to general anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and statistical reporting obligations. No exchange license, surrender requirement, or differentiated non-resident convertibility regime applies within the eurozone; capital movements with third countries remain protected under EU Treaty article 64 TFEU, with only exceptional safeguard measures under EU law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio in Germany. Export receipts denominated in EUR or foreign currency may be retained, used, or converted freely. However, German resident exporters are required to report significant cross-border payments and receivables under Bundesbank AWV reporting rules: receivables and payables with non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported monthly, and export/import transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 in goods/services must be declared in the external trade statistics.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Germany's central bank) and, for prudential supervision of credit institutions, the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); foreign trade and payments statistics are administered by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for purely ancillary liquidity management, Low for primary banking relationship. German law does not prohibit a UG from holding accounts at licensed European EMIs; however, BaFin-regulated e-money institutions cannot grant credit, offer full cash-account safeguards, or provide the UG with a German bank sort code under the Banking Act. A digital EMI used as sole treasury account creates director liability risk for tax and capital-contribution compliance, because capital verification for the UG's share capital requires a confirmation from a credit institution within the meaning of the German Banking Act (KWG). For international operations, a licensed EMI (e.g., operating with a German e-money license) may be viable only as a second-tier payments engine for supplier disbursements, subject to KYC/AML, sanctions screening, and account-completeness due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the UG's initial share-capital injection from abroad: the shareholder remits funds by SWIFT MT103 using a clear beneficiary line to the UG's German KWG-licensed bank account, with a payment purpose code that identifies the transfer as a 'contribution to capital of a limited company' (for German bank statistics, e.g. AWV code 'K-1' or appropriate balance-of-payments classification such as 'Capital injection into a direct-investment enterprise'), coupled with a written capital-increase or formation agreement. Funds should be transmitted in advance without mixing operating receivables; the UG should request the receiving bank to issue a capital contribution confirmation or a block-based certificate used for commercial-register registration. Transfer references must include the UG company name and the explicitly declared purpose 'Einlage UG (haftungsbeschränkt)', avoiding ambiguous reasons such as 'consulting services' or 'general payment'. If the capital exceeds EUR 12,500, the receiving/remitting German bank will automatically report the payment to the Bundesbank under AWV §67.
