Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and administrative assistance; local Finanzamt (tax office) for income tax and municipal trade tax. Electronic filing through ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German UG must first execute notarial incorporation and register with the commercial register (Handelsregister). The managing director then submits the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (national tax number) for corporate income, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax. Separately, the UG must apply to the BZSt for a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) using the official application form; for EU cross-border transactions, the VAT ID is mandatory for intra-Community supplies and acquisition reporting. Foreign direct sellers without a German establishment may also register for a VAT ID directly via the BZSt's special procedure for non-established entrepreneurs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No generic check-the-box or equivalent entity classification election exists under German corporate tax law. The UG is automatically taxable as a corporation at entity level (Körperschaftsteuer, Solidaritätszuschlag, Gewerbesteuer) regardless of ownership structure. Statutory elections are limited to specialised mechanisms: (1) corporate group taxation (Organschaft) under Sec. 14 Körperschaftsteuergesetz, requiring a profit and loss transfer agreement and financial/organisational integration, leading to income and trade tax pooling at the parent level; (2) VAT fiscal unity (Umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) under Sec. 2 Umsatzsteuergesetz, whereby a financially, economically, and organisationally integrated subsidiary is treated as one taxable person with the parent; (3) anti-hybrid mismatch elections and subordinated arrangements permitted under the German implementation of EU ATAD (e.g., Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 12 KStG) are available only insofar as they align with domestic hybrid mismatch rules; no elective pass-through regime exists for corporate entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic small businesses (Kleinunternehmer), the VAT exemption applies if prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000. However, for non-established foreign suppliers, every taxable supply in Germany triggers a VAT registration obligation with no de minimis threshold; alternatively, the EU-wide distance sales threshold of EUR 10,000 may allow use of the One-Stop-Shop (OSS), with German VAT applied at the applicable German rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Germany to a third-country destination qualify for a 0% VAT rate (Ausfuhrlieferung, Sec. 6 UStG) provided the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the exporter retains verifiable documentary evidence of exportation. Services supplied to non-EU customers are zero-rated if the place of supply is located in the third country (Sec. 3a UStG, Sec. 4 No. 1 UStG); for B2B services received by an entrepreneur outside the EU, the reverse charge shifts liability to the customer. Intra-Community supplies (B2B) to another EU member state are zero-rated under Sec. 4 No. 1 UStG only where the customer communicates a valid EU VAT ID, the goods are dispatched within the EU, and the supplier files a recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) with Electronic Export Accompanying Document (EAD) or ENS/EXS summary;
  • Commercial invoice containing export-specific wording '0% USt. Ausfuhrlieferung (Sec. 6 UStG)';
  • Packing list;
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (title of transport document);
  • Proof of dispatch and arrival (CMR consignment note, seaway bill, freight invoice, delivery note);
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration from the non-EU buyer;
  • VAT ID confirmation and Intrastat declaration (where applicable for intra-Community dispatch);
  • Authorisation from the exporter (Beleg- und Ausfuhrnachweis, Sec. 8 UStDV).

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system (no stamping, no mandatory API clearance per invoice). From 1 January 2025, a domestic B2B e-invoicing obligation applies: every in-state taxable supply between established German businesses requires a structured electronic invoice compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD). The transmission and archiving must use an EDI-compatible API or standard electronic delivery portal; paper invoices are no longer accepted for B2B transactions. For B2C e-commerce, the seller must still provide a receipt, but no fiscal digital stamping is required. Global e-invoicing interoperability obligations are limited to the EU VAT Digital Reporting requirements (expected rollout 2030), not currently applying per transaction.

Fraudulent or substantively deficient export zero-rating is treated as tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Sec. 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years or fines; negligent underdeclaration is subject to surcharges and penalties under Sec. 378 AO. A retrospective VAT clawback is issued for the entire period of assessment where export documents, payment receipts, or physical export evidence are absent, with late payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) from the original due date, plus forfeiture of the right to zero-rating and an additional penalty up to EUR 25,000 under Sec. 26a UStG for failure to issue compliant invoices. The BZSt and customs authorities routinely cross-match export declarations, shipping manifests, and FX repatriation data; any discrepancy or missing inward remittance within 180 days triggers a VAT audit, an assessment notice (Steuernachzahlung), and possible criminal referral.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies a fully liberalised euro-area payments regime. Current and capital account transactions are freely convertible under the free-movement-of-capital provisions of Articles 63-66 TFEU and the German AWG/AWV framework; there are no exchange-control licences, prior approval requirements, or balance-of-payments quotas on profit remittances. Dividend, interest, royalty, and liquidation distributions from a German UG to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted from a currency perspective. The only practical remittance hurdles are fiscal and documentary: dividend distributions bear 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%) unless reduced by an applicable double-taxation treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for an EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 10%), or section 43b EStG; the UG must maintain its Transparenzregister UBO filing; and the distributing bank will require a valid certificate of residence/tax residency declaration from the foreign shareholder, plus evidence that the UG is tax-compliant (Freistellungsbescheinigung or Niederlassungsbestätigung mechanics). No monetary approval is required for the outward transfer itself.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export-proceeds surrender rules in Germany. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent (0%), and no timeline is imposed for repatriating export receipts into EUR; exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely in freely convertible accounts. The only binding obligation is statistical disclosure: every cross-border payment received by the UG (including export sales proceeds) of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for external-statistics purposes under section 67 AWV, filed electronically through the Bundesbank General Reporting Portal (Allgemeines Meldeportal) by the 5th working day of the month following the month of payment. The UG must retain the associated SWIFT MT103 or SEPA settlement message, the underlying commercial invoice, and a bank confirmation in order to evidence each filing.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank, competent for external economic transaction statistics under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), with the Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (BMWK) as external-trade policy authority and the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as the licensing and prudential supervisor for banks, payment institutions, and EMI passporting into Germany.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for non-EEA offshore EMIs and post-Brexit UK-authorised EMIs that have not obtained a German ZAG licence or a valid BaFin passport notification; such entities may not lawfully provide payment services to a German-resident UG, and an account held with them cannot serve as the statutory operational or capital account. Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-licensed EMIs (e.g., Lithuanian, Luxembourg, or Irish e-money institutions) that have completed the freedom-of-services or freedom-of-establishment notification to BaFin; these may lawfully execute treasury clearing, multi-currency settlements, and FX trades on behalf of the UG, but should be deployed as a secondary liquidity-overlay rail rather than the primary corporate account, because German tax authorities and tier-1 banks expect payroll tax remittances, corporate tax payments, and dividend distributions to flow through a German or EEA credit institution. All EMI balances must be fully segregated, audit-supported, and reconciled monthly in the UG's financial statements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from non-EU shareholders or via SEPA Credit Transfer from EU/EEA shareholders, with the remittance-information field (MT103 field 70) clearly reading 'Stammeinlage' or 'Kapitalrücklage' and never stated as goods, services, or loan repayment. The UG must ensure that the sending bank's sanctions screening is clean, retain the source-of-wealth file for the sender, and credit the funds to equity in the statutory accounts rather than to revenue. Where the injection is a cross-border payment of EUR 12,500 or more, the UG must file the AWV external-payments report with Deutsche Bundesbank by the 5th working day of the following month using the financial-account capital-contribution category in the Bundesbank's General Reporting Portal. If the contribution is received in USD or other foreign currency, the UG should either maintain a multi-currency sub-account or convert immediately at the banking counterparty's published rate, recording the exchange gain or loss separately. The notarised subscription record, bank statement, and Bundesbank confirmation together constitute the audit-ready compliance chain.EUR-denominated disbursements within the EU/EEA are executed through SEPA Credit Transfer, with SEPA Instant for urgent payments and TARGET2 for high-value single transactions; customer refunds are handled by reversing the original PSP settlement or issuing direct SEPA CT payments from the UG's IBAN. Non-EUR trade disbursements for solar-panel and battery-generator procurement from Asian suppliers are executed by SWIFT MT103 through the corporate bank's correspondent network, or, for large open-account-risk procurements above approximately EUR 250,000, by an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700) with a confirming bank in the supplier's jurisdiction. Each payment must carry an AWV-consistent purpose code (e.g., 'Warenlieferung, Ankauf von Waren'), be reconciled to the underlying import invoices, customs entries, and Incoterms contracts, and be continuously monitored against the bank's transaction-monitoring thresholds. Marketplaces and PSP rails settle to the UG's bank account on a T+2 to T+3 cycle for Stripe; BNPL settlement flows (Affirm BNPL is not licensed or operationally available in Germany, so the UG should onboard a BaFin-supervised local BNPL partner such as Klarna or Ratepay) remain third-party PSP funds until settlement and must be reconciled monthly against PSP reports, with segregated liquidity reserved for contingent refund liabilities.EUR/USD outright forward (Devisentermingeschäft) with the house banking counterparty, documented against forecast USD inventory-purchase exposures and capped at projected procurement volumes under a board-approved hedging policy.EUR/CNH Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) transacted offshore in CNH to hedge onshore CNY supplier exposure, permissible for a German non-financial counterparty where the instrument is demonstrably linked to the UG's CNY import-payable position and settled in EUR or USD.EUR/USD and EUR/CNH FX swaps (swap-against-spot) permitted to roll forward hedge positions and manage short-term settlement liquidity, provided each leg is traced to an identified trade-payable or trade-receivable exposure.Cross-currency swap (CCS) for longer-dated EUR/USD working-capital or shareholder-loan alignment, permissible only where the UG maintains an open USD balance-sheet position and complies with EMIR trade-reporting obligations, the non-cleared margin rules if the NFC clearing threshold is exceeded, and Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) registration.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for non-EEA offshore EMIs and post-Brexit UK-authorised EMIs that have not obtained a German ZAG licence or a valid BaFin passport notification; such entities may not lawfully provide payment services to a German-resident UG, and an account held with them cannot serve as the statutory operational or capital account. Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-licensed EMIs (e.g., Lithuanian, Luxembourg, or Irish e-money institutions) that have completed the freedom-of-services or freedom-of-establishment notification to BaFin; these may lawfully execute treasury clearing, multi-currency settlements, and FX trades on behalf of the UG, but should be deployed as a secondary liquidity-overlay rail rather than the primary corporate account, because German tax authorities and tier-1 banks expect payroll tax remittances, corporate tax payments, and dividend distributions to flow through a German or EEA credit institution. All EMI balances must be fully segregated, audit-supported, and reconciled monthly in the UG's financial statements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax (effective 15.825%), before municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) typically adding 7%–17% for a combined effective tax burden of approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer (withholding tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, producing an effective statutory withholding rate of 26.375% on cash distributions to shareholders. Domestic corporate shareholders may qualify for a full exemption under the participation privilege.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No de minimis monetary threshold applies; documentation obligations under Section 90(3) AO arise for all cross-border related-party transactions involving tangible goods, intangible property, financial transactions, or services. Tax authorities may require contemporaneous 'Local File' and 'Master File' documentation in a responsible and risk-proportionate manner.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as applied in certain offshore jurisdictions. However, substance requirements are enforced through the general anti-abuse provision under Section 42 AO, the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and Limitation on Benefits clauses in tax treaties, transfer pricing substance rules, Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) taxation, and the requirement that a UG maintain effective management, employees, and business premises in Germany to claim treaty benefits and resist attribution of income to non-resident shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 10 years under Section 370 AO in aggravated cases; fines of up to EUR 50,000 for standard evasion; additional surcharges up to 50% of the evaded tax; mandatory late-payment interest at 6% per annum.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for restitution of evaded tax if the misconduct was wilful or grossly negligent; assets may be seized, a travel ban imposed, and the director may be sentenced to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust income under Section 162 AO; a documentation surcharge of at least EUR 5,000 and up to 5% of the additional assessment may be issued; late-payment interest at 6% per annum accrues on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved false or incomplete transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for resulting additional taxes; criminal prosecution for aiding and abetting tax evasion may also be pursued.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under PPT or LOB clauses; reconstruction under Section 42 AO; refusal of the German tax authority to issue residence certificates; reattachment of income to the actual beneficial owner; CFC taxation of passive income in the hands of non-resident shareholders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax deficits triggered by piercing the corporate veil; civil restitution, travel bans, and disqualification from board positions are available; criminal liability arises if the shell structure is used to conceal taxable income.
AML Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering under Section 261 StGB of one to five years' imprisonment; additional regulatory enforcement by the Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for the breach of AML obligations; personal fines, travel bans, and monetary damages may be ordered, and directors who knowingly execute or facilitate prohibited transactions risk imprisonment.
