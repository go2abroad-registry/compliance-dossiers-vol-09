Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de); local tax office (Finanzamt) registration required.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must file Form 1 (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the responsible local Finanzamt, online through ELSTER or by paper; a German VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) is then issued by BZSt. The federal tax number and VAT ID are separate registrations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must file Form 1 (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the responsible local Finanzamt, online through ELSTER or by paper; a German VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) is then issued by BZSt. The federal tax number and VAT ID are separate registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists. Available group mechanisms include Organschaft (VAT and corporate tax consolidation), but no optional pass-through regime is available for GmbH; hybrid mismatch elections are applicable under the German ATAD implementation where cross-border hybrid structures exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold under § 2 UStG; however, small-business exemption under § 19 UStG applies for turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 in the preceding year and ≤ EUR 50,000 forecast in the current year. Foreign entities providing digital services to domestic consumers are subject to the EU One-Stop-Shop threshold of EUR 10,000 cross-border B2C distance sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) exports of goods with actual physical transport to a third-country destination and proper export evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis); (ii) directly connected services to exported goods, and certain services (e.g., intellectual property transfers) usable outside the EU; (iii) services supplied to a non-EU recipient under § 4 No. 1 UStG in conjunction with § 6 UStG, provided the recipient is clearly identifiable and the service is performed outside the EU or directly benefits exports. The B2B general rule requires the customer to be located outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung / IT-ATLAS electronic summary)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR / airway bill for non-maritime transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (convincing proof of payment from foreign customer)
  • Export confirmation from the exporting forwarder
  • Order and contract documents identifying the foreign customer
  • Transport evidence such as cargo manifest or carrier certificates

Germany does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. As of 2025, all businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD) for domestic B2B supplies; a phased mandatory issuance regime will apply from 2027. No API-based real-time tax clearance system is in force.

German tax authorities scrutinize export zero-rating claims under § 6 UStG and § 152 AO. Missing or falsified export evidence triggers full VAT reassessment plus interest (§ 233a AO). Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under §§ 370 ff. AO, carrying imprisonment of up to 10 years, fines up to EUR 500,000 (or more for corporate offenders), and permanent denial of export evidence validity. Where foreign exchange receipts are not repatriated within the statutory deadline (typically 180 days), a clawback plus late-payment penalties at 0.15% per month on the unpaid VAT is imposed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility as part of European Economic and Monetary Union. There are no general exchange controls, no licensing requirements for profit remittance, and no mandatory approval for dividend, royalty, or service fee outbound payments. Repatriation of profits by a German GmbH is unrestricted provided the company is solvent, the statutory balance sheet test is satisfied, withholding taxes are discharged, and the payor can support the payments with accounting and contractual evidence. Certain statistical reporting obligations to the Deutsche Bundesbank apply for cross-border payments exceeding legal thresholds, but these reporting duties do not constitute convertibility or remittance restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export proceeds surrender duty and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters lawfully earn and retain foreign currency proceeds in freely designated bank accounts, subject to standard account terms and anti-money-laundering documentary verification. As a reporting obligation, German residents must report cross-border payments and receivables from non-residents to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV reporting rules; in particular, individual payments to or from non-residents equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported for balance-of-payments statistics, and external assets and liabilities are reportable at defined reporting thresholds. No conversion of export proceeds into EUR is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank). Foreign exchange administrative oversight is exercised primarily under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), with prudential and anti-money-laundering supervision of banks handled by the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-authorized electronic money institutions passported into Germany; Low as the sole primary banking rail; not feasible for offshore non-EU digital money service providers. An authorized EU/EEA EMI can provide efficient inbound collection, FX conversion, and low-value disbursements, and may maintain a German IBAN through a principal bank. However, an EMI does not provide the full treasury functionality of a licensed commercial bank, such as commercial lending, documentary credit, cash pooling, and high-value settlement. Offshore EMIs not passported under the EU Electronic Money Directive and Payment Services Directive cannot lawfully perform payment services for a German resident GmbH without creating unauthorized payment institution activity regulated by BaFin under KWG/ZAG.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must enter the German GmbH through its designated bank account using SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA transfers or the German IBAN and the SEPA credit transfer framework for euro-area counterparties. The instructing bank must include the GmbH's full name, IBAN, BIC, and the remitter's complete data. The payment reference field must clearly identify the legal reason for payment, typically 'Kapitaleinlage' or 'Capital Contribution under the German Act on Limited Liability Companies', and must reference the notarized contribution resolution. The receiving bank will require supporting documents, including the notarial deed of incorporation or capital increase, the shareholder resolution, the list of subscribers, and evidence that the contribution is being paid in cash or in kind. For cross-border capital receipts equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, the German reporting obligation to the Deutsche Bundesbank must be observed. The bank will also conduct source-of-funds verification for the inbound amount and may request bank statements, tax records, and the commercial rationale underlying the capital transfer.Outbound trade settlements for the 3D art outsourcing business should be cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA foreign-currency payments and SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the EEA. For structured high-value procurement or milestone payments, a commercial letter of credit may be issued under SWIFT MT700 and governed by UCP600, enabling settlement through a conforming presentation of documents. Where immediate liquidity is required, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer can be used for low-value euro payments. PayPal should be used only as a supplementary collection rail for small non-commercial receivables and is not an appropriate primary treasury or trade settlement rail because of transaction ceilings, FX margin costs, and weak reconciliation controls. All outbound payments must be supported by invoices, delivery proof, licensing or asset-delivery records, and disbursement authorization minutes to satisfy both the bank's KYC expectations and German tax documentation requirements.
