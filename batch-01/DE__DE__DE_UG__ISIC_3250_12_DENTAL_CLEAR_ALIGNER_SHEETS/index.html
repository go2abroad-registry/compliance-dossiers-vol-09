Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG has no statutory minimum nominal capital; the articles may set the share capital at EUR 1.00 or higher. Each shareholder's cash contribution must be fully paid to the managing directors in Euro before registration. Non-cash contributions require a notarial valuation report and may require a deposit/escrow arrangement at a German bank until the Commercial Register entry. The UG must maintain a statutory retained reserve of at least 25% of annual net profit under § 5a GmbHG.

Funding & unlock path: Local fire safety authority (Feuerwehr) and building authority (Bauaufsicht) must certify structural and fire-safety compliance for the production unit; the employer must complete the DGUV occupational health check for plastics processing, register employees with the statutory accident insurer, and maintain a first-aid and evacuation plan. No special civil defense license apart from standard workplace clearance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT ID allocation; locally competent Finanzamt (state tax office) for tax registration and ongoing compliance; ELSTER (www.elster.de) as the national electronic tax portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities register with the locally competent Finanzamt via written application or ELSTER online to obtain a tax number (Steuernummer). The VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is issued by the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern after a separate application; proof of existence, place of business, and intended taxable activities must be submitted. Registration is generally completed within 1-4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities register with the locally competent Finanzamt via written application or ELSTER online to obtain a tax number (Steuernummer). The VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is issued by the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern after a separate application; proof of existence, place of business, and intended taxable activities must be submitted. Registration is generally completed within 1-4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime. The UG is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Election mechanisms exist for Organschaft (tax group consolidation for corporate and VAT purposes), and for partnerships to opt into corporate income tax. Hybrid mismatch adjustments apply under ATAD implementation, but no elective pass-through treatment is available for a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 (from 1 Jan 2025, previous-year turnover limit for the small-business exemption; previously EUR 22,000). Non-resident taxable persons are subject to mandatory VAT registration in Germany without regard to any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Section 4 No. 1 and Section 6 UStG for export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferung) where tangible goods, including aligner sheets, are delivered to a non-EU recipient and are physically transported from Germany into a third country. Certain processing services connected to exported goods (Ausfuhrbearbeitung) are also zero-rated when supplied to a non-EU entrepreneur. The exporter must possess conclusive documentary evidence of the export (Beleg- und Buchnachweis) and record the VAT ID or ERIC of the foreign customer on the invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Bankbestätigung über Zahlungseingang)

From 1 Jan 2025, all resident businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices conforming to Cen EN 16931; mandatory issuance of e-invoices for B2B domestic transactions is phased in by 1 Jan 2027. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory using XRechnung/ZUGFeRD. Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance, no API clearance stamping, and no digital stamping; VAT compliance is via periodic pre-reports (USt-Voranmeldungen) through ELSTER.

Under German tax law, the Finanzamt may retroactively claw back zero-rated export VAT where documentary evidence is insufficient or missing, resulting in a VAT assessment at 19% with late-payment interest (0.15% per month from 15 months after the event). Fraudulent or negligent zero-rating claims incur accuracy penalties of 5% of the underassessed VAT, additional penalty orders under Section 370 AO with fines or imprisonment, and full criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). No restrictions on profit remittance or capital repatriation. However, cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes. The Euro is the sole legal tender; no exchange controls exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency accounts and are free to hold or convert proceeds. Reporting obligations apply to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes when the value of export receipts exceeds €50,000 per transaction or €12,500 for other cross-border payments.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and FX regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Fintech EMIs (e.g., Stripe Treasury, Revolut Business, N26 Business) are licensed by BaFin and can provide multi-currency accounts, payment processing, and basic FX services. However, they may impose lower transaction limits, lack trade finance instruments (e.g., LC), and have stricter compliance for high-volume cross-border flows. For a UG with limited capital, they are viable for initial operations but not for large-scale trade settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825 percentage points) plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax). Trade tax is assessed at 3.5% of trade income multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%–490%; average combined effective CIT plus trade tax burden approximately 29%–32% for commercial operations).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding amount). This rate applies to dividends paid by a German UG to non-resident shareholders absent DTA or EU relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed by 31 July of the year following the fiscal year. When a tax advisor prepares the return, the ordinary extension automatically applies and the return must be submitted by 28/29 February of the second year following the fiscal year. Administrative assessments remain provisional after that date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German TP law under §90(4) AO does not set a strict monetary floor, but the Federal Ministry of Finance administrative guidelines set materiality thresholds: (i) full contemporaneous Local File with supporting evidence is required for related-party goods transactions with annual aggregate volume exceeding EUR 6,000,000; (ii) services and intangible-related transactions exceeding EUR 600,000 annually generally require contemporaneous Local File documentation; (iii) exceptional transactions above EUR 2,000,000 must also be documented; (iv) Master File is required where the group’s consolidated annual revenue exceeds approximately EUR 100,000,000; (v) Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to offshore centres. However, economic substance is mandatory for treaty entitlement and anti-abuse purposes. A UG must demonstrate real local management, physical presence, qualified personnel, business assets, and actual decision-making in Germany. Letterbox or shell arrangements are disregarded under §42 AO, §50d(3) EStG and the German General Anti-Abuse Rule; substance will be examined for transfer pricing adjustments, VAT place-of-supply rules, and DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in severe cases, plus the immediate payment of evaded tax, late interest at 6% per annum, monetary fines up to the amount of tax evaded, confiscation of profits, and publication penalties.

Director personal liability: Geschäftsführer and beneficial owners can be held personally criminally liable; civil directors’ liability under §43 GmbHG (discussed for UG analogically) entitles creditors to restitution; travel bans, seizure of passports, and pre-trial detention can be imposed during criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Letterbox Company

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate form does not automatically eliminate prosecution. Under §42 AO the tax advantage is denied and income may be reallocated to the actual managing entity; administrative fines up to EUR 25,000 may be imposed for sham structures; denial of VAT registration and the refusal of German tax identification numbers can occur.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes as managing agents of a disregarded letterbox entity; criminal liability can be imposed for tax evasion and falsification of books; travel restraints and administrative sanctions may extend to the shadow and de facto managers.
Transfer Pricing Infringement

Statutory sanction: Under §162(4) AO, if the tax office makes an upward transfer pricing adjustment exceeding documented amounts, a 5% surcharge of the adjustment is imposed; if required TP documentation is not provided, the surcharge increases to 15% of the adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000). Tax underpayments additionally incur late-payment interest of 6% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve distorted transfer prices or fail to ensure proper documentation may be personally liable for the surcharges and for the recovery of the additional tax; in aggravated cases, criminal tax evasion charges may be brought, leading to fines, imprisonment, and the personal restitution of tax losses.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines under GwG and EU AML Directives can reach up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons or 10% of annual worldwide turnover; natural-person officers face fines up to EUR 100,000; additionally, penalties for non-payment of income taxes derived from non-compliant payment flows may be assessed.

Director personal liability: The Managing Director can be personally fined and disqualified if AML controls are systematically absent; civil restitution obligations are enforceable against the UG and, upon insolvency, the director may be personally liable for excessive debt of the GmbH/UG; travel bans may be imposed in criminal probes.
