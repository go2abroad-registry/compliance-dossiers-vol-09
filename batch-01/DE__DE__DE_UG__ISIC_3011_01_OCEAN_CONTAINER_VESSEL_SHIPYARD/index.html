Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); local tax office (Finanzamt) at the company's place of management for corporate income tax, trade tax, and payroll tax registration; all filings conducted electronically via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders or managing directors must file the Official Tax Registration Questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt immediately after commercial registration and before commencing activity; the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (corporate and trade tax number) and confirms registration for Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, and Umsatzsteuer. The UG then applies separately to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. via ELSTER or paper form, providing the Handelsregister extract, trust register proof, and copy of the partnership/company agreement. Issuance typically takes 2 to 8 weeks after all formation documents are notarized and filed with the Commercial Register.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German law; the UG is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer at 7-17% combined effective rate). Available elections include: corporate tax group (Organschaft) under KStG Sec. 14-19 for a 95%+ resident subsidiary, allowing profit/loss pooling with the parent; VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under UStG Sec. 2 para. 2 No. 2, aggregating intra-group flows; and ATAD hybrid mismatch adjustments per EStG Sec. 4k disallowing deductions where double-non-taxation arises. No territorial or tax-exempt-zone elections apply to the UG.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for residents and non-residents. Germany imposes no exchange-control approval or forced-conversion regime; both current-account and capital-account flows are liberalized under EU free movement of capital (TFEU Article 63) and AWG Section 1. Dividend and profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted and subject only to the 25% statutory dividend withholding tax (treaty reduction possible under an applicable DTT). Practical hurdles are purely documentary: AWV Section 67 statistical reporting for cross-border payments at the EUR 12,500 threshold, plus the bank's creditor-identity duty and money-laundering due diligence. No blocked-funds, prior-approval, or non-resident quota constraints apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory retention timeline. USD-denominated proceeds of vessel installments may be retained in foreign currency or converted at treasury discretion. The only statutory duty is the AWV Section 67 statistical declaration to the Deutsche Bundesbank: each cross-border receipt or payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 (or FX equivalent) must be reported by the fifth business day of the month following settlement via the bank's electronic BalStat channel; EUR/USD conversion applies the ECB reference rate at settlement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank): central monetary authority and registry of foreign-trade and balance-of-payments statistics; BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority): licensing and AML supervision of credit institutions; BAFA (Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control): dual-use and export-control interface. Primary legal instruments: Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury operations. Offshore UK/Lithuanian digital EMIs lack a German banking license (KWG) and cannot hold statutory capital-escrow funds, open commercial letters of credit, or issue refund guarantees. A BaFin-licensed EMI under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may provide payment accounts but is prohibited from granting credit or performing contractual escrow duties. The EMI channel is acceptable only as a contingency foreign-currency float capped at 10% of treasury liquidity; all installment LC and escrow flows must run through a tier-1 German credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection must be executed by SWIFT MT103 with the ordering customer matching the shareholder of record and a purpose reference of 'Kapitalzuführung / Einlage' allocated to the beneficiary UG's IBAN; EEA-origin euro credits should use the TARGET2/SEPA rail for same-day value. AWV Section 67 reporting to the Bundesbank applies above EUR 12,500 within five business days of the following month. The receiving bank verifies the shareholder resolution (Gesellschafterbeschluss), Handelsregister admission, and source of funds; USD funds from third countries must pass through a tier-1 correspondent under a pre-notified KYC packet. Cash, third-party payers, and unallocated credits are rejected. Funds are booked on the Capital Escrow Account with the explicit contract and project reference; movement to the general treasury occurs only against invoice and milestone releases.Euro-EEA disbursements use SEPA Credit Transfer/TARGET2; non-euro and extra-EEA payments use SWIFT MT103 with MT202 COV correspondent refinancing through CHIPS/FEDWIRE USD clearing. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge -> 15.825%) corporate income tax; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) levied at municipality-specific rates (7%–17%) resulting in a typical combined effective tax rate of 30%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, resulting in 26.375% domestic withholding tax on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders. DTA participation exemptions may apply (e.g., EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 0% for qualifying holdings of at least 10% held for 12 months; many DTAs allow 5% for ≥10% shareholders, 15% otherwise).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: aggregate cross-border controlled transactions with related parties exceeding €1,500,000 per fiscal year. Master File: annual consolidated group revenue exceeding €100,000,000. Transactions with a German permanent establishment are treated as related-party transactions. Substance and functional analysis must be contemporaneously documented.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but applies the general anti-abuse rule (§ 42 AO), the OECD Principal Purpose Test in DTAs, and the EU ATAD rules. For the UG to enjoy treaty benefits and deduct intra-group payments, it must have actual business premises, personnel, books, and decision-making in Germany; a mere letterbox entity or conduit without substance will be disregarded, transactions recharacterized, and tax benefits denied. Shipyards engaged in cross-border vessel construction must demonstrate operational substance and real execution of shipbuilding contracts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in especially severe cases under § 370 AO; criminal fines up to €50,000; full evaded tax remains payable with 6% annual interest; statute of limitations extended to 15 years in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers are personally liable for unpaid tax under § 69 AO and may be subjected to civil restitution, asset seizures, travel bans, and extradition requests. Criminal conviction results in director disqualification and potential imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Independent tax assessment under § 162 AO may impose a penalty equal to 6% of the net transfer pricing adjustment, with a minimum of €5,000 and a maximum of €250,000 for missing or materially inadequate documentation; substantial adjustments also attract 6% annual late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length contracts are exposed to § 69 AO liability if the UG cannot meet its tax liabilities; deliberate or grossly negligent failure to document can be prosecuted as tax evasion, leading to fines, imprisonment, and travel bans.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny treaty benefits and recharacterize the transactions under § 42 AO; the taxable substance will be imputed to the true economic owner, with an anti-abuse penalty of up to 30% of the tax benefit; VAT input deduction may be refused; EU anti-shell rules, once applicable, may impose a 25% annual shell entity tax cost.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell UG face personal liability for all denied credits and taxes; insolvency-triggered claims lead to directors' personal assets being pursued; criminal fraud and aiding tax evasion charges may carry imprisonment and travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Compliance (GwG / StGB)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under § 56 GwG up to €5 million for legal entities or 10% of annual worldwide group turnover, whichever is greater; individuals may be fined up to €500,000; intentional money laundering under § 261 StGB is punishable by imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years; asset freezes and business prohibitions apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to personal criminal prosecution, disqualification from management, and civil liability for damages to business partners; travel bans and extradition are common where laundering of shipbuilding proceeds is proven; the corporate veil cannot shield personal criminal conduct.
