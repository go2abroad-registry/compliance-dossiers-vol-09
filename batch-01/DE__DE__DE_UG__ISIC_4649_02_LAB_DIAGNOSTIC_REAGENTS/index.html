UG requires a statutory nominal capital of at least EUR 1.00; before registration, at least 25% of each cash contribution must be paid in and deposited on a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) in the company's name, with funds remaining at the free disposal of the managing director only after registration; a sole-founder UG must also provide security for the unpaid part of the contribution (para. 7(2) GmbHG). Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for EU VAT ID; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate and trade tax registration; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (Elster/Mein ELSTER).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German branch or participating in German taxable supplies must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt. This triggers issuance of a German tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income, trade tax, and VAT prepayment purposes. For cross-border EU transactions, the entity must separately apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the relevant application form. The process is electronic via ELSTER, with identity verified through accepted authentication credentials.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a German branch or participating in German taxable supplies must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt. This triggers issuance of a German tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income, trade tax, and VAT prepayment purposes. For cross-border EU transactions, the entity must separately apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the relevant application form. The process is electronic via ELSTER, with identity verified through accepted authentication credentials.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election for UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) or GmbH. The entity is in all cases a tax resident corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (15%) plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax. Elections available: formation of a fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Sec. 14 KStG and Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG if financial, economic, and organisational integration exists; VAT grouping under Sec. 2a UStG; small business election under Sec. 19 UStG only for domestic-established entrepreneurs below revenue thresholds (not generally available to foreign entrepreneurs).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for non-resident/foreign entities; any taxable supply of goods or services in Germany by a foreign entrepreneur triggers mandatory VAT registration. For German-established small businesses, the Sec. 19 UStG small business exemption applies only if prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim the 0% VAT rate (exemption with input VAT deduction) under Sec. 4 No. 1(a) and Sec. 6 UStG, the supplier must prove that the goods were physically transported to a third-country destination (outside the EU) or, for services, that the recipient is an entrepreneur with a place of business outside Germany and the place of supply is located in that third country. For goods, the supplying entrepreneur must be the exporter (or the exporter's agent) and must retain documentary and logistical evidence of export; consignment or delivery to a non-EU recipient qualifies only if the export is actually accomplished and the goods leave the EU in the name of the supplier or the customer under customs supervision.

  • Electronic Export Customs Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS) with customs acceptance reference
  • Customs Export Proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) confirming physical exit from the EU, including MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Commercial invoice (pro-forma/final) describing goods, parties, and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (increasingly electronic) evidencing transport to third country
  • Carrier's export intimation and consolidated shipping documents (booking confirmation, packing list)

Germany has phased B2B e-invoicing mandate from 1 January 2025: every business must be able to receive structured electronic invoices. From 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. Compliant formats are XRechnung and ZUGFeRD (or EDI). Transmission occurs via established exchange networks such as Peppol; no real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority API stamping, or digital stamping applies. VAT declarations and records are submitted electronically via ELSTER.

Fraudulent or insufficiently substantiated export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment, disallowance of input VAT deduction, and imposition of interest under Sec. 233a AO (0.5% per month on assessed VAT arrears). German fiscal authorities may apply penalty interest, and tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO carries imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years in especially severe cases) or fines; negligent tax shortfalls are fined under Sec. 378 AO. Assessment periods extend to 7 years where evasion is found, and civil/criminal liability affects responsible officers. The export documents must be retained for at least 10 years in accordance with Sec. 147 AO.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, prudential bank checks, and transaction-reporting obligations. A German UG may generally receive foreign-currency funds, convert them through an authorized bank, pay foreign suppliers, distribute profits, and remit dividends or liquidation proceeds to non-resident shareholders without a general exchange-control approval. Dividend distributions require distributable profits, proper annual accounts, shareholder approval, preservation of statutory reserves, and compliance with German withholding-tax rules. The standard German dividend withholding tax is 25 percent plus the solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty relief, European Union parent-subsidiary relief, or refund procedures where applicable. Interest, royalties, service fees, and related-party payments may require transfer-pricing support, beneficial-owner verification, withholding-tax analysis, and evidence of business substance. Non-resident shareholder loans and capital contributions should be documented through shareholder resolutions, executed agreements, bank records, and accounting entries. German banks may apply enhanced review to high-risk countries, unusual payment chains, third-party funding, virtual-asset exposure, sanctioned parties, or products subject to medical-device and dual-use controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a bank, convert export receipts into euros, or complete conversion within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts, subject to ordinary accounting, tax, sanctions, AML, and trade-documentation requirements. Exporters and importers may have statistical reporting obligations to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV, including certain cross-border payment, balance-of-payments, external-position, and foreign-claims reports. Reporting thresholds, forms, deadlines, and exemptions depend on the transaction category and are subject to change; the company should confirm current requirements with its bank and Deutsche Bundesbank before implementation. Intra-EU goods movements are generally reported through Intrastat where applicable, while customs declarations and export evidence apply to movements outside the European Union. No mandatory conversion ratio or statutory export-receipt surrender timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Federal financial-services supervision is principally conducted by the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin). Foreign-exchange controls are primarily governed by European Union law, the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), sanctions regulations, anti-money-laundering legislation, and statistical reporting rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment execution and foreign-currency collection, but Low to Medium as a complete substitute for a German commercial-bank relationship. A European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may passport services into Germany or provide SEPA and international payment services, subject to its home-state authorization, passporting status, safeguarding model, AML controls, sanctions screening, and account terms. Electronic-money balances are safeguarded and are not equivalent to deposits covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme. An offshore provider outside the European Economic Area generally cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or deposit services to German customers without an applicable German or European authorization or an appropriately authorized intermediary. Fintech onboarding may be unsuitable for statutory capital-confirmation letters, cash-intensive activity, trade finance, complex shareholder funding, documentary collections, or regulated medical-device supply chains. The company should maintain at least one German or European Economic Area bank account for payroll, tax, statutory payments, capital verification, and contingency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital contributions through a bank account held in the legal name of the contributing shareholder or approved funding entity into the UG's dedicated or operational account. Use a clear payment reference such as 'share capital contribution', 'shareholder capital contribution', or 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]'; do not use ambiguous descriptions or personal accounts. Before remittance, provide the bank with the executed articles, shareholder resolution, capital-contribution or loan agreement, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, and expected amount and currency. The bank should verify the remitter, beneficial owner, purpose, sanctions status, and source of funds. For a pre-registration UG capital contribution, obtain the notary's and bank's required account and confirmation procedures; after registration, record the contribution in the accounting ledger and reconcile it to the shareholder resolution. Convert foreign currency through the bank at an agreed rate or retain it in an approved foreign-currency account. Germany has no general FX purpose-code regime comparable to controlled-currency jurisdictions, but banks may request internal purpose classifications, and Deutsche Bundesbank statistical reports may apply under the AWV. Preserve SWIFT or ISO 20022 payment messages, bank confirmations, exchange-rate records, and accounting reconciliations.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and bank wire transfers for non-SEPA or foreign-currency settlements. Electronic-money balances are safeguarded and are not equivalent to deposits covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme. An offshore provider outside the European Economic Area generally cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or deposit services to German customers without an applicable German or European authorization or an appropriately authorized intermediary. Fintech onboarding may be unsuitable for statutory capital-confirmation letters, cash-intensive activity, trade finance, complex shareholder funding, documentary collections, or regulated medical-device supply chains.
