Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office – responsible for VAT identification numbers and central tax matters; local Finanzamt (tax office) responsible for tax registration, assessment and audits. Official filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

The UG must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the locally competent Finanzamt, typically within one month of incorporation. The questionnaire is filed electronically via ELSTER and yields the statutory tax number (Steuernummer). If the UG makes intra-Community acquisitions, supplies or exports, it must separately obtain a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) from BZSt using the 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr.' form. For customs export formalities the UG must also obtain an EORI customs registration number from the Generalzolldirektion.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German private limited company with separate legal personality and full tax personality. It is subject to German corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT as a taxable person under §2 UStG. It is not a pass-through entity and has no automatic US-style check-the-box eligibility. — The UG must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the locally competent Finanzamt, typically within one month of incorporation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German private limited company with separate legal personality and full tax personality. It is subject to German corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT as a taxable person under §2 UStG. It is not a pass-through entity and has no automatic US-style check-the-box eligibility.

Available election forms

German law provides no US check-the-box or general pass-through election for a UG. Available elections include: (1) small-business exemption under §19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung), subject to turnover limits of EUR 25,000 prior year and EUR 100,000 current year; (2) VAT accounting on a cash-received basis under §20 UStG (Ist-Besteuerung) instead of invoiced basis; (3) VAT corporate group/fiscal unity ('Organschaft') under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG combined with profit-and-loss transfer agreement; (4) tax group for corporate income tax/trade tax purposes; (5) no hybrid mismatch election, and anti-hybrid rules under §4k EStG apply to negative hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary registration threshold exists for a German-resident UG: every taxable entrepreneur must register for VAT once economic activity begins. The small-business exemption under §19 UStG applies only if previous-year turnover is ≤ EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is ≤ EUR 100,000. A foreign entity must register for German VAT when it makes taxable supplies in Germany, regardless of turnover volume; for intra-Community distance sales of goods, the EU-wide distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 per calendar year applies before an OSS election or destination-country registration is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of freeze-dried pet raw meat to customers outside the European Union are VAT-free with full input VAT deduction under §4 Nr. 1 and §6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave Germany and the customs territory of the EU. The exporter must prove export through the ATLAS electronic customs system and retain transport and commercial evidence. Intra-Community supplies to EU customers are likewise exempt under §4 Nr. 1 and §6a UStG if the customer has a valid EU VAT ID and the goods reach the customer in another Member State, substantiated by a Gelangensbestätigung. Offshore services that fall outside Germany under place-of-supply rules can also qualify for exemption under §4 Nr. 3 UStG. The exemption is not automatic: the tax office may demand contemporaneous documentation.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) and export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing transport out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Intra-Community supply proof (Gelangensbestätigung) where the customer is in another EU Member State
  • EORI number and, when requested, customs exit confirmation from the border customs office

Germany has a phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing regime under the E-Rechnungsgesetz. From 1 January 2025, every German-resident entrepreneur must be able to receive structured e-invoices in the formats XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x or EDI. From 1 January 2027, suppliers with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all suppliers must issue e-invoices. There is no real-time tax authority clearance, API mandate or mandatory digital stamping for ordinary B2B invoices, but cash-register software must use a certified technical security element and comply with GoBD archiving requirements.

The Finanzamt may audit export evidence after the claim is made. Where zero-rating is not substantiated by the required export documentation, the supply is treated as taxable in Germany at the standard 19% VAT rate; input VAT previously deducted may be clawed back and interest under §233a AO accrues at 0.5% per month. Negligent or intentional under-assessment can trigger administrative fines under §378 AO and criminal tax evasion penalties under §370 AO, including imprisonment for up to 10 years in aggravated cases. False customs declarations may additionally attract penalties under the Union Customs Code, and retrospective assessment periods extend to 7 years or longer in fraud situations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a member of the Eurozone and the EU, with full capital and current account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). No restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders, subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and tax compliance. Reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 (AWV §67) and for direct investment positions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments and transactions must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes if they exceed €12,500, and the reporting must be filed within 5 working days of the transaction (AWV §67). No minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision and the Federal Ministry of Finance for exchange control policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, N26, or Solarisbank can provide cross-border payment services and virtual IBANs. However, for a manufacturing entity with significant trade volumes and LC requirements, traditional banks are preferred. EMIs may face limitations on high-value transactions, trade finance instruments, and physical branch support. Legal constraints: EMIs must hold a BaFin license under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and are subject to strict AML/KYC obligations. They cannot offer credit or LC services directly.

Aggregate statutory burden for a German UG: approximately 29.8%-32.0% of pre-tax profit.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

By 31 July of the calendar year following the tax year (FY 2025 return due 31 July 2026). Where a tax advisor (Steuerberater) is engaged, the deadline is automatically extended to 28 February of the second year following the tax year (FY 2025 return due 28 February 2027). Assessment interest under Sec. 233a AO accrues from the 15th month after the end of the tax year irrespective of the extension.
