Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Finanzamt (locally competent tax office) for income, trade, and VAT assessments; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, www.bzst.de; filing portal ELSTER at www.elster.de) for the USt-IdNr. and cross-border VAT-ID confirmation; Generalzolldirektion / Hauptzollamt (www.zoll.de) for EORI, AEO status, and customs transit operations (NCTS/ATLAS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Register the UG in the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht; commercial freight operation additionally requires a Community licence under § 3 GüKG (Erlaubnis). Step 2 – Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the locally competent Finanzamt within four weeks of commencing business; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (operative TIN for all returns and ELSTER filings). Step 3 – After assignment of the Steuernummer, apply via ELSTER/BOP to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under § 27a UStG, which is mandatory for cross-border invoicing and EC sales lists. Step 4 – Register for an EORI number (DE prefix) with the Hauptzollamt before any customs declaration is presented. Step 5 – Apply for AEO-C status (Authorised Economic Operator – Customs Simplifications) and for authorisation as Authorised Consignor/Authorised Consignee under Articles 233–234 UCC to open and close T1/NCTS transit movements with the Generalzolldirektion. A UG has no minimum capital requirement but must retain 25% of annual surplus until the EUR 25,000 share capital threshold for conversion into a GmbH is reached.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG is a German capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with full German tax residency (registered office and/or place of effective management); it is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 0.825%, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate of roughly 11.9%–17.15% depending on the municipal multiplier (Hebesatz), producing an aggregate effective burden of approximately 30%–33%. The UG is not pass-through eligible; no territorial exemption applies and worldwide taxable income is assessed on an entity-level basis with dividend distributions subject to 25% capital gains tax/withholding (Abgeltungsteuer) at the shareholder level.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§ 14–19 KStG and § 2(2) Nr. 2 UStG, requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and financial, economic, and organisational integration under a German controlling entity; this permits VAT and corporate/trade tax consolidation for the bonded trucking group; (ii) small-business exemption under § 19 UStG (turnover below EUR 25,000) — electable but commercially inadvisable for a B2B carrier needing full input VAT recovery; (iii) option under § 9 UStG to waive VAT exemptions on real-estate supplies to recover input VAT; (iv) permanent-establishment trade tax apportionment under § 29 GewStG; and (v) ATAD/ATAD-2 hybrid mismatch limitations under § 4k EStG, which disallow double-deduction and deduction/non-inclusion outcomes. No entity pass-through election exists for a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual turnover (2025 threshold under § 19 UStG for the optional small-business (Kleinunternehmer) exemption; EUR 22,000 for 2024 and earlier). The UG operating a bonded road-feeder fleet is, however, a compulsory VAT registrant regardless of turnover: registration is triggered by the mere commencement of entrepreneurial activity (Gewerbeanmeldung), by the filing of mandatory VAT returns, and by any intra-community acquisition (annually exceeding EUR 12,500) or receipt of services under reverse charge (§ 13b UStG). No registration threshold applies to non-resident suppliers; the forward-charge/reverse-charge mechanism mandates national registration for the UG as supplier of freight services taxable in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with input VAT credit) is available under § 4 Nr. 3 UStG for (a) cross-border carriage of goods, including loading, unloading, and handling, performed over German route sections, and (b) ancillary services directly connected to the import or export of goods. The bonded road-feeder leg from a German international airport to a customs warehouse (Zollager) or directly to the border qualifies for zero-rating where the goods travel under the external Community transit procedure (T1/NCTS with a valid MRN) as part of a third-country movement, or where the truck transports export goods already released for export under § 6 UStG. Services on purely domestic legs (airport to a domestic non-bonded warehouse) are standard-rated at 19% unless the recipient is a taxable person outside Germany (reverse charge under § 13b UStG) or the service is documented as directly connected to export/import. The carrier must positively prove the transport nexus to the export/import of goods; burden of proof lies with the entrepreneur. Germany imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects documentary-credit practice and the customary audit expectation that consideration for zero-rated export services be received within six months.

  • Customs export declaration (ATLAS/AES Ausfuhranmeldung) or T1/NCTS transit declaration containing the Movement Reference Number (MRN), with customs warehouse arrival/departure records (Zollagerverfahren) for bonded movements
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (MAWB/HAWB) covering the underlying international carriage leg
  • CMR consignment note for the German bonded road leg showing customs office of departure and destination, weight, seals, and date stamps
  • Proof of export status per §§ 8–10 UStDV matching goods codes, quantities, weights, and dates between the customs declaration and the truck manifest/trailer list
  • Payment evidence — SWIFT MT103 or bank account statement (Kontoauszug) demonstrating receipt of the foreign consideration; Germany does not issue a formal Inward Remittance Certificate, and the bank statement is the statutory-recognised equivalent

Mandatory German B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) applies from 1 January 2025: every entrepreneur must be able to RECEIVE structured electronic invoices (XRechnung XML, ZUGFeRD 2.x hybrid PDF/A-3+XML, or EDI formats accepted by the recipient) under § 14 UStG. Issuance obligations are phased: from 1 January 2027, businesses with turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 in the preceding calendar year must ISSUE e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all businesses. Germany operates NO real-time fiscal clearance system, no SdI-style central exchange API, and no digital stamping or crypto-stamp mandate. Distinctive digital obligations are limited to: (i) BZSt VAT-ID validation and confirmation (§ 18e UStG via VIES/ELSTER); (ii) mandatory electronic transmission of all VAT returns, EC sales lists, and recapitulative statements through ELSTER with authenticated signature; and (iii) NCTS/ATLAS electronic movement and declaration processing (MRN routing) for all bonded and transit movements.

The Finanzamt and Generalzolldirektion apply a strict substantive-nexus review; zero-rating is clawed back retrospectively if the carrier cannot substantiate the physical export, the transit integrity, or the direct connection of the service to export/import. Contrived or fictitious export arrangements (Scheinausfuhren) constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment from six months to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases), plus VAT reassessment, 6% per annum default interest (§ 233a AO), late-filing surcharges (§ 152 AO), forfeiture of goods, loss of input VAT recovery, revocation of AEO-C status and freight licences, and personal personal-liability claims against UG managing directors under §§ 34 and 69 AO for taxes not paid from company funds. Customs-specific offences under § 382 AO (Zollhinterziehung) carry additional fines and custodial penalties. BFH jurisprudence requires stringent positive evidence for indirect exports via bonded carriers; reasonable doubt by the auditor shifts the burden entirely onto the UG, making the pre-emptive documentation bundle mandatory.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible in both current and capital accounts. Germany applies the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and payments (TFEU Arts. 63–66) with no substantive convertibility restrictions. Profit remittances by a UG holding statutory retained earnings may proceed freely via bank transfer after legal formalities (shareholder resolution and compliance with any thin capitalization or transfer pricing documentation under the German Foreign Tax Act), subject to withholding tax on dividends where applicable under tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No bureaucratic approval for outward remittance is required unless money laundering or sanctions concerns arise at the banking intermediary. Germany maintains an open investment regime with no surrender obligation on export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in Germany. Exporters may freely hold, transfer, or convert export receipts into Euro or foreign currencies. The sole compliance requirement is the Bundesbank's external statistics reporting: payments received from non-residents exceeding €12,500 (or the equivalent in foreign currency) per transaction or aggregated within a calendar month must be reported on the 'Z1 form' (Verbundmeldung) or electronically via the Bundesbank's external payments reporting portal, ordinarily within seven days of the transaction or month-end. For customs-bonded trucking services rendered to non-resident airlines and cargo handlers, each cross-border freight billing transaction must be captured for statistical reporting, but no surrender or conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and statistical monetary authority); primary lead for foreign exchange statistics and balance-of-payments reporting. Administrative enforcement of any residual exchange restrictions is shared with the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for tax-related cross-border payments and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) for foreign trade and investment screening under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for an EU/EEA regulated EMI only. A UG with a legitimate customs-bonded logistics business may use a licensed EU EMI (e.g., payments institution with a German BaFin license or passport) for disbursements, settlement of cross-border fees, and payroll; however, German commercial banks offering credit lines, escrow accounts, and SWIFT correspondent rails remain the dominant and least legal-reputational-risk choice for a customs bond operator. Offshore, unlicensed EMIs or cryptocurrency-linked payment intermediaries operating without MiFID/PSD2 authorization are legally excluded from holding client funds or providing payment accounts in Germany, and their use would trigger severe AML red flags at correspondent banks. Any such EMI must be legally identifiable as a licensed payment institution under § 1 Abs. 1 ZAG (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz) and must comply with the EBA's passported conduct-of-business regime.

Defense evidence: Compilation of a trade counter-party compliance file containing: the bonded warehouse license held by the truck entity (Bundeszollverwaltung authorisation), valid freight operator license, and CMR liabilities insurance certificate; annual compliance certificate of the counterparty's customs-agent qualification; no pending criminal tax or customs investigations declarations; and a documented internal trade compliance policy with periodic screening of counterparties against EU anti-money laundering and customs provisions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders to the UG's German operational or escrow account are conducted exclusively through SWIFT wire channels using the international payment purpose code appropriate to direct equity injection without creating a loan relationship. For a cash capital contribution to an Unternehmergesellschaft (UG), the shareholder remits from a bank account in their name and the SWIFT double-format payment message (MT103) must contain the business-payment type 'CAPC' or EU-wide operational R-Type 'D010' as the regulatory purpose code, referencing the notarial deed of the capital increase and the registered capital amount. The payment must arrive to an inbound 'nonresident capital transfer' code; the receiving German bank may request Form Z5 (Report of Inbound Capital) or schedule such transaction to the Bundesbank's balance-of-payments statistics automatically. To avoid a false positive as a loan, the MT103 field 70 must clearly state 'Einzahlung auf das Stammkapital' (payment of subscribed capital) and include the UG's commercial register number and the shareholder's unique tax reference. Wire must be denominated in EUR to the UG's Euro account; currencies other than EUR require conversion at the remitting bank or via a resident foreign-exchange provider, and the UG's German bank will reinforce the purpose code 'C01 – Investment in resident enterprise'. Verification of the inbound transfer is finished only when the weekly bank confirmation and the temporary capital contribution vouchers (Sondervermögen-Einlagen) are produced, and the UG must complete the capital maintenance ledger to permit onward operational disbursements without triggering lender-liability concerns.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements of the customs-bonded truck fleet — including rental invoices, fuel procurement, airport handling charges, foreign airline settlements, and maintenance payments — are executed over the three standard clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer for all euro-denominated payments to German/EU counterparties, using IBAN and purpose code CRESP or CSDB; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments in USD, GBP, CHF or third-country non-euro transactions, with adequate cover and compliance codes; and (iii) commercial letters of credit (L/C) for high-value or documentary-unfriendly counterparties, typically issued at the UG's request by Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank as an unconfirmed L/C, or a confirmed sight L/C for transactions with non-OECD counterparties. The MT103 messages must contain a full compliance chain, including the ordering-to-beneficiary names, address, purpose codes, and references to the underlying bonded-transport contract; for amounts above €50,000 the German bank will require customs transport authorization and manifest references. All disbursements are recorded in the UG's statutory accounting ledger; no capturable exchange-control approval is needed for trade payments because Germany imposes no requirement to surrender or convert trade-related foreign currencies.Plain-vanilla FX forward contracts (devisentermingeschäfte) with a licensed German bank or EU credit institution under CRR/CRD IV, permitted for hedging the contractually expected foreign-currency receivables (primarily USD) of the customs-bonded trucking operation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where the underlying reference currency is a non-convertible emerging-market currency (e.g., CNY, INR, BRL) and the UG has a verified exposure to that currency; NDFs are permissible for hedging but must be executed outside the European CCP or through a German broker to comply with MiFID II client-categorisation rules.Currency swaps (cross-currency interest rate swaps) used solely to align an existing foreign-currency liability with the EUR functional currency, subject to EMIR clearing or risk-reduction obligations.FX contracts for difference (CFDs) on major currency pairs, only when executed on a regulated German or EU trading venue and with an investment firm authorized under MiFID II; use is strictly limited to hedging, not speculative positions, to avoid breaching the UG's corporate treasury policy.Options (FX call/CX put) on USD/EUR with physical settlement, purchased from a Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank treasury desk, as permitted derivate for hedging future wholesale fuel or aircraft-loading service cost fluctuations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for an EU/EEA regulated EMI only. A UG with a legitimate customs-bonded logistics business may use a licensed EU EMI (e.g., payments institution with a German BaFin license or passport) for disbursements, settlement of cross-border fees, and payroll; however, German commercial banks offering credit lines, escrow accounts, and SWIFT correspondent rails remain the dominant and least legal-reputational-risk choice for a customs bond operator. Offshore, unlicensed EMIs or cryptocurrency-linked payment intermediaries operating without MiFID/PSD2 authorization are legally excluded from holding client funds or providing payment accounts in Germany, and their use would trigger severe AML red flags at correspondent banks. Any such EMI must be legally identifiable as a licensed payment institution under § 1 Abs. 1 ZAG (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz) and must comply with the EBA's passported conduct-of-business regime.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 0.825% Solidaritätszuschlag (aggregate 15.825% entity-level CIT); municipal Gewerbesteuer at an average Hebesatz of 400% adds approximately 14%, producing a combined effective corporate burden of about 29.8–31.5% depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% aggregate statutory dividend WHT, comprising 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the 25% (an additional 0.375 percentage points).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing due on 31 July of the year following the tax year; automatic extension to 30 September where the return is transmitted electronically; when a tax advisor is mandated, Section 149 (3) AO extends the deadline to 28/29 February of the second year following the tax year (e.g., FY 2024 return due 28 February 2026).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Local File documentation is mandatory when annual cross-border intra-group transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 on the revenue side or EUR 500,000 on the expense side; Master File is required when annual consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Below these thresholds the Finanzamt may nevertheless demand documentation under Section 90 (3) AO where specific facts indicate non-arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply an ESR-style filing regime for ordinary UGs, but formal substance requirements arise from Section 42 AO (general anti-abuse), the Principal Purpose Test under the MLI, the German CFC rules in Sections 7–13 AStG, and the anti-tax-avoidance directive (ATAD) minimum substance standard. A UG engaged as a GPS-sealed bonded road feeder operator must maintain business premises, employed drivers, fleet records, accounting functions and management decision-making in Germany; insufficiency of substance can lead to retroactive taxation, denial of treaty relief, re-qualification as a sham arrangement, and the immediate loss of the customs-bonded licence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung, Section 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years or a fine; in especially severe cases (commercial-scale evasion, typically exceeding EUR 50,000 per year) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years; back taxes plus default interest of 6% p.a.; confiscation of the proceeds of evasion; publication of the conviction in trade registers.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is personally exposed: criminal conviction, joint and several civil restitution under Section 71 AO, disqualification from acting as a director under Section 6 (2) No. 3e GewO, passport and travel-ban orders under Section 36a AO, and seizure of trucks, bank accounts and real estate as proceeds of crime under Section 73 StGB.
Hidden profit distribution (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung, Section 8 (3) KStG)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the excess payment as a dividend: 26.375% WHT payable by the UG, denial of the operating-expense deduction at the UG level, trade tax re-assessments, default interest and late payment surcharges; the total additional tax burden typically reaches 36% of the disguised distribution value.

Director personal liability: The shareholder-director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid WHT; criminal exposure under Section 370 AO arises if the concealed benefit is omitted from the tax return; liability to the UG exists under Section 43 GmbHG; insolvency claw-back of payments to the shareholder is available for five years under Section 135 InsO.
Embezzlement of wage taxes and social security contributions (Section 266a StGB)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years for each offence; commercial or long-term systematic abuse is treated as aggravated fraud with up to 10 years; confiscation of evaded sums; cancellation of the freight transport licence (Güterkraftverkehrsgenehmigung) and loss of customs AEO status.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally jointly and severally liable for the full unpaid wage tax and social security contributions, including employer shares, irrespective of corporate insolvency proceedings; Section 14 (1) No. 1 StGB directly attributes criminal liability to the director; this is the most frequent route to private-asset seizure and imprisonment for small transport companies.
AML and beneficial ownership non-compliance (Geldwäschegesetz - GwG)

Statutory sanction: Fines for legal entities of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual worldwide turnover under Section 56 GwG; publication of the compliance violation; revocation of the freight forwarder licence and customs authorization for repeated breaches; court-ordered dissolution of the UG for grossly improper management.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer acting as AML responsible officer is subject to direct fines of up to EUR 100,000 per negligent breach and may be prosecuted under Section 261 StGB for money laundering if undisclosed cash proceeds from bonded trucking are proven; the director faces personal asset recovery, disqualification from holding management office for up to five years, and, in cases of organized logistics-based laundering, imprisonment.
