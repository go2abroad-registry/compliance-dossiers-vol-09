Competent corporate registry

The Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the locally competent district court (Amtsgericht), acting through the relevant register division.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), including Sections 2 (notarial articles), 3 (articles’ contents), 5a (UG requirements and statutory reserve), 7 (capital contributions and registration), 8 (registration filing), and 10 (entry in the Commercial Register). Commercial Register procedure is governed by the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRV).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for first registration of a limited liability company in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister), electronically filed in publicly certified form by the managing director through a German notary.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The entire stated capital must be paid in cash before Commercial Register registration; contributions in kind are not permitted at formation. The GmbHG does not require an escrow account. A company bank account is commonly opened after notarization, and capital is deposited into it before the registration filing. The UG must allocate one quarter of its annual net profit, after any loss carryforward, to its statutory reserve under Section 5a(3) GmbHG. Registry, notarial, translation, and bank charges are separately payable; there is no fixed official fee denominated in USD.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupancy or process changes, confirm permitted building use and obtain any required building or change-of-use approval from the local building authority. Comply with applicable fire-protection requirements and any inspections or certificates imposed by the building permit or local fire authority; there is no universal standalone fire certificate for every workshop. Assess floor and equipment loads where relevant. The employer must meet German occupational-safety duties, including risk assessments, machinery and chemical controls, workplace requirements, and statutory accident-insurance registration; no general workplace clearance certificate applies to every operation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office) for German VAT identification numbers; local Finanzämter for corporate and trade tax registration; official portal ELSTER at www.elster.de and BZSt at www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

The UG must first complete commercial register registration and then submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung to the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER, obtaining the Steuernummer. For cross-border and EU transactions, the UG separately applies to BZSt under Section 27a UStG for a USt-IdNr. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment or fixed place of business in Germany apply directly to BZSt, supplying a certified commercial register extract or equivalent proof of legal existence, a completed VAT registration questionnaire, and evidence of the foreign owner’s identity and authorised signatories; BZSt then issues the German VAT ID, which must be quoted on all Etsy/Stripe invoices, EU sales lists, and customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election is available for a German UG; it is automatically a capital company taxed at entity level on corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. Available elections include: (i) VAT Organschaft by fiscal integration with majority-owned German group companies; (ii) corporate tax Organschaft through a profit-and-loss transfer agreement with a controlling domestic corporation; (iii) the Kleinunternehmer small-business VAT exemption election where domestic turnover remains below the statutory thresholds; and (iv) hybrid mismatch elections constrained by Section 4k EStG and ATAD II implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small entrepreneurs may elect the Kleinunternehmer VAT exemption only if previous-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000; the threshold was subject to legislative increase under the EU small-enterprise reform, but non-resident and distance-selling businesses have no general de minimis VAT registration threshold in Germany, and intra-EU distance sales may be streamlined through the One-Stop Shop scheme with the EU-wide EUR 10,000 distance-sales threshold being a compliance simplification rather than a registration exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT export exemption applies under Sections 4(1)(No.1) and 6 UStG to goods physically dispatched or transported by the UG to a destination outside the European Union, provided the UG holds proper export evidence and the export is completed within statutory time limits. Services provided B2B to non-EU customers are generally zero-rated on the reverse-charge/destination principle; offshore services must be effectively used or consumed outside Germany. For enamel lapel pins sold via Etsy or Stripe with direct cross-border shipment, the UG must maintain transport documents proving departure from Germany and arrival in the third-country destination.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) submitted through ATLAS with the accompanying export document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating Incoterms, quantities, and customs classification
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or combined transport document evidencing movement from Germany to outside the EU
  • Bank statement or inward remittance certificate confirming receipt of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer

Germany applies mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing under the E-Rechnung rules, requiring receipt of XML/XRechnung/ZUGFeRD/Factur-X formats from 1 January 2025 with phased mandatory issuance by 2027 and 2028 depending on turnover; there is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for invoices, but cash registers must use a certified TSE security module, all VAT returns and EC Sales Lists must be filed electronically via ELSTER, and Etsy and Stripe are subject to EU DAC7 consumer-platform seller reporting and CESOP payment transaction data reporting.

False or incomplete export substantiation results in retroactive VAT assessment at 19%, plus monthly interest of 0.15%, late-payment surcharges, and penalty interest. Intentional or negligent false export claims constitute tax evasion under Sections 369 and 370 AO, are subject to criminal fines or imprisonment, and extend the assessment period to 10 years. Customs and tax auditors additionally impose import/export penalties, deny refund claims, and may claw back previously zero-rated amounts when the inward remittance evidence is counterfeit or the export evidence is non-compliant.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. The euro is freely convertible; no exchange-control approval, licence, or profit-repatriation permit is required for any lawful transaction. A German UG may remit profits freely, subject only to corporate-law solvency tests and the UG-specific statutory reserve rule requiring 25% of annual net income to be retained until reserves reach EUR 25,000. Dividends attract 26.375% withholding tax (25% plus solidarity surcharge) unless a double-tax treaty reduces the rate. The sole procedural requirement is statistical: cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five banking days.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Germany imposes no mandatory surrender ratio, no statutory conversion timeline, and no minimum retention of export proceeds in EUR accounts. Export receipts in USD, GBP, CNH or other currencies may be held freely in foreign-currency accounts. However, each cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 is reportable to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics within five banking days under § 67 AWV; this is a statistical reporting duty, not a convertibility or surrender condition.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and statutory addressee of cross-border payment statistics under § 67 Außenwirtschaftsverordnung/AWV); corporate account supervision falls under BaFin; the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz/AWG) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action constitute the base FX regulatory mandate.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax under the Körperschaftsteuergesetz, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%. Trade tax is additional and varies by municipality; the national average combined burden is approximately 29.8% to 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital income withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic withholding burden of 26.375%, before church tax where applicable. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable double-tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany, or a statutory exemption, subject to anti-abuse and minimum holding requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due seven months after the end of the relevant fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer without a tax adviser, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where an authorized tax adviser prepares the return, the extended statutory deadline generally runs until the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions and possible administrative acceleration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, a Local File is generally required for extraordinary business transactions and, under the standard annual documentation thresholds, where aggregate cross-border transaction values exceed EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties and similar transactions. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100 million, where the statutory cross-border documentation conditions are met. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and supplied to the tax authority within the applicable statutory period, commonly 30 days after request and within shorter periods for certain extraordinary transactions.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a standalone rule titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to the UAE ESR regime. Substance is nevertheless relevant under German general anti-abuse rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, the principal-purpose test where applicable, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse rule, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-owner requirements and transfer-pricing analysis. A foreign holding or financing company should maintain genuine management, decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises where appropriate, risk control, bank-account control, records and commercial reasons proportionate to its functions. A German UG must document the actual functions, assets and risks supporting its manufacturing, e-commerce, payment-processing, procurement, marketing and intellectual-property arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Intentional tax evasion is generally punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. In especially serious cases, including large-scale evasion, organized conduct or abuse of official position, imprisonment can range from six months to ten years. The tax remains payable with interest, and the authority may impose tax-estimation and enforcement measures.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can incur personal civil liability for tax losses caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of their tax duties under the German Fiscal Code. Criminal liability may attach personally for participation, aiding or abetting. A travel restriction is not an automatic tax penalty, but criminal investigation measures, asset seizures and court-ordered restrictions may occur. Imprisonment is possible following criminal conviction.
VAT Fraud and Missing Intra-EU Reporting

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion is subject to the same criminal tax-evasion framework, including imprisonment or fines, repayment of improperly claimed refunds, interest, denial of input tax and possible tax-estimation proceedings. Administrative VAT penalties may also apply for late or incorrect returns.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT where they failed to ensure proper registration, invoicing, accounting, filing or payment. Criminal exposure depends on knowledge, intent and participation.
Withholding-Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: The UG may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, interest and ancillary charges. Intentional non-withholding may constitute tax evasion; reckless conduct may constitute tax understatement or an administrative offense depending on the facts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for withholding-tax losses caused by failure to exercise the required care. Recipient-side treaty relief does not automatically release the German payer from its withholding obligation.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, recharacterize payments as hidden profit distributions, impose withholding tax, assess trade tax, charge interest and apply documentation-related penalties. Failure to produce adequate transfer-pricing documentation can lead to adverse estimation and statutory surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for tax losses caused by deliberate or grossly negligent approval of non-arm's-length transactions. Civil claims may also arise from breach of fiduciary duties or unlawful distributions.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU relief may be denied, withholding tax may be reassessed, and the transaction may be disregarded or recharacterized under German anti-abuse rules. Fraudulent documentation or concealment may create criminal tax exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve abusive arrangements may be personally liable for tax losses and may face criminal liability for tax evasion or participation. No automatic travel ban arises solely from a shell-company finding; criminal-procedure measures may be imposed based on judicial orders.
Transparency Register and Beneficial-Owner Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act can be substantial, with ordinary maximums depending on the violation and responsible entity and aggravated maximums potentially reaching EUR 1 million or multiples of the economic benefit for certain obligated entities. Publication of final sanctions may also occur.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can be fined personally where they are responsible for the breach and may face civil liability for resulting losses. Imprisonment is generally not the ordinary sanction for a standalone reporting error but may arise where the conduct forms part of money laundering, fraud or another criminal offense.
Money Laundering and Prohibited Payments

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is a criminal offense generally punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, with aggravated cases carrying higher imprisonment ranges. Assets may be seized or confiscated, bank accounts may be frozen and commercial relationships may be terminated.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be prosecuted personally, including for aiding, abetting or facilitating. Civil restitution, confiscation and criminal asset recovery may reach personal assets. Travel restrictions can be imposed in criminal proceedings where legally justified.
Insolvency Delay and Wrongful Payments

Statutory sanction: The managing director must monitor insolvency status and file without undue delay, generally within three weeks after illiquidity and six weeks after over-indebtedness where statutory remediation conditions permit. Breach can result in criminal sanctions, disqualification consequences, repayment claims and personal liability for post-insolvency payments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments, tax and social-security losses, creditor damage and restitution claims. Criminal imprisonment or fines may apply. A corporate limited-liability shield does not protect directors from their own breaches of insolvency duties.
Accounting, Record-Keeping and Electronic Sales Evidence

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may reject records, estimate taxable income, deny deductions and impose late-filing or procedural penalties. Deliberate destruction or falsification can support tax-evasion, fraud or obstruction charges.

Director personal liability: The managing director is responsible for proper organization and supervision of accounting and tax compliance. Personal civil and criminal liability may arise where inadequate controls cause tax losses or concealment.
Unlawful Distribution and UG Reserve Breach

Statutory sanction: The distribution may be void or challengeable, recipients may be required to repay funds, and hidden profit distribution adjustments and withholding tax may arise. The UG must generally allocate 25% of annual surplus, after loss offset, to the statutory reserve until the GmbH capital threshold is reached.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unlawful payments, restitution, tax losses and creditor damage. Limited liability does not protect a director who authorizes prohibited distributions or knowingly depletes assets.
