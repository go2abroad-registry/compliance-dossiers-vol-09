Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires a nominal stated capital of at least EUR 1.00 (§ 5a(1) GmbHG). Cash contributions must be funded in full to the free disposal of the managing director before registration (§ 8(2) GmbHG); where a single founder subscribes all shares, full payment and a free-disposal declaration are mandatory. Funds may be held on a dedicated German business account opened in the name of the UG i.G. (in Gründung); withdrawal is not permitted until the Commercial Register entry and statutory capital-liability conditions are satisfied. In addition, if the stated capital is below EUR 25,000, the UG must retain 25% of its annual net profits in a statutory reserve until the stated capital plus reserves reach EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG). For a licensed temp-controlled cold-storage operator, a prudent initial paid-in capital of at least EUR 25,000 is strongly recommended for creditworthiness, insurance solvency, and licensing counterparty acceptance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern) for VAT ID issuance and central administration; local tax office (Finanzamt) for substantive tax registration and assessment; ELSTER e-filing portal (https://www.elster.de) is the statutory digital gateway.

E-government filing portal

A German Unternehmergesellschaft (UG) must obtain a local Steuernummer from the competent Finanzamt upon incorporation and registration with the commercial register (HRB). The VAT registration number (USt-IdNr.) is obtained through the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern via the ELSTER portal or by submitting USt 1 TG form. For foreign entities maintaining a domestic branch or permanent establishment, a tax number is reserved through the responsible Finanzamt and a VAT ID is applied for separately; a fiscal representative may be required if the foreign entity has no habitual residence in Germany. Supporting documents include certified articles of association, trade registration, directors' IDs, and evidence of domestic business premises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a corporation and subject to entity-level taxation; no US-style check-the-box election is available. Available German elections relevant to tax structuring include: (i) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under UStG §2(2)2, requiring financial, economic, and organizational integration; (ii) small-business exemption election under UStG §19 where turnover ceilings are met, although corporate entities are significantly restricted; (iii) trade tax group election for Gewerbesteuer; (iv) conformity and anti-hybrid rules under ATAD implementation which prohibit mismatch-driven reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate applies to supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supplier or a third party initiates the transport and documented proof of export is maintained under UStG §6 and the German VAT Implementing Regulations. Cold-storage and warehousing services supplied to a business customer established outside Germany are generally placed outside the scope of German VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (UStG §3a), giving an effective 0% VAT outcome. Pure domestic storage services performed in Germany for goods intended for export remain standard-rated at 19% unless the storage is in a customs-bonded warehouse or otherwise directly connected to the export movement as an ancillary supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration via ATLAS system (Elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice with final destination and terms of delivery
  • Packing list identifying goods, quantities, and references
  • Export dispatch certificate (Ausfuhrlieferungsschein) supported by transport orders and customs evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/wire transfer confirmation showing receipt of payment from the non-resident buyer

Germany has enacted mandatory B2B e-invoicing under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz). Since 1 January 2025, all German-resident businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices (formats: XInvoice, ZUGFeRD). Mandatory issuance applies tiered, with full obligation for domestic B2B invoices by 1 January 2027 and a further transitional phase for small and medium issuers until 1 January 2028. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control regime. Invoices must be transmitted through compliant electronic channels and archived securely under the GoBD rules, with retention periods of 8 years for digital and paper-based fiscal records.

Export zero-rating is accepted only upon complete and timely submission of the export and payment evidence. If the documentary proof is missing, incomplete, or fraudulent, the tax office retrospectively assesses VAT at 19% plus late-payment interest of 0.2% per month (6% per annum) from the original tax due date. Penalty surcharges under §378 AO and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under §370 AO may apply, with fines up to the evaded tax, imprisonment up to 10 years in aggravated cases, and a 10-year limitation period for tax fraud clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully convertible currency regime on both capital and current accounts. The legal basis includes Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital), the OECD Codes of Liberalisation and the AWG/AWV; no Bundesbank license or exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity contributions, shareholder loans, trade payments, dividends or profit repatriation. Current-account transactions, including import disbursements and export collections, are free from any rationing or licensing requirement. Profit remittance from a UG to non-resident shareholders is not exchange-controlled but is subject to German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) of up to 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reducible to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% direct holding and 12-month holding period) or reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty. The only practical constraints are the Bundesbank statistical reporting obligations, EU/UN sanctions and embargo screening, and anti-money-laundering source-of-funds verification under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion into euro exists under German law. A German UG may invoice exports in any convertible currency, retain the proceeds in foreign currency, and dispose of them freely. The only compulsory duty is statistical: every cross-border receipt and payment by a resident entity must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank if the individual transaction, or the aggregate of a calendar month, exceeds EUR 12,500 (Sections 66 et seq. AWV). Reports are due within five working days after the end of the reporting month and are filed electronically through the Bundesbank's General Reporting Portal (Allgemeines Meldeportal); the account-holding bank may file on the customer's behalf where the account terms so provide. Failure to report is an administrative offence (Bußgeld).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main), as the sole foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments statistics authority; Germany does not maintain a separate exchange-control administration. Export/embargo controls are administered by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and BAFA, while the Bundesbank is the statutory clearing institution for payments statistics, external assets/liabilities reporting and direct investment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act and Ordinance (AWG/AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High for a German UG in refrigerated logistics. A PSD2-licensed EEA electronic money institution (EMI), such as Qonto, Penta, Wise Business or Revolut Business, may passport into Germany and support remote account opening via video identification. The principal legal constraint is that an EMI is not a bank: client funds are held in a safeguarding account, not a deposit account, and are therefore not covered by German statutory deposit protection (Einlagensicherung). For a UG maintaining pharmaceutical cold-storage operations, the Finanzamt generally expects a German bank account and German IBAN for tax and VAT payments; furthermore, commodities with public-health sensitivity will trigger more stringent screening if USD correspondent clearing or third-country payment corridors are involved. The recommended structure is hybrid: a Tier-1 BaFin-licensed German bank as the main clearing treasury and cash domicile, plus one EEA-licensed EMI for fast, low-value cross-border supplier disbursements under a controlled cap. A pure cross-border EMI without German booking or safeguarding with a German credit institution will be assessed as Medium viability for the main account and Low viability for escrow or documentary credit operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections require no exchange-control approval. Compliance protocol: (a) the shareholder transfers EUR or foreign-currency proceeds via SWIFT MT103 to the UG's German account; (b) Field 70 (Remittance Information) must name the precise purpose, e.g. 'CAPITAL INCREASE UG - GESELLSCHAFTEREINLAGE per notarised resolution dated [date]' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - GESELLSCHAFTERDARLEHEN dated [date]'; (c) the receiving bank will require the notarised capital-increase resolution or loan agreement, the updated shareholder list, and confirmation of the natural-person UBO; (d) direct-investment reporting under Section 60 AWV applies if the non-resident investor holds at least 10% of shares or voting rights, with the report due within five working days after the month of the inflow; (e) each inbound payment above EUR 12,500 is included in the monthly Bundesbank balance-of-payments report filed via the General Reporting Portal (Allgemeines Meldeportal). For USD wires through a U.S. correspondent, the MT103 must carry the German beneficiary BIC/IBAN and an explicit capital-purpose text to avoid OFAC review. EUR inflows clear in TARGET2 (T2) and are completed on the same business day. The UG must record the contribution in its HGB books either as subscribed capital (Stammkapital) or as a shareholder loan with arm's-length interest, and must never treat capital as revenue.The UG's clearing rails are layered for efficiency and compliance: (i) SEPA Direct Credit and SEPA Instant for EUR payments to EEA suppliers, utilities, wages and tax authorities, with clearing in TARGET2/TIPS and instructions executed through EBICS or the bank portal; (ii) SWIFT MT103 international wires for non-EEA suppliers and non-EUR currencies (USD, CHF, GBP), with BIC/IBAN validation, dual-approval authorisation matrix and the field-70 purpose referencing the underlying invoice or contract; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 issued/advised by the Tier-1 German bank for high-value refrigeration or pharmaceutical inbound supply, with presentation of documents, bill of lading, quality certificates and insurance policy; (iv) documentary collections (D/P or D/A) as a lower-cost alternative for repeat purchases; (v) same-day zero-balance cash-pooling to concentrate receipts and disburse from a single treasury account; (vi) a capped EMI channel for small cross-border payments, with every transaction logged against the EUR 12,500 Bundesbank reporting threshold. All disbursements must reconcile to the monthly balance-of-payments report and to VAT and customs declarations, as German tax authorities align invoice references with SWIFT MT103 messages.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD, EUR/CHF and EUR/GBP FX forward contracts (Devisentermingeschäfte) concluded with a Tier-1 BaFin-licensed German bank under a signed ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY / currency interest-rate swaps) for long-term matched funding or hedging of foreign-currency shareholder debts.FX options and zero-cost collar strategies for contingent or budget-constrained exposures, with premiums payable in EUR.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or soft currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, TRY) where physical delivery is not available in Germany, subject to documented commercial underlying and Bundesbank statistical reporting where applicable.Tom/Next and forward/forward swap roll-overs for short-term balance-sheet hedging and foreign-currency account liquidity management.Interest-rate hedging instruments embedded in the UG's working-capital facility, structured as micro or macro cash-flow hedges under the HGB valuation-unit rules (Section 254 HGB).

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High for a German UG in refrigerated logistics. A PSD2-licensed EEA electronic money institution (EMI), such as Qonto, Penta, Wise Business or Revolut Business, may passport into Germany and support remote account opening via video identification. The principal legal constraint is that an EMI is not a bank: client funds are held in a safeguarding account, not a deposit account, and are therefore not covered by German statutory deposit protection (Einlagensicherung). For a UG maintaining pharmaceutical cold-storage operations, the Finanzamt generally expects a German bank account and German IBAN for tax and VAT payments; furthermore, commodities with public-health sensitivity will trigger more stringent screening if USD correspondent clearing or third-country payment corridors are involved. The recommended structure is hybrid: a Tier-1 BaFin-licensed German bank as the main clearing treasury and cash domicile, plus one EEA-licensed EMI for fast, low-value cross-border supplier disbursements under a controlled cap. A pure cross-border EMI without German booking or safeguarding with a German credit institution will be assessed as Medium viability for the main account and Low viability for escrow or documentary credit operations.
