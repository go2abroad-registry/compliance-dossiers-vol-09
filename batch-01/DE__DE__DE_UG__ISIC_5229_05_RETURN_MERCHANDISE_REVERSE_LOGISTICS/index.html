Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For an Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt), the nominal share capital must be at least EUR 1, and the full subscribed capital must be paid up in cash and remain at the free disposal of the managing directors before registration; a blocked/escrow-type account is commonly required until entry. The UG must retain at least 25% of annual net profit in a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: A fire safety certificate from the local fire authority and building supervision authority (Feuerwehr/Bauaufsicht) is required for warehouse occupancy. A structural load certification for racking systems must be obtained from a qualified civil engineer. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance) acting through the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification number issuance and the local Finanzamt for income, trade tax and VAT filing registration. Portal: ELSTER electronic filing platform (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign shareholder establishing a German UG must file the online 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the responsible local Finanzamt (tax office) immediately after notarial incorporation to obtain the tax file number (Steuernummer). The UG then applies separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number under § 14 UStG (format DE followed by 9 digits). If the foreign entity is directly engaged in German taxable activities without entering into a German legal entity, a direct VAT registration application is made to the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER, supported by a Certificate of Existence/Formular M and, where relevant, an economic activity declaration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign shareholder establishing a German UG must file the online 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the responsible local Finanzamt (tax office) immediately after notarial incorporation to obtain the tax file number (Steuernummer). The UG then applies separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number under § 14 UStG (format DE followed by 9 digits). If the foreign entity is directly engaged in German taxable activities without entering into a German legal entity, a direct VAT registration application is made to the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER, supported by a Certificate of Existence/Formular M and, where relevant, an economic activity declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The UG, as a corporately structured Kapitalgesellschaft, is taxed at entity level and has no US-style check-the-box pass-through election. Available elective mechanisms include (i) Organschaft – VAT and direct tax fiscal unity with a financially, economically and organisationally integrated German parent, requiring a valid profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Beherrschungs- und Gewinnabführungsvertrag); (ii) the small entrepreneur exception under § 19 UStG for turnover below the statutory thresholds; (iii) the § 9 UStG option to charge VAT on real-estate transactions; and (iv) the § 4(3) UStG election for export-related services. A UG itself cannot elect transparent treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 prior-year turnover and expected €50,000 current-year turnover under the § 19 UStG small entrepreneur scheme; non-resident entrepreneurs are required to register for VAT regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Germany to third-country (non-EU) territory are zero-rated under § 4(1)(1) and § 6 UStG if the exporter physically dispatches or transports the goods out of the EU customs territory, the foreign buyer is identified, and complete commercial and customs evidence is retained within the prescribed documentary periods. Services directly connected to exported goods (e.g. handling, storage, packing or transport in a cross-border logistics chain) may also qualify for zero-rating under § 4(3) UStG where the service is supplied to an export contract and the export is proved. The reverse logistics company must obtain the required customs evidence and supporting commercial documents to prove that a sale and physical removal actually occurred.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading (Sea Waybill / Air Waybill / CMR transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of receipt of consideration from the foreign buyer)
  • Export confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung) issued by the Hauptzollamt
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the foreign buyer and description of goods

Since 1 January 2025, German domestic B2B invoices must be issued as structured e-invoices using an EN 16931-compliant format (XRechnung, EDI or hybrid equivalents). There is no real-time tax-authority clearance, no API-based fiscal stamping, and no electronic fiscal PVC sealing. Public-sector invoices also require XRechnung. Transitional provisions apply until 2027 for small businesses and until 2028 for EDI users, but structured electronic transmission is the legally mandated path for all domestic B2B supplies.

German tax authorities will disallow the zero-rating if export proof is missing, incorrect or inconsistent, and will retroactively issue a VAT assessment at 19% plus interest of 0.5% per month under §§ 233a, 239 AO. Where the offence is committed intentionally or through gross negligence, penalties range from administrative late-payment/declaration fines under §§ 152, 240 AO (delay surcharge up to €25,000) to criminal tax evasion penalties under § 370 AO, which can reach fines and up to five years imprisonment. Fraudulent refund claims using forged customs declarations or fictitious exporter details also carry clawback risk over the extended ten-year limitation period under § 169 AO and activation of the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern's VAT fraud prevention audit procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full capital and current account convertibility under the eurozone legal framework. No exchange control approvals are required for foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or loan servicing. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard corporate formalities: audited financial statements, tax clearance for German withholding taxes on dividends and interest, and AEA (Allgemeine Finanzverwaltungsanweisung) export and import reporting for statistical and balance-of-payments monitoring. Non-resident shareholders may freely receive funds via normal banking channels; no individual conversion permit is required beyond the EU Anti-Money-Laundering Directive's source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists under German law. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or converted freely. German foreign trade regulations (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung) mandate only statistical declaration of payments and receivables to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments purposes, typically within five banking days after the transaction month. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; 100% of export proceeds can remain denominated in the invoiced currency.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking conduct and anti-money laundering supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with material limitations. A BaFin-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or licensed payment institution can provide a valid IBAN and execute SEPA payments, but cannot provide credit, cannot guarantee irrevocable SWIFT settlement in all corridors, and often lacks the correspondent banking depth needed for commercial letters of credit and large trade disbursements. Airwallex can serve as a complementary collection and pay-out rail for low-value reverse-logistics refunds, but the UG should maintain a Tier-1 German bank account as the primary clearing and capital-escrow gateway. Legal constraint: the UG's own contractual counterparties may reject EMI settlement as final payment due to non-bank status under German deposit protection and commercial dispute rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident shareholders must be executed through a compliant SWIFT MT103 (or real-time gross settlement via TARGET2 for euro intra-EU transfers) to the UG's German bank account. The remitter shall state the purpose code in line with ECB Balance of Payments standards, preferably 'D.1 – Direct investment capital contribution' (BOP code 210) or the German Abbreviated Purpose Code 'Vermögenseinlage' (capital contribution) to avoid mismapping. The receiving bank will require a capital contribution certificate (Kapitalaufbringungsbestätigung) and, for sums above the statutory minimum, evidence that no cash-outs were made during the contribution process. Verification includes confirmation of the UG's registered address, the existence of a shareholder resolution, acceptance of the capital reserve, and compliance with the UG's minimum capital rule of at least EUR 1.00 (realistic EUR capital is advised for trade credibility).Outbound trade settlements for reverse-logistics operations (supplier reimbursements, freight payments, liquidation auction payouts, VAT duties) should prioritize low-cost, irrevocable clearing rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for EUR payments within EEA, with a maximum execution time of one business day; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR payments and oversea counterparties, ensuring full correspondent-bank chain disclosure; (3) Documentary Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via a confirming German bank for high-value inbound procurement or refurbished-goods resale where the seller requires payment assurance before title transfer; (4) Airwallex or similar EMI rail only for low-velocity refund batches, but with fallback to the domestic bank if a counterparty rejects non-bank settlement.Plain-vanilla forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNH) traded with a German licensed bank or German branch of an ECB-authorized credit institutionNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing restricted currencies such as CNY offshore or INR, provided the contract is executed through an authorized counterparty and settled in EUR or USDCross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term refurnishment contracts, permitted if the underlying exposure is directly linked to trade receivables or payablesCurrency options as exchange-traded products on Eurex (EUR/USD and minor pairs) for hedging specific commercial tender exposuresFX swaps for short-term liquidity matching between refund obligations and incoming payments in foreign currency

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 for goods transactions; EUR 500,000 for services transactions with related parties

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separate ESR, but substance requirements apply for treaty benefits and anti-abuse rules (Section 42 AO). The UG must demonstrate real economic activity, including employees, premises, and decision-making functions, to avoid denial of treaty benefits or reclassification as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; additional penalty of up to 100% of evaded tax (Section 370 AO); interest on unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for evaded taxes if they knowingly participated; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential extradition
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, retroactive taxation, and penalties up to EUR 25,000 per violation; potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; travel bans and asset freezes; imprisonment up to 3 years for fraudulent abuse
AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 per violation; regulatory sanctions; potential revocation of business license

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for non-compliance; civil penalties and potential criminal prosecution for money laundering facilitation
