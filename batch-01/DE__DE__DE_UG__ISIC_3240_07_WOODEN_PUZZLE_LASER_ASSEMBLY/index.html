Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG may be incorporated with a nominal share capital of as little as EUR 1, fully paid in cash before registration under § 5a GmbHG; no statutory escrow account is mandated, but the managing director must declare under § 8(2) GmbHG that the contributions are at his or her free disposal and free of counterclaims. The UG must allocate 25% of annual net profit to a statutory reserve until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.); the competent local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for the tax number, corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT registration; electronic filings are generally handled through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung).

E-government filing portal

A German UG must submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The entity applies separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), generally using the BZSt online application process or through the Finanzamt. A foreign entity with a German permanent establishment, place of management, taxable German supplies, or intra-Community transactions must register with the competent Finanzamt and, where applicable, obtain a German tax number and USt-IdNr. The application should include constitutional documents, commercial-register evidence, registered address, business activities, management details, bank information, expected turnover, and evidence of German taxable transactions. Non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a German tax representative for specific VAT procedures, but Germany does not impose a universal fiscal-representative requirement for every non-EU taxable person.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German capital company and is ordinarily subject to entity-level corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a UG. A UG cannot generally elect transparent partnership treatment while retaining its corporate legal form. Certain cross-border hybrid-entity outcomes may be affected by the German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, including restrictions on deduction, inclusion, and imported mismatch arrangements. VAT treatment is separately determined under the German VAT Act (UStG), including possible small-business treatment under Section 19 UStG if the statutory conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany has no general turnover threshold below which a taxable business is automatically exempt from VAT registration. The small-business regime under Section 19 UStG may apply where turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions and transition rules. A business using the small-business regime generally does not charge German VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. Taxable persons making intra-Community supplies, imports, exports, domestic taxable supplies, or distance sales may require VAT registration regardless of the small-business thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt with the right to deduct input VAT under Sections 4 and 6 UStG where the goods are physically transported or dispatched to a third country, the exporter or its customer is responsible for the transport as required by the applicable export category, and the transaction is properly recorded and substantiated. The exporter must retain documentary and accounting evidence proving the export, normally including electronic customs export confirmation and transport evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State may qualify for VAT exemption under the applicable German and EU rules if the goods move to another Member State, the customer provides a valid VAT identification number, the supplier reports the transaction correctly, and transport and accounting evidence are maintained. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore. Their treatment depends on the place-of-supply rules: many B2B services are outside German VAT under the customer-location rule, often with reverse charge in the customer jurisdiction, while specific services have special location rules. Refunds or input-tax deductions require valid purchase invoices, customs evidence, correct VAT returns, and absence of disqualifying private or exempt use.

  • Electronic export declaration filed through the German customs system, normally including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and electronic exit confirmation.
  • Commercial invoice identifying the German exporter, foreign consignee, goods, value, delivery terms, and transaction details.
  • Packing list and transport document, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier transport record, as applicable.
  • Evidence that the goods physically left the European Union, including customs exit confirmation or an approved alternative proof of export.
  • Purchase order, sales contract, delivery records, and accounting records linking the exported goods to the zero-rated or VAT-exempt supply.
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung).
  • Proof of payment or commercial settlement where relevant to the audit trail; Germany does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption.

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or mandatory digital tax-stamping system. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies is subject to transitional periods: generally until the end of 2026, and for qualifying businesses with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 potentially until the end of 2027. From 2028, the structured e-invoicing requirements are intended to apply fully, subject to the detailed statutory rules. Acceptable structured formats include XRechnung and compliant ZUGFeRD formats; EDI may be used if the statutory requirements are satisfied. No universal API pre-clearance with the Finanzamt is presently required. Invoices must contain the statutory German VAT invoice particulars, including supplier and customer identity, tax numbers or VAT identification numbers where required, sequential invoice number, supply date, consideration, applicable rate, VAT amount, and legally required exemption or reverse-charge references.

Germany has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days and no general export foreign-exchange repatriation deadline. The exporter must nevertheless maintain reliable customs, transport, invoice, accounting, and VAT records for the statutory retention periods, generally ten years for tax-relevant accounting records and related documents, subject to applicable changes and document category. If zero-rating or VAT exemption is claimed without satisfying the substantive conditions or documentary requirements, the Finanzamt may deny the exemption, assess output VAT retrospectively, disallow related input-tax recovery, charge interest under the German Fiscal Code, and impose late-payment consequences. Incorrect or incomplete VAT returns may trigger administrative fines; intentional or reckless conduct may constitute a tax offence or tax fraud under the German Fiscal Code, potentially resulting in criminal penalties, fines, and personal liability for responsible officers. Export documentation deficiencies can also lead to customs reassessments, customs penalties, loss of simplified procedures, and enhanced audit scrutiny. Fraudulent invoices, fabricated export evidence, or knowingly false VAT refund claims may result in repayment of the tax benefit, interest, civil recovery, criminal prosecution, and exclusion from compliance simplifications.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full and unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation from a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft) may be freely executed in EUR or convertible foreign currency without prior approval. The only practical hurdles are corporate income tax withholding at 25% (plus solidarity surcharge and, where applicable, church tax) on dividend distributions, and compliance with anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and external economic statistics reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts freely. However, under Section 64 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), all cross-border payments and receipts from non-EEA residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes. Failure to report is an administrative offense punishable by fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for prudential banking supervision; foreign exchange administration is not a distinct authority as Germany operates under the EU single-market capital movement regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. Fintechs and European Payment Institutions licensed as E-Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 may open cross-border accounts for a German UG, offering faster onboarding and multi-currency IBANs. Legal constraints include the absence of deposit insurance (covered instead by safeguarding requirements), potential restrictions on holding escrow or settlement balances, and reliance on pass-through correspondent banking. Such EMIs are suitable for operational clearing but not as the sole treasury institution for capital escrow or high-trust commercial LC operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from a foreign shareholder to the UG must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a clearly stated purpose code reflecting direct capital participation, commonly 'CAPT' (Capital Payments) or 'GAIN' (Investment Income) as applicable, together with the UBO name, CNPJ-equivalent identifier of the German entity, and the narrative 'Kapitalerhöhung / Share Capital Contribution'. Verification requires the notarised capital-increase resolution, a bank confirmation of the escrowed amount, and German legalisation of the shareholder's corporate documents. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax (effective federal rate 15.825%). Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and depends on the municipality; the combined burden commonly approximates 30%, but varies with the municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective rate of 26.375%, before any church tax or relief. Treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief may reduce or eliminate the charge where all substantive and procedural conditions are met.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally, returns are due by 31 July of the year following the tax year where the taxpayer files without a tax adviser. Where a tax adviser files, the generally applicable extended deadline is the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions, year-specific extensions and any order by the tax authority. Advance corporate income tax and trade-tax payments are generally quarterly.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate income tax advance payments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December. Trade-tax advance payments are generally due on 15 February, 15 May, 15 August and 15 November. The assessment notice controls the amount and payment instructions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, German documentation requirements generally include transaction-specific records where annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for deliveries of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions. A Master File is generally required where the taxpayer's relevant group turnover exceeds EUR 100 million. Thresholds, record scope and timing are governed by the applicable version of the German Foreign Tax Act and implementing rules; current-year requirements must be verified.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. German transfer-pricing rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership standards, the principal-purpose test where applicable, controlled-foreign-company rules and general anti-abuse doctrine can deny tax benefits or adjust arrangements lacking commercial rationale or adequate functions, assets and risk control. A German UG must maintain genuine business records and substantiate the actual conduct of its operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and materially false tax returns

Statutory sanction: Under section 370 AO, tax evasion may be punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest and applicable surcharges remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability where they commit, direct or knowingly facilitate the offense. Directors also have statutory responsibility for proper tax compliance; civil liability, asset recovery and travel restrictions may follow from applicable criminal or enforcement proceedings, but are not automatic.
Negligent tax understatement and filing failures

Statutory sanction: Tax understatement by negligence may be an administrative offense under section 378 AO, punishable by a fine of up to EUR 50,000. Late-filing surcharges, tax, interest and other statutory charges may apply separately.

Director personal liability: A director may be personally exposed to fines or liability where statutory duties are breached or the director culpably contributes to the violation. Liability depends on conduct, authority and the applicable statutory standard.
Withholding-tax and dividend-distribution non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The company may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest and applicable surcharges. Intentional or negligent conduct may additionally trigger criminal or administrative sanctions under tax law.

Director personal liability: Under section 69 AO, directors may be personally liable for tax claims to the extent they intentionally or grossly negligently breach tax duties and cause non-payment. Liability is fact-specific and is not automatic.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Taxable income may be adjusted under the German Foreign Tax Act. Documentation failures can attract penalties generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to EUR 1 million, subject to statutory conditions and the amount or duration of the failure; additional assessment and interest consequences may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal exposure for culpable breaches of filing, recordkeeping or payment duties, but a transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Shell or sham arrangements and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied, withholding tax assessed or refunded amounts recovered, with interest and possible penalties. Criminal liability requires the elements of a criminal offense, including the applicable intent standard.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable or prosecuted where they knowingly participate in fraud or culpably breach statutory duties. Denial of treaty relief does not by itself establish personal criminal liability.
Accounting, corporate-record and insolvency-related misconduct

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include fines, tax assessments, repayment or restitution claims, disqualification and criminal penalties under applicable tax, accounting, corporate and insolvency laws. The specific sanction depends on the conduct and governing provision.

Director personal liability: UG managing directors may face personal civil liability for breach of statutory duties and criminal liability for qualifying offenses. Insolvency filing duties and restrictions on post-insolvency payments require prompt professional assessment.
Anti-money-laundering and suspicious-payment violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines and criminal sanctions may apply under the German Money Laundering Act and Criminal Code, depending on the regulated status, conduct and culpability. A business is not subject to every AML obligation solely because it is a German UG.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons may face personal administrative or criminal liability for their own violations or participation. Travel bans are not an automatic tax or AML sanction and require a separate legal basis.
