Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and certain federal tax functions; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax filings, VAT registration and the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). Electronic filings and the tax registration questionnaire are generally submitted through the ELSTER portal (Elektronische Steuererklärung). Customs matters are administered by the General Directorate of Customs (Generalzolldirektion), primarily through the ATLAS customs system.

E-government filing portal

1. Register the UG with the commercial register and obtain its registered-office and management details. 2. Submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt; provide the articles, commercial-register extract, directors, beneficial owners, business activities, expected turnover, bank details and VAT status. 3. Receive the domestic Steuernummer from the Finanzamt. 4. Apply separately to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr.; the application may be made through the BZSt online service or through the tax registration process. 5. If importing or exporting goods, obtain an Economic Operators Registration and Identification (EORI) number from German Customs and arrange ATLAS capability directly or through a customs representative. 6. Foreign shareholders and directors may own or manage a German UG, but the company must maintain a German registered office and comply with German accounting, tax filing and beneficial-owner obligations. A non-resident foreign entity conducting taxable German activities may require a German tax number and, where applicable, a German VAT ID without forming a German subsidiary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German corporation and is generally subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax at entity level. Germany has no United States-style check-the-box election for a UG and no general election to treat it as fiscally transparent. Distributions are generally taxed separately at shareholder level under the applicable shareholder regime. A corporation may apply for German tax-group treatment (Organschaft) if statutory financial, organizational and profit-transfer requirements are satisfied. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive implementation provisions may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not provide an elective hybrid classification. The UG must retain statutory minimum-reserve amounts until the legal reserve reaches the GmbH minimum capital threshold, subject to the German Limited Liability Companies Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is generally no turnover threshold that exempts a business from VAT registration where it makes taxable supplies in Germany. The small-business exemption under section 19 UStG may apply where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and effective-date rules. A business using the exemption generally does not charge VAT and cannot deduct input VAT. Separate registration and reporting obligations can arise for intra-Community acquisitions, distance sales, imports, platform transactions and other cross-border supplies. The EU-wide One Stop Shop threshold for certain intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied, telecommunications, broadcasting and television services is EUR 10,000 per calendar year across the relevant EU transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under sections 4 and 6 UStG if the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the statutory exporter and destination conditions are met. The supplier must retain documentary or electronic proof of export, normally including an electronic customs exit confirmation or equivalent approved evidence. The invoice should identify the legal VAT exemption basis and should not charge German VAT. Supplies to VAT-registered business customers in another EU Member State may qualify as tax-exempt intra-Community supplies, provided the customer gives a valid EU VAT ID, the goods move to another Member State, the supplier satisfies the required reporting and recapitulative-statement obligations, and transport evidence is maintained. Services to non-German business customers may be outside the scope of German VAT under the applicable place-of-supply rules, rather than zero-rated exports; the customer status, service type and reverse-charge provisions must be documented. Input VAT refunds require valid supplier invoices, import VAT documentation and a direct connection with taxable or qualifying exempt transactions.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, currency, delivery terms and destination
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as a bill of lading, airway bill, consignment note or carrier confirmation
  • Electronic export customs declaration submitted through ATLAS, including the relevant customs procedure and commodity classification
  • Customs export or exit confirmation proving that the goods left the European Union
  • EORI number and, where used, customs representation authorization
  • Proof of transport and delivery to the non-EU destination, including carrier tracking or delivery confirmation where relevant
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence and required recapitulative statement
  • Import-country customs records and import VAT documentation where relevant
  • Bank payment or remittance evidence as commercial support; Germany does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or mandatory digital-stamping system. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exclusions. Mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices is phased in: generally from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year German turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for other businesses, subject to the statutory transitional provisions. During the transition, compliant paper invoices and other electronic invoices may remain permissible in specified circumstances. A compliant e-invoice must generally use an EU-standard structured format, such as XRechnung or an appropriate hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD. E-invoices must be retained in an auditable form under German accounting and tax-retention rules. VAT returns and advance VAT returns are generally filed electronically through ELSTER. Export customs declarations are processed through ATLAS; customs pre-arrival, export and safety-and-security data requirements may apply.

Germany has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for export VAT exemption. The taxpayer bears the burden of substantiating the export or intra-Community movement and must correct VAT returns if the conditions are not met. The Finanzamt may assess the unpaid VAT retroactively, deny or claw back input-VAT deductions, impose late-payment interest under the German Fiscal Code, and apply penalties for incorrect or incomplete returns. Deliberate tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under section 370 of the Fiscal Code, with imprisonment or monetary penalties; serious cases may attract enhanced imprisonment. Negligent tax reduction may trigger an administrative fine under section 378 of the Fiscal Code. Incorrect invoices can create separate VAT liability under section 14c UStG. Customs violations may result in customs-duty recovery, import VAT recovery, administrative fines and, in serious cases, criminal liability. Tax records, invoices, transport evidence and customs documents should be retained for the applicable German statutory retention period, generally eight years for accounting records and ten years for specified tax-relevant records, subject to the relevant legal category and transitional rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains no exchange-control or approval regime; the euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under EU law (Art. 63 TFEU). A UG may freely remit profits and dividends to foreign shareholders without Bundesbank licensing, but statutory dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate) applies unless reduced by a double-taxation treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; foreign-currency treasury balances may be held offshore without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory-conversion obligation for export proceeds; a German UG may retain, convert, or reinvest foreign-currency receipts freely. Only statistical reporting obligations apply: cross-border payments from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 per payment or monthly aggregate must be reported to the Bundesbank under AWV sec. 67, and outstanding foreign receivables exceeding EUR 5,000 must be reported periodically under AWV sec. 71.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) is the sole German central bank and competent balance-of-payments statistics authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin, Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) supervises licensed banks and electronic money institutions for AML/KYC and payment-services conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability, conditioned on an EEA/EU PSD2 license. A digitally native EMI with a German or passporting EU license (e.g., Solaris, Finom, Qonto) may lawfully provide multi-currency operational accounts, GBP/USD rails, and SWIFT disbursements to a UG; however, an EMI cannot credibly confirm the statutory free availability of Stammkapital for the German commercial-register formation procedure and generally lacks LC-native trade-finance and central-bank statistical-reporting capabilities. Post-Brexit UK EMIs without EU passporting rights are not permissible as primary payment-service providers for a German UG.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a 15.825% combined federal charge. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional; its effective rate depends on the municipality’s multiplier. The combined burden is commonly approximately 30%, but is not a single nationwide rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, ordinarily 26.375% in total, before any church tax or relief. Treaty relief may reduce the rate; qualifying EU parent companies may be eligible for exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in German law, subject to ownership, holding-period, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual return is generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the ordinary extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions and any deadline changes. For a non-calendar fiscal year, the deadline is calculated from the fiscal-year end. Any extension or exceptional deadline must be confirmed for the relevant tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, a Local File is generally required where annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, subject to the applicable statutory rules and documentation requirements. A Master File is generally required where the German entity belongs to a multinational group whose consolidated revenue reaches EUR 100 million. Documentation must be prepared and retained within statutory time limits and provided to the tax authority when requested. Thresholds and applicable deadlines should be checked for the relevant tax year.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership and treaty-entitlement analysis, the foreign-tax-law anti-treaty-shopping rule, controlled-foreign-company rules, and general anti-abuse provisions. The UG should maintain appropriate personnel, decision-making, premises, functions, assets, risk control, and contemporaneous records consistent with its claimed tax position.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO may result in a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Taxes, interest, and applicable penalties remain payable. Negligent tax reduction may be an administrative offense under section 378 AO.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal or administrative liability for their own conduct and may be liable for tax shortfalls under sections 34 and 69 AO where they breach tax duties culpably. Criminal liability is not automatically avoided by acting through the UG.
Failure to remit withholding taxes

Statutory sanction: The payer may be liable for unpaid withholding tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, and applicable penalties. Intentional withholding-tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under the AO.

Director personal liability: A managing director responsible for withholding and remittance may be personally liable under section 69 AO for culpable non-payment and may face criminal exposure for intentional conduct.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, impose a documentation surcharge under section 162 AO, and assess additional tax and interest. The surcharge and adjustment depend on the statutory conditions and the nature and extent of the failure.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability where culpable breaches of tax duties cause tax losses, and criminal exposure where the facts establish intentional tax evasion.
Distributions in breach of UG capital-maintenance rules

Statutory sanction: Unlawful payments may be recoverable by the company under applicable GmbH Act rules. Insolvency-related payments may also give rise to repayment, insolvency, and civil liability consequences.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments and insolvency-related breaches. Shareholders may be required to repay unlawful distributions where statutory conditions are met.
Insolvency and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: The German Insolvency Code and relevant criminal statutes may impose repayment obligations, fines, or imprisonment depending on the conduct. Filing-delay and payment restrictions are governed by statutory insolvency rules.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal civil and criminal liability for culpable filing delay, prohibited payments, or creditor prejudice. A veil-piercing conclusion is not automatic; liability generally arises under specific statutory duties and causes of action.
Money laundering and beneficial-ownership non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Money laundering may be punishable under section 261 of the German Criminal Code, including imprisonment or fines. Breaches of transparency-register duties may attract administrative fines under the applicable legislation.

Director personal liability: Individuals involved may face personal criminal or administrative liability. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction and may arise only under applicable criminal-procedure or other legal measures.
Fictitious residence or shell arrangement used to claim treaty benefits

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied under German anti-abuse and treaty rules; additional tax, interest, surcharges, and criminal penalties may apply if the arrangement involves intentional false reporting or evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for their own intentional or culpable conduct and for breaches of statutory tax duties. Civil restitution, travel bans, or imprisonment are not automatic consequences of a shell-company finding and depend on specific statutory grounds and proceedings.
