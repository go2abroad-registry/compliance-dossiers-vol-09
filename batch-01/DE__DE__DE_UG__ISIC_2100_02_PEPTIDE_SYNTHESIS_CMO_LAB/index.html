Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and EU-level tax registrations; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for ongoing tax assessment and collection. Official portal: ELSTER / Mein ELSTER at www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation and commercial register (Handelsregister) entry of the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), the entity must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the German tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. Separately, the UG must apply to BZSt under §27a UStG for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) for intra-Community and international transactions. The application must include certified legal entity documents, commercial register excerpt, anticipated turnover, and a description of planned export activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a check-the-box election; the UG is per se subject to entity-level taxation. The principal statutory election available is the formation of an Organschaft (corporate tax and VAT fiscal unity), requiring at least 75% control, a profit-and-loss pooling agreement, and administrative integration. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD 2, as implemented in §4k EStG, apply to deny deductions in cross-border hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany has no general economic turnover threshold for VAT registration; any entrepreneur, including a UG, must register with the Finanzamt from the first taxable transaction. The small-business exemption under §19 UStG is available only if turnover in the prior calendar year did not exceed EUR 25,000 and the current year’s turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; a typical CDMO peptide laboratory exceeding these volumes is fully registrable and must charge/properly document VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of peptide raw materials, custom-synthesized peptides, and finished peptide solid-phase synthesis deliverables to non-EU customers qualify for exemption from German VAT with full input VAT deduction (effective 0% export VAT) under §4(1)(a) and §6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave the EU, the seller or buyer initiates shipment, and the export is declared to customs. Intra-Community supplies to EU customers are likewise exempt if the customer provides a valid VAT ID and the goods are transported to another Member State. For contract manufacturing services supplied to a non-EU business customer in respect of offshore goods, the place of supply is outside Germany under §3a(2) UStG, so German VAT is not chargeable; where the CDMO lab produces and supplies proprietary peptide substances to the foreign customer, the transaction is generally classified as a zero-rated export of goods.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with HS/CN code, Incoterms and peptide product composition
  • Transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU: Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR consignment note
  • Proof of customs clearance or receipt at destination country, where available under §9 UStDV export-evidence rules
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment confirmation from the foreign customer, used as due-diligence and foreign-exchange monitoring evidence

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased in under the Growth Opportunities Act. From 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive electronic invoices compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or EDI). From 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B transactions must be e-invoiced. Germany does not operate a real-time tax authority clearance or digital stamping system; no API pre-clearance is required, although ERP/EDI integration is necessary for compliant issuance. Tamper-proof fiscal data retention and TSE rules apply only to electronic cash registers/retail sales, not to B2B invoicing.

German tax authorities conduct substantive export-evidence audits; if the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export or intra-Community supply, the VAT exemption is retroactively denied and 19% German VAT is assessed, plus interest at 0.5% per month or 6% per annum from the original due date. Fraudulent or knowingly incorrect export zero-rating declarations constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, carrying fines of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of up to five years; in aggravated cases, imprisonment of up to ten years. The statute of limitations is four years for regular VAT assessments and ten years in cases of tax evasion. No statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline exists under German tax law; however, cross-border payments received from foreign customers equal to EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical trade reporting, and failure to report may trigger administrative penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full liberalization of capital and current account payments under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV) and its OECD/IMF obligations. Profits, dividends, interest, royalties, and liquidation proceeds are freely convertible and remittable in EUR or foreign currency without prior approval or exchange control authorization. Resident bank accounts and international payments are unrestricted; however, cross-border payments above statutory thresholds must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical balance-of-payments purposes, not for approval. There are no surrender requirements, no exchange taxation, and no blocked currency mechanisms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As a eurozone member and under the AWV, Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts at German banks or held abroad subject to AWV reporting. For commercial export transactions, payments or receivables exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days following receipt or accrual of the consideration, solely for statistical balance-of-payments reporting. The Ugandan or German exporting entity must maintain supporting commercial documentation for each reportable transaction.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. European fintech EMIs operating under a BaFin license or an EEA passport may provide German IBAN accounts and multi-currency wallets; however, for a medium-risk pharmaceutical CDMO subject to statutory licensing and enhanced AML scrutiny, fintech EMIs frequently restrict SWIFT correspondent coverage, beneficiary disclosure formats, high-value pharmaceutical payments, and documentary trade services. Fintech / EMI solutions are acceptable as secondary collection channels for fractional trade receivables, but are not recommended as the sole treasury or settlement rail for substantive export contracts or capital injection flows.
