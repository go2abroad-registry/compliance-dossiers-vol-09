Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG has no minimum statutory capital and may be formed with share capital of EUR 1 or more but below EUR 25,000. The entire subscribed share capital must be fully paid in cash or fully contributed in kind before the Commercial Register application is submitted. Cash contributions must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director in a German bank account; no statutory escrow account is mandatory, but the bank confirmation and the managing director’s formal solvency declaration are required for registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire department safety clearance (Brandschutzabnahme) for the production hall covering emergency exits, extinguishing systems and hazardous-substance storage; structural load certificate (Standsicherheitsnachweis) confirming floor and foundation capacity for injection-molding machines; occupational safety clearance under Arbeitsschutzgesetz and BetrSichV requiring machine guarding verification, electrical conformity, noise exposure not exceeding 80 dB(A) daily average, and provision of emergency medical and first-aid equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: A UG has no minimum statutory capital and may be formed with share capital of EUR 1 or more but below EUR 25,000. The entire subscribed share capital must be fully paid in cash or fully contributed in kind before the Commercial Register application is submitted. Cash contributions must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director in a German bank account; no statutory escrow account is mandatory, but the bank confirmation and the managing director’s formal solvency declaration are required for registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German state tax offices (Finanzämter) administer tax registration and issue tax numbers. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, format DE followed by nine digits). The principal electronic tax portal is ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung), operated through the German tax administration portal.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate and register the UG with the competent commercial register and obtain the commercial-register extract. 2. Submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt. 3. Provide the registered office, business activities, beneficial owners, bank details, expected turnover, expected taxable profits, accounting method, intra-EU transactions, import/export activities, and authorized tax representative details. 4. The Finanzamt issues the German tax number (Steuernummer), generally required for tax filings and correspondence. 5. Apply for the German VAT identification number through the BZSt, either directly where permitted or through the tax-registration process; the BZSt normally verifies the entity and its tax registration. 6. Register for VAT where the UG makes taxable supplies in Germany or otherwise has a German VAT liability. 7. Register for customs activities through the German customs administration and obtain an Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI) before importing or exporting goods. 8. Register for the One Stop Shop (OSS) where eligible intra-EU distance sales or qualifying cross-border B2C services are reported centrally. A foreign shareholder may own the UG, but ownership by a non-resident does not eliminate the UG's German tax-registration obligations. A German fiscal representative is not generally mandatory solely because the shareholder is non-resident, although representation may be required or commercially advisable for specific non-EU VAT arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German capital company and is subject to corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax at entity level. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a UG. The UG cannot elect transparent partnership treatment merely by filing a tax classification form. Statutory restructurings, such as conversion into a partnership where legally available, may change the tax classification but require separate corporate-law and tax analysis. The German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Interest Limitation and Anti-Hybrid provisions can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not provide an elective hybrid classification. The UG may elect or use the domestic small-business VAT regime if its statutory turnover conditions are satisfied, but that is a VAT exemption regime rather than an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general German VAT registration threshold for a business making taxable supplies in Germany. A taxable business must register and account for VAT from the commencement of relevant taxable activities, subject to specific exemptions and special regimes. From 2025, the domestic small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG generally applies where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000. The exemption does not apply to certain reverse-charge, import, intra-EU acquisition, or other separately regulated liabilities and is not a general exemption from VAT registration or identification obligations. Eligible EU-established businesses using the EU cross-border SME scheme are also subject to the EU-wide turnover ceiling and applicable notification requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax deduction under Sections 4(1)(a) and 6 UStG if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third-country destination and the statutory exporter, destination, and documentary evidence requirements are satisfied. The supplier must establish that the goods physically left the EU and must retain customs and transport evidence. Intra-EU supplies to a VAT-identified business customer may qualify for the separate intra-Community supply exemption, subject to valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative statement, and other conditions. Services supplied to business customers established outside Germany are generally governed by place-of-supply rules; many B2B services are located at the customer's place of establishment and are therefore outside the scope of German VAT rather than technically zero-rated. Evidence must support customer status, place of supply, contractual scope, and actual performance. Export treatment is unavailable where the goods remain in Germany or the statutory proof requirements are not met.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs export records, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN), exit confirmation, and, where relevant, the Export Accompanying Document.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract or order documentation, product description, quantity, value, commodity classification, and consignee details.
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or other reliable transport evidence showing dispatch to a destination outside the European Union.
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence where customs exit data alone does not establish the required export facts.
  • Evidence identifying the foreign customer and confirming the non-EU destination, including customer correspondence and delivery instructions where relevant.
  • German VAT invoice or export invoice containing the applicable statutory exemption reference and the supplier's VAT identification details where required.
  • Customs authorization, EORI information, and import-clearance or destination-country documentation where relevant.
  • Bank payment records or other commercial payment evidence where useful for audit substantiation. Germany does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption.

Germany does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or central digital-stamping system. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business supplies. Mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies is phased in: generally from 2027 for issuers whose prior-year domestic turnover exceeded EUR 800,000, from 2028 for all in-scope issuers, with transitional use of paper and other electronic invoices during the statutory transition periods. Qualifying structured formats include EN 16931-compliant formats such as XRechnung and suitable ZUGFeRD versions. Recipient consent and ordinary electronic-invoice requirements apply during transition. Federal public-sector invoicing generally uses XRechnung or another accepted EN 16931-compliant format. Invoices must contain the statutory VAT particulars, including supplier and customer details, tax numbers or VAT IDs where required, sequential invoice number, supply date, consideration, applicable tax rate, and VAT amount. No general API submission to the Finanzamt and no real-time invoice clearance are required for ordinary commercial invoices.

Germany does not impose a general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and does not condition export VAT exemption on repatriation within 180 days. The Finanzamt may deny or retrospectively revoke the exemption where export, destination, transport, customer, or documentary requirements are not satisfied and may assess the resulting VAT, late-payment surcharges, and applicable interest. Undue input-tax or VAT refunds may be reclaimed. Intentional or reckless false export documentation, concealed domestic supplies, or fraudulent refund claims may constitute tax evasion or related tax offences, exposing responsible persons to criminal fines or imprisonment under the German Fiscal Code, in addition to civil tax recovery and possible administrative penalties. Repeated non-compliance may result in enhanced audits, denial of refund claims pending verification, customs measures, and liability for directors or other responsible officers where statutory duties were breached.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro functions under a fully convertible regime for current account transactions within the European Union. Capital account transactions for EU-resident entities such as UG (haftungsbeschränkt) are liberalized with minimal restrictions. Profit remittances to shareholders are permitted without significant hurdles under EU freedom of capital movements, subject only to standard tax reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German exporters are not subject to mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Receipts must be declared for tax and statistical purposes within statutory timelines, typically 30 days for VAT purposes, with no fixed conversion ratios mandated as the currency is the Euro.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

PayPal Business and similar fintech EMI providers are viable for cross-border receipts but are subject to strict German AML/KYC obligations and potential restrictions on high-volume manufacturing payments. Offshore digital EMIs face legal constraints under EU PSD2 and German banking secrecy laws, requiring local banking integration or structuring.
