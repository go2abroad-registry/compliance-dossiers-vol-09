Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal tax administration: Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), including its online services for VAT identification numbers and EU VAT procedures; the competent local Finanzamt administers domestic tax registration and tax numbers. ELSTER is the principal electronic tax filing and registration platform. Customs matters are administered by the Generalzolldirektion and the relevant Hauptzollamt.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must submit the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung through ELSTER to the Finanzamt competent for its registered office, generally within one month after commencing business activity. The Finanzamt issues the domestic Steuernummer and assesses corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT registration. Apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), normally after or in connection with tax registration; an EU-established business may also apply through its competent tax office. A foreign-established taxable person without a German establishment applies to the Finanzamt designated for its country of establishment and provides incorporation documents, proof of business activity, address and representative details, and any requested evidence of taxable transactions. A non-EU business may be required to appoint a fiscal representative for specified VAT arrangements; representation does not replace the taxable person's liability. Obtain an EORI number from German customs before relevant customs operations. A German UG is not a foreign entity and follows the domestic registration route.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election for a UG. A UG is a separate corporate income tax taxpayer and is generally subject to trade tax. Under section 1a KStG, eligible commercial partnerships may elect corporate income tax treatment, subject to statutory conditions; that election is not a pass-through election available to a UG. German domestic and treaty rules determine entity characterization for cross-border purposes. Anti-hybrid rules, including section 4k EStG and related provisions, can deny deductions or impose other consequences for mismatches; they are not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic turnover threshold exempts a German-established UG from VAT registration and compliance where it makes taxable supplies. The small-business regime under section 19 UStG may apply if prior-year total turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year total turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; it is an exemption regime, not a general registration threshold, and has statutory exclusions. A foreign-established supplier making taxable supplies in Germany generally has no domestic registration threshold. Separate EU small-enterprise rules may apply to qualifying cross-border EU supplies subject to their conditions and turnover limits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may be exempt with credit (zero-rated) under sections 4 no. 1(a) and 6 UStG where the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier retains prescribed evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. The applicable evidence depends on whether the export is made by the supplier or by the non-EU customer. An intra-Community supply is treated under separate rules and requires, among other conditions, dispatch to another EU Member State, a qualifying business customer and valid VAT identification details, plus evidence and reporting compliance. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer or payment is offshore: place-of-supply rules govern, and a service may be outside German VAT or exempt only where a specific rule applies. Input VAT recovery is subject to the ordinary deduction rules and substantiation requirements.

  • Customs export declaration and export accompanying or exit confirmation evidence, including the electronic Ausgangsvermerk where available
  • Commercial invoice, order or contract, and records identifying the goods, quantity, purchaser, and shipment
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the transaction, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, or carrier documentation; a bill of lading is not universally required
  • Proof of delivery or other evidence of departure from the EU customs territory where required under the applicable evidence rules
  • Evidence of payment or inward remittance may support the audit trail but is not a universal statutory condition for export zero-rating
  • For intra-Community supplies, customer VAT identification evidence, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement reporting

Germany has no general real-time VAT clearance, government digital-stamping, or mandatory pre-approval system for invoices. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. The obligation to issue structured e-invoices is being phased in: transitional permission to issue other invoices generally applies through 2026, with a further transition through 2027 for specified suppliers with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000. From 2028, structured e-invoicing is generally required for in-scope domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory exceptions. A qualifying e-invoice must use a compliant structured format, such as EN 16931 or an accepted equivalent; ordinary PDF invoices alone are not structured e-invoices. Invoicing and VAT records must be retained in accordance with German law and GoBD principles.

Germany imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must substantiate the conditions for exemption and maintain compliant VAT records. If evidence is missing or the conditions are not met, the tax authority may assess German VAT, interest under the applicable rules, and related procedural charges. Intentional or reckless tax evasion may result in criminal or administrative penalties under the Abgabenordnung; inaccurate returns may also attract tax surcharges. Input VAT refunds or credits may be denied or reclaimed, with interest, where eligibility or supporting documentation fails. The applicable amount and consequences depend on the facts, culpability, and procedural rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains fully liberalized current and capital account convertibility within the EU Treaty freedoms of payment and capital movement. The euro is fully convertible; there are no exchange control permissions, surrender mandates, or administrative ceilings on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or withdrawal of contributed capital. Nevertheless, the AWV imposes a mandatory cross-border payments statistical declaration to Deutsche Bundesbank for every resident payment to or receipt from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500. Dividend/profit remittance from a German UG is subject only to standard withholding tax (25% KapErtSt plus solidarity surcharge and, where applicable, church tax) or, for treaty/EU tax-exempt shareholders, zero withholding under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; no central bank approval or exchange allocation is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no forced export proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion into EUR, and no prescribed holding period for foreign-currency receivables. Export receipts may be retained in the original currency or charged out freely. The statutory obligation is purely statistical: under AWV Section 67, a resident recipient must report receipt of payment from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 to Deutsche Bundesbank no later than the fifth working day after the day of receipt, stating the means of payment, counterparty, amount, currency, and purpose code. For bulk dried goji berry exports, the underlying commercial invoice, customs export declaration (ATLAS/Exit Summary Declaration), and transport documents constitute the evidential base for the Bundesbank report.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), in association with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking conduct and prudential supervision and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) for administration of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for non-EU offshore digital electronic-money institutions; MODERATE for EEA-licensed EMI/PI passporting into Germany under PSD2. Legal constraints: any payment account provider must hold a valid BaFin banking license or an EEA passport with notification under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). Offshore EMIs from third countries cannot lawfully provide payment accounts or execute settlements for a German resident without a BaFin license/passport and will trigger correspondent-bank de-risking. EU-licensed EMIs are viable only for ancillary pre-funded treasury flows, not as the primary settlement account, because they are not licensed to grant credit, handle documentary credits, or accept term deposits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a German UG, inbound shareholder equity injection must be routed to the German operational treasury account via SWIFT MT103/MT202 covering message using the IBAN starting with DE and the bank BIC. Wire instruction must bear a clear purpose text, e.g. 'Kapitaleinlage / Stammeinlage in die UG' or 'equity contribution', must not reference goods/services, and must be supported in bank records by (i) notarised share subscription/resolution (Übernahmevertrag), (ii) bank-book confirmation of contribution, and (iii) registration-ready declaration to the commercial register. Where the shareholder is a non-resident, the inward payment creates a statistical declaration obligation to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500; the German receiving bank may require the customer to complete the AWV cross-border receipt report. There is no approval requirement for the contribution, but the funds must be credited to an account in the UG's own name and may not be intermingled with trade settlement; a dedicated capital reserve sub-account is recommended.Outbound settlements for procurement and trade payables should be executed over clear electronic banking channels with SWIFT MT103 customer-credit transfers; high-value or documented-basis consignments should use irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit subject to UCP600, negotiated through the German relationship bank's trade-finance desk. For each disbursement above EUR 12,500 to a non-resident, the German resident must ensure the bank submits the AWV cross-border payment report to Deutsche Bundesbank. The instruction field must describe the underlying transaction (e.g. 'Payment for dried organic goji berry goods, invoice AB-1234') and include reference numbers, commodity description, currency, and beneficiary account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) separately at 7%–17% depending on Hebesatz, producing a combined effective corporate burden of approximately 29%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% capital gains/dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax); treaty relief commonly reduces the rate to 15% or 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders; a full or 95% exemption may arise under § 8b KStG only where the receiving shareholder is a German corporation in specific intra-group structures.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

31 July of the calendar year following the tax year; automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second following year when the return is prepared and transmitted by a German tax advisor (Steuerberater).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory filing timeline as the corporate income tax return: 31 July of the following year, extended to 28/29 February of the second following year for tax-advisor-prepared returns.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is required for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 for goods and EUR 500,000 for services in the relevant fiscal year; Master File documentation is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue above EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting applies where the group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not impose a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation; however, German tax authorities apply a robust substance-over-form doctrine, § 42 AO abuse-of-rights rules, the OECD Principal Purpose Test through the Multilateral Instrument, and § 50d(3) EStG beneficial-ownership requirements. A UG exporting dried goji berries must maintain a real German office, local employees, board decision-making power, contractual risk management, and actual economic activities; otherwise treaty benefits can be denied and income may be reallocated to the non-resident parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years or a statutory fine; especially serious cases carry imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years; tax understatements are collected with 6% annual late interest; property can be seized; limitation period extends to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable under §§ 69, 71 AO and in practice may face attachment of personal assets, removal from office, travel bans, and imprisonment; civil restitution to the UG and creditors can additionally be ordered under § 823 BGB.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Tax treatment as a sham under § 41 AO and § 42 AO; the arrangement is disregarded and taxable income is reattributed to the true economic owner; treaty relief is denied under the Principal Purpose Test; 6% annual late interest and penalty assessments apply.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for defensive tax assessments, revocation of German residence permits, confiscation of assets, and criminal liability for facilitating tax evasion; cancellation of residency or entry bans may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income is re-estimated under § 162 AO; transfer pricing documentation penalties of 5%–10% of the adjustment amount can be imposed up to a maximum, in addition to the underlying tax; administrative fines and late payment interest apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes caused by the transfer pricing adjustment; misleading or unavailable documentation exposes them to criminal fines and, where tax evasion is proven, imprisonment.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under § 261 StGB; imprisonment up to 5 years, in aggravated cases up to 10 years; confiscation of corporate assets; banking license and account terminations; administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or more for institutional failures.

Director personal liability: Directors face direct criminal liability, personal monetary fines, travel bans, and seizure of personal property; if the UG is used as a façade for laundering, courts may disregard limited liability and hold directors personally liable for all corporate funds involved.
Customs and Product Compliance Violations

Statutory sanction: Seizure of goods, revocation of licenses, customs fines, re-assessment of customs duties and VAT, and possible ban on future exports; unlawful customs declarations may be prosecuted as fiscal fraud with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false customs declarations are personally responsible, face structural liability to customs authorities, and can suffer travel restrictions, financial asset seizure, and imprisonment; the corporate veil will not protect deliberate wrongdoing.
