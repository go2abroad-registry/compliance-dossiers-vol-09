Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registration, and cross-border VAT refunds; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

The GmbH must complete the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (statutory tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER and submit it to the locally competent Finanzamt; concurrently apply to BZSt for a VAT registration number under the national and EU OSS procedures. Foreign shareholders require no German TIN; the entity obtains a Steuernummer (corporate tax number) upon registration; a German tax representative is advisable for service-of-process. VAT ID issuance normally occurs within a few weeks after eligibility and identity verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | The GmbH is a statutory corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) with unlimited corporate tax liability on worldwide income based on registered seat and place of effective management in Germany; no pass-through treatment is possible. Trade tax applies at municipal level on business profits; permanent establishment principles govern foreign income allocation and exemption/credit under double tax treaties.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; the GmbH is mandatorily taxable at entity level. Available elections include: (1) corporate and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §14 KStG and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG requiring financial, economic, and organisational integration plus a profit-and-loss transfer agreement; (2) the OSS special scheme under §18j UStG covering B2C electronic services and intra-EU distance sales; (3) cash-basis accounting for VAT (Ist-Versteuerung) under §20 UStG; (4) VAT small-business exemption under §19 UStG, though generally unavailable to entrepreneurs established solely abroad. Hybrid mismatch rules (HibitG/ATAD II) impose mandatory anti-abuse adjustments without elective application.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de-minimis threshold for foreign entrepreneurs supplying taxable services or goods in Germany; a zero-EUR turnover trigger mandates VAT registration for a German-established GmbH. The intra-EU OSS distance-selling and B2C electronic-service threshold is EUR 10,000; the domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer) applies only to EU-established entrepreneurs and requires prior-year net turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000 (2025 proposed amendment: EUR 25,000 / EUR 100,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods exported to third countries qualify for 0% VAT under §4 No. 1a and §6 UStG provided the goods physically leave the EU and are evidenced by customs documentation. B2B services supplied to non-EU entrepreneurs have their place of supply at the recipient's establishment (§3a Abs. 2 UStG) and are therefore not taxable in Germany. B2C electronic/digital services (including stock-photo downloads and licensed generative visual assets) supplied to consumers outside the EU fall outside German VAT scope under §3a Abs. 3 UStG; B2C electronic services to EU consumers are taxable at the consumer's residence and must be reported via OSS at the applicable member-state rate.

  • ATLAS electronic export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Exportbegleitdokument (EAD)
  • Commercial invoice or pro-forma invoice with INCOTERMS
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR waybill (Frachtbrief)
  • Proof of arrival or recipient acknowledgement (Gelangensbestätigung for intra-EU movements; customs-stamped exit certificate for third-country exports)
  • Payment and remittance evidence (SWIFT statement, foreign inward remittance certificate, or equivalent payment-rail records)

Domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate effective 1 January 2025: every German-based supplier must be able to receive electronic invoices in an EN 16931-compliant structured format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0+); issuance obligations phase in from 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with 2026 turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other taxpayers. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime; invoices are transmitted via PEPPOL/email and must be archived in a GoBD-compliant, immutable digital format. Cash-register receipts fall under KassenSichV, which is not materially relevant to a purely electronic stock-photo platform.

German trade law imposes no statutory 180-day export-receipt repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects standard banking due-diligence (MaRisk) and documentary-credit practice. Export zero-rating claims are subject to tax-office audit (Betriebsprüfung), BZSt cross-border data exchange, and customs records verification. Fraudulent or unsubstantiated zero-rating claims trigger: penalties for tax evasion under §370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, up to 10 years in aggravated cases under §370 Abs. 3 AO and §26c UStG), retrospective VAT assessments with 6% annual interest (§233a AO), monthly late-payment surcharges (Säumniszuschläge up to 1%), revocation of export certificates, and personal joint-and-several liability of managing directors (Geschäftsführer) under §§34 and 71 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany and the euro area maintain full euro convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). No foreign-exchange license or prior central-bank approval is required for equity injection, operational cross-border payments, royalty payments, or profit remittance by a German GmbH. Practical deterrents are limited to AML/KYC bank diligence, statutory balance-of-payments reporting to the Bundesbank for individual cross-border transfers equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, and the standard German withholding-tax regimen on dividend distributions (25% plus solidarity surcharge, and church tax if applicable). Qualifying EU/EU-parent shareholders may apply the Parent-Subsidiary Directive for near-zero or reduced withholding; treaty-eligible non-resident shareholders may seek refund from the Federal Central Tax Office.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. A resident GmbH earning stock-photo and AI-visual licensing revenue from foreign buyers may maintain the proceeds in any currency, whether in a domestic or foreign account, and convert them at freely negotiated banking rates. The only binding obligation is statistical: under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), the resident company must report each cross-border payment or receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the bank or the central bank's electronic reporting portal.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), acting in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM). Banking conduct and anti-money-laundering enforcement are jointly overseen by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EU/EEA-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions passporting into Germany under PSD2; Low for offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs. An offshore EMI without a German or EU banking license cannot lawfully operate as the primary settlement account holder for a German GmbH, cannot legally accept deposits from German residents, and cannot issue German IBANs for the company. Where a fintech payment institution is used, the underlying clearing and settlement must still be routed through a fully licensed EU credit institution. The GmbH remains solely responsible for all treasury, AWV reporting, tax declaration, and AML compliance obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder equity is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the GmbH's German bank account. The payment order must include the ultimate beneficial owner's full name, the corporate shareholder reference, and a remittance field identifying the purpose explicitly as 'Stammeinlage / Kapitalrücklage / Sonstige Einlage'. The transaction must be classified under the Deutsche Bundesbank's balance-of-payments statistics using the appropriate AWV/Zahlungscode for direct investment or shareholder capital contribution; where the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, either the bank or the GmbH must submit the electronic AWV report. The bank's internal settlement instructions should preserve the remittance purpose so that the capital increase or contribution can be posted to statutory share capital and capital reserve in the annual financial statements, and the Managing Director must provide the bank with a confirmation of legal capacity and beneficial ownership at onboarding.The principal clearing rails for a stock-photo and AI-asset platform are: (a) SEPA Instant or ISO 20022 XML credit transfers for euro-denominated payouts to EU contributors, sub-agents, and SaaS vendors; (b) SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) for non-EU dollar, sterling, or yen royalty payouts; (c) payment-institution batch processing via an EU-licensed EMI for low-value, high-frequency contributor proceeds; (d) where a large software or content-library acquisition is being settled, a confirmed irrevocable documentary credit subject to UCP 600 may be used but is not typical for recurring royalty trades. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount (0.825% effective), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at typical effective rates of 14–17%, producing an aggregate effective corporate burden of approximately 30–33% in most German municipalities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains tax (Abgeltungsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax amount, resulting in a combined statutory withholding rate of 26.375% on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The annual corporate income tax return must be filed by 31 July of the year following the tax year. If the taxpayer is represented by a tax advisor, the tax authorities routinely extend the deadline to the end of February of the second year following the assessment period.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory regime as the corporate income tax return: 31 July of the following year; tax advisor representation extends the deadline to late February of the second following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold is fixed for a Local File; contemporaneous documentation of all controlled cross-border transactions with related parties is required. A Master File is generally expected in administrative practice where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory where the group's global consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany is not an Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction. However, economic substance is enforced indirectly through the general anti-abuse rule under § 42 AO, the beneficial ownership principle, and the Principal Purpose Test embedded in the OECD/G20 BEPS actions. A GmbH lacking genuine business activity, local employees, premises, or decision-making functions may be denied treaty benefits, have its transactions recharacterized, or face mandatory tax assessment by risk of fiscal presumption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to five years under § 370 AO; aggravated commercial cases carry imprisonment of up to ten years. Monetary fines calibrated to daily rates are applied. The evaded tax must be repaid with evasion interest at 6% per annum under § 235 AO.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes where they breach their statutory duties under §§ 34 and 69 AO. In criminal proceedings, travel limitations, asset freezing, and imprisonment may be imposed. Civil restitution to the tax authority may be enforced through corporate veil-piercing.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief to the non-resident shareholder, recharacterization of income under § 42 AO, revocation of VAT identification number, compulsory delisting from the Commercial Register, and back tax assessments with penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for resulting tax debts and social security contributions; civil courts may pierce the veil where the GmbH is undercapitalized and used as a sham. A director may also face disqualification or arrest warrant in tax fraud investigations.
Transfer Pricing / Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax audit adjustment of income, treatment of the difference as a constructive dividend, and imposition of a documentation penalty under § 162 AO of 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, minimum EUR 5,000 and maximum EUR 1,000,000 per audited transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors are held personally liable if the inadequate documentation results from their fault. Payment of the adjusted tax and penalties may be collected from their private property through § 69 AO, and criminal liability may follow for deliberate misstatements.
AML Compliance / Beneficial Ownership Filing

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG) of up to EUR 100,000 for ordinary violations and up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious administrative breaches. In addition, the supervisory authority may suspend payment processing or require immediate remedial action.

Director personal liability: The managing director and the AML compliance officer may be personally fined for failure to implement controls. Willful obstruction of supervision may constitute a criminal offence carrying imprisonment of up to two years, while civil liability extends to consequences of non-compliance.
