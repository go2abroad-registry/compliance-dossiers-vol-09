Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under § 5a GmbHG, the UG does not require the ordinary €25,000 minimum capital; the nominal cash share capital fixed in the Articles must be fully paid into a German business bank account in the company's name before registration. In-kind contributions and partial payment are not admissible. The company must retain 25% of annual net profits as a legal reserve until the share capital reaches €25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification approved by the local fire protection authority; structural load inspection and stability certificate from a licensed structural engineer accepted by the building authority; workplace clearance under the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) and the Industrial Safety and Health Ordinance (BetrSichV), including emergency lighting, ventilation, solvent storage, and electrostatic grounding; explosion-protection documentation for electrolyte filling and solvent-handling stations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.); the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the entity tax number, corporate tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. Tax registrations and tax questionnaires are generally submitted electronically through the ELSTER portal.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must submit the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The Finanzamt issues the German tax number (Steuernummer). A separate VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is requested from the BZSt, generally through the BZSt online procedure or through the tax registration process. A foreign entity carrying on taxable activities in Germany must register with the competent Finanzamt, provide incorporation, ownership, business-activity, management, banking, and beneficial-owner information, and obtain a German tax number and, where required, a VAT identification number. Non-resident entities are not generally required to appoint a German fiscal representative, although representation may be required or commercially advisable in specific VAT and customs circumstances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a capital company subject to entity-level corporate income taxation and does not have a general United States-style check-the-box election. The UG is generally subject to German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax. The German corporate-tax election under Section 1a of the Corporate Income Tax Act applies principally to eligible partnerships and is not an election available to a UG. For VAT, an eligible small business may elect the Kleinunternehmer exemption under Section 19 of the VAT Act, subject to the applicable turnover limits; the election generally prevents input VAT recovery. Eligible taxpayers may also apply for cash-basis VAT accounting under Section 20 of the VAT Act, subject to statutory conditions and the applicable turnover ceiling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies for German VAT registration where the business makes taxable supplies in Germany. The domestic small-business exemption may apply where prior-calendar-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and applicable transitional rules. The exemption is not a general VAT registration safe harbor and generally eliminates input VAT recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input VAT credit where the goods are transported or dispatched to the third country and the supplier retains prescribed documentary evidence of export and the transaction. The supplier must establish the foreign destination and the export connection; the customer’s non-EU residence alone is insufficient. Intra-EU supplies may qualify for VAT exemption where the goods are transported to another EU Member State, the customer has a valid VAT identification number, the reporting and invoice requirements are met, and transport evidence is retained. Services are generally taxed according to place-of-supply rules rather than automatically zero-rated: qualifying business-to-business services supplied to a non-German recipient may be outside German VAT, while specific services and use-and-enjoyment rules require separate analysis.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and export accompanying document, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic confirmation of exit or equivalent customs export evidence
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and applicable VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, freight forwarder confirmation, or courier export record
  • Packing list and product description sufficient to reconcile the goods with the customs declaration
  • Evidence linking the export movement to the relevant invoice and transaction
  • For intra-EU supplies, the customer VAT identification number, recapitulative statement, and transport evidence

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system or mandatory transaction-by-transaction digital stamping regime. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for qualifying domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices is being phased in: generally permitted transition formats and paper invoices remain available during the statutory transition period, with broad mandatory issuance applying from 1 January 2027 for larger businesses and from 1 January 2028 for other businesses, subject to the statutory turnover and transaction conditions. Compliant formats include EN 16931-based formats such as XRechnung and suitable ZUGFeRD versions. Invoices must contain required VAT particulars, and electronic transmission, retention, and audit-access requirements must be satisfied.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. If export exemption or an input VAT refund is claimed without satisfying the substantive or documentary requirements, the Finanzamt may deny the exemption, reassess output VAT, reverse input VAT credits, impose late-payment interest, and assess tax surcharges where applicable. Deliberate or reckless false VAT declarations may constitute tax evasion or tax understatement under the German Fiscal Code, potentially resulting in fines or criminal penalties; responsible officers and participants may face personal liability. Customs infringements may trigger customs debt, administrative fines, seizure, and additional sanctions. Records must be retained for the statutory periods and be sufficiently complete to withstand a VAT audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current and capital account convertibility with no exchange controls or licensing requirements. The Euro is fully convertible; cross-border payments are unrestricted but subject to statistical reporting thresholds under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV): payments and remittances exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within the statutory reporting period. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders by way of dividend distribution is lawful and free of exchange-control barriers. The operative hurdle is German dividend withholding tax at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%), which may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable double tax treaty. For a UG, profit distribution is permitted only after audited or notarially confirmed annual accounts and compliance with German insolvency-test and capital-maintenance rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory time limit for retaining export proceeds in foreign currency. Exporters may lawfully keep export receipts in foreign currency in German or foreign bank accounts. Receipts from non-resident customers exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics purposes by the 7th day of the month following the reporting month.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank; responsible for foreign exchange statistics and reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs) are regulated by BaFin under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and may serve as collection accounts or payment-passage vehicles, but they cannot grant commercial credit, issue letters of credit, or provide capital-escrow services. Their ability to route inbound capital injections is subject to enhanced KYC and stricter risk appetite. For this treasury blueprint, EMIs are legally permissible only as ancillary collection rails and must not displace a tier-1 German commercial bank as the primary liquidity and LC clearing counterparty.
