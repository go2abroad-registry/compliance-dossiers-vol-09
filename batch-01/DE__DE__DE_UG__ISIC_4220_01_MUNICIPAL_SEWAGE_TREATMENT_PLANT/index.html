Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) may be formed with a minimum share capital of EUR 1, but the full subscribed capital must be paid in cash before filing for registration and must remain at the free disposal of the managing director; a bank confirmation of payment is required. No escrow account is mandated by the GmbHG. The company must set aside 25% of annual net profit in a statutory reserve until the subscribed capital is increased to EUR 25,000.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; local Finanzamt for income, corporate, trade and VAT administration; ELSTER electronic filing portal (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German UG must first register with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt), obtain a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) from the competent Finanzamt, and submit the questionnaire via ELSTER or paper form. For VAT purposes, the entity applies separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) using the official application form; the local Finanzamt assigns a general tax number (Steuernummer) for regular VAT, income and trade tax filings. Non-resident shareholders and managing directors may file through a German tax representative; the process is digital and confirmation is typically issued within 2-4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide check-the-box pass-through elections for UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)). The UG is treated as a corporation taxable at entity level on corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Statutory elections available include VAT grouping (Organschaft) under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG, tax consolidation for corporate income tax (Organschaft for Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer), and the option to apply the small-enterprise exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Sec. 19 UStG, subject to turnover thresholds. Hybrid mismatch elections and adjustments are governed by the ATAD Implementation Act; no separate check-the-box regime exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-resident and resident businesses engaged in taxable supplies, VAT registration is mandatory without a general minimum threshold; the small-enterprise exemption applies only to resident entrepreneurs with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 in the prior year and EUR 100,000 in the current year (as of tax year 2025). For intra-EU distance sales, the EU-wide annual threshold of EUR 10,000 applies before the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime becomes mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to supplies of goods physically exported to territories outside the European Union (Sec. 6 UStG), and to certain services directly linked to export of goods, as well as to intra-community supplies (Sec. 4 No. 1 letter a and Sec. 6a UStG) where the acquirer is a VAT-registered business in another EU Member State. For design, engineering and commissioning services of reverse osmosis water treatment works, zero-rating is available only when the services are performed for a foreign entrepreneur or relate to exported movable goods and the recipient is an entrepreneur whose registered seat is outside Germany, and the services are not used in Germany. Export zero-rating requires evidence that the goods departed the territory of the European Union and that payment has been received in compliance with foreign exchange repatriation rules unless an exception applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Export Accompanying Document (EAD) or electronic ATLAS confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial Invoice and underlying supply contract
  • Proof of export commission/transfer order (Auftragserteilung)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency
  • Authorized Economic Operator (AEO) certificate or exporter registration, if applicable

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions: from 1 January 2025 every business must be able to receive e-invoices; phase-in for mandatory issuance applies gradually with all B2B issuers obliged by 1 January 2027 or later under the final statutory deadlines. Accepted formats are XRechnung and ZUGFeRD 2.x (Factur-X); uses of EDI are permitted. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; invoices are transmitted directly between parties and VAT returns are filed periodically. Export transactions must be reported to customs via ATLAS (Automotive/Tax and Liability Accounting System?) — more precisely the ATLAS export clearance system; no separate in-country electronic invoice clearance is required.

Export zero-rating is subject to rigorous customs and tax audit scrutiny. Any false declaration of export entitlement or missing export evidence triggers immediate VAT clawback (Steuernachforderung) plus interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum). If the exporter intentionally or negligently claims zero-rating without lawful basis, the tax office may impose a penalty surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag), fines under Sec. 382 AO (tax avoidance) or initiate criminal tax fraud proceedings under Sec. 370 AO, with imprisonment sanctions and unlimited financial penalties. Repeated violations can additionally lead to revocation of export privileges and joint liability of directors under German corporate laws.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime as an EU member state. Capital account transactions are unrestricted for residents and non-residents alike. Current account transactions, including trade remittances and service fees, are freely convertible without prior approval. Profit remittances from German UG entities to foreign shareholders are permitted subject only to standard corporate tax compliance, thin capitalization rules under § 8a KStG, and beneficial ownership declarations. No material convertibility hurdles exist for EU-based operations; non-resident ownership is explicitly allowed under German commercial law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German exporters are not subject to any mandatory surrender of foreign exchange proceeds. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency accounts offshore. Banks may request documentary proof of export transactions (commercial invoices, transport documents, certificates of origin) solely for tax reporting and AML verification. No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies; voluntary surrender may be arranged through correspondent banks for liquidity optimization.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMI providers (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business) are viable for limited cross-border payments and FX transfers but lack full corporate banking licenses under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz). Offshore digital EMIs face strict legal constraints including mandatory registration as payment institutions under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz) and compliance with EU AMLD5/6 directives. High-volume FX or treasury functions require full BaFin licensing; personal accounts do not extend to corporate treasury operations.

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%. A municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) also applies. Its effective rate depends on the municipality's Hebesatz; the statutory base multiplier is 3.5%, and common municipal rates produce an aggregate corporate burden generally between approximately 22% and 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German withholding tax on dividends, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in a combined domestic rate of 26.375%. A qualifying German corporate parent may generally obtain withholding relief for dividends under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation and Section 43b EStG, commonly requiring at least a 10% direct participation and satisfaction of anti-abuse and minimum-holding conditions. Treaty rates commonly reduce dividend withholding to 5% or 15%, depending on the treaty, ownership percentage, beneficial ownership, and applicable limitation-on-benefits or anti-abuse provisions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally by the last day of the seventh month following the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser is engaged, statutory extensions may apply under the applicable annual administrative rules. The tax authority may impose an earlier deadline in individual cases.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Periodic VAT returns are generally due by the tenth day after the end of the relevant period, subject to a permanent filing extension where available. The annual VAT return is generally due by the end of the seventh month following the calendar year, subject to applicable adviser extensions and administrative rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For mandatory contemporaneous documentation under Section 90 AO, a Local File is generally triggered where annual cross-border related-party transactions with a counterparty exceed EUR 6,000,000 for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties, and similar dealings. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group where the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding financial year. The tax authority may request documentation below these thresholds, and the documentation must generally be provided within 30 days of request.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore ESR statutes. Nevertheless, substance is material under German transfer-pricing rules, the beneficial-ownership requirement, the general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 AO, treaty anti-abuse provisions, Section 50d(3) EStG, CFC rules, anti-hybrid rules, and permanent-establishment analysis. The UG should maintain genuine German decision-making, appropriately qualified personnel, operational premises, commercial risk control, technical and project-management capability, bank-account control, contract authority, and contemporaneous records. A conduit, cash-box, or nominee structure without commercial functions may lose treaty benefits and may generate transfer-pricing adjustments, withholding tax, trade-tax exposure, and criminal risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Section 370 AO generally provides imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months up to ten years. Back taxes, interest, confiscation, and extended assessment periods may apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal tax liability under Section 69 AO where they intentionally or gross-negligently fail to assess, declare, withhold, or pay tax. Directors may face criminal prosecution, asset seizure, professional disqualification, and, in serious criminal cases, restrictions on movement or travel under judicial orders. Corporate limited liability does not protect deliberate personal misconduct.
VAT carousel, invoice fraud, and improper reverse charge

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion provisions under the AO, VAT assessment, denial of input VAT, joint and several liability in applicable cases, interest, late-payment surcharges, confiscation, and criminal fines or imprisonment may apply. Fraudulent invoice conduct can also trigger general criminal fraud provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for unpaid VAT and may be prosecuted where they knew or recklessly ignored the absence of supplies or the customer's VAT status. Insolvency-related payment restrictions can increase exposure after the company becomes unable to pay.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and incomplete documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make an adjustment under Section 162 AO, impose additional corporate income tax, trade tax, withholding tax, and interest, and apply documentation penalties. Failure to submit or materially incomplete documentation can trigger a minimum penalty generally of EUR 5,000; additional penalties may be based on the adjustment amount, including 5% to 10% in specified cases, and delay penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly approve artificial pricing, false documentation, or concealment of related-party transactions. Criminal tax liability may arise if the adjustment was deliberately concealed and tax was understated.
Withholding-tax failure and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: The German payer may be liable for the unwithheld tax, solidarity surcharge, interest, and administrative penalties. Treaty relief may be denied under Section 50d(3) EStG and general anti-abuse rules. Deliberate non-withholding may constitute tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are responsible for proper withholding and remittance and may face personal liability under Section 69 AO, criminal prosecution, and claims for restitution of the withholding tax. A foreign parent or nominee cannot automatically transfer the German payer's statutory withholding responsibility.
Shell company, beneficial-owner concealment, and AML breach

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act may cause administrative fines, reporting restrictions, beneficial-owner register consequences, and regulatory measures. Money laundering under Section 261 StGB may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; aggravated cases can result in imprisonment from six months up to ten years. Assets may be seized or confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face personal criminal liability, confiscation of personal assets, disqualification from management, and judicial travel restrictions. A limited-liability form does not shield individuals who personally participate in concealment or laundering.
Insolvency and late-filing misconduct

Statutory sanction: Failure to file insolvency in time under Section 15a InsO may carry imprisonment of up to three years or a fine, with aggravated consequences in serious cases. Directors may face repayment claims, insolvency administrator claims, tax claims, and disqualification.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments made after insolvency, unpaid payroll taxes, VAT, and other losses caused by breach of duty. Personal civil restitution, asset enforcement, management bans, and criminal proceedings may result.
Construction, environmental, and municipal-project licensing failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative orders, project suspension, permit withdrawal, regulatory fines, remediation costs, contractual damages, exclusion from public procurement, and criminal sanctions under applicable environmental, construction, occupational-safety, and procurement laws may apply. Tax deductions and input VAT may also be challenged where transactions are unlawful or fictitious.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines or criminal liability for knowingly authorizing unlawful operations, environmental damage, corruption, or safety violations. Civil claims may include remediation, restitution, third-party damages, and public-authority recovery costs.
Corruption, public procurement, and escrow abuse

Statutory sanction: Criminal bribery, fraud, embezzlement, procurement exclusion, confiscation, contract termination, civil damages, and imprisonment may apply. Payments may be non-deductible for tax purposes, and related tax understatement may create separate tax-evasion liability.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be personally prosecuted and may face imprisonment, fines, asset confiscation, director disqualification, travel restrictions, and civil restitution. Corporate limited liability does not protect individuals who authorize or benefit from corrupt or misappropriated payments.
