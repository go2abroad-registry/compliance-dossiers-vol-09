Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern - BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.); Local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for income, corporate, trade, and VAT registration; electronic portal ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A resident UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must first register under the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung filed electronically via ELSTER with the competent Finanzamt based on the registered seat or place of management. The Finanzamt assigns a local Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. For intra-community or EU-wide transactions, the UG must separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using form USt 1 A or online. Foreign non-resident owners do not require a separate local tax ID unless the UG is deemed to have a German permanent establishment or the owner personally triggers German-source taxable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German law. Mandatory entity-level taxation applies to a UG. Tax consolidation (Organschaft) may be elected for corporate income tax under Sec. 14 KStG and for VAT under Sec. 2 (2) No. 2 UStG, subject to profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial/economic integration. Limited hybrid mismatch elections are available under Sec. 4k EStG to align tax characterisation with EU ATAD directive.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No absolute VAT registration threshold for a UG; VAT liability arises from every taxable supply in Germany. The small-entrepreneur exemption under Sec. 19 UStG applies only if prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000, but a UG as a commercial legal entity generally cannot invoke this exemption without losing VAT deduction; mandatory registration is required when taxable supplies are made in the course of commercial enterprise regardless of turnover level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third-country destinations (outside the EU) qualify for zero-rating under Sec. 4 No. 1 and Sec. 6 UStG if the goods actually leave the EU customs territory and are exported by the supplier. Intra-community supplies to another EU Member State qualify for zero-rating under Sec. 4 No. 1 and Sec. 6a UStG when the acquirer has a valid EU VAT ID and the supply is reported in the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List). Offshore services (e.g., software or digital pet-care services) may be outside the scope of German VAT under the place-of-supply rules of Sec. 3a UStG if supplied to a B2B customer established outside Germany, thereby enabling tax-free treatment with input VAT deduction only if the customer is a VAT-registered entrepreneur.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/Ausfuhranmeldung with successful export release)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR Road Consignment Note evidencing physical dispatch
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT advice showing export consideration credited to a German bank account

Since 1 January 2025, every domestic B2B invoice in Germany must be issued in a structured electronic format (E-Rechnung) that complies with EN 16931. Accepted formats include XRechnung, ZUGFeRD (hybrid PDF/XML), and EDI formats (ANSI X12, EDIFACT D96A). Invoices must be transmitted via the Peppol network, email with XML attachment, or direct API integration. No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or central government invoice clearance system exists for standard B2B sales. Public-sector e-invoicing via Peppol/Ugov remains mandatory for supplies to federal authorities.

German tax authorities closely audit export zero-rating evidence. Missing or fraudulent documentation triggers retrospective VAT assessment at 19% plus subsidiary liability. Statutory default interest accrues at 0.5% per month on the assessed underpayment. Administrative fines up to EUR 30,000 apply for misuse of VAT identification numbers under Sec. 26a UStG. Criminal tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO may lead to imprisonment of up to five years and confiscation of profit. Improperly refunded input VAT may be clawed back together with late-payment penalties and penalty surcharges up to EUR 25,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full current and capital account convertibility as part of the EU's free movement of capital. No exchange controls or central bank approval are required for inward or outward capital transfers. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject only to 26.375% (25% + 5.5% solidarity surcharge) capital gains/corporate withholding tax on dividends to corporations unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty (DTA), or to 26.375% (incl. solidarity levy) on income unless exempt. The EUR is fully convertible; no trade-invoicing currency restrictions apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds or conversion into EUR for German resident entities. Export receipts can be retained in foreign currency in a German domestic bank account. However, all cross-border receipts and payments exceeding EUR 12,500, including export proceeds, must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven (7) calendar days for balance-of-payments statistics under AWV Section 67/68. No minimum conversion quota or timeline applies beyond Anti-Money Laundering (AML) source-of-funds checks performed by the bank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in cooperation with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for licensing and supervision of banks, and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) on restrictive measures, all operating under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German law permits electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions licensed in the EEA to offer payment accounts on a passporting basis. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Intergiro, or Verto can provide IBANs for EUR receipts and cross-border clearing, but typically do not constitute a German domestic bank account for all statutory purposes (e.g., trade register deposit accounts, court escrow, or certain tax-relevant direct debits). In addition, the UG must maintain a German payment institution/bank relationship to ensure full SEPA direct debit capability, local-language customer service and compatibility with BaFin anti-money-laundering expectations. Offshore EMIs are acceptable for auxiliary transaction but cannot replace a domestic KYC-aligned operational current account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder equity (Stammeinlage) or shareholder loans: standard SEPA/SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the UG's German bank account, using payment purpose code 'CAPITAL' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' and narrative 'Equity contribution / Stammeinlage' or 'Loan under agreement dated [x]'. For non-EU originating parties, the beneficiary bank will request a SWIFT MT202 cover if the credit is temporarily forwarded via a correspondent. Any receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 from a non-German source must be reported to the Bundesbank under AWV Section 67 within 7 days; no administrative authorization is required. If the capital injection exceeds a threshold requiring foreign direct investment notification (generally non-EU direct investment in German security publisher or critical infrastructure), the UG may need to file a BAFA FDI screening notice, but a standard e-commerce pet supplies business qualifies as low-risk and does not trigger substantive review. For all wire inbounds, the UG must have a signed shareholder resolution and a complete updated cap table to promptly satisfy source-of-funds queries.Clearing rails for trade settlements include: (i) Core SEPA Direct Debit and SEPA Credit Transfer for intra-EU supplier invoices (standard MT103 within SEPA transparently); (ii) SWIFT MT103 for third-country supplier payments, including goods from China or the US, referencing the commercial invoice number and ISIC scope; (iii) Documentary letters of credit (Irrevocable Commercial LC) governed by UCP 600 for high-value bulk purchases from uncleared markets or high-risk suppliers; and (iv) Receivables purchase/merchant payment platforms (e.g., Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer) that settle into the UG's SEPA account after occurrence of the e-commerce merchant transaction. Each outgoing payment exceeding EUR 12,500 requires AWV reporting to the Bundesbank within 7 days. For supplier countries subject to sanctions or OFAC list screening, full KYC of the beneficiary and transaction invoice must be included to avoid bank blocking. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer + 0.825% Solidaritätszuschlag + municipality-dependent Gewerbesteuer of approximately 14-17%; aggregate effective cash tax rate approximately 30-33% on taxable commercial profit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag, aggregate 26.375% on gross dividends distributed by the UG. A 0% rate applies if the parent is a corporation resident in an EU/EEA state and holds at least 10% of the UG's share capital for an uninterrupted period of 12 months (Parent-Subsidiary Directive / §43b EStG). Treaty reductions generally apply where statutory withholding is otherwise reduced to 5% or 0% depending on ownership level and the applicable DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory filing deadline is 31 July following the calendar year-end for electronically submitted returns; paper returns must be filed by 31 May. Where a certified tax adviser prepares the return, an automatic extension to 28 February of the second year following the tax year applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation are required in Germany under §90 Abs. 3 AO and Gewinnabgrenzungsaufzeichnungsverordnung if the aggregate annual value of cross-border controlled transactions with a related party exceeds EUR 100,000. A Master File is required if the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million, and Country-by-Country Reporting is triggered at EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those in Cyprus or the UAE, but the substance of the German UG is decisive for treaty entitlement and tax administration. Under §50d Abs. 3 EStG and the general anti-abuse rule of §42 AO, a UG with no real economic activity, no premise, no employees, and no local decision-making will be denied treaty benefits, its foreign shareholder may be taxed on deemed income, and the company's separate personality may be disregarded for tax collection. For a UG operating a German e-commerce business, substance is evidenced by the German registered office, managing director resident in Germany, employment contracts, local inventory or fulfillment operations, bank accounts, and substantive decision-making documentation for pricing and marketing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Suppressed E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under §370 AO punishable with imprisonment from six months up to five years, or up to ten years for particularly serious cases, plus monetary fine up to EUR 1.5 million; back-tax interest of 6% per annum and late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Geschäftsführer is personally liable under §34 AO and §69 AO for taxes withheld but not remitted and for corporate tax debts where the director fails to file accurate returns or declares insolvency in due time; criminal conviction may additionally lead to travel bans, asset freezes, and a ban from holding director positions in the EU.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: German tax authorities may make a discretionary income adjustment under §162 AO; a transfer pricing documentation surcharge of 5% of the adjustment amount, minimum EUR 1,000, is imposed if documentation is missing; if intentional, penalties may be treated as §370 AO evasion.

Director personal liability: The managing director may be jointly and severally liable for the additional tax assessment plus surcharges if the transfer pricing documentation was knowingly deficient; directors of the UG risk personal contribution orders in insolvency and disqualification proceedings.
Artificial Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of the UG's direct tax treaty benefits, recharacterization of payments as constructive dividends subject to 26.375% withholding, cancellation of rollover relief, and application of §42 AO anti-abuse add-back; criminal fines and reputational debarment from public tenders.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for the additional corporate tax and withholding tax under §69 AO; travel bans may be imposed where deemed tax debt exceeds EUR 500 and there is a flight risk; repeated shell-company operations may be prosecuted as abusing legal structures.
Anti-Money-Laundering / UBO Regime Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG) of up to EUR 150,000 per violation for ordinary non-compliance and up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious breaches; criminal prosecution under §261 StGB for money laundering if proceeds stem from tax evasion or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors are directly liable for GwG violations and for failing to prevent the UG from being used as a conduit; sanctions include personal fines, seizure of corporate funds, disqualification from acting as managers in financial or commercial companies, and imprisonment up to five years.
