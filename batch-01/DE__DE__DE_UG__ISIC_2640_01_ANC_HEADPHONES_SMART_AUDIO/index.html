Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

UG has no fixed minimum share capital: the Articles must fix cash share capital of at least EUR 1 (each share EUR 1); contributions in kind are statutorily excluded under § 5a(2) GmbHG. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern - BZSt) and the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office); electronic filing and registration via the ELSTER portal (https://www.elster.de); issuance of the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is centralized at BZSt.

E-government filing portal

A UG incorporated under German law must file the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt through ELSTER immediately after notarial incorporation/registration, thereby obtaining a Steuernummer (tax number) covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. The VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is then applied for separately from BZSt using Formular USt 1 TN. Non-resident foreign entities without a German establishment must register directly with BZSt using the questionnaire for VAT registration of foreign entrepreneurs (umsatzsteuerliche Erfassung) and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative; alternatively, distance sales can be declared under the EU One Stop Shop (OSS) in the supplier's home Member State up to the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold. In addition to the VAT ID, foreign sellers invoicing German VAT-registered business customers need a German Steuernummer for reverse-charge and EC Sales List purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime; a UG is a domestic capital company mandatorily taxable at entity level at 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available elections include: (i) fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §14 KStG / §2 UStG combining a controlling corporation and its financially and organisationally integrated subsidiary for corporate tax and VAT; (ii) the small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, §19 UStG) where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 in the year of business commencement and EUR 100,000 in the preceding year; and (iii) the option under §9 UStG to subject real-estate letting to VAT. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; anti-hybrid rules under §4k EStG deny deductions in cross-border mismatched arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold for resident German entrepreneurs: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply. For non-resident suppliers, German VAT applies to distance sales of ANC headphones and similar goods from the first euro under the EU E-commerce VAT Package, but the Union-wide EUR 10,000 distance-sales threshold allows declaration through the OSS regime instead of direct German registration; non-resident B2B service supplies are effectively taxed in Germany under the reverse-charge mechanism without mandatory local registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate (Ausfuhrlieferung, §6 UStG) applies when the UG exports physical goods, including ANC headphones and smart audio devices, to a territory outside the EU, provided the goods leave Germany under a valid customs export declaration, the supplier substantiates the export transport, and the export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) is retained in the accounting records. Intra-Community B2B supplies are exempt at 0% (innergemeinschaftliche Lieferung, §6a UStG) only if the customer is a VAT-registered entrepreneur in another Member State, the goods physically move to that Member State, and the supplier files timely EC Sales Lists. The UG may also zero-rate qualifying B2B services supplied to non-resident customers under the reverse-charge regime. Direct B2C e-commerce exports outside the EU are zero-rated; B2C distance sales to EU consumers are generally taxed at the destination rate via OSS.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung and Export Accompanying Document, processed through the German ATLAS customs system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing cross-border transport of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement evidence (supporting evidence; German law additionally requires the electronic export confirmation (elektronischer Ausfuhrnachweis) from customs, a commercial invoice, and transport documents)

Germany has introduced mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung): from 1 January 2025 every domestic VAT-registered business must be able to receive structured e-invoices; issuance obligations are phased in during 2025/2026, with full mandatory issuance for all domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2027. Acceptable formats are XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, and EDI-compliant syntax. Germany does not operate a real-time tax-authority clearance, API-based fiscal stamping, or a central electronic invoice clearance database; VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and OSS returns are submitted electronically through ELSTER without requiring prior pre-clearance from the tax authority.

German export zero-rating is not statutorily conditioned on foreign-exchange repatriation; the 180-day figure is the pipeline's standard documentary benchmark. However, the export proof must be available at the time of the tax assessment and audit. If the 0% rate is claimed without valid export documentation, the Finanzamt retroactively revokes the zero-rating and assesses 19% German VAT on the sales price, adding late-payment interest of 0.5% per month under §233a AO and late-filing penalties. Intentional or grossly negligent false zero-rating claims qualify as tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under §370 AO, punishable by fines or imprisonment of up to five years, increasing to up to ten years for especially severe cases (§370a AO); managing directors face personal liability under §§69 and 71 AO, and proceeds may be confiscated under §73 AO. The standard limitation period is five years, extended to ten years in cases of deliberate tax evasion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Germany operates an open capital and current account without exchange controls, in compliance with EU Article 63 TFEU and IMF Article VIII obligations. The UG may freely receive equity injections, shareholder loans, intercompany payments, export proceeds, and may remit dividends, royalties, or loan repayments in EUR, USD, CNY, or any freely convertible currency without central bank approval. Practical hurdles are purely documentary: (a) every cross-border payment received or made by the UG must be declared to the Deutsche Bundesbank in payment statistics if the aggregate amount with the same foreign counterparty exceeds EUR 50,000 per month; (b) goods-export receipts from non-residents must be reported when the payment exceeds EUR 12,500 per reporting month; (c) dividend distributions require proof of withholding tax treatment, with a German statutory withholding of 26.375% (incl. solidarity surcharge) reduced under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive to 0% for an EU corporate shareholder holding 10% or more, or under applicable treaties to 5% (US) or 10% (China, China holding at least 25%).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists under German law. The UG may retain export proceeds indefinitely in EUR, USD, CNY, or any third-country currency in German domestic accounts or foreign accounts, with no statutory repatriation timeline. The only obligation is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank: invoiced goods-export receipts must be reported if they exceed EUR 12,500 per calendar month, and all other payment claims against foreign debtors must be declared at month-end if the aggregate foreign receivables exceed EUR 5,000,000. Non-compliance is an administrative offence under Section 23 AWG with fines of up to EUR 30,000, but does not affect the legal availability of the funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) is the monetary authority and administrator of the German external payment statistics system under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV); BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) supervises banking conduct and Anti-Money Laundering compliance; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) administers foreign trade restrictions under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the CIT amount, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); aggregate effective rate approximately 30% (varies by municipality, commonly 29.8%-31.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375% aggregate German withholding tax on dividends distributed by a UG. Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU), the rate is reduced to 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the UG's capital for an uninterrupted period of at least 12 months. Various German double tax agreements (DTAs) further reduce the rate to 5% or 10% (e.g., Germany–Switzerland: 5% for 10% shareholding).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory for German resident entities (including UG) where cross-border transactions with related parties exceed EUR 6,000,000 in aggregate for goods or EUR 3,000,000 for services per fiscal year. Master File mandatory for German group entities that are part of a group with worldwide consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the arm's length principle under § 88a AO, the substance-over-form doctrine, and the general anti-abuse rule (§ 42 AO). Although no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist as in offshore centres, a UG must maintain observable substance—physical office, employee payroll, management decisions taken in Germany—to defend transfer pricing positions, secure DTA treaty protection, and avoid recharacterization as a letterbox entity. The German CFC rules (§§ 7-13 AStG) additionally impose current taxation on low-taxed passive income held through controlled foreign companies if the German UG exercises dominant influence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO: imprisonment for up to 5 years or a monetary fine; aggravated cases (commercial-scale or abuse of authority) punishable by 6 months to 10 years' imprisonment. Fines are based on personal profit and can reach €1,000,000; additional late-payment interest of 0.5% per month accrues.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally jointly and severally liable under § 69 AO for unpaid corporate taxes and ancillary claims if they breach their statutory duty to pay company taxes, and this breach causes a revenue shortfall. Criminal conviction may further trigger a travel ban, forfeiture of assets, and disqualification from serving as a director in any German company.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 AO, the tax authorities may estimate taxable income with a punitive margin. Penalty amounts to 5% of the transfer pricing adjustment, minimum €5,000 and maximum €1,000,000 per audit; for late submission, an administrative surcharge of €100 to €1,000 is imposed. Interest of 6% per annum on additional tax assessments accrues.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the penalty payments if they failed to establish an adequate transfer pricing control system or consciously allowed the documentation deficiency. In case of intentional TP mispricing to evade tax, criminal liability as a principal or accomplice leads to imprisonment and personal seizure of assets.
Anti-Substance / Shell Abuse (Letterbox Company)

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the structure under § 42 AO, reattribute income to the German shareholders, and apply deemed dividend treatment. Retroactive tax assessments with a 10-year limitation period, 6% annual interest, and penalty up to 15% of the amount assessed. Criminal tax fraud charges may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors as legal representatives are liable for company taxes to the extent they caused the artificial structure; personal travel bans may be imposed during criminal proceedings; imprisonment up to 5 years for direct involvement in letterbox arrangements intended to evade German taxes.
Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under § 261 StGB: imprisonment for 3 to 5 years (aggravated up to 10 years) and confiscation of corporate assets. Administrative fines of up to €1,000,000 under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) for regulatory violations; trade license may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal prosecution for knowingly or negligently participating in money laundering; civil compensation claims from damaged parties; personal travel restrictions and extradition risk; loss of corporate veil protection where the UG was used as a mere instrument for illicit transactions.
