Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister), Section B (HRB), maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht München) in the district of the registered office, operating through the electronic court portal (eHunter/XJustiz).

Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation, either an individual Gesellschaftsvertrag or the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll), accompanied by the application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) signed by the founding Managing Director and transmitted electronically by the notary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for the UG (§ 5a(1) GmbHG); however, each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 1.00 and the full subscribed capital must be paid in cash and be at the free disposal of the Managing Director before registration, evidenced by a bank confirmation; no statutory escrow account is prescribed, but in-kind contributions require a formation audit report; the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until registered capital reaches EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG).

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the Munich Fire Brigade / Brandschutzdienststelle covering escape routes, fire-extinguishing equipment, alarm systems and, for larger premises, proof of the required fire-resistance classification; structural-load clearance from the building-supervision authority (Bauaufsicht of Landeshauptstadt München) supported by a certified static engineer's load statement; occupational-safety clearance from the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt under the Workplaces Ordinance (ArbStättV) and DGUV Vorschrift 1 employer obligations; staff handling food must additionally hold certificates under §§ 42–43 of the Infection Protection Act (IfSG).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for the UG (§ 5a(1) GmbHG); however, each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 1.00 and the full subscribed capital must be paid in cash and be at the free disposal of the Managing Director before registration, evidenced by a bank confirmation; no statutory escrow account is prescribed, but in-kind contributions require a formation audit report; the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until registered capital reaches EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT IDs and the local tax office (Finanzamt) for income/corporate/trade tax registration; official portal: ELSTER (https://www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File for commercial registration with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt) and register the UG in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Step 2: Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER to the local Finanzamt, stating the UG's shareholders, managing director, business activities, and expected turnover; the Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer. Step 3: If intra-Community or cross-border supplies are intended, apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the official application (Formular USt 1). Step 4: Register any employees for wage tax and obtain an employer tax number (not applicable if no payroll). Step 5: Maintain proof of economic substance in Germany, especially if foreign-owned, to prevent denial of treaty benefits and to confirm management seat.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime. The principal statutory elections are: (i) Organschaft (corporate/national tax group) under Sec. 14 KStG and Sec. 2(2) no. 2 UStG, allowing a fiscal unit for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; (ii) real estate election for option on VAT on real property leases under Sec. 9 UStG; and (iii) anti-hybrid elections are not elective—Germany applies mandatory hybrid-mismatch limitations under Sec. 4k EStG and Sec. 8b KStG pursuant to ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for all taxable persons without a general economic threshold; the small-business exemption (Sec. 19 UStG) applies only to prior-year turnover ≤ €22,000 and current-year turnover ≤ €50,000, which is unavailable for an UG operating a franchise licensing/syrup supply model; the €10,000 OSS threshold applies to intra-Community distance sales of goods only.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to a third country qualify for 0% VAT upon proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) under Sec. 6 UStG, including a matching customs declaration and transport evidence. Intra-Community supplies are zero-rated under Sec. 6a UStG if the buyer provides a valid VAT ID and the goods physically leave Germany. B2B services (including franchise licensing fees and royalties) are generally not subject to German VAT when supplied to a non-resident entrepreneur under the place-of-supply rule in Sec. 3a UStG; for EU customers, the customer's VAT ID must be declared, and a reverse charge mechanism applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS successfully released export declaration / Ausfuhranmeldung)
  • Commercial Invoice (with full seller-buyer details and Incoterms)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (transport document evidencing departure from EU)
  • Proof of Export (Ausfuhrnachweis) – either electronic customs confirmation or stamped export accompanying document
  • Payment Evidence / Inward Remittance Certificate (bank statement verifying foreign settlement)
  • Intra-Community dispatch log and matched recap statement (for EU suppliers)

From 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B invoices must be issued in a structured electronic format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) compliant with CEN EN 16931; recipients must be able to receive them from 1 January 2027; B2G invoices must be sent via the e-invoice portal (PEPPOL). Germany does not mandate real-time tax clearance, fiscal stamping, or API transaction approval; the tax authority does not operate a centralized clearance network, but XML schema validation and e-invoice metadata retention are required.

If the German tax examination determines that export zero-rating was claimed without substantive export or with fabricated documentation, the full German VAT becomes immediately due, plus monthly default interest (Section 233a AO) at 0.5% per month and late-payment penalties. Deliberate evasion triggers criminal prosecution under Sec. 370 AO with imprisonment up to 10 years, fines, and confiscation; additionally, Sec. 26c UStG imposes administrative fines up to €30,000 for e-invoicing breaches; retrospective VAT assessments may extend up to 10 years for fraud (Sec. 169(2) AO), and the exporter must re-introduce the VAT amount into the tax return, with no right to refund until corrected.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the liberalized provisions of the AWV. There are no exchange controls, no central bank surrender requirements, and no discriminatory tariffs on profit remittances for a German UG. Cross-border dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely remittable in EUR or any convertible currency; practical hurdles are tax-related rather than FX-related, primarily the requirement to file withholding tax returns, invoke treaty relief under the applicable DTA, or rely on the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and to meet Bundesbank statistical reporting thresholds for cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts and to determine conversion timing. The only binding requirement is statistical in nature: under the AWV, all inbound and outbound cross-border payments with non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the end of the reporting month, including a payment purpose code and the underlying transaction classification. Non-compliance may result in administrative fines, but the rule does not restrict the currency, amount, or conversion rate.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF); the Bundesbank administers statistical foreign trade and cross-border payment reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — offshore digital EMI platforms are viable as complementary ancillary rails for collecting franchise royalties, settling supplier invoices, and funding cross-border disbursements, but they cannot replace a German domestic bank account. An offshore EMI must hold a valid payment institution licence passed into Germany under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and must be registered with BaFin or operate under an EEA passport; UK-only EMIs without an EEA passport have lost cross-border market access after Brexit. The risk factors include the FCA-to-BaFin gap, narrower segregation of client funds, absent German IBAN issuance, and reduced acceptance by German tax authorities and Finanzamt for cash-flow evidence. A full domestic commercial bank account remains mandatory for registered capital administration, statutory profit retention, and dividend distribution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% federal component (15% Körperschaftsteuer plus Solidaritätszuschlag of 0.825%, i.e. 5.5% on the corporate tax), before municipal trade tax; aggregate effective burden of approximately 30.2%–33.0% depending on the municipal Gewerbesteuer multiplier (standard trade tax base 3.5% × municipality Hebesatz of 410%–490%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the dividend tax, resulting in a combined statutory burden of 26.375% on gross distributions (§ 43a(1) No. 1 EStG), to be withheld and remitted by the UG as the paying company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Standard statutory filing deadline: 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end (§ 149(3) AO); automatic extension: 31 July of the second calendar year following the fiscal year-end when the return is prepared by a certified tax advisor (Steuerberater); electronic submission to the competent Finanzamt is mandatory in all cases.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 9 – Steuernummer / Identification fields Official tax number allocated by the local Finanzamt; legal form (UG haftungsbeschränkt), commercial register number, registered seat, fiscal year dates, and foreign ownership data. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation under § 90(3) AO and the GAufzV is triggered when annual cross-border related-party transactions for goods and services exceed EUR 5,000,000, or when non-ordinary transactions (loans, IP licensing, royalty flows) exceed EUR 500,000; a Master File is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000, with a corresponding CbC Report; documentation must be submitted within 60 days (30 days for extraordinary transactions) upon request, failing which the tax authority may issue delay fees (Verzögerungsgeld) and make adverse income estimations with a penalty surcharge under § 162(4) AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; the equivalent substance-over-form doctrine is embedded in § 42 AO (abusive arrangements), § 8c KStG (loss forfeiture), §§ 7–14 AStG (controlled foreign company rules), and § 50d(3) EStG (denial of treaty entitlement). A UG acting as the global franchise IP holder must demonstrate real premises, qualified staffing, actual management decision-making in Germany, and meaningful operational substance in IP development and marketing; otherwise the Finanzamt will disregard the structure, attribute income to the foreign parent, and deny both treaty benefits and intercompany expense deductibility.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Concealed Franchise Royalty Income (§ 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 10 years in particularly serious cases (§ 370(3) AO); monetary fines up to EUR 50,000; default interest at 6% per annum; extension of the assessment limitation period to 10 years; seizure and confiscation of the evaded amounts plus interest.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for the underpaid corporate taxes plus interest under § 69 AO in conjunction with § 34 AO; civil restitution is enforceable against the director's personal assets; a travel ban and passport seizure apply when tax arrears exceed EUR 500; criminal conviction can lead to up to 10 years of imprisonment; piercing the corporate veil applies in undercapitalized UG cases under the Existenzvernichtungshaftung case law (Bremer Vulkan).
Failure to File, Pay, or Cooperate (§§ 152, 240, 378 AO)

Statutory sanction: Verspätungszuschlag of 0.25% per month, capped at 10% of the assessed tax (minimum EUR 25, maximum EUR 50,000); Säumniszuschlag of 1% per month on the unpaid tax; default interest of 0.5% per month; estimated assessments to the detriment of the taxpayer; delay fees for non-cooperation and penalty surcharges up to 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment; regulatory fines up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors must exercise the UG's statutory duties, including timely filing, payment, and insolvency triggering; personal liability arises under § 69 AO for all amounts the UG could not pay due to the director's breach; failure to file for insolvency within three weeks of illiquidity creates civil liability to creditors under § 15b InsO, including withheld wage tax and employer social security contributions; non-resident directors face the same criminal exposure and may be prevented from entering or leaving Germany where arrears exceed EUR 500.
Shell Company and Letterbox Substance Abuse (§ 42 AO; GwG/Transparenzregister)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the legal structure under § 42 AO with income reattribution to the foreign parent; denial of treaty relief under § 50d(3) EStG; disallowance of losses and intercompany royalty costs; administrative fine of up to EUR 1,000,000 under § 334 GwG for identity registration violations; additional assessments with 6% default interest.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability for wilfully false beneficial-ownership declarations (imprisonment up to 1 year or fines); personal liability under § 64 GmbHG analog / § 15b InsO for payments made after insolvency, including tax remittances; the corporate veil is pierced in asset-mixing cases, making shareholders and directors jointly liable for all unpaid German corporate taxes and franchise-related debts.
Withholding Tax and Treaty Relief Abuse (§§ 50d(3), 4j EStG)

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief by the BZSt; recharacterisation as a hidden profit distribution subject to 26.375% withholding tax, with interest at 6% per annum; disallowance of the royalty deduction under § 4j EStG; administrative fines under § 50d EStG; amendment of assessments within the extended 10-year period; criminal prosecution under § 370 AO for withholding tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for the unwithheld withholding tax under § 69 AO and must make the company's tax obligations possible through proper liquidity management; travel restrictions and passport seizure apply to non-resident directors where withholding debts exceed EUR 500; imprisonment of up to 5 years for simple and up to 10 years for aggravated evasion; civil restitution claims by the tax authority against the director's personal estate and subsequent ban from serving as managing director in Germany.
