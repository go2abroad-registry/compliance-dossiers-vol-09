A purely virtual office consisting only of a postal address and no operational physical premises will not satisfy German commercial register and registration requirements for a manufacturing entity. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG has no fixed minimum capital; share capital must be at least EUR 1, be fully paid in cash before registration, be freely disposable to the managing director, and be evidenced by bank confirmation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for EU/third-country VAT identification; local tax office (Finanzamt) for domestic tax registration and administration; official electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or registering a branch in Germany must apply to the competent local Finanzamt for a domestic tax identification number (Steuernummer) using form UStVA and registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung). For cross-border intra-Community transactions and refunds, a separate EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) must be obtained from the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt). The application must be filed before commencing taxable activity; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer, and the BZSt issues the VAT ID with an 'DE' prefix upon verification of identity and business purpose.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not apply check-the-box elections. A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is inherently opaque for income tax and VAT purposes. Elections available include forming an Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT where a controlling parent and financially integrated subsidiary are treated as one taxable person for VAT and one taxpayer for income tax; and applying small-enterprise treatment under Section 19 UStG only if the entity's prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed €50,000 (foregoing input VAT recovery).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general mandatory VAT registration threshold applies to manufacturing businesses; any person who performs taxable supplies in Germany must register. For small entrepreneurs, an optional exemption under Section 19 UStG applies when prior calendar-year turnover did not exceed €22,000 and current-year turnover is not anticipated to exceed €50,000. For cross-border distance sales and electronic platforms, special thresholds apply as per EU e-commerce rules (€10,000 for intra-Community distance sales, and €150,000 for imported goods subject to IOSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third-country territories are zero-rated under Section 4 No. 1(a) of the German VAT Act (UStG), provided that the goods actually leave the EU through the exporter's own export. Intra-Community supplies to EU-based VAT-registered customers are also zero-rated under Section 4 No. 1(b) UStG if the customer's VAT ID is valid and the transport evidence is documented. The UG must hold proof of export (Gelangensbestätigung), commercial invoice, and transport logs; for services related to goods and ancillary services (e.g., freight or customs handling) zero-rating may apply to directly linked services under stricter conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/ATLAS customs declaration with MRN)
  • Export accompanying document / Proof of export (Gelangensbestätigung)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or CMR/air waybill evidencing dispatch to non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Zahlungsbestätigung / bank credit advice) evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Germany does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement. Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies of resident taxable persons was phased in from 1 January 2025: recipients must be able to process electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) and transmission must be interoperable. There is no national continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance system; invoices must be retained in a form compliant with GoBD and made available to tax auditors on request.

German tax authorities require export evidence to be compiled promptly and retained for at least 10 years. Where zero-rating is claimed without adequate documentary proof or with intent to evade, the VAT is assessed retrospectively plus interest (0.5% per month) and late-payment penalties. Fraudulent export VAT claims constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to five years or fines, unlimited retrospective clawback, and denial of input VAT deductions. Customs and tax audits may verify the actual physical export, identity of the recipient, and correspondence of funds repatriated with the invoiced export price.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility. There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisations for inbound or outbound capital movements, and no restrictions on dividend, interest, royalty, or profit remittances by a German UG to non-resident shareholders. Capital flows are protected by EU Treaty free movement of capital (TFEU Art. 63) and only statistical reporting obligations (Bundesbank AWV reporting) apply. The UG operates in EUR; trade-related settlements in CNY, USD, or other currencies are freely executable via licensed correspondent banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German law imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Receipts may be retained in EUR or any foreign currency and deployed freely, subject only to cross-border payment statistical reporting thresholds under the AWV regime.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and cross-border payments statistics authority under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); European Central Bank (monetary policy for the euro area); BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) for prudential banking conduct and AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as an ancillary collection/disbursement layer and Low as the sole primary treasury rail. Legal constraints: any EMI serving the UG must hold a German BaFin licence or a valid EEA passport under PSD2 (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex). A non-EEA offshore EMI without a passported authorisation cannot lawfully hold or operate a payment account for a German corporate in EUR; EMI accounts are payment accounts rather than deposit accounts and cannot support documentary trade instruments such as SWIFT MT700 letters of credit or MT760 bank guarantees. The EMI layer is acceptable only for low-value disbursements and must be mirrored by a full-licence commercial bank for statutory capital, tax, and trade settlement functions.

The EMI layer is acceptable only for low-value disbursements and must be mirrored by a full-licence commercial bank for statutory capital, tax, and trade settlement functions.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) on taxable income, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritaetszuschlag) on the CIT amount, yielding a baseline federal burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) applies in addition at a statutory assessment rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%-490%, common level 400%), producing a combined aggregate corporate burden of approximately 29.8% to 33.0% on distributable profit before trade tax.
