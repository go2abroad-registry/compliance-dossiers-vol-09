Competent corporate registry

Local Court (Amtsgericht) - Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the registered office, filings submitted electronically through the certified notary; subsequent trade notification to the local Trade Office (Gewerbeamt/Ordnungsamt).

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz - GmbHG), Sections 1, 2, 5, 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft), 8, 10, 11, 35-44; German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch - HGB) Sections 106-108; Notarisation Act (Beurkundungsgesetz) Section 20.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG mini-GmbH: minimum nominal capital is EUR 1.00 per shareholder (no EUR 25,000 minimum); all cash contributions must be fully paid up before registration and placed at the free disposal of the managing director; shareholder contributions are evidenced to the notary by bank confirmation; no statutory escrow account is required; annual net profits must be retained at 25% until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG Section 5a(3)).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety approval by the local fire prevention authority (Brandschutzdienststelle) including lithium battery storage compartments, smoke detection and extinguishing systems; building authority structural load and fire compartment certification; occupational health clearance under the Arbeitsschutzgesetz and DGUV Information 205-035 with trained personnel for battery handling, short-circuit protection, and emergency response.

Paid-in capital rule: UG mini-GmbH: minimum nominal capital is EUR 1.00 per shareholder (no EUR 25,000 minimum); all cash contributions must be fully paid up before registration and placed at the free disposal of the managing director; shareholder contributions are evidenced to the notary by bank confirmation; no statutory escrow account is required; annual net profits must be retained at 25% until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG Section 5a(3)).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — centralized VAT identification number and international tax affairs (www.bzst.de); local Finanzamt (Tax Office) at the UG's registered seat — corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT assessment and VAT returns; mandatory electronic filing through the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

The UG (registered in the German Handelsregister) must file the Tax Registration Questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER within four weeks of incorporation. The competent local Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer (tax number) from the questionnaire data; the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) is applied for separately from BZSt (Form USt 1 TN) and is required for intra-Community trade, export invoicing and B2B reverse-charge verification. For a foreign entity without a permanent establishment in Germany performing a first taxable supply, the extended questionnaire is filed via ELSTER in advance of that supply; provisional registration by letter is accepted if portal activation is pending. Standard processing is 2–8 weeks; urgent written application can accelerate issuance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a domestically resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income (§ 1 KStG). Corporate income tax 15% plus solidarity surcharge 5.5% of CIT (combined federal 15.825%) plus municipal trade tax (federal base rate 3.5% multiplied by local rate between 200% and 490%, producing an effective range of approximately 7%–17%). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a domestically resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income (§ 1 KStG). Corporate income tax 15% plus solidarity surcharge 5.5% of CIT (combined federal 15.825%) plus municipal trade tax (federal base rate 3.5% multiplied by local rate between 200% and 490%, producing an effective range of approximately 7%–17%). No pass-through; no territorial exemption for resident corporations; no tax-exempt zone status applicable.

Available election forms

No check-the-box mechanism and no partnership opt-out: a UG is strictly opaque for German tax purposes. Available elections include: (i) corporate tax and VAT group (Organschaft) with a resident controlling corporation under §§ 14, 17 KStG and § 2 UStG, subject to profit-and-loss pooling agreement and financial, economic and operational integration; (ii) small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG where prior-year turnover ≤ €25,000 and current-year turnover ≤ €100,000; (iii) ordinary depreciation and inventory valuation options under §§ 6, 7 EStG; (iv) hybrid-mismatch positions are non-elective — § 4k EStG applies, and a rebuttal exists only where the instrument is treated as equity in both contracting states and no deduction/non-inclusion outcome arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business threshold: prior-year turnover ≤ €25,000 and current-year turnover ≤ €100,000 (§ 19 UStG) for exemption; registration is mandatory as soon as either threshold is exceeded or upon explicit waiver. Foreign-established entities (no seat or management in Germany) have no turnover relief and must register for VAT before the first taxable supply in Germany. The EU-wide €10,000 OSS threshold and Import-One-Stop-Shop regime apply to B2C distance sales and imports, not to the UG's B2B battery exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of LiFePO4 battery packs from the German UG to third countries are VAT-exempt with input VAT recovery under § 4 Nr. 1 and § 6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave the EU in unchanged condition and the exporter retains reliable proof of export. Intra-Community supplies to EU customers are zero-rated under § 4 Nr. 1b and § 6a UStG, subject to the customer's valid EU VAT-ID, entry in the monthly recapitulative statement (EC sales list) and transport documentation. Ancillary services (warranty work, engineering, technical testing for foreign customers) qualify for zero-rating under § 4 Nr. 3 UStG; standard B2B services fall under the reverse-charge mechanism at the recipient's place of establishment (§ 3a UStG).

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with MRN and Ex-A approval
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice stating INCOTERMS, battery pack HS classification (HS 8507.60 / 8507.90) and the statutory German VAT-exemption reference
  • Bank transfer (TT wire) or SWIFT MT103 / Letter of Credit documents confirming foreign exchange remittance from the customer
  • For intra-Community supply: customer's valid EU VAT-ID, EC sales list entry and CMR transport documentation in lieu of a customs declaration

E-Rechnung mandate: from 1 January 2025 all domestic (DE-DE) B2B taxpayers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or EN 16931 XML); domestic issuers are required to issue e-invoices in that format. Mandatory issuance through issuers with prior-year turnover above €800,000 applies from 1 January 2027, and for all taxpayers from 1 January 2028. No continuous transaction control (real-time fiscal clearance) platform is operational as of 2025; the Federal Ministry of Finance's draft Digital Transformation of VAT Act (UStDiTraG, published 16 May 2025) proposes transaction-based reporting to BZSt from 2026 in anticipation of the EU ViDA framework (currently stalled). ECS/ATLAS customs declarations, Intrastat filings and digital MRNs remain binding for cross-border goods; no digital stamping or fiscal marks apply.

Export zero-rating is audited by the local Finanzamt and the Central Customs Authority (GZD). Missing or defective export proof under §§ 8–13 UStDV triggers retrospective correction: VAT is re-assessed at 19%, default interest accrues at 0.5% per month (§§ 233a, 240 AO), input VAT credits are recaptured, and penalty surcharges up to €25,000 apply under § 378 AO. Fraudulent zero-rating or fabricated export documentation constitutes criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO with penalties up to 10 years' imprisonment in aggravated cases and forfeiture of economic proceeds. Separately, the permanence rule of § 1(3d) AStG requires that export receivables from related or controlled foreign parties be realized within the statutory 12-month default window; the 2025 Tax Development Act draft (Steuerfortentwicklungsgesetz) reduces this to 6 months (180 days), which is adopted here as the conservative compliance benchmark — non-repatriation triggers deemed income recapture and transfer-pricing adjustments under the German Foreign Transaction Tax Act (AStG).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a member of the EU Economic and Monetary Union, provides full convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the German Free Movement of Capital Act. Profit remittances by a German UG to its shareholders are not subject to foreign exchange controls or central bank approval. The only operational hurdles are (i) German dividend withholding tax at 26.375% (25% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax) subject to a 0% rate under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the beneficial owner is a qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder, and (ii) mandatory monthly statistical reporting of cross-border payments above the AWV threshold. No conversion surrender requirement, no prior exchange control licensing, and no profit-repatriation quota applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender or conversion of export proceeds. A German UG may hold export receipts in foreign currency or convert them to euros freely. However, under AWV, all payments received from non-residents in connection with merchandise exports must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for external-sector statistics. Payments must be reported on a monthly calendar basis when the aggregate value of cross-border payments with a given counterparty exceeds EUR 12,500, with the report due by the fifth working day after the end of the reporting month. The UG must also retain underlying trade documents (invoices, customs declarations, shipping documents) for five years for audit and balance-of-payments verification purposes.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), together with the Federal Ministry of Finance and the German Customs Administration for sanctions and export-control enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for transaction execution, Low as a sole treasury rail. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions can lawfully operate in Germany under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) on a passport basis and may provide multi-currency IBANs, FX conversion, and cross-border payment execution. However, such EMIs generally do not have direct SWIFT membership or documentary trade finance capacity. They cannot independently issue commercial letters of credit or handle compliance for export LC presentations. A German UG should use an EMI only as a non-core receivable acceleration rail; the primary liquidity and settlement backbone must be a fully licensed German commercial bank or a UK/CH GTB with a German branch or EU passport.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholders of the German UG may inject equity capital via SWIFT MT103 with the payment reference 'capital contribution' and the corresponding AWV statistical purpose category for capital transactions. The payment must originate from the shareholder's account held at a bank in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction; the payee account at the German bank must be designated as the capital contribution account referencing the Handelsregister file number. The German bank will require an allocated capital escrow account before notarization if the UG is newly formed; after notarization, the funds are freed for operating use. All inbound payments above EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank in the monthly external-sectors report, including the remitter's country, the exact purpose code, the value date, and the beneficiary. If the injection is a shareholder loan, the loan agreement must be submitted to the bank and the AWV code for intercompany financing is used.German clearing rails for battery pack trade settlements are governed by SEPA for euro-denominated EU/EEA transactions and by the SWIFT network for non-EU wires and documentary collections. Commercial bank sales involving an irrevocable documentary credit are handled via SWIFT MT700 (issuance of LC), MT710/MT720 (advice or transfer), and MT202/MT103 for settlement on maturity, with presentation documents (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, insurance documents, and quality certificates for lithium iron phosphate batteries) forwarded to the confirming bank. Direct non-EU supplier payments are effected by SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate transaction purpose code; all outbound payments must be screened against EU autonomous sanctions, German sanctions lists (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung), and OFAC sanctions when USD clearing is used. For export proceeds denominated in USD, the German bank will typically require a USD intermediary clearing bank and the UG may receive settlement in the original currency. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the CIT amount (0.825 percentage points), plus municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at a typical rate of 14%-17%; combined effective statutory burden approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the dividend tax) for distributions to non-resident shareholders; for a German-resident corporate shareholder, 95% of the dividend is tax-exempt under §8b KStG, leaving an effective tax burden of approximately 1.375% because of the 5% non-deductible business expense add-back.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending on or after 31 December, returns must be filed by 31 July of the following calendar year. If prepared by a tax adviser (Steuerberater), the deadline automatically extends to the last day of the fourteenth month following the 31 July deadline, i.e. generally by 30 September/31 October of the second subsequent year, depending on the assessment year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party sales transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 for goods (including finished battery packs or components) or EUR 1,000,000 for services; Master File is mandated when the worldwide group turnover exceeds EUR 60,000,000 and the group is required to prepare consolidated financial statements, regardless of the UG's own revenue.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the style of offshore jurisdictions, but substance-over-form and anti-avoidance rules are vigorously enforced through §42 AO (abuse of legal structures), the BEPS MLI Principal Purpose Test, §8c KStG, and the German CFC rules (§§7-13 AStG). A UG manufacturing or distributing lithium battery packs must demonstrate actual operational substance — physical licensed premises, technical staff, board decisions taken in Germany, an inventory ledger, transport logistics, and electrical-battery testing records — before treaty benefits and financing structures are respected.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment under §370 AO of up to 5 years, aggravated to 6 months to 10 years in especially severe cases; additionally, final assessment of all evaded taxes plus late-payment interest of 1% per month (0.15% per month in other interest cases), forfeiture of assets derived from evasion, and accumulation of a criminal record that disables export-licence benefits.

Director personal liability: Managing directors of the UG are personally jointly and severally liable for evaded taxes and associated surcharges under §§69-70 AO when they act willfully; successful conviction triggers immediate seizure of personal assets, biometric passport revocation, judicial travel bans (Einreiseverbot), and imprisonment. Corporate veil-piercing occurs when the UG is capitalised below EUR 12,500 or when directors use the UG as a fraudulent conduit with no genuine board oversight.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: German tax authorities re-characterise the transaction under §42 AO and substance-over-form doctrines: the UG may be treated as a non-entity for tax purposes, its intercompany contracts disregarded, profits allocated to the true foreign shareholder, trade tax and corporate income tax assessed at the shareholder level, plus a late-assessment interest of 0.15% per month. A criminal fine of up to EUR 25,000 and confiscation of transaction proceeds can be imposed under ancillary administrative offences.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign annual financial statements that falsely confirm substance are personally liable for the UG's unpaid corporate taxes to the extent of fraudulent trading under §§64, 64a GmbHG (analogous for UG); German courts issue worldwide asset freezes, prohibit the director from leaving the EEA if a travel risk exists, and order personal restitution of all distributions made when the UG had insufficient assets.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of taxable income by imputation of arm's-length prices under §162(1) AO; mandatory penalty of 5% of the upward income adjustment when documentation is unavailable, not less than EUR 5,000 and not exceeding EUR 100,000 per tested transaction; additional 1% monthly late-payment surcharge and 0.15% per month underpayment interest; in criminal cases, conviction for tax evasion under §370 AO.

Director personal liability: The managing director who certifies the transfer-pricing documentation incurs personal liability for penalties and back taxes if the documentation is knowingly or grossly negligently false; the statute of limitations for TP-related tax evasion is extended to 10 years, and travel bans are commonly issued by the German fiscal enforcement agency once a preliminary criminal investigation is opened.
AML / Money Laundering Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) and §261 StGB, penalties include fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or five times the laundered amount, seizure of bank accounts and trade receivables, confiscation of all laundered property, and imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years; a company may be barred from public procurement and licensing registrations.

Director personal liability: Directors and the statutory money-laundering officer are personally criminally liable for failing to file suspicious transaction reports with the FIU, or for knowingly processing suspicious funds; German courts impose travel bans and extradition orders for non-compliant directors, and may issue personal judgment-hold orders to secure restitution to victims or the state for all funds that passed through the UG's account.
Administrative Non-Compliance and Withholding Tax Collection Failure

Statutory sanction: Late filing surcharge under §152 AO up to EUR 25,000; late payment surcharge under §240 AO at 1% per month; penalty for unsupported e-balance-sheet transmission of up to EUR 250,000 under §379 AO; additional interest of 0.15% per month for back taxes and a separate fine for failure to withhold wage tax or dividend tax of up to EUR 30,000 per officer.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for the collection and payment of all withholding taxes (including dividend WHT and payroll taxes) if the UG's corporate assets are insufficient, regardless of corporate limited liability; the state may extend liability to personal bank accounts, issue an asset freeze disposition, escalate to a personal bankruptcy petition, and impose a ban on serving as a director in any German limited company for up to ten years.
