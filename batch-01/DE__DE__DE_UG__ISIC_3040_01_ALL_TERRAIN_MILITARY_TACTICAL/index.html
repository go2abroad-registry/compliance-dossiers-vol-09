Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permissible for ordinary trading UGs but materially restricted for the ISIC 3040 defense scope: § 4a GmbHG requires the statutory seat (Satzungssitz) to be located in Germany together with a domestic address for service of process, corporate records, and statutory correspondence. The use of a virtual office for a defense-tactical-vehicle manufacturer creates a high risk of refusal of the preliminary export-control classification and of the war-weapons production license.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Local Court (Amtsgericht) acting as the Commercial Register Court (Registergericht) for the registered office (e.g., Amtsgericht München), in conjunction with the German Federal Chamber of Notaries (Bundesnotarkammer) and the electronic register portal (www.unternehmensregister.de).

Governing statute

German Act on Limited Liability Companies (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), in particular § 1, § 2, § 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), § 6 (managing directors), § 7 (registration application), and § 8 (contents of application); German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB) §§ 8, 9 and 10 (Commercial Register, electronic publications); Notarisation and Certification Act (BeurkG) §§ 8 et seq.; Money Laundering Act (GwG) §§ 3, 10 and 11 (beneficial ownership).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified instrument of incorporation (Errichtungsniederschrift), Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or statutory sample protocol (Musterprotokoll), and notarized application for entry into the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung im Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 48 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00 fully subscribed and fully paid immediately prior to registration; per § 5a GmbHG, 25% of the annual net profit must be retained in a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.00; cash contributions must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director; escrow accounts are not statutorily mandatory for cash contributions but are commonly used; contributions in kind (Sacheinlagen) require a proof of valuation and are not eligible for the Musterprotokoll procedure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) as policy authority; the locally competent tax office (Finanzamt) of the UG's seat as assessing authority; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification number and withholding procedures; national electronic filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident founders or the newly formed UG submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent local Finanzamt through ELSTER, indicating statutory seat and place of management, to obtain the Steuernummer (corporate tax number). The BZSt subsequently issues the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) via Form USt 1 TU or the integrated questionnaire. The UG must simultaneously register for trade tax and, before any taxable supply, obtain the required Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) license for defense manufacturing, as no tax clearance is granted without the statutory export-control license.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a UG is an incorporated capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of the corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, approximately 14-17% effective depending on the municipal Hebesatz). Tax residence is based on statutory seat or place of effective management, triggering worldwide income taxation with foreign-tax-credit relief; the UG is not eligible for pass-through treatment. — Non-resident founders or the newly formed UG submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent local Finanzamt through ELSTER, indicating statutory seat and place of management, to obtain the Steuernummer (corporate tax number). The BZSt subsequently issues the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) via Form USt 1 TU or the integrated questionnaire. The UG must simultaneously register for trade tax and, before any taxable supply, obtain the required Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) license for defense manufacturing, as no tax clearance is granted without the statutory export-control license.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a UG is an incorporated capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of the corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, approximately 14-17% effective depending on the municipal Hebesatz). Tax residence is based on statutory seat or place of effective management, triggering worldwide income taxation with foreign-tax-credit relief; the UG is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box election; a UG is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections include formation of a VAT group (Organschaft) under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 of the German VAT Act (UStG) where the UG is financially integrated into a controlling entrepreneur; small-entrepreneur waiver under Sec. 19 UStG in de minimis cases; trade tax loss-relief and apportionment elections under the Trade Tax Act (GewStG); and the substantive hybrid mismatch restrictions under Sec. 4k EStG implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, which deny deductions in double-dip or stateless-income arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 expected current-year turnover as the small-entrepreneur exemption threshold under Sec. 19 UStG (effective 2025); however, the UG manufacturing tactical vehicles exceeds this threshold and is mandatorily VAT-registered, while non-resident entrepreneurs making any taxable supply in Germany are unconditionally registrable without any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supply zero-rating under Sec. 6 UStG applies where the all-terrain military tactical vehicle is physically dispatched or transported to a non-EU third-country destination, the exporter retains conclusive documentary proof, and the recipient is the party acquiring commercial disposal power; an intra-community supply to an EU taxable person may be zero-rated under Sec. 6a UStG against a valid VAT ID. Defense exports additionally require a positive export license under the War Weapons Control Act (Kriegswaffenkontrollgesetz) or the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV/Ausfuhrliste) issued by BAFA; services directly related to exported goods, including freight, handling, and international transport, are zero-rated under Secs. 8 and 9 UStG.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with MRN assignment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting the non-EU final destination
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical outbound dispatch
  • Original BAFA export license (Kriegswaffenliste or Ausfuhrliste) with end-user certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice proving receipt and conversion of export proceeds in Germany within 180 days

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B transactions aligned to EN 16931 (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD): recipients must accept e-invoices from 1 January 2025; businesses with turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices from 1 January 2027; all businesses must do so from 1 January 2028. Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, central authorization stamping, or continuous transaction control; audit relies on archival proof, while all customs export declarations must be filed electronically through ATLAS.

Zero-rating is clawed back retroactively where documentary proof is incomplete, the recipient identity is disputed, or goods are diverted inside the EU; the tax office assesses 19% VAT plus interest under Sec. 233a AO (0.5% per month after 15 months). Intentional misdeclaration triggers a penalty up to EUR 5,000 under Sec. 26a UStG; criminal tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO carries imprisonment of up to 10 years, asset forfeiture, and a 10-year retrospective assessment period; BAFA license violations lead to fines under AWG and prosecution under the War Weapons Control Act, including permanent debarment from export authorization and mandatory forensic audits by the customs criminal office.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital account convertibility under EU law. Current account transactions, including profit remittances to foreign parent entities, are unrestricted and face no prior approval requirements. Repatriation is permitted subject only to standard AML, tax, and beneficial ownership disclosure obligations enforced via the German Commercial Register.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies. Exporters must submit customs declarations and supporting documentation solely for VAT refund eligibility. Free convertibility into EUR is standard; no statutory timelines or conversion ratios are prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital e-money institutions are subject to BaFin licensing under the Payment Services Supervision Act and EU PSD2 framework. Cross-border issuance of payment instruments for defense-related entities is restricted without full authorization and enhanced AML controls.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer), plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the CIT (0.825 ppt), plus Gewerbesteuer (municipal trade tax, typically 14–17%); aggregate effective rate approx. 30–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer (dividend withholding tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that amount (total 26.375%). No church tax for corporate payees. Trade tax is generally not deducted on distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline: 31 May of the follow-year after the fiscal year-end (for calendar-year taxpayers). Extended automatically to 31 August if filed by a tax advisor (due to general administrative practice based on §150 AO in conjunction with §109 AO – currently for 2024 filings). For non-calendar-year fiscal years, within three months after the fiscal year close.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where cross-border controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 10 million for goods (Waren) or EUR 3 million for services/services remuneration in a given fiscal year. Master File required when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Country-by-Country (CbC) report threshold: group consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (or equivalent). Lower materiality thresholds apply for profit booking deviations in administrative practice under §90(3) AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, for treaty benefits, entities must satisfy the 'beneficial owner' requirement under §50d Abs.3 EStG and demonstrate German substance (office, personnel, business capability, decision-making). Anti-abuse rules under §42 AO and §42d AO override arrangements lacking substance. German GmbH/UG with no real economic activity is exposed to treaty override, deemed dividend classification, and denial of participation exemptions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud (§370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years plus fine; in aggravated cases (e.g., commercial-scale tax evasion) one to ten years and an order to pay the full evaded amount with interest (Säumniszuschläge). Criminal proceeds confiscated.

Director personal liability: Under §§34 and 69 AO, managing directors and responsible officers are personally liable for tax debts caused by intentional or gross negligence; civil liability to the German treasury (Dritthaftung) extends to the full amount of evaded tax, plus interest. Imprisonment, travel ban, and criminal record for the natural person.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard for tax purposes under §42 AO; income allocation to actual economic operator; WHT recapture on deemed distributions; trade tax reassessment; suspension of §8c loss offset. For treaty abuse, denial of benefits and full tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Director or shadow director who knowingly structures a shell entity is liable under §69 AO for the corporate tax shortfall. Further personal claims under §43 GmbHG and §823 German Civil Code for breach of duty; for failure to prevent substance violation causing tax evasion, imprisonment possible.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure (TP Defect)

Statutory sanction: Under §162 AO, tax authorities may estimate profits, impose an uplift of up to 50% of the adjustment amount, plus a specific surcharge (Zuschlag) of at least 5% of the profit adjustment (minimum EUR 1,000) but capped at EUR 100,000; back-taxes and interest at 6% p.a.

Director personal liability: Directors as 'responsible persons' under §69 AO face personal liability for the company's TP adjustment and surcharge if they failed to ensure documentation existed or deliberately destroyed documents. Criminal exposure under §274 AO (document concealment) and §370 AO.
Export Control and Arms Embargo Violation (KrWaffKontrG / AWG)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of one to ten years under §22a AWG or §18 KrWaffKontrG; in less serious cases fines; revocation of business license and exclusion from public tenders. For companies, collateral administrative penalties up to EUR 1 million and mandatory compliance monitors.

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally sign export documents; criminal liability as natural parties (no limited-liability protection) may result in imprisonment of up to ten years; permanent entry in register of disqualifications; freezing of corporate assets by customs; travel restriction and interdiction from board roles.
Money Laundering (AML) / Asset Transparency Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000; for business purposes, the Benelux sanctions are imposed by BaFin; in criminal cases, imprisonment up to five years (§261 StGB). Confiscation of goods and assets; statutory exclusion from public contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for failure to install section requirements under §19 GwG; civil damages to whistleblowers; criminal liability with imprisonment up to five years and personal fine if the company is dissolved without tax declaration due to asset placement.
