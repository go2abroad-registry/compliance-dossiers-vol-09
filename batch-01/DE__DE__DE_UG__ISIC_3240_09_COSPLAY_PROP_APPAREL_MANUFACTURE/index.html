Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register), administered by the Registry Court of the local Amtsgericht (Local Court); electronic gazette publication via the Bundesanzeiger and search via the Unternehmensregister.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG), in particular Sec. 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschraenkt), Secs. 1-11 (formation), Sec. 13 (legal personality), Secs. 35-44 (managing directors), together with Secs. 8-16 of the Commercial Code (HGB) on Commercial Register filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Deed of Incorporation (Gesellschaftsvertrag or Musterprotokoll) together with the Application for Registration to the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG requires a stated capital (Stammkapital) of at least EUR 1.00; each share must be paid at least 25% of its nominal amount in cash and paid in before registration; cash contributions are placed in a designated escrow/contribution account (Sperrkonto) releasing funds only upon registration; the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the stated capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG Sec. 5a(3)).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutznachweis) and inspection by the local fire authority for the production and storage premises; structural load verification for storage mezzanines and racking per the state building code; workplace clearance under ArbStaettV (Workplaces Ordinance) and ASR rules covering escape routes, lighting, ventilation, and first aid; enrollment with the Berufsgenossenschaft and display of the accident insurance notice.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG requires a stated capital (Stammkapital) of at least EUR 1.00; each share must be paid at least 25% of its nominal amount in cash and paid in before registration; cash contributions are placed in a designated escrow/contribution account (Sperrkonto) releasing funds only upon registration; the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the stated capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG Sec. 5a(3)).

19%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Legal zero-rating for exports to non-EU third countries (§§ 4 Nr.1, 6 UStG) applies when goods are physically moved to a destination outside the EU and the supplier retains documentary evidence of movement. B2B services related to exported goods or responsible for such goods are zero-rated if conditions under §4 Nr.1(a) or §4 Nr.3 UStG are met; B2C cross-border services taxed in importing country per MOSS/OSS rules.

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions phased in from 1 January 2025 (receiving capability), 1 January 2027 (issuance for businesses with prior-year turnover > €800,000), and 1 January 2028 (all businesses). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible Euro currency regime with no capital controls on current account transactions. Capital account transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 per calendar year or per individual transaction require mandatory prior notification to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the reporting channel of the involved credit institution. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders or parent entities are unrestricted provided the underlying profits derive from current account operations. Standard corporate FX transactions, including those related to manufacturing exports, face no convertibility restrictions or surrender obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under German law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or domestic foreign currency accounts. All FX transactions above EUR 50,000 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the credit institution's automated reporting system. Conversion occurs at prevailing market rates with no statutory mandatory ratio or timeline. Intra-EU exports are exempt from any reporting or surrender formalities.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Electronic Money Institutions such as PayPal Business or Stripe offer limited cross-border payment rails suitable for retail and small-volume corporate transactions. Full corporate treasury operations require traditional banking relationships. Legal constraints include mandatory authorization as an EMI under the Payment Services Directive 2 and ongoing supervision by BaFin; cross-border EMI issuance and redemption services remain restricted for manufacturing entities.

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and CbC Report are mandatory for groups with ultimate parent consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more. A German Local File must be prepared when the annual value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 5 million for goods, EUR 1 million for services, EUR 1 million for intangibles or financial transactions; below these volumes a reduced documentation setup must still be available upon tax audit request within 60 days

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the OECD substance requirements and the domestic General Anti-Abuse Rule under § 42 AO. The UG must demonstrate real economic activity (staff, premises, business decisions, bank accounts, production and sales functions) in Germany; mailbox companies without substance are disregarded for tax treaty benefits, their income is reattributed, and retroactive assessments with penalty surcharges are issued

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for the managing director for up to 5 years, and up to 10 years in aggravated cases (especially large tax loss or misuse of position); corporate fine up to EUR 1 million per offense under § 30 OWiG, with disgorgement of the full tax evaded; loss of licenses, public contracts, and banking facilities

Director personal liability: The managing director is sued as principal offender; the tax office issues a direct personal Haftungsbescheid (§ 69 AO) for all unpaid corporate income tax, trade tax, and surcharges; travel bans and Schengen-area security measures can be imposed; the director is barred from forming or managing companies for up to five years
Shell Company / Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities invoke § 42 AO and disregard the UG’s legal structure; all German-source income is reattributed to the foreign parent’s permanent establishment, triggering income tax, trade tax, and a 6% penalty surcharge on the reconstructed profits; VAT invoices issued by the UG are declared invalid, causing loss of input VAT deduction for customers; the foreign parent may be assessed as tax resident in Germany

Director personal liability: The UG’s directors face personal liability to the German tax office for the full assessed corporate liabilities under §§ 34, 69 AO; civil liability to third-party creditors for negligent management under GmbHG § 43; criminal prosecution for money laundering (§ 261 StGB) if the shell structure was used to conceal payments
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 150,000 per violation under § 56 GwG; severe AML violations can trigger fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover; the German Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) can suspend payment accounts and impose special audit orders; non-compliance with Transparency Register reporting is punishable with fines up to EUR 100,000

Director personal liability: Directors are personally fined and can be banned from serving as directors under § 60 GwG; they are criminally liable for intentional money laundering and negligent AML failures, with imprisonment up to two years for negligence and up to five years for intent; in international cases, European investigation orders and asset freezes are used to enforce personal restitution
