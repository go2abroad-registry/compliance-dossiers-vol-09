Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) may be formed with stated share capital below EUR 25,000, including EUR 1, but the capital must be fully paid in cash before registration. Contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. No statutory escrow account is required; a dedicated bank account is normally opened after notarization and funded before the registration filing. The company must retain 25% of annual net profit, reduced by losses carried forward, as a statutory reserve until capital reaches EUR 25,000. Court and notarial fees are payable separately and vary by capital, articles and transaction complexity.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupancy, obtain any required building-use approval and satisfy the local fire authority regarding escape routes, fire compartments, extinguishers, emergency lighting and storage of flammable materials. Conduct machinery risk assessments, electrical and structural-load inspections, ventilation checks and workplace-safety reviews under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, Workplace Ordinance and applicable technical rules. Register the business with the competent employers' liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office – for VAT identification numbers and withholding tax matters via its online portal (https://www.bzst.de); locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for Steuernummer (corporate tax number), corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT filing; ELSTER portal (https://www.elster.de) as the central electronic filing and registration gateway

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned UG must first be registered with the commercial register (Handelsregister) and obtain a Gewerbeanmeldung (trade licence application) from the relevant municipality/Gewerbeamt. Within one month after commencement of business, the managing director files the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt, receiving the statutory corporate Steuernummer. In parallel, the USt-IdNr. (German VAT ID) application is submitted to the BZSt online portal, quoting the Steuernummer and tax office details. If the UG imports or exports goods, the company must also obtain an EORI number registered with the Generalzolldirektion (German Customs). Non-resident directors may execute these registrations with a valid power of attorney; identity documents and certified articles of association are required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)) is a German-resident capital company with unlimited tax liability in Germany, taxed at entity level on corporate income tax (15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% thereof) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, approximately 7%–17% effective). No pass-through tax classification or US-style check-the-box election exists; worldwide income is subject to German taxation, with foreign tax credits available under tax treaties. — A foreign-owned UG must first be registered with the commercial register (Handelsregister) and obtain a Gewerbeanmeldung (trade licence application) from the relevant municipality/Gewerbeamt. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)) is a German-resident capital company with unlimited tax liability in Germany, taxed at entity level on corporate income tax (15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% thereof) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, approximately 7%–17% effective). No pass-through tax classification or US-style check-the-box election exists; worldwide income is subject to German taxation, with foreign tax credits available under tax treaties.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election; statutory elections available under German tax law include (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election consolidating corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT among a financially and organisationally integrated group; (ii) VAT small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Section 19 UStG if turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and EUR 50,000 in the current year; (iii) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for intra-EU distance sales, cross-border B2C digital services and certain imports; (iv) EU VAT reverse-charge attribution rules; and (v) hybrid mismatch restriction elections under Section 4k EStG implementing ATAD. Germany does not provide territorial tax election, tax-exempt zone designation, or a pure cash-basis corporate tax regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and EUR 50,000 in the current calendar year (small-business exemption threshold, Section 19 UStG); any entrepreneurial activity exceeding these thresholds creates mandatory VAT registration. A separate threshold of EUR 10,000 applies to intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2C digital services subject to the Union-wide One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme; the UG may nonetheless register voluntarily and obtain a VAT ID for intra-EU B2B transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (i) export supplies (Ausfuhrlieferungen) to destinations outside the EU under Section 4(1)(1a) and Section 6 UStG, provided the goods physically depart Germany and the exporter retains the required export evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis); (ii) intra-Community supplies (innergemeinschaftliche Lieferungen) under Section 4(1)(1b) and Section 6a UStG, requiring a valid EU VAT ID of the buyer, cross-border movement of goods within the EU, and compliant invoice documentation; and (iii) cross-border B2B services in respect of trading-card publishing, royalties, digital card assets, printing and design services, which fall outside the place of taxation in Germany under Section 3a(2) UStG if the recipient is an entrepreneur established in another country and, for intra-EU services, holds a valid VAT ID (reverse-charge zero-rating). B2C digital and distance services may nonetheless remain subject to German VAT unless the OSS regime properly covers the transaction.

  • Export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) or exit summary declaration issued by the customs office

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B transactions with effect from 1 January 2025. Domestic taxable suppliers must issue structured e-invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD or PEPPOL/XML) in accordance with Section 14 UStG; from 1 January 2026 the e-invoicing issuance obligation applies to suppliers whose prior-year turnover exceeded EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2027 to all German B2B suppliers; receipt-capability obligations apply from 2025. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance mechanism, government API tax stamp, or continuous controls framework for EDC; instead, integrity and authenticity of the structured digital invoice must be ensured via transmission protocols and interconnected audit trail systems.

German tax authorities, the BZSt and the EU antifraud units (AEOI/IGA) routinely audit export transactions, in particular invoice-only exports, chain transactions and high-frequency trading-card shipments. Missing or defective export evidence retroactively reverts the 0% rate, exposing the UG to German VAT at 19%, default interest under Section 233a AO, late-payment surcharges and administrative fines. Intentional or grossly negligent tax evasion (Sections 370 and 378 AO) carries fines of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years for especially serious cases. Under Section 25d UStG, a German exporter may be held liable for the unpaid VAT of a fraudulent EU customer where the exporter knew or should have known of the evasion. Export documentation must be retained for ten years under Section 147 AO; a failure to repatriate foreign-currency consideration within 180 days may trigger reassessment under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV), customs penalty proceedings, and full clawback of the zero-rating benefit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is in the euro area. The euro is freely convertible, and Germany generally imposes no exchange controls on current-account payments, capital transfers, or repatriation of profits. Cross-border transfers remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, and financial-crime controls. Dividends and other profit distributions must be supported by valid corporate approvals, distributable profits, applicable withholding-tax analysis, and any required tax documentation. Payments to or from non-residents may require AWV statistical reporting when applicable thresholds and exemptions are met; this is a reporting obligation, not a prior exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds by a fixed deadline or convert a prescribed percentage into euros. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to ordinary tax, accounting, sanctions, and reporting rules. AWV cross-border payment reporting thresholds and exemptions should be checked for each transaction; the applicable threshold has been €50,000 since 2025, subject to the regulation's scope and exceptions. Reporting rules and thresholds may change, so confirm current Bundesbank instructions before filing.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers balance-of-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action administers foreign-trade policy; BaFin supervises regulated financial institutions and payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to high for routine euro collections and payments if the provider is authorized as a credit institution or payment institution/e-money institution in the EEA and may lawfully provide services in Germany. An offshore provider without applicable EEA authorization or passporting is not a suitable substitute. E-money is not a bank deposit and may not receive deposit-guarantee protection. Confirm account ownership, safeguarding, local IBAN availability, payout limits, card-processing reserves, and support for the company's actual trading-card and printing activities. Stripe and Authorize.Net are payment processors, not substitutes for a corporate bank account.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% Koerperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), before municipal Gewerbesteuer; combined headline corporate incidence typically 30–33% depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% on gross dividends distributed by the UG (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge); treaty relief may reduce the rate to 0–15% for a qualifying non-resident beneficial owner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR: mandatory Local File / transaction documentation when cross-border related-party deliveries of goods exceed EUR 5,000,000 per year or cross-border related-party services exceed EUR 500,000 per year; Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000; domestic related-party transactions require documentation above EUR 2,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code of the Gulf format, but substantive economic presence is enforced through Section 42 AO anti-abuse doctrine, Section 50d EStG treaty benefit anti-abuse provisions, CFC rules under the AStG, and ATAD implementation. The UG manufacturing collectible trading cards must be structured with real operational substance: physical premises, production equipment, employees, decision-making, risk assumption and independent IP ownership in Germany. An entity lacking these factors may be disregarded for tax purposes, denied treaty relief, re-characterized as a transparent pass-through, or subject to veil-piercing in tax enforcement proceedings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, or a fine; in aggravated cases imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years; confiscation of proceeds; monthly late payment interest at 0.5%; loss of the voluntary disclosure amnesty if evasion is already discovered; administrative penalty up to 10% of the evaded tax in serious cases.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable under Sections 69 and 34 AO for corporate taxes not paid; civil restitution for the UG's losses; personal assets may be frozen; travel bans, arrest orders and passport revocation may be imposed; conviction can disqualify an individual from managing a company for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Disregard of artificial arrangements under Section 42 AO; denial of DTA relief under Section 50d EStG; attribution of income to the shareholder or to a German permanent establishment; CFC income inclusion under the AStG; additional tax assessment up to 10% of the reallocated profit; revocation of the UG's limited liability protection where the corporate form is abused.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the UG's assessed tax liabilities, social security and wage tax arrears; veil-piercing allows recovery against personal assets; travel bans and custodial arrest are available where tax recovery is at risk.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Estimation of taxable income by the Finanzamt under Section 162 AO; penalty for insufficient transfer pricing documentation under Section 162(4) AO, generally 10% of the upward TP adjustment subject to statutory ceilings; mandatory 6% annual interest on recomputed tax; refusal to accept unilateral pricing without contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: The managing director who signs inaccurate TP documentation or fails to maintain records can be held civilly liable for resulting tax shortfalls; aiding or abetting tax evasion can lead to criminal prosecution, fines and personal imprisonment; director disqualification in aggravated cases.
AML / Payment Rail Fraud / Shell Invoice Fraud

Statutory sanction: Money laundering imprisonment under Section 261 StGB from 3 months to 5 years; forfeiture and confiscation of the gross proceeds; administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) up to EUR 1,000,000 or higher for serious institutional violations; exclusion from regulated payment providers.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly set up or direct fraudulent payment flows are personally liable for restitution, administrative fines and criminal penalties; travel bans, asset freezes and pretrial detention may be imposed; industry disqualification for financial sector governance.
Withholding Tax Default / Delinquent Payroll and VAT Remittances

Statutory sanction: The UG is liable for the withholding amount already collected or owing to the state; late payment interest at 0.5% per month; fines under Section 378 AO for tax endangerment up to EUR 100,000; criminal prosecution under Section 370 AO where concealment is intentional; director liability extends to unpaid wage tax and VAT even without personal fault under the insolvency triggers of Section 64 GmbHG.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable, jointly and severally, for all withheld employee taxes and VAT not remitted to the treasury; civil enforcement may target private assets; travel bans and arrest orders are issued where the director attempts to leave Germany while tax debts remain outstanding.
