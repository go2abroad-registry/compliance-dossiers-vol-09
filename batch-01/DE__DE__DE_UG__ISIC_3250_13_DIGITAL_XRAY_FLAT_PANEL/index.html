Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) and Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt); local Finanzamt; ELSTER online portal (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must complete the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) through the competent local Finanzamt or via BZSt to obtain a German tax number (Steuernummer) and, for cross-border transactions, a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). The application may be filed digitally through ELSTER or by paper; supporting documents include articles of association, register extract, and foreign tax residence certificates.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must complete the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) through the competent local Finanzamt or via BZSt to obtain a German tax number (Steuernummer) and, for cross-border transactions, a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). The application may be filed digitally through ELSTER or by paper; supporting documents include articles of association, register extract, and foreign tax residence certificates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Germany; corporate taxation is mandatory. Available elections include forming a fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT purposes, and making hybrid mismatch elections pursuant to §4k EStG, together with collateral documentation of the EU ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general threshold for non-resident suppliers; any taxable supply in Germany triggers registration. For domestic small businesses, the §19 UStG exemption applies below EUR 25,000 (2025 threshold), with restrictive conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to physical exports of goods outside the EU under §4 Nr.1 and §6 UStG, provided the exporter is a German entrepreneur and the goods depart from the EU in intact commercial circumstances. Export services, including offshore manufacturing services performed for foreign principals, are zero-rated under §3a UStG and §4 Nr.1, provided the recipient is an entrepreneur and the place of supply is outside Germany.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument/ATLAS logout)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice with export destination
  • Proof of transport or despatch records
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) under §8-10 UStDV

Germany mandates B2B e-invoicing from 1 January 2025 for domestic transactions, requiring structured XML formats such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime; invoices must be transmitted electronically in the mandated format and be interoperable with the buyer's systems. EDI and API-based exchange qualify if format requirements are met.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating triggers VAT correction, interest, and penalties under §14c UStG and §§369, 370 AO. Retrospective clawbacks for up to five years, and up to ten years under fraud, may be assessed; criminal penalties include imprisonment and fines for tax evasion and customs offences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has no general capital controls, mandatory domestic-currency conversion, or prior approval requirement for ordinary cross-border capital transfers. The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, export-control rules, tax requirements, and applicable AWV reporting. Dividends, interest, royalties, and liquidation proceeds may generally be remitted abroad after corporate authorization and satisfaction of applicable German withholding-tax, treaty-relief, accounting, and tax-filing requirements. Banks may require evidence of the transaction's commercial purpose, source of funds, beneficial ownership, tax treatment, and corporate approvals. Distributions by a UG are additionally subject to German corporate-law capital-maintenance rules and the UG statutory reserve requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no general statutory deadline for repatriating export proceeds and no mandatory foreign-exchange conversion or surrender ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in permitted domestic or foreign-currency accounts. Cross-border payments and claims may be reportable to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV, subject to statutory exemptions and thresholds. As of 1 January 2025, the general AWV reporting threshold for reportable cross-border payments is more than EUR 50,000 per payment; the applicable transaction category, exemptions, filing deadline, and reporting party must be verified for each transaction. Statistical reporting is distinct from exchange-control approval.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers balance-of-payments and external-payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises regulated banks and payment institutions. The European Central Bank and applicable EU law govern relevant euro-area monetary and payments matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro and supported foreign-currency payments through an appropriately licensed EU payment institution or electronic-money institution passported into Germany. An EMI account is not a substitute for a German bank deposit account, does not provide the same deposit-guarantee protection, and may not support capital-account, escrow, documentary-credit, or complex trade-finance services. Verify the provider's authorization, passporting status, safeguarding arrangements, supported currencies, payment limits, and acceptance by counterparties. Offshore providers without the required EU authorization or passporting should not be used to provide regulated payment services to the UG in Germany.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer): 15%, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax amount, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional; it is calculated using a 3.5% federal base rate multiplied by the municipality's assessment factor. The combined burden is municipality-dependent and is commonly approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Domestic dividend withholding is generally 25% plus a solidarity surcharge equal to 5.5% of the withholding tax, producing an effective 26.375%, excluding any applicable church tax. Treaty relief may reduce the rate, often to 15%, 5%, or 0% depending on the relevant treaty, ownership, beneficial ownership, and other conditions. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may provide an exemption for qualifying distributions, subject to its conditions and German anti-abuse rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer without a tax adviser, the annual return is generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser prepares the return, the ordinary statutory extension generally runs to the end of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions and tax-office orders. Special legislative extensions can apply to specified assessment years. A non-calendar fiscal year generally uses the corresponding deadline measured from fiscal-year end. Payment is due on the date stated in the assessment notice; advance tax installments have separate statutory due dates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany generally requires contemporaneous transfer-pricing records for cross-border related-party transactions. A Local File is generally required where annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, subject to statutory rules and exemptions. A Master File is generally required where the relevant multinational group's consolidated revenue in the preceding fiscal year is at least EUR 100 million. Documentation must be produced within the statutory period after a request; shorter deadlines may apply to extraordinary transactions. These thresholds and deadlines should be checked for the relevant tax year and transaction category.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a general separate Economic Substance Regulations regime of the type found in some offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership and treaty anti-abuse tests, the principal-purpose test, controlled foreign company rules, and the general anti-abuse rule. A German UG should maintain personnel, decision-making, premises, records, and functions consistent with its actual business and reported profits. For this medical-imaging equipment activity, document research and development, manufacturing or outsourced production, regulatory and quality-control functions, intellectual property ownership, and allocation of risks and returns.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax filings

Statutory sanction: Under the German Fiscal Code, intentional tax evasion may be punished by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Negligent tax understatement may attract an administrative fine. Tax, interest, and related civil liabilities remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability and fines where they act intentionally or negligently. They may also incur personal liability for tax claims where statutory duties to file, withhold, declare, or pay are breached. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction; they may arise only under applicable criminal-procedure measures.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and inadequate records

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make income adjustments, estimate the tax base, impose late or documentation penalties, and assess additional tax and interest. German transfer-pricing documentation penalties can include a percentage-based penalty and statutory minimum and maximum amounts, depending on the breach and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for intentional or negligent breaches of tax duties and may face tax-evasion prosecution if transfer-pricing misstatements are deliberate. Corporate liability is not displaced merely because a shareholder or group company directed the pricing.
Unlawful dividend distribution or withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The UG may owe the withholding tax, interest, and penalties. An unlawful distribution may be recoverable under company law and may expose recipients to repayment claims. A UG must generally allocate one quarter of annual profit, reduced by any loss carryforward, to its statutory reserve until the statutory capital threshold for conversion is reached.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for tax amounts not properly withheld or paid and may be responsible for unlawful payments under company-law rules. A veil-piercing finding, travel ban, or imprisonment is not automatic and requires a separate statutory basis and facts.
Undeclared permanent establishment or mischaracterized cross-border activity

Statutory sanction: Potential additional corporate and trade tax, registration and filing obligations, interest, late surcharges, tax-base estimates, and penalties; intentional concealment may constitute tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for culpable breaches of filing and payment obligations and may face criminal exposure for intentional misstatements. Liability depends on the director's duties, conduct, and applicable statutory provisions.
AML, sanctions, and payment-rail violations

Statutory sanction: Sanctions vary by the applicable AML, sanctions, customs, and criminal statutes and may include transaction blocking, asset freezing, administrative fines, confiscation, and criminal penalties. The payment rail itself does not determine tax liability.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible personnel may face personal administrative or criminal liability for their own conduct or culpable supervisory failures. No automatic veil piercing or travel ban applies.
Medical-device regulatory and customs non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Potential market restrictions, recalls, customs reassessments, duties, administrative fines, product-liability claims, and criminal penalties for serious intentional violations, depending on the conduct and applicable EU and German law.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for culpable regulatory or customs offenses and may incur civil liability under applicable company, product-safety, and criminal rules. Tax filing does not substitute for medical-device, customs, or market-access compliance.
