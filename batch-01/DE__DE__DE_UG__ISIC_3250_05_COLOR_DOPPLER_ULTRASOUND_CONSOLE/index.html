Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) and Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID issuance; operational registration and audit by the local tax office (Finanzamt) of the UG's place of management; electronic filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Within one month of commencing business and after commercial registration in the Handelsregister, the UG must submit the official tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') to the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER or in paper form, disclosing shareholders (including non-resident ultimate beneficial owners), legal form, minimum capital, business activity (ISIC 3250 medical imaging), turnover projections, and bank accounts. The Finanzamt thereupon assigns the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), determines VAT return periods (monthly or quarterly advance returns), and triggers the obligation to file Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen. The UG must separately apply to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under § 27a UStG and, where it sells into other EU Member States, may register via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS). A foreign shareholder does not require a German TIN; the UG's Steuernummer and USt-IdNr. constitute the sole statutory tax identifiers for this export operation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not provide a check-the-box regime. Relevant elections for a UG exporting medical devices are: (i) fiscal unity for VAT and corporate tax purposes ('Organschaft') under § 2(2) No. 2 UStG and § 14 KStG, if the UG is financially and organizationally integrated into a German parent; (ii) the option to tax real property supplies under § 9 UStG; (iii) the small-entrepreneur exemption under § 19 UStG (available in principle but economically harmful because it forfeits input VAT recovery and is inconsistent with export zero-rating); (iv) OSS/IOSS elections for intra-EU distance sales; and (v) the choice between monthly and quarterly VAT advance-return filing under § 18 UStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No absolute registration threshold for entrepreneurs; a VAT-registration duty exists from the first taxable supply. The small-entrepreneur relief in § 19 UStG applies only if turnover in the preceding calendar year did not exceed EUR 22,000 and the expected current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000. An exporting UG manufacturing color Doppler ultrasound consoles at EUR/USD export scale will invariably exceed these de minimis limits and must register for VAT immediately; the equalization threshold for intra-EU distance sales is EUR 10,000 and for intra-EU acquisitions EUR 10,000, but B2B exports to Uganda trigger no domestic registration threshold beyond the general entrepreneur concept.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of the Color Doppler Ultrasound Console to a Ugandan buyer qualifies for 0% VAT (tax-exempt with input VAT credit) under § 4(1) No. 1 in conjunction with § 6(1) UStG if: (i) the UG exports the goods to a third-country territory, i.e., the goods are dispatched or transported from Germany to Uganda by the UG or by the buyer on the UG's behalf; (ii) the risk passes to the buyer in Uganda in accordance with the Incoterms 2020 agreement (e.g., EXW/FCA, DAP, CIP); (iii) the goods do not return to the EU and are not subject to EU customs supervision; and (iv) the delivery is substantiated by complete export documentation (Ausfuhrnachweis) under §§ 8-10 UStDV. For offshore services related to the medical console supplied to a Ugandan business customer, the place of supply is Uganda under the B2B recipient-location rule of § 3a(2) UStG, so the UG may omit German output VAT if the Ugandan customer's VAT ID and the nature-of-supply test are documented; ancillary transport insurance and freight services are likewise zero-rated or subject to the reverse charge as appropriate.

  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) issued by the UG showing HS Code 9018.12 (or 9022.14/9022.12 per product characteristics), Incoterms 2020, full Ugandan consignee details, serial numbers of the consoles, and declared customs value
  • Packing list (Packliste) and the underlying transport contract with the Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) in the name of the exporting UG proving physical removal from German/EU customs territory to Uganda

  • Cargo insurance certificate and any arrival/receipt confirmation from the Ugandan hospital or customs (Uganda Revenue Authority customs declaration) evidencing the goods' arrival in Kampala/Entebbe
  • Buch- und Belegnachweis documentation (books and records evidence) preserved for at least ten years, including the export correspondence, letter of credit (Swift LC), and the TT wire transfer confirmation

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, sales-tax API, or mandatory digital stamping. Statutory invoicing under §§ 14 and 14a UStG requires sequential invoice numbers, VAT ID of the supplier, and complete particulars. Mandatory structured B2B electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) applies from 1 January 2025 under the Growth Opportunities Act: the UG must issue/transmit invoices in an EN 16931-compliant structured XML format (e.g., XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) and must be able to receive e-invoices; exemptions apply to certain invoices issued by small entrepreneurs and to foreign input invoices. Customs export formalities are performed electronically in the ATLAS system, and VAT returns, including export proof schedules, are transmitted digitally through ELSTER; the BZSt operates e-invoicing validation and the central EMCS-like systems for excise but no pre-clearance stamp.

The Finanzamt scrutinizes export zero-rating through the Buch- und Belegnachweis, the ATLAS export data, and bank cross-checks. If the UG cannot present a valid ABD/MRN, transport document, or inward remittance record within the statutory substantiation obligations, the zero-rating is retroactively clawed back: the Finanzamt assesses 19% output VAT on the export consideration plus late-payment interest under § 233a AO (0.5% per month) and Säumniszuschläge (§ 240 AO, 1% per commenced month); input VAT credits on related purchase invoices are disallowed. Intentional or reckless submission of false export evidence constitutes VAT evasion under § 370 AO (fine or imprisonment up to five years; aggravated cases from six months to ten years) and can additionally trigger primary liability for unpaid VAT under §§ 25d and 25f UStG in fraudulent supply chains, extended cessation/assessment periods (four years' standard; up to seven years for serious negligence and ten years for intentional evasion under § 169 AO), and cross-border mutual-assistance audits under EU Regulation 904/2010. The UG may also face export/deemed-export penalties under German customs law (up to EUR 5,000 per declaration) and loss of the trade-license/medical-device permissions if the export documentation is deliberately falsified. The 180-day FX repatriation deadline is a compliance benchmark; failure to repatriate does not automatically void zero-rating but requires a documented commercial justification and is treated as a transfer-pricing red flag in a follow-up audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions within the territory of Germany. Legal basis: Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) which prohibits all restrictions on the movement of capital and payments between Member States and third countries, together with Council Directive 88/361/EEC. Germany has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement (since 1961) and therefore imposes no exchange restrictions on current payments or profit remittances. Dividends, loan repayments, interest, intellectual-property royalties, and the repatriation of liquidation proceeds from the German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft) may be transferred freely without exchange-control approval. No German ministry or agency requires prior authorisation for outward direct investment, shareholder loan repayment, or trade settlement. The only mandatory compliance element is the AWV statistical reporting duty for cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or more (or the foreign-currency equivalent); this is not a convertibility restriction and does not require any licence, existing only for accurate balance-of-payments measurement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule in Germany. Exporters are not required to convert earnings into euro, to repatriate receivables into Germany, or to observe any mandatory conversion ratio. A German UG may hold settlement receipts denominated in USD or any other currency in domestic accounts, foreign bank accounts, or non-bank balances indefinitely. The only legal engagement is statistical: under Sections 67-70 of the AWV, all cross-border payments (including export proceeds) exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank; in practice the reporting is assumed by the bank, but the UG must clearly indicate the statistical payment purpose (e.g., goods export, medical imaging equipment under HS 9018.12) on its payment instructions, and must provide the Bundesbank reporting reference when requested. Exporters of medical devices (ultrasound consoles) may also have to evidence underlying trade flows for customs and BfArM/CE-MDR record-keeping, but no monetary surrender or compulsory conversion mandate applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) acting within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); the European Central Bank (ECB) defines euro-area monetary policy, while the Bundesbank executes refinancing operations, oversees payment statistics, and administers the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV) reporting framework. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises credit institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz - KWG) jointly with the Bundesbank. The Bundesbank is the statutory recipient of balance-of-payments statistical reports for all cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 or equivalent.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary disbursement operations; Low as a primary treasury or clearing tier. A German UG may open accounts with EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or selected German PSD2 entities (e.g., Solaris-supported platforms); these operate under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) rather than the KWG and therefore cannot issue letters of credit, confirm LCs, provide documentary collection, or execute infrastructure-based foreign-exchange settlements. German banks may also view offshore EMIs with enhanced due diligence because the funds are not covered by the German statutory deposit guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz). The prudential layering is: (1) sound domestic banking house for all regulated trade instruments and the AWV reporting; (2) a fintech EMI only for rapid low-value operational disbursements where no LC is required, with liquidity swept daily into the primary KWG-licensed bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound funding into the German UG must follow conventional bank wire channels with full audit trail. For EU-resident shareholders, route through SEPA SCT (TARGET2 settlement) tagging the payment 'Einzahlung auf Stammkapital' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' with the UG's legal name, registered office, and VAT ID; for non-EU shareholders (including East African investors), use SWIFT MT103 from a regulated correspondent bank; credit must be received as cleared funds in EUR. The bank is mandated to issue an 'Einzahlungsbestätigung' (capital contribution confirmation) for the notary and commercial register filing, evidencing that the registered capital is at the managing director's free disposal. Any amount exceeding EUR 12,500 is automatically reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV statistics; the UG must specify the statistical purpose code (e.g., direct investment or shareholder loan) and avoid generic descriptions. To avoid legal qualification as hidden contribution (verdeckte Sacheinlage), the first contribution tranche must be made in cash; only subsequent injections may be set off against claims. Where the founding director is abroad, the UG may open a notarised Vorgründungskonto or lawyer-administered Anderkonto before commercial registration, followed by conversion into the final operating account.Outbound settlement for medical-device exports from the UG should use the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for direct telegraphic transfers; use SWIFT gpi for tracking and confirm that the payment instruction cites the invoice number, HS code 9018.12, and the AWV purpose code for goods export; value dating must conform to the trade contract. (2) Irrevocable letters of credit under UCP 600, typically issued on MT700/MT707 and advised/confirmed by a German trade bank, used for buyers in jurisdictions where bank risk or country risk is elevated; the UG must present all documents (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, insurance certificate, CE certificate, and any importer permits) through its confirming bank. (3) Documentary collections under URC 522 (documents against payment (D/P) or documents against acceptance (D/A)) for established clients with acceptable risk appetite. (4) SEPA Instant and SEPA B2B for intra-EEA settlements and disbursements to European component suppliers. All treasury instructions are executed via EBICS (Electronic Banking Internet Communication Standard), which is the German corporate banking protocol; the UG should maintain at least two authorisation principles (Prima/Sequenta role separation) to satisfy German dual-control expectations. No cryptocurrency or informal value-transfer corridors are permissible as clearing rails.EUR/USD spot and outright forward foreign-exchange contracts (Devisentermingeschäfte) with maturities aligned to order-to-cash cycles, covering the UG's commercial invoicing currency exposure.FX swaps (Devisenswaps) for tenor-matched liquidity management, permitting the UG to convert temporary euro surpluses into USD without residual maturity risk.Cross-currency swaps (Zins- und Währungsswaps) for long-term USD/EUR financing or cashflow conversion where the UG has non-EUR assets or liabilities.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD options (call/put) and cost-effective zero-premium structures (collars/risk reversals) used only for committed order pipelines with enforceable purchase orders.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on currencies with limited offshore tradability (e.g., UGX) executed through an international dealer bank or booked as an EUR/USD synthetic hedge, given the absence of an active deliverable UGX market.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary disbursement operations; Low as a primary treasury or clearing tier. A German UG may open accounts with EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or selected German PSD2 entities (e.g., Solaris-supported platforms); these operate under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) rather than the KWG and therefore cannot issue letters of credit, confirm LCs, provide documentary collection, or execute infrastructure-based foreign-exchange settlements. German banks may also view offshore EMIs with enhanced due diligence because the funds are not covered by the German statutory deposit guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz). The prudential layering is: (1) sound domestic banking house for all regulated trade instruments and the AWV reporting; (2) a fintech EMI only for rapid low-value operational disbursements where no LC is required, with liquidity swept daily into the primary KWG-licensed bank account.
