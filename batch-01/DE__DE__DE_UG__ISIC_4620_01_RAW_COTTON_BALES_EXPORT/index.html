Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID issuance and cross-border VAT matters; local Finanzamt (tax office) for tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT registration; ELSTER online portal for electronic filing and communication.

E-government filing portal

For a German UG (haftungsbeschränkt): (1) notarized incorporation deed and Handelsregister extract; (2) tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) submitted electronically via ELSTER to the competent local Finanzamt; (3) allocation of a domestic Steuernummer and, where intra-EU/export activity is declared, issuance of a USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) by BZSt; (4) non-resident shareholders/directors may need to provide foreign tax residence certificates and, if applicable, certificate of good standing/apostilled documents. Foreign entities not establishing a German branch but making taxable supplies may register directly with the Finanzamt and BZSt; fiscal representation is not generally mandatory for EU entities but may be required in specific VAT warehouse or import scenarios.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and solidarity surcharge at entity level, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); shareholders are taxed separately on distributions. Territorial_Scope: worldwide income for corporate income tax, with foreign-source income generally covered by unilateral/exemption or treaty relief; VAT applies to supplies within Germany/EU with export zero-rating available. No Tax_Exempt_Zone default classification for a standard UG engaged in raw cotton brokerage/export.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election. Relevant elections/options include: (1) Option zur Körperschaftsbesteuerung under §1a KStG for partnerships, not applicable to UG; (2) consolidation/Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax and VAT under §§14, 17 KStG and §2 UStG; (3) VAT group treatment under §2 Abs. 2 UStG; (4) small business exemption under §19 UStG; (5) flat-rate agricultural/forestry taxation under §24 UStG is not relevant to pure brokerage/export; (6) retention of profits versus distribution has no hybrid mismatch election under German domestic law. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD/§4k EStG may deny deductions for certain cross-border mismatches but are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply in Germany regardless of turnover if the entity is an entrepreneur; no general minimum turnover threshold for VAT registration. The small business threshold under §19 UStG is EUR 25,000 for the preceding calendar year and EUR 100,000 for the current calendar year (2025 thresholds; subject to annual adjustment), allowing exemption from VAT on domestic supplies if not waived. For intra-EU acquisitions, the acquisition threshold is EUR 12,500 per calendar year, with optional voluntary registration below that amount. For distance sales into Germany by non-EU/third-country sellers, the EU-wide threshold is EUR 10,000 across all EU member states.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods to destinations outside the EU are zero-rated under §4 Nr. 1a in conjunction with §6 UStG, provided the exporter is an entrepreneur, the goods are transported or dispatched to a third-country destination, and the exporter proves exportation with customs documentation. Offshore services may be zero-rated/exempt under §4 Nr. 1a, §6a UStG or outside the scope under §3a UStG when supplied to non-EU business customers, subject to place-of-supply rules and reverse charge. For raw cotton bales exported directly by a German UG, zero-rating applies if the UG is the exporter of record and holds valid export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / EX-A / ATLAS MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice with exporter and consignee details and VAT ID
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation (for payment tracing)
  • Proof of exit confirmation (Ausgangsvermerk) issued by German customs
  • Packing list and transport insurance documents where applicable
  • Certificate of origin or EUR.1/statement on origin where preferential treatment is claimed
  • Phytosanitary certificate or cotton-related inspection certificates where required by destination country

Germany mandates electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B transactions. Since 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices; issuance obligations are phased in: from 1 January 2027 for businesses with turnover above EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all remaining businesses. The required format is EN 16931-compliant, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD from version 2.0.1. Real-time fiscal clearance via a government platform is not yet mandatory; reporting obligations are planned under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, including digital reporting requirements expected after 2028–2030. Current e-invoicing does not require central clearance but must be transmitted and archived in structured format; PDF invoices remain valid only for transitional periods and only if they meet e-invoice readability requirements after 2025. No digital stamping regime exists as of 2025.

German VAT audits (Betriebsprüfung) and customs audits scrutinize export zero-rating claims for authenticity, transport chain, and payment flows. If export evidence is missing or fraudulent, the tax authority may retroactively assess VAT at 19% on the transaction value, plus late payment interest (currently 1.8% per month under §233a AO for assessments after 2018) and penalties. Fraudulent export zero-rating or VAT carousel activity can constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, leading to criminal fines, imprisonment up to 10 years in serious cases, and potential liability for directors. For incorrect export documentation, customs may impose fines under §382 AO and the Customs Code; retrospective clawback can extend to the statutory assessment period, generally 4 years, extended to 10 years in cases of tax evasion. The UG’s limited liability does not shield directors from personal criminal or tax liability in cases of gross negligence or intent.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general German approval, quota, surrender, or repatriation restriction applicable to a German UG receiving export proceeds, paying foreign suppliers, distributing lawful profits, repaying shareholder funding, or purchasing foreign currency. Payments remain subject to anti-money-laundering, sanctions, customs, tax, beneficial-ownership, and bank transaction-monitoring requirements. Dividend distributions require distributable accounting profits, adoption of annual accounts, compliance with German corporate-law capital-maintenance rules, and withholding-tax processing. Shareholder loans and capital contributions must be documented, correctly classified, and priced on arm's-length terms where applicable. Cross-border payments may trigger statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV), generally through the Bundesbank electronic reporting system. A German UG must retain invoices, contracts, transport evidence, customs records, bank statements, and tax documentation sufficient to substantiate the commercial purpose and tax treatment of each transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank and no mandatory conversion ratio or deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into euros. The company may retain foreign-currency balances, subject to bank terms, accounting, tax, sanctions, and reporting obligations. Export declarations and customs formalities are generally completed through the German customs system for exports outside the European Union. Intra-EU goods movements may require Intrastat reporting when applicable. Cross-border payments exceeding the applicable AWV reporting threshold, currently generally EUR 50,000 per transaction from January 2025, may require statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank, subject to exemptions and transaction-specific rules. Reports are generally submitted electronically by the seventh calendar day of the following month; the relevant current Bundesbank instructions must be checked because thresholds, exemptions, and reporting categories can change. AWV reporting is statistical and does not constitute an authorization or currency-conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the Eurosystem and administering foreign-exchange reporting obligations under the German Außenwirtschaftsverordnung. The European Central Bank determines euro-area monetary policy. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises regulated German financial institutions and payment-service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary treasury and payment functionality; low to medium as the sole banking relationship for a raw-cotton trading business. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot automatically provide German deposit-taking or banking services. The provider must be authorized in its home jurisdiction and, where operating in Germany, passported or otherwise lawfully permitted under the applicable EU framework. E-money balances are safeguarded and are not equivalent to bank deposits; deposit-guarantee protection may not apply. The UG must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, permitted currencies, SEPA and SWIFT capabilities, client-money segregation, sanctions controls, insolvency protections, account-closure rights, and tax-reporting support. Offshore fintech use does not remove German AWV, AML, sanctions, bookkeeping, VAT, customs, or beneficial-ownership obligations. A regulated German or EU bank should normally remain the primary account for share capital, payroll, taxes, customs-related payments, and material trade flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share capital and shareholder funding from an account held in the name of the shareholder or clearly identified funding entity to the UG's German bank account using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments or SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-euro payments. Use the UG's exact registered name, IBAN, BIC, commercial-register details, and a precise payment reference such as 'share capital contribution', 'capital reserve contribution', or 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]'. Do not mix share capital, shareholder loans, dividends, trade receipts, and personal funds in one unexplained payment. Before registration, the notary and bank should confirm the account and payment mechanics; after receipt, retain the bank confirmation and provide evidence of full payment to the notary or Commercial Register process where required. For non-euro transfers, disclose the original currency, remitter, intermediary banks, purpose, and complete underlying agreement. Screen all remitters and beneficial owners against EU, UN, German, UK, and US sanctions as relevant to the bank's policy. Review whether the transaction requires AWV statistical reporting, direct-investment reporting, or other Bundesbank reporting. Maintain the capital-contribution or loan agreement, corporate resolution, source-of-funds file, bank advice, accounting entry, and tax analysis.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro-area and SEPA counterparties; use SWIFT MT103 for individual cross-border commercial payments and MT202 COV for correspondent-bank settlement where applicable. For documentary letters of credit, use SWIFT MT700 for issuance, MT707 for amendments, and bank-to-bank document handling under UCP 600. Documentary collections should follow URC 522, with D/P or D/A terms expressly documented. A commercial LC should identify the applicant, beneficiary, amount, currency, expiry, shipment window, Incoterms, ports, required transport documents, insurance, certificates of origin, quality and weight certificates, inspection documents, sanctions representations, and discrepancy procedures. Match each payment to the purchase contract, invoice, shipping documents, customs records, and approved counterparty file. Prohibit payments to personal accounts or unrelated third parties unless a documented, commercially justified, bank-approved exception exists. An offshore electronic-money institution cannot automatically provide German deposit-taking or banking services. The provider must be authorized in its home jurisdiction and, where operating in Germany, passported or otherwise lawfully permitted under the applicable EU framework. E-money balances are safeguarded and are not equivalent to bank deposits; deposit-guarantee protection may not apply. The UG must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, permitted currencies, SEPA and SWIFT capabilities, client-money segregation, sanctions controls, insolvency protections, account-closure rights, and tax-reporting support. A regulated German or EU bank should normally remain the primary account for share capital, payroll, taxes, customs-related payments, and material trade flows.
