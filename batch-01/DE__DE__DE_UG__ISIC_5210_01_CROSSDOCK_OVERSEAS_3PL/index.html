Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) — responsible for VAT identification numbers, OSS/IOSS registration, EC Sales Lists, EORI issuance, and central withholding matters; principal tax administration for ordinary corporate tax affairs: local Tax Office (Finanzamt) — via the ELSTER national e-filing portal (www.elster.de); customs and export clearance: German Customs Administration (Generalzolldirektion) with regional Main Customs Offices (Hauptzollämter) — service portal www.zoll.de.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Notarize the UG articles (Musterprotokoll) and secure entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) through the local Amtsgericht; obtain the registered legal entity name and registered office. Step 2 — File the business registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) with the local trade office (Ordnungsamt / Gewerbeamt), which issues a business registration certificate. Step 3 — Submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER within four weeks of formation; the locally competent Finanzamt assigns the permanent tax number (Steuernummer). Step 4 — Apply separately to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.), which is mandatory for intra-EU trade and e-commerce OSS use. Step 5 — Apply to BZSt for an EORI number (DE-format) where customs exports/direct exporters or customs representatives are used in the 3PL cross-dock flow. Step 6 — Maintain GoBD-compliant accounting records; where any POS/cash register activity occurs, install a certified technical security system (TSE). Foreign non-resident owners may structure management via a domestic managing director to ensure legal presence; Germany permits 100% non-resident ownership of a UG, but a German VAT/tax representative may be required for indirect tax procedures where no domestic establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft under §5a GmbHG) fully tax resident in Germany. It is subject to corporate income tax at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 15.825%) and municipal trade tax at 3.5% federal base rate multiplied by the local Hebesatz (effective range approximately 7.0%–17.2%, typical major city rate ~14%–15%); total effective tax burden on retained profits approximately 30%–33%. The UG is not a pass-through entity; distributed dividends are subject to withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) of 25% plus solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%) unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty. Territorial scope: worldwide income is taxable for residents; CFC rules and §4h EStG interest limitation apply mandatorily. No revenue-exempt special economic zone applies to mainland Germany; customs free zones (Freihäfen) may defer customs/import VAT on non-EU goods but do not exempt corporate or trade tax.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections: (1) Tax group (Organschaft) for corporate income tax and trade tax under §14 KStG coupled with a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and shareholding of at least 75%; (2) VAT fiscal unity (Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft) under §2(2) Nr. 2 UStG allowing the integrating undertaking to become the single VAT taxpayer of affiliated entities; (3) Small-business VAT exemption (§19 UStG) electable when prior-year turnover ≤ €25,000 and current-year ≤ €100,000; this is materially unsuitable for the cross-dock/3PL model as it disables input VAT recovery; (4) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS Union/Non-Union scheme) and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) elections for e-commerce supply chains, triggered when pan-EU distance sales exceed €10,000; (5) Reverse-charge declaration for B2B logistics services where the customer is taxable in another EU state; (6) Customs status of authorized economic operator (AEO) may be elected to simplify export procedures; (7) Anti-hybrid rules under §4k EStG (ATAD) are mandatory and cannot be waived; no elective check-the-box hybrid classification is recognized by German tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestically established entrepreneurs are obliged to register for VAT regardless of turnover volume; the §19 UStG small-business relief (exemption from charging VAT) applies only where prior-year turnover does not exceed €25,000 AND current-year turnover is expected to remain below €100,000 — generally inapplicable to the UG 3PL/export fulfillment model. Non-established foreign suppliers are compulsorily registrable for any taxable activity in Germany without a de-minimis threshold. Intra-EU distance sales of goods are taxable in Germany until global EU cross-border distance sales exceed €10,000, above which the OSS/destination-state regime applies. Intra-EU acquisitions of goods into Germany are registrable above €12,500 per calendar year (§1a UStG). Cross-dock warehousing services B2B are generally subject to reverse charge at the customer’s establishment; B2C warehousing and storage services linked to German premises are taxable in Germany from the first euro. No general turnover threshold applies to non-resident logistics providers holding goods in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available only as an export supply (Ausfuhrlieferung) under §6 UStG: goods must be transported or dispatched by the supplier or a licensed third-party (3PL) from the German customs territory to a non-EU country (or a bonded customs free zone) without interruption, and the recipient must be a foreign merchant or its agent. The UG must be the direct exporter (named as exporter in the ATLAS export declaration field 2) or hold a valid customs representative/agent mandate; preserving ownership and transport control is critical. Pure storage and cross-dock handling fees are NOT zero-rated: B2B warehousing services supplied to foreign merchants are, in principle, taxed according to the place of business of the customer (§3a(2) UStG) or as services connected with immovable property (§3a(3) UStG) where German warehouse premises form the essential basis, resulting in 19% German VAT or reverse charge in the customer’s EU state; only the physical outward flow of goods to third countries qualifies for the 0% export rate. Intra-EU zero-rated supplies (§6a UStG) additionally require the customer’s valid VAT ID and compliant evidence. The contract should separate the goods value, logistics fees, and customs clearance costs so that the 0% rate applies only to the transfer of goods.

  • Electronic export declaration (ATLAS / Ausfuhranmeldung) showing the UG as exporter, with the MRN (Movement Reference Number) confirmation
  • Export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / ABD) or electronic exit confirmation (Ausgangsvermerk) stamped by German or EU customs at the exit office
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) in accordance with §6 UStG and Article 221 UCC IA — full description of goods, quantities, Incoterms, customs tariff codes, and net/gross values
  • Transport evidence — Bill of Lading (Konnossement), Airway Bill (Luftfrachtbrief), or CMR waybill, consigned to a third-country party and bearing the shipper/consignee signatures and the official exit customs stamp
  • Customs broker mandate or power of attorney (Zollvollmacht) where the UG engages an agent for the export clearance
  • Proof of payment — banker’s credit transfer record/inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in the UG’s German bank account within the invoice term
  • Warehouse cross-dock records (WMS report of goods outwards) reconciling inventory with the export declaration and transport documentation

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic B2B supplies introduced by the Growth Opportunities Act: from 1 January 2025 every German taxable person must be able to receive e-invoices in the EN 16931-based formats (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); from 1 January 2027 issuance of e-invoices becomes compulsory for businesses whose prior-year turnover exceeded €800,000; from 1 January 2028 issuance of e-invoices is mandatory for all domestic B2B transactions. No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level clearance mechanism exists at the German national level (in contrast to Italy, Spain, or Hungary), but GoBD record-keeping rules require complete, digital, auditable retention of all accounting and invoice data for 10 years, with certified TSE requirements only for cash-register/POS receipts. Cross-border export invoices may still be paper or non-machine-readable, provided the full statutory mandatory content is present; periodic EC Sales Lists (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and standard Intrastat reports remain separate obligations. EU ViDA real-time digital reporting for intra-EU supplies is expected no earlier than 2030.

Export zero-rating is subject to retrospective audit by the Finanzamt in coordination with the Main Customs Offices and the Zollfahndungsamt. Under the statutory proof regime, transport evidence must be obtained within three months of the supply; missing or incomplete substantiation results in a retrospective VAT assessment at 19% of the consideration, plus monthly interest of 0.5% (§233a AO), commencing 15 months after the end of the assessment year. Where the UG fails to repatriate or evidence receipt of export proceeds within approximately 180 days, the tax authorities may assume a sham export or embedded service fee, leading to reassessment and potential §17 UStG corrections. Intentional misuse constitutes tax evasion (§370 AO) punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years and a criminal fine up to the amount evaded; negligent under-declaration triggers administrative fines of €5,000–€50,000 (§378 AO). For customs offences and export-subsidy abuse, additional penalties under §33 AWG and §372 AO apply. The managing director is personally liable under §§34, 69 AO for withheld and unpaid VAT, and the UG may face cancellation of its VAT ID, removal from exporter master data, EORI suspension, and reputational blacklisting under the DAC7 transparency framework.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a fully convertible Eurozone jurisdiction with no exchange-control licensing, no capital/current-account differentiation, and no restrictions on the repatriation of dividends, royalties, intercompany loans, or liquidation proceeds under Article 63 TFEU and the AWV. For the UG, profit remittance is legally constrained only by (i) the mandatory annual reserve accumulation of 25% of annual surplus under § 5a(3) GmbHG, which must continue until the registered capital reaches €25,000, and (ii) withholding taxes: a qualifying EU parent receives dividends with 0% German withholding under § 43b EStG implementing the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; a non-EU shareholder must invoke the applicable double-tax treaty (generally 5% to 10% on dividends) and meet anti-abuse substance requirements. Loan interest and royalties are likewise freely payable, subject only to arm's length and BEPS documentation rules; no central-bank prior approval is required for any current or capital transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender obligation, no exchange-conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline on export proceeds. Export receivables may be held indefinitely in foreign currency in offshore or onshore accounts. The sole statutory duty is the AWV payment-statistics notification to the Deutsche Bundesbank: any single receipt from a non-resident of more than €12,500 (or its foreign-currency equivalent), or a monthly aggregate with the same foreign payer exceeding €12,500, must be reported within five working days using the Bundesbank's electronic reporting channel. The obligation is purely archival and statistical and carries no penalty on currency retention; penalties apply only to late or erroneous notification.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) as the competent monetary authority for Germany's cross-border payment-statistics regime, operating with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking conduct and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) for trade-payment screening; the Bundesbank administers mandatory notifications under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as an ancillary cross-border settlement rail, MEDIUM as a substitute for a German operating account. Licensed EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Platform and comparable EU-passported institutions) may lawfully provide the UG with multi-currency balances, FX conversion, batch payouts and virtual IBANs under the PSD2 e-money passport. Legal constraints: EMIs may not take deposits from the public or grant credit; client funds must be safeguarded in a Tier-1 credit institution or invested in secure liquid assets (German implementation of PSD2 Art. 10); funds are not covered by the German statutory deposit guarantee (Einlagensicherung); and the UG remains responsible for its own GwG/Transparenzregister compliance. Recommended split: use EMIs for marketplace payout sweeps and FX optimisation, cap monthly EMI throughput at approximately 30% of total treasury volume, and automatically re-balance balances above €50,000 to the German principal operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injection protocol: the foreign shareholder transfers EUR via SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV to the UG's blocked capital-deposit account (Einlagenkonto) at the chosen German bank, or to the bank's capital escrow sub-account under the notarised formation instruction, with the beneficiary reference 'Stammkapital-Einlage gemäß § 5a GmbHG' and a copy of the notarial deed and shareholders' resolution forwarded to the bank pre-arrival. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH as an ancillary cross-border settlement rail, MEDIUM as a substitute for a German operating account. Licensed EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Platform and comparable EU-passported institutions) may lawfully provide the UG with multi-currency balances, FX conversion, batch payouts and virtual IBANs under the PSD2 e-money passport. Recommended split: use EMIs for marketplace payout sweeps and FX optimisation, cap monthly EMI throughput at approximately 30% of total treasury volume, and automatically re-balance balances above €50,000 to the German principal operating account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, and municipal Gewerbesteuer at a variable multiplier, yielding an aggregate effective rate of approximately 30%–33% for a profitable UG.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%) withheld at source by the UG on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% for a corporate parent holding at least 10% of the UG's capital for 12 months and meeting substance conditions).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A German UG must prepare contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation where cross-border related-party goods transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 per annum or other related-party services (including management fees, logistics fees, royalties, and financing transactions) exceed EUR 500,000 per annum; Master File is additionally mandatory where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' filing regime in the manner of UAE/BVI. Economic substance is nevertheless decisive under the German General Anti-Abuse Rule (§ 42 AO), the treaty-entitlement rule of § 50d(3) EStG, the German CFC rules (§§ 7–13 AStG), and Limitation-on-Benefits provisions in treaties. A UG engaged in cross-border e-commerce fulfillment must prove genuine substance (own premises, qualified staff, actual management decisions, physical inventory and order-processing functions, bookkeeping, and financial risk-bearing) to obtain treaty benefits and to avoid recharacterization as a strip-mall shell. Without substance, relief under DTAs and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive is denied and the UG is taxed on deemed income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years where committed on a large scale as part of a gang) and a monetary fine of up to EUR 50,000 or the amount of tax evaded; civil reassessment for the full concealed tax plus late payment interest and 6% tax surcharge.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for the corporate tax liability under § 34 AO if the liability was caused by gross negligence or intent; a separate criminal conviction may trigger a travel ban, asset freeze, withdrawal of passport, and an exclusion from managing a company for a specified period.
Shell Company / Substance Abuses

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of tax treaty relief, reclassification of transactions under § 42 AO, assessment of hidden dividends at 25% plus solidarity surcharge, denial of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and a penalty of 5%–10% of the cross-border payments (minimum EUR 10,000); the German tax office may also attribute the UG's income to the actual beneficial owner abroad.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held liable to restitution of the taxes avoided, and may face disqualification as a director for up to 5 years, a civil penalty of up to EUR 25,000, and refusal of German residence permits.
AML / Economic Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 1 million for the company (or up to 10% of annual turnover for serious AML violations); regulatory sanction includes stripping of the payment processor license, suspension of bank accounts, and compulsory liquidation for repeat offenders.

Director personal liability: Directors face individual fines up to EUR 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG); travel bans and asset seizures are routinely imposed where bank transfers show evasion of embargoes.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment to the arm's length price under § 1 AStG, plus an innocent-documentation penalty of 5%–10% of the adjustment (minimum EUR 10,000, maximum EUR 1 million if no documentation filed); late documentation surcharge of up to EUR 100,000; default interest applied.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the surcharge if they signed the tax return and knowingly or negligently failed to keep contemporaneous records; the tax authority may assert a personal claim under § 73 AO and seek a writ of execution against private assets.
Dividend Reinvoicing / Right of Assessment for WHT

Statutory sanction: The UG is made jointly and severally liable for the under-withheld 25% dividend withholding tax and solidarity surcharge; a penalty up to 10% of the undisclosed amount is levied; tax arrears bear 0.15% monthly interest from the statutory due date.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal liability for the withheld and unpaid taxes under § 44(1) and § 69 AO; those who intentionally arrange a profit-repatriation scheme through sham consulting invoices are additionally subject to criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 5 years and travel-bank restrictions.
Non-Filing and Estimated Assessments

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of up to EUR 25,000; tax office may estimate income at a higher amount (self-protective estimation) with full burden of proof on the UG; estimated assessments are not appealable on the ground of mere unreasonableness; payment of assessed tax plus 6% surcharge and interest is enforced by seizure of bank accounts and customer receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the entire estimated tax if they failed to exercise customary due diligence in filing (negligent violation); civil penalty up to EUR 100,000 can be imposed, and for the criminal offense of tax evasion, imprisonment up to 5 years.
