Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and cross-border tax matters; competent local tax office (Finanzamt) at the UG's registered office or permanent establishment for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT filing; official electronic filing and retrieval portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs matters administered by the General Directorate of Customs (Generaldirektion Zoll) through the ATLAS electronic export system.

E-government filing portal

Domestic German UG: the notary files the commercial register formation and the shareholder tax questionnaire with the competent Finanzamt, which automatically opens the tax file and issues a Steuernummer (statutory tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. The Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.) is separately applied for via ELSTER with BZSt. Foreign entities without a German seat or permanent establishment: file the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) at the Finanzamt responsible for non-resident taxpayers to obtain a Steuernummer, and simultaneously apply to BZSt for a German VAT ID; registration is mandatory before supplying taxable domestic services or invoicing German recipients.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German law; the UG is mandatorily a separately taxable legal entity subject to unlimited corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available statutory elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Secs. 14-19 KStG where a corporate parent holds more than 75% of voting rights and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement is concluded, consolidating the UG into the parent for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; (ii) small-corporation accounting exemptions under Secs. 264 and 267a HGB; (iii) election of small-business VAT treatment under Sec. 19 UStG for domestic turnover up to the statutory threshold. Hybrid-mismatch election mechanisms are restricted by the ATAD II anti-hybrid rules effective 1 January 2020, which override divergent entity classifications in cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption threshold: EUR 25,000 annual gross turnover (Sec. 19 UStG, effective 1 January 2025, previously EUR 22,000); reduced rate of 7% applies to qualifying goods. Foreign businesses without a domestic place of business or fixed establishment are subject to mandatory German VAT registration for any taxable supply performed in Germany, with no de minimis turnover exemption; port terminal stevedoring services rendered in a German seaport to domestic cargo owners or foreign vessel operators therefore generally trigger registration absent a reverse-charge structure under Sec. 13b UStG.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (Steuerfreiheit) under Sec. 4 No. 1 UStG and Sec. 6 UStG for supplies of goods exported from Germany to third-country territory where the exporter substantiates physical movement outside the EU; stevedoring and ancillary cargo-handling services physically rendered at a German seaport in direct connection with goods destined for export qualify for zero-rating under Sec. 4 No. 3 UStG, read together with Sec. 30 UStDV; services supplied to seagoing vessels engaged in international traffic, including loading, unloading, and handling of bulk cargo, are zero-rated under Sec. 4 No. 2 UStG. The UG must demonstrate a direct and immediate economic link between the handling service and the specific export goods and retain general commercial documents proving the sequence of the export transaction.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) cleared through the ATLAS system with a Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs Export Accompanying Document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) and/or customs office export confirmation (Ausfuhrbestätigung)
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), Sea Waybill, or equivalent multimodal transport document for the bulk cargo
  • Commercial invoice quoting the zero-rating statutory basis and applicable Incoterms
  • Port/terminal tally sheets, cargo manifest, and stevedoring work orders evidencing the handling services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Bankbescheinigung) or SWIFT MT103 credit advice confirming receipt of export proceeds and FX repatriation to a German bank account controlled by the UG
  • Evidence of payment and foreign-buyer identity documents sufficient for the Finanzamt to trace the underlying export sale

Mandatory German domestic B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) effective 1 January 2025 under the Wachstumschancengesetz: every domestic supplier of taxable services, including stevedoring and terminal handling, must issue structured electronic invoices in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x formats transmittable through designated digital channels; transitional reliefs permit other EDI-compatible formats until 31 December 2027. Germany does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system akin to Latin American models, but under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) framework and the national digitalization agenda, invoice-level transaction data reporting to BZSt is being phased in, with full near-real-time digital reporting expected from 2028/2030; API integration with tax-administration interfaces will then become mandatory for large port operators.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims are prosecuted as tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO (Abgabenordnung), carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases) and monetary fines; the tax administration retroactively reassesses the VAT liability (Steuernachforderung) with monthly interest accrual of 0.5% under Secs. 233a and 238 AO (6% per annum). Negligent or knowingly false documentation triggers administrative penalty proceedings under Sec. 378 AO (up to EUR 50,000 per violation); forwarding agents, customs brokers, and terminal operators participating in sham export schemes may incur joint and several liability under Sec. 71 AO. Repeated irregularities elevate the UG to a permanent audit-risk category with monthly VAT returns, customs compliance monitoring by the General Directorate of Customs, revocation of zero-rating privileges, contractual debarment by port authorities, and publication of the taxpayer in official collection/default bulletins.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility. Germany applies no foreign exchange control restrictions on either current account or capital account transactions under Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital) and the German AWV. A non-resident shareholder of the UG may inject equity, grant shareholder loans, and repatriate dividends without any Bundesbank or BMWK approval requirement. The only obligatory layer is the AWV statistical reporting obligation for every cross-border payment of EUR 12,500 or above, which is a reporting duty, not an authorisation gate. Profit remittance is unobstructed by FX law; dividend withholding tax under Section 43 EStG is a tax-layer matter and may be reduced under the applicable double taxation treaty, but it is not a currency convertibility hurdle. The UG is equally free to hold multi-currency balances in Germany or abroad.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio of foreign currency receipts into EUR. Export collections in USD, CNY, DKK or other currencies may be retained indefinitely onshore or offshore at the free disposition of the UG. The only compliance duty arises under AWV Section 67 et seq.: inbound payments received from non-residents must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank if the transaction value equals or exceeds EUR 12,500, with the statutory ZPZ statistical purpose code for goods (e.g., stevedoring services rendered) declared through the banking gateway; reporting is due within seven days of the payment. Additionally, foreign receivables and payables of EUR 5 million or more must be reported monthly to the Bundesbank for external sector statistics.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (principal authority for external sector statistics, balance-of-payments and AWV reporting compliance), jointly administered with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) is relevant only for banking licence and KWG matters, not for FX control approval.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — ancillary support only, not a substitute for the principal clearing bank. Legally permissible where the electronic money institution holds an EU/EEA licence passported to Germany under PSD2 with a BaFin or EBA notification (e.g., Solarisbank AG, N26 Bank GmbH, or a licensed passported EMI). Offshore EMIs without a European passport cannot act as the primary banking rail for the UG because German AWV statistical reporting is linked to resident payment accounts, HPA PortLog port authority clearing requires a settled German banking relationship, and stevedoring counterparties typically demand an MT103-capable commercial bank. Cross-border digital EMIs are viable for FX conversion sweeps, employee expense cards and low-value vendor disbursements, but unsuited to commercial letters of credit, escrow warehousing, port concession payment undertakings or high-value bulk cargo settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder equity injection: the non-resident shareholder remits EUR funds via SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT MT103 quoting the UG IBAN at the German account-holding bank; field 70 of the SWIFT message must contain the German AWV statistical purpose code (ZPZ) for capital participation together with the remittance text Einlage / Kapitalerhoehung. Where the shareholder funds are in USD or another non-EUR currency, the UG treasury instructs a simultaneous conversion onshore so that the escrowed capital account receives EUR; the UG then files the electronic Z1 report with the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days if the payment equals or exceeds EUR 12,500. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium — ancillary support only, not a substitute for the principal clearing bank. Legally permissible where the electronic money institution holds an EU/EEA licence passported to Germany under PSD2 with a BaFin or EBA notification (e.g., Solarisbank AG, N26 Bank GmbH, or a licensed passported EMI). Offshore EMIs without a European passport cannot act as the primary banking rail for the UG because German AWV statistical reporting is linked to resident payment accounts, HPA PortLog port authority clearing requires a settled German banking relationship, and stevedoring counterparties typically demand an MT103-capable commercial bank. Cross-border digital EMIs are viable for FX conversion sweeps, employee expense cards and low-value vendor disbursements, but unsuited to commercial letters of credit, escrow warehousing, port concession payment undertakings or high-value bulk cargo settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on the CIT amount, equivalent to 0.825 percentage points, producing a combined statutory burden of 15.825% before municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); trade tax is separately assessed at a municipal rate equal to a 3.5% federal base multiplied by the local Hebesatz (typical effective total for a port-city UG: approximately 29.8%–32.8%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) is 25% on the gross distribution, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax amount, producing a combined rate of 26.375%; compulsory for dividends declared by a German UG to both resident and non-resident shareholders, reduced only by an applicable double-taxation agreement or EU parent-subsidiary directive relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory electronic filing deadline is 31 July following the end of the tax year; where the return is prepared by a German licensed tax adviser (Steuerberater), automatic extension applies to 28/29 February of the second following year; further discretionary extensions under §109 AO are possible.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Gewerbesteuererklärung has the same filing deadline as the CIT return — 31 July following the tax year; tax-adviser-assisted returns are automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second following year; any extension granted for CIT applies to trade tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German transfer-pricing documentation under §§90, 162 AO requires a Local File where intercompany transactions with non-resident related parties exceed EUR 5 million for goods or EUR 500,000 for services in a fiscal year; a Master File is compulsory where the consolidated group revenues exceed EUR 100 million; in specific audit cases, documentation must be delivered within 60 days of written demand, and a contemporaneous benchmarking/arm's-length study is expected.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no label 'Economic Substance Regulations' in the Gulf sense, but a robust substantive economic-substance doctrine applies: §42 AO permits disregard of abusive arrangements lacking economic reality; §50d(3) EStG denies DTA relief to a foreign entity without valid business grounds and material substance; CFC rules (§§7–14 AStG) tax passive/controlled foreign profits where the German entity is a shell. For a port stevedoring UG, the Finanzamt expects the company to maintain in Germany its own staff, port terminal concessions, stevedoring equipment, operational management, office, bank accounts, registrations and full commercial contracts; absence of these exposes the UG to recharacterisation and denial of loss deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung — §370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of the responsible natural person for up to 5 years; aggravated or commercially-organised evasion carries up to 10 years; monetary fine of up to EUR 50,000 per act; the company itself may be fined up to EUR 10 million under §30 OWiG; all evaded taxes, late-payment interest and surcharges remain collectible by seizure.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and de facto officers are jointly and severally liable for hidden tax debts; personal assets may be seized, travel bans and German arrest warrants issued, professional disqualification under §6 GmbHG imposed, and civil restitution ordered for distributions made while the UG was insolvent.
