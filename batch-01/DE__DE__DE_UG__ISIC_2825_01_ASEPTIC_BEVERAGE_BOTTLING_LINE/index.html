Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG has no statutory minimum capital floor beyond one share with a stated denomination of at least EUR 1.00 (customarily EUR 1,000-3,000 for banking viability); the entire subscribed cash contribution must be paid in and be at the free disposal of the managing director before registration, evidenced by a bank confirmation; no escrow or blocked account is mandated by law; the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the stated capital reaches EUR 25,000 per GmbHG Section 5a(3)

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: official fire inspection (Brandschau) and fire-protection acceptance of the assembly hall by the local Fire Prevention Authority (Brandschutzdienststelle), including functional fire-alarm and extinguishing systems; structural: statutory building permit (Baugenehmigung) with structural static calculation (Standsicherheitsnachweis) for the hall floor, heavy overhead cranes, and bottling-line test rigs; occupational: workplace clearance per Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) with notification of the assembly facility to the state factory inspectorate (Gewerbeaufsichtsamt), registration with the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft Handel und Warenlogistik, BGHW), initial and recurring safety inspection (Betriebsbegehung) of machines and electrical installations, and, for the hydrogen-peroxide sterilization modules of aseptic lines, an ATEX explosion-protection assessment and technical documentation under Directive 2014/34/EU.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID and federal tax matters; local Finanzamt for income tax and trade tax assessments; primary electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a UG applies online or by paper form to the local Finanzamt for a tax number (Steuernummer) and to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). The UG must first be registered in the commercial register (Handelsregister); the tax office automatically assigns the Steuernummer based on the registration, and the BZSt issues the VAT ID after a separate application. Non-resident shareholders and directors may complete the process with a German fiscal representative or authorised tax advisor; proof of identity, articles of association, and commercial register excerpt are required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a UG applies online or by paper form to the local Finanzamt for a tax number (Steuernummer) and to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). The UG must first be registered in the commercial register (Handelsregister); the tax office automatically assigns the Steuernummer based on the registration, and the BZSt issues the VAT ID after a separate application. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under German law; a UG is per se a taxable corporation for corporate income tax and trade tax purposes. Elections relevant for cross-border planning include the German option to file consolidated group treatment (Organschaft) provided the subsidiary is financially and organisationally integrated, and certain hybrid mismatch election notices under section 4k EStG / ATAD rules; the UG cannot elect pass-through taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT registration is mandatory without a general turnover threshold for entrepreneurs; a small business exemption applies to turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 in the previous year and EUR 50,000 forecast in the current year (Kleinunternehmer). For cross-border distance sales of goods into Germany, the EU-wide registration threshold is EUR 10,000 total annual remote sales; a UG exporting industrial machinery will typically be VAT-registered regardless of these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available where the aseptic bottling line or its components are physically removed from the EU in the course of an export sale to a third-country customer. The UG must be the exporter, the goods must leave EU territory within statutory time limits (normally three months from dispatch), and the exporter must obtain proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) including customs release for the shipment. Offshore services linked to the installed machinery may qualify as zero-rated B2B services if the customer is a taxable person outside the EU and the recipient is subject to taxation abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung / Electronic Export Declaration with MRN),
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing movement out of the EU,
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency

As of 1 January 2025, all German B2B invoices must be electronically receivable (E-Rechnung) in a structured format; XRechnung and ZUGFeRD 2.0 are the recognised standards. Issuance of e-invoices becomes mandatory for affected businesses according to phased deadlines: from 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 2028 for all other businesses. No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime applies in Germany; e-invoices are transmitted bilaterally via PEPPOL or other exchange methods, with no mandatory government API pre-clearance.

The tax office will claw back the zero-rating if the export evidence is incomplete, missing, or non-contemporaneous; the VAT becomes due locally at 19%, plus late-payment interest (0.5% per month) and default penalties. Fraudulent or abusive claims for export zero-rating are treated as tax evasion under section 370 AO, subject to up to five years' imprisonment, additional monetary fines, unlimited retrospective assessment periods during statute of limitation, and disqualification from export VAT regimes. Substantiation records must be retained for at least ten years after the end of the calendar year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full current- and capital-account convertibility under EU treaty rules; no central bank approval is required for investments, dividends, or trade payments. Profit remittances abroad are unrestricted after domestic tax obligations are satisfied, though dividend withholding tax at 26.375% (incl. solidarity surcharge) applies to non-residents unless reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is imposed by German law. Exporters may hold foreign currency balances freely; only statistical reporting of exports (Zahlungsbilanz) to the Bundesbank is mandatory for transactions exceeding €12,500.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) oversee monetary system and banking supervision respectively; foreign exchange regulations are administered by the Bundesbank under the External Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. German/EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Solarisbank or PPS provide multi-currency IBANs and expedited onboarding. Legal constraints include PSD2 authorization, secure fund segregation, and restricted credit services; EMIs may issue local anti-money-laundering-driven onboarding but often lack full Commercial LC (MT700) capabilities, making them suitable as auxiliary clearing rails rather than primary treasury counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital contributions should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the UG's segregated 'Capital Escrow Account' (Kapitalrücklage) using ISO 20022 purpose code 'FCAP' (Foreign Direct Investment in Capital) or 'CACQ' (Capital Transfer). For EU-based funding, SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with a reference to the notarial capital subscription protocol is acceptable. Funds must be verified against the register of shareholders, and any remittance exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank under the AWV statistical reporting obligation.High-value trade settlements to suppliers and licensors should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct invoice payments, with field 70 (Remittance Information) referencing the commercial invoice, contract number, and applicable Incoterms. For complex export transactions involving milestone payments, a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) is recommended, supported by SEPAmail/electronic bills of lading. Additionally, the UG must observe the Common European Foreign Policy by including an export control declaration (Ausfuhrliste) and ensuring no arms-embargo or dual-use restriction applies on the automated packaging machinery.Plain vanilla FX forward contracts (DE-Non-Deliverable Forwards)Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currency exposureCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) for long-term financing alignment

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous German TP documentation) required for cross-border controlled goods transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 per year and for services/other transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 per year; Master File required when worldwide group turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting threshold at EUR 750,000,000 consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply sector-specific Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore centres; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (§42 AO) and established case law require genuine economic substance (real office, staff, management functions, commercial equipment, local bank accounts, decision-making) for a German UG to retain tax benefits. A letter-box UG lacking substance can be treated as tax-transparent or ignored, its losses disallowed, and its treaty benefits denied under abuse clauses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine and imprisonment up to five years; in especially serious cases imprisonment from six months to ten years; automatic civil tax assessment plus default interest at 6% per annum; possible forfeiture and professional bans.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who participated in or negligently failed to prevent tax evasion are personally liable under §§69, 71, 34 AO for unpaid taxes and fines; tax debt can lead to Erzwingungshaft (enforcement detention), travel bans, and disqualification from directorship; criminal conviction results in imprisonment.
Artificial shell company / abusive structuring (letter-box UG)

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities disregard the corporate form under §42 AO or §8c KStG, deny loss carryforwards, reallocate profits to the economic owner, impose estimated assessment and penalty surcharges; administrative fines up to €25,000; criminal treatment if evasion is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell entity are jointly liable for re-assessed taxes and penalties under civil liability principles; courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose restitution, travel restrictions and imprisonment for continuing evasion.
Withholding tax and profit-repatriation failures

Statutory sanction: Personal withholding-agent liability under §42d EStG for the gross tax; late-payment surcharge of 1% per month or part of month; default interest; exposure to criminal tax evasion if concealment is intentional; civil fines up to €150,000 for officers.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is a statutory tax debtor under §34 AO for unpaid withholding taxes and is personally liable to the taxable entity for compensation; where assets are intentionally depleted, civil restitution and criminal liability arise.
Money laundering and AML compliance (GwG) in trade-payment rails

Statutory sanction: Fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) of up to €5 million for serious/systematic violations, or up to 10% of annual worldwide turnover for legal persons; money laundering under §261 StGB carries imprisonment of three months to five years (up to ten years in organised-crime contexts).

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and managing directors can be prosecuted and fined; if they knowingly used trade payments for laundering proceeds, they face imprisonment and confiscation of assets; travel bans and professional restrictions may follow.
Transfer pricing and documentation violations

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may estimate the German UG's income, adjust pricing to an arm's-length profit, and impose a documentation surcharge under §162 AO of 20% of the resulting income adjustment (minimum €1,500; maximum €1,000,000); interest accrues at 6% per annum; serious cases escalate to tax evasion penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved undervalued exports or omitted TP documentation are personally liable for tax shortfalls and penalties under §§34, 69, 71 AO; persistent violations expose the director to criminal conviction and detention, while civil claims may be brought by co-shareholders or insolvency administrator.
