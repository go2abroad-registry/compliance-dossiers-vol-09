Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires a minimum nominal share capital of EUR 1. All cash contributions must be fully paid up and at the free disposal of the managing director before the application is filed; a bank confirmation or notarial substitute deposit proof is required. In-kind contributions are not admissible for a UG under §5a GmbHG. No state escrow account is statutory, but the paid-in capital must be demonstrated with bank-side evidence.

Funding & unlock path: The operating site must obtain a fire safety certificate from the local fire prevention authority (Feuerwehr / vorbeugender Brandschutz), structural approvals and load inspections from the municipal building authority (Bauamt), and an occupational workplace clearance from the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt / Arbeitsschutzbehörde. The employer is required to affiliate with the occupational accident insurance scheme (Berufsgenossenschaft) and to comply with the safety inspection regime of the DGUV and the Occupational Health and Safety Ordinance (Arbeitsschutzgesetz and BetrSichV) before operation begins.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account operations in Germany. No licensing, allocation or prior-approval mechanism applies to profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation or trade settlement. Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital and payments vis-à-vis third countries, subject only to Articles 64 and 66 emergency safeguards and EU sanctions regulations. The residual practical hurdle is statutory reporting: any resident (the UG) whose cumulative monthly cross-border receipts or disbursements exceed EUR 12,500 must report the transaction to the Bundesbank under AWV Section 68 within seven calendar days of the month's end. Dividend remittances from the UG incur statutory withholding of 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reducible to the treaty rate through the BaFin-certified refund or withholding-reduction procedure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. The UG may freely retain USD, CNY or other convertible currencies in domestic or foreign accounts. The only binding constraint is statistical disclosure under AWV Section 68: a batch of export proceeds that brings cumulative payments from non-resident counterparties above EUR 12,500 must be declared electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank with the merchandise-export payment-type code, the TARIC code (3001.90.90 for heparin) and the ATLAS export-declaration movement-reference number. The declaration is due by the seventh working day of the month following the value date. Supporting documents - commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, CMR or airway bill - must be preserved for Bundesbank and customs verification; no conversion or surrender follows from them.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) as the operator of the TARGET2 euro settlement layer and administrator of cross-border payment statistics, acting with BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) as prudential regulator of banks and payment institutions and with the Federal Statistical Office for trade-data cross-checks. Exchange-control administration is exercised under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), monitored by the Bundesbank Payment Statistics Directorate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium for transactional payment accounts and Low for the documentary-credit function mandatory in this ISIC 3250 use case. BaFin-licensed or EU-passported electronic-money institutions (Solarisbank AG, Raisin Bank AG, PPRO Financial Ltd) can supply SEPA and SWIFT-enabled IBAN accounts, automated FX execution and API treasury interfaces with narrower KYC burden. However, only full credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG) may issue or confirm irrevocable letters of credit under UCP 600, which is the indispensable rail for crude-heparin import chains; EMIs lack correspondent-banking depth for LC confirmations in sourcing jurisdictions. Offshore non-EU EMIs, including UK-authorized firms after Brexit, are third-country institutions that cannot passport into Germany without a BaFin-licensed branch or subsidiary; their use would add sanctions-screening gaps and AWG export-control exposure without delivering clearing capacity. The blueprint assigns such institutions a peripheral, pre-funded subsidiary-payment role only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from the foreign shareholder are cleared via SWIFT MT103 in EUR with ISO 20022 purpose code COLP (Capital Contribution) and remittance line 'Kapitalzuführung gemäß Gesellschafterbeschluss vom [date]'; shareholder loans use purpose code DGCC. Germany maintains no exchange-approval gate for non-resident contributions - free movement of capital applies per TFEU Article 63 - but the AWV Section 68 reporting duty attaches once cumulative monthly receipts exceed EUR 12,500. The account bank requires the notarised Gesellschafterbeschluss, the capital-receipt confirmation (Ausgabebestätigung), UBO declarations for shareholders holding more than 25%, and source-of-funds evidence (audited foreign-parent financial statements or tax returns) to discharge GwG Section 10 due-diligence obligations. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 15.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); combined effective statutory rate typically 29–33%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% capital yield tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) on dividends distributed by a German UG; reductions to 0%, 15% or 5% are only available under treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Returns must be filed by 31 July following the fiscal year end; if prepared by a qualified tax advisor, the automatic deadline is 28 February of the second year after the assessment period (Sec. 149(2),(3) AO).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5 million (annual aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions) for Local File; Master File required where worldwide group turnover exceeds EUR 100 million. For services, a EUR 500,000 threshold applies per German administrative practice.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, Sec. 50d(3) EStG imposes a substance requirement on foreign companies claiming DTA/EU relief with respect to German withholding taxes. A German UG itself must be able to demonstrate sufficient commercial activity, physical premises, employees, and risk controls to avoid the classification as a shell for treaty entitlement and to comply with the CFC rules of Sec. 7 AStG.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine (Sec. 370 AO); in severe cases imprisonment up to 10 years; corporate fines of up to EUR 10 million under Sec. 30 OWiG; in addition, tax under-assessment is backtracked with a 6% per annum interest surcharge (Sec. 233a AO).

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for taxes withheld and not paid to the fiscal authorities (Sec. 69 AO); travel bans and confiscation of assets may be ordered under Sec. 47 and 48 AO; criminal conviction can lead to disqualification from acting as a director for 5 years and extradition-related measures.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Company Application

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief under Sec. 50d(3) EStG; withholding tax increased to statutory 26.375% (or 15.825% for royalties); back taxes plus penalty of 50% of the evaded tax in accordance with Sec. 398 AO; if the substance abuse is organized as a sham, additional criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to semi-personally guarantee the tax of the company; personal financial liability for unpaid withholding tax under Sec. 69 AO; travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed if there is a flight risk.
Transfer Price Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: Estimate of income to the detriment of the taxpayer plus a surcharge of 6% of the subsequent income adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000) under Sec. 162(4) AO; additional penalty up to EUR 50,000 for non-cooperation; if fraudulent, criminal tax evasion charges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to maintain the documentation are jointly and severally liable for the resulting company loss (Sec. 43 GmbHG); civil claims by the company or insolvency administrator; in aggravated cases, criminal liability for evasion.
AML / Money-Laundering Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines up to EUR 1 million per AML breach under the German Anti-Money-Laundering Act; imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering per Sec. 261 StGB; confiscation of goods, arrest of bank accounts; revocation of the manufacturing license.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal exposure under Sec. 261 StGB and may be disqualified; travel bans and preventive arrests; obligation to compensate injured parties if the physical or financial damage arises.
