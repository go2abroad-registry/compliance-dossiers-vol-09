Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) — USt-IdNr (VAT ID), OSS/IOSS and BZSt Online Portal (BOP) administration; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) — Steuernummer assignment, corporate/trade/VAT assessment and audits via ELSTER portal; General Customs Directorate (Generalzolldirektion) — EORI registration and export clearance.

E-government filing portal

UG: (1) notarial incorporation and Commercial Register (Handelsregister) entry; (2) file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the locally competent Finanzamt within four weeks of business commencement to obtain the Steuernummer (tax number), the definitive fiscal identity for corporate income tax, trade tax and domestic VAT; (3) apply for the USt-IdNr (VAT ID) separately via the BZSt Online Portal (BOP) with director identity verification, adjudicated in approx. 2–6 weeks; (4) obtain the EORI number from the Generalzolldirektion for export declarations. Non-resident sellers without a German establishment must register solely via BOP, are allocated to Finanzamt Berlin (Neukölln), and non-EU sellers must name an authorized recipient (Empfangsbevollmächtigter) under Sec. 22a AO; simplified OSS/IOSS registration can cover distance sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (ELF_CUSTOM_UG) is a resident capital company taxed as an absolute taxpayer (Sec. 1 KStG) on worldwide income: 15.0% corporation tax, 5.5% solidarity surcharge thereon, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 7–17% effective rate depending on Gemeinde; no pass-through/check-the-box transparency is available; dividend distributions bear 26.375% withholding on the statutory default (subject to treaty relief); profits are accumulated subject to trade-tax add-backs and the interest barrier (Zinsschranke).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; classification is fixed by the UG's civil-law form. Available elections/structural mechanisms: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity/tax group) under Sec. 14 KStG and Sec. 2 para. 2 no. 2 UStG for CT/VAT consolidation, requiring financial, economic and organizational integration plus a profit-and-loss transfer agreement; (2) small-business exemption waiver (Sec. 19 UStG) allowing the entrepreneur to elect standard VAT taxation; (3) VAT grouping with subsidiaries; (4) OSS/IOSS election for EU-wide distance sales and low-value import sales; (5) use of the Zinsschranke escape clauses and EBITDA carry-forward; (6) hybrid-mismatch alignment under Sec. 4k EStG (ATAD 1/2) — no elective hybrid carve-outs exist, so the UG must document classification for withholding and CFC purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 (prior-year) / EUR 100,000 (projected current-year) optional small-business threshold (Sec. 19 UStG) for domestic entrepreneurs from 1 Jan 2025; below these figures VAT registration is optional. However, the UG as a commercial corporation is a taxable entrepreneur with mandatory advance VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) from the first euro, and foreign-distance and online-marketplace suppliers have no registration threshold in Germany (the only relief is the EU-wide EUR 10,000 distance-sales threshold in the OSS scheme of the establishment state).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export rating applies under Sec. 6 UStG when automotive parts are dispatched/transported to a non-EU destination, evidenced by a customs export declaration (ABD) and exit of the goods from EU territory within the statutory period; intra-Community supplies (Sec. 6a UStG) are zero-rated when the buyer's VAT ID is quoted, the goods are dispatched to another EU state within three months, the legal-basis reference is on the invoice, and arrival proof (e.g., CMR, Gelangensbestätigung) is documented. Offshore services follow B2B place-of-supply rules (Sec. 3a UStG) and are generally taxable at the recipient's location via reverse charge rather than treated as zero-rated exports. Input VAT refunds are restored via the advance VAT return and, for exporters, through monthly settlement submissions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Export Accompanying Document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument, ABD)
  • Transport Evidence: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or postal/express export receipt
  • Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung) with export notation and delivery terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / payment evidence (Zahlungsbeleg + bank transfer SWIFT) evidencing receipt of export proceeds — aligned with the 180-day FX benchmark

Mandatory structured e-invoicing (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x or EN 16931) for domestic B2B supplies: all German businesses must be able to receive e-invoices from 1 Jan 2025; issuance becomes compulsory on 1 Jan 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and on 1 Jan 2028 for all taxpayers. Germany does NOT operate a state-run real-time fiscal clearance, API authorization, or digital-stamping system; compliance is via EDI/structured-data exchange between parties, monthly/quarterly advance VAT returns (Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldung) and recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung), transitioning to EU ViDA digital reporting. For auto-parts e-commerce, marketplace-facilitation rules (Sec. 3 para. 3a UStG) and payments data (Stripe/PayPal) are swept into the tax-determination systems, with OSS/IOSS digital reporting for B2C distance sales.

The Finanzamt and Generalzolldirektion cross-check exporter identity, transport schedules, and bank remittances. Defective or missing evidence recharacterizes the supply as taxable at 19% with retrospective VAT assessment, 0.5% monthly interest (Sec. 233a AO), reversal of credited input VAT, and clawback of refunds. Fraudulent zero-rating (false ABD declarations, circular export schemes, shell-buyer chains) constitutes tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years (aggravated cases 6 months to 10 years) or criminal fines, plus administrative penalty fines up to EUR 50,000 on negligent misdeclarations under Sec. 378/382 AO, goods seizure, and customs-debt liability under the UCC; EU ViDA data-sharing will extend the audit lookback and electronic cross-examination of export claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies the EU framework of free movement of capital and payments. The euro is freely convertible, and there are no general German exchange controls or routine approval requirements for capital contributions, dividends, loan repayments, or ordinary cross-border commercial payments. Transfers remain subject to bank AML, sanctions, source-of-funds, tax, and payment-purpose checks. Dividends must be supported by valid corporate approvals and distributable profits under German law; withholding tax and treaty or EU relief procedures may apply. Cross-border payments may trigger reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), generally where reportable payments exceed EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exemptions and reporting rules. Reporting is not an exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory deadline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds received by a German UG. Export receipts may be retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts, subject to applicable tax, accounting, sanctions, AML, and AWV statistical reporting obligations. There is no general compulsory repatriation or surrender of foreign-currency earnings.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank; banking supervision is conducted by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin), with the European Central Bank (ECB) directly supervising significant institutions within the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for routine euro collections and payments where the provider is authorized in the EEA and permitted to passport services into Germany. An electronic-money institution is not a bank: safeguarded client funds are not equivalent to a bank deposit and generally do not receive statutory deposit-guarantee protection. Verify the provider's authorization, German service permissions, safeguarding arrangements, account-name compatibility, payment limits, and ability to support UG formation or capital-payment evidence. Stripe and PayPal are payment processors, not substitutes for a corporate bank account or a share-capital account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital from an account in the shareholder's own name to the UG's designated formation or corporate account, identifying the payment as share capital or, separately, as a documented shareholder loan. Obtain the bank credit confirmation and retain the notarial formation documents, shareholder resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and bank statement for the managing director, notary, and commercial-register process. Do not characterize a loan as equity or use unexplained third-party remitters. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within SEPA and SWIFT for other currencies or jurisdictions; include accurate remitter and beneficiary details and a specific payment narrative. Germany has no general exchange-control purpose-code system for ordinary transfers. Assess AWV statistical reporting and applicable exemptions for cross-border payments, including the generally applicable EUR 50,000 reporting threshold, and make required reports to the Bundesbank within the prescribed deadline.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within SEPA, subject to bank limits and recipient availability. Use SWIFT cross-border transfers, commonly MT103 customer credit transfers, for non-SEPA payments or supported foreign currencies; verify beneficiary details, intermediary-bank charges, sanctions screening, and invoice purpose. Commercial letters of credit, including documentary credits subject to UCP 600 where agreed, may be arranged through a bank for material or higher-risk imports; documentary collections may be used where commercially suitable. Reconcile payment-service-provider settlements separately from bank transfers and maintain controls over refunds, chargebacks, and payment-provider reserves.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with an authorized bank or investment firm, supported by an identifiable commercial exposure and documented treasury approval.Non-deliverable forwards where offered and appropriate for a restricted or non-deliverable currency exposure; confirm counterparty eligibility, product classification, and settlement mechanics.Foreign-exchange swaps for short-term liquidity and currency conversion management, subject to counterparty documentation and applicable EU financial-market regulation.Currency options where suitable for the exposure and governance capacity; assess MiFID II conduct and product requirements, EMIR reporting, clearing and risk-mitigation obligations where applicable, and bank collateral or credit-support terms.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for routine euro collections and payments where the provider is authorized in the EEA and permitted to passport services into Germany. An electronic-money institution is not a bank: safeguarded client funds are not equivalent to a bank deposit and generally do not receive statutory deposit-guarantee protection. Verify the provider's authorization, German service permissions, safeguarding arrangements, account-name compatibility, payment limits, and ability to support UG formation or capital-payment evidence. Stripe and PayPal are payment processors, not substitutes for a corporate bank account or a share-capital account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax (resulting in approximately 15.825% combined corporate income tax and solidarity surcharge) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with a standard levy rate of 14% to 17% depending on the municipality (average effective combined corporate tax burden approximately 29.8%–31.9%); the statutory corporate income tax headline rate is 15.0%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% German withholding tax on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax); the solidarity surcharge component is 1.375%, resulting in a combined statutory rate of 26.375% on gross dividends distributed by a German UG to shareholders, unless reduced by treaty

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 (companies must prepare and submit Local File documentation if the aggregate value of intercompany transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 1,000,000 per year; Master File and country-by-country reporting threshold is EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue; documentation must be prepared within 60 days after being requested, with a minimum content requirement under Sec. 90(3) AO and the German Administrative Principles on Transfer Pricing)

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in Cayman/BVI; however, anti-abuse substance requirements exist under Sec. 42 AO (abuse of legal arrangements), the OECD Pillar Two global anti-base-erosion (GloBE) rules (effective 2024 for groups over EUR 750 million), and the EU ATAD rules, including interest barrier, controlled foreign company (CFC) rules under Sec. 7-13 AStG, anti-hybrid rules, and the requirement that a German UG must have adequate substance (business premises, employees, management decisions) to claim treaty benefits and avoid being treated as a shell company under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive; the UG, as a domestic German entity, is automatically tax-resident and cannot be deemed a pure conduit unless it lacks economic substance for treaty or EU directive purposes

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years and a monetary fine under Sec. 370 AO; if the tax evaded is exceeding EUR 50,000, imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years (Sec. 370(2) AO); in extremely severe cases, imprisonment up to 10 years for commercial-scale evasion; penalty up to 100% of the evaded tax; additional interest of 0.5% per month on the evaded tax for the period up to 5 years (Sec. 233a AO); the court may order forfeiture of assets and confiscation of the entire online business profits; permanent denial of tax clearance certificates (Unbedenklichkeitsbescheinigung) affecting public procurement and banking

Director personal liability: Managing directors (Geschäftsführer) are personally liable for unpaid corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT withheld from employees or charged to customers if they fail to pay or declare despite knowing financial distress, under Sec. 69 AO, Sec. 34 AO, and Sec. 823(2) BGB; civil restitution orders up to the full amount of evaded taxes; travel bans and seizure of passports for repeated evasion; imprisonment for directors personally if they intentionally participate in the evasion; piercing the corporate veil may be ordered if the UG is undercapitalized or commingles personal and corporate assets
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Sec. 42 AO the tax advantage is denied and income is recharacterized to the economic substance; the UG may be treated as a sham entity, causing the corporate tax, trade tax, and VAT to be assessed against the real beneficial owner; fines up to EUR 25,000 for administrative violations; registration may be struck off the commercial register (Löschung) and the UG dissolved; if used for cross-border tax fraud, the organizer may be penalized under Sec. 363 AO with imprisonment up to 5 years; EU ATAD rules and the OECD Global Minimum Tax may impose additional top-up tax or deny treaty benefits

Director personal liability: Directors who maintain a shell UG without substance can be personally liable for corporate debts under the principle of 'Durchgriffshaftung' (piercing the corporate veil) if they abuse the legal form; they face civil liability for all taxes and social security contributions unpaid, plus interest; travel bans and criminal prosecution for breach of fiduciary duty under Sec. 266 StGB (unfaithfulness) may be imposed; disqualification from serving as a director for up to 5 years
Transfer Pricing Penalties and Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 10% of the understated taxable income if documentation is missing or materially inadequate, subject to a cap of EUR 1,000,000 (Sec. 162(4) AO); additional penalty of 5% of the adjustment amount for failure to provide information; interest on the additional tax assessment; if the transfer price adjustment triggers under-capitalization, the excess interest under Sec. 4h EStG is disallowed; the tax authority may estimate taxable income and impose a fine up to EUR 250,000 for administrative non-compliance

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally cause false transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for the additional tax assessment under Sec. 69 AO and may be prosecuted for aiding tax evasion; civil damages claims by the company and shareholders for breach of director duties; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years in cases of deliberate tax evasion
AML/Know-Your-Customer Violation in Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 for minor violations and up to EUR 1 million or up to 10% of annual turnover for serious violations under Sec. 56 GwG; orders to cease business operations and terminate all payment processing accounts; criminal penalties under Sec. 261 StGB for money laundering (imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years); the UG's bank and payment accounts may be frozen; Stripe/PayPal may permanently terminate the merchant account and withhold funds

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for AML compliance; failure to appoint a money laundering officer when required or to implement internal security measures leads to personal fines and criminal conviction; directors can be held civilly liable for all assets laundered through the entity, including compensation orders and forfeiture of personal assets; travel bans may be imposed as a preventive measure
VAT Fraud and Missing Trader (Carousel) Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years under Sec. 370 AO for serious VAT evasion; penalty up to 100% of the evaded VAT; liability for the full unpaid VAT under Sec. 25d UStG if the operator of an online marketplace knew or should have known of the seller's fraud; VAT registration suspended; the UG must pay VAT even if the customer has not paid (under German law, VAT accrues on invoiced sales); the tax authority may hold the UG's bank accounts for the VAT liability

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the full amount of unpaid VAT if they knew or ought to have known that the company was insolvent and failed to file the GmbH insolvency petition (within 3 weeks of insolvency); civil recovery proceedings and attachment of personal bank accounts; travel bans and initiation of extradition proceedings for cross-border VAT fraud syndicates; disqualification from directorship for up to 5 years
False Certification and Intellectual Property/Parts Counterfeiting

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EUR 500,000 or 5 years' imprisonment for commercial fraud under Sec. 263 StGB; customs penalties up to 300% of evaded duties; destruction of counterfeit goods and suspended import/export registration; proceedings under the German Administrative Offenses Act (OWiG) with fines up to EUR 250,000 per violation; additional liability for product liability claims from injured consumers

Director personal liability: Directors may face imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years for commercial fraud, unlawful trademark infringement (Sec. 143 MarkenG), and personal joint and several liability for product damages; travel bans and seizure of assets in cross-border cases; piercing the corporate veil if the UG is run as one-person decision-maker without proper corporate governance
