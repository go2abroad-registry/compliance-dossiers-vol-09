Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with share capital from EUR 1. The stated capital must be paid in full in cash before registration; contributions in kind are not permitted at formation. No statutory escrow is required: funds are paid into a company account or otherwise evidenced to the notary and registry. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen); Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) – EU VAT ID issuance; local tax office (Finanzamt) – registration and assessment; ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) – mandatory electronic filing; Generalzolldirektion / Hauptzollamt – customs and EORI matters.

E-government filing portal

1) Notarise the UG articles of association and file the application for registration in the commercial register (Handelsregister) via the local registry court (Amtsgericht). 2) Register the business with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt). 3) Submit the electronic 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt; the UG is then assigned a permanent tax number (Steuernummer) covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. 4) Apply separately to BZSt for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr), mandatory for intra-EU B2B invoicing and export substantiation. 5) Obtain an EORI number from the Hauptzollamt only if physical goods movements occur; pure crew recruitment services do not require EORI. Foreign shareholders do not require a German TIN to incorporate the UG, but must disclose their identity and tax residence in the tax registration questionnaire. The entire procedure is paperless via ELSTER.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the UG is a domestic corporation with unlimited German tax liability on worldwide income; no US-style check-the-box pass-through exists in Germany; corporate income tax 15% plus solidarity surcharge 5.5% of CIT = 15.825% combined, plus municipal trade tax at 7–17%; distributions to shareholders are subject to 25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge; § 8b KStG participation exemption applies to qualifying foreign dividends).

Available election forms

1) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under § 2(2) No. 2 UStG – permitted where financial, economic and organisational integration with a controlling parent exists. 2) Corporate income tax Organschaft under §§ 14–19 KStG – requires a domination and profit-and-loss transfer agreement for at least 5 years. 3) Small-entrepreneur VAT option under § 19 UStG – available only if prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast ≤ EUR 100,000 (from 1 Jan 2025). 4) No hybrid mismatch or check-the-box election is available; the UG cannot elect transparent treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to resident entities – the UG must register from its first taxable supply. The only threshold-based regime is the small-entrepreneur exemption (§ 19 UStG): optional disapplication of VAT where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast does not exceed EUR 100,000; above these amounts registration is mandatory. Intra-EU distance-selling thresholds (EUR 10,000) are irrelevant to this B2B crewing model.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Crew recruitment and manning agency fees are supply-of-labour services rendered to the shipowner, not services directly to the vessel; German administrative practice (BMF) does NOT extend the maritime zero-rating provision (§ 4 No. 2 i.V.m. § 8 UStG) to crewing fees. Therefore 0% VAT can only be achieved through the B2B place-of-supply rule (§ 3a(2) UStG): if the customer is a non-resident shipowner, operator or manning company with a valid VAT ID (EU), or a third-country business, the supply is outside German VAT and effectively 0%. Where the customer is a German shipowner, the supply is subject to 19% German VAT unless a specific exemption applies. For EU customers the invoice must state 'Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers' (reverse charge under § 13b UStG); for third-country customers the non-resident status must be substantiated and the service must demonstrably benefit international shipping operations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) – not applicable to pure crew services; required only if the UG also exports goods or ships' stores
  • Bill of Lading / vessel embarkation or crew landing manifest, ship registry extract with IMO number, and ISM Company Certificate proving the vessel's international traffic engagement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice cross-referencing the export service invoice and vessel name
  • Signed crew placement / crewing service agreement identifying the foreign principal and the vessel
  • VIES validation record or official VAT registration extract for EU customers subject to reverse charge
  • Non-resident establishment evidence (foreign business register extract) for third-country customers

German e-invoice mandate (E-Rechnungsverordnung, § 14(1) UStG): from 1 Jan 2025 all domestic B2B invoices must be received in a structured electronic format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X as XML); from 1 Jan 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 Jan 2028 all businesses must issue e-invoices domestically. Germany does NOT operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API pre-clearance or digital stamping system comparable to the Italian SDI; control is audit-based with decentralised archiving. Cross-border B2B invoices to non-resident customers remain compliant in PDF/paper, but structured formats are strongly recommended for export substantiation and reverse-charge documentation.

If the 0% export position is disallowed, the UG is reassessed for 19% VAT plus default interest of 0.5% per month from the statutory due date (§ 233a AO). Standard VAT assessment period is 4 years, extended to 10 years for concealed tax; reckless misstatement triggers penalty surcharges up to 10% of the under-declared tax or EUR 25,000; intentional tax evasion (§ 370 AO) is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases, with asset confiscation and potential loss of commercial reliability (Gewerbezuverlässigkeit). German law imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is retained as a documentary-preservation and audit-safety metric – inward remittance evidence must be kept for 10 years, and failure to remit or to substantiate the payment within the benchmark materially elevates the risk of retrospective clawback of the zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU free movement of capital rules (Article 63 TFEU) and the AWV. There are no exchange controls, no mandatory conversion requirements, and no separate capital-export licensing regime for legitimate trade and investment flows. Profit remittance is free for dividends, loan repayments, and service fees, subject only to standard German taxation: 25% withholding tax on dividends plus solidarity surcharge, possibly reduced by double-tax treaties, and no withholding tax on most arm's-length trade and service payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in the UG's foreign-currency bank account without limitation. However, all inward payments from non-residents exceeding €12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes under AWV; the reporting covers the recipient, the currency, the amount, the payment purpose, and the remitter, and must be made by the resident German company or its bank within the statutory reporting period.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) jointly administering the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), and BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) supervising banking and payment-services conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for purely offshore digital EMIs. An offshore EMI or non-licensed payment institution cannot lawfully open accounts or provide payment services for a German company unless it holds a valid BaFin license or passport under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) and PSD2. EEA-licensed EMIs passporting into Germany are viable for operational disbursements, but they are not adequate as the sole treasury rail because full-service commercial banking remains required for corporate credit, documentary commercial LC issuance, USD payroll settlement, and compliance with German balance-of-payments reporting obligations. Any non-EEA digital EMI, particularly UK-only or US-only money transmitters, should be rejected for primary treasury use.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, or other capital contributions must be routed through a licensed commercial bank using SWIFT MT103 (single credit) or MT202 (cover payment) with full sender beneficiary identity. Compliant verification includes: (i) a written shareholders' resolution documented in the UG's corporate file, with notarisation for formal capital increases; (ii) clear purpose description in the wire narrative, such as 'contribution to paid-in capital' or 'subordinated shareholder loan'; (iii) Deutsche Bundesbank balance-of-payments reporting under AWV where the amount exceeds €12,500; (iv) complete source-of-funds documentation from the remitting shareholder; and (v) confirmation that the capital is booked in accordance with German accounting rules (GoB) and disclosed in the UG's annual financial statements. The UG must not receive equity injections through third-party accounts, cash deposits, or non-passported EMIs if the funds are intended to build German banking substance.Outbound crew wages, ship-agent fees, and vessel-operating payments should be cleared through: (i) SWIFT MT103 and ISO 20022 payment messages (pain.001/pacs.008) for non-EUR cross-border salary and supplier payments; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR payments to EU-based crew and agents; (iii) multi-currency global payroll services operated by the German relationship bank; (iv) documentary and standby commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 and MT760 series) for trade and crew-provision settlements; and (v) bank-hosted trade finance platforms supporting digital presentation of invoices and bills of lading. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for purely offshore digital EMIs. An offshore EMI or non-licensed payment institution cannot lawfully open accounts or provide payment services for a German company unless it holds a valid BaFin license or passport under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) and PSD2. EEA-licensed EMIs passporting into Germany are viable for operational disbursements, but they are not adequate as the sole treasury rail because full-service commercial banking remains required for corporate credit, documentary commercial LC issuance, USD payroll settlement, and compliance with German balance-of-payments reporting obligations. Any non-EEA digital EMI, particularly UK-only or US-only money transmitters, should be rejected for primary treasury use.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for cross-border controlled goods transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 per year and for non-goods transactions (services, intangibles, financial arrangements) exceeding EUR 500,000 per year; Master File required when the worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000; UG is advised to prepare contemporaneous documentation even below thresholds given Finanzamt audit focus on crew-management fees

Economic substance scope: Germany follows OECD/BEPS-aligned substance and anti-abuse doctrines; a UG engaged in crew recruitment must demonstrate real physical offices, operational staff, contracts, payroll systems, decision-making management, and maritime placement licences in Germany; artificial structures with no local substance (Briefkastengesellschaft) are recharacterised under §42 AO; DTA benefits can be denied for treaty-shopping under §50d EStG and beneficial-ownership rules

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years and fine under §§369, 370 AO; payment of evaded tax plus monthly default interest of 0.5%; additional penalty up to EUR 50,000 per count; seizure of hidden assets; denial of DTA relief

Director personal liability: Managing directors who order deliberately false declarations are personally criminally liable; civil restitution claims may pierce the corporate veil; travel bans, passport confiscation, and asset freezes under fiscal enforcement law apply; disqualification from directorship of German companies
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Finanzamt profit adjustment with interest at 6% p.a.; formal documentation penalty of up to 10% of unreported profit; additional penalties for incomplete or inaccurate TP documentation; criminal investigation if conduct is intentional

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for preparing and submitting TP documentation on request; failure attracts personal fines up to EUR 50,000; misdirection of funds from UG may lead to director liability for tax debt under §69 AO
Payroll and Social Insurance Withholding Violations

Statutory sanction: Recovery of all unpaid wage tax and social contributions; late-payment surcharges; administrative fines and criminal penalties under §§370, 380 AO and Social Security Code; imprisonment up to 5 years for serious wage-tax evasion

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for unpaid wage tax and social charges if statutory duties are breached (§69 AO); personal bank and private asset seizure; travel ban and exit restriction during enforcement; court-ordered manager disqualification
Shell Company / Abusive Structuring (Briefkasten UG)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the structure under §42 AO; reassessment of taxable income to the UG; denial of losses, treaty benefits and parent-subsidiary advantages; tax arrears with 6% interest and penalties; in criminal cases, tax evasion conviction and imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors can be sued by creditors and the tax office for damages; veil-piercing doctrine applied for fraud or undercapitalisation; travel bans and asset freezing; disqualification as director; foreign bank accounts exposed through mutual assistance disclosure
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 100,000 (or 5% of turnover for severe breaches); criminal liability under §§261, 265c StGB for money laundering and organised crime; seizure of proceeds; potential annulment of operating licence for crew recruitment agency

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to individual arrest and prosecution if they personally ordered or tolerated violations; civil liability for damages; office disqualification; travel bans; extradition risk to flag-state jurisdictions and enforcement of employee wage claims through director personal assets
