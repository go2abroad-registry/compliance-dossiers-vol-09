Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with share capital of at least EUR 1. The entire cash contribution must be paid before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. A statutory reserve equal to 25% of annual surplus must be accumulated until the capital threshold for a GmbH is reached. No statutory escrow account is required, but a dedicated bank account and documentary proof of payment are customary and may be requested by the registry court.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF); local tax office (Finanzamt) for income and trade tax; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) and EU administrative cooperation. Electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online-Portal.

E-government filing portal

A domestic UG (haftungsbeschränkt) automatically obtains a tax number (Steuernummer) upon incorporation through the notarial filing and the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) submitted to the local Finanzamt. Non-resident entities with taxable activities in Germany must file the same questionnaire, obtain a Steuernummer, and apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) using the application for issuance of a VAT identification number. The registration must state the place of management, permanent establishment or legal representative in Germany and the declared business scope, including TIR international road haulage.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) on its corporate income. It is not pass-through eligible; distributions to shareholders are subject to German withholding tax where applicable. Resident UGs are taxed on worldwide income, with treaty relief and participation exemption applying only where German law and applicable DTTs so provide.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German law. Available statutory elections relevant to a UG: (1) corporate fiscal unity (Organschaft) under § 14 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, permitting profit-and-loss pooling and consolidated VAT treatment with a controlling entity; (2) VAT grouping as part of Organschaft; (3) limited taxpayer elections under § 4k EStG implementing EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules, primarily to align deduction/non-deduction treatment of hybrid instruments and disregarded permanent establishments. Partnerships, not UGs, are eligible for tax-transparency election where the UG is a partner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary registration threshold applies to established German businesses; any taxable supply in Germany triggers VAT registration requirement. Small-enterprise relief (Kleinunternehmer § 19 UStG) exempts VAT on turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and EUR 50,000 in the current year, without relieving the obligation to register. Non-established foreign suppliers must register whenever making taxable supplies within Germany, subject to the EU-wide intra-Community distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 for goods under § 3c UStG and the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) procedure for distance sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with full input VAT deduction) is available for (i) exports to third-country territories under § 6 UStG evidenced by a customs export declaration; (ii) intra-Community supplies under § 6a UStG, including TIR cross-border trucking where goods exit Germany to another EU Member State, the customer holds a valid VAT ID, and the supply is recorded in the EU summary declaration; (iii) international transport of goods to/from third countries under § 4 Nr. 3 S. 1 Buchst. a UStG. A TIR haulier must demonstrate that the transport and cargo are covered by the TIR carnet, that the departure and destination points are outside the substantive scope of German VAT where applicable, and that documentary evidence (Belegnachweise) compliant with §§ 6, 10 UStDV is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung, ATLAS)
  • TIR Carnet and CMR consignment note
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent road-transport proof of crossing
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Devisenbescheinigung / Bankbescheinigung) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Supplier's and customer's VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.)
  • EORI number and road-haulage operating licence / transport authorization

Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance system, or digital stamping. Mandatory e-invoicing is phased in for domestic B2B supplies: as of 1 January 2025 every resident business must be able to receive electronic invoices in EN 16931-compliant formats (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, EDI equivalent); issuance obligations begin 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and 1 January 2028 for all other businesses. B2G invoices must already be issued electronically in XRechnung or EDI format since 27 November 2020. VAT returns and payments are transmitted periodically through ELSTER; no per-transaction fiscal approval is required.

Claiming export zero-rating without statutory evidence or through false customs declarations triggers immediate VAT reassessment, denial of input VAT deduction, interest of 0.5% per month under § 233a AO, and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month (aggregate cap generally 10% of assessed tax). Administrative fines for negligent breach of documentary and customs duties may reach EUR 30,000 under § 379 AO. Deliberate evasion constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment from six months to ten years and full clawback of assessed tax, interest and costs; in particularly severe or commercial-scale cases, sentences of one to ten years apply. Officers and controlling persons of the UG may be held personally liable for the corporate tax debt and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under EU primary law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG). There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation requirements for foreign direct investment in a German Unternehmergesellschaft, and no restriction on profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder loan repayment, or payment to foreign transport subcontractors. The practical hurdles for outbound profit repatriation are company-law capital maintenance (the UG may distribute only from retained distributable profits and never if net assets fall below the subscribed capital or if distribution would cause insolvency), formal annual financial statement preparation and adoption, and German withholding tax on dividends at 25% plus solidarity surcharge and church tax where applicable, mitigated by double taxation treaties via a withholding-tax relief or refund procedure. For current-account items such as cross-border freight invoices, payment is free of exchange control but the payer must evidence the commercial substance for anti-money-laundering (GwG) purposes and, where statutory tax withholding applies, comply with the German Federal Central Tax Office processes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Germany and the Eurosystem impose no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline by which export receipts in foreign currency must be converted into EUR. A German UG may lawfully retain and operate foreign-currency accounts at a domestic bank, settle TIR transport invoices in the contractually agreed currency, and choose when or whether to convert balances. The only statutory obligation is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV): German residents must report cross-border payments received from non-residents at or above the EUR 12,500 materiality threshold, aggregated for related transactions, no later than the fifth or seventh banking day after the end of the reporting month, and the reporting duty is normally fulfilled by the commercial bank through the Bundesbank's payment-statistics reporting system; the UG remains legally responsible for the accuracy of the purpose code and counterparty data.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) acting as the prudential banking supervisor and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) responsible for foreign-trade administrative matters under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Limited and conditional. A German-licensed or EU-passporting electronic money institution (EMI), such as Penta, Qonto, Pliant, or Revolut Business, may be used as a secondary operational payment rail for the UG after full GwG-compliant KYC, but it cannot replace a Tier-1 corporate bank for cross-border TIR trucking because TIR carnets and customs guarantees must be issued by licensed guarantee chains and recognised credit institutions, and European customs/fuel-tax procedures require bank-confirmed statements and documentary audit trails. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA, holding only a non-EEA payment licence, are not viable as the UG's principal treasury account provider: they have no German banking licence, no EEA passport, and BaFin would classify their unauthorised activity in Germany as unlawful financial services without a licence. Such EMIs may only be used for low-value ancillary payments if they operate through an EEA-authorised EMI or a licensed bank under PSD2 or the new EU payment framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound capital injection into the German UG, whether as equity share capital or shareholder loan, the compliant clearing route is an SWIFT MT103 wholesale wire from the shareholder's bank to the UG's IBAN at a licensed German bank. The remittance information must identify the economic purpose, such as 'Gesellschaftereinlage' (shareholder contribution) or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' (shareholder loan), and must correspond to a board-approved capital call or loan agreement. The German recipient bank will verify the source of funds and the Transparenzregister disclosure; if the payment is EUR 12,500 or above, the bank reports the incoming payment to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics under a direct-investment or capital-inflow purpose code. For a newly founded UG, the shareholder contribution may first be transferred to a notary-controlled trust account (Anderkonto) or to a specifically designated contribution account, with release to the operating account only after the UG is entered in the commercial register. No exchange-control approval is required, but the UG must retain the capital-call resolution, notarised deed, and source-of-wealth documentation for at least five years under GwG and the German Commercial Code.The UG's outbound treasury will use SEPA and SEPA Instant for euro-denominated payments inside the EEA, SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA suppliers, fuel-card programme settlements, toll service providers and high-value bonded-transport disbursements, and commercial documentary letters of credit or standby letters of credit for large consignments where the vendor requires payment security. TIR carnet guarantee fees are cleared through the TIR guarantee chain and the issuing association, using the UG's corporate bank guarantee or cash collateral deposited in a pledged account. Fuel purchases and motorway tolls are settled through dedicated fuel-card networks, predominantly DKV, UTA, Routex and TCS Cardplus, which clear through SEPA or SWIFT against the UG's operating account; each fuel-card statement and toll invoice must be reconciled to the vehicle registration and transport order to satisfy German tax and cargo-customs audits. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited and conditional. A German-licensed or EU-passporting electronic money institution (EMI), such as Penta, Qonto, Pliant, or Revolut Business, may be used as a secondary operational payment rail for the UG after full GwG-compliant KYC, but it cannot replace a Tier-1 corporate bank for cross-border TIR trucking because TIR carnets and customs guarantees must be issued by licensed guarantee chains and recognised credit institutions, and European customs/fuel-tax procedures require bank-confirmed statements and documentary audit trails. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA, holding only a non-EEA payment licence, are not viable as the UG's principal treasury account provider: they have no German banking licence, no EEA passport, and BaFin would classify their unauthorised activity in Germany as unlawful financial services without a licence. Such EMIs may only be used for low-value ancillary payments if they operate through an EEA-authorised EMI or a licensed bank under PSD2 or the new EU payment framework.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and depends on the municipality's multiplier; the combined burden for a corporation commonly falls around 30%, but no national single rate applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic rate is generally 25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective rate of 26.375%, before any church tax that may apply. A treaty may reduce the rate, commonly to 15% or another treaty-specific rate. A qualifying EU parent company may be eligible for exemption under the EU Parent-Subsidiary regime as implemented in Germany, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and other statutory conditions. Relief is not automatic.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, Germany's documentation thresholds generally require a Local File where annual transaction volume exceeds EUR 6 million for deliveries of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services. A Master File is generally required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue in the preceding fiscal year is at least EUR 100 million. Thresholds, covered transactions, filing or submission deadlines, and documentation requirements should be checked against the rules applicable to the relevant fiscal year, including current German transfer-pricing documentation legislation.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime to ordinary German companies. German tax consequences can nevertheless depend on actual management, functions, assets, risks, decision-making, and business purpose. Relevant controls include transfer-pricing rules, treaty beneficial-ownership and anti-abuse tests, the Foreign Tax Act, controlled-foreign-company rules, the general anti-abuse rule in section 42 AO, and tax-residence and permanent-establishment principles. A UG should retain evidence of genuine operations, personnel, premises, commercial decision-making, and the allocation of trucking, fleet, customer, and financing risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax returns

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO may be punished by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, and possible late-payment or penalty assessments remain payable. Reckless tax reduction under section 378 AO may result in an administrative fine.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal or administrative liability where the statutory elements are met. Directors can also be personally liable under tax-procedure rules for culpable breaches of tax duties. Criminal exposure does not automatically pierce the corporate veil; civil recovery, asset measures, or travel restrictions require a specific statutory or procedural basis.
Payroll, withholding, and remittance failures

Statutory sanction: Unpaid tax, interest, surcharges, and enforcement costs may be assessed. Intentional non-remittance may constitute tax evasion or another criminal offense; negligent conduct may attract administrative penalties. Wage-tax and social-security violations can create separate liabilities.

Director personal liability: A managing director may be personally liable for culpable failure to satisfy the company's tax obligations, particularly where available funds are not applied in accordance with statutory payment duties. Criminal liability requires proof of the applicable offense.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax office may adjust taxable income, impose tax and interest, and apply statutory documentation surcharges. Under section 162 AO, documentation failures can result in surcharges generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to EUR 10 million, subject to statutory calculation rules, minimums, caps, and circumstances. A rebuttable presumption may apply in specified documentation-failure cases.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for culpable tax-duty breaches and may face criminal exposure if false documentation is knowingly used to evade tax. The company remains subject to primary tax assessments.
Shell, sham, or treaty-abuse arrangements

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief, deductions, or tax benefits may be denied under applicable anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership rules; the company may face reassessments, interest, surcharges, and, where the elements are met, criminal or administrative proceedings. There is no automatic penalty merely because a company has limited staff or outsourced operations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional participation, false filings, or culpable breaches of tax duties. Criminal liability and asset recovery depend on the facts and statutory elements; veil piercing is not automatic.
Customs, TIR, and transit-procedure violations

Statutory sanction: Customs debt, import duties, import VAT, interest, administrative fines, and possible criminal penalties may arise under applicable EU and German customs law. The precise consequence depends on the conduct, goods, procedure, and applicable customs rules; a TIR breach is not automatically a corporate income tax offense.

Director personal liability: Directors or responsible employees may face personal criminal or administrative liability where they intentionally or negligently commit or direct an offense. The company may remain liable as customs debtor or for administrative consequences.
Money laundering and beneficial-ownership violations

Statutory sanction: Money laundering may carry substantial criminal penalties, including imprisonment, and corporate or administrative fines may apply under relevant laws. Breaches of German beneficial-ownership transparency duties can result in administrative fines under the Money Laundering Act. Applicable duties depend on the company's role and transaction.

Director personal liability: Individuals may be prosecuted for personal participation or culpable failure to discharge applicable duties. Asset seizure and confiscation may be available under law; a travel ban is not an automatic sanction and requires a legal basis.
Insolvency and unlawful distributions

Statutory sanction: Directors may face civil repayment or compensation claims and, where statutory elements are met, criminal penalties under insolvency law. A UG must also observe the statutory reserve requirement applicable to UG profits until the prescribed capital threshold is reached; this requirement does not replace insolvency-law restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments, culpable delay in filing for insolvency, or breaches of capital-maintenance rules. Criminal penalties may apply in qualifying cases; liability is fact-specific and does not arise solely from insolvency.
