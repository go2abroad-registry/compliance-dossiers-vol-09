Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires minimum nominal share capital of EUR 1.00, fully paid in cash before Commercial Register entry; only cash contributions are permitted (no in-kind contributions). No statutory escrow account is prescribed, but for a single-person UG the paid-in contribution must remain at the free disposal of the managing director until registration. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profits as a statutory reserve until the capital reaches EUR 25,000, at which point it may convert to a standard GmbH.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required from the local fire-protection authority based on building inspection, alarm systems, and emergency escape routes; structural load certification of the gantry/overhead-crane runway and plant steel structure must be prepared by a licensed structural engineer; the complete workplace must receive clearance under the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV), including ventilation, lighting, and emergency exits; before first use, the overhead cranes and gantry cranes must be inspected by a TÜV or accredited inspector under BetrSichV, and thereafter undergo regular inspections by a competent person as required by DGUV Vorschrift 52.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) at www.bzst.de for VAT ID allocation and withholding procedures; local municipal tax office (örtliches Finanzamt) for income, corporate, and trade tax assessment; electronic filing via ELSTER portal at www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

For a German Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) with foreign ownership: (1) file notarial deed of incorporation with the commercial register (Handelsregister) to obtain registered company number; (2) submit electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, via ELSTER or authorized tax advisor, to obtain the statutory Steuernummer; (3) apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) under § 27a UStG, using the official form (USt 1) or verified ELSTER application; (4) for intra-Community or export trades, register the VAT ID with the EU VIES system and obtain an EORI number via the General Directorate of Customs (Generalzolldirektion) for customs declarations; identity verification of foreign shareholders requires apostilled or legalized documents, certified translations, and bank confirmation of paid-up minimum capital where applicable; timelines typically 4–8 weeks, with provisional tax number available for retroactive invoicing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — UG is a German corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) with unlimited domestic tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) on worldwide income if its seat (Sitz) or place of management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) is in Germany; subject to 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), 5.5% solidarity surcharge thereon, municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at rates of 7%–17% (Hebesatz-dependent), and VAT as a taxable entrepreneur; profits distributed as dividends are subject to 25% withholding tax at the shareholder level; no territorial remittance exemption applies to foreign-source income except under treaty tie-breaker or DTA provisions. — For a German Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) with foreign ownership: (1) file notarial deed of incorporation with the commercial register (Handelsregister) to obtain registered company number; (2) submit electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, via ELSTER or authorized tax advisor, to obtain the statutory Steuernummer; (3) apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) under § 27a UStG, using the official form (USt 1) or verified ELSTER application; (4) for intra-Community or export trades, register the VAT ID with the EU VIES system and obtain an EORI number via the General Directorate of Customs (Generalzolldirektion) for customs declarations; identity verification of foreign shareholders requires apostilled or legalized documents, certified translations, and bank confirmation of paid-up minimum capital where applicable; timelines typically 4–8 weeks, with provisional tax number available for retroactive invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — UG is a German corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) with unlimited domestic tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) on worldwide income if its seat (Sitz) or place of management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) is in Germany; subject to 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), 5.5% solidarity surcharge thereon, municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at rates of 7%–17% (Hebesatz-dependent), and VAT as a taxable entrepreneur; profits distributed as dividends are subject to 25% withholding tax at the shareholder level; no territorial remittance exemption applies to foreign-source income except under treaty tie-breaker or DTA provisions.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election comparable to the United States exists in German tax law; the UG is legally and tax-relevantly a corporation by mandatory statute. Elective instruments available: (1) formation of an Organschaft (corporate tax and VAT group) under §§ 14–19 KStG / § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG between a German parent GmbH/UG and financially, organizationally, and economically integrated German subsidiaries, with profit-and-loss transfer agreement; (2) election under the consolidated group taxation file (Zusammenfassende Meldung) for intra-Community supplies; (3) optionally apply § 4k EStG and § 4j EStG hybrid mismatch election carve-outs to resist double non-taxation under ATAD; (4) no partnership pass-through election is lawful for a UG; (5) permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte) filing and allocation elections under DTA, including application for treaty-based fiscal residency certificates via form ESt 1 DB. All elections require Finanzamt approval and must be exercised on an annual, irrevocable basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 — Section 19 UStG small-business exemption threshold (prior-year supplies ≤ EUR 25,000 and current-year projected supplies ≤ EUR 100,000); however, an UG engaged in cross-border or export trade of gantry cranes is required to register for VAT regardless of turnover from its first transaction, as the small-business exemption is unavailable for inter-Community acquisitions or exempt-with-input-credit supplies; every German entrepreneur (§ 2 UStG) with a domestic seat or permanent establishment has a statutory duty to register, with no de-minimis threshold; non-established foreign suppliers must register for German VAT only if no reverse-charge applies, with a distance-sales registration threshold of EUR 10,000 (OSS one-stop-shop scheme) applicable to B2C supplies of goods within the EU.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under § 4 Nr. 1 lit. a UStG in conjunction with § 6 UStG to deliveries of gantry cranes and overhead crane components transported by the supplier or by a third party to third-country territory (outside the EU), provided the export proofs under §§ 8–10 UStDV are rendered; intra-Community supplies (B2B exports to EU Member States that are not Germany) are zero-rated under § 4 Nr. 1 lit. b UStG and § 6a UStG, with the customer's valid VAT ID and a recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) filed monthly/annually; offshore services (e.g., crane installation engineering, repair, and design services supplied to non-resident customers without domestic use) qualify for zero rating under § 4 Nr. 8–12 UStG and §§ 3a, 3b UStG (B2B place-of-supply rules) where the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside the EU; physical export must be completed within 3 months from the date of the liable supply (section 6 UStG), except for assembly/test deliveries (Montage-Lieferungen) which are deemed supplies of services; the supplier must retain transfer notes (Versendungsbelege), transport orders (Frachturkunden), and customs-validated export declarations (Ausfuhranmeldung / Buchungsbestätigung) in original form to support the zero-rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) via ATLAS-Customs system (for port cranes, SA/HS code 8426/2816-specific classification; Declaration number and MRN) with proof of actual physical dispatch to the third-country customer, such as the Bill of Lading (Seefrachtbrief/konnossement)
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) with the UG's VAT ID, the foreign buyer's VAT ID or tax reference, letter of credit terms, Incoterms, and exact HS classification (ISIC 2816 corresponds to HS 8426; NAICS 333923 alignments)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill / CMR transport document (CMR Frachtbrief) evidencing the goods departing German territory and arriving in the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Deviseneingangsanzeige) issued by the German bank confirming receipt of export consideration in EUR or USD under the documentary LC or project escrow payment rail, required by German banking AML rules and customs audit practice to substantiate commercial reality
  • ATLAS export declaration status confirmation (Schlussnachricht) and proof of customs clearance at the exit office of the EU as required by § 10 Abs. 2 UStDV
  • Project escrow agreement or documentary LC exhibit (SWIFT MT700) identifying the underlying export contract for value substantiation
  • Certificate of origin (for preferential tariff claims, only where applicable, not a statutory VAT zero-rating condition)

Germany mandates B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) as of 1 January 2025: all German-established taxable persons must be able to receive structured, machine-readable e-invoices (XRechnung format or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1/2.2 extensions) for domestic B2B transactions; mandatory issuance of e-invoices applies from 1 January 2027 to entrepreneurs with a prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 to all entrepreneurs; exports must still be documented with customs export declarations, but the e-invoice standard also covers export invoices; Germany has not yet introduced real-time fiscal clearance, however, from 2026, the use of digital reporting of intra-EU transaction data (DGUV 1 system) becomes mandatory under the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age package, with a structured e-invoice clearance obligation (transaction-level reporting to BZSt) anticipated by 2030; the BZSt API (Schnittstelle) requires A2A (machine-to-machine) submission under ELSTER XRechnung API with X.509 mutual TLS authentication and digital signature stamp (qualifizierte elektronische Signatur) for self-billing or continuous transaction control; QR codes or digital stamping alone do not constitute German tax compliance absent the underlying XML/CII/UBL structure.

German tax authorities conduct external audits (Außenprüfung, § 193 AO) for exporters at a rate materially above the corporate average; export zero-rating is denied retroactively if the proof of export under § 10 UStDV is missing, inconsistent, or deemed forged, resulting in VAT being reassessed at 19% on the net invoice together with late-payment interest of 0.5% per month under § 233a AO (6% p.a.) from the statutory filing date; false or non-returned export documentation triggers penal exposure under § 370 AO for tax evasion with imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines up to EUR 500,000 (cumulative), plus under § 26c Abs. 1 UStG fines up to EUR 25,000 for substantiation failures; the BZSt may automatically exchange export data via the ATLAS and EU OLAF anti-fraud network (AFIS), and a reverse-charge or zero-rating claim fraudulently asserted for the port crane and gantry segment triggers seizure of export goods and the freezing of VAT refunds; retrospective clawback period extends to the full statutory limitation of 10 years (6 months for simple errors, § 14b UStG) for missing invoices, with the burden of proof on the UG acting through its tax advisor (Steuerberater); notarial and documentary evidence must be retained in German and English translation, and non-compliance also disqualifies the UG from later VAT refund applications under § 18 Abs. 9 UStG.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account framework. The euro is freely convertible with no exchange-control approval or surrender requirements for trade, service, dividend, profit transfer, or loan repayment payments. Dividends and profits of a German UG may be remitted to non-resident shareholders after audited financial statements and tax clearance, subject only to anti-money-laundering, transparency, and Bundesbank statistical reporting obligations. There is no capital account permit or central bank conversion ration, though payments above EUR 12,500 crossing the German border require a mandatory AWV statistical declaration to Deutsche Bundesbank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The German foreign trade regime does not impose any statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters have unrestricted disposition over proceeds in euro or foreign currency and may retain them abroad or convert them as market conditions dictate. Only statistical reporting applies: any payment exceeding EUR 12,500 between a German resident and a non-resident must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days of receipt via the AWV electronic reporting interface, using the correct payment purpose code and counterparty details.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) acting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. An offshore digital EMI not licensed by BaFin or not EEA-passported under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) cannot lawfully provide commercial payment accounts to a German UG; such providers lack the required MiFID/KWG authorisation and would be treated as unlicensed payment services. EEA-licensed EMIs and payment banks may operate in Germany under passporting, and a small number of German digital banks (e.g. licensed specialised banks or PSD2 EMIs) can support basic settlement. However, documentary credit transactions and project escrow arrangements are usually only accepted by full German commercial banks or by their project finance units, not by mainstream fintech EMIs. The UG should therefore treat fintechs as a secondary recovery-account solution only.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge thereon; trade tax excluded and separately determined by municipality)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge thereon (total 26.375%), withheld and remitted by the UG on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders; trade tax and corporate income tax are entity-level taxes and do not apply to the recipient shareholder

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when cross-border transactions with related parties exceed 500,000 EUR in a fiscal year for goods or 100,000 EUR for services; Master File: mandatory when group consolidated revenue exceeds 100 million EUR; German Documentation Requirements under Section 90(3) AO and GAufzV; exemption for transactions where counterparty is a CFC and aggregate arm's length adjustment is below 100,000 EUR

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the UAE; instead, the German general anti-abuse rule (Section 42 AO) and the controlled foreign company rules (Section 7-13 AStG) deny tax benefits for shell entities lacking real economic activity. The UG must maintain actual substance: own business premises, qualified personnel, active commercial functions, and risk management. For UAE shareholders, the UAE ESR registration and filing deadlines apply only to the UAE entity itself, not to the German UG; however, the German-incorporated UG must file annual financial statements with the Handelsregister and the tax returns, proving substance to satisfy the OECD BEPS and EU ATAD directives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Section 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 10 years (Section 370(2) AO) and monetary fine up to 50,000 EUR; additional tax assessment plus late payment surcharge; mandatory publication of the penalty under Section 379(3) AO

Director personal liability: Managing director (Geschäftsführer) personally liable for unpaid corporate tax amounts under Section 34(1) AO and Section 69 AO for culpable breach of tax obligations; civil restitution of the full tax underpayment; travel ban and seizure of passport under Section 132 StPO; disqualification from serving as director for up to 5 years; criminal conviction recorded
Late Filing and False Documentation (Section 152 AO, Section 379 AO)

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty up to 25,000 EUR; false documentation penalty up to 50,000 EUR; estimated assessment based on the best of the tax authority's knowledge (Schätzung) with potential 10% surcharge on the difference

Director personal liability: Director must assert the interest claim of the UG to the tax authority; if the director fails to file and caused the tax shortfall, personal liability for the tax difference and the penalty cannot be satisfied by the corporate assets (Section 69 AO)
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits under Section 50d(3) EStG; application of CFC rules under Section 7 AStG causing dividend and interest income to be reclassified as German taxable income; withdrawal of trade tax and corporate income tax exemptions; penalty under Section 42 AO for abusive transactions

Director personal liability: Director jointly and severally liable for treaty-shopping claims; personal liability for under-withheld capital gains tax under Section 44(5) EStG; criminal liability for aiding tax evasion under Section 27 StGB; travel restrictions applied
Anti-Money Laundering (GwG) Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to 1 million EUR for natural persons and up to 5% of annual turnover for legal persons (Section 56 GwG); criminal money laundering charge under Sections 261-262 StGB with imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Director of the UG can be held personally liable for AML failures; under Section 43(2) GwG, the person responsible for compliance (Geldwäschebeauftragter) must be a natural person and is liable; the managing director who acts with gross negligence is subject to civil damages claims and potential prosecution for money laundering under Section 261(2) StGB
Incorrect Profit Repatriation / Failure to Withhold

Statutory sanction: Personal liability of the UG's managing director for the unpaid withholding tax (Section 44(5) EStG); penalty up to 50,000 EUR under Section 379(2) AO; late payment surcharge of 1% per month

Director personal liability: Managing director is personally liable for the full withheld amount if not remitted to the Finanzamt; civil restitution claim by the UG; criminal prosecution for embezzlement of tax funds under Section 354 StGB; travel ban if the director attempts to leave Germany
