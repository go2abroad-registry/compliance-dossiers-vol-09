Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under § 5a GmbHG the UG has no statutory minimum share capital and may commence with as little as EUR 1; however, the fully subscribed registered share capital must be paid in full in cash (in-kind contributions are excluded) and held in a blocked bank account (Sperrkonto) until registration; 25% of annual net profits must be allocated to the statutory reserve each year until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000; the core capital must be maintained and may not be repaid to shareholders

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local Finanzamt administers corporate income tax, trade tax, and domestic VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.). ELSTER is the official electronic tax filing and communication portal.

E-government filing portal

After formation and commercial-register entry, submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt, generally within one month of commencing business. The Finanzamt assigns a tax number (Steuernummer) and assesses advance tax obligations. Apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number, ordinarily through the tax registration process or the BZSt application procedure. A foreign-established entity carrying out taxable transactions in Germany must register with the competent Finanzamt before or when its German VAT liability arises; it generally cannot rely on the domestic small-business threshold. Provide formation and registry documents, constitutional documents, director and beneficial-owner details, business and German activity information, bank details, and evidence of authority to act. Non-German documents may require certified translations. A fiscal representative is generally not required for an entity established in another EU Member State; a non-EU-established entity may be required to appoint one, subject to statutory exceptions and applicable mutual-assistance arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A German UG is a corporation and is not eligible to elect US-style pass-through or check-the-box treatment under German domestic tax law. It is generally subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). No general election converts it into a transparent entity. EU cross-border reorganizations and hybrid-mismatch rules may affect particular transactions but do not provide a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in Germany, subject to specific exemptions and special schemes. The domestic small-business exemption under section 19 UStG generally applies only if prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; these are exemption limits, not a general registration threshold. A German UG may use the exemption only if the statutory conditions are met and it has not waived it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with credit (effectively 0%) under section 4 no. 1(a) in conjunction with section 6 UStG, provided the statutory export-supply conditions are met and the supplier retains prescribed evidence. Intra-EU supplies are potentially exempt with credit under separate rules, including valid customer VAT identification and evidence of transport. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: the place-of-supply rules determine whether German VAT applies. Refractory masonry or thermal-insulation work directly connected with German immovable property is generally located in Germany for VAT purposes and is ordinarily taxable at 19%, unless a specific exemption applies.

  • Commercial invoice, sales contract or order, and transport or delivery records identifying the goods and consignee
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport evidence, where applicable
  • Evidence of the supplier's involvement in arranging or commissioning transport, where required to establish the applicable export-supply category
  • For intra-EU supplies rather than exports to third countries: valid customer VAT identification evidence and appropriate transport and accounting records
  • Bank receipt or foreign-exchange remittance certificate is not a general German statutory condition for export VAT exemption

Germany does not operate general real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital stamping. From 1 January 2025, German-established businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Issuance obligations are phased: during 2025–2026, paper invoices and qualifying non-structured electronic invoices may generally continue with recipient consent; specified transition relief extends into 2027 for qualifying issuers, and general mandatory structured e-invoice issuance applies from 1 January 2028, subject to statutory conditions and exceptions. Structured formats must comply with the applicable European standard, including EN 16931, or an accepted equivalent. No general government clearance API is required. Tax-compliant invoice content, archiving, and transmission requirements apply.

Germany imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must substantiate the applicable export exemption and preserve records under German VAT and tax-recordkeeping rules. If proof is absent or the statutory conditions are not met, VAT may be assessed retrospectively, generally with interest under the applicable tax-procedure rules. Intentional or reckless false claims may result in tax penalties or criminal liability for tax evasion; procedural fines and additional assessments may also apply. Penalties depend on the facts, culpability, and amounts involved.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency for both capital and current account operations; Germany imposes no exchange-control licensing, no prior approval, and no quantitative limits on inward or outward transfers for resident or non-resident shareholders of a German UG. Profit remittance and dividend distributions from the UG to foreign shareholders are legally unrestricted, subject only to statutory withholding tax (25% Kapitalertragsteuer and, where applicable, the solidarity surcharge; the effective rate is reduced to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under section 43b EStG or under treaty relief) and to double-tax-treaty provisions. The only material administrative burden is the mandatory balance-of-payments reporting obligation for every cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month (AWV sections 7, 8-15): payment receipts require report type Z3, funds sent by the UG require report type Z4, and export or import goods payments require Z5 or Z8. Late or missing filings are administrative offences (Ordnungswidrigkeit) with fines up to EUR 30,000, and the Bundesbank may impose an escalating late-notification penalty (Verspätungsgeld).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation obligation for export proceeds in Germany because the euro is the domestic settlement currency. A German UG may retain 100% of export proceeds in euros or in foreign currency, either in an account with any German or EU bank or in an external account, without any conversion requirement. The sole hard requirement is report-based: export receipts from non-EU or third-country debtors must be reported to the Bundesbank under AWV section 10 using report type Z5 when the total amount received per calendar month exceeds EUR 12,500, and the monthly declaration must be submitted by the statutory working-day deadline of the following month (the Bundesbank merges Z1-Z9 into a single monthly payments declaration). If the invoice currency is a foreign currency, the equivalent euro amount must be stated at the reference exchange rate in the statutory reports, and a foreign-currency account balance held by the UG above EUR 12,500 must additionally be disclosed in the Z8 report.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank), operating under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) and in coordination with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK); all cross-border capital and payment statistics are filed electronically with the Bundesbank through the Allgemeines Meldeportal (General Reporting Portal).

Statutory-compliant route for a non-resident shareholder injecting cash into the UG: (i) execute the initial-subscription deed or capital-increase resolution before a German notary, referenced to sections 55-56 GmbHG for increases and to section 5a GmbHG for the UG formation; (ii) originate a SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire from the shareholder's own account to the UG's dedicated capital-escrow account (IBAN) with a precise purpose clause such as 'Einlage in UG DE_UG — Kapitalerhöhung gemäß §55 GmbHG' or 'Gründungseinlage gemäß §5a GmbHG'; (iii) use the correct Bundesbank balance-of-payments statistics code for direct investment, using report type Z3 for payments received from abroad and the statistics code class for capital contributions (code 2100/2110 'Kapitalkonten und Grundkapital'); (iv) reconcile the exact euro amount of the wire instruction to the notarised subscription amount, because any difference raises a money-laundering suspicion under section 25c KWG; (v) upon credit, the UG's bank issues a free-disposal declaration (Freigabeerklärung) confirming that the subscribed capital is fully paid in and available; and (vi) report the inbound payment to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the statutory monthly Z3/Z4 declaration, noting that every capital contribution is reportable regardless of the EUR 12,500 threshold. Non-compliant routing, including settlement through a third-country intermediary without Bundesbank reporting or the use of a director's personal account as a conduit, is an administrative offence and may trigger a refund or reversal at the account level.Primary rails for the refractory-lining procurement and subcontractor chain: (1) SWIFT MT103 (customer bank-to-bank transfer) with the mandatory purpose code 'Handelsgeschäft' or 'Warenlieferung', plus references to purchase order, pro-forma invoice, and, where goods cross borders, the ATLAS export declaration; this is used for progress-wire payments to subcontractor masons and for third-party aggregate suppliers. (2) SEPA CT (ISO 20022 pain.001.001.03) for EU-domiciled suppliers and subcontractors, a zero-fee intra-EEA rail that is immediately credited but cannot be used outside the euro domain. (3) Documentary collection (D/P or D/A), or, where supplier risk materialises, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) issued by the UG's Tier-1 bank for the purchase of refractory ceramic bricks from Asia, with the LC advising bank and authenticity verified through bank-to-bank confirmation. (4) Telegraphic transfer (TT) variants retained as fallback for urgent supplier payments at a higher centre-rate spread. For every outbound wire above EUR 12,500 in a calendar month, the UG or its bank on its behalf must submit the Z4 report to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics; the bank will require the UG to pre-register the intended counterparty, the destination country (ISO 3166 code), and the purpose-of-payment statistics code in its KYC form prior to releasing a payment order.Outright foreign-exchange forward contract (Devisentermingeschäft) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY, or EUR/TRY, with tenor aligned to the underlying refractory-supply or project-invoice date (typically 2-12 months) and full bank-assessed commercial exposure; permitted without exchange-control approval and enforceable under the German Master Agreement for Financial Hedges (Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte).Close-out non-deliverable forward (NDF) on restricted or inconvertible settlement currencies (for example CNY, TRY, INR, or SAR where the bank's onshore arm handles fixings), documented with a standard ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and an agreed reference fixing (WMR 10:00 CET for EUR or 16:00 London for USD); legally permissible under sections 1(11) and 32(1) KWG and not prohibited by AWV, but offered to the UG only with an executed credit-support annex (positive or negative pledge).Cross-currency swap (CIRS), for example EUR/USD or EUR/CNY, used when the UG receives USD project retainage but pays EUR-denominated subcontractor costs, or vice versa; permits principal and coupon netting over the job cycle; permitted under the general freedom-of-capital principle of Article 63 TFEU, reported in the bank's derivatives register, and subject to MiFID II eligible-counterparty and EMIR reporting requirements.Plain-vanilla purchased FX option (long EUR/USD call or protective floor structure) that protects the UG's monthly project proceeds without unlimited mark-to-market risk; permitted without AWV licensing but subject to EMIR transparency duties for off-exchange OTC trades and to mandatory clearing if the counterparty is a non-EEA bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary flows, Low as a primary banking relationship. Legal constraints: a fintech or electronic-money institution (EMI) may lawfully service the UG in Germany under the EU PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and E-Money Directive passport only if it is licensed in an EEA state and has notified BaFin; however, such entities cannot execute Deutsche Bundesbank AWV balance-of-payments reporting on behalf of the UG, cannot hold UG capital-escrow evidence, cannot issue commercial letters of credit or guarantees, and routinely cap cross-border throughput. Recommended use: high-speed sub-EUR 12,500 supplier disbursements and card-based payroll via providers such as Airwallex (Penso), Wise Business, or Qonto; the UG must nevertheless retain a domestic Tier-1 bank as the statutory reporting anchor.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Koerperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a typical levy factor of 340%-490% (weighted national effective approx. 14.0%-17.0%); combined headline burden approx. 29.8%-33.3%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) withheld by the UG on dividend distributions and any hidden profit distributions to non-resident shareholders; final withholding regime for individuals; corporate shareholders may claim treaty relief.
