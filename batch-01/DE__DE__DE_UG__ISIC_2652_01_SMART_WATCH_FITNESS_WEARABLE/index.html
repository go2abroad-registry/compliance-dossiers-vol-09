Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.); responsible local tax office (Finanzamt) for income and trade tax registration; electronic filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de)

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-owned UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must first complete commercial register registration with notarized incorporation. Within one month of business commencement, file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt, disclosing shareholders, directors, activity, and projected turnover, to obtain the Steuernummer (income/trade tax number). Separately, apply for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) from BZSt, typically after tax registration is acknowledged. Foreign directors/shareholders must submit identity documents, certified translations if needed, and evidence of registered office or business address in Germany. Non-resident ownership is permitted; no minimum capital is required for UG formation, but annual retention of 25% of net profit applies. Tax registration processing generally takes 2 to 6 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
Germany does not permit check-the-box classification. A UG is strictly treated as a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5%), and trade tax (local rate around 14-17%). Available statutory elections include: (i) tax group / fiscal unity (Organschaft) under sections 14-17 KStG for corporate income tax and VAT, allowing profit and VAT pooling with a domestic parent; (ii) small-entrepreneur treatment (Kleinunternehmer) under section 19 UStG if prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover ≤ EUR 50,000, waiving output VAT and input VAT recovery; (iii) simplified profit determination under section 4(3) EStG for low-turnover UGs; (iv) anti-hybrid elections and ATAD implementation adjustments are limited but must be assessed in cross-border structures.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Domestic small entrepreneurs: EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year projection for Kleinunternehmer relief. Foreign entrepreneurs supplying goods or services in Germany: mandatory VAT registration without any turnover threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods to non-EU third countries qualify for a 0% VAT rate under section 4 No. 1(a) and section 6 UStG, provided the supplier substantiates the physical export from Germany into the third-country territory. The recipient must be the purchaser/consignee abroad; goods must leave the EU within the statutory timeframes. Supplies to EU member states are intra-Community supplies under section 4 No. 1(b) UStG, zero-rated with a valid EU VAT ID of the customer and a recapitulative statement. Manufacturing of smartwatches and fitness wearables qualifies where the goods are physically shipped to non-EU customers. Services closely related to exported goods or certain B2B services to third-country entrepreneurs may also be zero-rated under section 4 No. 3 UStG. Export refunds are administered through regular VAT returns; excess input VAT is refundable.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims result in reversal of input VAT and output VAT assessed at 19%, plus late-payment interest (0.5% per month or 6% per annum). Fines of up to 25% of the tax under section 162 AO may apply; criminal tax evasion under section 370 AO carries penalties up to 5 years imprisonment or monetary fines. The Finanzamt and customs authorities conduct risk-based audits of export documentation and may demand physical verification of shipped goods or third-party transport evidence. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Germany uses the euro and has full current-account and capital-account convertibility under applicable European Union law. There is generally no exchange-control approval for receipt of foreign investment, shareholder loans, intercompany funding, trade receipts, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. Banks must nevertheless apply sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, beneficial-ownership, tax-residency, and payment-transparency controls. A UG may remit dividends only from distributable profits approved through proper corporate accounts and resolutions, subject to German capital-maintenance rules, insolvency restrictions, the mandatory UG statutory reserve, withholding-tax procedures, and any applicable treaty or European Union relief. Dividend payments do not replace documentary requirements for related-party loans, service fees, royalties, or other outbound transfers, which must be commercially substantiated and arm's-length.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Germany has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion, subject to bank compliance controls, accounting in euros for German statutory financial reporting, applicable VAT and customs evidence, and accurate tax records. German residents may be required to report certain cross-border payments or positions for statistical purposes under applicable Bundesbank reporting rules; reporting thresholds, forms, and exemptions must be checked for the relevant transaction date and payment category. Exporters should retain contracts, invoices, transport documents, customs records, payment confirmations, and foreign-exchange confirmations.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the oversight of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange, prudential, payment-services, and anti-money-laundering supervision may also involve the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium for payment operations and low-to-medium as a complete substitute for a German relationship bank. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA services, cards, foreign-exchange conversion, and certain international transfers under passporting or local authorization. It does not generally provide a German bank deposit account, statutory capital-account functionality, deposit insurance equivalent to a bank deposit, full documentary trade-finance capability, or unrestricted commercial-credit facilities. Offshore digital EMIs without applicable European Economic Area authorization or lawful passporting are unsuitable for regulated payment services into Germany. The UG must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, client-money segregation, sanctions controls, transaction limits, supported currencies, account-ownership name matching, data-access arrangements, and insolvency protection. A German or European bank account should remain the primary account for share-capital funding, tax payments, payroll, customs payments, and high-value supplier settlements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-German or layered ownership, nominee shareholders, trusts, foundations, or inability to identify the natural-person ultimate beneficial owner.
        
Root cause: Non-German or layered ownership, nominee shareholders, trusts, foundations, or inability to identify the natural-person ultimate beneficial owner.

        
Defense evidence: Certified or bank-acceptable constitutional documents for every ownership layer; current commercial-register extracts; shareholder registers; ownership and control chart; passports and proof of address for all beneficial owners and directors; beneficial-owner declaration; trust or foundation instruments where applicable; explanation of voting rights and control rights; tax-residency information; and legalized translations where requested.

      

      
      

        Newly incorporated UG with limited paid-in capital, no operating history, or projected cross-border volumes disproportionate to its capital and business plan.
        
Root cause: Newly incorporated UG with limited paid-in capital, no operating history, or projected cross-border volumes disproportionate to its capital and business plan.

        
Defense evidence: Notarial formation deed; articles of association; commercial-register extract or registration confirmation; shareholder resolution; capital contribution evidence; three-year business plan; rolling twelve-month cash-flow forecast; customer and supplier pipeline; signed purchase orders; distribution or OEM agreements; logistics model; expected countries, currencies, monthly volumes, counterparties, and payment purposes.

      

      
      

        Foreign shareholder funding that is not clearly classified as share capital, shareholder loan, intercompany funding, or a third-party payment.
        
Root cause: Foreign shareholder funding that is not clearly classified as share capital, shareholder loan, intercompany funding, or a third-party payment.

        
Defense evidence: Executed capital-contribution or loan agreement; shareholder resolution; funding-source statement; bank statements tracing funds to the beneficial owner or group treasury; loan terms and repayment schedule; transfer-pricing support for related-party debt; accounting treatment memorandum; payment reference identifying the purpose; and confirmation that the remitter, contracting party, and beneficial owner are consistent.

      

      
      

        Payments involving countries, banks, counterparties, intermediaries, or products subject to sanctions, export controls, dual-use restrictions, or heightened corruption risk.
        
Root cause: Payments involving countries, banks, counterparties, intermediaries, or products subject to sanctions, export controls, dual-use restrictions, or heightened corruption risk.

        
Defense evidence: Sanctions-screening results for all relevant parties; end-use and end-user statement; product classification; export-control analysis; customs and shipping documents; beneficial-ownership verification of counterparties; compliance approvals; contractual sanctions clauses; screening date and methodology; and evidence that no prohibited party, vessel, bank, or jurisdiction is involved.

      

      
      

        Use of virtual offices, mailbox addresses, residential registered offices, shared premises, or an unclear German operating footprint.
        
Root cause: Use of virtual offices, mailbox addresses, residential registered offices, shared premises, or an unclear German operating footprint.

        
Defense evidence: Commercial-lease or serviced-office agreement; proof of actual management and operations; warehouse or fulfillment agreement; employee or contractor evidence; website and product documentation; supplier and customer contracts; telephone and domain ownership evidence; explanation of remote-management arrangements; and confirmation of the company's principal place of business.

      

      
      

        Wearable-health-product risk, including possible medical-device claims, personal-health data processing, or unexplained product classification.
        
Root cause: Wearable-health-product risk, including possible medical-device claims, personal-health data processing, or unexplained product classification.

        
Defense evidence: Product catalogue; technical specifications; intended-use statement; determination whether products are consumer electronics or regulated medical devices; CE and conformity documentation where applicable; distributor and manufacturer agreements; privacy and data-processing documentation; insurance evidence; recall and quality-control procedures; and confirmation that payment activity corresponds to lawful goods and services.

      

      
      

        Unusual cash activity, third-party payments, rapid pass-through transactions, crypto exposure, cash-intensive sales, or unexplained round-value transfers.
        
Root cause: Unusual cash activity, third-party payments, rapid pass-through transactions, crypto exposure, cash-intensive sales, or unexplained round-value transfers.

        
Defense evidence: Treasury policy; approved counterparty list; invoice-to-payment reconciliation; source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence; transaction-monitoring rules; prohibition or controls for third-party payments; crypto-asset risk assessment; cash-handling policy; and documented escalation and suspicious-activity reporting procedures.

      

      
      

        Incomplete tax-residency, CRS, FATCA, or German beneficial-owner-register information.
        
Root cause: Incomplete tax-residency, CRS, FATCA, or German beneficial-owner-register information.

        
Defense evidence: German tax number and VAT identification number when available; CRS self-certifications; FATCA classification; tax-residency certificates; beneficial-owner registration evidence in the German Transparency Register where applicable; managing-director declarations; and consistency checks across bank, tax, corporate, and accounting records.

      

      
      

        Expected use of documentary trade finance without sufficient transaction history or evidence of genuine underlying trade.
        
Root cause: Expected use of documentary trade finance without sufficient transaction history or evidence of genuine underlying trade.

        
Defense evidence: Purchase and sales contracts; pro forma and commercial invoices; bills of lading or airway bills; packing lists; customs declarations; certificates of origin; insurance documents; inspection certificates; counterparty due diligence; Incoterms; payment terms; and a transaction flow diagram linking goods, funds, documents, and beneficial owners.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Use a bank account held in the exact legal name of the UG. For initial share capital, obtain the bank's capital-contribution account or equivalent formation-account instructions, remit from an account held by the shareholder or documented group treasury entity, and state a precise payment reference such as 'Einlage Stammkapital gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag' or the applicable shareholder-loan purpose. The bank should be supplied with the formation deed, shareholder resolution, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, and funding agreement before or immediately after the transfer. For cross-border euro transfers, use SEPA Credit Transfer where available; for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions, use SWIFT with complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, purpose, and beneficial-owner data. Reconcile the incoming transfer to the general ledger and retain MT103 or equivalent payment confirmation, bank credit advice, and corporate documentation. Do not mix share capital, shareholder loans, customer receipts, and third-party funds without separate accounting and documentary classification. For material cross-border payments, assess Bundesbank statistical reporting obligations and applicable German tax, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, and interest-limitation consequences.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro supplier payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly evidenced by an MT103 or successor ISO 20022 payment message, for international payments. Payment instructions should include accurate originator and beneficiary information, invoice number, purchase-order reference, goods description, Incoterms, currency, and purpose. For higher-risk or higher-value shipments, use a documentary commercial letter of credit, preferably an irrevocable and, where appropriate, confirmed LC subject to the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600). Banks may also offer documentary collections under URC 522, bank guarantees, standby letters of credit, and supply-chain finance. Match invoices, customs declarations, transport documents, sanctions screening, export-control classification, and proof of delivery. Apply maker-checker controls, dual authorization, beneficiary-name verification, callback controls for amended bank details, segregation of duties, payment limits, and daily sanctions screening. Avoid unsupported third-party settlement, split payments designed to evade controls, and payment routing through unrelated jurisdictions.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards entered into with an authorized bank or investment firm for documented commercial exposures, subject to counterparty limits, confirmations, valuation, collateral terms, accounting designation, and applicable European Market Infrastructure Regulation (EMIR) obligations.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies or jurisdictions where physical delivery is restricted or impractical, provided the instrument is offered lawfully by an authorized counterparty and the UG documents the underlying exposure, settlement currency, valuation, sanctions position, and regulatory classification.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for matching longer-term foreign-currency receipts, payables, shareholder funding, or intercompany debt, subject to appropriate documentation, credit support, collateral, valuation, and EMIR reporting or clearing requirements where applicable.Foreign-exchange options, collars, and option-based hedges for budget protection, subject to suitability, product-governance, premium, termination, market-value, and corporate-authority controls. The UG should avoid speculative trading and maintain a board-approved treasury policy.Natural hedging through currency matching of supplier liabilities, customer receipts, inventory purchases, and intercompany balances; this is generally the lowest-complexity method but must be supported by documented forecasting and exposure measurement.Forwards, swaps, and options may constitute financial instruments under MiFID II. The bank must classify the UG, apply applicable appropriateness or suitability processes, and provide contractual disclosures. The UG should maintain ISDA or equivalent master agreements where appropriate, perform counterparty credit assessments, record hedge effectiveness, and review accounting under the applicable German statutory or IFRS framework.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment operations and low-to-medium as a complete substitute for a German relationship bank. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA services, cards, foreign-exchange conversion, and certain international transfers under passporting or local authorization. It does not generally provide a German bank deposit account, statutory capital-account functionality, deposit insurance equivalent to a bank deposit, full documentary trade-finance capability, or unrestricted commercial-credit facilities. Offshore digital EMIs without applicable European Economic Area authorization or lawful passporting are unsuitable for regulated payment services into Germany. The UG must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, client-money segregation, sanctions controls, transaction limits, supported currencies, account-ownership name matching, data-access arrangements, and insolvency protection. A German or European bank account should remain the primary account for share-capital funding, tax payments, payroll, customs payments, and high-value supplier settlements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), producing a statutory national income tax burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is applied separately at 3.5% of the trade income base multiplied by the municipal assessment rate (Hebesatz, generally 200%–490%), yielding an additional 7.0%–17.15%; the combined effective marginal burden is approximately 29.8%–33.4% depending on the municipality.
