Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for the UG, but nominal share capital must be at least EUR 1.00 and below EUR 25,000.00. The full subscribed capital must be paid up in cash before the registration application is filed. Cash contributions must be credited to a German business bank account in the company's name and confirmed by the bank as being at the free disposal of the managing directors (§8(1) No.3 GmbHG). No statutory escrow account is mandated. After registration, the UG must annually retain 25% of net profits in a capital reserve until the nominal capital reaches EUR 25,000.00 (§5a(3) GmbHG).

Funding & unlock path: Before utilizing any office, warehouse or dispatch facility, the operator must perform a workplace risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung), register employees with the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft), and ensure fire protection per state building regulations: approved fire extinguishers, unobstructed escape routes, and structural load certification from the landlord or building-control inspector where required. A separate civil defense clearance is not applicable to a private commercial mail-order unit.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt; Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT identification number issuance (USt-IdNr.) and EU/VIES cross-border clearance; local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) at the UG's registered seat for Steuernummer, corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT (Umsatzsteuer) advance and annual returns, and wage tax; national electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); customs portal: Generalzolldirektion for EORI and export declarations.

E-government filing portal

The UG is formed by notarial deed and registered in the Handelsregister (commercial register) of the local Amtsgericht. Within one month of formation or commencement of activity, the Geschäftsführer (managing director) must file the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER (Section 138 AO), declaring the intended business scope (mail-order retail, ISIC 4791) and estimated turnover. The responsible Finanzamt then allocates the Steuernummer (tax number). For cross-border supplies, the UG must separately apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. (VAT ID; Form USt 1 C via the BZSt online portal) and register with the Generalzolldirektion for an EORI number for goods exports. Foreign-resident owners and managers may file via ELSTER without a German fiscal representative; a customs agent (Zollagent) may be appointed for physical export formalities. Non-resident ownership is fully permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany applies statutory legal-form classification and has no US-style check-the-box election. A UG is per se a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) taxed at entity level on corporate income, trade tax and VAT without transparency. Available statutory elections relevant to a UG: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax (Sections 14, 17 KStG) and VAT (Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG), requiring a profit-pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and at least 75% shareholding; (ii) the Kleinunternehmer (small-enterprise) VAT exemption under Section 19 UStG; (iii) the Section 1a KStG option to be taxed as a corporation is not available to a UG because it is reserved for partnerships; (iv) ATAD hybrid-mismatch disallowance rules under Section 4k EStG apply mandatorily and cannot be elected away.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted euro convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and EU internal-market treaty provisions. Current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalized; there are no foreign-exchange controls, no prior administrative approvals for capital movements, and no limitations on dividend or profit remittance by a German Unternehmergesellschaft (UG) to non-resident shareholders. Remittances are subject only to standard corporate law formalities, tax withholding requirements, and statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank; no permit or explicit central-bank approval is required for profit transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in Germany. Export receipts may be retained freely in EUR or any convertible foreign currency, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. However, the exporter must report individual cross-border payments and receipts to the Deutsche Bundesbank for external statistics purposes under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) when the transaction value exceeds EUR 12,500 or equivalent; this is a statistical and monitoring notification, not a surrender or exchange-control measure. Failure to file may lead to fines but does not block settlement or remittance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry of Finance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability for auxiliary treasury rails. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized under PSD2 may provide commercial accounts to German legal entities by passporting; such accounts are feasible for low-value cross-border disbursement and payment collection, particularly with providers like Airwallex, but they are not subject to German deposit insurance and are generally unsuitable as a sole settlement account. Offshore, non-EU EMIs without BaFin-issued or euro-area passport licenses are rated low viability: they cannot open a regulated account in Germany and may face severe correspondent-banking restrictions, operational unreliability, and compliance ambiguity. Optimal structure is a domestic German bank account as treasury anchor, supplemented by a licensed EMI rail for emerging-market COD payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections into the German UG must be executed via bank-to-bank wire, never in cash. For EU/SEPA transfers, use SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR; for non-EU or foreign-currency injections, use SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary name and UG's IBAN. The underlying purpose must be stated on the wire and matched to the Deutsche Bundesbank external-statistics classification: direct-investment capital contribution or shareholder loan, with the proper AWV purpose code. The UG must file the applicable balance-of-payments notification with the Bundesbank within the statutory reporting deadline, attaching the shareholder resolution, the loan agreement if any, a declaration of source of funds, and the updated share register/transparency-register confirmation. For foreign-currency conversions, the conversion should be made through the German bank's on-shore FX desk to establish clean audit trail.For operational outflows, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments and SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-wide settlements. For high-value trade settlements, commercial letters of credit (standby and documentary, issued subject to UCP 600 and confirmed by the German bank or a top-tier emerging-market bank) provide settlement security and comply with trade-compliance screening. For cash-on-delivery logistics disbursements in emerging markets, use regulated EMI rails such as Airwallex local-currency payout to local logistics partners, with daily batch files and supporting settlement reports. The UG should always maintain a clear remittance record: invoice number, tracking number, shipping route, and clearing reference. Trade tax assessments subject to 0.5% monthly interest; late-filing penalties and enforcement surcharges as under corporate income tax rules.
Box 4 Trade tax liability Calculate liability as tax base multiplied by 3.5% basic rate and municipal multiplier; payable on assessment. Trade tax assessments subject to 0.5% monthly interest; late-filing penalties and enforcement surcharges as under corporate income tax rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 6,000,000; Master File: group with consolidated revenues exceeding EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country Report threshold is group revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: No standalone ESR statute in Germany, but the substance-over-form principle and anti-abuse rules under section 42 AO and section 50d(3) EStG apply. For UG to claim financing and IP structures, it must show actual offices, personnel, decision-making, risk assumption, and economic activity in Germany; otherwise Tax authorities may reattribute income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases; unlimited monetary fines; additional interest of 0.5% per month on evaded tax; criminal confiscation of economic benefit.

Director personal liability: Managing director and de facto directors personally liable as perpetrators or participants; civil liability for corporate tax and surcharges; travel bans, arrest, and seizure of personal assets in criminal proceedings; corporate veil pierced for fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of income under section 42 AO; denial of DTA and EU Directive benefits; retroactive tax assessments, interest and penalty surcharges up to 25% of understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders jointly and severally liable for tax arrears if assets are transferred out or entity is undercapitalised; forfeiture of limited liability protection; personal liability for intentional misuse.
Transfer Pricing Violation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment based on adverse estimation; penal surcharge of 10% on adjusted income (minimum EUR 10,000; maximum EUR 250,000); taxable interest at 6% per year; criminal proceedings for obstruction when documentation is withheld.

Director personal liability: Director responsible for transfer pricing documentation; personal surcharge liability if documents are destroyed or false; risk of criminal sentence for aiding tax evasion; tort liability for group understatement.
Withholding Tax Evasion (Dividend / Royalty)

Statutory sanction: Tax liability imposed on the debtor under section 50a EStG; security payments demanded; late-interest at 0.5% per month; administrative penalty up to EUR 25,000; criminal fines where intentional.

Director personal liability: Managing director as the responsible person accountable for WHT obligation; personal civil liability to remit unpaid WHT; seizure of personal assets and restriction of business travel; possible extradition double criminality.
Money Laundering / AML Violation (Cash-on-Delivery)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million for ordinary administrative breaches; up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover for severe institutional breaches; imprisonment up to 2 years for negligent facilitation and up to 5 years for intentional money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML officers exposed to criminal liability for breaching GwG duties; individual fines and confiscation of illicit cash flows; professional disqualification; personal insolvency risk due to non-dischargeable monetary judgments.
