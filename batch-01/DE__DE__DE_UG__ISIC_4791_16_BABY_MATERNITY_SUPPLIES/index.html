Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital; UG may incorporate with a subscribed capital of at least EUR 1, but 25% of annual net profit must be retained as a statutory reserve until cumulative capital reaches EUR 25,000. The subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash and proven to be at the free disposal of the managing directors by a bank confirmation before registration.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for pure office and online sales activity. If the company operates a warehouse or fulfillment space, mandatory work safety compliance under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) applies, including fire extinguishers, escape routes, sufficient lighting, and structural load certification at light storage levels. Local fire brigade approval is required for storage areas above 200 m² or where hazardous goods are handled; the UG has no such handling and may rely on standard workplace certifications.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital; UG may incorporate with a subscribed capital of at least EUR 1, but 25% of annual net profit must be retained as a statutory reserve until cumulative capital reaches EUR 25,000. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both capital and current accounts pursuant to the Foreign Exchange Act (AuWGe). Profit remittances to foreign shareholders encounter no capital account restrictions for EU-resident entities; only standard AML/KYC verification and tax reporting obligations apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds is required for current-account transactions, including intra-EU trade. Receipts must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes within 30 days; no statutory conversion ratios or timelines apply.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% flat corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of typically 14% to 17%; aggregate statutory cash tax burden approximately 29% to 32%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% statutory dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge). Church tax may additionally apply when the shareholder is a qualifying individual subject to church tax; corporate shareholders are not subject to church tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending 31 December, the annual corporate income tax return is due by 31 July of the following calendar year. If prepared by a tax advisor, the deadline is automatically extended to 28 February (or 29 February) of the second calendar year after the tax year. All returns must be submitted electronically.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same as CIT: due by 31 July following the tax year, extended to 28 February/29 February of the second subsequent year when a tax advisor prepares the return.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual VAT return due by 31 July following the tax year (extended to 28 February/29 February with tax assistance). Advance returns are due by the 10th day of the month following each period. e-commerce sellers using marketplaces may be subject to marketplace VAT collection rules.

Late payment interest of 0.5% per month. Failure to file may result in an estimated VAT assessment and penalty proceedings.

Non-resident UG can recover German input VAT through a VAT refund procedure (§ 18 Abs. 9 UStG) if no German PE, or via VAT return if a German PE exists. Late filing surcharge of 1% of the VAT amount per month, capped at €25,000 for annual returns; separate surcharges for advance returns up to €5,000 per notification. Late payment interest of 0.5% per month. Failure to file may result in an estimated VAT assessment and penalty proceedings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany does not impose a fixed euro threshold for mandatory Local File/Master File preparation. A CbC Report is mandatory where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds €750 million. All German entities and German PEs of non-resident entities must maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for material intercompany transactions and produce it within 60 days of request (30 days for extraordinary transactions); article 162(4) AO penalty applies when documentation is not supplied and the tax authority makes a resulting adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the economic-substance and general anti-abuse doctrine under § 42 AO. An UG used as a pass-through shell without genuine business purpose is disregarded for tax purposes. The place of effective management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) determines German tax residence; a non-resident e-commerce operator with a substantial German PE must maintain real presence (office, employees, contracts, bank account, decision-making). Germany also has domestic controlled-foreign-company rules (§ 7-14 AStG) and follows BEPS substance requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO, imprisonment up to five years for simple tax evasion; up to ten years in particularly serious cases (large-scale evasion). Monetary fines are calculated per § 369 AO and can reach the amount evaded; additionally, the full evaded tax plus interest at 0.5% monthly is collected. Tax concealment itself triggers an additional 15% surcharge under § 378 AO if unintentional.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable for unpaid taxes if he fails to pay from company funds (§ 69 AO). In tax evasion, the director is the criminal subject; imprisonment sentence attaches in person. Civil liability for the full tax loss arises under § 823 BGB and § 826 BGB if intentional. German courts may also order a travel ban and seizure of passports where flight risk is present.
Shell Company and Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will disregard the entity under § 42 AO, reallocate income to its ultimate beneficial owner, impose penalty assessments, and proceed to collection. Fines up to €25,000 for administrative registration violations under § 79a AO; criminal penalties for Evasion or dishonest declarations may be imposed.

Director personal liability: If substance is demonstrably absent, courts may pierce the corporate veil under German corporate law and § 826 BGB, making shareholders and directors personally liable for corporate debts, including tax liabilities. Directors who run such a shell without proper board minuting risk personal detention for unpaid tax arrears and are also exposed to travel bans in the course of criminal tax investigations.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's-Length Violation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate taxable income and impose a penalty under § 162 (4) AO of 5% of the adjusted profit, minimum €5,000 and maximum €1,000,000. Interest on underpaid tax accrues at 12% per year (0.5% monthly) from 15 months after the tax period. Additional fines can be ordered in a criminal context if evasion is proven.

Director personal liability: The managing director has a statutory duty to ensure correct accounting and tax reporting. Failure to document transfer prices personally exposes the director to fines and liability for penalties (§ 34 AO). In severe cases of intentional understatement of profits to non-resident affiliates, the director may be prosecuted for tax evasion, punishable with imprisonment, and the corporate veil may be pierced to recover unpaid corporate taxes.
AML/CFT Violations and Payment Processing Abuse

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) can trigger administrative fines up to €100,000 for minor breaches and up to €1 million for severe breaches. In a criminal case, money laundering under § 261 StGB carries imprisonment of three months to five years; commercial money laundering can carry one to ten years. Authorities can also confiscate funds from merchant accounts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under § 13 GwG for implementing AML controls; non-compliance leads to personal fines. Money laundering conviction brings imprisonment and a ban from company management positions. Travel bans are commonly imposed before trial; after conviction, the director is personally obliged to pay confiscation amounts and cannot hide behind the UG's corporate personality.
