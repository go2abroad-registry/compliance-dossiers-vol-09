Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht), Abteilung B, via the electronic Justice Service Portal of the respective German federal state.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG), primarily §§1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 35, 39, and 40; complemented by notarial law (Beurkundungsgesetz) and trade registration provisions (§14 GewO).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) together with the electronic Application for Registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The GmbH requires statutory minimum share capital of €25,000; at least €12,500 must be paid in cash and made irrevocably available to the managing directors before registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked or trust account opened in the company's name, and the bank must confirm free disposal for registration purposes.

Funding & unlock path: No factory clearance required. For the registered office, the local building authority may require a basic fire safety inspection for commercial premises; the company must maintain workplace safety in line with the Occupational Health and Safety Act (ArbSchG) and DGUV accident prevention regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: The GmbH requires statutory minimum share capital of €25,000; at least €12,500 must be paid in cash and made irrevocably available to the managing directors before registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked or trust account opened in the company's name, and the bank must confirm free disposal for registration purposes.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – no industrial manufacturing; the relevant registration authority is the municipal Trade and Order Office (Gewerbeamt) under §14 GewO for professional regulatory consulting services.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) and cross-border VAT administration; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT returns. Electronic filings are generally made through the ELSTER platform; certain federal electronic invoicing processes use the Zentrale Rechnungseingangsplattform des Bundes (ZRE) or Onlinezugangsgesetz-Rechnung (OZG-RE).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate and register the GmbH with the commercial register and obtain the business registration confirmation where applicable. 2. Submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt, identifying the registered office, business activities, expected turnover, VAT status, bank account, managing directors, and related-party information. 3. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), which is used for corporate income tax, trade tax, and domestic VAT compliance. 4. Apply separately to the BZSt for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), normally through the tax registration process or the BZSt application service; the number is required for qualifying intra-EU supplies, acquisitions, and cross-border services. 5. Register for VAT returns and advance returns through ELSTER and maintain German accounting and invoicing records. 6. A non-established foreign entity making German taxable supplies must generally register with the competent Finanzamt unless the supplies are fully covered by the domestic reverse-charge mechanism or another statutory exception. 7. The German Economic Identification Number (Wirtschafts-Identifikationsnummer, W-IdNr.) is being introduced by the BZSt on a phased basis and does not replace the Steuernummer or USt-IdNr. for all operational purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A GmbH is a capital company subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and solidarity surcharge at entity level and to municipal trade tax; it has no US-style check-the-box election. German partnerships may elect corporate-income-tax treatment under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, but that election is generally unavailable to a GmbH. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD restrict deductions, exemption outcomes, or dual inclusion treatment for specified hybrid arrangements under the German Foreign Tax Act (AStG).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is generally no turnover threshold that permits a taxable GmbH to avoid VAT registration when it conducts taxable German supplies. The German small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) may apply if prior-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and effective-date rules. A business using the exemption does not charge VAT and generally cannot deduct input VAT. The exemption is not a general exemption from obtaining a tax number or from other registration and record-keeping duties. Foreign businesses may have a VAT registration obligation from the first relevant taxable German supply unless reverse charge, OSS, import VAT, or another exception applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU may qualify as VAT-exempt with input-tax deduction (steuerfreie Ausfuhrlieferung) if the goods are transported or dispatched to the non-EU destination, the purchaser is a non-resident of the relevant German VAT territory where required, and the supplier proves the export and fulfillment of the statutory conditions through customs and commercial records. Supplies to another EU Member State are generally intra-Community supplies rather than exports and require valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative statement compliance, and other statutory conditions. For regulatory consulting and other services, the place-of-supply rules apply: B2B services are generally located at the customer’s establishment under the general rule and may be outside German VAT, while B2C services generally follow separate rules. An offshore service is not automatically a 0% export supply; the supplier must apply the applicable place-of-supply and exemption rules. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, tax records, and proof that the related transactions support the right to deduct input VAT.

  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the applicable customs system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs exit evidence.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list where applicable, transport contract, and transport evidence such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier confirmation.
  • Evidence linking the goods, supplier, purchaser, customs declaration, and destination outside the EU, including purchase order and delivery records where relevant.
  • Proof of delivery or receipt at the non-EU destination where required by the specific export structure.
  • VAT invoice containing the applicable exemption reference and required invoice particulars.
  • For intra-Community supplies instead of exports: valid customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and timely and accurate recapitulative statement.
  • An inward remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory German VAT export document. Payment evidence may support commercial substantiation but does not replace customs export or transport proof.

Germany has no general real-time VAT invoice clearance or continuous transaction control system. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices is phased: during the transitional period, paper and ordinary PDF invoices remain permitted subject to the statutory conditions; from 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year domestic turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 generally must issue structured e-invoices for qualifying domestic B2B supplies; from 1 January 2028, the structured e-invoice obligation is generally extended to all qualifying domestic B2B transactions. The invoice must comply with the European EN 16931 data model, commonly through XRechnung or an acceptable ZUGFeRD format. Federal B2G invoicing is subject to separate mandatory electronic submission requirements through ZRE or OZG-RE, with additional state and municipal rules possible. Electronic invoice retention, authenticity, integrity, readability, and audit-access requirements apply; no digital tax stamp is generally required.

Germany has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for export proceeds. Export exemption is vulnerable to assessment if the supplier cannot substantiate transport, export, purchaser status, destination, or invoice-chain requirements. The Finanzamt may deny or retrospectively reverse the exemption, assess the German VAT that should have been charged, reclaim improperly deducted input VAT, impose late-payment interest under the German Fiscal Code, and issue surcharge or correction assessments. Intentional or reckless false documentation, sham exports, or fraudulent VAT claims may constitute tax evasion or related tax offences; tax evasion is generally punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, with aggravated cases punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years. Administrative fines, customs penalties, repayment of unlawfully obtained refunds, and audit-related record-production obligations may also apply. No clawback is avoided merely because payment was received in a foreign currency or routed through a foreign bank.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under EU free movement of capital (Articles 63-66 TFEU). The euro is freely convertible; no exchange controls limiting import/export of funds exist for a German GmbH. Profit remittances of a GmbH are not quantitatively restricted, subject only to standard corporate tax, trade tax, and dividend withholding tax, if applicable. Non-resident shareholders may repatriate capital, dividends, and loan repayments freely. The only material hurdle is the Bundesbank's cross-border payment reporting threshold (EUR 12,500 or equivalent) and full compliance with EU sanctions and AML legislation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists in Germany for export proceeds received by a GmbH. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted to EUR at the company's discretion. The mandatory Bundesbank statistical duty under § 10 AWV requires every German resident receiving payment from non-residents in excess of EUR 12,500 to report the receivable to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the 7th business day of the month following receipt. The bank normally files the report, but the account holder must supply the correct purpose code and documentation to prevent penalties.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, in coordination with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), enforcing the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), and EU Treaty freedoms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low for primary treasury, Low for statutory capital escrow. A German GmbH may lawfully hold funds with an EU-authorized e-money institution (EMI) or payment institution under the PSD2 passporting regime. Such EMI accounts are operationally viable for low-volume cross-border fee disbursements and vendor payments, provided the EMI holds a BaFin-licensed German branch or EU license with German passported status. Legal constraints: the EMI must not offer credit, does not have access to Bundesbank's Target2 directly, and is not a credit institution under the German Banking Act (KWG), so the EMI account cannot serve as the special registered capital account (Festkapitalkonto) required by German GmbH law. For statutory capital escrow, a full KREDITINSTITUT is required. Offshore digital EMIs outside EEA are generally unacceptable for corporate statutory records and may create permanent establishment risk; only EU-authorized entities with exactly matching AML/KYC/CDD are permissible for ancillary collection flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A shareholder capital contribution for the GmbH must be transferred via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank account to the GmbH's Deutsche Bank/Commerzbank deposit account. The payment detail should state: 'Kapitalzahlung auf Stammeinlage Nr. [X] laut Gesellschaftsvertrag vom [date]'. The MT103 field 70 (Remittance Information) should include the purpose code required by the beneficiary bank for the Bundesbank 'Zahlungsbilanzstatistik', typically 'Direct Investment-Inflow (Kapitalverkehr)'. The capital escrow account must be blocked until the managing director releases it after entry in Commercial Register. The bank will verify the payment origin under AML law and file the § 10 AWV balance-of-payments report. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (0.825%) + Gewerbesteuer (municipal rate; combined effective rate approx. 30%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, resulting in 26.375% all-in statutory rate; treaty reduced rates (typically 0%, 5%, or 15%) apply only to the 25% base and only if the recipient is the beneficial owner and has substance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 per cross-border controlled transaction type/counterparty triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File; Master File is expected for multinational groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated group revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not use a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations label, but substance is mandatory under the general anti-abuse rule (§ 42 AO), § 50d sec. 3 EStG, BEPS MLI and EU ATAD. A GmbH engaged in FDA 510(k) and CE Mark regulatory consulting must demonstrate real place of management, employees, signed client contracts, internal project files and substantive decision-making in Germany; without this, treaty/tax benefits can be denied and the company may be disregarded as a conduit or shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Under-Declaration of German-Source MedTech Consulting Fees

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under § 370 AO: imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, aggravated cases up to 10 years; evaded taxes are collected with 6% annual late interest; tax authorities may estimate income to the maximum credible level and impose a late-filing penalty up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under § 69 AO for the unpaid corporate income tax and trade tax caused by culpable failure to file and pay; criminal prosecution can lead to seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment, and the corporate veil is pierced where knowing concealment is shown.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief under § 50d sec. 3 EStG and the GAAR under § 42 AO; all profits and fees are reattributed to the offshore beneficial owner; German trade tax and corporate income tax are re-assessed with 6% interest; false substance declarations may be treated as tax fraud; commercial register strike-off proceedings can be initiated.

Director personal liability: If the GmbH is a sham, directors cannot rely on limited liability; they are jointly and severally liable for all corporate taxes and third-party debts (Durchgriffshaftung). Directors may face travel bans in cross-border shell investigations and personal criminal conviction for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Mis-Priced Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under § 162 AO plus a 5% documentation surcharge on additional taxable income (minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 500,000); late or defective documentation can trigger additional penalties up to EUR 1,000,000 or EUR 100 per day; interest at 6% per annum applies to the resulting tax deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or intentionally incomplete transfer pricing documentation commit tax evasion and are personally liable under criminal and fiscal law; civil liability under § 43 GmbHG for breach of director duties allows shareholders to claim restitution; court orders may impose travel bans in international investigations.
Withholding Tax / Dividend Repatriation Violations

Statutory sanction: The company remains liable for the withheld tax under § 73 AO; tax authorities collect the unpaid withholding amount and impose a late payment surcharge of 1% per month; penalties up to EUR 25,000 may be assessed, and the payments are reclassified as dividends or royalties with full withholding and income adjustment.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is the statutory withholding agent under § 34 AO; if he or she knowingly distributes net payments without withholding, personal liability under § 69 AO attaches; criminal conviction under § 370 AO may include imprisonment, asset seizure and travel bans.
AML / Sanctions / Transparenzregister Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under GwG up to EUR 160,000 for negligent violations and up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of total annual turnover for intentional severe breaches; Transparenzregister omissions incur separate daily fines; money laundering under § 261 StGB is punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for establishing and operating AML controls; they may receive individual fines, be disqualified from office, and face criminal prosecution for money laundering or sanctions evasion; travel bans and cross-border freeze orders are increasingly imposed in German corporate enforcement cases.
