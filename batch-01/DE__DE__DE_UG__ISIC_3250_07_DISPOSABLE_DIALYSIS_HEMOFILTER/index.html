Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID and cross-border operations; local Finanzamt (local tax office) for corporate income tax and trade tax; unified ELSTER (ELektronischer STEUER-ERklärung) online portal

E-government filing portal

A non-resident establishing a UG must file a commercial register application with the local Amtsgericht and apply for a Steuernummer by submitting an extensive tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt. The Steuernummer is issued for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. After registration, the UG may separately apply to the BZSt for a legal-entity VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), which is required for EU B2B transactions and for claiming export or intra-Community VAT exemptions. Non-resident foreign companies without a permanent establishment performing taxable transactions in Germany must register through the BZSt or a local Finanzamt using the simplified non-resident procedure, providing evidence of legal existence, VAT status in their home jurisdiction, and German or EU-based bank details.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime. A UG is default-resident for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), with no pass-through election. The principal statutory elections are the corporate income tax organization (Organschaft) and the VAT fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft), allowing a consortium of entities to be treated as a single taxable person. German tax law also recognizes limited hybrid mismatch elections for EU/EEA entities pursuant to anti-hybrid rules implemented by the ATAD Implementation Act (the AbzStEntModG), mainly for the classification of certain disregarded payments, but no general entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a resident UG: no mandatory registration threshold, but the small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) applies only when annual turnover does not exceed €25,000 in the preceding calendar year and is expected to remain below €100,000 in the current year (2025 onwards). For a non-resident supplier: mandatory German VAT registration arises as soon as taxable supplies are made in Germany, irrespective of turnover volume, unless the reverse-charge procedure transfers the tax liability to the German buyer or an optional OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme applies to distance sales and certain B2C services. Intra-Community distance sales to Germany become taxable in Germany once the customer’s location thresholds are exceeded, generally at €100,000, unless the seller has exercised an OSS election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to direct exports of tangible goods from Germany to countries outside the EU/for export territories if the supplier furnishes verifiable evidence that the goods have physically left the EU within specific timeframes (typically three months, extendable under certain conditions). The supplier must be registered in Germany, must issue a commercial invoice, and must retain the customs export declaration (Ausfuhrnachweis) issued by the customs authority. Certain offshore supplies and supplies deemed as exports for specific maritime/aircraft use also qualify. Intra-Community supplies of dialysis consumables to another EU taxable person are not zero-rated but are exempt with credit for input VAT, subject to the purchaser’s valid VAT ID and the EU Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung). For a UG manufacturing hemodialyzers in Germany, hands-on proof of physical export is indispensable for zero-rating; use of a third-country warehouse or deemed-supply structures triggers different VAT rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrnachweis / Exportbegleitdokument)
  • Commercial Invoice / Proforma Invoice
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB)
  • Packing List
  • Export contracts and purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof / SWIFT confirmation of export proceeds

Germany has mandated B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic taxable supplies from 1 January 2025. Suppliers must issue invoices in a structured electronic format that is machine-readable, notably XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, and transmit them through commercial EDI/API channels or the ELSTER portal for state submission. There is no real-time tax administration clearance system, no digital stamping, and no continuous transaction control (CTC) mechanism analogous to Italy or Spain. Receipt of e-invoices by a German taxable person is mandatory, though transitional periods apply until 2027/2028 for smaller suppliers. Export invoices may still be issued in paper/PDF if they meet documentary proof requirements but the 2025 mandate extends to export transactions only where domestic B2B invoicing is involved; pure export VAT zero-rating requires the underlying customs documentation rather than e-invoice clearance.

Export zero-rating is subject to intensive audit scrutiny by the Finanzamt and Hauptzollamt. If the required documentary proof is fabricated, incomplete, or inconsistent, the VAT exemption is revoked and the full 19% (reduced rates where applicable) VAT is retrospectively assessed against the supplier, plus late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) under §233a AO. Fraudulent zero-rating, e.g. illusory exports, circular transactions, or forged export certificates, is a criminal tax evasion offense under §370 AO and can lead to imprisonment of up to five years, additional fines, and confiscation of profits; in aggravated cases, imprisonment up to ten years is possible. The UG as a corporate entity also faces loss of trade tax and corporate tax deductions, and the managing director may be personally liable under §71 AO for VAT deficiencies and penalties arising from fraudulent export claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany does not generally impose exchange controls, mandatory repatriation of foreign currency, restrictions on dividend or profit remittances, or a general requirement to convert export proceeds into euros. Outbound dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, loan repayments, and capital returns remain subject to German corporate, withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, substance, beneficial-ownership, sanctions, and anti-abuse rules. Bank execution may require corporate approvals, invoices or agreements, tax documentation, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, and confirmation that the transaction is not subject to sanctions or financial-crime restrictions. Cross-border payments may be reportable under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), generally using Bundesbank reporting procedures and applicable threshold exemptions. Payments involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or regulated medical-device trade may require separate sanctions or export-control analysis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or transferred to Germany. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, banking, and contractual requirements. Cross-border payment reporting under the AWV may apply when the relevant reporting threshold is exceeded, subject to statutory exemptions and current Bundesbank rules; such reporting is statistical and does not constitute a currency-conversion obligation. Trade-statistics reporting, VAT evidence, customs documentation, and receivables accounting remain separate obligations. The company should confirm current thresholds, filing channels, and exemptions with the Bundesbank before implementation because reporting rules can change.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank; banking and payment-service supervision is principally administered by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin), with the European Central Bank exercising relevant supervisory authority within the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and routine EUR transfers; low to medium for statutory capital formation, regulated medical-device trade finance, and complex multi-currency treasury. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide services only if appropriately licensed and, where required, passported or otherwise authorised for Germany and the relevant services. Safeguarded EMI funds are not equivalent to a German commercial-bank deposit, credit facility, or statutory capital account. The provider must support SEPA and international payments, identify the German UG and its beneficial owners, comply with applicable AML, sanctions, safeguarding, outsourcing, and transaction-monitoring rules, and provide reliable account statements. German counterparties, customs brokers, auditors, tax authorities, and trade-finance banks may require an account held with a regulated credit institution. Offshore providers may be rejected because of jurisdictional risk, limited correspondent-bank access, inability to support SWIFT or documentary trade finance, or inadequate German-language compliance documentation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount (0.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the statutory 3.5% base rate multiplied by each municipality's Hebesatz; combined effective burden in major industrial cities is approximately 29.8% to 32.8%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, total 26.375%; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a corporate shareholder holding at least 10% for 12 months; generally 5% under tax treaties for at least 10% holdings and 15% for portfolio shareholdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany applies no de minimis monetary threshold for cross-border related-party transactions: contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation is mandatory for all intercompany transactions except de minimis cases; Country-by-Country Reporting is required for groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not implement a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code; however, §42 AO disregards abusive legal arrangements lacking economic substance, §50d Abs. 3 EStG strips treaty benefits from conduit/shareholder arrangements, and CFC rules under §§7-13 AStG tax passive low-tax income of controlled foreign companies; a UG without real employees, physical premises, and genuine operating activity in Germany may be recharacterised as a shell and lose treaty relief and expense deductibility.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a monetary fine under §370 AO; aggravated cases involving large-scale or commercial intent attract imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years; additional back-tax interest, late-payment surcharges, confiscation orders, and upward tax assessments apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal civil liability for evaded taxes under §§34, 69 AO where taxes remain unpaid because of intentional or grossly negligent breach of duties; they may also face criminal prosecution, travel bans, arrest, attachment of personal assets, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and withholding tax relief under §42 AO and §50d Abs. 3 EStG; retroactive withholding taxes assessed against the UG; penalties for non-filing and abusive structures; secondary dividend distributions may be imputed; coercive fines up to EUR 25,000 apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for taxes withheld from dividends and royalties that are not remitted, and for unlawful distributions to shareholders; they can be pursued for civil restitution and face criminal charges for abetting tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward profit adjustment by the tax authorities; penalty of 5% of the adjusted additional income, minimum EUR 5,000, increased to 10% for delayed or missing documentation (§162 Abs. 4 AO); secondary adjustments treated as constructive dividends subject to 26.375% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for the resulting corporate tax, trade tax, and withholding tax if they negligently approved non-compliant transfer prices; where intent is proved, criminal liability for tax evasion may attach.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) of up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of total annual turnover for legal entities; confiscation of illegal proceeds; export licence revocation; penalties under the Foreign Trade Act (AWG) and EU sanctions enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted under §261 StGB for money laundering, carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years; they face personal fines, freezing orders, travel bans, and civil claims from the company or third parties for involvement in the laundering scheme.
