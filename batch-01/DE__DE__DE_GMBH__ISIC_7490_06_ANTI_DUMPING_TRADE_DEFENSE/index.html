Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office, Berlin, for VAT Identification Number and EU/EEA registration matters; local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT returns; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) with certified authentication where mandatory electronic filing applies.

E-government filing portal

Resident German GmbH: register with the local Finanzamt via the statutory questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' submitted through ELSTER immediately upon incorporation/registration in the Commercial Register, resulting in an 11-digit Steuernummer (TIN), VAT identification number, and trade tax assessments. Non-resident foreign entity supplying taxable services or goods in Germany: no de minimis threshold applies; apply directly to the BZSt for a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the official 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer' together with certified constitutional documents, Commercial Register extract, VAT registration certificate from the jurisdiction of establishment, and, where applicable, a fiscal agent mandate under § 22a UStG.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the GmbH is a fully opaque, resident tax subject with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income; corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) 15.0%, solidarity surcharge 5.5% of corporate income tax (removed from 1 January 2025 for tax years commencing after that date), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at approximately 7-17% effective depending on the local Hebesatz; aggregate profit burden approximately 30-33%; non-resident shareholders face 26.375% withholding tax (incl. surcharge) on dividends absent treaty relief; no transparency election available to shareholders. — Resident German GmbH: register with the local Finanzamt via the statutory questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' submitted through ELSTER immediately upon incorporation/registration in the Commercial Register, resulting in an 11-digit Steuernummer (TIN), VAT identification number, and trade tax assessments. Non-resident foreign entity supplying taxable services or goods in Germany: no de minimis threshold applies; apply directly to the BZSt for a VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the official 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer' together with certified constitutional documents, Commercial Register extract, VAT registration certificate from the jurisdiction of establishment, and, where applicable, a fiscal agent mandate under § 22a UStG.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the GmbH is a fully opaque, resident tax subject with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income; corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) 15.0%, solidarity surcharge 5.5% of corporate income tax (removed from 1 January 2025 for tax years commencing after that date), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at approximately 7-17% effective depending on the local Hebesatz; aggregate profit burden approximately 30-33%; non-resident shareholders face 26.375% withholding tax (incl. surcharge) on dividends absent treaty relief; no transparency election available to shareholders.

Available election forms

Germany does not offer a US-style check-the-box or branch classification election. Applicable statutory groupings: (i) VAT Organschaft (fiscal unity) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, requiring financial, economic and organizational integration; (ii) corporate income tax and trade tax Organschaft under §§ 14-17 KStG, requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and majority shareholding; (iii) ATAD hybrid mismatch elections are restricted by § 4k EStG and the German Anti-Treaty-Shopping rules; certain EU-Directive-based options exist for input VAT recovery via § 18 Abs. 9 UStG refund procedure for non-established entrepreneurs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung, § 19 UStG): no VAT registration liability up to EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year ceiling (2025 threshold per Growth Opportunities Act); the exemption is materially unavailable to a commercial GmbH qualifying for input VAT deduction. Foreign non-established suppliers: zero threshold - VAT registration or reverse-charge treatment under § 13b UStG is mandatory upon any taxable supply with a German place of supply, regardless of volume; intra-Community acquisition registration is required above EUR 12,500 or at the first acquisition for a GmbH, but domestic B2B reverse charge generally protects non-resident service providers subject to identification by BZSt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating as an export supply under §§ 4 Nr. 1 and 6 UStG where legal power of disposition is transferred to the buyer and the goods physically depart the EU customs territory in a commercial context; proof requires both avoidance-of-evidence and export confirmation by German customs (ATLAS). Services: cross-border trade remedy defense consulting (ISIC 7490) with a non-German business customer is placed in the customer's country under the B2B general rule in § 3a (2) UStG; services to third-country entrepreneurs or where the recipient is not established nor habitually resident in the EU are outside the German taxable scope (0% German VAT exposure), while EU B2B supplies fall under the reverse-charge mechanism in the recipient's member state; zero-rating of export-related consulting services is available where supplied to a customer outside Germany with documentation that German VAT does not apply and that input VAT on procured costs remains creditable under the general deduction rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) filed electronically in the German ATLAS customs system with customs release confirmation and MRN.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical transportation of exported goods to destination outside the EU; for digital/consulting ISIC 7490 services, the equivalent export-evidence set consists of a signed engagement letter, service delivery report and customer confirmation of establishment outside Germany.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/telegraphic transfer advice evidencing receipt of export sales proceeds in convertible foreign exchange on the German exporter's bank account.
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and ISIC 7490 scope-of-work documents describing the anti-dumping defense services, place-of-supply analysis under § 3a UStG, and customer VAT registration number or third-country identity evidence.
  • BZSt-approved simplified proof for low-value consignments or triangulation cases where documentary verification is possible through bookkeeping records alone (§ 6 Abs. 4 UStG).

Mandatory structured e-invoicing (EN 16931, XRechnung, and ZUGFeRD formats) for all domestic B2B transactions: every taxable person established in Germany must be able to receive e-invoices from 1 January 2025; mandatory issuance for entrepreneurs with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 from 1 January 2027 and for all domestic entrepreneurs from 1 January 2028; parallel B2B transaction-level digital reporting to BZSt introduced via 'my e-Invoice' regime effective 2027/2028; no real-time fiscal clearance, no state-imposed API stamping, no QR-code digital stamping; transmission must occur electronically (e.g. via EDI/PEPPOL or a dedicated interface), and paper or PDF invoices are non-compliant within the mandate window; penalties for failure to issue or receive compliant e-invoices under §§ 379, 380 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per administrative offense and denial of input VAT deduction on non-compliant invoices under § 14 UStG.

Zero-rating claims are scrutinized by the responsible Finanzamt and customs authorities; the proof-of-export burden lies strictly with the supplier, and missing, incomplete or contradictory export evidence triggers retrospective VAT reassessment at 19% plus statutory default surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag, § 240 AO) and interest at 6% per annum; negligent or intentional misdeclaration triggers criminal proceedings under §§ 369, 370 AO: imprisonment up to 10 years for serious tax evasion, confiscation of evaded VAT amounts, full clawback of wrongly credited input VAT, loss of deduction rights under § 15 UStG, administrative fines (Geldbuße) for regulatory violations, and a 10-year limitation period for deliberate evasion; the company may also be debarred from future export refund procedures and subjected to joint-and-several liability of management under § 69 AO; fraudulent invoices attract § 14c UStG liabilities equal to the tax shown.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in Germany: both current-account and capital-account operations are liberalized under Section 1 of the AWG and Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no general exchange controls, no approval requirement for inward equity injections, dividend remittances, or repatriation of capital, and no restriction on maintaining bank accounts in foreign currencies. Practical compliance hurdles are limited to: (i) monthly statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for all payments to or from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500; (ii) German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) on profit remittances, which may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 25% participation held for at least 12 months) or to 5-15% under applicable double tax treaties; (iii) automatic exchange of information (FATCA/CRS) on the beneficial ownership structure; and (iv) the fully digital documentation of legal entity ownership in the German Transparency Register (Transparenzregister). There are no sector-specific convertibility or profit-remittance licensing requirements for ISIC 7490 trade remedy defense consulting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion obligations apply in Germany. Germany has abolished all foreign exchange surrender rules; export proceeds and fee income from non-resident clients (e.g., USD-denominated invoices for anti-dumping defense services rendered to foreign producers) may be retained indefinitely in any currency, whether held in a domestic foreign-currency account or an offshore account, and may be freely reinvested or remitted. The only statutory requirement is the monthly statistical reporting duty to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500; the reporting is normally discharged by the German bank on behalf of the entity at the time of transaction execution.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main), acting jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin - Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) for banking and payment services licensing/supervision, and with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) for foreign trade and payments control under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Non-bank Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe SA, Revolut Bank UAB, or Airwallex (NL) are legally viable in Germany only if the provider holds a full e-money license under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or a valid EEA home-state license passported into Germany with the required BaFin notification. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs may not hold client funds under a statutory deposit-protection scheme comparable to the German Einlagensicherung; safeguarding obligations under Section 4 ZAG apply but do not confer full banking-privilege protection; (ii) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit and from maintaining German-law escrow/Anderkonto structures, making them unsuitable for capital escrow or settlement of anti-dumping guarantee obligations; (iii) because the entity receives funds from non-EU clients in anti-dumping proceedings, a German Tier-1 correspondent banking relationship remains the primary treasury infrastructure, while a passported EMI is acceptable only for small working-capital float and FX conversion; (iv) UK-headquartered EMIs without a German branch have limited post-Brexit passporting rights and are excluded from the primary delivery model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound equity funding of the GmbH: the non-resident parent must execute a SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer if the remitting bank is within the EEA/SEPA zone) to the German operational account using the IBAN and Bank Identifier Code (BIC) of the German Tier-1 bank. The overall disbursement architecture is deliberately redundant: primary EUR liquidity is cleared via SEPA/SWIFT through the German Tier-1 bank, while foreign-currency disbursements are covered by the EUR-denominated account with the bank's internal FX desk or by a resulting short-term foreign-currency position.Deliverable FX Forwards in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNH (offshore renminbi) documented under the German Master Agreement for Financial Futures Transactions (DRV) or ISDA 2002 with the Tier-1 bank; no underlying export transaction is required beyond the anticipated professional fee receipts from non-EU clients.FX Swaps (swap points arrangements) for the temporary conversion of EUR working capital into USD and back, used for the funding of short-dated advance payments to overseas counsel.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (EUR/USD CCS) for longer-dated cash-flow matching where the consulting entity has a receiving contract in USD or CNY over a period exceeding 12 months.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) in currencies without an active deliverable onshore market, such as onshore Chinese Renminbi (CNY) and Indian Rupee (INR), executed by the German bank under the bank's ISDA/DRV framework; these are permissible because German law fully liberalizes over-the-counter derivative transactions, while the underlying USD/EUR market itself remains fully deliverable.Plain Vanilla EUR/USD FX Options (call/put and zero-cost collars) under ISDA/CSA for hedging the foreign-currency fee income in anti-dumping administrative reviews; the bank requires the entity to demonstrate that the hedging is back-to-back and proportionate to the expected net exposure to avoid classification as speculative trading.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Non-bank Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe SA, Revolut Bank UAB, or Airwallex (NL) are legally viable in Germany only if the provider holds a full e-money license under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or a valid EEA home-state license passported into Germany with the required BaFin notification. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs may not hold client funds under a statutory deposit-protection scheme comparable to the German Einlagensicherung; safeguarding obligations under Section 4 ZAG apply but do not confer full banking-privilege protection; (ii) EMIs are prohibited from granting credit and from maintaining German-law escrow/Anderkonto structures, making them unsuitable for capital escrow or settlement of anti-dumping guarantee obligations; (iii) because the entity receives funds from non-EU clients in anti-dumping proceedings, a German Tier-1 correspondent banking relationship remains the primary treasury infrastructure, while a passported EMI is acceptable only for small working-capital float and FX conversion; (iv) UK-headquartered EMIs without a German branch have limited post-Brexit passporting rights and are excluded from the primary delivery model.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) is 15% of taxable income, plus a solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality; the overall combined burden is commonly approximately 23%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic rate is generally 25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective rate of 26.375%, before any applicable church tax. An exemption or refund may reduce the rate under an applicable double tax agreement or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to beneficial-ownership, holding, anti-abuse, and procedural requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: German documentation thresholds generally require a Local File for cross-border related-party transactions where annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions. A Master File is generally required where the group’s consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Thresholds, transaction aggregation, exemptions, and documentation deadlines must be checked for the relevant year. Documentation must be produced within the applicable statutory period following a request; current rules generally provide 30 days, subject to the applicable transitional provisions.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty and EU anti-abuse provisions, the German general anti-abuse rule, permanent-establishment analysis, and rules governing foreign-controlled structures. Maintain evidence of personnel, decision-making, functions, assets, risks, and actual service performance. Anti-dumping and trade-remedy advisory services should be supported by engagement records, deliverables, time records, and a defensible allocation of intercompany costs and margins.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax returns

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, and potential surcharges remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal criminal liability and tax-liability exposure for culpable breaches of their duties. Criminal penalties depend on individual conduct; imprisonment is possible. Travel restrictions are not an automatic statutory consequence.
Late or unpaid tax and payroll withholding

Statutory sanction: Late-filing and late-payment surcharges, interest, assessments, enforcement measures, and, where statutory elements are met, criminal or administrative proceedings may apply.

Director personal liability: A managing director may be personally liable for tax debts where culpable breach of tax duties causes non-collection, including under section 69 of the Fiscal Code. Criminal exposure may arise from intentional non-payment or evasion.
Transfer-pricing misstatement or missing documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income and impose transfer-pricing documentation surcharges under applicable law. A material records failure may permit adverse estimates and additional tax, interest, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors must ensure appropriate records and controls. Personal liability may arise where a culpable breach causes tax loss; intentional falsification may also result in criminal liability.
Unlicensed legal or tax advisory services

Statutory sanction: Regulatory orders, administrative fines where prescribed, injunctions, contractual and professional-liability claims, and potential invalidity or unenforceability issues may arise. The precise consequence depends on the service, provider status, and statutory exception.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal regulatory or civil exposure for their own participation or culpable oversight failures. Confirm that anti-dumping defense work is within the permitted scope of the provider’s authorizations and engagement structure.
AML, sanctions, and trade-control breaches

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include administrative fines, asset freezes, transaction prohibitions, regulatory enforcement, and criminal penalties under applicable AML, sanctions, and foreign-trade laws. The exact sanction depends on the provision and conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible personnel may incur personal administrative or criminal liability for their own acts or culpable failures to implement required controls. Travel bans are not automatic and depend on a specific legal basis or order.
Fraudulent insolvency or asset stripping

Statutory sanction: Potential insolvency-law avoidance, repayment claims, damages, director disqualification, and criminal penalties may apply under the Insolvency Code and Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal civil liability, insolvency-related repayment claims, and criminal prosecution. Any travel restriction requires a separate legal basis and is not an automatic consequence.
