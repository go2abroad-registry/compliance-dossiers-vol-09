Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG minimum registered share capital is EUR 1 (practically EUR 1,000 to avoid restrictions); every share must be fully paid before filing; cash contributions must be credited to a German business account under management control; no escrow account is generally required for cash contributions, but in-kind contributions require a formation audit/capital maintenance report. Also mandatory statutory reserve under GmbHG § 5a(3): 25% of annual net profit must be retained until share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

For small e-commerce offices with a warehouse of less than 200 m², a general trade inspection and simplified fire safety statement satisfy the administrative obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local Finanzamt administers corporate tax and domestic VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) and operates the BZSt online services portal. ELSTER is the principal electronic tax filing and communication system.

E-government filing portal

Register the UG with the competent Finanzamt by submitting the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung through ELSTER, generally within one month after commencement of business. The Finanzamt assigns a domestic tax number and assesses VAT registration based on the anticipated activities. Apply separately to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr.; provide the assigned tax number and entity and registration details. A foreign-established taxable person without a German establishment registers with the Finanzamt designated for its place of establishment; non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a German fiscal representative, subject to statutory exceptions. A USt-IdNr. does not replace the domestic tax number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Register the UG with the competent Finanzamt by submitting the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung through ELSTER, generally within one month after commencement of business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a corporation subject to German corporate income tax and trade tax; Germany has no general US-style check-the-box election for a UG. No general election permits the UG to be treated as fiscally transparent. VAT small-business treatment under section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply if statutory turnover conditions are met, but it is not an entity-classification election and generally precludes input VAT deduction for exempt transactions. Qualifying small businesses may also use the EU cross-border SME scheme where its conditions are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts an ordinary taxable business from VAT registration: registration and VAT obligations generally arise from taxable activities. The section 19 small-business exemption applies, subject to eligibility, where prior-calendar-year relevant turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; this is an exemption regime, not a general registration threshold. Special rules apply to non-established businesses and specific transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the EU may be exempt with credit (effectively zero-rated) if the statutory export-supply conditions are met and the seller retains prescribed evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies are generally exempt with credit only where the purchaser is appropriately VAT-identified in another Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies applicable reporting and evidentiary requirements. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer is offshore: their VAT treatment depends on place-of-supply rules and any applicable exemption. For online resale, the seller must separately determine whether it acts as principal or intermediary and whether domestic, intra-EU distance-sale, export, or margin-scheme rules apply. The second-hand goods margin scheme may be available for qualifying goods and purchase chains; it does not permit ordinary input VAT recovery on the qualifying purchase.

  • Customs export declaration and export accompanying/reference data, where applicable
  • Customs confirmation or other prescribed evidence of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, purchaser, consideration, and transaction
  • Transport and delivery evidence, such as bill of lading, carrier documentation, or proof of delivery
  • For qualifying intra-EU supplies: customer VAT identification validation, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement records
  • Inward remittance certificate: not a general German statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption

Germany has no general real-time VAT invoice clearance or fiscal-stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for relevant domestic B2B transactions. Mandatory issuance is being phased in: transition rules generally permit paper or other invoices through 2026, with qualifying issuers able to continue certain transitional formats through 2027; from 2028, structured e-invoices are generally required for in-scope domestic B2B transactions. The format must satisfy statutory requirements, generally EN 16931 or an interoperable equivalent. B2C invoices are not subject to a general e-invoice mandate. VAT return and reporting obligations are filed electronically through ELSTER.

The supplier bears the evidentiary burden for claiming an export exemption. Insufficient proof can result in denial or reversal of the exemption, assessment of output VAT, late-payment interest, and tax reassessments under the German Fiscal Code. Intentional or reckless false claims may trigger tax-evasion or tax-reduction liability, fines, or criminal penalties under applicable law. Input VAT refunds may be reduced or reclaimed where underlying deductions are unsupported or the transactions do not qualify.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), the EU Capital Free Movement Directive (88/361/EEC), and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). There are no general exchange controls, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts, and no restrictions on profit remittance or dividend repatriation for non-resident shareholders, provided the UG satisfies corporate law (dividend distribution restrictions under the UG's maintenance-of-capital rules) and tax withholding obligations (5% German dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, reduced under double tax treaties; no trade tax on dividends for shareholders). Remittance proceeds are payable in any freely convertible currency; bank confirmation or AWV reporting may be required for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists in Germany. The AWV does not impose mandatory conversion ratios or repatriation deadlines for export proceeds. Residents must simply report cross-border payments in euros or foreign currencies to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics, generally within ten days of the transaction or receipt, subject to the thresholds and exemptions under Section 61 et seq. AWV. In practice, monies may be retained abroad or held in foreign-currency accounts without restriction; commercial invoices should cite the applicable currency and payment terms.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank) in coordination with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the German Federal Ministry of Finance for exchange-control-related reporting and regulatory matters; the Bundesbank administers the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) reporting framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium to Low. German-registered or EU/EEA-authorized payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, or PayaPilot have legal capacity to hold IBANs and clear SEPA and SWIFT payments under the EU PSD2 regime. However, for a UG whose core business is cross-border vintage luxury resale, most tier-1 German banks and merchant acquirers treat such EMI accounts as sub-accounts for collection only; they are not accepted as full operational treasury accounts for the following reasons: (i) EMIs generally do not offer fiat credit facilities or overdrafts; (ii) funds are not deposit-guaranteed under the German Deposit Protection Scheme (Einlagensicherung); (iii) account naming and AML ownership structures may not satisfy the bank's own KYC when the UG later seeks merchant services; (iv) many digital EMIs restrict use for 'high-risk' or consignment marketplace flows. Legal and reputational constraints therefore make an EMI unsuitable as the primary clearing account. It remains viable as a supplementary collection channel, provided the EMI holds a valid BaFin or EEA Central Bank license and maintains a German IBAN (DE) under SEPA.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The founding director or shareholder may transfer capital into the UG's German bank account via SEPA Credit Transfer (in EUR) from any EEA-accessible account or via SWIFT MT103 (for USD or non-SEPA jurisdictions). The remittance instruction must state 'Einzahlung Stammkapital – Kapitalerhöhung' or 'Kapitalmaßnahme – Gesellschafterdarlehen' to distinguish equity injection from trade debt. The German bank will request a declaration of source of funds; where the equity is in cash, a notarized confirmation (Sachgründungsbericht) is required if assets in kind are contributed. For inward remittances exceeding €12,500 or equivalent, the UG (or its German clearing bank on its behalf) must submit a Bundesbank AWV statistical report (Zahlungsbilanz) using the purpose code V41 'Kapitalbeteiligung/Transaktionskode 610' (direct investment in equity). The bank may also require a Form S (für Auslandszahlungsverkehr) if the transfer is non-EPC or originates from a non-EEA country. FX conversion is unregulated and can be executed at market rate; no surrender or registration is necessary, but the bank may issue a negative statement of tax status if the beneficiary is a tax-haven jurisdiction.The primary clearing rails for outbound trade settlements are: (i) SEPA SCT for euro payments to EEA suppliers; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA foreign currency payments (USD, GBP, JPY, etc.) to overseas consigners or trade creditors; (iii) Feed-through of CHAPS/TARGET2 for interbank same-day euro liquidity; (iv) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credit) for high-value import of luxury inventory from non-EEA sellers, especially when the seller requires bank-issued guarantee. In the vintage luxury resale model, the bank account must accept free inbound SWIFT credits from marketplaces such as eBay, Vestiaire Collective, The RealReal, and Escrow.com, and provide API-enabled zengin/MT940 statements for reconciliation. For outbound disbursements to consigners, the recommended rail is API-driven SEPA Credit Transfer with ISO 20022 pain.001 messages and UETR tracking. The instruction must include the purpose code: 'Payment for goods – Purchase of luxury vintage items' with relevant commercial invoice; for payments exceeding €12,500, an AWV outbound report via bank channel is mandatory. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to Low. German-registered or EU/EEA-authorized payment institutions and electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, or PayaPilot have legal capacity to hold IBANs and clear SEPA and SWIFT payments under the EU PSD2 regime. However, for a UG whose core business is cross-border vintage luxury resale, most tier-1 German banks and merchant acquirers treat such EMI accounts as sub-accounts for collection only; they are not accepted as full operational treasury accounts for the following reasons: (i) EMIs generally do not offer fiat credit facilities or overdrafts; (ii) funds are not deposit-guaranteed under the German Deposit Protection Scheme (Einlagensicherung); (iii) account naming and AML ownership structures may not satisfy the bank's own KYC when the UG later seeks merchant services; (iv) many digital EMIs restrict use for 'high-risk' or consignment marketplace flows. Legal and reputational constraints therefore make an EMI unsuitable as the primary clearing account. It remains viable as a supplementary collection channel, provided the EMI holds a valid BaFin or EEA Central Bank license and maintains a German IBAN (DE) under SEPA.
