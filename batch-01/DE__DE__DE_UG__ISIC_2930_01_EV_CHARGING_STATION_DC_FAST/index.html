Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office acceptance for a German UG cannot be completely virtual. The UG must maintain a physical registered office in Germany (authentic office location) that is accessible for service of process and registry correspondence; however, use of a professional co-working or business-center address as the registered office is accepted if a mail-holding and acceptance-of-service arrangement is in place and the actual business operations (sales, procurement, or logistical coordination) are located at another physical EU location or German facility. For a manufacturer of high-power DC ultra-fast EV chargers, the UG is expected to have at least a contractual warehouse, repair hub, quality-control assembly point, or engineering office in Germany, because the product is infrastructure hardware subject to CE-marking, German product safety law, and technical inspection demands. A pure virtual office for the entire business will violate the registered-office doctrine (Sitztheorie) and can lead to refusal of the commercial register entry or to post-registration cancellation under § 59 FamFG. Additionally, the German administrative practice requires a letter of consent from the owner of the registered office premises, if the owner is not the UG, and the notary must certify this.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG may fix its share capital at a minimum of EUR 1.00; every equity interest (Stammeinlage) must be denominated in at least EUR 1.00. Contributions fixed in the articles must be paid into a German business bank account in the company's name and must remain at the free disposal of the managing director before registration, evidenced by a bank free-disposal confirmation (Freiverfügungsbestätigung). No statutory escrow account is mandatory; if funds are paid to the notary pre-registration, they must be held strictly on a separate notarial trust account (Anderkonto) and returned if registration is refused. Upon commencement, 25% of annual net profits must be retained in the statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000 in accordance with § 5a(3) GmbHG.

Funding & unlock path: Prior to operation, the production premises must obtain fire safety acceptance (Brandschutzabnahme) by the fire inspection authority or lower building supervisory authority, covering fire alarm, extinguishing systems, and escape routes. A structural engineer's certificate must confirm the floor load capacity for high-power charging and testing equipment. The workplace must pass the workplace safety clearance under the Arbeitsstättenverordnung, electrical installations tested to DIN VDE 0100, and occupational safety review per DGUV regulations, including electrical safety testing (DGUV V3) and, if applicable, explosion protection documentation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern / BZSt) for national VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.), non-resident VAT refunds, and central tax registers; the locally competent tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax, and VAT return processing. Official filing and registration portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); supervisory oversight by the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the UG via notarial deed and register it in the commercial register (Handelsregister) to obtain the statutory company identity and register number. Step 2 - Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt, which registers the UG for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT and issues the municipal tax number (Steuernummer). Step 3 - Submit a separate electronic application to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) once intra-Community or transborder transactions are conducted. Step 4 - Foreign non-resident entities without a German seat or permanent establishment file the same questionnaire with the Finanzamt designated for foreign-controlled corporations (in practice Finanzamt Berlin Neukölln) under the Zuständigkeitsverordnung and must appoint a fiscal representative (steuerlicher Vertreter) under Sec. 22a UStG where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box or opt-in fiscal transparency election exists under German tax law: the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a fully taxable corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate income tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), producing an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 29.8%-31.6%. Hybrid-mismatch recharacterization is nonetheless possible under the ATAD implementation (Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 8b KStG), disregarding or reclassifying payments to hybrid entities and reverse hybrids; onward attribution to foreign shareholders can trigger German controlled-foreign-company rules (Sec. 20 AStG). A US parent may file US Form 8832 (check-the-box) solely for US federal tax purposes, but the election has no binding effect on German classification and may increase subpart F/GILTI exposure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold applies to domestic entrepreneurs: the first taxable supply establishes registration duty before the Finanzamt, except for the Sec. 19 UStG small-business relief (prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000), which is restricted to resident entrepreneurs of limited scale. Non-resident sellers are registrable from the very first German supply without any monetary threshold; intra-EU distance sales are governed by the EUR 10,000 Union-wide OSS threshold for escalation to the German B2C VAT liability, and foreign suppliers making taxable supplies must name a fiscal representative under Sec. 22a UStG unless the reverse charge or OSS applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations qualify for the 0% rate (tax exemption with input-credit) under Sec. 4 No. 1(a) and Sec. 6 UStG where the goods are demonstrably transported to a destination outside the EU and the exporter retains both the bookkeeping evidence and the documentary proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis per Sec. 8-11 UStDV). Intra-Community dispatches to another Member State are exempt under Sec. 4 No. 1(b) and Sec. 6a UStG, conditioned on the acquirer holding a valid USt-IdNr., shipment to the other Member State, and the arrival receipt evidence (Gelangensbestätigung). Installation, commissioning and maintenance services performed abroad on DC fast chargers, together with software-as-a-service supplied to non-resident entrepreneurs, are zero-rated or reverse-charged under the B2B place-of-supply rules of Sec. 3a UStG; the UG must record the statutory basis of exemption on the invoice and substantiate the export within the applicable documentary windows.

  • Customs export declaration (elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung) transmitted through the German ATLAS system, generating the export accompanying document and MRN reference
  • Transport evidence (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or signed CMR consignment note together with the foreign customs arrival certificate; for EU dispatch, the Gelangensbestätigung or electronic arrival record)
  • Inward remittance certificate and payment evidence (SWIFT MT103/LC documentation) reconciled against the commercial invoice and export customs value

Germany applies a phased domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate (E-Rechnung): effective 1 January 2025 every German entrepreneur must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or EN 16931 / CIUS formats); effective 1 January 2027 suppliers with previous-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies; effective 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B invoices must be issued electronically. Transmission occurs via delegated exchange (E-Mail or secure portal) without real-time fiscal clearance or Italy-style digital stamping; ZUGFeRD 2.0 hybrid files remain permitted transitionally. VAT pre-registrations (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen, monthly or quarterly) and the annual VAT return must be filed electronically via ELSTER; intra-Community supplies and exports must be reported in the recapitulative statement to BZSt, and physical exports must clear customs through the ATLAS platform.

If the export proceeds and supporting transport/customs records are not secured within the statutory evidentiary period (Sec. 6(4) UStG, generally 12 months from the supply), the Finanzamt reverses the zero rating, assesses VAT at 19% plus interest under Sec. 233a AO and a late-payment surcharge under Sec. 240 AO (up to 10% of the reassessed amount). Forged customs stamps, fabricated shipment documents, or sham LC/remittance arrangements constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to 10 years and monetary fines; the limitation period for assessing evasion-based VAT is extended to 10 years under Sec. 169(2) AO, with retrospective clawback of the full exempt value. Invoices stating VAT that is not legally deemed payable trigger liability under Sec. 14c UStG even where the counter-party declines payment, and managing-director liability plus corporate veil-piercing can attach under Sec. 69 AO and Sec. 64 GmbHG. The 180-day repatriation mark in this dossier reflects the banking/financial compliance standard for export proceeds and payment-receipt evidence before the statutory 12-month outer deadline.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany generally imposes no approval, quota, surrender, or repatriation requirement for cross-border investment, intercompany funding, dividends, interest, royalties, or trade payments. Transfers remain subject to German tax, corporate-law, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, payment-services, beneficial-ownership, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, and statistical-reporting requirements. A UG may remit distributable profits only after preparation and approval of annual accounts, satisfaction of statutory reserve requirements, recognition of accumulated losses, and compliance with capital-maintenance rules. Dividends to non-residents may be subject to German withholding tax, generally 25% plus solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, exemption procedures, beneficial-ownership requirements, and anti-treaty-shopping rules. Interest and royalty payments may require withholding analysis and arm's-length support. Banks may delay or reject transfers where source of funds, beneficial ownership, sanctions screening, tax residence, transaction purpose, or corporate authority is unclear.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or general deadline requiring exporters to convert foreign-currency receipts into euros. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion. German residents may have statistical reporting obligations to the Deutsche Bundesbank for certain cross-border payments, claims, liabilities, securities, or foreign direct investment positions. Reporting thresholds, forms, exemptions, and deadlines depend on the transaction category and are subject to change; the company should confirm current requirements through the Bundesbank reporting portal or its bank. Export documentation, customs records, invoices, contracts, payment evidence, VAT treatment, and sanctions controls should be retained under applicable German commercial, tax, customs, and AML record-retention rules.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, with banking, payment-services, and anti-money-laundering supervision shared with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin). Germany has no general foreign-exchange administration comparable to a capital-control authority. The European Central Bank applies to euro monetary policy and relevant euro-system rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and foreign-currency collection, but not a complete substitute for a German bank account. A regulated European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide IBAN accounts, payment initiation, card services, and selected FX services under its licence and passporting or branch arrangements. It may not necessarily provide a German bank deposit account, statutory UG capital escrow, lending, deposit protection equivalent to a bank deposit, unrestricted cash-management functionality, or all commercial-LC services. Offshore providers outside the EEA require separate legal and regulatory analysis and may be rejected by German counterparties, auditors, customs brokers, or banks. The company must verify licence status, safeguarding model, insolvency treatment, German availability, sanctions controls, transaction limits, safeguarding disclosures, and data-access arrangements. Customer funds must not be commingled with company operating funds, and the company should maintain a regulated German or EEA bank as its primary treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital to a bank-designated German UG capital account using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro transfers or SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA currencies. The remitter should be the shareholder or documented financing entity named in the formation or financing documents; third-party funding should be avoided unless fully documented. Use a payment purpose such as paid-in share capital, shareholder contribution, or shareholder loan, as applicable, and ensure the legal classification matches the accounting entry. Provide the bank with the notarial formation documents, capital-payment instruction, shareholder register, source-of-funds evidence, ownership chart, financing agreement, and expected remittance details. The bank should verify sender identity, beneficiary account, beneficial ownership, sanctions status, source and purpose of funds, and any required Bundesbank statistical reporting. Do not treat a shareholder loan as equity or an equity contribution as debt without corresponding corporate approvals, accounting treatment, transfer-pricing support, and tax analysis. For non-euro funding, document the conversion rate, bank charges, value date, and any foreign-exchange exposure.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA scheme, subject to bank limits and beneficiary verification. Use SWIFT MT103 or the current ISO 20022 equivalent for non-SEPA cross-border wires, with complete originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, and purpose information. For larger or documentary transactions, use a commercial letter of credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or open-account settlement supported by credit insurance and counterparty controls. A commercial LC should specify the issuing and confirming banks, governing rules such as UCP 600 where agreed, document requirements, Incoterms, shipment deadlines, discrepancy handling, sanctions clauses, and charge allocation. Match every payment to an approved purchase order, contract, invoice, customs or transport record, and three-way accounting reconciliation. Screen all parties, vessels, banks, goods, routes, and end users; preserve payment and trade records; and monitor unusual overpayments, split payments, refunds to unrelated accounts, and requests to change beneficiary details. German banks may require enhanced review for trade with higher-risk jurisdictions, dual-use goods, unusual intermediaries, or payments inconsistent with the declared EV-charger manufacturing profile.Deliverable forward exchange contracts with a regulated bank or authorised investment firm, used to hedge documented foreign-currency receivables, payables, capital expenditure, or forecast exposures within the institution's approved risk limits.Non-deliverable forwards where the relevant bank offers the product lawfully and the reference currency, counterparty jurisdiction, documentation, and settlement mechanics are acceptable; NDFs are generally cash-settled and may be unsuitable where the company needs physical currency delivery.Currency swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps for documented funding or longer-term currency exposures, subject to ISDA or equivalent master documentation, collateral terms, valuation, close-out, accounting, and counterparty-credit review.Foreign-exchange options, collars, and participating forwards where appropriate and transparently priced; the company should obtain management approval because premiums, contingent obligations, and potential mark-to-market losses can be material.Spot foreign-exchange transactions for operational conversion of receipts and payments. Maintain a written treasury policy covering permitted instruments, eligible counterparties, hedge ratios, tenor limits, authority levels, confirmation, independent valuation, collateral, accounting, and prohibition of speculative trading.Use EMIR, MiFID II, EMIR Refit, benchmark, margin, reporting, and transaction-documentation analysis where the company enters into derivatives with an EU or non-EU financial counterparty. The company should assess whether it qualifies as a non-financial counterparty and whether clearing, margin, reporting, or collateral obligations apply. Hedging should be demonstrably connected to commercial exposures and should not be used to evade sanctions, capital-maintenance rules, tax rules, or bank risk controls.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational payments and foreign-currency collection, but not a complete substitute for a German bank account. A regulated European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide IBAN accounts, payment initiation, card services, and selected FX services under its licence and passporting or branch arrangements. It may not necessarily provide a German bank deposit account, statutory UG capital escrow, lending, deposit protection equivalent to a bank deposit, unrestricted cash-management functionality, or all commercial-LC services. Offshore providers outside the EEA require separate legal and regulatory analysis and may be rejected by German counterparties, auditors, customs brokers, or banks. The company must verify licence status, safeguarding model, insolvency treatment, German availability, sanctions controls, transaction limits, safeguarding disclosures, and data-access arrangements. Customer funds must not be commingled with company operating funds, and the company should maintain a regulated German or EEA bank as its primary treasury account.
