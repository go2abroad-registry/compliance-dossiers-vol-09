Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available structuring elections for a German UG include: (i) consolidated corporate income and trade tax fiscal unity ('ertragsteuerliche Organschaft') under Section 14 of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, established through a profit-and-loss pooling agreement; (ii) VAT fiscal unity ('umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft') under Section 2(2)(2) UStG enabling group VAT consolidation; and (iii) hybrid and branch mismatch election rules under Sections 4k and 4j of the German Income Tax Act, implementing the ATAD, for importation into a multi-entity cross-border structure. The UG is an opaque corporation and cannot elect transparent pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A resident German trading UG is registered for VAT mandatorily from its first commercial activity, with no general turnover threshold. The small-business exemption ('Kleinunternehmerregelung') under Section 19 UStG applies only up to EUR 25,000 prior-year and EUR 100,000 current-year turnover, but this relief is not available in substance for an actively trading agricultural commodity entity. Intra-Community distance sales and OSS electronic window registration apply above EUR 10,000 annual EU distance-sales turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries ('Ausfuhrlieferungen') are exempt from VAT with input VAT recovery under Sections 4(1)(a) and 6 of the German VAT Act. Eligibility enshrines: (i) the goods (soybeans, yellow corn, other bulk agricultural commodities) must be physically transported to a third-country destination outside the EU customs territory; (ii) the dispatch or transport must be initiated by the UG supplier or by a carrier commissioned by the UG; (iii) the identity and business status of the buyer must be documented; (iv) the supplier must possess a legally valid export proof ('Ausfuhrnachweis'), including the customs export declaration accepted in the German ATLAS customs system, a transport document (ocean Bill of Lading for bulk vessel delivery), and, where applicable, the 'Inward Remittance Certificate' evidencing receipt of export proceeds. The export substantiation package generally must be completed within the statutory period ending on the 15th day of the second calendar month following the month of supply, with administrative extensions available. For ocean bulk shipments on FOB or CIF terms, the original Bill of Lading constitutes the primary transport evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Zollanmeldung) filed via ATLAS, with assigned Movement Reference Number (MRN) and electronic export acceptance record
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (or equivalent multimodal CMR / Air Waybill) evidencing direct shipment to a third country
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / FX repatriation evidence (SWIFT wire transfer record) proving receipt of export proceeds within the trade and transfer-pricing benchmark window
  • Export Proof Certificate (Ausfuhrnachweis) including the electronic 'Abgangsbescheinigung' confirming that the goods left the EU customs territory

Germany initiated a phased mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing obligation under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (EN 16931 format, i.e., XInvoice or ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue compliant e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all businesses must issue e-invoices. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital tax authority approval mechanism comparable to South European regimes; compliance is evidenced by interoperable structured XML transmission and preservation of the fiscal data set. From 2028, EU-mandated transaction-level digital reporting via the ViDA package is expected to apply to intra-Community and domestic transactions. Export invoices must also satisfy Section 14 UStG formal requirements and record the zero-rated export reason.

The local Finanzamt applies heightened audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims under Section 6 UStG and the German Export Documentation Regulation (Ausfuhrnachweisverordnung). If the export proof is incomplete, missing, or untimely, the exemption is denied retrospectively; the UG becomes liable to charge 19% VAT on the export consideration, with interest under Section 233a of the German Fiscal Code (AO) and late-payment surcharges of up to 1% per month under Section 240 AO. Tax evasion penalties under Section 370 AO apply to deliberate under-declaration or fraudulent export substantiation, potentially resulting in imprisonment from six months to ten years; negligent misconduct is punishable by fines under Section 379 AO. The assessment window extends to ten years for fraudulent tax evasion and four years for serious negligence. The managing director of the UG faces personal secondary liability (Section 69 AO) for unremitted VAT and related penalties in the event of a clawback.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible currency regime for both current account and capital account items under EU Treaty law and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). No general licence or exchange-control approval is required for imports, exports, dividend repatriation, profit remittance, shareholder loans, or capital distributions. Practical hurdles are not convertibility-based but procedural: all cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or more must be statistically reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank; bank disbursement departments demand documentary support (invoice, transport document, contract, sanctions screening) before releasing funds; and remittances that appear to be dividends, interest, or royalties trigger German withholding tax. For a UG trading bulk grain, soybeans, and yellow corn, the free-flowing euro will not be obstructed, but the bank will treat high-frequency commodity payments as low-risk yet high-volume and will enforce strict documentary and purpose-code discipline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no remittance deadline in the German foreign exchange regime. Exporters may retain proceeds in any currency, use them for reinvestment, or convert them freely. The only binding obligation is statistical reporting: receipt of export proceeds from a non-resident of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank by the fifth business day, normally through the collecting bank, using the balance-of-payments reporting code. The exporting UG must therefore ensure that every inbound payment references the commercial contract, invoice number, and the correct AWV purpose code, and must retain bills of lading, warehouse receipts, and weight certificates.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht – BaFin) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action for foreign trade and payments administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for full-scale grain treasury operations; acceptable only as a nimble operating overlay. German-licensed or EEA-passported fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) subject to BaFin/Deutsche Bundesbank oversight can offer remote onboarding, IBANs, SWIFT MT103 initiation, and multi-currency wallets. However, most EMIs cannot issue, confirm, or settle documentary Letters of Credit under UCP600, cannot provide commercial-value agricultural trade finance, and frequently route USD payments through correspondent banks with weaker screening and slower clearing. A UG brokering bulk soybeans and yellow corn should use an EMI only for low-value receivables or ancillary working capital, while anchor the full liquidity and trade-clearing framework in a tier-1 German commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder will remit the UG's registered capital or a shareholder loan through a SWIFT MT103 wire. The purpose code in field 70 should state unequivocally: 'Gesellschaftereinlage in Unternehmergesellschaft' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen', referencing the notarial deed date and shareholder resolution. For a UG, the minimum statutory capital of EUR 1 is not a practical blocker, but the bank treats the initial deposit as the baseline of commercial substance. All equity injections must be posted to the prohibited-interest account only after the German Commercial Register filings and the managing director's signature file are verified. Verification protocol: (i) bank screens payee and beneficiary names against sanctions lists; (ii) bank checks collection of Transparenzregister and UBO; (iii) bank reconciles the wire amount and value date to the notarial deed; (iv) bank issues a Bundesbank reporting code for inbound capital transfer; (v) tax withholding is irrelevant to equity contribution, but a shareholder loan must have a signed agreement supporting market-rate interest and repayment terms.For open-account grain settlements, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 executed through a euro-zone TARGET2 transfer or USD via Fedwire/CHIPS through a US correspondent. Documentary collections (CAD/DP) are the cheaper fallback rail, handled via MT798.
