Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the VAT identification number and centralized functions; local tax office (Finanzamt) with jurisdiction over the registered seat of the UG for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment. Main portal: www.elster.de; BZSt VAT ID portal: www.bzst.de

E-government filing portal

The UG, represented by its managing director, must file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung, official form U1) with the competent local Finanzamt via Elster or paper. The form requires the notarial registration certificate, shareholders, share capital, intended activities, expected annual turnover and profits, bank details, and for non-resident shareholders additional statements on beneficial ownership and DTT eligibility. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (tax number) within a few weeks. Separately, for cross-border/export transactions, the UG must apply to the BZSt for a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID number) using form U1 and ancillary evidence; this is a digital procedure with no statutory fee.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no general check-the-box regime. The UG may elect to consolidate with a domestic parent or subsidiary under the Organschaft (fiscal unity) rules of Secs. 14–17 KStG, Sec. 2(2)(2) UStG and trade tax grouping principles, provided the controlled entity is financially, economically and organisationally integrated, a legally effective profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) is in place, and the consequence is single taxpayer treatment for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. With respect to hybrid instruments and hybrid entities, written elections/disclaimers and documentation must satisfy Sec. 4k EStG, the ATAD anti-hybrid rules and German CFC rules; no comparable domestic 'entity classification election' is available for a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for resident corporations. A UG is a taxable person from its first supply in Germany and must register with the local Finanzamt. Small-business relief under Sec. 19 UStG applies only to natural persons or sole merchant structures where previous-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000; a UG formed for rope-access facade services is ordinarily not entitled to this relief if engaged in qualifying commercial activities. For intra-EU distance selling and cross-border acquisitions, separate thresholds apply in the destination member state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for (i) deliveries of goods from Germany to a third-country territory when the goods physically leave the EU customs territory (Sec. 4 No. 1(a) UStG and Sec. 6 UStG); (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a registered VAT customer in another member state (Sec. 4 No. 1(b) UStG and Sec. 6a UStG); and (iii) supplies of business-to-business services where the place of supply is deemed to be in another country under Sec. 3a UStG, or services closely related to exports and qualifying under the place-of-supply rules. A rope-access facade replacement service performed on buildings outside Germany may fall outside German VAT if the place of supply is the third country; by contrast, services physically performed at German building sites for a foreign client are generally taxable in Germany at 19% unless the reverse-charge mechanism applies to the recipient or the service is directly connected with an exported movable good. Zero-rating requires the UG to hold commercial evidence and transport documents proving the actual transport/dispatch, the recipient's VAT ID number in intra-Community cases, and the performance of the service in the declared jurisdiction. The exporter must issue an invoice with the statutory warning 'export' or 'intra-Community supply' and an explicit zero-VAT indication.

  • Customs Export Declaration – Ausfuhranmeldung/Ausfuhrbegleitdokument generated through ATLAS with the status 'Ausfuhr' and customs release, including the exporter’s EORI number and export item details.
  • Bill of Lading / transport document – bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or airway bill evidencing physical departure from the EU customs territory and delivery to the third-country destination.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – bank credit confirmation / Zahlungsnachweis showing receipt of payment in convertible foreign currency on the German UG’s bank account, the invoiced amount, and the export reference; for services, the BZSt may additionally require a service declaration or work completion certificate.

From 1 January 2025, German resident taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or EN 16931-compliant XML/EDIFACT) for domestic B2B transactions. From 1 January 2027, larger taxable persons must issue e-invoices for domestic business-to-business supplies; from 1 January 2028, smaller businesses face the same obligation. Germany does not yet mandate continuous transaction control / real-time fiscal clearance via an API, nor a digital stamping regime for invoices; however, invoices to federal/public contracting authorities must already conform to XRechnung and the E-Rechnung-VO. All VAT invoices must show both the issuer’s and recipient’s VAT identification numbers where applicable, and paper invoices are prohibited for mandatory e-invoicing scenarios after the transition.

Zero-rating is denied if documentary evidence is incomplete, internally inconsistent, not retained for the statutory ten-year retention period, or not produced on demand to the Finanzamt or BZSt. Where denied, output VAT is assessed at 19% from the date of supply, plus default interest of 0.15% per month pursuant to Sec. 233a AO and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month on the outstanding VAT pursuant to Sec. 240 AO. Fraduulent export zero-rating, including fictitious exports, forged customs declarations, or circular carousel transactions, triggers joint and several liability under Sec. 25d UStG, reopening of final assessments under Sec. 173 AO, and guaranteed retrospective clawback. Criminal consequences for tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO may reach five years’ imprisonment, or ten years in aggravated cases, with additional confiscation orders, monetary fines, and publication of final judgments; OLAF and the German customs anti-fraud unit (Kontrolleinheit) may also impose customs penalties and administrative fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the German Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV). Germany imposes no exchange-control approvals, no quota limitations, no import-cover ratios, and no differentiated convertibility regime between current and capital flows; profit remittance and dividend distributions from an Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) are unrestricted from a foreign-exchange perspective, although the tax system applies withholding tax on dividends at the statutory flat rate of 25% plus solidarity surcharge where applicable (aggregate up to 26.375%), reduced under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive transposition or an applicable double tax treaty. The sole regulatory impediment to cross-border payments is statistical: any cross-border payment from Germany exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for balance-of-payments statistics, which is a declarative duty, not a prior-authorization or convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Germany does not impose statutory surrender obligations, mandatory conversion ratios, prescribed repatriation windows, or designated export proceeds accounts for receipts from construction services or any other exported goods or services. Foreign-currency proceeds may be held freely in domestic or offshore accounts. The only compliance duty is the Bundesbank's AWV Section 67 statistical reporting trigger of EUR 12,500 or more per cross-border transaction; failure to report is an administrative regulatory offense and is wholly unrelated to convertibility or surrender.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), acting as the national central bank and integral member of the Eurosystem and Target2/T2, with prudential banking supervision and AML conduct oversight additionally shared with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) under the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG) and Geldwäschegesetz (GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as primary treasury anchor, High as disbursement overlay. KWG-licensed neobanks such as N26 Bank GmbH possess a full German banking license and may legally hold operating balances and execute SEPA and SWIFT payments; however, pure electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (e-money licenses issued under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG) passporting into Germany, including Revolut, Qonto, and Penta, may not issue commercial documentary letters of credit, do not provide the same high-value MT103 settlement efficiency, and are not treated by counterparties as equivalent to a German KWG credit institution for contract security or escrow undertakings. For an ISIC 4390 rope-access facade contractor executing high-value progress-billing and trade-backed payments, the structurally recommended blueprint is a KWG-licensed corporate bank (Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank) as the Single Treasury Anchor, supplemented by one regulated EMI (N26 Business or Kontist) limited to sub-threshold SEPA disbursements for staff, subcontractor, and low-value supplier payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The sole shareholder pays the registered share capital (Stammkapital) into a dedicated blocked capital escrow account (Gründungskonto) opened in the name of the UG 'in formation' (UG i.G.); payment denomination must be EUR and the purpose line (Verwendungszweck) must literally state 'Einlagenzahlung / Stammeinlage / Anteile' to permit the notary's confirmation of deposit before Handelsregister registration. For EEA-remitted shareholder contributions, the standard SEPA Credit Transfer (IBAN-to-IBAN) is sufficient; the corporate bank then converts the account to a fully operational account after registration. For a non-EEA shareholder, the remitting bank sends a SWIFT MT103 field 23B CRED, field 50K shareholder legal name and address, field 71A OUR (all charges by sender), and field 72 reference to the Einlage purpose; the receiving German bank performs origin-of-assets and source-of-wealth verification under Sections 10-11 GwG before crediting. Cross-border contribution amounts of EUR 12,500 or more per transaction must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV. No Bundesbank or BaFin prior license, permit, or exchange-control declaration is required for inbound subscription proceeds; the UG's statutory distribution restriction (only 25% of the annual net profit of a UG may be distributed until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000 per the UG legislative scheme) is a company-law constraint, not a foreign-exchange control.Recommended clearing-rail hierarchy for the UG's outbound payment streams: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer / SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for all EUR-denominated payments within the EEA, used for payroll, German subcontractors, and EU vendor supplies, with immediate finality for instant transfers; (2) SWIFT MT103 via the KWG-licensed anchor bank for non-EUR and non-EEA payments, supporting USD, GBP, and CHF supplier settlements; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credit issued by the UG's bank and advised/confirmed by the beneficiary's bank, governed by ICC UCP 600, internationally transmitted via SWIFT MT700, with amendments via MT707) for high-value procurement of tempered glass and structural steel from Asia or other overseas suppliers; (4) Standby letters of credit (SWIFT MT760) posted as performance security for facade contracts or statutory warranty periods under German BGB Section 650a; and (5) for EUR high-value settlements above approximately EUR 5 million, same-day irrevocable settlement via T2 (the Trans-European Automated Real-time Gross settlement Express Transfer system, the successor to TARGET2 as of March 2023) using the anchor bank's central-bank liquidity. All outbound non-EEA transactions of EUR 12,500 or more carry the Bundesbank Section 67 AWV statistical report duty; invoices and proforma contracts should be referenced in the SWIFT field 70 for the bank's compliance review.EUR/USD plain-vanilla foreign-exchange forward contracts with tenors aligned to project progress-billing milestones (typically 6-24 months), executed under a German ISDA or master forward agreement with the anchor bank.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps referencing the Euro Short-Term Rate (EUR €STR) and either USD SOFR or GBP SONIA for structural balance-sheet hedging of multi-year international facade supply contracts.OTC FX options (European-style calls and puts) on USD, GBP, and CNY purchased for risk-management purposes only, subject to mandatory trade and life-cycle reporting under EMIR Refit (ESMA) via a licensed clearing member or reporting delegate.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) solely in third-country emerging-market currencies (e.g., USD/CNH, USD/INR, EUR/TRY) as hedge instruments for sub-supplier contracts denominated in those currencies; direct NDFs in the euro are not applicable or permissible since the euro is a fully convertible deliverable currency.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as primary treasury anchor, High as disbursement overlay. KWG-licensed neobanks such as N26 Bank GmbH possess a full German banking license and may legally hold operating balances and execute SEPA and SWIFT payments; however, pure electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (e-money licenses issued under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG) passporting into Germany, including Revolut, Qonto, and Penta, may not issue commercial documentary letters of credit, do not provide the same high-value MT103 settlement efficiency, and are not treated by counterparties as equivalent to a German KWG credit institution for contract security or escrow undertakings. For an ISIC 4390 rope-access facade contractor executing high-value progress-billing and trade-backed payments, the structurally recommended blueprint is a KWG-licensed corporate bank (Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank) as the Single Treasury Anchor, supplemented by one regulated EMI (N26 Business or Kontist) limited to sub-threshold SEPA disbursements for staff, subcontractor, and low-value supplier payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax amount, resulting in an effective federal CIT rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a typical combined rate of 14%–17% is also imposed on commercial profits, yielding a total effective income tax burden of approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% statutory withholding tax on dividends (25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that 25%). Non-resident shareholders are exempt from trade tax on dividends; the WHT is collected at source by the distributing German company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under §90(3) of the German Fiscal Code (AO) and the Transfer Pricing Documentation Act, a Local File must be prepared for controlled transactions with related companies if the value of the cross-border delivery of goods exceeds EUR 5,000,000 per fiscal year or the value of other services exceeds EUR 500,000 per fiscal year. A Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 100,000,000. Germany's CbC reporting obligation applies to groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be submitted to the tax authorities within 30 days upon request (60 days for extraordinary transactions). Failure to produce documentation may result in a penalty of 5% of the determined income adjustment, capped at EUR 1,000,000, plus reversal of the burden of proof and a tax rate presumption.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to offshore centres. However, the concept of economic substance is embedded in the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under §42 AO, which denies tax benefits that would not exist if the corporate structure or transactions were economically justified. Additionally, the OECD/G20 Multilateral Instrument (MLI) and EU ATAD rules impose substance requirements for treaty and Beneficial Ownership claims. A UG operating rope-access facade services on supertall skyscrapers must demonstrate the presence of business activities, a physical office, actual employees, decision-making infrastructure, and a contractual database to remain taxable-resident and treaty-eligible; absence of substance triggers a reclassification as a shell company under anti-avoidance doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 for misdemeanors and criminal tax evasion penalty under §370 AO; for serious evasion, imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years, plus confiscation of corporate tax assets and exclusion from trade licenses. Additional civil 6% annual interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers face direct criminal liability as principals; unlimited personal liability for civil restitution of the underpaid tax plus penalties; travel bans and significant bail are common; for the UG (haftungsbeschränkt), the corporate veil is pierced if the UG is used deliberately for evasion or insolvency-driven fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Denial of German DTA and EU treaty benefits; adjustment of taxable income to allocate profits to actual economic nexus in foreign jurisdiction; 15.825% withholding tax on constructive dividends; possible trade license revocation; administrative fine up to EUR 25,000; permanent exclusion from public procurement.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate tax if the shell company was stripped of assets; also civil liability for intentionally assisting in the abuse; criminal liability for aiding tax evasion; directors may face a 5-year travel ban and extradition procedures under EU arrest warrant.
AML and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to EUR 1,000,000 for administrative violations for legal entities; higher penalties for supervised businesses; seizure of proceeds; license cancellation under §35 GewO; collateral ban on opening bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors as AML compliance officers are personally liable for administrative fines up to EUR 2.5 million and unlimited damages in civil suits; previous cross-jurisdictional conviction may trigger professional disqualification; repeated violations lead to personal bankruptcy and asset freezing.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 5% of adjusted profit (minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 1,000,000); additional late-documentation penalty of EUR 100 per day up to EUR 1,000,000; shift of burden of proof to the taxpayer; gross-up of royalties; if deemed wilful, criminal tax evasion penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable for penalty amounts if they knowingly caused the documentation omission; individual criminal liability for tax evasion if the failure is part of a broader scheme to shift profits; civil liability for breach of fiduciary duty.
Withholding Tax and Dividend Repatriation Violations

Statutory sanction: Assessed underpaid WHT plus interest (6% annually); the tax authority may shift liability to the corporate entity as the withholding agent; penalty of up to EUR 150,000; potential criminal liability for evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for non-deducted WHT under §44 EStG as withholding agents; restitution liability to the German treasury; subject to travel ban and disclosure obligations; in case of fraud, imprisonment up to 10 years.
