Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

UG minimum share capital is EUR 1 and each contribution must be fully paid in cash or in kind according to the notarial deed before registration; no escrow or blocked-account requirement applies to cash contributions. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve under GmbHG § 5a(3) until registered capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety confirmation is required only for the company's offices or workshop (building fire-protection certificate); staff working on existing substations must obtain site-specific safety inductions, and high-voltage access requires the operating company's authorization; workplaces require compliance with ArbStättV on workstations, and electrical works must follow DGUV V3 inspection and safety training.

Paid-in capital rule: UG minimum share capital is EUR 1 and each contribution must be fully paid in cash or in kind according to the notarial deed before registration; no escrow or blocked-account requirement applies to cash contributions. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve under GmbHG § 5a(3) until registered capital reaches EUR 25,000.

(2) The registered office is adopted as the address stated in the articles of association, replacing all interim business addresses. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; local tax offices (Finanzämter) for income, corporate, trade and wage tax registration; ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) for electronic filing and official data communication.

E-government filing portal

For a German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of formation or commencement of business, obtaining a Steuernummer. For a foreign entity not yet registered, apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. using the application for VAT identification number of non-resident entrepreneurs, submitting the commercial register extract, proof of incorporation, identity of managing directors, and evidence of German VAT nexus. If a permanent establishment is created, register the PE with the competent Finanzamt before commencing activities and obtain a separate Steuernummer for the PE.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German law. German corporate income tax is imposed at entity level under the KStG. Available statutory elections include corporate tax consolidation (Organschaft) under §14 KStG, VAT consolidation under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, the §19 UStG small-business exemption, and certain reorganizational step-up/asset-transfer elections under the UmwStG. Hybrid mismatch treatment is mandatory under §4k EStG and cannot be neutralized by an elective entity classification; the UG is conclusively regarded as a corporation for German tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold triggers German VAT registration for an entrepreneur with a domestic establishment or permanent establishment; all taxable entrepreneurs must register if they regularly supply taxable goods or services in Germany. The §19 UStG small-business exemption applies only where previous-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and current-year expected turnover does not exceed €50,000, but this is an exemption from VAT collection, not an exemption from tax registration. For intra-EU distance sales, the €10,000 annual distance-selling threshold may determine place of supply; foreign businesses supplying services in Germany under the reverse-charge mechanism must still be registered where a German tax liability arises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods (Ausfuhrlieferung) under §6 UStG are zero-rated when the goods physically leave the EU customs territory within three months of dispatch, supplied by the exporting entrepreneur to a third-country recipient or consignee. The supplier must hold export declarations and transport documents evidencing exit from the customs area. B2B services supplied to an entrepreneur established in a third country are generally outside the scope of German VAT where the place of supply is the recipient's country; related services directly connected with exported goods may also benefit from zero-rating or tax-free treatment under §§4 Nr. 1, 4 Nr. 3 UStG and the reverse-charge rules of §13b UStG. Services physically rendered on German real property or at a domestic permanent establishment do not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic ATLAS export declaration or accompanying document with Movement Reference Number)
  • Commercial invoice showing exporter, consignee, Incoterms, value and 6-digit HS classification
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR proving physical exit from the EU customs territory
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice confirming export proceeds received)
  • Packing list and, where applicable, export accompanying document or transport insurance certificate

Germany does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping system comparable to certain other EU Member States. Since 1 January 2025, domestic B2B suppliers must issue and receive electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) in a structured XML format, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x. Transmission via Peppol or direct EDI/API integrations is permitted; there is no mandatory government API pre-clearance or QR code stamping requirement. Full enforcement of supplier-side e-invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions is phased in from 2027–2028.

If zero-rating is claimed without valid export evidence or based on forged or inaccurate documentation, the tax office retrospectively assesses the supply at 19% VAT, denies input VAT recovery, and may claw back any previously granted refund. Late-payment interest accrues under §233a AO at 0.5% per month, and late-filing penalties can be imposed. Intentional tax evasion or the use of fraudulent customs or transport documents triggers criminal prosecution under §370 AO with imprisonment of up to five years, monetary fines, confiscation of unlawful proceeds, and joint and several liability of managing directors and authorised representatives. Voluntary disclosure before detection may mitigate criminal exposure only if the disclosure meets §371 AO requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full euro convertibility on both current and capital account. No central bank approval or licence is required to convert, remit, or receive cross-border funds. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted, subject only to ordinary tax obligations: dividends to a non-resident shareholder suffer 26.375% German withholding tax (25% capital gains tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty or exempted under EU directives and §43b EStG. The only other hurdles are standard anti-money-laundering source-of-funds verification, tax registration formalities, and statistical payment reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio exists under German law. Export proceeds may be retained, sold, or used in any permitted currency. The only statutory obligation is the Deutsche Bundesbank cross-border payment report: any payment received from or made to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported electronically to the Bundesbank under §67 AWV. The report is due monthly, generally by the fifth business day after the month in which the payment was credited or debited. No conversion timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and balance-of-payments statistics authority); the foreign trade and payments regulatory framework is administered jointly with the Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (BMWK) and, for export/import control enforcement, the Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle (BAFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational disbursements only. Non-German or offshore digital EMIs are not full German credit institutions and are generally unacceptable for capital escrow accounts, statutory share-capital deposit verification, notary escrow, or documentary letter-of-credit operations. An EEA-licensed EMI passported into Germany may open e-money accounts denominated in EUR and is useful for small-value supplier payments or travel/expense cards, but the account is not a full current account and funds are not protected by the statutory German deposit guarantee scheme. Treasury policy should therefore maintain the primary corporate account at a licensed German bank and use EMIs solely as pre-funded payment vehicles with low balance caps.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and investor-loan proceeds must move exclusively through licensed credit institutions. For EUR transfers originating inside the SEPA area, use a SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) crediting the German IBAN with remittance field coded as 'Kapitalerhöhung / Beteiligung' and referencing the notarised resolutions. For non-EEA currencies, use SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover) with a clear beneficiary line matching the UG's registered commercial name. The purpose code must align with Deutsche Bundesbank payment-statistics requirements, using the capital/equity contribution category; common bank purpose codes include 'SOD1/Equity capital', 'DRL/Direct investment', or the bank-specific equity capital indicator, plus the explicit textual reference 'SCADA_SUBSTATION_AUTOMATION'. The funds must be described as paid-in capital under §8 GmbHG and remain at the free disposal of management; retain the SWIFT confirmation, MT103 message, correspondent bank account statements, notarised capital increase deed, and the commercial register proof of registration to demonstrate that the receipt is equity, not a disguised loan.For supplier payments for PLC controllers, SCADA modules, switchgear components, and subcontracted integration services: use SEPA Credit Transfer when settlement currency is EUR and the supplier account lies inside the EEA/Single Euro Payments Area; for non-EUR or non-SEPA settlement, issue an SWIFT MT103 cross-border payment with the beneficiary's exact SWIFT/BIC and correspondents' routing details, remittance text citing the pro-forma invoice and purchase order, and the UG's company name and VAT number. For large-value or complex equipment purchases, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) issued through a German bank via SWIFT MT700/MT707, with a confirmed or unconfirmed advising bank at the seller's location; the LC must require full set of clean bills of lading or multimodal transport documents, commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate, and any required inspection certificate meeting the exact Incoterms agreed. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

29.98% average combined rate (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax plus average municipal trade tax of approximately 14.2%; exact trade tax rate varies by municipality).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% German withholding tax on dividends (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) where no DTA relief applies; reduced to 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (15.825% effective) under the Germany-Germany ordinary scenario only where an actual DTA partner state is involved, but for a domestic German UG no dividend distribution to a non-resident shareholder generally attracts the 25%/26.375% withholding tax unless a treaty rate is claimed by the shareholder; for distributions to corporate shareholders within an EU/EEA context the parent-subsidiary directive may apply, reducing to 0% only subject to substantial shareholding and anti-abuse conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 55 million annual related-party cross-border turnover threshold for the OECD Master File requirement; Local File required where aggregate cross-border related-party revenues exceed EUR 5 million and related-party expenses exceed EUR 1 million; German taxpayer with less than EUR 5 million cross-border related-party sales may be exempt from contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation but remains subject to arm's length verification.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the OECD substance-over-form and anti-abuse doctrine (Section 42 Tax Code), the German anti-treaty-abuse provisions, and the EU ATAD substance rules; a UG conducting SCADA substation automation must maintain demonstrable German substance including own operational premises, qualified engineering and administrative staff, real decision-making power in Germany, own equipment, and actual invoicing and contract performance in Germany; the German General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) can disregard artificial structures and allocate income to the substance-holding jurisdiction; the UBO registry (Transparenzregister) substance confirmation and economic employer rules apply for payroll tax and social security; under the directives implementing ATAD, the interest capping rule and controlled foreign company rules may attribute passive income to German shareholders if the UG lacks real German economic activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung under Section 370 Tax Code)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or monetary fine according to the criminal court's discretion (up to EURO 50,000 in minor cases and substantially higher where large tax loss); confiscation order of tax evaded; accumulated late payment interest and penalty surcharges; denial of loss carryforwards and loss of administrative grace.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal criminal prosecution, personal fines, attachment of private assets and possible extradition in serious cases; civil liability under Section 69 of the German Tax Code where taxes were not paid from managed assets; confiscation of personal bank accounts; three-year travel ban and possible passport seizure during criminal investigation.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the entity as a sham under case law and Section 42 Tax Code: contracts disregarded, profits reallocated to German beneficial owner, VAT input credit denied, trade tax and corporate income tax reassessed plus 6% per annum interest; possible withdrawal of trade registration and fines up to EUR 25,000; denial of a DTA residence certificate and exposure to 15% trade tax reallocation for income attributable to non-German acting companies.

Director personal liability: Unlimited joint and several tort liability for false VAT registrations and unpaid taxes; direct tax liability where the UG is a sham means creditors can pierce the veil and the German tax authority will enforce tax liens against personal assets; directors may be banned as managing directors of any German company for up to five years; personal liability for social security contributions evaded.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Thin Capitalization Violation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income based on the arm's length price; decrease of taxable income due to unfavorable documentation failure by 5% of net adjustment, but not less than EUR 5,000; interest backdating up to 6 years with interest surcharge; penalty up to EUR 100,000 for failure to produce documentation; secondary adjustment requiring an additional deemed dividend assessed at 25% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for false documentation declarations and knowable mispricing; management responsibility under Section 34 Tax Code triggers personal enforcement of corporate unpaid taxes; board members may receive personal enforceable tax assessments and can be pursued before the administrative courts; criminal liability for aiding tax evasion in cross-border transfer pricing schemes; restrictions on international travel where unfunded tax liabilities are outstanding.
Anti-Money Laundering and UBO Registry Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 100,000 for minor violations, EUR 800,000 for serious AML violations in commercial transactions, and EUR 2,000,000 for large enterprises; court-ordered dissolution of the UG for grave register falsification; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) can freeze bank accounts; aggravated penalties under Section 261 Criminal Code where assets are criminal proceeds.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability for failing to file UBO declarations; fines on the individual director up to EUR 100,000; imprisonment up to two years for intentional AML obstruction; directors of a German UG held personally liable in civil damages by creditors for inadequate AML systems; curtailment of personal banking privileges and personal travel ban possibility where investigation is in progress.
VAT Fraud / False VAT Accounting for SCADA Works

Statutory sanction: Reversal of input VAT credits, reassessment with 6% annual penalty interest, late filing surcharge, fine up to EUR 40,000 for each VAT misdemeanor; criminal VAT fraud prosecution with imprisonment up to ten years for particularly serious cases involving organized commercial VAT evasion; seizure of construction equipment and real property; revocation of German trade license and prohibition from practicing as SCADA contractor.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are strictly personally liable for unpaid VAT and tax arrears under Section 69 Tax Code where the corporate assets were depleted; directors guilty of VAT evasion face personal prosecution and criminal fines; joint and several civil liability with any de facto shadow director; travel bans, asset freezes, and denial of entry into Schengen area may be enforced in serious cases.
DTA Relief Abuse and Anti-Treaty Shopping Violation

Statutory sanction: Denial of all treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test and German anti-abuse rules; immediate assessment at 26.375% dividend withholding tax or 15.825% royalty withholding tax with penalty interest; supplementary assessment and fines up to EUR 50,000 per calendar year for inaccurate returns; possible recharacterization of royalties as deemed dividends and associated further penalty.

Director personal liability: Residual liability of managing director for any treaty refund granted and subsequently recovered due to abuse, including execution against personal assets; personal criminal exposure for fraudulent treaty claims; loss of good repute and automatic exclusion from serving as commercial director; under certain conditions the foreign shareholder's personal assets may be taxed as German taxable assets through permanent establishment attribution.
