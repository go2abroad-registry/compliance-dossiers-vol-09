Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum stated capital (UG may be formed with a stated capital from EUR 1.00 under Section 5a(1) GmbHG); the full subscribed capital must be confirmed as paid-in and at the free disposal of the managing director before registration, with cash contributions deposited through a German credit institution’s escrow account (Sperrkonto). For a Class III medical device manufacturer, a market-recommended paid-in floor of EUR 25,000 is observed to support product liability, insurance and supply-chain credibility.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT assessment number (Steuernummer); Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern (BZSt) for the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and withholding procedures; Hauptzollamt / Generalzolldirektion (GZD) for EORI and customs export matters. Electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); BZSt Online-Services (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschraenkt) with non-resident shareholders: (1) file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of commercial activity commencement; identify the AG as resident capital company, its managing director(s), shareholders, and expected contract/manufacturing/export activities including Class III drug-eluting stent production and cross-border sales; (2) receive the Steuernummer for corporation tax, trade tax and VAT assessment; (3) apply separately to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. using form USt 1 TU or the ELSTER online procedure; (4) register with GZD for an EORI number (mandatory for export declarations); (5) open a German bank account in the UG's name for SWIFT/Letter of Credit settlement and report payment-receipt statistics to Deutsche Bundesbank. For a foreign entity establishing a permanent establishment/branch, the identical Fragebogen procedure applies under Sec. 138 AO and the branch must be disclosed in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister) before tax registration is completed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; opaque corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) resident in Germany, no pass-through treatment. German tax residence is based on statutory seat or place of management; worldwide income subject to Koerperschaftsteuer 15%, Solidaritaetszuschlag 5.5% of corporate tax, and municipal Gewerbesteuer (3.5% federal base rate multiplied by local Hebesatz of approx. 200%-490%; total effective corporate burden approx. 22%-30%). Distributions to shareholders are subject to 25% Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge (26.375% combined) unless tax treaty relief applies. Losses are computed at entity level and are not available to shareholders; the UG can carry forward losses without expiration (with the Sec. 8c KStG 25% minimum threshold restrictions on share transfers).

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime; the UG cannot elect pass-through for domestic law. Available elections: (1) Organschaft (tax group) under Secs. 14-19 KStG and Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG if financial, economic and organizational integration exists, allowing profit/loss pooling for corporate and trade tax and entity-level VAT consolidation; (2) Kleinunternehmer small-business VAT exemption election under Sec. 19 UStG for turnover below statutory thresholds (not economically relevant for a stent manufacturer; (3) input VAT deduction and partial-exemption pooling options under Sec. 15 UStG; (4) flat-rate VAT for cross-border distance sales via Union OSS / One-Stop-Shop; (5) hybrid mismatch adjustments under Sec. 4k EStG apply mandatorily - no advantageous election is granted; and (6) deferral of trade-tax base amounts under Sec. 7c/7d GewStG for certain assets. Any multi-entity arrangement must respect the EU ATAD requirements and the German transfer pricing documentation rules (Sec. 90 AO).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small businesses (Kleinunternehmer): EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 expected current-year turnover (Sec. 19 UStG as amended from 1 January 2025). Foreign non-established entities: no threshold - any first taxable supply of goods or services in Germany triggers mandatory VAT registration. Intra-Community distance sales of goods to German consumers: EUR 10,000 EU-wide OSS threshold. Domestic B2B medical device sales of Class III coronary drug-eluting stents: standard 19% VAT applies (the stent is a device, not a human-use Arzneimittel, so the 7% reduced rate does not apply); export supplies outside the EU are 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under Sec. 4 No. 1a UStG in conjunction with Sec. 6 UStG when goods (coronary drug-eluting stents) physically leave the EU customs territory in the course of a supply by the UG to a non-EU buyer (e.g., destination markets in Africa/Asia). Eligibility requires: (1) actual cross-border movement of the goods evidenced by transport documents; (2) operative sale or consignment in which title passes to the foreign purchaser; (3) goods leave Germany within the statutory period stated in the export document; (4) compliance with proof-of-export rules under Secs. 8-13 UStDV - normally the electronic customs declaration (ATLAS/AES) with the Ausfuhrbegleitschein, plus Bill of Lading or Air Waybill; (5) the UG is the exporter in the customs meaning; (6) procurement/import of raw materials (cobalt-chromium alloy, bioresorbable polymer, drug substance such as everolimus/sirolimus) is separately documented to support input VAT deduction and the 0% output rate; (7) intra-Community supplies to EU customers are instead exempt under Sec. 4 No. 1b, Sec. 6a UStG only with a valid EU VAT ID and Intra-Community recapitulative statements. The UG may claim a VAT refund on inputs attributable to exports; overclaimed refunds are subject to retrospective correction.

Germany is implementing the E-Rechnung mandate for domestic B2B transactions: since 1 January 2025 every German VAT-registered business must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0.1 or any EN 16931-compliant format); from 1 January 2027 issuers with annual turnover above EUR 800,000 must send E-Rechnungen; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B issuers must send them. No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTR) system is in force; there is no government API stamping for standard B2B invoices. B2G (public-sector procurement) invoicing through the Federal e-Invoicing portal with Peppol/XRechnung has been mandatory since 27 November 2020. The stents manufacturer must additionally maintain full VAT invoice content under Sec. 14 UStG including the USt-IdNr, 0% export indication, and reference to the applicable export document.

German tax authorities conduct systematic export-proof audits (Ausfuhrnachweispruefung). Failure to provide complete and consistent export evidence within the statutory retention period (7 years, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud) leads to retrospective reversal (clawback) of the 0% rate and invoiced output VAT assessed at 19% plus interest at 0.5% per month under Sec. 233a AO. Negligent or intentional misdeclaration triggers penalties under Sec. 378 AO (up to EUR 50,000) or criminal prosecution under Sec. 370 AO (tax evasion: imprisonment from 6 months up to 5 years, or up to 10 years for deliberate fraudulent export chains). Sec. 26b UStG imposes joint and several liability on persons exploiting fraudulent VAT loop schemes; Sec. 25d UStG permits liability claims against remuneration agents. The Bundesbank AWV reporting obligation requires quarterly/transactional reporting of export receipts above EUR 12,500; undeclared receipts may result in regulatory fines (Bußgeld) and adverse transfer-pricing adjustments. Repatriation of proceeds beyond 180 days without documented commercial justification (e.g., secured payment terms, Letter of Credit discrepancies) is treated as a red flag for fictitious exports and withheld input VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account regime; the euro is fully convertible with no exchange-control restrictions, no prior authorisation requirement for inward or outward transfers, and no limitation on profit remittances. Dividends and shareholder loans from the UG can be distributed abroad after confirming corporate income tax withholding (Kapitalertragsteuer plus solidarity surcharge, if applicable) and, where relevant, treaty-based relief. There is no practical obstacle for a German UG in the coronary drug-eluting stent sector to receive foreign venture capital, import production machinery, export devices, or maintain foreign-currency balances. The only requirement is the cross-border balance-of-payments reporting obligation under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV) for payments exceeding €12,500, which does not constitute a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any export proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or statutory timeline. Exporters are permitted to retain proceeds in any freely convertible currency and may remit them to resident bank accounts or keep them in offshore accounts subject to tax transparency obligations. The UG manufacturing coronary stents was therefore fully entitled, ab initio, to invoice in USD, EUR, or CNY and to convert receipts at market rates without prior approval from the Deutsche Bundesbank. The only compliance trigger is the AWV reporting of each export payment above €12,500, which is performed on an aggregated basis through the Bundesbank electronic reporting portal.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (as the monetary authority and balance-of-payments statistics gateway) acting in coordination with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for bank licensing, anti-money-laundering enforcement, and financial regulatory oversight under the German Banking Act (KWG) and Anti-Money-Laundering Act (GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex cannot serve as the primary financial infrastructure for a German UG engaged in Class III coronary drug-eluting stent manufacturing. Although some EMIs hold an e-money licence under PSD2 and can offer an IBAN, they are not credit institutions and cannot issue documentary credits, open standby L/Cs, provide working-capital loans, or maintain escrow arrangements for product components. In addition, the high-risk medical-device classification and the expected transaction volumes cause enhanced AML screening to be outsourced or repeatedly suspended, resulting in unacceptable clearing delays. The UG should therefore rely on a full-service German bank with international SWIFT correspondence and maintain only an ancillary EMI account for low-value, non-commercial payments, if necessary.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax under the German Corporate Income Tax Act, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective corporate-level income tax burden of 15.825% before trade tax. Municipal trade tax generally produces a combined effective burden of approximately 29.8% to 33.3%, depending on the municipal trade-tax multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax on dividends, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. A domestic exemption may apply to qualifying corporate recipients under the EU Parent-Subsidiary regime or other statutory provisions, subject to minimum participation, holding-period, anti-abuse, substance and procedural requirements. Treaty rates commonly reduce the dividend withholding tax to 5%, 10% or 15%, depending on the applicable treaty and ownership conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a German resident entity, local-file documentation is generally required for extraordinary business transactions where the annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties and similar transactions. A master file is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100 million where cross-border related-party transactions exist. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Documentation may be requested even below thresholds where the tax authority considers it necessary.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the German general anti-abuse rule, treaty anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership requirements, EU Parent-Subsidiary anti-abuse rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules and transfer-pricing requirements. A German UG should maintain genuine decision-making, qualified personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure appropriate to its business, accounting records, commercial risk control, bank-account control, board activity and evidence that transactions have a real business purpose. A low-substance conduit, artificial royalty structure, circular financing arrangement or contract manufacturer lacking decision-making and risk control may be challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate False Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax must be reassessed with interest, and confiscation or extended confiscation may apply. Participating persons can be liable as perpetrators, accomplices or aiders.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal tax liability for culpable breach of tax duties under section 69 of the Fiscal Code, including failure to file, collect or pay taxes. Criminal liability, civil restitution, asset seizure and insolvency claims may arise. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty but may occur through criminal or insolvency proceedings in legally justified cases; imprisonment is possible after criminal conviction.
VAT Fraud and Carousel Transactions

Statutory sanction: VAT evasion may result in criminal penalties of up to five years' imprisonment or fines, with aggravated cases subject to higher imprisonment ranges. Input VAT may be denied, VAT assessments and interest imposed, and unlawfully obtained refunds recovered.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT where they breached supervisory, filing or payment duties. Criminal investigation, asset freezing, director disqualification and insolvency-related claims may follow.
Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: The German payer may remain liable for the tax that should have been withheld, together with interest and possible administrative or criminal sanctions. Intentional evasion may constitute tax evasion; negligent failures may constitute an administrative offense or tax liability.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can face personal liability for culpable failure to withhold or remit tax, particularly where payment controls and treaty documentation were knowingly disregarded.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make adjustments, impose additional corporate income tax, trade tax and withholding tax, and apply documentation-related surcharges. A transfer-pricing documentation surcharge may generally range from 5% to 10% of the adjustment amount, subject to statutory minimums and caps, where legally applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for deliberate or culpable tax loss, criminal exposure if the pricing was knowingly fraudulent, and shareholder or insolvency claims for value transfers lacking corporate benefit.
Hidden Profit Distribution and Capital Preservation Breach

Statutory sanction: The payment may be reclassified as a hidden profit distribution, denied as a deduction, subject to withholding tax and treated as taxable income to the recipient. Breaches of UG capital-preservation rules may create repayment obligations and insolvency consequences.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may owe restitution to the UG, face liability under corporate capital-preservation and insolvency rules, and incur criminal liability for fraudulent conduct or delayed insolvency filing.
Insolvency and Wrongful Trading

Statutory sanction: Mandatory insolvency filing duties apply when the company is illiquid or over-indebted, subject to statutory filing periods. Breaches may trigger criminal penalties, repayment claims, damages, fines and disqualification consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments after insolvency, tax and social-security failures, creditor losses and delayed-filing damages. Imprisonment is possible for criminal insolvency offenses.
AML, Beneficial Ownership and Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Violations may produce substantial administrative fines under the Money Laundering Act, transaction restrictions, confiscation and criminal penalties for money laundering or sanctions violations. Sanctions offenses may involve significant fines and imprisonment depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for intentional participation or negligent control failures, face asset seizure and professional disqualification, and become subject to travel restrictions where ordered by competent authorities.
Medical-Device Regulatory and Product-Safety Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory authorities may prohibit manufacture or sale, order recalls, seize products, impose administrative fines and pursue criminal penalties for serious violations. Tax deductions and commercial income treatment may be affected by unlawful or sham operations.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal, civil product-liability and regulatory exposure for knowingly placing non-compliant medical devices on the market, including damages, professional disqualification and imprisonment in serious cases.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax benefits may be denied under anti-abuse rules, treaty relief refused, income reallocated and withholding tax assessed. Artificial arrangements may trigger tax evasion proceedings where facts were intentionally misrepresented.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur tax, civil and criminal liability for knowingly maintaining an artificial structure, including restitution, asset seizure, insolvency claims, disqualification and imprisonment after conviction.
