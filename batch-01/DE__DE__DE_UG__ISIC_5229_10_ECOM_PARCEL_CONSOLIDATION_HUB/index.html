Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG (haftungsbeschränkt) may be formed with registered share capital from EUR 1. At least 25% of the nominal amount of each cash share subscription must be evidenced as paid into the company's escrowed business bank account before filing the registration application; a minimum contribution of EUR 1 per share is sufficient. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt; www.bzst.de) for VAT identification number (USt-IdNr), OSS/IOSS and non-resident registration; locally competent Finanzamt for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT assessment, and the statutory Steuernummer; Federal Customs Directorate (Generalzolldirektion – GZD; www.zoll.de) for EORI and ATLAS export declarations; e-filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned UG: (1) notarize the articles of association, subscribe at least EUR 1 of registered capital (Euro 1 UG; EUR 25,000 if structured as a GmbH from the outset), and file for entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister); (2) submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the locally competent Finanzamt via ELSTER within one month of business start, declaring the managing director, beneficial owners, ISIC 5229 business scope, and place of effective management; (3) obtain the statutory corporation tax number (Steuernummer) from the Finanzamt; (4) apply separately to the BZSt for the cross-border EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr) and, where applicable, OSS/IOSS registration; (5) obtain a German EORI number (DE + 9 digits) from the GZD for customs declarations and ATLAS export clearances. Foreign principals without a German establishment use the non-resident VAT registration form (USt 1 TU via ELSTER) and may be exposed to a permanent establishment under Art. 5 OECD MTC if the UG is a dependent agency of the foreign shareholder.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime; the UG is mandatorily taxed at entity level under KStG and GewStG. Available statutory elections: (a) corporation-tax and trade-tax Organschaft under Sec. 14 KStG / Sec. 2(2) GewStG through a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with a German corporate parent; (b) VAT Organschaft under Sec. 2(2)(2) UStG where the UG is financially, economically, and organizationally integrated into the controlling entrepreneur, so the hub's supplies are attributed to the controlling company; (c) small-business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG (turnover caps of EUR 22,000 prior year / EUR 50,000 current year) – generally inadvisable for an export hub because input VAT recovery and USt-IdNr issuance are lost; (d) hybrid-mismatch documentation and adjustments under Sec. 4k EStG (ATAD 2); (e) interest-stripping (Zinsschranke) carry-forward election under Sec. 6a EStG; (f) group VAT refund procedures under Sec. 2a UStG for foreign taxable persons. No classification statement is filed with the BZSt; the UG is per se a separate taxed corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for established German entrepreneurs: registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply (Sec. 2, 18 UStG); the optional Sec. 19 UStG small-trader exemption applies only below EUR 22,000 prior-year / EUR 50,000 current-year turnover and blocks VAT ID issuance. Non-resident foreign distance sellers face a single pan-EU EUR 10,000 distance-sales threshold (Sec. 3c UStG / Art. 59c Directive) for intra-Community distance sales and digital services; third-country low-value consignment sellers must register or use IOSS without a threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) export dispatches of goods to third-country destinations under Sec. 4(1)(a) and Sec. 6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the seller holds the customs-validated export proof; (b) intra-Community supplies to a foreign entrepreneur under Sec. 4(1)(b) and Sec. 6a UStG, requiring the recipient's valid USt-IdNr, physical dispatch to another Member State, and inclusion in the EC Sales List; (c) transport and freight-consolidation services directly connected with export or cross-border transport under Sec. 4(3) UStG, where the carriage begins or ends outside Germany or the recipient is a foreign entrepreneur. For the parcel-consolidation hub, mere sorting, temporary warehousing, and handling performed before the goods are placed at the carrier's disposal are NOT transport-ancillary and are taxable at 19%; the hub must map each service to the underlying export chain and collect declarations from the buyer/forwarder to sustain 0% treatment.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) filed electronically through ATLAS with MRN (Movement Reference Number) and release confirmation
  • EORI number certificate (DE + 9 digits) and, if applicable, authorized-consignor T1/T2 transit authorization of the consolidation hub
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with full HS classification and customs value
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving physical departure of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) in the form of the customs-endorsed declaration, ATLAS status output, or exporter confirmation per Sec. 8 UStG
  • Buyer's purchase order, forwarding instructions, and transport contract linking the hub's consolidation/linehaul services to the export supply
  • Inward remittance certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or account statement) evidencing collection of the foreign-currency export receivable
  • EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and validated VAT ID of the recipient for intra-Community zero-rating claims
  • Warehouse inbound/outbound movement records (TI/TE or equivalent) showing that the parcel left the consolidation hub into an export movement

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) implemented by the Growth Opportunities Act: from 1 January 2025 every German resident business must be able to receive structured e-invoices in EN 16931 formats (XInvoice 2.2, ZUGFeRD 2.2.2/2.3, or EDI conforming to EN 16931); from 1 January 2027 residents with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue such invoices to domestic B2B recipients; from 1 January 2028 all German resident businesses must issue e-invoices. Transmission via Peppol network, DE-Mail, or interoperable interfaces is permitted. Germany operates a post-audit model: there is no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory Finanzamt API stamping, and no digital stamp/signature requirement for invoice authenticity; XML records must be retained for 10 years under Sec. 147 AO. B2C simplified invoices and foreign trade invoices remain governed by the general UStG invoicing rules.

The German tax administration audits export zero-rating through documentary checks of the export proof and the payment trail. If the Ausfuhrnachweis, transport documents, or remittance evidence are missing, incorrect, or not retained for 10 years (Sec. 147 AO), the Finanzamt recharacterizes the zero-rated supply as a domestic taxable supply at 19% VAT, denies or reduces the corresponding input VAT deduction under Sec. 26b UStG, and issues a supplemental VAT assessment with 6%-per-annum default interest under Sec. 233a AO. An administrative penalty of up to EUR 25,000 applies under Sec. 26c UStG where the failure creates a risk of tax evasion; intentional false zero-rating is punishable under Sec. 370 AO by imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years for aggravated commercial fraud), extends the assessment period from 4 to 7 years (10 years in extreme cases), and precludes the record-retention defence. The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is jointly and severally liable for VAT shortfalls under Sec. 69/71 AO and GmbH transfer-of-assets rules; cross-border data matching of parcel flows and customs declarations under the 2021 EU e-commerce VAT package and DAC 7 enables retrospective clawback of input VAT on warehousing and linehaul costs when the output-side export substantiation fails.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible, and Germany generally imposes no exchange controls or restrictions on current-account or capital-account transfers. A German UG may receive and remit funds domestically and internationally, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, banking documentation, tax obligations, and applicable statistical reporting. Dividend or other profit distributions to non-residents are not subject to a general foreign-exchange approval or surrender requirement; they remain subject to corporate-law distribution rules, available distributable profits, withholding-tax rules, and any applicable treaty relief or refund process. A distribution must be properly authorized and documented. Cross-border payments may trigger AWV reporting, which is statistical and is not, by itself, a requirement to obtain prior approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio for export receipts. Proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts, subject to the UG's accounting, tax, sanctions, and bank-compliance obligations. AWV external-payments reporting may apply to reportable payments between German residents and non-residents, including qualifying receipts and payments; the general reporting threshold is an amount exceeding EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exceptions and current Bundesbank instructions. The reporting obligation is generally discharged by the resident payer or recipient, as applicable, using the Bundesbank's prescribed electronic process. Confirm the current threshold, exemptions, reporting party, and deadline for each payment with the Bundesbank or a German adviser.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and the principal authority for external payments statistics and foreign-exchange reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banks and relevant financial-services providers. Germany applies the EU single-market and euro-area framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium to High for routine EUR collections and payments if the provider accepts German UGs, the business model, and the ultimate beneficial owners. Airwallex may be considered as a supplementary cross-border payment provider, subject to its current German and EU permissions, product terms, safeguarding arrangements, supported currencies, and onboarding approval. An electronic-money institution account is not equivalent to a German bank deposit account; safeguarding is not the same as statutory deposit protection. Do not rely on an offshore EMI as the sole operating or statutory banking channel. Verify the provider's authorization in the relevant regulator's register and confirm that the account supports the required legal-entity name, payment rails, currencies, and counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital contributions from an account in the shareholder's legal name to the UG's account designated for the contribution, using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the relevant SEPA area or a SWIFT customer credit transfer for other corridors and currencies. State the precise purpose, such as 'share capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan', and identify the contributor and UG; do not label a loan as equity or vice versa. Retain the subscription and corporate approvals, bank evidence of receipt, source-of-funds documents, and accounting records. Coordinate the timing and evidence with the notary and commercial-register filing where the contribution relates to formation or a capital increase. Check whether the cross-border payment is reportable under the AWV; reporting is separate from any bank purpose description, and Germany does not use a universal mandatory FX purpose-code system for ordinary incoming transfers. Do not use third-party accounts or split payments to avoid screening or reporting.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT customer credit transfers (commonly MT103 messaging, or the applicable ISO 20022 format) for non-SEPA or foreign-currency settlements. Include accurate beneficiary, bank, invoice, contract, and payment-reference details; provide supporting documents when requested by the bank. Use commercial documentary credits, including an irrevocable commercial letter of credit where commercially appropriate, subject to the issuing and advising banks' terms and applicable rules such as UCP 600 if incorporated. For recurring carrier, warehouse, and supplier payments, use controlled payee setup, dual authorization, invoice matching, and reconciliation. Apply sanctions screening and assess AWV reporting for reportable cross-border payments; retain invoices, contracts, shipment evidence, and payment confirmations. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax, plus trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates typically 14–17% effective, resulting in a combined effective rate of approximately 30%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax (total 26.375%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed €5 million for goods or €500,000 for services; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to UAE, but the general anti-abuse rule (Section 42 AO) and treaty abuse provisions (e.g., Principal Purpose Test under MLI) require genuine economic substance. A UG must demonstrate real business activity, premises, employees, and decision-making in Germany to claim treaty benefits and avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years (Section 370 AO). Additional civil penalties up to 100% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they intentionally or negligently caused the evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential extradition in severe cases.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, additional tax assessments, and penalties up to 50% of the tax adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for taxes and penalties if they orchestrated the sham structure; potential criminal charges for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of up to 50% of the adjustment amount (Section 162 AO), plus late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the additional tax and penalties if they failed to ensure proper documentation and pricing; criminal charges for tax evasion if intent is proven.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for serious violations (GwG); imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering (StGB §261).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; civil liability for damages; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
