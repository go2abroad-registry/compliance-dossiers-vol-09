Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG is a juridical person subject to unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht (worldwide residence taxation) as a corporation: 15% Körperschaftsteuer, 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporate income tax, and municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at a statutory multiplier typically 200%-490% depending on municipality. No pass-through election is available in German domestic law.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available elections: (i) ertragsteuerliche Organschaft under §14 KStG (corporate tax consolidation) requiring financial, economic, and organizational integration with a resident controlling head company; (ii) umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft under §2(2) UStG (VAT grouping) for consolidated VAT compliance; (iii) hybrid mismatch mandatory adjustments under §4k EStG (ATAD implementation) with no elective opt-out; (iv) conversion of the UG into a fully paid GmbH under §5a GmbHG by capital increase; (v) treaty-based dividend withholding reduction under §32a KStG upon non-resident ownership, with 26.375% headline withholding on dividends (incl. solidarity surcharge).

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries to third-country destinations are zero-rated under §6 UStG if the entrepreneur proves export through the Buch- und Belegnachweis (record- and documentary-evidence rules under §§8, 13 UStDV) confirmed by ATLAS customs release; intra-EU supplies are zero-rated under §6a UStG upon recipient's valid VAT ID and transport evidence; ancillary services directly connected to exported goods (lyophilization, cold-chain logistics, sterility testing, customs brokerage) may qualify for zero-rating under §7 UStG; supplies into customs warehouses or German free zones qualify as deemed exports. The zero-rated turnover must be declared separately in the VAT return (Kennziffer 41/44 UStVA). German VAT law does not condition zero-rating on FX repatriation or a fixed inward-remittance deadline; the 180-day indicator in this dossier represents documentary and accounting best practice for export proof retention, IFRS revenue recognition, and transfer-pricing consistency.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under EU freedom of capital movement. Cross-border dividends, profit remittances, service fees, trade settlements, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred without prior exchange-control approval. Currency convertibility is not restricted in law or practice for EUR or freely traded foreign currencies. Documentary or regulatory checks relate only to statute-of-limitations, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and product-specific licensing; they do not create a material remittance hurdle. For the botulinum toxin business, the controlling factor is the pharmaceutical-licensing dossier, not foreign-exchange convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement under German law. Export receipts need not be repatriated, credited to a German bank account, or converted into EUR. The exporter may freely hold foreign currency in offshore accounts, use the proceeds directly to finance imports, or sell them in the interbank market. The only compliance duty is statistical: cross-border payments and foreign claims/liabilities exceeding EUR 12,500 per reporting period must be captured and reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV, using the applicable balance-of-payments codes.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), acting as the central bank and competent authority for balance-of-payments statistics; overall foreign-exchange regulation is liberalized under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz - AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), supported by BaFin for banking conduct. No separate foreign-exchange control commission exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW-MEDIUM for offshore digital EMIs. An offshore fintech EMI may be used only as a secondary collection vehicle in specific payment corridors and is not acceptable as a primary treasury bank. Legal constraints: the EMI must be fully licensed or passportable within the EU/EEA under E-Money Directive and supervised by BaFin or a home-state authority; an unlicensed offshore EMI is prohibited. More importantly, an EMI cannot support capital escrow, cold-chain wire clearing, commercial letters of credit, pharmacy-payment identification, or sanctions-screening depth required for botulinum toxin trade. The UG must therefore maintain its primary clearing, escrow, and trade-finance accounts at a full EU/EEA-licensed commercial bank.

Legal constraints: the EMI must be fully licensed or passportable within the EU/EEA under E-Money Directive and supervised by BaFin or a home-state authority; an unlicensed offshore EMI is prohibited. More importantly, an EMI cannot support capital escrow, cold-chain wire clearing, commercial letters of credit, pharmacy-payment identification, or sanctions-screening depth required for botulinum toxin trade. The UG must therefore maintain its primary clearing, escrow, and trade-finance accounts at a full EU/EEA-licensed commercial bank.
