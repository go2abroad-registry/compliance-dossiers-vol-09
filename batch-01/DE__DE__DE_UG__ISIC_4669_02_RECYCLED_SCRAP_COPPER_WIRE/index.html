Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register), held by the local Amtsgericht (Local Court) in the district of the registered office.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), notably §§ 1, 2, 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft), 7, 8, 39 and 40; supplemented by the German Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 106-108 for registration and publication.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation including the articles of association or statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll), followed by the electronic registration application with the Handelsregister.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 31 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under § 5a GmbHG, the UG has no statutory minimum above EUR 1.00; it may be formed with share capital below EUR 25,000, but the subscribed capital must be at least EUR 1.00 and fully paid in cash before registration. Payment is made to a bank account opened in the name of the company with proof of deposit submitted for notarial verification; no court-designated escrow account is required for cash contributions, but the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until reaching the EUR 25,000 threshold of an ordinary GmbH.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (Brandschutzzertifikat) from the local building authority based on a structural inspection of the warehouses and processing area, ensuring compliance with German Fire Protection Regulations (MBO/VV-TB). Structural load inspection by a certified statics engineer is required for storage racks and shear/crusher installations. Occupational safety clearance from the local Gewerbeaufsichtsamt includes proof of first-aid equipment, spill containment for oils and coolants, and compliance with the Industrial Safety Ordinance (BetrSichV) for machinery guarding.

Paid-in capital rule: Under § 5a GmbHG, the UG has no statutory minimum above EUR 1.00; it may be formed with share capital below EUR 25,000, but the subscribed capital must be at least EUR 1.00 and fully paid in cash before registration. Payment is made to a bank account opened in the name of the company with proof of deposit submitted for notarial verification; no court-designated escrow account is required for cash contributions, but the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until reaching the EUR 25,000 threshold of an ordinary GmbH.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, that the company confirms the subscription of the entire issued share capital by the founding shareholders and declares the statutory paid-up capital to be fully deposited in the company's bank account. RESOLVED FURTHER, that the registered office specified in the articles of association is adopted as the company's legal seat and official service address. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification number issuance; responsible local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration; ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) as the official digital filing portal.

E-government filing portal

The UG must file the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with its competent local Finanzamt to obtain a Steuernummer (corporate tax number), which covers corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT. Thereafter, the UG must apply to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) using the online form through the BZSt portal (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). A non-resident shareholder entity must additionally submit the relevant foreign-entity declaration forms and evidence of its beneficial ownership; processing generally takes two to four weeks. A valid Steuernummer and USt-IdNr. are prerequisites for customs clearances, reverse-charge invoicing, and export zero-rating substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a check-the-box regime comparable to the United States. Available elections include: (a) VAT Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG, permitting the UG to be consolidated within a controlling parent's VAT registration; (b) elective small-business VAT relief under Section 19 UStG subject to turnover ceilings, which is practically unavailable for cross-border metal trading; (c) elections concerning trade tax loss carry-forward and real-estate-transfer-tax grouping; and (d) formal designation of the tax representative and customs representation relationships. Anti-hybrid rules under the ATAD implementation (Section 4k EStG, Section 10a KStG) and CFC rules under Sections 7-13 AStG restrict hybrid mismatch benefits, and no pass-through status can be elected for the UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold exempts a domestic incorporated entity from mandatory VAT registration when it undertakes taxable supplies. For sole proprietors and unincorporated small businesses, the Section 19 UStG small-business relief applies where previous-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is projected not to exceed EUR 100,000 (with effect from 2025). A UG engaged in cross-border recycled copper wire trading must register regardless of turnover level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies are exempt with input VAT deduction (economic zero-rating) under Section 4 No. 1a in conjunction with Section 6 UStG where the UG or a commissioned carrier transports the goods outside the EU customs territory. Eligibility requires conclusive proof that (i) the physical goods departed Germany and the EU, (ii) the purchaser or consignee is resident in a third country, (iii) the transport documents (CMR, bill of lading, or air waybill) and customs export declaration (ATLAS-generated MRN) are consistent with the commercial invoice, and (iv) any EU transit procedure is properly discharged. For consignments routed through another member state, an exit confirmation or transit certificate must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS-Ausfuhranmeldung) with MRN
  • Export Accompanying Document and Exit Confirmation (Beförderungs-/Ausfuhranmeldung) evidencing departure from the EU
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill covering the full route to the third country
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or documentary credit/SWIFT TT evidence showing the consideration flow
  • Proof of export in accordance with Sections 8-10 UStDV, including commissionaire declarations where applicable

Germany mandates domestic B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) from 1 January 2025 in an EN 16931-conformant XML structure (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD hybrid); issuance obligations phase in for businesses with turnover above EUR 800,000 from 1 January 2027 and for all taxable persons from 1 January 2028. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime; however, cash-register systems must incorporate a certified Technical Security System (TSE) and all accounting records must be retained in a GoBD-compliant format for ten years. Supplies of recycled non-ferrous scrap metal, including shredded copper wire, are subject to mandatory reverse charge under Section 13b UStG for domestic transactions; invoices must state the reverse-charge basis and the recipient's VAT identification number and must not show separate VAT.

Where export zero-rating is claimed without sufficient documentary evidence or is found fraudulent, the tax authority retroactively disallows the VAT exemption and assesses 19% VAT on the full consideration together with interest of 6% per annum under Section 233a AO, denies input VAT deduction, and may impose penalty surcharges up to EUR 25,000 under Sections 26a and 26b UStG. Tax evasion under Section 370 AO carries imprisonment of up to five years or fines, rising to up to ten years in aggravated cases. Managing directors and responsible officer holders may incur personal liability, and administrative offenses under Section 30 OWiG expose the UG itself to entity-level fines; customs and foreign-trade law violations are prosecuted cumulatively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro and has full current-account and capital-account convertibility within the applicable European Union legal framework. There is no general German exchange-control approval requirement for foreign investment, dividend payments, loan repayments, service fees, or trade settlements. Profit remittances by a UG are subject to distributable-profit rules, preparation and approval of annual accounts, preservation of statutory reserves, insolvency-law restrictions, withholding-tax analysis, treaty relief procedures, and bank-level sanctions, AML, and source-of-funds checks. A UG must generally allocate 25% of annual surplus, reduced by loss carryforwards, to its statutory reserve until the reserve and share capital reach the GmbH minimum share-capital level. Cross-border payments remain subject to German anti-money-laundering rules, EU sanctions, export-control rules, tax documentation, and statistical reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a German bank and no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory conversion deadline for export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in euros or foreign currency accounts, including accounts with banks in other European Economic Area jurisdictions, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, AML, and bank contractual requirements. Cross-border payment and trade-statistics reports may be required to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV reporting rules, subject to applicable thresholds, exemptions, filing deadlines, and electronic reporting procedures. The bank should confirm the current reporting threshold and exemption status for each payment because Bundesbank reporting requirements may change and are transaction-specific.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the Eurosystem and under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-trade restrictions and sanctions are additionally administered by the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle, BAFA), the European Union, and the German customs authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for secondary payment operations and treasury support; low as the sole primary bank for a German UG conducting physical cross-border scrap trading. An electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, foreign-exchange conversion, and international transfers within its license perimeter, but it is not necessarily a credit institution, may not provide full deposit-taking or documentary-LC capability, and may impose transaction, corridor, currency, or safeguarding restrictions. Offshore digital EMIs must be appropriately authorized in the relevant jurisdiction and, for EU passporting, satisfy applicable EU notification and local-law requirements. The UG must retain complete beneficial-owner, transaction-purpose, invoice, transport, customs, counterparty, sanctions, and source-of-funds records. High-volume commodity flows, third-country counterparties, cash-like transactions, rapid pass-through activity, and mismatches between business registration and payment activity may trigger enhanced due diligence, payment holds, or account closure.
