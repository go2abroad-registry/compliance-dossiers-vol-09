Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr) and EU-wide tax data exchange; the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT assessments; federal policy and oversight by the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF); electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) with structured XML interfaces; customs, excise and export supervision by the Generalzolldirektion (GZD) through the ATLAS customs IT system.

E-government filing portal

After notarial incorporation and entry in the commercial register, the UG files the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt via ELSTER; the Finanzamt assigns a single fiscal Steuernummer covering corporation tax, trade tax and VAT. The UG must then apply separately to BZSt for a USt-IdNr (EU VAT identification number) for cross-border and intra-EU invoicing. For plant and equipment importation or export operations, the UG must obtain an EORI number through the GZD customs portal. Foreign resident shareholders or managers must appoint a domestic authorized recipient for tax service (Zustellungsbevollmächtigter); monthly VAT returns (UStVA), recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and annual tax returns are mandatory electronic filings. The business also requires sectoral registration with the competent food safety authority as a food business operator under Regulation (EC) 852/2004 and the German Lebensmittel- und Futtermittelgesetzbuch (LFGB).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election is recognized for a German UG; the UG is mandatorily a resident corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. Available domestic elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) with a qualifying parent for VAT, corporation tax and trade tax consolidation, requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement, financial and economic integration; (ii) the option under Section 9 UStG to treat otherwise VAT-exempt real estate supplies as taxable; (iii) elections under the interest restriction (Zinsschranke) and loss carry-forward provisions; (iv) customs facilitations such as authorized economic operator status and inward/outward processing procedures; and (v) the statutory classification of foodstuffs, including plant-based beverages, at the reduced VAT rate of 7% rather than the standard rate. Hybrid mismatch restrictions under Section 4k EStG and EU ATAD rules preclude entity-character arbitrage in cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general VAT registration turnover threshold for resident incorporated entities; the UG is required to register from the first taxable supply, so the effective threshold is EUR 0. The small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Section 19 UStG, applying to prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000, is economically unavailable to an industrial UHT oat-milk manufacturing plant. Non-resident entities without a domestic establishment are registrable whenever their supplies are subject to German VAT under the place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations are zero-rated with full input VAT credit under Section 4(1)(a) in conjunction with Section 6 UStG, provided the goods are physically removed from the EU customs territory in a business context and the exporter obtains reliable proof of exit. Intra-EU dispatches of goods are zero-rated under Section 4(1)(b) and Section 6a UStG when the recipient is an entrepreneur in another Member State holding a valid VAT ID and the transport to the other Member State is substantiated. Offshore services supplied to non-EU entrepreneurs, and auxiliary services directly linked to exported goods, are also zero-rated under Section 4(1)(b) UStG in line with the place-of-supply rules of Section 3a and Section 3e UStG. The exporter must maintain the documentary and accounting evidence required by Sections 8 to 14 UStDV, including a declaration by the forwarder or carrier confirming the goods left the EU territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronically lodged in ATLAS, with assigned MRN for the outbound movement)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or CMR international consignment note evidencing dispatch from the EU exit point
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis / Abgangsnachweis) issued by the relevant customs office or approved consignor/carrier
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the export contract, Incoterms, and the MRN of the customs export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank transfer confirmation evidencing foreign-currency settlement of the export receivable
  • For intra-EU dispatches: validation of the customer's USt-IdNr via the VIES system together with the recapitulative statement (ZM) record and transport evidence

Germany does not operate a real-time tax-authority clearance API or a national digital stamping system. Instead, the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz) introduces phased B2B structured e-invoicing: sellers must receive XRechnung or ZUGFeRD (CEN EN 16931) e-invoices from 1 January 2025; businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue compliant e-invoices from 1 January 2027; all businesses must issue e-invoices from 1 January 2028. Export and import declarations are processed exclusively through the ATLAS customs system with EORI validation; Intrastat declarations and EU recapitulative VAT statements (ZM) are electronic. Where on-site retail or factory-shop sales occur, certified cash-register software with a TSE technical security module is mandatory.

The Finanzamt and GZD may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and claw back input VAT where the export evidence, transport documents, or accounting records are missing, internally inconsistent, or fraudulent. The VAT at 19% is then reassessed from the original due date with late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum). The regular tax assessment period is four years, extended to ten years in cases of tax evasion (Section 169 AO). Breaches of documentary and accounting duties constitute administrative offences punishable by fines up to EUR 30,000 (Section 26a UStG). Intentional evasion or systematic export fraud is criminal tax evasion under Section 370 AO, carrying imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated commercial cases. Customs duty debt may be revived against the declarant under Articles 77 to 79 of the Union Customs Code. An invalid invoice exposes the recipient to liability under Section 25d UStG, and incorrect Intrastat or ZM filings carry separate administrative penalties. Although German tax law does not itself impose an explicit 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day standard represents the usual trade-financing and settlement convention; failure to repatriate export proceeds within that window may trigger transfer-pricing, sham-transaction or bad-debt scrutiny, and consequently jeopardize the zero-rating claim if the evidence suggests the consideration was not collected.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. However, cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV reporting). No prior approval is required for capital inflows or outflows, but documentation must be retained for audit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border transactions exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank via the AWV reporting system, typically through the bank, within five business days. No conversion ratio is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; foreign exchange controls are liberalized under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Legal constraints: Fintech EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) licensed under PSD2 (e.g., N26, Penta, Qonto) can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services. However, for a manufacturing entity with significant trade flows and LC requirements, traditional banks are preferred due to their established correspondent banking network and trade finance capabilities. EMIs may lack full LC issuance and discounting services. Additionally, some EMIs impose restrictions on high-volume transactions or require additional compliance for UHT plant operations. For a UG with low capital, EMIs may offer easier onboarding but with higher transaction fees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers. The purpose code must be 'Capital Contribution' (e.g., 'S001' for equity injection) or 'Shareholder Loan' (e.g., 'S002'). For equity, a notarized capital increase resolution and commercial register filing (Handelsregister) are required. For loans, a formal loan agreement with interest terms must be in place. The receiving bank will verify the purpose code and may request supporting documentation. For amounts above €12,500, the bank will report to the Bundesbank under AWV. Ensure the sender's bank includes the correct beneficiary account details and the UG's tax number (Steuernummer) to avoid delays.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers (TT) for payments to suppliers, with purpose codes such as 'Purchase of Goods' (e.g., 'S001' for trade). (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700 for large or recurring shipments, especially for raw materials (e.g., oats, packaging). The LC must be issued by the UG's bank and confirmed by the beneficiary's bank. For UHT plant operations, typical disbursements include equipment purchases (via LC) and raw material imports (via TT or LC). All outbound payments above €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 annual cross-border intragroup revenues trigger mandatory Local File; EUR 100,000,000 group revenues trigger Master File; prepared contemporaneously in German or English upon request.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the substance-over-form doctrine, anti-abuse rules under § 42 AO, and the minimum substance requirements under the EU ATAD-transposed interest limitation and Controlled Foreign Corporation rules apply; a UG producing oat milk in Germany with physical plant, personnel, and management in Germany inherently satisfies economic substance requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Criminal Tax Fraud (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (higher if done on a large scale or by a public official), fines up to the evaded amount, plus interest of 0.15% per month on back taxes; civil tax assessment remains fully payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for restitution of evaded taxes and can be subject to arrest, travel bans, and forfeiture of assets; criminal conviction bars directorship for up to 5 years (professional ban).
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration / Concealed Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Upward income adjustment, 6% interest per annum on transfer pricing adjustments, penalties up to 10% of the adjustment amount (§ 162 AO), plus late filing penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length terms face personal indemnity claims for resulting tax undercharges; the tax organ may issue personal enforcement and, if criminal intent established, imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance disguised as German Resident

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification as a non-resident or sham entity, recapture of tax incentives, and additional assessments; permanent establishment attribution of profits to the actual management seat.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for resulting tax deficiencies up to the company's unpaid debts if they continued trading while insolvent or intentionally used the UG to defraud creditors; travel bans may issue.
AML / Financial Sanctions Violations (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious breaches, publication of penalty decisions, and revocation of business license; criminal sanctions for intentional violation of EU sanctions up to 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the UG must personally ensure AML compliance; non-compliance leads to personal fines and imprisonment, and authorities may issue professional bans and prevent re-entry for non-EU directors.
