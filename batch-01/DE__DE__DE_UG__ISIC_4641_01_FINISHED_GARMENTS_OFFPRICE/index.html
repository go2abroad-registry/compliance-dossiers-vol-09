Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) and the competent local Finanzamt (tax office); official e-filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de), with VAT-ID procedures through BZSt (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

The UG must be entered in the German commercial register (Handelsregister). On commencement of business, file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the Finanzamt via ELSTER or in paper form, disclosing the entity, shareholders, planned activities, and estimated turnover. The Finanzamt allocates the Steuernummer (tax number) used for VAT returns, corporate income tax, trade tax, and wage tax. For intra-Community trade, separately apply to BZSt for a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) using the designated BZSt form. Non-resident shareholders do not require a German TIN unless they derive German-source taxable income. Foreign entities without a German establishment must coordinate with the local Finanzamt under § 18 UStG to obtain VAT registration for German taxable supplies, or use reverse-charge procedures under § 13b UStG.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant elections include: (i) fiscal unity (Organschaft) under § 14 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT purposes; (ii) small-business treatment (Kleinunternehmer) under § 19 UStG below the turnover thresholds; (iii) hybrid mismatch adjustments under § 4k EStG; (iv) treatment as a commercial trader for trade tax purposes where supported by actual business operations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All resident entrepreneurs must register for VAT irrespective of turnover; no de minimis threshold exists for a UG engaging in taxable business. Small-business relief is available only if turnover in the previous calendar year does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is forecast not to exceed EUR 100,000. For intra-Community distance sales and certain cross-border B2C services, the EU-wide threshold of EUR 10,000 annual turnover triggers destination-state registration under the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime. Non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in Germany are subject to VAT registration without any threshold, unless reverse charge applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For finished garments sold to purchasers located outside the European Union, a 0% VAT supply (tax exemption with input VAT deduction) applies under § 6 UStG if the goods are actually dispatched or transported from Germany to a third-country territory, exit is evidenced by the customs export declaration, and the exporter retains documentary proof of export shipment. For sales to EU VAT-registered customers, intra-Community supply under § 6a UStG qualifies as 0% VAT if the recipient is declared as an entrepreneur with a valid USt-IdNr., the goods are transported to another Member State, and the required recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) is filed. The off-price wholesale liquidation model qualifies under these provisions provided the commercial documentation precisely identifies the physical trail of each consignment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Elektronische Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document (e.g. Air Waybill, CMR)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank payment proof evidencing settlement from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Proof of export / transport evidence including delivery or arrival acknowledgment

Germany does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, API-based mandate, or digital stamping. A phased mandatory e-invoicing regime for domestic B2B transactions applies from 1 January 2025: resident taxpayers must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant structured invoices (e.g. XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) and must issue e-invoices according to the transitional deadlines (full issuer obligation phased in from 1 January 2027/2028 by turnover threshold). Invoicing may be transmitted via EDI, API, or other interoperable channels. Export and customs documentation remains subject to the ATLAS electronic customs system without a fiscal-clearance approval step.

If the exporter or supplier cannot evidence the actual exit or intra-Community transport, the tax office will retrospectively assess VAT at 19%, deny input VAT deduction, and impose interest under § 233a AO (0.5% per month on the assessed amount). Knowingly filing false export evidence constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment up to five years, or up to ten years in especially severe cases, plus unlimited financial penalties. The exporter may also be held jointly and severally liable under § 25d UStG for the VAT that another trader evades where identity evidence or bona fide reliance was insufficient. In cases of fraudulent zero-rating, the assessment limitation period extends to ten years, and administrative fines and criminal sanctions may be imposed concurrently.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, financial-crime controls, and any applicable foreign-trade restrictions. Ordinary cross-border trade payments, equity funding, shareholder loans, and dividends do not generally require prior foreign-exchange approval. Profit distributions remain subject to German company-law requirements, including distributable profits, a valid shareholder resolution, solvency and capital-maintenance rules, and any UG statutory-reserve requirement. Dividends to non-residents may be subject to German withholding tax, treaty relief or refund procedures, and documentation requirements. Interest, royalties, and other payments may have separate tax and withholding consequences.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no general requirement to repatriate export proceeds, surrender foreign currency, convert receipts into euros, or convert a prescribed percentage of export proceeds. Payments and receipts involving non-residents may require Bundesbank reporting under AWV reporting rules. As a general rule, reportable cross-border payment transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 are reported monthly, subject to statutory exemptions, transaction-specific rules, and current Bundesbank instructions. Reporting is statistical, not a prior approval or payment-blocking requirement. The company should confirm the applicable reporting category, deadline, exemptions, and electronic filing procedure with the Bundesbank or its bank for each relevant transaction type.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers statutory external-tr payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) administers certain foreign-trade controls. Germany has no general foreign-exchange licensing authority or capital-control regime for ordinary commercial payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro payments and card or payment services, subject to the provider's German corporate-entity eligibility, risk appetite, and onboarding policy. A European Economic Area-authorised electronic money institution may provide services under its home-state authorisation and applicable passporting arrangements, but an EMI account is not necessarily a deposit account and may not support UG formation capital requirements, cash services, trade finance, commercial letters of credit, or all international payment corridors. Verify safeguarding arrangements, deposit-guarantee status, payment limits, supported currencies, and the legal entity providing the service. Maintain a German bank account for formation, tax, payroll, and operational resilience.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the withholding tax, yielding an effective statutory rate of 26.375% on gross dividends. No church tax applies to corporate shareholders. Trade tax does not apply to dividend distributions by a UG.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate income tax return for the 2024 fiscal year must be filed by 31 July 2025 if prepared without tax advisor; if prepared by a tax advisor, the deadline is automatically extended to 31 December 2025. Further discretionary extensions to 31 May 2026 or later can be granted by the Finanzamt upon application under Sec. 109 AO. Prepayment deadlines for corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax are 10 March, 10 June, 10 September, and 10 December of the current fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 (per related-party transaction threshold for mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, Local File and Master File in Germany) – if the UG’s related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000 in a fiscal year, full documentation is required under Sec. 90 AO and the German Transfer Pricing Act (Verordnung zu § 90 AO). Master File is mandatory for groups with consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 100 million (harmonised with the OECD TP Guidelines and EU TP Documentation).

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the arm’s length principle and substance-over-form doctrine through Sec. 42 AO and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. The UG, as a German-resident entity, must have actual economic substance: a registered office, place of management in Germany, employees, business records, and real decision-making functions for the off-price garment resale operations. There is no formal Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) certificate regime, but the German tax authorities will deny treaty benefits, re-characterise transactions, and categorise the UG as an artificial arrangement if it lacks sufficient economic substance, employees, and risk control. For an apparel liquidation/trading UG, the critical substance elements are inventory ownership, purchase and sales contracts, logistics management, pricing and risk decisions, and bank accounts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under Sec. 370 AO, in aggravated cases up to 10 years under Sec. 370a AO; fines unlimited up to the amount of tax evaded; confiscation of profits and assets; additional 6% annual interest on unpaid taxes; late filing penalties; revocation of trade registration; listing in the tax offender register; possible dissolution of the corporate entity under criminal and administrative law

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable for violating tax payment and disclosure obligations; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and fines; civil liability for recovered taxes, interest and penalties; non-resident subscribers can be extradited for German tax crimes; travel bans on directors and beneficial owners; piercing of the corporate veil to recover unpaid taxes from personal assets when the UG is a sham or where directors acted in bad faith
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Inadequate TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Estimation of income by the Finanzamt, upward profit adjustments, penalty surcharge of 5% to 10% of the adjustment amount under Sec. 162(4) AO if documentation is inadequate; additional fixed penalties up to EUR 1,000,000 (Sec. 162(4) AO); interest on underpayments at 6% p.a.; denial of foreign tax credit; where the adjustment creates hidden profit distribution, withholding tax at 26.375% is assessed on the non-resident shareholder

Director personal liability: Directors and tax advisors who sign false transfer pricing documentation face criminal liability for tax evasion and civil liability for the resulting underpayment; personal liability for restitution of the tax shortfall and penalties; disqualification from serving as managing director under Sec. 6(2) GmbHG; travel restrictions for non-resident managing directors and shadow directors who participated in pricing decisions
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Substance Abusive Structure)

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the entity as a sham under Sec. 42 AO; all income reattributed to the true economic owner; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test of MLI/BEPS; withholding tax on deemed dividend distributions; addition of commercial substance requirements; trade tax and corporate income tax assessed on the total net income of the UG without reduction by interposed entity expenses; penalty interest and fines

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable for taxes if the corporate form is disregarded; personal restitution claims by the German tax authority; travel bans and passport restrictions for non-resident beneficial owners; criminal charges for tax evasion where the artificial structure was knowingly used; disqualification from holding directorships under German law
AML Violations / Failure to Maintain Beneficial Ownership Register (Transparenzregister)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1,000,000 for regulatory AML violations under Sec. 56 GwG (or up to 10% of annual global turnover for serious breaches); criminal penalties under Sec. 261 StGB (money laundering) of up to 5 years imprisonment; Freezing of bank accounts; Confiscation of assets tied to unexplained wealth; Exclusion from public contracts and industry licences; Order to correct the register with periodic penalty payments

Director personal liability: Managing directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal prosecution under GwG; imprisonment up to 5 years for negligent or intentional money laundering; travel bans by investigating judges; seizing of personal bank accounts and assets; disqualification from acting as beneficial owner or director in German-registered entities; joint and several civil liability for damages caused by AML breaches
