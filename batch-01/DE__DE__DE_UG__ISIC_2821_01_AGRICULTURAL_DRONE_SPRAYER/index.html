Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for USt-IdNr and central tax administration; local Finanzamt (tax office) for tax registration and assessments; ELSTER (e-Steuerportal) for electronic filing.

E-government filing portal

For a German Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt): (1) file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the competent Finanzamt immediately after notarial formation and commercial register entry; (2) receive a personal Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; (3) apply separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) under §27a UStG for cross-border supplies; (4) use ELSTER for registration, filing and amendment. If applied by a non-resident foreign owner/parent or foreign entity without a branch, registration is effected via BZSt Form USt 1 TN and liaison with the competent Finanzamt; the UG remains entity-level taxable on worldwide income as a German tax resident from incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law has no check-the-box pass-through election. Available elections relevant to drones manufacturer: corporate fiscal unity (Organschaft under §§14, 17 KStG) for VAT and income-tax consolidation; small-enterprise exemption under §19 UStG (limited; generally inappropriate for export manufacturer); trade tax fiscal unity; ATAD-compliant interest limitation 'escape clause' election (EBITDA carry-forward) under §4h EStG; group financing elections under §4i EStG subject to substance; optional application of cross-border VAT grouping (§2a UStG) for an Organschaft. Hybrid mismatch rules under §4j EStG operate statutorily, not by election, and disallow deductions for hybrid payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration turnover threshold for an established UG; VAT liability starts from first taxable supply in Germany. Small-enterprise exemption (Kleinunternehmer) under §19 UStG is available only below €22,000 prior-year turnover and €50,000 current-year turnover, but an export-oriented manufacturer normally waives or does not qualify because the exemption prevents input VAT recovery.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of agricultural drone sprayers to a third country is zero-rated under §4 Nr.1 and §6 UStG if (a) the seller or an authorized buyer dispatches goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU, (b) the goods physically leave the customs territory, and (c) the exporter proves the customs export and removal through the prescribed documentary evidence. Intra-community supplies to an EU customer with a valid VAT ID are zero-rated under §4 Nr.1b and §6a UStG if the goods are transported to another Member State. Services directly related to exported drones (e.g., logistics, installation offshore) may qualify as ancillary to the export. Substantiation must be preserved in accordance with §§8-12 UStDV.

  • Customs Export Declaration validated in ATLAS (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / Abgangsanmeldung) with customs status 'ausgeführt'
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical destination outside the EU (or CMR waybill for road transport)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the export declaration
  • Export contract, order confirmation and Incoterms record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence (SWIFT/MT103) substantiating consideration in hard-currency rails
  • Proof of buyer's entrepreneur status and foreign VAT ID / destination-country customs import evidence, where available

Germany mandates structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions under the growth opportunities act; as of 2025 resident suppliers must receive structured e-invoices, with phased issuer obligations (2027 for turnover > €800,000; 2028 for all). Accepted formats are XRechnung and ZUGFeRD 2.0 (XML) and EDI; invoices must be transmitted electronically with authentic origin (EDI or qualified electronic signature). There is no real-time government clearance, fiscal API or digital stamping; however, e-invoices and source documents must comply with GOBD (GoBD) audit trail rules and remain accessible for German tax audits.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict documentary audit by the Finanzamt and customs authorities; the exporter bears the burden of proof. If the required evidence is not produced or is falsified, the Finanzamt retroactively reverses the zero-rating and assesses 19% VAT plus interest (§233a AO) and, in evasion cases, criminal sanctions under §§369,370 AO (imprisonment up to 10 years, fines, confiscation). The regular assessment period is four years; for grossly negligent or intentional omission/extended evasion it extends to five or ten years respectively, triggering retrospective clawback. False or forged export documents additionally attract customs penalties under §382 AO, driver/qualified penalty orders and export license revocation in defense/dual-use controlled drone technology.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany imposes no foreign-exchange controls. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under EU treaties and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz) and Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung). Profit remittance from a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is unrestricted once German corporate law conditions for dividend distribution are met, including the legal requirement to build a statutory reserve (Sperrvermögen) of 25% of annual profits until the UG reaches EUR 25,000 share capital. No prior exchange authorization or profit repatriation license is required. However, the management of the UG must file statistical reports with the Deutsche Bundesbank for all cross-border payments and receivables exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month, including dividend remittances, capital injections, and trade payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory surrender or compulsory conversion rules for export proceeds. Exporters in the agricultural drone sector may invoice in EUR or any freely convertible currency, maintain foreign-currency accounts with German banks or banks abroad, and convert proceeds at market-determined exchange rates. The only mandatory requirement is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank: every cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported via the AWV reporting system (P4/P5/P6 forms as applicable). There is no required time limit for holding proceeds abroad and no percentage obligation to repatriate or convert export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) acting as monetary authority and balance-of-payments reporting collector; German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht – BaFin) for banking, payment services, and anti-money-laundering supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal feasibility is Low-to-Medium for a UG operating as an agricultural drone manufacturer. EU-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport financial services into Germany under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), without a full BaFin banking license. However, such EMIs are not allowed to accept deposits or provide commercial lending and documentary credits. More critically, the AgTech/drone manufacturing classification, especially if any drone model incorporates components that could be considered dual-use or subject to EU export-control regimes, will trigger severe AML/KYC and sanctions risk aversion. Most EMIs will decline this corporate segment or drastically limit payment corridors. EMIs are viable only as a subsidiary collection rail for local e-commerce or small-ticket customer payments, never as the primary treasury and clearing institution.
