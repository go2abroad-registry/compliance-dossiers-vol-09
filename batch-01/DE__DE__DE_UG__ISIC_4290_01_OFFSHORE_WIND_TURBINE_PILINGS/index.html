UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal AML/CFT statute is the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), as amended, including Sections 1, 3, 10-17, 19-23, 43, and 56. The beneficial-owner definition and transparency obligations are principally governed by Sections 3, 19, and 20 GwG. The Transparency Register and Transparency Register Data Retrieval Regulation (Transparenzregisterdatenabrufverordnung, TrDüV) governs relevant register procedures and data retrieval. Corporate formation is governed principally by the German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), including Sections 5a, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, and 13. The German Commercial Register and related filing rules are supplemented by the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRV).
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A German UG must have a domestic registered office and a domestic business address for service under the GmbHG. A virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine accessible business address, reliable service receipt, legally effective document forwarding, and access to corporate records. A mailbox-only, purely nominal, or non-operational address is unsuitable and may trigger registration, banking, tax, AML, or service-of-process problems. For offshore wind foundation installation, a virtual office is not an adequate substitute for operational substance where the company contracts for installation, employs technical personnel, stores equipment, operates vessels, manages project safety, or performs regulated construction activities. The company should maintain demonstrable management, accounting, project records, contractor controls, and technical and safety capabilities in Germany or an appropriately documented operating location.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Local District Court (Amtsgericht) maintaining the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), acting through a German notary public; entries are published in the Federal Gazette (Bundesanzeiger).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz, GmbHG), Secs. 1-4 and Sec. 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft with reduced capital), Secs. 7-8 (application for registration), Sec. 10 (contents of commercial register entry), Sec. 11 (legal effect of registration), and Secs. 35-44 (managing directors).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized deed of incorporation (Errichtungsniederschrift) with the articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and the list of shareholders, submitted electronically to the Commercial Register pursuant to the Electronic Commercial Register Act (EHUG).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 38 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG (mini-GmbH) requires a statutory minimum share capital of only EUR 1.00 under Sec. 5a(1) GmbHG; the fully paid subscriptions must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director on a bank account in the company's name before registration. No mandatory escrow account is imposed by law, but notarial practice commonly uses a notarial escrow account (Notaranderkonto) until the commercial register entry.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate under the applicable state building code (MBO/LBO), structural integrity and crane load certification by the Technical Inspection Association (TÜV), occupational safety verification under the Occupational Safety Act (ArbSchG) issued by the local Labour Inspectorate, and offshore personnel survival and medical fitness certificates (GWO Basic Safety Training) for crew and site personnel.

Paid-in capital rule: A UG (mini-GmbH) requires a statutory minimum share capital of only EUR 1.00 under Sec. 5a(1) GmbHG; the fully paid subscriptions must be placed at the free disposal of the managing director on a bank account in the company's name before registration. No mandatory escrow account is imposed by law, but notarial practice commonly uses a notarial escrow account (Notaranderkonto) until the commercial register entry.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification and federal tax administration; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and Steuernummer registration; electronic filing portal: ELSTER and BZSt Online-Portal

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities complete the official tax registration questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' and submit it to the responsible Finanzamt (generally the place of management or permanent establishment in Germany). The Finanzamt issues the statutory tax number (Steuernummer). Separate application is made to the BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). Non-resident entities may be required to appoint a process agent or fiscal representative for VAT registration if no permanent establishment exists and German VAT liability arises.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities complete the official tax registration questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' and submit it to the responsible Finanzamt (generally the place of management or permanent establishment in Germany). The Finanzamt issues the statutory tax number (Steuernummer). Separate application is made to the BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). Non-resident entities may be required to appoint a process agent or fiscal representative for VAT registration if no permanent establishment exists and German VAT liability arises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime is available in Germany. Domestic corporate groups may elect the German fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT on a contractual profit-and-loss pooling basis. For VAT, an Organschaft requires financial, economic, and organisational integration. Hybrid mismatch elections are limited to ATAD implementation rules, including designated substitution and anti-reverse-hybrid adjustments; there is no elective pass-through classification for an Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general minimum registration threshold applies to entrepreneurs supplying goods or services in Germany. The small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG applies only to domestic small businesses with turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 in the previous calendar year and expected to remain below EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year. Foreign suppliers that do not qualify for the small-business exemption must register from the first taxable supply with a place of supply in Germany, subject to reverse-charge and One-Stop Shop rules for cross-border supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Section 4 No. 1(a) and Section 6 UStG applies where the supplier exports goods outside the European Union, the goods physically leave Germany, and the supplier is the exporter named on the export declaration. For intra-EU supplies, zero-rating requires a valid customer VAT ID and a mandatory EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung). Offshore installation services for wind turbine pilings are generally subject to the place-of-supply rules: B2B services to non-EU recipients are located at the recipient's place of business and thus fall outside German VAT, provided documentary evidence is retained. Zero-rating must be substantiated by complete export and payment documentation and is subject to routine BZSt data matching with customs records.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhrkontrolle/IE-Athen approved export confirmation)
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung)
  • Bill of Lading or other transport document evidencing export (e.g. multimodal bill of lading)
  • Export confirmation (Ausfuhrnachweis) issued by the customs office
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT202 payment instruction showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Muster/contract documents proving the direct export relationship and recipient identity

Germany has introduced a phased domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate. From 1 January 2025, all German entrepreneurs must be able to receive structured electronic invoices in XInvoice, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or PEPPOL-compatible formats; from 2027 large taxpayers (prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000) must issue e-invoices, and from 2028 all domestic B2B suppliers must issue structured e-invoices. There is no real-time central fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime yet; invoices are transmitted to the recipient and VAT returns are filed periodically via ELSTER, with the BZSt retaining access to transactional summary data. Public-sector invoices must use XRechnung.

German tax authorities apply rigorous export-proof tests. Missing or inconsistent export evidence causes the denial of zero-rating and assessment of German VAT at 19% on the full export price; retrospective clawback includes statutory interest at 6% per annum from the original VAT return date. Intentional or reckless false zero-rating is tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable by fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; negligent underdeclaration may trigger penalty surcharges under Section 378 AO. Records must be digitally archived in a readable format for 10 years, and the BZSt uses automated risk-scoring and customs export data matching to detect fictitious exports.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; Germany maintains no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital both within the EU and vis-à-vis third countries, subject only to limited EU derogations under Article 64/66 TFEU, which are not applicable to standard wind-turbine project equity, debt service, profit repatriation, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. No approval is required for profit remittance or foreign direct investment into a UG; practical frictions are limited to statistical reporting (≥ EUR 12,500 per cross-border payment) and German withholding-tax compliance for dividend distributions (26.375% standard rate incl. solidarity surcharge, 0% on interest/royalties under relevant DTTI and EU directives with appropriate reductions).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or holding-period rule on export proceeds; exporters may keep all foreign-currency receipts onshore or offshore. Reporting obligations: (i) any payment received from abroad of at least EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under the balance-of-payments statistics (Zahlungsmeldung) within 5 business days; (ii) goods exports to non-EU destinations with a value of at least EUR 20,000 per shipment require a separate export-statistics declaration to Bundesbank; (iii) export-control or dual-use matters in monopile/turbine equipment must be screened via BAFA, requiring an export licence for any sanctioned or military-end-use item; none of these obligations constitutes a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (official central bank of Germany, Frankfurt am Main) within the Eurosystem; European Central Bank (ECB) sets euro-area monetary policy; Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) enforces foreign-trade and export-control statutes under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV).
