Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the competent local district court (Amtsgericht) for the company's registered seat; the responsible court is determined by that seat.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), particularly sections 2 (notarial form and model protocol), 3 (articles), 5a (UG requirements and reserve), 7 (registration prerequisites), 8 (registration particulars), 10 (register entry), and 35 (management representation). Commercial Register filings are governed by the German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB), including sections 8 and 12.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister), executed in notarized form and transmitted electronically by a German notary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with share capital below EUR 25,000, with a statutory minimum of EUR 1. The subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash before the Commercial Register application; contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. A separate escrow account is not legally required, but the capital must be paid into an account held for the company in formation and evidenced to the managing director. The stated USD fee is an approximate conversion of a typical court registration fee; actual court and notarial charges vary under the German Court and Notarial Costs Act (GNotKG), the capital amount, and the transaction.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. The principal electronic portal is ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de. Customs registrations, including the Economic Operators Registration and Identification (EORI) number, are administered through the German Customs Administration (Generalzolldirektion) and its online services.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must register with the competent Finanzamt after commencing or preparing business activities by submitting the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer used for corporate income tax, trade tax, and domestic VAT filings. The company applies separately to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr.; the application requires the legal name, registered address, legal form, commercial-register details, domestic Steuernummer, and responsible Finanzamt. A non-resident company carrying on taxable German activities must identify the responsible Finanzamt based on its German activities or fiscal representative arrangements and submit the applicable tax-registration questionnaire. For intra-EU goods transactions, the business must obtain and use a valid USt-IdNr. and may need an EORI number for customs declarations. VAT registration is generally required before taxable supplies begin unless a statutory exemption applies; Germany does not issue a single universal taxpayer number covering all tax types.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a capital company and is subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at entity level, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a UG. The corporate-law form cannot ordinarily be elected into transparent partnership taxation. Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act permits certain eligible partnerships and partnership-like entities to elect corporate taxation, but it does not provide a classification election for a UG. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD II may deny deductions, include income, or neutralize double deductions or imported mismatches; they are not elective classification regimes. A German group may make statutory elections relevant to tax grouping, including a fiscal unity (Organschaft), subject to strict legal, financial, and operational requirements. VAT grouping (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) may apply by operation of law where financial, economic, and organizational integration exists; it is not a free-form check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany has no general VAT registration threshold for an established German business making taxable supplies. The domestic small-business regime under Section 19 UStG may exempt qualifying supplies where total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to statutory conditions and exclusion rules. The exemption is not a general registration-free threshold and may be waived by election, normally binding for five calendar years. For cross-border EU business-to-consumer distance sales, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold may apply where the supplier is established in only one Member State and does not elect the One Stop Shop (OSS). German businesses selling goods to German consumers must generally charge German VAT from the first taxable supply unless a specific exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt with input-tax recovery under Sections 4 No. 1(a) and 6 UStG if the goods are physically transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination, the statutory export transaction conditions are satisfied, and the supplier retains prescribed evidence of export. The supplier must generally establish that the goods left the EU customs territory and connect the transport or dispatch evidence to the specific supply. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for VAT exemption under Sections 4 No. 1(b) and 6a UStG where the customer is VAT-registered in another Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier meets invoice, record, and recapitulative-statement requirements. Offshore or non-German services are analyzed under the place-of-supply rules; many B2B services supplied to a business customer outside Germany are outside the scope of German VAT rather than technically zero-rated. Export-related input VAT is generally refundable through the German VAT return where the claimant is taxable, properly registered, and maintains complete supporting evidence.

  • Electronic customs export declaration submitted through the EU customs system, normally including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, packing list, transport documents, and proof of the contractual recipient
  • Proof of physical exit from the EU customs territory, such as the electronic exit confirmation, export accompanying document, carrier confirmation, bill of lading, airway bill, or equivalent transport evidence
  • Evidence linking the exported goods to the relevant sales invoice, including product descriptions, quantities, value, shipment date, and destination
  • For intra-EU supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement; customs export documents are not ordinarily applicable
  • Bank payment records and commercial contracts where relevant to audit substantiation; a German inward-remittance certificate is not generally a statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption

Germany does not generally operate real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital fiscal stamping. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance of structured e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions is subject to transitional rules: paper or other electronic formats may generally continue during 2025 and 2026; issuers with prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally continue through 2027; structured e-invoicing becomes generally mandatory from 1 January 2028, subject to statutory exceptions. A compliant e-invoice must use an approved structured format, principally XRechnung or an equivalent EN 16931-compliant hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD. The invoice must preserve authenticity, integrity, legibility, required VAT data, and an auditable electronic record. B2C ecommerce invoices are not generally subject to the domestic B2B e-invoice issuance mandate. VAT returns and reporting are submitted electronically through ELSTER; there is no universal transaction-level API clearance requirement comparable to Italy or Poland.

Germany has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export VAT exemption depends on the underlying supply and documentary proof, not on a universal foreign-exchange repatriation deadline. If export conditions or evidence are deficient, the Finanzamt may retrospectively deny the VAT exemption, assess German VAT, disallow or claw back related input-tax recovery, charge statutory interest, and impose late-payment consequences. Incorrect VAT returns may result in administrative fines, tax-offence proceedings, or prosecution for tax evasion under the German Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung), with imprisonment of up to five years or a fine and, in particularly serious cases, imprisonment of six months to ten years. Late filing penalties may apply, generally calculated at 0.25% of the assessed tax for each commenced month, subject to statutory minimum and maximum limits. Customs violations may trigger recovery of customs duties and import VAT, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture measures, and criminal liability under applicable EU customs and German criminal provisions. Voluntary disclosure may mitigate criminal exposure only if all statutory requirements are satisfied before discovery or initiation of qualifying enforcement action.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies the EU Treaty freedoms and has no general restrictions on current-account payments, capital transfers, profit distributions, or conversion between euros and foreign currencies. Transfers remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax rules, corporate-law restrictions, and any applicable EU or German investment-control or export-control measures. A UG may distribute legally available profits subject to annual accounts, tax liabilities, statutory reserve requirements, and solvency and capital-maintenance rules; a UG must allocate 25% of annual net profit, less any loss carryforward, to its statutory reserve until the required GmbH share capital is reached. Cross-border dividends may require withholding-tax analysis, treaty or EU directive relief, and evidence of beneficial ownership. Banks may request contracts, invoices, tax documentation, source-of-funds evidence, and corporate approvals before processing material transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no general statutory deadline requiring a German exporter to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement. Export proceeds may be received and retained in a foreign-currency account, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, and reporting obligations. Cross-border payments and certain claims or liabilities with non-residents may be reportable to the Bundesbank under AWV rules; the general reporting threshold for reportable payments is an amount exceeding EUR 50,000, subject to statutory exemptions and transaction-specific rules. Reports are generally due by the seventh working day of the month following the relevant transaction. This is statistical reporting, not prior approval or a requirement to convert funds. Confirm the current threshold, reporting category, exemptions, and filing procedure with the Bundesbank for each transaction type.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is Germany's central bank and administers statutory external-payments and cross-border position reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) administers foreign-trade controls; BaFin supervises regulated credit institutions and payment service providers. Germany has no general exchange-control licensing authority for ordinary commercial transfers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine collections and payments where the provider is an appropriately authorized EU payment institution or electronic-money institution operating in Germany under passporting or local authorization. Verify the provider and permitted services in the relevant regulator registers. E-money is not a bank deposit and generally does not benefit from statutory deposit-guarantee protection; safeguarding arrangements apply instead. An EMI account may not satisfy a bank's requirements for share-capital payment evidence, credit facilities, cash services, or certain marketplace and payment-processor arrangements. An offshore provider without applicable EU authorization or lawful German access should not be used as the sole operating account. Maintain a German or other EEA bank account for core treasury and statutory evidence where practicable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital from an account in the subscriber's or documented funding entity's name to the UG's formation or corporate account. Use EUR where practicable; otherwise agree the conversion rate, charges, and correspondent-bank route in advance. Identify the sender, recipient, and purpose in the payment reference, for example 'UG share capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]'; do not describe a loan as equity or vice versa. Retain the subscription or loan agreement, shareholder resolution where required, notarial formation documents, sender-account evidence, and bank credit confirmation. There is no general German FX licence or prescribed capital-injection purpose code for an ordinary domestic corporate account. Assess Bundesbank AWV reporting separately for reportable cross-border payments or positions, apply exemptions and thresholds, and file required statistical reports on time. An offshore provider without applicable EU authorization or lawful German access should not be used as the sole operating account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (computed on CIT) plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at 14–17% effective trade tax, yielding a combined ordinary corporate income tax burden of approximately 29–31% on pre-tax profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) is 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25% tax, giving an aggregate statutory withholding rate of 26.375%. When a German UG distributes profits to its shareholder(s), this rate must be withheld and remitted to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern unless a treaty exemption or reduction applies.

Filing due by 31 July following the calendar tax year. Automatic extension to 28/29 February of the second subsequent year applies when the return is prepared by a licensed tax adviser (§149(3) AO, §6(2) EStDV); final assessment permits up to 4-year statute of limitations for correct returns.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadline as the CIT return: 31 July following the tax year; extension to 28/29 February when filed by a tax adviser.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany imposes mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation on the UG as a German resident entity. A Local File must be prepared for each fiscal year if the sum of the UG's controlled transactions for goods (deliveries to/from related parties) exceeds €5,000,000 in a fiscal year, or if the sum of all services and other transactions with related parties exceeds €1,000,000 per fiscal year. A Master File is required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds €100,000,000. Additionally, the UG must submit a 'revenue information declaration' (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation) upon tax office request within 60 days; a late or insufficient response can lead to penalty of 5% of the adjusted transfer price difference or €10,000 maximum per request. The German authorities may assess a default surcharge of up to EUR 100,000 for non-submission of documentation under §162(4) AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code like offshore jurisdictions, but the doctrine of economic substance is deeply rooted in §42 AO ('Missbrauch von rechtlichen Gestaltungsmöglichkeiten'). For the UG fast-fashion e-commerce structure, the local entity must demonstrate actual substance: physical registered office, local management and decision-making, substantive employment contracts, genuine commercial functions (procurement, warehousing, marketing, risk management), and local bank account operations. If the BZSt or tax office determines that the UG lacks substance and serves only as a conduit for foreign owners or receives profits artificially allocated to reduce tax, the entire structure may be disregarded under §42 AO and the relevant income reattributed to the beneficial owner. In addition, the Federal Fiscal Court and ATAD anti-abuse rules deny treaty benefits if the main purpose or one of the main purposes of the arrangement is obtaining a tax advantage in contradiction to the object and purpose of the relevant DTA. The German tax authorities may also impose a deemed profit distribution and trigger 26.375% WHT on that constructive dividend.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years and criminal fine under §370 AO, §369 AO in conjunction with §38 StGB, plus confiscation of benefits. In especially severe cases (large-scale commercial fraud, use of nominee shareholders) imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years under §370(3) AO. Additionally, the UG will be subject to 6% interest per annum (0.5% monthly) on all under-declared tax, and late payment penalties of 5% of the arrear tax per §233a AO. Publication of the conviction can be ordered.

Director personal liability: Managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally jointly and severally liable under §69 AO for tax debts when the UG intentionally or negligently fails to make tax payments; criminal conviction may result in personal criminal liability, seizure of personal assets, travel bans and surrender of passport if flight risk exists; professional disqualification from serving as director under §6(2) GmbHG; extradition treaties are operative in an international context.
Shell Company / Artificial Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under §42 AO the German tax office can disregard the UG's legal form and tax the economic reality, meaning all e-commerce profits are reattributed to the foreign principal and are fully taxable as German-sourced income subject to CIT, trade tax, and 26.375% dividend WHT if reclassified as a deemed distribution. The tax office may impose a 5% surcharge on the disallowed transfer-pricing amount under §162(4) AO, with a minimum penalty of €10,000. DTA benefits will be refused under the principal purpose test (ATAD/MLI).

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in an artificial arrangement face personal liability for the UG's fraudulent transactions; civil liability for breaching the statutory obligation to maintain capital (§64 GmbHG for trading while insolvent); liability for unpaid CIT and Gewerbesteuer under §69 AO; possible criminal sanctions for aiding tax evasion; public travel bans, prohibition from leaving Germany, asset attach orders, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for tax evasion resulting from the sham structure.
Money Laundering / AML-BNPL Abuse

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under §261 StGB for money laundering: imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years (up to 10 years in commercial or gang-based cases); confiscation of all assets and proceeds (§73 StGB); administrative fine under §56 GwG up to €100,000 for deficient AML duties; prohibition from using payment rails; closing of bank accounts by German financial institutions; registration of the UG in public sanctions databases.

Director personal liability: Directors or beneficial owners participating in money-laundering transactions are personally exposed to criminal proceedings; a preventive travel ban and European Arrest Warrant may be issued; personal assets are subject to seizure; under §14 StGB all acts of the UG are imputed to the director; imprisonment directly affects the individual and the UG is not protected; permanent exclusion from the management of a German commercial entity may follow.
Unpaid Wages / Social Security / Trade Tax

Statutory sanction: Fines under §266a StGB (non-payment of social security contributions) with imprisonment up to 5 years; tax office can issue enforceable attachment orders (Pfändungs- und Einziehungsverfügungen) against UG bank accounts; penalties equal to 6% p.a. late payment interest and late payment surcharges; forced liquidation of the UG by the registration court because of insolvent trading (§64 GmbHG).

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is personally liable for unpaid social security contributions pursuant to §823 BGB and §266a StGB; personal liability for PAYE unpaid after company insolvency under §69 AO; management can be personally seized for the company's unpaid trade tax if the failure is attributable to gross negligence; directors may be prohibited from leaving Germany pending criminal proceedings and may be subject to arrest if there is a flight risk; the personal bank guarantees of the director are enforceable.
