Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister Abteilung B at the competent Amtsgericht (Local Court) — the commercial register department of the local court in whose district the registered office is located; all filings are formally lodged through a licensed German notary (Notar).

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG — German Limited Liability Companies Act). Instrumental sections: § 1 (formation as UG), § 2 (notarized articles), § 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) special regime), §§ 7-8 (application for commercial register registration and required documents), § 10 (registration effect), §§ 35-37 (management authority), §§ 46 and 48 (shareholder resolutions); supplemented by § 12 HGB and the court fee schedule of the Gerichts- und Notarkostengesetz.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the certified statutory model form (Musterprotokoll) together with the Anmeldung zur Eintragung (application for registration in the Commercial Register) pursuant to §§ 7 and 8 GmbHG, including the list of managing directors and specimen signature declarations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG may be formed with a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00, which must be fully subscribed and paid in cash and placed at the free disposal of the managing director before registration; contributions in kind are not permitted under the Musterprotokoll route. A bank confirmation of payment is filed with the commercial register application. No statutory escrow account is mandated for cash formation, but the notary must warrant that the capital has been paid into a formation account and is available; the managing directors must ensure that the capital is not disproportionately low relative to the intended business purpose under § 5a(2) GmbHG. The UG is further required to appropriate 25% of its annual net profits to a statutory reserve until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000.00.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutzabnahme) by the municipal fire protection authority, including alarm system approval and evacuation plan validation; structural load inspection (Standsicherheits- und Lastnachweis) reviewed by the building authority (Bauaufsichtsbehörde) as part of the building permit; occupational safety clearance (arbeitsschutzrechtliche Abnahme) by the Gewerbeaufsicht or approved inspection body, covering machine guards, ventilation controls, and hazardous-substance storage; and mandatory registration of the industrial plant with the Berufsgenossenschaft (DGUV) for statutory accident insurance and workplace inspection scheduling

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full euro-area member and maintains unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account items. There are no exchange controls, surrender requirements, central-bank licensing, or prior approval requirements for the repatriation of profit, repayment of shareholder loans, or liquidation proceeds payable to non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance is constrained only by German tax law, principally corporate income tax and the dividend withholding tax regime, not by FX regulations. Cross-border capital movements are subject to German balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligations, and transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions will be blocked or frozen as a consequence of EU and German sanctions law. Overall, the regime is materially FX-permissive for a GmbH/UG operating in the precision optics export sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may lawfully hold export proceeds in any designated foreign currency, in domestic or foreign accounts, without conversion to EUR or repatriation within a fixed timeline. The only substantive statutory obligation is statistical: cross-border payments and settlement claims, including export receipts, must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank where the relevant payment threshold is exceeded. In practice, the bank or the reporting party must provide the applicable AWV purpose code; the monetary threshold is normally EUR 12,500 for a single or aggregated payment, and reporting is due by the statutory deadline under AWV, generally by the 7th working day of the month following the transaction. This is a reporting regime, not a foreign-exchange surrender or conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, with regulatory complement from the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin). The Bundesbank is the competent authority for external-sector statistics and for the General Reporting Requirement under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). BaFin supervises banking and financial conduct, while the Bundesbank operates and oversees the German clearing and settlement infrastructure, including TARGET2 participation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. An offshore, non-EU fintech or e-money institution cannot lawfully operate as a clearing counterparty or hold funds in Germany without a BaFin licence or a valid EEA e-money/banking passport. German and EEA-licensed EMIs may be used for multi-currency operating accounts, FX conversion and fast payments, but most do not provide commercial letters of credit, SWIFT MT700 documentary credit issuance, or high-value capital escrow services. For this UG, EMIs should be treated as a secondary liquidity channel only and never as the primary clearing rail for LC or MT103 trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection should be routed as an inbound SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the shareholder’s bank to the UG’s EUR IBAN at the German account-holding bank. If the contributor is inside SEPA, the transfer may be initiated as a SEPA Credit Transfer using the same remittance logic. The beneficiary name in the wire instruction must exactly match the UG’s registered legal name. The remittance information field must clearly state 'Kapitalerhöhung / Gesellschaftereinlage' (capital increase / shareholder contribution) and the bank-specific AWV or Bundesbank purpose code for direct investment into a domestic company; the code must not be a goods-trade code. The receiving bank will verify the contribution against the notarial capital contribution resolution or share subscription document, the commercial register extract, and the UBO confirmation. No FX conversion is required because the UG is EUR-functional and Germany maintains full currency convertibility. After credit, the UG should retain the SWIFT confirmation, the notarial contribution evidence and the bank statement as proof of paid-in capital for tax and financial-statement purposes.Outbound payments for optical lens component purchases and production inputs should settle over SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers, SEPA Credit Transfers within the EEA, or documentary trade instruments where deferred payment or delivery risk exists. Best-practice rails are: (i) MT103 TT transfers for advance payments and final invoices; (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by the UG’s German bank by SWIFT MT700 for supply contracts exceeding EUR 100,000; (iii) confirmed LCs with the seller’s confirming bank for high-risk jurisdictions; and (iv) documentary collections using MT400/MT410 where commercial risk is moderate. Payment instructions must contain the contract and invoice reference, and the AWV purpose code matching the underlying goods trade.
