Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and local Finanzamt; electronic filing and VAT-ID applications via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must submit the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt through ELSTER or in paper form. The local Finanzamt assigns the German tax number for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT accounting purposes. For the separate VAT identification number, the foreign entity files an application with the BZSt under Section 27a UStG, demonstrating an economic activity, fixed place of business, or tax representative in Germany. Issuance is conditional on submission of identity documents, corporate registration evidence, and signed German fiscal declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (UG is a non-transparent, resident corporation subject to unlimited corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax; no pass-through treatment is available) — Foreign entities must submit the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent local Finanzamt through ELSTER or in paper form. The local Finanzamt assigns the German tax number for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT accounting purposes. For the separate VAT identification number, the foreign entity files an application with the BZSt under Section 27a UStG, demonstrating an economic activity, fixed place of business, or tax representative in Germany. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (UG is a non-transparent, resident corporation subject to unlimited corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax; no pass-through treatment is available)

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime. Entity classification under German tax law is mandatory and statutory. A UG may elect to be the parent or subsidiary in an Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax and VAT purposes where financial, economic, and organizational integration exists. Hybrid mismatch rules under the ATAD/German Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive Implementation Act apply mandatorily; no hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration turnover threshold for businesses established in Germany; registration is required from the first taxable supply in Germany. For intra-Community B2C distance sales and cross-border digital services, the OSS registration threshold is EUR 10,000 annual Union-wide turnover. Non-established foreign suppliers must register for German VAT whenever supplies are deemed taxable in Germany, unless reverse-charge or OSS rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies under Section 6 UStG are VAT-exempt with input VAT recovery where goods physically leave the EU customs territory under a customs export procedure and the exporter holds contemporaneous documentary proof of export. Freight transport services for goods carried between the EU and a third territory, and ancillary services directly connected to the export or import of goods, are zero-rated under Section 4 No. 3 UStG where the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside the EU. Intra-Community rail freight movements and intermodal forwarding within the EU may be zero-rated under Section 6a UStG only with valid VAT identification numbers and recapitulative statement reporting.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/Ausfuhrzollanmeldung or certified EX-A form evidencing placement into export procedure)
  • Bill of Lading or CIM/SMGS international rail consignment note evidencing intermodal transportation and border exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank transfer record evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Transport and insurance documents showing physical departure from the EU customs territory and delivery to the third-country consignee
  • Third-country importer/consignee registration and identification data

Germany mandates phased B2B e-invoicing under Section 14 UStG. From 1 January 2025, every VAT-registered business must be able to receive e-invoices; from 1 January 2027, businesses with turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all B2B e-invoices are mandatory. Accepted formats are EN 16931-based XML conformant structures such as XRechnung and ZUGFeRD. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no government API clearance protocol, and no digital stamping requirement; invoices are exchanged bilaterally, through PEPPOL-compatible networks, or via the ELSTER interface.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary scrutiny and retrospective correction by the Finanzamt, BZSt, and customs authorities. Missing or false export evidence triggers retrospective VAT reassessment, revocation of the zero-rating, statutory default interest under Section 233a AO, and input VAT clawback. Intentional or fraudulent claims constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment up to five years or fines, and up to ten years in aggravated cases. The assessment period extends to ten years for deliberate concealment; records must be retained for ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalized capital and current account within the euro area, implementing EU free-movement-of-capital rules (Art. 63 TFEU) and OECD Codes of Liberalisation. The euro is fully convertible; there are no exchange-control licensing regimes, no prior central-bank approvals, no quotas, and no restriction on profit or dividend remittance by a UG to non-resident shareholders. The only frictions are: (i) German corporate income tax at 15% plus solidarity surcharge (15.825% effective) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, municipality-dependent), each at entity level; (ii) 26.375% withholding tax on dividend distributions (Abgeltungsteuer) unless treaty-reduced (typically 0%–15% with substance-backed holding structures, or 5% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders); (iii) the statutory profit-retention rule of §5a(3) GmbHG requiring 25% of annual profit to be reserved until the UG's capital reaches €25,000; and (iv) electronic balance-of-payments reporting to the Bundesbank for each cross-border payment from Germany equal to or exceeding €12,500 (AWV §§67–69). None of these constitutes a convertibility impediment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion obligations on export proceeds. A German UG may freely keep EUR or foreign-currency export receivables, choose settlement in any currency, and hold funds at any licensed bank without remittance deadlines or mandatory conversion ratios. Mandatory duties are statistical only: each cross-border payment received from abroad of €12,500 or more must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV §§67–69 using the approved balance-of-payments report (Zahlungsverkehr report with ZKA code and transaction classification; freight services are coded under the service-transaction category). Invoicing must reference the AWV goods/services nomenclature and the UG's VAT ID; no customs or exchange-surrender certificate is required for rail-freight intermodal earnings.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) — German central bank, responsible for external-sector statistics, balance-of-payments reporting, and monetary policy implementation; joint banking-supervisory authority with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG). Foreign-trade and capital-movement monitoring is exercised through the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung — AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs without a German or EEA banking/e-money license are rated LOW for primary treasury and escrow use: accepting client funds or operating a euro-denominated payment account without BaFin authorization, or without a valid EEA passport under the PSD2/E-Money Directive, breaches §1 and §32 KWG; funds held are not protected by German deposit-guarantee or safeguarding rules, and no statutory Bundesbank reporting channel exists. EEA-passported digital banks and EMIs (e.g., N26 Bank GmbH, Solaris SE, Pliant GmbH, Qonto (French IIA passport), Wise Europe (Belgian EMI passport)) are rated MEDIUM-HIGH as secondary payment rails for freight-receivables collection, supplier payouts, and fast FX conversion, but are not substitutes for a tier-1 German settlement bank for documentary credits (LC), guarantee issuance, capital-escrow mechanics, or AWV statistical reporting. Recommended architecture: tier-1 German bank as settlement/custodian; one EEA-passported EMI as an operational liquidity layer for SEPA and small-value corridor disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection into the UG by a German or foreign shareholder requires no exchange-control approval or Bundesbank permit. Compliant routing: (A) Formal capital increase (Kapitalerhöhung): a notarized shareholders' resolution naming the subscribing shareholder and the contribution amount; transfer by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA from the shareholder's bank account to a dedicated contribution/escrow account of the UG (Verkehrs-/Treuhandkonto) held at the onboarding German bank; the bank issues a confirmation of payment for the commercial-register filing; following registration, funds are released to the UG's operational account. (B) Capital-reserve contribution (Kapitalrücklage) without nominal increase: wire instruction with Verwendungszweck referencing 'Kapitalrücklage gem. §26 GmbHG' and the commercial-register number; recording in the UG's financial statements. (C) Shareholder loan: signed loan agreement with arm's-length interest and term to avoid hidden-equity recharacterization under German corporate income-tax principles. For all cross-border payments of €12,500 or more, the bank or the UG files the Bundesbank external-statistics report (AWV) using purpose code D1 (participation in a resident company) for equity and the corresponding loan code for shareholder debt; the Transparenzregister must be updated for any new UBO; if a non-EU investor acquires control, an AWV investment-screening filing applies only where the rail-freight activity falls within protected infrastructure or critical-sector thresholds.Outbound clearing protocol for rail-freight intermodal settlements: (i) SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for freight-invoice payments, rail-operator invoices, terminal and customs fees, with the payment narrative referencing the CIM/SMGS or CMR consignment number and the INCO term; MT202 COVER for larger inter-bank and corridor aggregations where a cover bank is designated. (ii) Commercial documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) issued by the German tier-1 bank (MT700/MT760) are used for East-West corridors where rail operators require bank payment undertakings; the presentation schedule includes CIM/SMGS consignment notes, FBL (negotiable FIATA multimodal transport bill) where applicable, cargo insurance certificates, commercial invoices, and customs proof; a confirming bank in the destination corridor is added for country-risk mitigation. (iii) SEPA Credit Transfers for European legs and intra-German rail charges. (iv) For collections: documentary collections (DP/DA) through the German bank's trade-finance desk and correspondent banks at destination border points. Every outbound disbursement of €12,500 or more is subject to AWV balance-of-payments reporting, real-time sanctions screening, and placement of transport documents in the bank's trade-finance portal where reimbursement is documentary.Outright FX Forward Contracts (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, EUR/RUB and corridor currencies, documented under an ISDA 2002 or German framework master agreement (Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte) with the tier-1 bank.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) for CNY, RUB, KZT, and other restricted or liquid-constrained currencies, with EUR or USD cash settlement in a freely convertible offshore center.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS / FX Interest-Rate Swaps) for matching EUR funding against USD/RUB/CNY trade receivables and for multi-year block-train contract financing.Vanilla European FX Options for downside protection on syndicated corridor payment schedules.FX Swaps (short-term swap-type) for bridging temporary liquidity mismatches during rail-transit and demurrage cycles.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax, plus trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates typically 14–17%, resulting in a combined effective rate of approximately 30–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in a total effective rate of 26.375%).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Taxable income (steuerpflichtiges Einkommen) – computed per German GAAP with tax adjustments. Non-resident entities with a permanent establishment in Germany must report income attributable to that PE. If no PE, no filing obligation unless German-source income (e.g., rental, royalty) is present. Late filing penalty: 0.25% of the assessed tax per month (minimum €25 per month, maximum €25,000). Late payment interest: 0.5% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for understatement: up to 10% of the understated tax. Criminal prosecution for intentional evasion.
Box 2 Loss carryforward (Verlustabzug) – amount of prior-year losses used to offset current-year income. Only losses from German-source income can be carried forward; foreign losses are not deductible. Late filing penalty: 0.25% of the assessed tax per month (minimum €25 per month, maximum €25,000). Late payment interest: 0.5% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for understatement: up to 10% of the understated tax. Criminal prosecution for intentional evasion.
Box 3 Corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) – calculated at 15% of taxable income. Non-residents are subject to tax only on German-source income; the rate applies to that portion. Late filing penalty: 0.25% of the assessed tax per month (minimum €25 per month, maximum €25,000). Late payment interest: 0.5% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for understatement: up to 10% of the understated tax. Criminal prosecution for intentional evasion.
Box 4 Solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) – 5.5% of the corporate income tax amount. Surcharge applies to the tax on German-source income. Late filing penalty: 0.25% of the assessed tax per month (minimum €25 per month, maximum €25,000). Late payment interest: 0.5% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for understatement: up to 10% of the understated tax. Criminal prosecution for intentional evasion.
Box 5 Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) – assessed separately via GewSt 1, but the KSt return includes a reference to the trade tax base. Trade tax is levied on the PE's profit; non-residents with a PE are subject to trade tax. Late filing penalty: 0.25% of the assessed tax per month (minimum €25 per month, maximum €25,000). Late payment interest: 0.5% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for understatement: up to 10% of the understated tax. Criminal prosecution for intentional evasion.
Line 1 Trade income (Gewerbeertrag) – taxable profit adjusted for add-backs and deductions (e.g., 25% of interest expenses, 25% of rental payments). Non-residents with a PE in Germany must report income attributable to that PE. Same as KSt: late filing penalty, interest on arrears, and potential criminal sanctions for evasion.
Line 2 Trade tax allowance (Freibetrag) – €24,500 for individuals and partnerships; for corporations, no allowance is granted. Corporations (including UG) do not receive the allowance. Same as KSt: late filing penalty, interest on arrears, and potential criminal sanctions for evasion.
Line 3 Trade tax rate (Hebesatz) – municipal multiplier, typically 200–490% of the base rate (3.5%). The rate is set by the municipality where the PE is located. Same as KSt: late filing penalty, interest on arrears, and potential criminal sanctions for evasion.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Dokumentation) is mandatory if cross-border transactions with related parties exceed €5 million for goods or €1 million for services per fiscal year. Master File is required if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds €100 million. Additionally, a CbC Report is required for groups with consolidated revenue ≥€750 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the general anti-abuse rule (Section 42 AO) and the substance-over-form doctrine apply. For treaty benefits, the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI is applicable. A UG must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., office, employees, decision-making) to avoid being classified as a shell company and to secure treaty relief. The Federal Fiscal Court (BFH) has denied treaty benefits to entities lacking substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €50,000 for minor cases; imprisonment up to 5 years for serious cases. Additional penalty of up to 10% of the evaded tax (Zuschlag).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to fulfill their duties (e.g., not filing returns, not paying withholding taxes). Civil restitution for damages, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of additional taxes plus interest. Penalties up to €25,000 for non-compliance with substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes if the company is deemed a sham and they acted negligently. Civil liability for unpaid taxes, potential criminal charges for aiding tax evasion.
AML Violations (Geldwäschegesetz)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €100,000 for minor violations; up to €1 million for serious violations. Criminal liability for money laundering (up to 5 years imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent money laundering. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, additional tax, and interest. Penalties for late or incomplete documentation: up to 5% of the adjusted income (minimum €5,000, maximum €500,000).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they intentionally or negligently fail to ensure compliance. Civil liability for damages, and in extreme cases, criminal charges for tax evasion.
