Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) — www.bzst.de; Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF); electronic filing and taxpayer registration via ELSTER portal — www.elster.de; substantive tax assessment and audits by the locally competent tax office (Finanzamt), with centralized non-resident assessment for a UG without a German branch or permanent establishment carried out by Finanzamt Neukölln, Berlin

E-government filing portal

Before commencing any taxable supply in Germany, the foreign UG must (1) prepare and file the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) — obtainable on ELSTER — with the competent Finanzamt to secure the local tax number (Steuernummer) for corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; (2) apply separately to the BZSt for the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, format DE + 9 digits) using the statutory form under § 27a UStG, attaching certified corporate documents (trade register excerpt, articles of association, certificate of incorporation, proof of foreign VAT ID, appointment of directors, and identity documents of the managing directors); (3) complete registration in advance of the first supply, notwithstanding any reverse-charge structure, because Germany requires non-established suppliers to be registered even where VAT is declared by the recipient; for a UG with no German seat, the questionnaire is filed with Finanzamt Neukölln, Berlin, while a UG establishing a branch shifts jurisdiction to the local Finanzamt at the branch seat.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime and no pass-through election for a UG; the UG is mandatorily treated as a capital company subject to entity-level corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) on its entire German-taxable income. Available statutory elections affecting the tax identity include: (i) waiver of the VAT small-business exemption under § 19(2) UStG so as to charge VAT and claim input VAT deductions; (ii) option to tax real-estate transactions under § 9 UStG; (iii) formation of a VAT fiscal unity/profit-and-loss consolidation group (Organschaft) under § 2(2) Nr. 2 UStG; and (iv) limited hybrid-mismatch alignment only within the express boundaries of § 4k EStG — no election exists to reclassify the UG as transparent or to exclude it from entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 (prior-year turnover) and EUR 100,000 (current-year forecast) under the German small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG) for established taxable persons as of 2025; however, a non-established foreign supplier has no de minimis turnover threshold — any taxable supply of goods or services physically rendered in Germany triggers mandatory VAT registration with the BZSt unless the reverse-charge mechanism transfers the VAT liability to a German-registered business customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

An export supply (Ausfuhrlieferung) under §§ 4(1)(a) and 6 UStG qualifies for the 0% VAT rate where (i) the goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier, the customer, or a third-party carrier to a non-EU third-country destination (including certain free zones); (ii) the goods physically depart the EU customs territory within the statutory export chain; (iii) commercial and customs documentation individually proves the removal from the EU. For cryogenic stainless-steel pipe, prefabricated spools, and welding consumables supplied for an LNG receiving terminal, zero-rating applies to the goods component where title passes to the foreign customer and transport documents evidence arrival at the target third-country jetty. Offshore installation, welding, and insulation services physically rendered at the foreign LNG terminal are, depending on the recipient's status, either outside the scope of German VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (recipient jurisdiction) or zero-rated as ancillary services under § 4 Nr. 2 UStG, provided the recipient is a taxable person and contemporaneous contracts evidence the offshore nature of the work. All supporting evidence must be compiled and retained in accordance with §§ 8–10 UStDV.

  • Customs Export Declaration: electronic Ausfuhranmeldung in ATLAS with movement reference number (MRN) and certified customs export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) evidencing exit from the EU
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or equivalent transport document — Air Waybill or CMR consignment note — naming the exporting UG, goods description, quantity, and the non-EU destination port/jetty of the LNG receiving terminal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or foreign-exchange receipt certificate evidencing that the export proceeds have been credited to the UG's German or EU bank account within the statutory period
  • Supplementary bundle: commercial invoice, packing list, export contract/purchase order, Incoterms 2020 clause, and, where applicable, Intrastat records or EUR.1/Form A movement certificate

Germany mandates receipt of electronic B2B invoices (E-Rechnung) from 1 January 2025 and mandatory issuance for all German B2B suppliers from 1 January 2028, with an earlier issuance obligation from 1 January 2027 for suppliers whose prior-year turnover exceeded EUR 800,000; accepted machine-readable formats are XRechnung (EN 16931 XML standard) and the hybrid PDF/A-3 format ZUGFeRD 2.x; invoices must be transmitted electronically through automated data interchange — PDF-only or download-link invoices are non-compliant. Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, API tax stamping, or digital seal regimes; the model is post-audit compliance, but from 1 January 2028 transaction-level data on domestic B2B supplies must be transmitted annually to the BZSt under new § 18(4a) UStG, creating a structured transaction-reporting obligation.

Export zero-rating is subject to intensive audit scrutiny by the Finanzamt and criminal enforcement by the customs investigation service (Zollfahndung). Incomplete or deficient export evidence triggers denial of the 0% rate and retrospective re-assessment of 19% VAT on the full consideration, with default interest accruing at 0.5% per month (6% p.a.) under § 233a AO. Fabricated or sham export documentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO and document fraud under §§ 264 and 267 StGB, punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated commercial cases), monetary fines, and confiscation of the benefit. The limitation period for evasion-related assessments is extended to 10 years under § 169(2) AO, keeping VAT assessments continuously open for correction. The UG's managing director is personally exposed to a deficiency liability assessment (Haftungsbescheid) under §§ 69 and 71 AO, and the UG may be denied input VAT deduction under § 15(5) UStG. Repeat non-compliance results in mandatory permanent tax audit (Außenprüfung), seizure of project and piping-works records, and revocation of the export certification privileges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions for EUR and all major currencies. No exchange control permits are required for capital injection, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or loan servicing. The only requirements are statistical reporting obligations under §60 AWV for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500, including the two-digit compulsory purpose code (Zahlungszweck) to be reported to the Bundesbank via the reporting platform. Profit remittance is subject to the ordinary German corporate income tax and withholding tax framework (e.g., withholding taxes under the Investment Income Tax Act) but not to FX regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in any freely available currency in an authorized resident bank account. Cross-border receipts must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics if the transaction value equals or exceeds EUR 12,500 within 5 calendar days after the month of receipt; the report is filed electronically with the purpose code and counterparty data.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action for foreign trade and payments control under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border Financial Technology EMIs and neo-banks (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Revolut Business) with an EU banking/EMI licence may provide auxiliary payment wallets and cheaper spot FX for operating expenses. However, they are rated only 'Low-to-Medium' for primary treasury use in the LNG piping sector because: (i) most EMIs cannot issue or confirm letters of credit or access TARGET2/SEPA clearing for high-value supplier settlements; (ii) German banks and project sponsors require a traditional correspondent banking relationship for escrow and completion guarantees; (iii) AML compliance on project counterparties is often stricter when the contracting entity is a low-capital UG. Use EMIs only for petty cash and staff expense reimbursements; the majority of cross-border settlement must run via SWIFT MT103/MT700 through a tier-1 commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax under the Körperschaftsteuergesetz, plus a solidarity surcharge equal to 5.5% of the corporate income tax, producing a federal corporate tax burden of 15.825%. A UG is also subject to municipal trade tax, generally producing an aggregate corporate and trade tax burden of approximately 23% to 33%, depending on the municipal trade-tax multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital-yield withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic dividend withholding burden of 26.375%, before treaty relief or exemption. A German corporate shareholder may generally obtain partial exemption treatment under the participation exemption, subject to statutory conditions. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain full withholding-tax relief under the German implementation of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring a qualifying minimum participation, minimum holding period, subject-to-tax conditions, anti-abuse compliance, and an exemption certificate or refund procedure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border controlled transactions, a Local File is generally required when annual transaction volumes exceed EUR 6,000,000 for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, and royalties. The German taxpayer must maintain transaction documentation under Section 90 AO and the German documentation regulation. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group whose consolidated annual revenue is at least EUR 100,000,000. Documentation may be requested by the tax authority and must generally be supplied within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate statute labelled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to the regimes of certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless material under German transfer-pricing rules, the treaty beneficial-ownership requirement, the general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 AO, anti-treaty-shopping rules, CFC legislation, anti-hybrid rules, and corporate-residence principles. The UG should maintain genuine German management, decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises appropriate to its activities, commercial risk control, project records, bank-account control, and evidence that intercompany charges correspond to functions, assets, and risks. A low-substance intermediary receiving royalties, interest, or dividends may be denied treaty or directive relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 AO may result in imprisonment for up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax must be assessed and recovered with interest; aggravated cases may involve asset recovery and professional or procurement consequences.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal liability, personal tax liability for culpable failure to discharge tax duties under Section 69 AO, civil restitution, fines, and disqualification consequences. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from criminal proceedings, bail conditions, arrest warrants, or enforcement measures. Imprisonment is possible for culpable tax evasion.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The German payer may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, and penalties. Intentional or reckless withholding-tax violations may constitute tax evasion or tax-order offences, depending on the facts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they intentionally or grossly negligently failed to ensure withholding and remittance. Civil recovery, criminal fines, and imprisonment may apply in intentional cases; travel restrictions may arise only from separate criminal-procedure or enforcement measures.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, impose additional corporate and trade tax, deny deductions, assess interest, and impose documentation-related surcharges. Inadequate or late documentation can trigger a surcharge generally ranging from 5% to 10% of the adjustment, subject to statutory minimums and maximums, with additional consequences for non-cooperation.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for intentional or grossly negligent tax underpayment and may face criminal tax proceedings if manipulation was deliberate. Civil restitution and imprisonment are fact-dependent; travel restrictions are not an automatic transfer-pricing consequence.
Permanent-establishment concealment

Statutory sanction: Germany may tax attributable business profits, impose corporate income tax, trade tax, wage tax, VAT, interest, late-payment surcharges, and penalties. Failure to register or file may lead to administrative and criminal tax exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they culpably failed to register, file, or pay taxes. Intentional concealment may lead to fines or imprisonment, with civil restitution and possible procedural travel restrictions.
Shell company and treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: Treaty or directive benefits may be denied under beneficial-ownership, anti-treaty-shopping, principal-purpose, and German general anti-abuse rules. Germany may collect withholding tax, interest, penalties, and, where deception is intentional, criminal tax sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for knowingly implementing abusive arrangements, false certifications, or unsupported exemption claims. Personal tax liability, civil restitution, criminal fines, and imprisonment may arise; travel bans are possible only under separate judicial or enforcement conditions.
Accounting and corporate-law violations

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include administrative fines, forced filing or disclosure proceedings, rejection of tax positions, tax estimates, late-filing penalties, and liability for resulting tax losses. Serious fraudulent accounting may constitute a criminal offence.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can face civil claims from the UG, creditors, insolvency administrators, and tax authorities. Personal liability may include restitution and damages; imprisonment is possible for intentional fraudulent accounting or related criminal conduct.
Insolvency and unlawful distributions

Statutory sanction: The managing director may be required to repay prohibited payments and compensate creditors for losses. Insolvency offences can result in criminal fines or imprisonment, and tax claims remain enforceable against the company and potentially responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors may face direct civil restitution, creditor damages, disqualification, criminal fines, and imprisonment for delayed filing or fraudulent conduct. Travel restrictions may arise from insolvency-related criminal proceedings but are not automatic.
Anti-money laundering and sanctions breaches

Statutory sanction: Violations may trigger reporting duties, administrative fines, forfeiture, account restrictions, criminal prosecution, and sanctions under the German Money Laundering Act, Criminal Code, EU restrictive-measures regime, and applicable foreign sanctions laws.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines, criminal liability, asset forfeiture, civil restitution, and imprisonment for intentional laundering, sanctions evasion, or facilitation. Travel bans or asset freezes may apply under judicial or sanctions measures.
Construction withholding tax and labor-tax non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The principal may be liable for construction withholding tax, payroll tax, social-security contributions, interest, surcharges, administrative fines, and criminal sanctions for deliberate evasion or illegal employment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid wage tax and social-security amounts where culpable. Intentional payroll or social-security fraud may result in criminal fines or imprisonment, together with civil restitution and possible procedural travel restrictions.
