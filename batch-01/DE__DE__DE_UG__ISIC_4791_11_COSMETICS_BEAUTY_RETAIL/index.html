Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.), EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and certain central filings; local tax office (Finanzamt) at the UG's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment. Primary electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de). Customs authority: Generalzolldirektion / Hauptzollamt via the ATLAS electronic declaration system; EORI number issuance by Generalzolldirektion.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the UG articles of association and register in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht. Step 2: Submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt within four weeks of commencing business; this triggers issuance of a permanent income/corporate tax number (Steuernummer), the municipal trade tax base (Gewerbesteuermessbetrag) and the VAT delivery relationship for prepayment returns. Step 3: Apply separately to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) required for intra-EU supplies, reverse-charge documentation and OSS participation. Step 4: Obtain an EORI number from the Generalzolldirektion to run customs export declarations. Step 5: For B2C e-commerce into other EU member states, register for the Union-OSS scheme via the BZSt portal once the EUR 10,000 annual cross-border distance-sales threshold is exceeded; below the threshold supplies remain taxable in Germany. Non-resident ownership of the UG does not alter this domestic registration process, but foreign shareholders face 25% withholding tax (plus solidarity surcharge) at distribution, subject to treaty relief.

It is not pass-through eligible. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a domestic quasi-corporation (mini-GmbH) and a body corporate for corporate income tax (Secs. 1, 8 KStG). It is not pass-through eligible. Tax stack: 15% corporate income tax + 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal assessment rate (Hebesatz, commonly 200%-490%, average effective roughly 14%) with a EUR 24,500 trade tax allowance; combined effective burden approximately 30%-33%. The managing director's salary is deductible for CIT/trade tax but triggers wage tax and social security. Territorial scope: unlimited residence-based taxation on worldwide income; foreign permanent-establishment profits are treaty-exempt if a DTT applies and the subject-to-tax condition is met; the UG is not a tax-exempt zone entity.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; classification is statutory. Available elections and structural mechanisms: (i) Organschaft (corporate and trade tax fiscal unity) requiring a domination and profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Beherrschungs- und Gewinnabführungsvertrag) and financial, economic and organizational integration; (ii) VAT Organschaft under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG consolidating closely integrated enterprises for VAT; (iii) small business VAT exemption under Sec. 19 UStG if prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and expected current-year turnover not exceed EUR 50,000 (EUR 25,000/EUR 100,000 from 2025) — generally disadvantageous because input VAT recovery is forfeited; (iv) election of monthly VAT prepayment with permanent deadline extension (Dauerfristverlängerung) against special prepayment of 1/11th of prior-year VAT, or relief from prepayments where prior-year VAT is at most EUR 1,000; (v) Union-OSS and IOSS elections for distance sales and low-value consignments; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules (Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 8b KStG, ATAD II implementation) apply mandatorily without an elective opt-in; (vii) tax-neutral conversion of the UG into a GmbH under Sec. 20 UmwStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 — domestic entrepreneurs must register for VAT on their first euro of taxable supply; the small-business exemption threshold is EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover (2024; EUR 25,000 from 2025) with an expected current-year cap of EUR 50,000 (EUR 100,000 from 2025). For EU B2C distance sales, German VAT remains applicable until the Union-wide EUR 10,000 annual cross-border threshold is exceeded, above which destination-state VAT becomes due and Union-OSS registration at BZSt is the compliance path (Sec. 3c UStG).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT with input VAT recovery) applies to: (i) export deliveries to non-EU destinations (Ausfuhrlieferung, Sec. 6 UStG) where goods physically leave the EU customs territory by or on behalf of the seller; (ii) intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered businesses (Sec. 6a UStG) requiring a valid customer USt-IdNr. validated via VIES, transport evidence and compliant invoicing; (iii) certain offshore services under Sec. 3a UStG, though cosmetics retail is predominantly a goods supply. For EU B2C distance sales zero-rating does not apply: VAT is chargeable in the destination member state via OSS or local registration above the EUR 10,000 threshold. Non-EU consumer exports (e.g. UK, Switzerland, US) qualify for zero-rating upon documentary proof. Substantiation must follow the dual-evidence standard of Secs. 8-9 UStDV (Belegnachweis documentary proof) and Sec. 13a UStDV (Buchnachweis accounting proof); evidence should be complete when the VAT return is filed, with the tax office typically allowing a grace period of up to 60 days (Sec. 109 AO), and all records must be retained for ten years.

  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) stating seller, buyer, Incoterms, cosmetic product description, HS codes and value in EUR
  • Bill of lading / CMR waybill / air waybill / postal or courier receipt with tracking status evidencing physical departure from the EU customs territory
  • Transport dispatch confirmation (Versandanzeige) and, where the contract is ex-works, a certificate of arrival (Empfangsbescheinigung) from the foreign customer or carrier
  • Inward remittance certificate (Eingangsrechnung) — SWIFT MT103, bank statement, or Shopify Payments/PayPal settlement report demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in EUR
  • EORI number of the exporting UG and, for intra-Community supplies, the customer's validated USt-IdNr. via VIES together with a summary invoice where applicable
  • EU sales list (Zusammenfassende Meldung) confirmation for IC supplies; OSS return filing confirmation for EU B2C distance sales; Intrastat dispatch declaration where the reporting threshold is exceeded

Phased B2B e-invoicing mandate under the Growth Opportunities Act: from 1 January 2025 all B2B sellers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931 formats: XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 issuance is mandatory for all businesses. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital escrow regime comparable to Italy's SDI; compliance is retrospective via ELSTER VAT prepayment returns (monthly/quarterly) and annual VAT returns, all filed electronically. B2C invoices must be issued within six months with mandatory statutory content (Sec. 14 UStG). Customs exports are declared electronically through ATLAS with an MRN; intra-EU dispatches/arrivals require Intrastat monthly filings where thresholds are met (2024: EUR 500,000 dispatches / EUR 800,000 arrivals); EU sales lists (Zusammenfassende Meldung) must be filed for intra-Community supplies.

Export substantiation files are a designated audit focus in German VAT audits (Außenprüfung and the tax authorities' minimum audit examination criteria). Missing or defective proof of export causes retrospective revocation of the 0% rate with assessment of 19% German VAT on the export invoice, plus cross-border exposure where the foreign customer cannot evidence tax liability. Reassessments accrue interest at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum, Sec. 233a AO); late payments incur a surcharge of 1% per commenced month (Sec. 240 AO). Fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger criminal liability under Sec. 370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, or 6 months to 10 years in aggravated cases) and fines under Sec. 378 AO up to EUR 50,000 for negligent under-declaration. Personal exposure extends to the managing director under Secs. 34, 69 and 71 AO, who is liable even before company assets are realized. Limitation periods: 4-year standard assessment period, 5 years for negligent under-declaration, and 10 years for deliberate evasion. BZSt and customs cooperate in joint audit and intelligence programs; although German law does not impose a general statutory FX repatriation deadline, a payment traceability gap beyond 180 days from dispatch materially weakens the export substantiation file and invites clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, the EU Capital Movements Directive (Directive 88/361/EEC) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV). There are no foreign exchange controls, no surrender or conversion mandates, and no exchange licensing requirements for the repatriation of profits, dividends, interest, royalties, or the repayment of intra-company loans. Profit remittances by a German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft) are legally unrestricted; however, a dividend distribution to a foreign shareholder is subject to German withholding tax at 26.375% (25% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, before any applicable double-tax treaty relief), and trade tax may affect distributable profits depending on local municipal rates and the business's local permanence. All cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes under the AWV, but this is a statistical reporting duty, not a control or prior-approval requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts. A German exporter may hold foreign currency proceeds in either domestic or foreign bank accounts indefinitely, subject to general banking and anti-money-laundering obligations. The only applicable duty is the periodic or transaction-level reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) section 61 et seq. for claims and liabilities against non-residents or payments received from non-residents, where the reporting threshold is an amount equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 or an aggregate reporting threshold under the Bundesbank's external statistical reporting instructions.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), acting in conjunction with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border digital e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under a European passport may be used for e-commerce payment facilitation and settlement into a German UG's treasury structure. Under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG), an EU/EEA-licensed EMI may provide payment services in Germany on a cross-border basis via freedom to provide services, provided it is registered with BaFin in the payment services register for passporting. Feasibility rating: High for operational treasury layers (receivables collection, supplier disbursement in EUR and foreign currency) but Low for a full primary banking relationship, because an EMI is not a credit institution, does not offer statutory deposit insurance under the German Deposit Guarantee Act (Einlagensicherungsgesetz), and cannot provide credit, FX hedging lines, or hold shareholder capital as escrow in the same commercially prudent manner as a fully licensed German bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital contributions to a German UG must be routed via compliant cross-border wire protocols using the SWIFT MT103 message with a precise purpose code. For a contribution into the company's capital reserve or as a shareholder loan, the remitter should instruct its bank to use SWIFT MT103 with the BNPR currency final credit code, and in the remittance information field state the purpose as 'Kapitalrücklage' or 'Einlage in die Kapitalrücklage' and reference the UG's commercial register number. Where the Deutsche Bundesbank statistical reporting is required, the receiving German bank will use the Bundesbank's Aufgabenträger identification and the 'Vermögensübertragung' category. The inbound capital injection is fully liberalized; no exchange control permission is required, but the UG must record the receipt in its accounting records, ensure the notarial capital increase formalities are completed if the share capital itself is being increased, and issue a shareholder contribution notification for the bank's files. The preferred routing is a direct SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the UG's account at a tier-1 German bank, with the shareholder identified as the ordering customer and the UG as the beneficiary, and with the originator and beneficiary IBAN fields populated accurately.Outbound trade settlements for the procurement and distribution of cosmetics and personal care products should use the following clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR payments within the SEPA zone; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA cross-border EUR and foreign-currency payments with full structured remittance data; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (Documentary Credits) governed by UCP 600 for high-value supplier contracts where security of payment is required; and (iv) e-commerce marketplace settlement rails for transaction-level disbursements. For non-EUR supplier payments, the German bank will convert EUR into the target currency using standard FX spot execution unless a forward hedge has been contracted. All outbound payments must contain accurate purpose codes under the AWV reporting framework if the payment amount exceeds EUR 12,500, and the underlying invoice, customs declaration, or supply contract should be retained to evidence the commercial substance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border digital e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under a European passport may be used for e-commerce payment facilitation and settlement into a German UG's treasury structure. Under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG), an EU/EEA-licensed EMI may provide payment services in Germany on a cross-border basis via freedom to provide services, provided it is registered with BaFin in the payment services register for passporting. Feasibility rating: High for operational treasury layers (receivables collection, supplier disbursement in EUR and foreign currency) but Low for a full primary banking relationship, because an EMI is not a credit institution, does not offer statutory deposit insurance under the German Deposit Guarantee Act (Einlagensicherungsgesetz), and cannot provide credit, FX hedging lines, or hold shareholder capital as escrow in the same commercially prudent manner as a fully licensed German bank.
