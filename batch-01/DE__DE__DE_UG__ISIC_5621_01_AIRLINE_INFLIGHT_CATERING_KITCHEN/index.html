Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum statutory share capital: a UG may be formed with nominal capital of at least EUR 1.00 (§ 5a GmbHG). Contributions must be made in cash only (no contributions in kind). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen – BMF) sets policy; tax administration is decentralized: the locally competent Finanzamt assesses and collects corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT; the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) issues VAT IDs (USt-IdNr.) and administers EU OSS and VAT refunds; official portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for tax filings and BZSt Online-Portal (BOP) for VAT ID and OSS filings.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: notarize the articles of incorporation (UG with share capital of at least EUR 1, no statutory minimum capital, but with a statutory reserve-building requirement) and register in the commercial register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht; also file Gewerbeanmeldung at the municipal trade office, which is forwarded to the tax office. Step 2: file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER within four weeks after business commencement; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer for CIT, trade tax and VAT. Step 3: apply for the EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) through the BZSt Online-Portal once the Steuernummer is assigned; processing typically takes 2–6 weeks and is mandatory for intra-EU transactions. Step 4 (non-resident parent): foreign shareholders may own 100% of a German UG without a special tax permit; if a foreign parent uses the UG as its deemed permanent establishment, the Finanzamt may require appointment of a domestic agent for service of process (Zustellungsbevollmächtigter) and security (Sicherheitsleistung). All tax returns and VAT returns must be filed electronically via ELSTER.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the UG is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with independent tax personality, subject to unrestricted German tax liability on worldwide income based on statutory seat and/or place of effective management; applies to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), solidarity surcharge (0.825% effective) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate approximately 14–17% depending on municipal multiplier); aggregate corporate tax burden approximately 30–33%; no look-through or pass-through treatment for German tax purposes; the UG itself is the taxable entrepreneur (Unternehmer) for VAT purposes regardless of shareholder residence.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box or free entity-classification election; a UG is mandatorily a taxable corporation. Available statutory elections include: (1) corporate-income-tax and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §14 KStG and §2(2) UStG if the UG is financially, economically and organizationally integrated into a German parent under a profit-and-loss pooling agreement; (2) small-business exemption under §19 UStG for annual turnover up to EUR 25,000 (2025 threshold; EUR 22,000 in 2024), including the binding five-year option to waive the exemption under §19(2) UStG to enter regular VAT and recover input VAT; (3) intra-EU supply substantiation methods under §6a UStG (Gelangensbestätigung); (4) option to tax real property transactions under §9 UStG, which is not relevant to inflight catering; (5) hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory, not elective: §4k EStG and §20(6) KStG override any contractual or structural hybrid arrangements by disallowing expense deductions or requiring corresponding income inclusions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for a domestic UG: a UG is a taxable person from its first taxable supply. The small-business exemption threshold is EUR 25,000 annual turnover from 1 January 2025 (EUR 22,000 in 2024), but the UG in airline catering will waive it to claim input VAT recovery. Foreign (non-EU) suppliers must register for German VAT from the first euro of taxable activity in Germany; intra-EU distance-sales and B2C digital-services threshold is EUR 10,000 (pan-EU); the German intra-Community acquisition threshold is EUR 12,500.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT with full input-VAT recovery (echte Steuerbefreiung) applies through two routes: (1) export of meals and goods physically removed to non-EU third countries under §4(1)(1) and §6 UStG, evidenced by customs export formalities; (2) international aviation catering under §4(1)(2) and §8(1)/(2) UStG, implementing Article 148(e) of the VAT Directive: supplies of on-board meals, beverages, cutlery, disposables and equipment delivered directly to aircraft ramp-side are exempt when the airline is an entrepreneur and the aircraft operates international air traffic with at least one non-EU destination (route-based test). Supplies for flights serving exclusively intra-EU legs are taxable at 19% in the Member State of departure (place-of-departure rule); the reduced food rate does not apply because in-flight meal supply is a catering service. The exemption requires documentary traceability of each delivered batch to a specific international flight, including airline route schedules and signed ramp delivery notes; BMF administrative guidance demands these records for every zero-rated delivery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Ausfuhranmeldung via the ATLAS system with customs exit confirmation/Abgabevermerk)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (AWB) or CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the foreign consideration (banking substantiation; German tax law imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, but audit practice expects payment within 180 days and unreliable cash flow may trigger §17 UStG VAT adjustment)
  • Commercial invoice (Handelsrechnung) with a zero-rating clause and statutory VAT-exemption annotation under §6 UStG or §8 UStG
  • Gelangensbestätigung (delivery arrival confirmation) for intra-EU movement of goods where applicable
  • Airline catering order form, flight route schedule and signed ramp-delivery receipt proving the aircraft's international (non-EU) sector for every zero-rated batch

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or government pre-approval of invoices (unlike Italy's SdI). Mandatory structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) applies for domestic B2B supplies: from 1 January 2025, every German taxable person must be able to receive electronic invoices in a structured data format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X 2.x), transmitted via the Peppol network, a secure API or qualified e-mail; mandatory issuance is phased in from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all businesses. For the inflight-catering kitchen, invoices issued to German airlines and invoices received from German suppliers must comply; non-compliance can trigger fines under §26a UStG up to EUR 5,000 and may cause the recipient to lose input VAT deduction.

The Finanzamt and customs authorities (Zoll) audit export zero-rating strictly. If the export proof or international-route evidence is missing or incomplete, VAT is retroactively assessed at 19% for the relevant supplies within the statutory assessment period (generally 4 years after filing; 7 years for lightly negligent omissions; up to 10 years for deliberate evasion under §§169–171 AO), plus statutory late-payment interest under §233a AO (6% per annum for periods before 2025; 1.8% per annum from 1 January 2025 under the 2025 Tax Development Act) and input-VAT corrections under §17 UStG. Regulatory fines for invoice and documentary violations apply under §26a UStG up to EUR 5,000 per case. Intentional use of forged export evidence, shell buyers or circular transactions constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years for aggravated or organized cases), monetary fines, confiscation orders and publication of the judgment; BZSt and Eurofisc data exchange links German zero-rated export declarations with foreign customs and banking data, materially increasing detection risk for fraudulent inflight-catering export schemes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides unrestricted full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions, consistent with EU Treaty obligations. The EUR is fully free-floating and legal tender. Profit distribution and dividend remittance from the UG to non-resident shareholders are permitted without foreign-exchange approval; the only limitations are (i) the obligation to file statistical cross-border payment reports to the Bundesbank when a transaction exceeds EUR 12,500 or a monthly balance exceeds EUR 25,000; (ii) compliance with EU/OFAC sanctions, which may forbid specific payment routes or counterparties; and (iii) tax clearance thresholds for German corporate income tax (e.g., submission of Form ZQ to the Federal Central Tax Office for reduction of withholding tax). No general capital control, deposit requirement, or exchange admission scheme applies to the inflight-catering sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since 1991, Germany has had no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement. Exporters, including an airport inflight-catering kitchen, may keep revenue in any convertible currency. No mandatory conversion ratio exists. Yet the Deutsche Bundesbank requires monthly (or quarterly) external transactions reporting for every cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 or for net claims/receivables beyond EUR 25,000 per month; the reporting duty is discharged by the bank or by the company (depending on the reporting category). For a catering kitchen supplying foreign airlines, export invoices should be recorded under the 'transport auxiliary services' or 'goods' statistical code and include the client's airline operator code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of Germany) as implementing authority for external-sector statistical reporting; the European Central Bank (ECB) for Eurosystem monetary policy and TARGET2 clearing; and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for prudential and AML/CFT supervision over banks and banking products. The policy and regulatory legal source is the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) and the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz – GwG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore (non-EU/EWR) digital EMIs have a LOW viability as the principal corporate banking rail for a German UG, because BaFin does not recognise the EMI's home license for 'German account' status, the guarantee of deposit insurance is absent, and German tax authorities expect the company to maintain a genuine domestic payment operation. EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., a BaFin-licensed electronic money institution or passported ECA/EMI such as Penta, Airbank, Wise Business, etc.) provide a MEDIUM-HIGH feasibility for an additional operational/card-settlement wallet but cannot replace a true German current account; they are prohibited from performing credit operations and must maintain customer funds in separate accounts. The UG must nevertheless record all payments in its German books and ensure the EMI's KYC accepts the non-resident shareholder; the legal constraint under §25a KWG requires a 'sedes et administration' of payments in Germany.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag, effective 15.825%) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), producing a typical combined aggregate rate of 30–33% depending on the local Hebesatz.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% for shareholders resident in Germany (25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag). For non-resident corporate shareholders, the statutory rate is likewise 26.375% unless reduced by an applicable DTA or by the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU), which permits a 0% rate for a qualifying EU parent holding at least 10% for 12 months.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The UG must prepare contemporaneous Local File documentation for cross-border transactions with related parties if its aggregate related-party goods transactions exceed EUR 6,000,000, or services/intangibles/other transactions exceed EUR 600,000 in a fiscal year. A Master File is mandatory when the group's consolidated worldwide revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Local File must be submitted within 60 days of a tax audit request; Master File within 30 days. In the absence of acceptable documentation, penalties under § 162(4) AO apply.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no formal Economic Substance Regulations analogous to those in offshore jurisdictions. However, the principle of economic substance is embedded in § 42 AO (abuse of legal forms), the OECD DTA main purpose test, German anti-treaty-shopping rules, and controlled foreign company provisions (§§ 7–14 AStG). A UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) engaged in airline inflight catering must be operated with real staff, kitchen assets, procurement contracts, delivery logistics, and board control in Germany in order to be recognised for treaty/directive entitlement and to avoid reclassification as a shell or conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; in especially serious cases (large-scale fraud, fraudulent use of official documents) imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years. Civil assessment is computed on the full undeclared profit plus default interest at 6% p.a. and late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is jointly and severally liable under §§ 34 and 69 AO for taxes not declared or paid to the German Treasury. In case of intentional evasion, the director faces personal criminal conviction, seizure of business assets, travel bans, and disqualification from holding managerial positions. Failure to remit wage or sales taxes can trigger immediate personal recovery orders (Vermögensarrest).
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: German tax authorities may deny the taxpayer's pricing position and apply a tax-increasing arm's-length adjustment. § 162(4) AO imposes a penalty of at least EUR 5,000 and up to 10% of the adjusted additional income; for late or incomplete documentation, an additional penalty of up to EUR 100,000 may be assessed. The burden of proof shifts against the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved deficient documentation may be personally assessed for the penalty under § 69 AO, and if the failure was intentional or grossly negligent, they may face separate fines or imprisonment for aiding tax evasion. The company's management can be ordered to produce an acceptable transfer pricing study at substantial cost.
Shell Company or Conduit With Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the MLI principal purpose test and § 42 AO, the disallowance of deductions for payments to the conduit, recharacterisation of interest or royalties as constructive dividends, CFC inclusion of passive income under §§ 7–14 AStG, and a 30%-based penalty under § 162(3) AO. The company license for catering may be revoked by the Luftfahrt-Bundesamt or the local trade authority for lack of effective business operations.

Director personal liability: Directors of the UG become personally liable for all underwithheld taxes and penalties; in cases of intentional abuse, they face imprisonment for tax evasion and may be banned from future corporate management. The corporate veil is pierced for restitution purposes, allowing personal assets and foreign accounts to be seized.
VAT, Customs and Excise Infringements in Bonded Catering

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under § 370 AO and the national Customs Law: imprisonment up to 5 years or fine where tax/customs duty is evaded; unpaid duties plus interest will be assessed, and customs findings may force the withdrawal of the importation and shipping licence. For negligent violation, fines of up to EUR 30,000 and mandatory closure of the bonded warehouse can be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally bound to ensure customs and excise compliance under the German Foreign Trade and Customs Law. Personal liability includes the unpaid customs tax, fines, seizure of catering vehicles and equipment, and if the offense was committed deliberately, travel bans and up to 10 years imprisonment for organised commercial-scale diversion. The authorities may also enforce personal criminal liability against the head of logistics even if he acted under instruction.
