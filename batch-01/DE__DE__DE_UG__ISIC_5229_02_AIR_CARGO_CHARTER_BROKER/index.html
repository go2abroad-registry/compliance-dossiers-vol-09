UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: German Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), in particular sections 1(13)-(14), 10-17, 19-23 and 20; Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB), section 325 and related disclosure provisions; Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), sections 8, 10 and 40; German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG); applicable directly effective EU AML, sanctions and restrictive-measures regulations; Transparency Register and Transparency Register Data Platform Regulation (Transparenzregister- und Finanzinformationsgesetz and related regulations). Germany has a transparency register rather than a generally public beneficial-ownership register. The beneficial owner is generally the natural person who ultimately owns more than 25 percent of capital or voting rights or exercises control by comparable means; if no such person can be identified after documented investigation, the legal representatives or managing directors are deemed notional beneficial owners under section 3(2) GwG.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A UG must have a German domestic business address capable of receiving official service under sections 8 and 10 GmbHG. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine accessible business address, reliable receipt and forwarding of official correspondence, and the entity can be contacted there. A mailbox-only, accommodation or fictitious address is not acceptable. For international air-freight forwarding and cargo-aircraft charter brokerage, a virtual office is legally weak and commercially high-risk unless supplemented by documented management, brokerage, compliance, records, communications and operational arrangements in Germany. The entity must separately assess trade-registration, customs, aviation, freight-forwarding, sanctions, export-control, data-protection and tax requirements. Brokerage does not itself make the company an air carrier; operating aircraft or performing regulated air-carrier functions may require an EU operating licence, Air Operator Certificate and other aviation approvals.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Unternehmensregister (official Company Register platform: unternehmensregister.de), with filings submitted through the German commercial-register filing system and reviewed by the competent local Amtsgericht/Registergericht

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no general statutory company-name reservation procedure or uniform 30-day reservation window. Obtain a written IHK name-opinion before notarization, conduct commercial-register, trademark, domain, trade-name and sanctions-name searches, and request the notary to pre-clear the proposed name with the competent Registergericht. If the Registergericht rejects the name or issues an intermediate order, respond within the stated deadline with amended articles, evidence of distinctiveness, IHK correspondence and any required authorization. A judicial complaint against a final register-court decision is generally available under the FamFG appeal framework, normally through the issuing court within the applicable statutory appeal period stated in the decision. A separate trademark objection or cancellation matter must be handled before the German Patent and Trade Mark Office or competent civil court. Do not commence trading under a rejected name.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID and federal matters; local tax offices (Finanzämter) for income/corporate tax registration; official portal ELSTER (Elster Online Portal).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with German tax nexus must file registration with the competent local Finanzamt via Form Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung, obtaining a personal tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income and trade tax; the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) is applied for separately through BZSt using Form USt 1 VA / specific online application. A fiscal representative may be required for non-EU entities in certain VAT cases. The UG must register under its German commercial register file, providing certified constitutional documents, proof of legal representative, and expected taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a general check-the-box regime; a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) and GmbH are treated as corporations subject to Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer at entity level. Available statutory elections include Organschaft (fiscal unity) election for corporate income tax and VAT between a domestic controlling entity and a financially integrated subsidiary, and the small-enterprise VAT exemption election under Section 19 UStG. Hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed under Section 4k EStG with corresponding adjustment elections in cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business exemption: prior-year turnover under EUR 22,000 and current-year prospective turnover under EUR 50,000; non-resident suppliers performing taxable supplies in Germany generally have mandatory VAT registration without an economic threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating under Section 4 No. 1 UStG applies to goods dispatched or transported to a third-country destination (export supply) and to certain cross-border services, including international air cargo transport and ancillary services relating directly to cargo transportation from or to non-EU territory. The UG broker must hold documentary evidence proving the goods left the EU, that the services are directly connected to export cargo transport, and that the recipient is a non-taxable or third-country person where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhr- bzw. Zollanmeldung, ATLAS/NCTS confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing carriage to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/transfer proof confirming foreign currency settlement from the non-EU buyer
  • Commercial invoice and contract of carriage/charter relating to the air cargo services

Since 1 January 2025, Germany mandates B2B electronic invoicing (E-invoice) for domestic taxable persons; invoices must be transmitted in a structured electronic format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or PEPPOL BIS-compatible). Germany does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime; no API-based validation by tax authorities is required before invoice issuance, but electronic invoices must be machine-readable and archivable for 10 years.

The tax office may conduct documentary audits and require substantiation of export supplies. Missing, incomplete, or falsified evidence leads to denial of zero-rating, retrospective VAT clawback, interest under Section 233a AO (0.5% per month), and penalties under Sections 369–376 AO and 26a/26b UStG, including forfeiture, imprisonment up to 10 years for tax evasion or serious tax fraud, and exclusion from export-verified procedures. The burden of proof rests on the taxable person.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU capital movement liberalization (Article 63 TFEU). Profit remittances are unrestricted, subject to standard corporate documentation (e.g., audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates) and anti-money laundering (AML) verification. No exchange control approval is required for dividend distributions or repatriation of capital, but statistical reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 (AWV reporting).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border receipts and payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance of payments statistics within five business days of the transaction (AWV §67). No conversion ratio is mandated; the exporter may freely choose currency denomination.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry of Finance; foreign exchange statistics and reporting are administered by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. German-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, N26 Business, or Solarisbank offer multi-currency IBANs and SWIFT connectivity. Legal constraints: EMIs are not full banks and cannot provide credit facilities or hold client funds as deposits; they are subject to BaFin supervision and must comply with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). For a UG with medium-risk air cargo brokerage, EMIs are viable for operational liquidity but may face enhanced due diligence for high-volume cross-border flows. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK-based) are less viable due to post-Brexit passporting loss and stricter German correspondent banking requirements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) + 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 8%-17.15%, yielding combined effective rate of approximately 28%-32% in major cities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag). Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, a non-resident EU parent holding 10% or more for at least 12 months can receive dividends free of German WHT; DTA rates typically 5%-15% for substantive foreign shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Base statutory deadline: 31 July following the fiscal year end (calendar-year taxpayers). Automatic extension to 28 February of the second following year when the return is prepared by a German tax advisor. A court-order extension under §109 AO can be applied. Payments remain due irrespective of filing extension.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same as corporate income tax return: 31 July after fiscal year end; automatic extension to 28 February of the second subsequent year with tax-advisor preparation; the municipality may separately apply a later deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 for controlled cross-border goods transactions; EUR 500,000 for controlled services (including charter brokerage, freight forwarding fees and management fees) per fiscal year. If these thresholds are exceeded, a Local File must be prepared and submitted within 60 days upon Finanzamt request; Master File is required when group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations resembling the UAE ESR. Instead, the German general anti-abuse rule (§42 AO) and §50d(3) EStG / treaty principal-purpose test apply. A UG structured as a bare intermediary for air cargo charter brokerage without its own office, employees, business direction, bank account or client contracts will be disregarded for tax treaty relief and its income reallocated to the controlling foreign shareholder. Real economic substance—genuine management decisions, local operational staff, physical office, German bank account and commercial inventory—is the precondition for claiming DTA benefits and for avoiding immediate deemed dividend treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years under §370 AO (10 years in aggravated cases with gross commercial falsehood and amount exploiting irregular sources); criminal fine up to EUR 250,000 plus confiscation of the illegally obtained tax advantage; statutory late-payment interest at 1.8% p.a.; penalties can reach 25% of unpaid tax for evasion; trade tax adjustments increase municipal liability.

Director personal liability: Managing director is personally liable under §§34, 69 AO for the company’s unpaid tax arrears if taxes were not paid from managed funds; for evasion, the director faces direct criminal prosecution, personal fine, asset seizure, Interpol alert where foreign travel is attempted, revocation of German residence or entry ban under §6 AufenthG for third-country nationals, and disqualification from directorship for up to 5 years.
Treaty Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief under §50d(3) EStG and the principal-purpose test of the Multilateral Instrument; recharacterisation of the income as deemed dividend subject to 26.375% WHT; additional corporate income tax at 15.825% plus trade tax on reallocated profits; fines and negligence penalties up to EUR 185,000; if criminal intent is proven, prosecution under §370 AO for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrated the sham company can be held personally liable as beneficial owners and for improper management under §§34, 69 AO; civil restitution of taxes, travel bans imposed by German customs authorities while criminal tax proceedings are pending, extradition on a European Arrest Warrant, and disqualification from future management positions under §6 Abs. 2 GesO?
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward income adjustment under §1 AStG to the arm's length profit; penalty surcharge under §162(4) AO of 5%-10% of the adjustment amount, minimum EUR 5,000; automatic interest under §233a AO of 1.8% p.a.; if documentation is not submitted within 30/60/90 days, mandatory estimate and additional penalty of up to EUR 50,000; potential criminal prosecution if the non-arm's length pricing was part of a tax evasion scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the extra corporate tax if they signed a false corporate return (secondary liability under §§69, 71 AO in certain cases); the criminal court can impose a fine on the managing director as a natural person; professional disqualification under §7? and civil derivative actions by foreign parents are possible.
Sham Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax office will disregard the UG under §42 AO and assign income to the persons performing the function; treaty relief is unavailable; dividend withholding tax is imposed on deemed profit distributions; registration in the Trade Register alone is not sufficient; fines up to EUR 250,000 for violation of §370 AO if the sham was designed to evade taxes; the trade license for air cargo charter brokering may be revoked by the Luftfahrt-Bundesamt for lack of business establishment.

Director personal liability: Directors who allowed the sham acquisition of the UG are personally liable for tax shortfalls; the German tax authority may seize private assets; a personal travel ban and arrest warrant can be issued if the foreign owner attempts to leave Germany; disqualification from holding a transport broker licence and from acting as managing director of any German company for up to 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 5 million or 10% of total group turnover under §56 GwG; criminal fine and imprisonment under §261 StGB for money laundering up to 5 years (10 years in aggravated cases); confiscation of bank balances and real estate; the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) may impose an industry ban.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecuted under §261 StGB as principals or accomplices if they ordered or failed to prevent the transaction; personal fines calculated from company turnover; travel bans under §§112, 132 StPO for the duration of the investigation; the court may order an asset freeze and seizure of passport; conviction results in a ban from managing commercial enterprises under §70 StGB.
