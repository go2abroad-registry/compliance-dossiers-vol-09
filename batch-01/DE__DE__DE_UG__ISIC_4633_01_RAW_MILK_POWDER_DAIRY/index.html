Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) — competent for the national VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and central taxpayer services; locally competent tax office (Finanzamt) — competent for corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll tax and VAT assessment; official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must first be registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the local Amtsgericht; the managing director then submits the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' via ELSTER to the Finanzamt having jurisdiction over the registered seat (Verwaltungsvermögen/Betriebsstätte). The Finanzamt allocates the Steuernummer, the statutory local tax number used for all ongoing tax filings. Where the entrepreneur declares a need for intra-EU or export transactions in the Fragebogen, the Finanzamt automatically forwards the request to BZSt, which assigns the national Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer; foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic seat or permanent establishment apply directly to BZSt using Form USt 1. Non-resident shareholding does not alter the domestic registration procedure; the UG must at all times maintain a resident managing director authorised to receive tax correspondence. Registration must be effected promptly upon commencement of business; failure to register or use of an unallocated tax number exposes the issuer to liability for incorrectly stated VAT under §14c UStG and to administrative fines.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law has no check-the-box regime; the UG, as a Kapitalgesellschaft, is irrevocably subject to entity-level taxation (Körperschaftsteuer 15%, solidarity surcharge 5.5% of corporate tax, and municipal trade tax Gewerbesteuer). Available statutory elections include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under KStG §§14–19 and UStG §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2, allowing the UG to be fiscally integrated into a German parent group for corporate, trade and VAT purposes; (ii) the small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG, available only where preceding-year turnover did not exceed €22,000 and current-year expected turnover does not exceed €50,000; (iii) trade tax allocation and apportionment elections under GewStG §§28 ff. for multi-establishment businesses; and (iv) alignment options for hybrid entities and hybrid instrument mismatches under the ATAD implementation (§4k EStG, §8b KStG), including the election to align taxable periods and the option to treat a foreign transparent entity as corporation for German tax purposes so as to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies from the first euro of entrepreneurial activity for all entrepreneurs under §2 UStG; the domestic UG active in cross-border milk powder commodity trade cannot rely on the §19 UStG small-business exemption unless immediately preceding-year turnover was ≤ €22,000 and current-year expected turnover ≤ €50,000. Intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2C dispatches trigger registration in the destination Member State from €10,000 annual cross-border turnover unless the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime is used; imports and intra-EU acquisitions are separately taxable and registrable as applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of whole and skimmed milk powder are tax-exempt with full input VAT recovery (effective 0%) under §4 Nr. 1a UStG in conjunction with §6 UStG where the goods physically leave the customs territory of the EU as a result of the supply. The UG must hold an uninterrupted and conclusive chain of documentary proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) under §§8–13 UStDV; the electronic customs export declaration must name the UG as exporter of record, and the transport documents must evidence actual border crossing to the third-country destination. Intra-EU consignments of milk powder are exempt under §4 Nr. 1b UStG only where the EU customer's valid VAT identification number is recorded and the supply is properly reported in the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung); ancillary transport, insurance and handling services closely connected to the export supply qualify for the corresponding exemption. Zero-rating is denied where the exporter fails to substantiate removal from the EU or where the goods are diverted for domestic consumption.

  • Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung) satisfying §14 UStG content requirements and specifying Incoterms 2020
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or Air Waybill (Luftfrachtbrief) evidencing shipment to the non-EU destination
  • Packing List (Packliste) and certificate of origin for the milk powder consignment
  • Proof of export per §§10–13 UStDV comprising the electronic customs confirmation or, alternatively, the commercial Belegnachweis (transport order, delivery note, payment and bank transfer evidence)
  • Intra-EU package where applicable: EU Sales Listing (Zusammenfassende Meldung), Intrastat declaration, and the EU customer's valid VAT identification number confirmation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of the foreign currency sales proceeds via SWIFT LC or TT wire, required under the corporate treasury compliance benchmark and corroborating the export-proof chain

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) system; no digital fiscal stamp or central government invoice clearance exists. From 1 January 2025, under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz), every German entrepreneur must be technically capable of receiving structured electronic B2B invoices in an EN 16931-compliant format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); paper or PDF invoices remain permissible during the transitional period through 31 December 2026 with recipient consent. From 1 January 2027, e-invoicing becomes mandatory for issuers whose preceding-year turnover exceeded €800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all issuers; EDI-based exchange formats are grandfathered until 31 December 2027. B2G invoicing already requires XRechnung in the PEPPOL/OZG environment. Export invoices for milk powder must satisfy the full content requirements of §14 UStG and be issued no later than six months after supply (for intra-EU dispatches, by the 15th day of the month following the month of supply under §14a UStG); no electronic stamping or real-time reporting is imposed on export transactions.

The Finanzamt and German customs (Zoll) conduct automated audit matching between export declarations (ECS/ATLAS), Intrastat data and VAT returns; zero-rating is accepted only where the Ausfuhrnachweis is admissible, complete and conclusive. Unsupported or false zero-rating claims are retrospectively recharacterised as taxable domestic supplies at 19% VAT, triggering a supplementary assessment with monthly interest at 0.5% under §233a AO (6% per annum), a monthly late-payment surcharge of 1% under §240 AO, and a filing-delay surcharge under §152 AO of up to 25% of the VAT assessed. Where export documents are forged, the export is fictitious, or the transaction forms part of a missing-trader or carousel fraud chain, the UG and its managing director are exposed to criminal tax evasion proceedings under §370 AO with imprisonment up to five years (up to ten years for aggravated cases), confiscation of the proceeds of crime, joint and several liability under §70 AO, administrative fines under §§378, 26a and 26c UStG-registered violations, immediate revocation of the VAT identification number by BZSt, and an extended assessment limitation period of ten years for fraudulent conduct (eight years for gross negligence, four years as the standard period). The burden of proof for the actual physical export rests squarely on the exporter under §6 Abs. 3 UStG and §§8–13 UStDV; records and underlying transaction documents must be retained for the statutory retention period (6–10 years under §147 AO and §257 HGB) and remain subject to customs and tax inspection at all times.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility prevails for both current account operations (goods, services, royalties, freight) and capital account operations (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend distributions); the euro is a freely convertible reserve currency, and Germany imposes no exchange-control authorisation, no central bank approval, no profit-remittance ceiling and no prior administrative clearance for a German UG. The dairy commodity trade (whole and skimmed milk powder under CN codes 0402.10 and 0402.21) may be invoiced, settled and repatriated in EUR, USD, GBP or any freely convertible currency. The only material frictions are fiscal rather than FX-related: dividends paid by a UG to a non-resident shareholder attract 26.375% German withholding tax (25% Abgeltungsteuer on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on such tax, i.e., 1.375 percentage points) unless reduced by an applicable double taxation treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) or a valid exemption certificate (Freistellungsbescheinigung). For Ugandan (UGX) counterparties, convertibility constraints arise under Bank of Uganda regulations rather than German law; the UG should therefore invoice in EUR or USD and use NDF hedging if UGX-denominated exposures arise.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion quota: a German UG may retain, hold, invest or convert 100% of its milk powder export receipts in any currency, onshore or offshore, with no repatriation obligation. The sole binding statutory duty is statistical reporting: under section 67(1) AWV, every cross-border payment (inbound export proceeds, outbound supplier settlement, capital contribution, dividend remittance) equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days using the electronic payment-balance reporting system and the Bundesbank's purpose-code classification (goods exports - Warenausfuhr; goods imports - Wareneinfuhr; equity contributions - Direktinvestition). SEPA membership does not remove the obligation for non-German-area counterparties; the UG must retain the underlying sales contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading and SWIFT confirmation to reconcile the reported purpose code with documents.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank, Frankfurt am Main), acting as the monetary, payment-statistics and exchange-control-monitoring authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz - AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV); the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz - BMWK) holds export/import control policy competence, while the Bundesbank operates the mandatory payment-balance reporting system. Germany maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration requiring licensing of capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium, subject to binding legal constraints. German-licensed or EEA-passporting Electronic Money Institutions, for example Penta, Qonto, Airwallex and Wise Business, provide rapid remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs and SWIFT MT103 execution, making them credible for operational treasury and low-value supplier wires. However, EMIs are unsuitable as the primary banking rail for this dairy commodity trader because: (i) under section 1(2) sentence 2 of the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), they may not issue or settle documentary credits, provide payment guarantees, discount trade bills or open LC trust accounts; (ii) tier-1 banks classify the UG's LC-based milk powder trade with African-linked counterparties as enhanced due diligence despite the Low_Risk_Standard tier, and most EMI automated onboarding cannot evidence the full KYC chain required by prevailing German supervisory practice; (iii) offshore non-EU EMIs without a BaFin licence or EU passport violate the ZAG reservation of rights and cannot lawfully maintain German corporate accounts, exposing the UG to regulatory fines and void-contract risk. The recommended structure is a tier-1 German commercial bank as the LC-issuing and clearing anchor, with an EEA EMI used only as a secondary FX-optional disbursement rail and never as custodian of export proceeds awaiting LC settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for equity and working-capital inflows: (i) execute a notarised shareholders' resolution (Gesellschafterbeschluss) authorising the contribution; (ii) book the transfer via SWIFT MT103 from the parent or corporate shareholder's bank to the UG's account at a tier-1 German bank, stating the Bundesbank payment-balance purpose code for direct investment (Direktinvestition - Einlage in das Eigenkapital eines inländischen Unternehmens) and the payment reference 'DE_UG capital contribution [date/sequence]'; (iii) do not commingle trade proceeds with capital: export proceeds must be reported under the goods-export purpose code (Warenausfuhr), never as equity; (iv) file the payment-balance report with the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days of the value date if the amount equals or exceeds EUR 12,500; (v) submit the UBO confirmation to the receiving bank before credit release, retain the incoming SWIFT confirmation, and record the contribution as a deemed contribution (Einlage) in the UG's accounts; (vi) if the shareholder is Uganda-resident, obtain Bank of Uganda approval or confirm the remittance is made from an authorised dealer foreign-currency account, and require the sending bank to state the purpose code in MT103 field 70 to prevent the German clearing bank from rejecting or freezing the credit under KWG section 25h.Primary rail for the German UG is a tier-1 German bank. (a) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer (TT/Wire) for open-account supplier payments and freight, with purpose code 'Wareneinfuhr' or 'Fracht- und Transportleistungen' as applicable, amount, beneficiary name and contract reference in field 70, and Bundesbank payment-balance reporting if the payment exceeds EUR 12,500; execution requires the underlying import documentation to be attached on the bank's records. (b) Commercial Letter of Credit (Documentary Credit) issued by the UG's German bank to overseas dairy suppliers under ICC UCP 600, routed by SWIFT MT700 (issue), MT710 (advice), MT754 (confirmation) and settled upon presentation of compliant documents (bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, health certificate, weight certificate) at the bank's trade desk; for Ugandan sellers, a confirmed irrevocable LC is required. (c) SEPA credit transfers are used for EU suppliers and intra-group netting. (d) Each outbound payment must match the underlying import contract amount; the UG must retain SWIFT confirmations, customs entries and LC maturity schedules for six years under German commercial-law retention duties. LC issuance consumes the UG's trade line and may require a 10-30% cash margin or a shared discount on the receivable; payments must never be executed without a matching goods reference to avoid both Bundesbank reporting discrepancies and anti-money-laundering charges.Outright FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäft) on EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, executed under the Deutscher Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte (German Master Agreement for Financial Futures Transactions, published by the Association of German Banks) with a German counterparty bank, documented as cash-flow hedges against USD-denominated milk powder sales and purchases.FX Swaps (Devisenswap) combining spot and forward legs, permitted for rolling short-term liquidity mismatches arising from LC disbursement timing, under the same DRV / ISDA 1998 FX Definitions master documentation.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on USD/UGX or EUR/UGX legs, permitted solely where a Ugandan-origin receivable or payable creates genuine currency risk; UGX is non-convertible and the reference rate is the Bank of Uganda indicative rate, with settlement in EUR/USD and denominated net amounts, subject to the German bank's counterparty-country credit limits and BaFin market-risk rules.Plain-Vanilla FX Options (Währungsoptionen) on EUR/USD, permitted for protecting a confirmed LC receivable without obligating conversion; premium is recognised in profit and loss, strike alignment follows the commodity trade cycle, and documentation is governed by the ISDA 1998 FX Definitions.Exchange-Traded Currency Futures on Eurex for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, permitted for exchange-cleared hedge execution where a documented exposure exceeds the bank's linear-forward tolerance or the UG lacks an OTC derivative credit line; traded through the tier-1 bank's custody account, requiring no OTC counterparty limit.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, subject to binding legal constraints. German-licensed or EEA-passporting Electronic Money Institutions, for example Penta, Qonto, Airwallex and Wise Business, provide rapid remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs and SWIFT MT103 execution, making them credible for operational treasury and low-value supplier wires. However, EMIs are unsuitable as the primary banking rail for this dairy commodity trader because: (i) under section 1(2) sentence 2 of the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), they may not issue or settle documentary credits, provide payment guarantees, discount trade bills or open LC trust accounts; (ii) tier-1 banks classify the UG's LC-based milk powder trade with African-linked counterparties as enhanced due diligence despite the Low_Risk_Standard tier, and most EMI automated onboarding cannot evidence the full KYC chain required by prevailing German supervisory practice; (iii) offshore non-EU EMIs without a BaFin licence or EU passport violate the ZAG reservation of rights and cannot lawfully maintain German corporate accounts, exposing the UG to regulatory fines and void-contract risk. The recommended structure is a tier-1 German commercial bank as the LC-issuing and clearing anchor, with an EEA EMI used only as a secondary FX-optional disbursement rail and never as custodian of export proceeds awaiting LC settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 5 million (goods) or EUR 500,000 (services). Master File required if group revenue exceeds EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, but requires genuine economic substance for treaty benefits and to avoid anti-abuse rules under § 42 AO (General Anti-Abuse Rule). A UG must have real business activity, premises, employees, and decision-making in Germany to claim treaty benefits and avoid being classified as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to EUR 50,000, and additional penalty of 10% of the evaded tax (per § 370 AO).

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if they intentionally or negligently fail to meet tax obligations; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential extradition.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of 25% withholding tax on deemed dividends; penalties up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for back taxes and penalties if they knowingly operate a sham entity; potential criminal charges for fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1 million for serious violations; revocation of business license; criminal prosecution for money laundering (up to 10 years imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment for willful non-compliance.
