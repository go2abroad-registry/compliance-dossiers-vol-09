Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (German Federal Ministry of Finance); operational tax enforcement by local Finanzämter under the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office). Digital filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Following notarial incorporation and registration in the commercial register (Handelsregister), the UG must file a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt, which issues the Steuernummer (income tax number). For VAT purposes, the UG must separately apply for a USt-IdNr. (German VAT ID) via the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, using form USt 1 and, where applicable, USt 1 A; all filings and updates are processed electronically through ELSTER.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not maintain a check-the-box regime; the UG is mandatorily taxed as a corporation (Körperschaft, establishment under §1 KStG). Available statutory elections include the Organschaft fiscal unity election for corporation tax and VAT (§14 KStG, §2 UStG), the small-entrepreneur VAT exemption election under §9 UStG, and the option to be treated as a trading entity for Gewerbesteuer purposes; hybrid mismatch rules under §4k EStG may impose mandatory recharacterization where double deduction or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes arise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 annual turnover threshold for domestic small-enterprise status in the preceding year, plus EUR 50,000 projected current-year threshold; non-resident entrepreneurs deriving taxable supplies in Germany are generally required to register for VAT with no de minimis turnover threshold, subject to reverse charge mechanisms for B2B services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third-country destinations are exempt from German VAT (§4 Nr.1 UStG, §6 UStG) where the supplier proves that the goods physically left the EU within the statutory period and holds an export certificate (Ausfuhrnachweis). For offshore services, including container leasing to non-German business customers, the place of supply for B2B services is the customer's place of business (reverse charge/passive supplier treatment), so German VAT generally does not apply; export zero-rating substantiation is relevant only to the physical export of goods and directly connected export-related services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/Zollanmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR / Air Waybill transport evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (hard-currency export proceeds receipt)
  • Export invoice and contract
  • Container master lease or sales/purchase documentation identifying equipment

Since 1 January 2025, a mandatory structured e-invoicing regime applies to domestic B2B supplies: German-established businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XML) and must issue e-invoices in a standardised format such as XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x, or EDI (EANCOM/cXML). The obligation to issue e-invoices is phased for certain smaller turnover suppliers until 2027/2028. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API tax-authority clearance, or digital stamping regime in Germany; invoices are not transmitted for prior approval to the tax administration.

The tax office (Finanzamt) and customs authorities conduct substantive post-clearance and VAT audits of export evidence. If zero-rating is disallowed, German VAT is retrospectively assessed at 19% plus statutory interest under §233a AO at 0.5% per month, and the exporter may face additional penalties under §370 AO for tax evasion, up to five years' imprisonment or fines. Intentional or negligent misuse of export documentation triggers clawback of input VAT, administrative monetary penalties, and, in aggravated cases (organised VAT fraud), liability under §71 AO and extended limitation periods (up to 10 years).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU free movement of capital framework (Article 63 TFEU). No exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. However, mandatory reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 (per AWV §67) and for certain capital transactions to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes. Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax (15% plus solidarity surcharge) and withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% surcharge on dividend, reduced under tax treaties).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments (including export receipts) exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank within 7 days of receipt (AWV §67). No conversion ratio is imposed; the EUR is the functional currency for tax purposes, but foreign currency balances are permitted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and payment oversight; the Bundesbank administers foreign exchange statistics and reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, Solarisbank, or N26 Business can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment rails. However, they are not full-service banks and may lack the capacity for large-scale trade finance (LCs) or complex FX hedging. Legal constraints: EMIs must hold a BaFin license under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and are subject to strict AML/KYC. For a container leasing entity with international counterparties, a full-service bank is recommended for core treasury operations; EMIs can be used for ancillary payment processing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax under the German Corporate Income Tax Act, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal burden of 15.825% before municipal trade tax. Municipal trade tax generally results in an aggregate burden of approximately 22.83% at a 400% trade-tax multiplier, but the actual rate depends on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German withholding tax on dividends, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic deduction of 26.375%. A German corporate recipient may generally qualify for the participation exemption. A non-resident corporate recipient may qualify for relief under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany or under an applicable double tax treaty, subject to minimum ownership, holding-period, anti-abuse and substance requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the corporate income tax and trade tax returns are generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser files the return, the statutory adviser-extension rule generally permits filing by the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions, administrative extensions and special rules for particular tax years. A non-calendar fiscal year uses the corresponding period after fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding tax must generally be withheld at payment or deemed payment and remitted by the statutory due date. Dividend withholding-tax certificates must be issued to the recipient. Treaty-reduction or exemption documentation should be obtained before payment where relief at source is requested.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany has no single monetary threshold that automatically applies to every Local File. Under the German transfer-pricing documentation rules, extraordinary business transactions require documentation, and routine cross-border controlled transactions generally trigger detailed documentation at statutory transaction thresholds. Common annual thresholds are EUR 6,000,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing and royalty transactions. A Master File is generally required where the multinational group exceeds EUR 100,000,000 in consolidated annual revenue and the German entity is part of the relevant cross-border group. Documentation must generally be prepared within the statutory period after the relevant financial year and supplied to the tax authority upon request within the applicable short deadline.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is assessed through general anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, transfer-pricing rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, principal-purpose and anti-treaty-shopping standards, and corporate-law principles. A German UG leasing containers should maintain genuine local business activity, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capability, decision-making evidence, commercial premises or operational infrastructure where appropriate, bank and accounting controls, asset registers, insurance, customer contracts and arm's-length related-party agreements. A pure conduit with no commercial rationale may lose treaty or directive benefits and face recharacterization, denial of deductions or tax assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Intentional tax evasion may be punished by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, late-payment surcharges and administrative penalties remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur civil liability for tax losses caused by culpable failure to perform tax duties under the Fiscal Code, and may face criminal prosecution. German law does not impose an automatic tax-related travel ban, but criminal courts may order measures affecting liberty and authorities may impose restrictions in specific enforcement or insolvency circumstances.
Withholding-Tax Misappropriation

Statutory sanction: The UG and responsible persons may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, interest and late-payment surcharges. Intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion or aiding tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for withholding tax that should have been withheld and remitted, particularly where payment was made despite insufficient funds or without verifying exemption documentation. Criminal fines or imprisonment may apply for intentional conduct.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, recharacterize payments as hidden profit distributions, assess additional corporate income tax, trade tax, withholding tax, interest and penalties, and impose documentation-related sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for tax losses and shareholder claims arising from prohibited distributions or negligent tax compliance. Criminal exposure arises where the manipulation is intentional and materially reduces tax.
Shell Company, Treaty Shopping and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU relief may be denied under German anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership rules. German withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties may be assessed retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for underwithheld tax where they knowingly rely on inadequate documentation or artificial arrangements. Criminal liability may arise if the structure is used deliberately to evade tax.
VAT and Customs Misclassification

Statutory sanction: The UG may owe output VAT, import VAT, interest, late-payment surcharges and penalties. Incorrect EC Sales Lists, customs declarations or invoice content may cause additional administrative sanctions and denial of VAT recovery.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be liable for VAT shortfalls caused by culpable failure to implement invoice, customer-verification, customs and evidence controls. Intentional VAT fraud may attract criminal prosecution.
Insolvency and Late-Filing of Insolvency

Statutory sanction: Directors may face restitution claims, disqualification consequences, insolvency clawback, fines and criminal sanctions, including imprisonment for insolvency-related offenses. Tax claims remain enforceable against the company and may be accompanied by director liability.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments and unpaid employee or tax-related amounts in circumstances prescribed by law. Courts may impose professional restrictions and criminal penalties; a general automatic travel ban is not a standard consequence.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Beneficial-Owner Breaches

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, reporting obligations, account restrictions, asset seizure and criminal money-laundering sanctions may apply. Criminal money laundering can result in fines or imprisonment, with aggravated cases carrying higher penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance-responsible persons may face individual fines or criminal liability for intentional or grossly negligent AML failures. Personal exposure may include confiscation, restitution and professional disqualification.
Corporate-Law Capital Maintenance and Prohibited Distributions

Statutory sanction: Recipients may be required to repay prohibited distributions. The company may claim damages from directors, and unlawful distributions may also produce withholding-tax, corporate-tax and insolvency consequences.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited payments and breaches of capital-maintenance duties. If the conduct conceals tax evasion or insolvency, additional civil and criminal sanctions may apply.
