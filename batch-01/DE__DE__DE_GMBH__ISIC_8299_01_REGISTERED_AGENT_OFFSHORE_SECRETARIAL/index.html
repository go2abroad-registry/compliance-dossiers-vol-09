Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers, certain federal excises, withholding tax and central administrative matters; the local taxpayer-relevant Local Tax Office (Finanzamt) at the GmbH's registered seat for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT assessments and audits; electronic filing portal ELSTER at https://www.elster.de; BZSt portal at https://www.bzst.de; Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) as supreme policy authority.

E-government filing portal

(1) Notarial incorporation deed, registration of the GmbH in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister); (2) within four weeks of commencing business, file the statutory tax registration questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (§ 138 AO / § 27a AO) with the competent local Finanzamt through ELSTER; (3) obtain from the Finanzamt the individual tax number (Steuernummer, format 10/123/45678) covering corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT and payroll tax; (4) apply online via ELSTER or the BZSt to obtain the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr., format DE123456789) which is mandatory for intra-EU B2B reverse-charge transactions and export evidence; (5) the same questionnaire registers the GmbH for trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with the relevant municipality (Hebesatz applied by the Finanzamt); (6) a small entrepreneur below the § 19 UStG threshold must still obtain a Steuernummer and may abstain from a USt-IdNr. while it remains within the exemption.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A German GmbH is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with statutory seat and/or place of management in Germany, therefore subject to unlimited domestic tax liability on worldwide income; corporate income tax 15% plus solidarity surcharge 5.5% of the corporate tax (combined 15.825%), plus trade tax with local multiplier typically between 200% and 490%, yielding an aggregate effective profit tax burden of approximately 29%–33%; the GmbH is a separate legal taxable person — no fiscal transparency with shareholders; distributions are separately subject to shareholder-level withholding or final withholding tax.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the GmbH's entity-level taxation is mandatory. Relevant elections include: (1) Organschaft (corporate tax group / fiscal unity) under §§ 14–17 KStG, requiring a 75% shareholding and a valid profit and loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with a domestic controlling entity; (2) the small business (Kleinunternehmer) exemption election under § 19 UStG; (3) application of § 13b UStG reverse charge for intra-EU and domestic B2B supplies; (4) structural choices on hybrid instruments (shareholder loans, profit-participation certificates, subordinated debt) are disciplined by the German ATAD II hybrid mismatch rules (§ 4k EStG, § 8a, § 8b, § 12a KStG) which deny deductions or re-characterize payments where cross-border treatment diverges; double taxation treaty tie-breaker provisions may be invoked for dual-resident situations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany operates no general turnover threshold for VAT registration of resident entrepreneurs: any person performing taxable supplies within the scope of § 2 UStG must register. The § 19 UStG small-business exemption permits exclusion from VAT where the preceding calendar year's turnover does not exceed €25,000 and the current year's expected turnover does not exceed €100,000 (2025 values, raised from €22,000 / €50,000 under the Growth Opportunities Act 2024). A GmbH rendering statutory registered-agent and corporate secretarial services normally exceeds these levels and is required to register; a non-established foreign supplier is registerable without reliance on the small-business concession if it performs supplies taxable in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods (e.g., corporate kits, seals, share certificates physically dispatched abroad): zero-rating as export delivery (Ausfuhrlieferung) under §§ 4(1)(1) and 6 UStG if the goods are removed by the supplier or the foreign customer to a third-country territory, leave the EU within three months, and the supplier substantiates the export chain by documentary proof of physical arrival abroad (Gelangensbestätigung). For services of the GmbH — registered agent office, statutory company secretarial, commercial offshore administration — there is no German percentage zero-rating in the strict sense; the supply is outside the scope of German VAT because the place of supply is abroad: under § 3a(2) UStG, B2B services are taxed at the customer's establishment, and under § 3a(3) UStG certain B2C services are located at the private customer's residence outside Germany or the EU. The GmbH must document the recipient's entrepreneurial status and, for EU customers, must hold and verify a valid USt-IdNr. before excluding German VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/EZT electronic filing) for tangible goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer advice demonstrating receipt of consideration
  • Service Agreement, Statement of Work and corporate-secretarial engagement letter evidencing cross-border place of supply
  • Customer's document of establishment: valid EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) for EU customers, or commercial register extract / tax residence certificate for non-EU customers

Phased German e-invoicing mandate under the E-Rechnungsverordnung (Growth Opportunities Act 2024): from 1 January 2025 every resident business taxpayer must be able to receive structured electronic invoices in the allowed formats (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) in its receivables system; issuance obligations are phased from 1 January 2027 for suppliers with more than €800,000 prior-year turnover and from 1 January 2028 for all B2B suppliers. Germany has no mandatory real-time fiscal pre-clearance or digital stamping regime comparable to Italy's Sistema di Interscambio; invoices flow directly between parties, and electronic filing of VAT advance returns, annual VAT returns and EC Sales Lists is performed through the ELSTER portal or its authenticated REST/SOAP APIs. Tax-record retention must comply with the GoBD principles: machine readability, tamper protection, and provision of fiscal audit interfaces (digitale Betriebsprüfung).

Local Finanzamt and BZSt auditors conduct targeted examinations (Außenprüfung) of export and cross-border service cases; if the zero-rating or non-taxability is overturned for missing documentation, defective evidence of arrival, invalid VAT ID, failure to repatriate consideration within 180 days, or fraud indicators such as round-tripping and contra-trading, the tax office will assess or re-assess German VAT at 19% and add late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (§ 233a AO). Assessment and clawback periods are four years for ordinary non-declaratory cases and extend to ten years for § 370 AO tax evasion; deliberate evasion, forged Gelangensbestätigungen, or involvement in cross-border VAT missing-trader fraud triggers criminal liability under § 370 AO (up to five years' imprisonment, up to ten years in aggravated cases, plus fines), joint and several liability under § 73 AO, and — under settled CJEU and German jurisprudence — denial of zero-rating or input VAT deduction where the entrepreneur knew or should have known of the customer's fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current account convertibility and full capital account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no exchange restrictions, no profit remittance hurdles, and no mandatory conversion or repatriation requirements. EUR is a freely traded, floating currency. Cross-border transfers, dividend repatriation, and capital injections are unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/KYC and reporting obligations. Foreign investors may freely transfer profits, royalties, and capital without prior authorization. Germany's capital account is fully open, and no prior approval from the Bundesbank is required for inbound or outbound investments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement. Export proceeds are not required to be repatriated or converted into EUR. However, under Section 67 of the AWV, German residents (including GmbHs) must report receivables and payables from cross-border transactions if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500 and the payment is delayed by more than 90 days. Additionally, under Section 59 AWV, any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank via the SWIFT MT101/MT103 reporting system. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Federal Bank of Germany); foreign exchange administration governed by the Bundesbank Act (Bundesbankgesetz) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), with oversight from the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy (BMWi).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) are viable for operational accounts but generally NOT for capital escrow accounts (Stammkapital). The GmbH capital formation requirement (EUR 12,500 minimum of the EUR 25,000 registered capital) must be deposited in a German bank account held by a licensed credit institution (CRR credit institution) or a German branch of a foreign credit institution. EMIs cannot hold capital escrow accounts. Cross-border capabilities: Fintech EMIs can process SEPA and SWIFT payments, but may impose limits on high-value transactions and often require enhanced due diligence for offshore ownership. Legal constraints: EMIs must comply with the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz - GwG) and the Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Non-EU EMIs can passport into Germany only under specific equivalence regimes; otherwise, a German branch or subsidiary license is required.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Complete UBO declaration according to GwG Section 3, including notarized share certificates, trust deeds, and a corporate structure chart certified by a lawyer. Banks generally require full disclosure and will not accept undisclosed nominees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (Single Customer Credit Transfer) to the GmbH's capital escrow account at a German credit institution. The payment purpose code must be clearly stated, e.g., 'Stammkapital' or 'Gesellschaftereinlage' (Shareholder Contribution). Required verification: The transferring bank must include the shareholder's name and the GmbH's name (in formation) in the remittance information. The German bank will verify the funds against the notarized incorporation documents. For amounts exceeding EUR 12,500, the bank must report the transaction to the Bundesbank under Section 59 AWV. No prior approval from the Bundesbank is required. Capital can be injected in EUR or foreign currency; if foreign currency, the bank will convert at the prevailing rate. The capital must be freely available to the GmbH upon registration. After registration, funds can be transferred to the operational account.Outbound trade disbursements can be executed via SWIFT MT103 for clean payments and SWIFT MT700/MT760 for documentary credits (Commercial LC). For trade settlements, SWIFT MT202 (General Financial Institution Transfer) is used for bank-to-bank transfers. SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) are available for EUR payments within the SEPA zone. For non-EUR payments, SWIFT MT103 is standard. Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are governed by UCP 600. The GmbH must provide commercial invoices, bills of lading, and other documents as required by the LC. For payments exceeding EUR 12,500, the bank reports to the Bundesbank. No export proceeds surrender requirement applies. Foreign exchange hedging can be executed through forward contracts, NDFs, and currency swaps with Tier 1 banks.Forward Foreign Exchange Contracts (Devisentermingeschäfte) - OTC derivatives with fixed exchange rate for future settlement, commonly used for hedging trade receivables/payables.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - Cash-settled forwards used for currencies with restrictions, but generally not needed for EUR/USD or EUR/CNY as these are freely convertible; however, NDFs are permissible for hedging purposes.Currency Swaps (Währungsswaps) - Interest rate and principal exchange agreements to manage FX and interest rate risk, commonly used for cross-border financing.FX Options (Devisenoptionen) - Purchased call/put options to hedge against adverse currency movements, with premium paid upfront.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (Zins-Währungsswaps) - Combined interest rate and currency swap to hedge long-term debt exposures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz - ZAG) are viable for operational accounts but generally NOT for capital escrow accounts (Stammkapital). The GmbH capital formation requirement (EUR 12,500 minimum of the EUR 25,000 registered capital) must be deposited in a German bank account held by a licensed credit institution (CRR credit institution) or a German branch of a foreign credit institution. EMIs cannot hold capital escrow accounts. Cross-border capabilities: Fintech EMIs can process SEPA and SWIFT payments, but may impose limits on high-value transactions and often require enhanced due diligence for offshore ownership. Legal constraints: EMIs must comply with the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz - GwG) and the Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Non-EU EMIs can passport into Germany only under specific equivalence regimes; otherwise, a German branch or subsidiary license is required.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT (0.825 percentage points) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); combined effective rate approx. 30% depending on municipality

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% German dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that withholding tax, resulting in an aggregate rate of 26.375%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation must be maintained and submitted where cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 500,000 per fiscal year; Master File is generally required where the multinational group has annual consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 100 million and the German entity is part of the group; contemporaneous records must cover transactions with non-resident related parties

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a formal ESR regime but enforces substance-over-form through the permanent establishment concept, Sec. 42 AO anti-abuse doctrine, CFC rules under Secs. 7–13 AStG, beneficial ownership requirements in German DTA law and the principal purpose test in MLI-compliant treaties; a German GmbH that lacks meaningful staff, premises, contracts, assets and risk management functions will be vulnerable to DTA relief denial, recharacterisation and anti-abuse taxation

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and to 10 years in aggravated cases, plus pecuniary fines, reassessment including evasion interest at 0.5% per month on the evaded amount, late payment surcharges, and an extended limitation period of up to 10 years for tax assessment

Director personal liability: The GmbH managing director is personally liable under Sec. 69 AO for wilfully or negligently caused tax arrears; criminal liability attaches to legal representatives under Sec. 14 StGB; upon reasonable flight risk German courts may impose pre-trial detention, travel bans, passport withdrawal and seize funds to secure tax and penalty collection
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under Sec. 1 AStG, interest surcharge for transfer pricing adjustments (6% per annum on the adjusted amount), estimation of taxable income under Sec. 162 AO, documentation penalties of up to EUR 1,000,000 or 5% to 10% of the adjustment, and denial of deduction for the abusive payment

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create false documentation or cause tax evasion through transfer pricing manipulation may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties under the Fiscal Code and may face criminal imprisonment; courts may disregard the corporate form if the GmbH is found to be an artificial tax avoidance vehicle
Shell Company / Lack of Substance / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity or arrangement under Sec. 42 AO, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test / beneficial ownership rule, application of CFC attribution, reclassification of distributions or income to the actual shareholder and, for serious violations of trade licensing laws, fines, licence withdrawal, forced dissolution and criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be held personally liable for taxes, sanctions and restitution; personal travel bans and international arrest warrants may be issued in criminal tax and money laundering proceedings; the corporate veil is pierced where the GmbH is abused as a mere trust or sham arrangement
AML / Registered Agent and Secretarial License Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Sec. 56 GwG of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of relevant annual turnover for institutions, plus confiscation of criminal proceeds; criminal liability under Sec. 261 StGB for money laundering with imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines; regulatory suspension or revocation of the commercial trade licence is mandatory for serious breaches

Director personal liability: Managing directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners are personally liable in civil damages and criminal fines; assets can be frozen, travel restrictions and entry bans imposed, and imprisonment ordered; because the GmbH is used as an instrument of an illicit trade, courts may lift limited liability and hold directors jointly and severally liable for the criminal gains
