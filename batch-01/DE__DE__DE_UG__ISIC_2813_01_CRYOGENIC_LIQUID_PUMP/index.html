Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority is the local Finanzamt responsible for the UG's registered office or German permanent establishment. Federal tax administration functions are administered through the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), including issuance of the German VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Tax filings and electronic communications are generally submitted through the ELSTER platform operated by the German tax administration.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate and register the Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) with the commercial register and obtain the commercial registration documentation. 2. File the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt through ELSTER, disclosing registered office, directors, beneficial owners, business activity, expected turnover, bank details, accounting method, and anticipated VAT obligations. 3. Obtain the German tax number (Steuernummer) from the Finanzamt for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. 4. Apply separately to the BZSt for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), normally using the ELSTER tax registration data and the commercial-register details. 5. If the entity imports, exports, or operates customs procedures, obtain an Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI) from German customs. 6. Non-resident owners or foreign entities must provide certified or otherwise acceptable constitutional documents, proof of foreign tax residence where requested, director and beneficial-owner identification, evidence of German business activity or taxable supplies, and a German service address or authorized representative where required. 7. Register for payroll withholding, statutory accounting, and other tax obligations when personnel or relevant payments arise. A VAT registration threshold does not generally exempt a business making taxable German supplies; the small-business exemption is a substantive VAT exemption and does not provide an automatic USt-IdNr.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German capital company and is generally subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax at entity level. Germany does not provide a general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a UG to be treated as a fiscally transparent partnership. A UG cannot ordinarily elect partnership treatment merely by filing a tax form. German tax law contains specific legal-form and restructuring elections, including certain options under the German Reorganisation Tax Act, but these are transaction-specific and do not convert an ordinary UG into a general pass-through entity. Germany has implemented anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework, including restrictions on deduction, inclusion, and dual-residence mismatch outcomes; these are statutory rules rather than elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold applies to a business making taxable supplies in Germany. The German small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG may apply where the prior calendar year's total turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and the applicable transitional rules. The exemption is not available for all transactions, does not permit input VAT recovery, and does not eliminate registration or reporting obligations where taxable transactions, intra-Community transactions, imports, or other special rules require identification. A non-established foreign business generally must register from its first German taxable transaction unless a reverse-charge or other statutory relief applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt exports with input VAT recovery under the German export exemption rules, provided the goods are transported or dispatched to the third country and the exporter can substantiate the required export, destination, and entrepreneurial status conditions. The export declaration must be accepted by customs and the goods must be shown to have exited the EU customs territory. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-identified business in another EU Member State may qualify for exemption under separate rules and are not third-country exports. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer is foreign. Their VAT treatment depends on the place-of-supply rules, including the B2B general rule, customer establishment, use-and-enjoyment provisions, and special rules for services connected with immovable property, transport, events, and goods. A German VAT refund or input-tax recovery claim requires valid invoices, proper accounting, and evidence supporting the relevant exemption or place of supply.

  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the applicable customs system, including the MRN and export accompanying or exit confirmation where applicable
  • Commercial invoice identifying exporter, consignee, goods, value, delivery terms, and destination
  • Transport evidence such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or carrier confirmation
  • Proof of exit from the European Union customs territory, normally the electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence
  • Packing list and purchase or sales contract where necessary to establish the transaction and goods identity
  • EORI number and customs representation records where customs formalities are conducted by an agent
  • Proof of payment or other commercial evidence where required to corroborate the transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not a general statutory German export-VAT document
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT number, acquisition-related evidence, transport documentation, and required recapitulative statement

Germany does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or continuous transaction control system. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exclusions. Issuing obligations apply through transitional periods: generally from 2027 for larger issuers and from 2028 for all relevant domestic B2B transactions, subject to the statutory turnover and implementation conditions. Permitted structured formats include EN 16931-compliant formats such as XRechnung and suitable ZUGFeRD versions. Ordinary PDF invoices are not structured e-invoices. Federal public-sector invoicing requirements may apply separately, including XRechnung and specified transmission channels. E-invoices must be retained in an unaltered, auditable form for the statutory retention period. There is no routine German tax-authority pre-clearance, digital VAT stamp, or mandatory invoice API approval for ordinary domestic supplies.

Germany does not impose a general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds, including a mandatory 180-day rule. Export proceeds may nevertheless be relevant evidence in a tax audit, customs audit, transfer-pricing review, anti-money-laundering review, or bank compliance process. If export-exemption conditions are not substantiated, the Finanzamt may retrospectively assess German VAT, deny or reverse input VAT recovery, impose interest under the German Fiscal Code, and correct prior VAT returns. False, incomplete, or deliberately misleading declarations may constitute tax evasion under Section 370 of the Fiscal Code, attempted tax evasion, or an administrative tax offense, with criminal fines or imprisonment depending on the circumstances and severity. Administrative late-filing or cooperation penalties may also apply. Customs violations may trigger duty and import-tax recovery, customs penalties, and, in serious cases, criminal liability under applicable German and EU customs law. The taxable person bears the evidentiary burden for the statutory requirements of export VAT exemption.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account purposes. There is no general German exchange-control approval for foreign ownership, ordinary cross-border dividend payments, intercompany loans, trade payments, or repatriation of sale proceeds. A German UG may generally remit dividends, interest, royalties, and principal to non-residents, subject to distributable-profit rules, corporate-law solvency constraints, withholding-tax rules, treaty relief procedures, transfer-pricing requirements, anti-avoidance rules, beneficial-owner requirements, sanctions, and bank anti-money-laundering controls. Dividend distributions require properly adopted financial statements and shareholder resolutions and may be restricted by the UG statutory-reserve requirement until the statutory capital threshold is reached. Payments involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, dual-use goods, military end-use, or foreign-investment and export-control concerns may require authorization or may be prohibited. Cross-border payments and certain foreign assets or liabilities may be subject to Deutsche Bundesbank statistical reporting under the AWV; such reporting is not an exchange-control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no compulsory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or deposited with a German bank. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, bank-compliance, and reporting requirements. The UG should maintain commercial evidence for export invoices, shipping documents, customs records, payment references, and the underlying customer contract. Cross-border payments and positions may require AWV reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank, subject to applicable thresholds, exemptions, filing deadlines, and reporting formats in force on the transaction date. The business must verify the current reporting threshold and exemption applicable to each payment because the rules and administrative instructions may change. No export-receipt conversion is required solely because the entity is a German UG.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-exchange, external-payment and statistical reporting obligations are principally administered under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG), the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), and applicable European Union sanctions and capital-movement rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payments and foreign-currency collections; Low as the sole treasury bank for a newly incorporated, foreign-owned UG operating in regulated or technically sensitive industrial goods. An electronic-money institution or payment institution licensed in Germany or another European Economic Area state may provide services under PSD2 passporting, subject to its license scope, local servicing model, safeguarding arrangements, sanctions screening, and customer-risk policy. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot rely solely on its offshore license to provide regulated payment services in Germany and may require a German or EEA-authorized intermediary or authorization. EMI safeguarded funds are not equivalent to deposits and generally do not receive the same deposit-guarantee protection as bank deposits. The UG should retain a German or EEA bank account for statutory capital evidence, payroll, taxes, customs, large trade settlements, and liquidity redundancy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital to an account in the UG's legal name at a German or EEA credit institution, using the exact registered company name and commercial-register details. Use a payment reference such as "share capital contribution pursuant to formation documents" or "capital increase contribution," and avoid third-party remitters unless fully documented and accepted in advance by the bank. For shareholder loans, use a separately executed loan agreement, arm's-length interest and maturity terms, transfer-pricing support, and a payment reference identifying the loan. The bank may request the articles, notarial formation documents, shareholder resolution, subscription statement, source-of-funds evidence, and beneficial-owner information before crediting or releasing funds. Maintain a complete audit trail from originator to the UG account. Reportable cross-border payments or positions must be included in Deutsche Bundesbank AWV statistical filings where applicable; these filings do not replace bank KYC or tax reporting. Capital injections must not be disguised as sales revenue, trade advances, or payments for goods.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area. Use SWIFT cross-border payments, normally through ISO 20022-compatible bank channels; an MT103 is a legacy SWIFT customer-credit-transfer format and may remain available depending on the bank, but payment data should be captured in the bank's current ISO 20022 workflow. For import and export settlements, retain the commercial contract, pro forma or final invoice, purchase order, packing list, transport document, customs evidence, Incoterms, sanctions screening, product classification, and end-use information. Documentary letters of credit may be issued, advised, confirmed, or discounted through a relationship bank under UCP 600 and, where applicable, ISBP. Documentary collections may use URC 522. Match payment references to the underlying invoice and avoid split payments or unexplained third-party settlement. For a commercial LC, define issuing bank, advising or confirming bank, document list, shipment window, Incoterms, discrepancy rules, insurance, sanctions clause, and governing law. Check VAT, customs, withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, and AWV reporting consequences separately from the banking instruction.Deliverable EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, and other bank-offered forward exchange contracts for documented forecast or committed commercial exposures, subject to bank master agreements, appropriateness or suitability procedures, collateral terms, and accounting documentation.Currency options, collars, and participating forwards where the bank has approved the product, the UG understands premium and contingent-delivery risks, and the transaction is connected to a genuine underlying exposure rather than speculative trading.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies that are not freely deliverable or where the relevant market convention uses cash settlement, subject to counterparty eligibility, local-currency rules, sanctions screening, ISDA or equivalent documentation, and EMIR reporting and risk-mitigation requirements where applicable.Cross-currency swaps and foreign-exchange swaps for documented funding, working-capital, or balance-sheet exposures, subject to bank credit approval, collateral and margin provisions, valuation controls, and EMIR obligations where applicable.Spot foreign-exchange transactions for ordinary trade settlement and treasury conversion. Maintain a written treasury policy limiting derivatives to hedging identifiable exposures, requiring two-person approval, independent valuation, counterparty limits, maturity limits, confirmation matching, and periodic hedge-effectiveness review.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payments and foreign-currency collections; Low as the sole treasury bank for a newly incorporated, foreign-owned UG operating in regulated or technically sensitive industrial goods. An electronic-money institution or payment institution licensed in Germany or another European Economic Area state may provide services under PSD2 passporting, subject to its license scope, local servicing model, safeguarding arrangements, sanctions screening, and customer-risk policy. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot rely solely on its offshore license to provide regulated payment services in Germany and may require a German or EEA-authorized intermediary or authorization. EMI safeguarded funds are not equivalent to deposits and generally do not receive the same deposit-guarantee protection as bank deposits. The UG should retain a German or EEA bank account for statutory capital evidence, payroll, taxes, customs, large trade settlements, and liquidity redundancy.
