Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The statutory share capital is EUR 25,000. Before registration, at least EUR 12,500 must generally be paid in for a cash formation, with at least one-quarter of each subscribed share paid. The GmbH has no statutory escrow requirement. A dedicated bank account is normally opened after notarization and funds are transferred before the Commercial Register filing; the managing directors must confirm the capital contribution and its free availability. Contributions in kind require additional disclosure and valuation compliance.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building-owner or municipal confirmation that the premises may be used as an office. Comply with the German Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung), workplace risk-assessment duties, escape-route and occupancy requirements, fire protection, emergency signage, first-aid arrangements, electrical safety, and accessibility rules. A separate fire-service certificate or structural-load inspection is generally required only where demanded by the building authority, change-of-use approval, lease conditions, or the premises' risk profile.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is part of the euro area; the EUR is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no repatriation restrictions and no profit remittance hurdles. Dividends, royalties, management fees, service fees, shareholder loans and liquidation proceeds may be freely remitted abroad, subject only to AML/CFT verification, sanctions screening, withholding tax on dividends (25% plus solidarity surcharge unless reduced by treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive), and compliance with GmbH capital maintenance rules under Section 30 GmbHG. Cross-border payments by German residents exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV; this is a statistical reporting obligation, not a surrender or approval requirement. Capital account transactions are open, but inbound and outbound flows remain subject to beneficial ownership transparency, sanctions and source-of-funds checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced conversion or mandatory retention period applies to export proceeds or service receipts. Exporters and service providers may retain EUR or foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no central bank approval for keeping foreign currency. Reporting under Section 67 AWV applies to cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 and to certain receivables/payables, but reporting is declaratory and statistical only. For immigration/study-abroad advisory receipts, normal commercial invoicing, VAT treatment and AML documentation govern the funds; no FX surrender requirement exists.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; Deutsche Bundesbank for foreign exchange reporting, external sector monitoring and banking oversight; Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking, AML/CFT and payment services supervision; Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) / BAFA for foreign trade licensing under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payment flows and foreign exchange settlement, but Low for statutory GmbH capital escrow and regulated client-fund escrow. BaFin-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may open operational accounts, execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers, and provide multi-currency collection accounts. However, EMI safeguarding accounts are not deposit accounts, do not carry German deposit insurance, and are generally not accepted as capital escrow accounts for GmbH incorporation. If the entity holds client funds for visa, tuition or immigration escrow, it may trigger ZAG licensing, escrow-agent regulation or BaFin financial services authorization. Offshore digital EMIs are not directly passported into Germany unless authorized under ZAG/PSD2; reliance on non-EEA EMIs creates correspondent-bank, sanctions and AML audit risk.

Box 3 Allowances and deductions Standard allowance of EUR 24,500 for corporations; no special non-resident adjustments. Same as corporate income tax: late filing penalty 0.25% per month (min EUR 25, max EUR 25,000); late payment interest 0.5% per month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 for cross-border goods transactions and EUR 50,000 for services with related parties; Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, but substance is required to establish tax residency and avoid treaty abuse under BEPS. The GmbH must have real economic activity, employees, office premises, and decision-making in Germany to benefit from DTA relief and avoid being classified as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 50,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years; additional penalty of up to 10% of evaded tax; interest on unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for civil restitution of evaded taxes; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, additional tax assessments, and penalties up to EUR 25,000; potential revocation of trade license.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes if they knowingly operate a shell company; travel bans and restrictions on leaving Germany may be imposed.
AML / Compliance Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal prosecution for money laundering; revocation of license.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; may face imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans and seizure of assets.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 5-10% of the adjustment amount, and interest on additional tax; documentation penalties up to EUR 1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they failed to ensure compliance; travel bans may be imposed in severe cases.
