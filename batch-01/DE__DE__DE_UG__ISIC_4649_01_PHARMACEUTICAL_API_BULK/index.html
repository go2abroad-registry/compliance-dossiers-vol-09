Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt – Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and EU-wide VAT ID validation; local Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT assessment and audit; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must, upon incorporation, file the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER, resulting in issuance of the Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. Where the UG conducts intra-Community supplies, acquisitions or other cross-border transactions, it must additionally apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number under §27a UStG. Foreign entities not resident in Germany that make taxable supplies in Germany, effect intra-Community acquisitions, or engage in distance sales of goods must register for German VAT through the non-resident entrepreneur registration procedure (USt 1 C via ELSTER or the responsible Finanzamt); non-EU applicants may be required to appoint a fiscal representative. After registration, the taxpayer must use the assigned tax number for domestic filings and the USt-IdNr. for EU recapitulative statements and invoices. Validation of third-party VAT IDs is performed through BZSt's electronic confirmation service.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - A UG is a German capital company with legal personality and is not pass-through. It is subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax, currently 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, yielding 15.825%), Gewerbesteuer (trade tax at a municipal rate generally between roughly 7% and 17%), and VAT as a taxable person under §2 UStG. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A UG is a German capital company with legal personality and is not pass-through. It is subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax, currently 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, yielding 15.825%), Gewerbesteuer (trade tax at a municipal rate generally between roughly 7% and 17%), and VAT as a taxable person under §2 UStG. Residence is based on place of management/registered seat, and the UG is taxed on worldwide income; non-resident shareholders are taxed on distributions under domestic law and applicable tax treaties.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box classification regime; entity classification follows mandatory statutory rules based on legal form. Available statutory elections include: (i) VAT and corporate income tax grouping (Organschaft) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG and §14 KStG, requiring financial, economic and organisational integration; (ii) option under §9 UStG to waive VAT exemption on real estate supplies; (iii) small-business relief under §19 UStG may be waived so that the UG can recover input VAT; (iv) domestic reverse-charge applies to certain supplies as prescribed by law. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective; Germany applies non-elective anti-hybrid rules under §4k EStG and ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold above which VAT registration becomes mandatory for taxable entrepreneurs established in Germany; a de minimis small-business relief applies where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is unlikely to exceed EUR 50,000, in which case VAT may be omitted. A UG engaged in wholesale pharmaceutical API distribution will normally exceed this relief and must register for VAT immediately upon commencing taxable supplies. Non-resident entrepreneurs do not benefit from the domestic small-business threshold for supplies taxable in Germany and must register where they perform taxable transactions; in many B2B cases the reverse-charge mechanism shifts the obligation to the German recipient.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to third-country destinations qualify for the 0% VAT rate under §6 UStG when the supplier proves that the goods were actually transported out of the European Union by the supplier or a commissioned carrier, and that the goods left EU customs territory in the course of the supply. The UG must hold proper commercial documentation and obtain customs export confirmation. Intra-Community supplies to an EU VAT-registered customer qualify for 0% under §6a UStG subject to valid VAT ID, delivery evidence and recapitulative statement filing. B2B services supplied to non-EU recipient entrepreneurs are generally outside German VAT by place-of-supply rules under §3a UStG rather than zero-rated; they must be documented as cross-border B2B services. Export of pharmaceutical APIs additionally requires compliance with German medicines law and any applicable export license conditions; such licenses do not replace the VAT export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted by German customs (ATLAS/AES electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving departure from Germany and destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of export under §10 UStDV, including electronic customs confirmation or carrier-signed release of goods

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B invoices must be receivable in a structured electronic format (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x or EDI-compliant formats); phased mandatory electronic issuance applies from 1 January 2027 for suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all taxable persons. Electronic VAT registration, pre-registration, monthly/quarterly advance returns and the annual VAT return are filed through ELSTER (XML/API), including the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) filed with BZSt. There is no API stamping or pre-clearance requirement, but the tax administration may require transmission of digital records during audit.

Export zero-rating claims are closely scrutinized by German tax offices, customs authorities (Zoll), and the BZSt. If documentary proof of export is missing, incomplete or contradictory, the zero rate is retroactively denied and VAT at 19% is assessed, together with interest under §233a AO after the expiry of the 15-month interest-free period. Late payment surcharges under §240 AO and penalty interest for tax evasion of 6% per annum apply. Intentional or fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under §370 AO and may be punished by up to 10 years' imprisonment, fines and confiscation of unjustified VAT refunds; negligent conduct attracts penalties under §378 AO. The assessment period extends to 10 years for VAT fraud and up to 4 years for negligent underdeclarations. Directors and responsible employees may be jointly and severally liable for VAT clawbacks where false export documentation is knowingly used.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates under a fully convertible currency regime within the Eurozone. The euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions, subject to EU/CFSP sanctions, anti-money-laundering (AML) screening, and reporting obligations under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV). Profit remittances by a German UG to non-resident shareholders are generally unrestricted, but require proper corporate resolutions, statutory tax clearance (where applicable), and AWV statistical reporting (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) for transactions exceeding €12,500 per month or an aggregate of €12,500 per month in cross-border payments. Capital account transfers, including inbound equity injections and outbound dividend remittances, must be reported to the Bundesbank via the standard 'Z4' reporting forms where thresholds apply. No mandatory conversion of euro-denominated export proceeds is required, as the euro is domestic legal tender.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies in Germany. The euro is the domestic currency, so export receipts invoiced in EUR require no conversion. For export proceeds denominated in foreign currency, a German entity may hold such foreign currency in a foreign currency account domestically or abroad, subject to general AWV reporting duties. German AWV §67 requires immediate reporting of cross-border payments received on foreign accounts if the amount is at least €12,500 or the monthly aggregate exceeds €12,500. Export proceeds must be recorded in the statutory books, and any conversion into EUR must be executed at arm's length market rates in compliance with ECB/EBA supervision. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation; however, commercial prudence and banking covenants typically require timely settlement per Incoterms and letter-of-credit terms.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the German Federal Ministry of Finance in coordination with the European Central Bank and the European Commission for capital controls and sanctions enforcement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-Medium for primary treasury rail. German regulators (BaFin) do not recognize non-EU/EEA e-money institutions (EMIs) as 'passportable' banking counterparties for high-risk pharmaceutical API wholesale. A third-country EMI may only provide ancillary payment services if licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and must not handle more than €3 million per month in payment transactions to qualify for the limited license exemption. For an API wholesale operation with expected monthly throughput above €3 million, an EU/EEA-licensed EMI (e.g., with a German or Maltese passported license) remains viable only for disbursements under €100,000 per transaction before enhanced due diligence (EDD) triggers. However, settlement of LC-based trades and high-value API procurement requires a full commercial bank relationship. Fintech EMIs are rated 'Low Viability' for the capital escrow account and 'Medium Viability' for operational petty cash/disbursement purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection to the German UG must be routed via the euro (EUR) payment area using SEPA wire transfers or SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA. The purpose code must align with the Deutsche Bundesbank's AWV reporting catalog: for direct investment/equity capital – use 'Kapitalverkehr' statistical code '503' (Direktinvestitionen) and the reserve bank group '1'. A compliance-complete capital injection file should include: (i) a notarized shareholders' resolution (Gesellschafterbeschluss) evidencing the contribution to the capital reserve; (ii) the bank beneficiary account statement issued in the UG's name; (iii) the AWV report submitted to the Bundesbank (Form Z4) within one month of the payment; and (iv) the supporting bank reference (IBAN – BIC) and possibly a purpose-of-code description 'Erhöhung des gezeichneten Kapitals / Kapitaleinlage'. For inbound transfers from non-EU countries exceeding €12,500 per transaction, the UG must file the cross-border payment statistics report. All inbound funds must be cleared through a KYC-compliant bank account in the UG's own name; no third-party mixing or crowdfunding accounts are acceptable.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements involving active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) procurement and distribution are cleared through two primary rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent banking transfers in EUR, USD, or foreign currency, and (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) with SWIFT MT700/MT707 series for high-value bulk API shipments. For L/C transactions, the UG's bank will issue a documentary credit in favor of the overseas manufacturer, with required documents including a commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of analysis (CoA), pharma wholesale license, and proof of GDP compliance. All outbound payments must be tagged with the correct AWV statistical reporting codes, e.g., for payment of imports of goods use code '3005' or '3006' under 'Warenverkehr' (Trade in Goods); for service payments use '601/602' as appropriate. Where the UG disburses funds in a currency other than EUR, the bank will execute a spot FX conversion or use a pre-arranged FX hedging contract. Settlement times of 2–5 business days are typical. The UG must implement a dual-approval signatory matrix to prevent unauthorized disbursements; ideally, the managing director and a second signatory (e.g., an external tax advisor with bank signatory power) authorize all transfers above €25,000. For L/C disbursements, the UG must ensure strict documentary compliance to avoid discrepancies; a trade finance operations manual and a trained trade finance officer are recommended.Currency Forwards (Devisentermingeschäft) – OTC forward contracts with bank counterparties under ISDA or German Master Agreement (Rahmenvertrag) for fixing EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, or other API trade currency pairs up to 12 months tenorNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – Permitted for settlement in EUR against non-convertible or restricted currencies (e.g., certain Asian currencies), documented under an ISDA 2002 or local German master agreement, subject to counterparty credit limitsCurrency Swaps (Devisenswap) – FX swap/swap-point transactions exchanging fixed amounts of principal in two currencies with an agreed future reversal, permitted for cash-flow funding and intercompany clean-upsFX Options (Währungsoptionen) – Purchased OTC call/put options on major convertible currencies for hedging transactional exposure, subject to the bank's internal risk policy and MiFID II suitability assessmentCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) – Applicable only where the UG has an underlying foreign-currency loan or substantial multi-year latent exposure; requires board approval and documented hedge effectiveness testing under IFRS/IAS or German GAAP

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Low-Medium for primary treasury rail. German regulators (BaFin) do not recognize non-EU/EEA e-money institutions (EMIs) as 'passportable' banking counterparties for high-risk pharmaceutical API wholesale. A third-country EMI may only provide ancillary payment services if licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and must not handle more than €3 million per month in payment transactions to qualify for the limited license exemption. For an API wholesale operation with expected monthly throughput above €3 million, an EU/EEA-licensed EMI (e.g., with a German or Maltese passported license) remains viable only for disbursements under €100,000 per transaction before enhanced due diligence (EDD) triggers. However, settlement of LC-based trades and high-value API procurement requires a full commercial bank relationship. Fintech EMIs are rated 'Low Viability' for the capital escrow account and 'Medium Viability' for operational petty cash/disbursement purposes.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Körperschaftsteuer 15.0% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 0.825% (5.5% of corporate tax) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a statutory rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal assessment rate (Hebesatz), typically 14%–17%: composite statutory burden approx. 30%–32.5% of taxable income in major business locations (e.g., Berlin ~30.6%, Munich ~32.7%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% aggregate statutory rate (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax), to be withheld by the UG from any dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders and paid to the competent tax office.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 of aggregate related-party business transactions in a fiscal year triggers the mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation duty (§ 90(3) AO read with the Verrechnungspreisdokumentations-Verordnung). A Master File is required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000; country-by-country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenues above EUR 750,000,000 (OECD BEPS Action 13).

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a standalone onshore Economic Substance Regulation (ESR). Instead, economic substance is judicially enforced through (i) § 42 AO, which denies tax benefits where an arrangement lacks economic purpose other than tax saving, (ii) treaty residence analysis (Art. 4 OECD DTA model) requiring place of effective management, staff, business premises and decision-taking in Germany, and (iii) BEPS minimum-standards substance requirements. A UG functioning as a letterbox – a German shell without employees, warehouse, bank account or active pharmaceutical wholesale licence under § 52a AMG – will be deprived of treaty benefits and its income will be recharacterised to the foreign principal; the Federal Fiscal Court (BFH I R 15/18, I R 88/13) upheld such reattribution in comparable structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under § 370 AO punishable with imprisonment of up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases (commercial, gang or cross-border structures); assessed tax plus 1.8% p.a. default interest; additional penalty surcharges under § 162 AO; administrative fines for negligent tax shortfall (leichtfertige Steuerverkürzung) up to EUR 50,000; under § 376 AO institutional corporate fines of up to EUR 5 million.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable under § 69 AO for taxes not paid from corporate assets where the failure is caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of official obligations; criminal convictions of directors may be accompanied by travel-verbots under § 132 AO, seizure of personal passports, and personal disqualification under § 14(1) StGB.
Shell Company / Letterbox Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and directive benefits with re-assessed dividend/royalty withholding at 26.375% or 15.825%; income reattribution to the non-resident principal under § 42 AO and § 1 AStG; cancellation of the DTA relief; withdrawal of the Arzneimittelgroßhandelsvertriebserlaubnis when the § 52a AMG licence was granted on false substance statements (criminal effect under §§ 95–96 AMG).

Director personal liability: Civil piercing of the corporate veil under § 826 BGB where the director intentionally caused creditor damage; personal liability for payments made after insolvency under § 64 GmbHG; criminal breach of trust (§ 266 StGB) for misapplication of corporate assets; federal fiscal act allows 48-hour travel bans and, in persistent non-compliance, personal arrest orders against the managing director.
AML and Trade Sanctions Violations in Pharma Wholesale

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine under § 56 GwG of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of global annual turnover for corporate violations; money laundering under § 261 StGB punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years; freezing of bank accounts, seizure of funds, revocation of the wholesale pharma licence and prohibition of the legal person's activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed AML compliance officer are individually liable under § 9 OWiG with personal fines; deliberately conduct through structured wire transfers exposes them to criminal conviction for aiding and abetting money laundering whereby the director's personal assets are subject to disgorgement and confiscation; cross-border arrest and extradition risk pursuant to European Arrest Warrant for proceeds exceeded EUR 50,000.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment at the upward-adjusted arm's-length profit, with the Ministry's burden-of-proof reversal (§ 162(2) AO); surcharge for insufficient documentation under § 162(4) AO equal to 5% of the adjusted income, minimum EUR 5,000 and maximum EUR 500,000, or 10% where the documentation is materially unusable; statutory default interest of 1.8% p.a. applied on the additional assessment.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must ensure complete and timely documentation; personal fines for silence or false statements under § 378 AO; director liability for resulting tax damages under § 93 GmbHG negligence standard; if the documentation is falsified invoices, criminal exposure under §§ 370 AO, 263 StGB, and personal restitution orders for cumulative tax-adjusted APA allocations.
