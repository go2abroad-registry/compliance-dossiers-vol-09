Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer); competent local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and wage/personal taxes; official national tax e-portal: ELSTER (https://www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) obtains its corporate tax number (Steuernummer) by first completing commercial register registration (Handelsregister) and thereafter filing the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt. The VAT ID is applied for electronically through ELSTER pursuant to Form USt 1 TU addressed to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern. There is no minimum-capital condition for the UG, and non-resident ownership is permitted without restriction; the entity is subject to the same registration and filing obligations as an ordinary GmbH.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style 'check-the-box' election is available for a German UG; it is per se a corporation subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge rules. The UG cannot elect partnership pass-through treatment. Hybrid mismatch anti-abuse rules under Section 4k of the German Income Tax Act (EStG) disallow deductions arising from hybrid mismatch arrangements and are applied in coordination with the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small-business exemption under §19 UStG applies when prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed €50,000; any resident entrepreneur exceeding this threshold is obliged to register for VAT. Non-resident suppliers performing taxable supplies in Germany are generally subject to VAT registration irrespective of turnover level, except for certain electronic-services simplifications.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment under §4 Nr. 1a UStG applies to goods physically exported to a third-country destination (outside the EU) provided the supplier or the purchasing entrepreneur exports the goods within the statutory time limit (generally three months) and the supplier retains conclusive export documentation (Ausfuhrnachweis). Intra-Community supplies (EU destinations) are likewise zero-rated when the conditions of §4 Nr. 1b UStG are met, including the buyer's valid VAT ID and the monthly recapitulative statement. For the covered fiber laser metal sheet cutting systems, export zero-rating is available for both the machinery and directly connected services such as transport, installation, and commissioning.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) processed through ATLAS/ECS with a valid Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Exit Confirmation / Print-Nachweis from the German customs system (proof of exportation)
  • Export Accompanying Document (EAD) or relevant transit/carrier document
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing physical leaving of the EU
  • Commercial invoice and written sales contract (original or certified electronic format)
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or SWIFT payment evidence under the export contract

From 1 January 2025, all German domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive electronic invoices (structured format XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); from 1 January 2027, issuing e-invoices for all domestic B2B supplies becomes mandatory. Germany does not operate a real-time centralized fiscal clearance framework (no API fiscal stamping); electronic invoices are exchanged between trading parties, and data are retained for tax audit, with the BZSt providing only validation guidance, not mandatory clearance.

Germany imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is used as a documentary-consistency benchmark for export substantiation. If zero-rating is claimed without complete and timely export evidence, the supply is reclassified as domestic and VAT is assessed at 19% plus late-payment interest (§233 AO), interest accrual (§233a AO), and possible penalty under §26a UStG up to €25,000. Intentional or negligent false documentation, counterfeit export certificates, or fraud triggers criminal tax evasion proceedings under §370 AO, with imprisonment applicable, indefinite or ten-year assessment limitation, and full clawback of any wrongly refunded input VAT.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has unrestricted euro convertibility under EU law, relying on the free movement of capital (Articles 63-66 TFEU). There are no current-account or capital-account exchange controls, no approval requirements for profit remittance, and no restriction on holding foreign-currency accounts. A UG may distribute dividends freely after a formal shareholders' resolution and satisfaction of corporate-law balance-sheet tests; remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject only to administrative tax procedures, principally dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) for individuals, while a qualifying corporate shareholder may benefit from the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% where the parent holds at least 10% for 12 months) or from an applicable double tax treaty (0%-15%). Remittance is administrative rather than approval-based; banks require the final approved annual financial statements, a dividend voucher, and the AWV statistical report for payments above the reporting threshold. The practical hurdles are anti-abuse documentation (e.g., substance evidence, DTT relief applications under Section 50d of the German Income Tax Act) and the UG's statutory-reserve constraint requiring retention of 25% of annual net profit until EUR 25,000 is accumulated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes neither statutory surrender obligations nor mandatory conversion ratios; export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any foreign currency (0% conversion requirement). Compliance duties are statistical only under AWV: (i) export receipts from non-residents must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank where a single cross-border payment exceeds EUR 12,500, using the Bundesbank's electronic reporting interface; and (ii) exporters must submit monthly balance-of-payments position reports where outstanding export receivables from non-residents exceed EUR 500,000 for goods. Reports are generally due by the tenth working day following the reporting month as indicated in the Bundesbank reporting calendar, although individual payment reports may be triggered earlier by the bank's compliance cycle. No approval, licence or conversion documentation is required from BAFA solely for the receipt of proceeds, unless the underlying high-power fiber laser cutting system itself is classified as dual-use under EU Regulation 2021/821.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank), acting jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) for foreign-trade and export-control enforcement. The Bundesbank administers all Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV - Außenwirtschaftsverordnung) reporting, cross-border payment statistics and balance-of-payments data collection.

Wise Fit: High

Rated MEDIUM for ancillary operating treasury and LOW as a primary clearing rail. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must be settled in EUR or major currencies through a BaFin-supervised bank account in the UG's full legal name; pass-through accounts, fintech omnibus accounts and third-party agent settlement are prohibited for equity or capital injections. For a cash capital increase, the shareholders' notarised resolution is filed with the commercial register (Handelsregister) before or concurrently with the SWIFT transfer; the wire message must state the legal purpose in the remittance field, e.g., 'Einlage Kapitalerhöhung UG' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' for loans. Because the UG's statutory minimum capital is only EUR 1, no capital escrow account is required for formation; however, the 25% annual-profit reserve obligation must be visible in the bank relationship to avoid re-characterisation of dividends. Inbound receipts above EUR 12,500 trigger an AWV balance-of-payments report to Deutsche Bundesbank; forwarding the report and SWIFT GPI tracking record to the relationship manager facilitates clearing. Only the actual corporate shareholder may appear as originator, preserving the equity-versus-loan distinction and preventing KYC re-classification as a third-party payment.The UG should use the following clearing rails by purpose: (i) supplier advances and T/T settlements in Asia and North America via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, with MT202 COV cover where an intermediate bank is involved; (ii) import machinery and laser-component purchases via Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700), preferably confirmed by a tier-1 German bank to protect the UG in cross-border contracts; (iii) advance payments to overseas contractors and raw-material suppliers secured by standby letters of credit or advance-payment bonds under URDG 758; (iv) EU low-value disbursements via SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) up to EUR 100,000; (v) EUR-leg settlement of all external operations via TARGET2 for final and irrevocable settlement; and (vi) CLS settlement for the delivery-versus-payment leg of FX transactions where both clearing banks are CLS members. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rated MEDIUM for ancillary operating treasury and LOW as a primary clearing rail. Any payment or e-money institution serving the UG must hold a BaFin licence under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or passport under PSD2/EMD2 from an EEA state. UK-authorised EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Currencycloud) lost EU passporting after Brexit and may operate only through a BaFin-licensed agent or a German/EEA subsidiary. Offshore non-EEA EMIs are not acceptable for export receivables, capital injections or LC settlement because they cannot provide documentary-credit services, correspondent-bank clearing under SWIFT, or an auditable source-of-funds trail. Recommended architecture: a tier-1 domestic bank for all trade settlement, FX hedging and capital flows; fintech rails may be used only for payroll, VAT, utility and low-value procurement.
