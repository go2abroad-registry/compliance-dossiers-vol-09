Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the Amtsgericht (Local Court) of the registered office district; electronic filing is effected by the notary via the common register portal (handelsregister.de / justiz.de).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Central Federal Tax Office, with local administration by the respective Finanzamt (tax office); electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de) for VAT IDs and other central tax matters.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt), the company must first be registered with the local Trade Office (Gewerbeamt) and the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The local Finanzamt then assigns a corporate tax registration number (Steuernummer) based on the trade registration filing. Upon application to the BZSt, a separate VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is issued for intra-Community and certain export transactions. Foreign entities without a domestic establishment that make taxable supplies in Germany must register for VAT through the Federal Central Tax Office via the simplified procedure under Section 18(9) UStG, using Form USt 1 TX, and obtain a domestic VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A UG is per se a corporation subject to entity-level taxation on corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag). Statutory election mechanisms include the formation of an Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Sections 14-19 KStG and 2 UStG, which consolidates CIT, VAT, and trade tax (where applicable) among a controlling parent and dependent legal entity; the optional small-enterprise treatment (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Section 19 UStG for resident entrepreneurs with low turnover (subject to strict limits and no entitlement to input VAT deduction); and the recognition of a profit- and loss-pooling agreement for corporate tax group allocation. Anti-hybrid rules under Section 4k EStG and the ATAD implementation restrict elective treatment of hybrid instruments and entities, with no elective neutralization mechanism for UG shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for established businesses; any person performing independent economic activity must register for VAT. The reduced small-enterprise exemption applies only where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000. For non-established foreign suppliers of digital services or distance sales of goods, an EU-wide distance sales threshold of EUR 10,000 per year triggers German VAT registration; outside that threshold, supplies to German consumers require immediate German VAT registration. Supply of goods or services from abroad to a German business customer generally shifts liability to the recipient via reverse charge, with no forced supplier registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Section 4 No. 1 UStG in conjunction with Section 6 UStG for goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union (export supplies). The exporter must be an entrepreneur and the goods must leave the EU through a customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted by customs. Adequate documentary proof in accordance with Sections 8-13 UStDV must be retained, usually comprising the customs export document, transport documents, and commercial invoices. Intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered customers in another Member State are also zero-rated under Sections 4 No. 1(b) and 6a UStG when the customer’s valid intra-Community VAT number is obtained and the goods are dispatched to another Member State. Offshore services to non-EU customers are generally zero-rated under Section 4 No. 2 UStG, provided the place of supply is outside the EU and the recipient is an entrepreneur who is a non-EU resident; the provider must maintain documented evidence of the customer’s status and the service’s actual use or enjoyment location.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic ATLAS/AES clearance summary with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing transportation out of the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export confirmation from customs authorities (Ausfuhrbestätigung)

Germany does not operate a continuous real-time fiscal clearance system comparable to those in Italy or Spain, and there is no mandatory digital stamping requirement. However, mandatory structured electronic invoicing is being phased in for domestic B2B transactions: as of 1 January 2025, invoicing software must be capable of receiving e-invoices; from 1 January 2027, businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices in a structured XML format (EN 16931, XRechnung, or ZUGFeRD). From 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B taxpayers must issue e-invoices. E-invoices must be transmitted via interoperable channels (e.g., Peppol) rather than PDF email. Export documentation continues to rely on customs processing via the ATLAS/AES electronic export system. B2B B2C payment transaction data is not cleared through a real-time tax-authority API in Germany.

Export zero-rating is subject to rigorous audit scrutiny by the German tax authorities. Documentation must be maintained for at least 10 years in accordance with Section 147 AO. If the exporter fails to provide credible and complete proof of export, the tax office reverses the zero-rating and assesses the full 19% VAT with late-payment interest at 0.5% per month (Section 233a AO, equating to 6% per annum). Fraudulent claims of export exemption, forged customs documents, or intentional non-repatriation of proceeds are treated as tax evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or by a fine, and in cases of grossly negligent conduct, a fine up to EUR 50,000 under Section 378 AO. In addition, all overclaimed input VAT deductions are clawed back retroactively, and administrative penalties are imposed under the Tax Code and VAT rules, including seizure of goods, personal liability of the managing director, and professional debarment for repeated violations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a euro-area member with fully convertible current-account transactions and an open capital account under EU capital movement liberalization (Art. 63 TFEU). Profit remittances by a German UG to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted subject to standard corporate law solvency tests, capital maintenance rules per Sec. 30 GmbHG as applied to UG, and tax compliance documentation (individual tax clearance certificates, withholding under Sec. 50a EStG for certain services). A UG is subject to German corporate income tax and trade tax at entity level with a flat 15% corporate tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax amount, and trade tax at municipal rates; dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to a statutory 25% capital yield tax plus solidarity surcharge unless reduced under an applicable double taxation treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No general exchange control authorization is required for cross-border payments; however, entities must file external economic reporting (External Assets Status Reporting, Externe Wirtschaftsprüfungsreporting) and payment statistics with the Deutsche Bundesbank for thresholds specified in the AWV.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export invoices in any convertible currency may be retained and used offshore or credited to German accounts freely. Under Sec. 67 AWV, cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 are reportable for balance-of-payments statistics, but the reporting is purely statistical and must be submitted to the Deutsche Bundesbank electronically within five business days of receipt.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) and the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking oversight; the Bundesbank administers external economic reporting and foreign trade payment statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium regulatory ceiling for day-to-day treasury operations; a German-registered or EU-passported Fintech/EMI (e.g. PPRO, Airwallex, or Solaris-backed entities) can support cross-border collections and disbursements as payment initiation or e-money accounts, but such accounts do not substitute for a full corporate banking relationship with credit facilities, commercial documentary letters of credit, or large-value SEPA/TARGET2 settlement. Offshore, non-EU EMIs without a BaFin license or EU passport are legally restricted from providing payment services to a German domestic entity unless operated under an entity-based FinTech license or as a contractually authorized agent; to avoid regulatory friction, treasury architecture must maintain a primary Tier-1 bank account and use EMIs only for sub-limit operational flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound euro transfers to a UG must use SEPA (single euro payments area) where the creditor has a German IBAN; non-EUR remittances must be converted to EUR at a bank-negotiated mid-market rate and executed by SWIFT MT103 with an explicit purpose code (e.g. 'TQ' for equity subscription or '400' for acquisition/provision of capital under AWV classification). The corporate bank must receive a board resolution approving the inward capital injection, proof of shareholder identity, and for non-resident shareholders a compliance declaration establishing the legal source of funds; remittance must not be posted to the operational account without marking against the capital reserve account in the notional cash ledger, and the corporate accountant must preserve the Bundesbank record of receipt for external asset reporting period. Large non-euro injections can be settled via an offshore foreign currency account and immediately converted in the German bank to EUR, subject to applicable due diligence and beneficial-ownership confirmation.Operational outbound settlements for industrial edge computing exports are primarily executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for supply-chain payments and via irrevocable, confirmed commercial letters of credit (documentary collection under UCP600) for third-party export sale settlements. Disbursements to vendors outside the euro-zone are routed via TARGET2 or correspondent banking chains under settlement currency matching rules, requiring the treasury to submit commercial invoices, transport evidence, and periodic AWV transaction reports for balances exceeding EUR 12,500.
