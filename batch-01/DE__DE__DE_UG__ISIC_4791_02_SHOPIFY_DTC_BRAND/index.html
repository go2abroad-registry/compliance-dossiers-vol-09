Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. Electronic tax filings and registrations are generally handled through the ELSTER platform.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must notify the competent Finanzamt of its business commencement through the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER. The Finanzamt then issues the domestic Steuernummer. The entity may apply to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr., generally through the BZSt application process or through the relevant tax registration process. A non-resident entity conducting taxable German activities must identify its German tax obligations, register with the Finanzamt responsible for the relevant activity or representative, submit the tax registration questionnaire, and apply for a USt-IdNr. where intra-Community transactions require it. The USt-IdNr. does not replace the domestic Steuernummer for German tax filings. The application normally requires legal name, legal form, registered address, commercial-register details, business activity, management information, and evidence of taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German capital company and is ordinarily subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and applicable VAT. Germany has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a UG. The corporate form generally determines entity-level taxation. Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act permits certain eligible partnerships to elect corporate taxation, but it does not provide an election for a UG. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules, including Section 4k of the Income Tax Act and related ATAD implementation provisions, operate by law and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for a general German business VAT registration threshold: there is no universal turnover threshold below which a business carrying out taxable German supplies is automatically outside VAT registration. The German small-business regime under Section 19 UStG may exempt qualifying domestic supplies where prior-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and applicable transitional rules. For EU intra-Community distance sales and certain electronically supplied consumer services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold is relevant, with taxation in the customer’s Member State above the threshold or upon use of the One Stop Shop regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods supplied from Germany and transported or dispatched to a destination outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt exports with the right to deduct input VAT under Sections 4 No. 1(a) and 6 UStG, provided the goods leave the EU customs territory and the supplier satisfies the required export, purchaser, and documentary conditions. For intra-Community supplies of goods, the exemption generally requires transport to another EU Member State, a valid customer VAT number, inclusion in the required recapitulative statement, and proof of transport or dispatch. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is outside Germany. Their VAT treatment depends on the place-of-supply rules, including the customer’s status, service type, and actual place of use or enjoyment. Export-related services may be outside German VAT or exempt only where the statutory place-of-supply and exemption conditions are met.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and export MRN where required
  • Customs confirmation of exit or equivalent electronic export evidence
  • Commercial invoice, order record, and evidence identifying the goods, purchaser, destination, and transaction value
  • Transport or dispatch evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, courier tracking record, or carrier confirmation, where applicable
  • Proof of delivery or import documentation where required to establish departure from the EU customs territory
  • For intra-Community supplies: valid customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and recapitulative statement documentation
  • Inward remittance or bank payment evidence is supporting evidence in appropriate cases but is not a universal German statutory condition for export VAT exemption

Germany does not generally operate a real-time VAT invoice-clearance or continuous transaction-control system. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exclusions. Mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices is phased in: generally from 2027 for larger issuers and from 2028 for other issuers, with turnover and transitional-rule conditions. Accepted structured formats include legally compliant formats such as XRechnung and certain ZUGFeRD versions. E-invoices must satisfy German VAT invoice requirements and be retained in an audit-compliant manner. There is no general requirement for API pre-clearance, government digital stamping, or real-time transmission for ordinary domestic B2B or B2C invoices. Federal and state public-sector B2G requirements may impose separate electronic-invoicing specifications.

Germany generally imposes no universal statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or input VAT recovery may be denied or clawed back if export, transport, customer-status, place-of-supply, or documentary requirements are not satisfied. The tax office may assess unpaid VAT retrospectively, recover incorrectly refunded input VAT, impose statutory interest under the German Fiscal Code, and apply late-filing or inaccurate-return penalties. Intentional or reckless false VAT claims may result in administrative fines or criminal tax-evasion liability, including imprisonment under the German Fiscal Code, depending on the facts and amount involved. Records must be retained for the applicable German tax-retention periods and be available for tax audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU Treaty and the AWV. There is no exchange control, no surrender requirement, and no restriction on profit remittance. Dividends, royalties, loan repayments, and disposal proceeds may be freely repatriated to non-resident shareholders, subject only to standard tax withholding (25% plus solidarity surcharge, where applicable, unless reduced under a tax treaty) and to statutory reporting thresholds for cross-border payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists. Foreign currency export receipts may be retained in any currency. Reporting obligations apply under Sections 66-70 AWV to Deutsche Bundesbank for payments exceeding EUR 12,500; export proceeds received from abroad on bank accounts must be reported via Z5/Z6 reporting forms or the Bundesbank's electronic channel by the fifth business day of the month following receipt.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank as the German central bank and foreign payment reporting authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy (BMWK) and BAFA overseeing trade controls; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banking and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary liquidity and payment-rail channel only. BaFin-licensed or BaFin-passported EEA electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, and similar EMI agents registered with the Bundesbank) may legally hold funds and provide multi-currency IBANs, but they cannot be used as the primary statutory capital escrow or as the sole operating bank for a German UG in a fully tax-compliant treasury structure. Offshore non-EEA EMIs without a BaFin passport are not permitted to provide payment services to German residents under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); their accounts may trigger adverse tax and AML credibility assessments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions must be executed via direct bank transfer in EUR using the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or, for non-EUR transfers, via SWIFT MT103. The transfer purpose code must reference 'VW130 – Einlage / Kapitalerhöhung' (or the Bundesbank's corresponding Geschäftsvorfall code for paid-in corporate capital) under the AWV reporting framework. The essence-of-validity for a UG subscription is the unconditional receipt of deposits; therefore the bank must issue a 'Geldeingang' confirmation, and the notarial deed must show the contribution as secured. For larger intragroup equity injections from a German intermediate holding, a cleaner treasury route is a shareholder loan under the AWV statistical code 'VW720' (Darlehen), documented with a written loan agreement and arm's-length interest rate.Outbound trade payments should be executed on SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire transfers, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for European suppliers, and commercial nstrument accelerators through scheme rails (Visa/Mastercard) against real e-commerce refunds.
