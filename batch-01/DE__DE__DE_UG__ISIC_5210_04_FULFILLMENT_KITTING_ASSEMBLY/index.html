Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and locally competent Finanzamt; electronic filing via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A newly formed UG or a non-resident entrepreneur engaged in taxable German activities must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer); for VAT purposes, the entrepreneur submits form USt 1 TJ to obtain a value added tax identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) issued by the BZSt. Non-EU entrepreneurs may be required to appoint a fiscal representative; EU entrepreneurs may register directly and must name a domestic recipient for service of process where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A newly formed UG or a non-resident entrepreneur engaged in taxable German activities must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer); for VAT purposes, the entrepreneur submits form USt 1 TJ to obtain a value added tax identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) issued by the BZSt. Non-EU entrepreneurs may be required to appoint a fiscal representative; EU entrepreneurs may register directly and must name a domestic recipient for service of process where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No German check-the-box regime exists; the UG is a corporation subject to entity-level Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer and Solidaritätszuschlag. Available elections include the VAT option to tax real property letting under §9 UStG, formation of a VAT and corporation tax group (Organschaft) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG and §§14 ff. KStG, business valuation and trade tax apportionment elections, and hybrid mismatch corresponding adjustments under §4k EStG (ATAD implementation).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year turnover for the small business exemption (§19 UStG, as amended effective 1 January 2025); non-resident entrepreneurs are subject to mandatory VAT registration with no materiality threshold for taxable supplies performed in Germany or distance sales of goods into Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods qualify for the 0% rate under §4 Nr. 1a and §6 UStG where the goods are dispatched or transported by the supplier or on the supplier's account to a third-country territory (including China) and the supplier is the exporter under customs rules. For fulfilment and kitting services performed in Germany for non-resident principals, such services are generally B2B supplies of services taxable at the recipient's place of establishment under §3a Abs. 2 UStG (reverse-charge or non-taxation in Germany) rather than zero-rated exports; actual transport from the German warehouse to the third country is required for goods zero-rating. The supplier must obtain and retain documentary evidence of the export under §§8-9 UStDV.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung) evidencing MRN and the exporter of record (§8 UStDV)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing transport to the third-country destination (§9 UStDV)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (Handelsrechnung und Packliste) describing goods and export delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment records evidencing unconditional receipt of consideration

Germany mandates B2B e-invoicing under §14 UStG (growth opportunities law). As of 1 January 2025, all German businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); large enterprises must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies from 2025, and all businesses must issue e-invoices from 1 January 2027. No real-time continuous transaction control clearance or digital stamping is currently operative; the e-invoice must be transmitted machine-readable via the E-Rechnung format. Paper invoices remain permissible for B2C and exempt supplies.

German tax audits strictly verify the completeness of export evidence on a real-time basis. Deficient or falsified proof triggers retrospective zero-rating clawback, additional VAT assessments, interest of 0.5% per month under §233a AO, and penalties under §§370, 378 AO for tax evasion or negligent tax shortfall, including prison terms of up to 5 years for deliberate evasion (up to 10 years in aggravated cases). Suppliers bear the burden of proof (Feststellungslast) and cannot cure missing documentation after the audit begins except in narrowly defined unforeseeable circumstances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No central bank license, exchange-certificate, or prior approval is required for trade payments, service disbursements, profit remittances, or capital transfers. The euro is fully convertible; foreign currencies may be purchased and transferred freely. Profit remittance is not an exchange-control issue; it is a tax issue: a German UG distributing dividends to a non-resident shareholder triggers dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, equal to 26.375% gross withholding, unless reduced under an applicable income tax treaty. The UG remains subject to ordinary German corporate income tax at 15% plus trade tax on retailed profits, but no additional convertibility or monetary restriction applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender, no forced conversion ratio, and no stipulated settlement period for export proceeds in Germany. Export collections in EUR or foreign currency may be retained or converted freely. The sole binding duty is statistical: any receipt from abroad exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days under Section 67 AWV. The report is for balance-of-payments statistics and does not condition the receipt, use, or conversion of the export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), in conjunction with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action and the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV), administered through the Bundesbank's External Sector Statistics and Balance of Payments Section.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: High as a supplementary multi-currency clearing and collection rail; Low as a sole principal banking relationship. Airwallex, Wise Business, and similar EMIs hold e-money licences under PSD2 / EEA frameworks and can provide EBICS/API-driven accounts, short virtual IBANs, and SWIFT MT103 gates. They are not full credit institutions under the German Kreditwesengesetz and therefore cannot provide sweep deposits, documentary letters of credit, or deposit protection. For a German UG, domestic tax, customs, and KYC obligations require a real German bank account or a fully interoperable partner-bank settlement structure; offshore digital EMI balances must be backed with outgoing transfer evidence and AWV statistical declarations for payments exceeding EUR 12,500.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825% effective) plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at 3.5% base rate × municipal multiplier (typically 200–490%), yielding a combined effective burden of approx. 30% (e.g., Berlin: 30.175%; Munich: 32.975%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer (§§ 43, 43a EStG) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the WHT, yielding an aggregate statutory withholding of 26.375% on gross dividends; trade tax does not apply to the dividend recipient's worldwide income in the case of a UG distributing to a non-resident corporate shareholder (dividends are subject to KeSt at source only)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory for cross-border controlled transactions aggregating more than EUR 1,500,000 per related party per fiscal year (German administrative practice under GAufzV); Master File: mandatory where worldwide group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country Report: groups with consolidated group revenue ≥ EUR 750,000,000; German tax authorities may impose documentation surcharges of 5% of the adjusted profit or EUR 500 (up to EUR 5,000) for incomplete or late filings

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations like UAE/offshore centres; substance is nevertheless determinative: § 50d(3) EStG denies treaty benefits to letterbox entities lacking business activity, staff, premises and economic risk; the German CFC rules (§§ 7-13 AStG) re-attribute passive income of low-taxed controlled foreign subsidiaries lacking real substance to the German shareholder; the ATAD-based interest limitation rule and anti-hybrid rules (§ 4k EStG) require a real presence; the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must demonstrate genuine operational substance (warehouse, kitting labour, IT systems, local management) to preserve the tax-residency status and the deductibility of intercompany charges; register in the German transparency register and maintain arm's-length intragroup supply-chain documentation to avoid reclassification as a shell under § 42 AO abuse doctrines.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine under § 370 AO; in especially severe cases (large-scale evasion > EUR 50,000) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years; criminal fines up to the amount of the tax advantage; disputed civil interest of 0.5% per month not applicable - assessment interest of 6% p.a. under § 233a AO and criminal penalty may be combined; contested tax assessments can be enforced with immediate effect

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under § 69 AO for unpaid taxes and interest if duties were intentionally or grossly negligently breached; criminal courts may impose travel bans (passaußerweisung) and confiscate passports during the investigation; the corporate veil is pierced under § 826 BGB and § 64 GmbHG for unlawful distributions; directors face imprisonment up to 5 years and civil restitution for the full tax loss, including social security contributions withheld from employees
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under § 50d(3) EStG and OECD MLI principal-purpose test; reattribution of income to the beneficial owner with 25%-26.375% withholding on deemed distributions and 15.825% royalty WHT; additional 5% documentation surcharge and 6% p.a. assessment interest; exclusion of the UG from the German corporate tax consolidation; administrative penalty of up to EUR 30,000 under § 42 AO for abusive arrangements

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall (Haftungsschaden) under § 69 AO; if the public prosecution establishes abandonment of insolvency or fraudulent concealment of assets, directors may face imprisonment under §§ 15a InsO and 283 StGB; travel bans and passport revocation are routinely imposed in cross-border profit-stripping investigations; civil claims by the tax office may attach to personal assets after a FIBA inquiry
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax office may estimate the profit by upward adjustment (§ 162 AO) and impose a documentation surcharge of 5% of the adjusted amount, minimum EUR 500 and up to EUR 5,000; in severe cases, the full adjustment plus interest of 6% p.a. is collected; if the adjustment exceeds EUR 10,000, criminal fines under §§ 369-370 AO may be triggered for intentional false statements

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return without a TP file are deemed negligent; liability under § 69 AO extends only to taxes in the shortfall; a criminal conviction for evasion carries imprisonment up to 5 years and disgorgement of benefits; asset freezes under § 111b StPO may precede the final verdict; the UG's director is additionally liable for unpaid VAT and wage tax collected on employees
