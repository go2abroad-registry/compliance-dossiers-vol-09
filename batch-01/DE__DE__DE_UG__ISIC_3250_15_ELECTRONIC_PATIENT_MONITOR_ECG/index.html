Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is formed with a minimum share capital of EUR 1, fully paid in cash or kind before registration; cash contributions must be at the free disposal of the managing director. The UG is statutorily obliged to build an annual legal reserve of 25% of net profit until the registered share capital reaches the regular GmbH minimum of EUR 25,000. No court escrow or blocked-account deposit is otherwise required for the UE format when the registered capital is fully paid.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the competent fire protection authority confirming compliance with escape routes, fire alarms, fire extinguishers and insurance requirements; structural load inspection by the building authority for the installation of calibration benches and loading areas; workplace clearance from the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt confirming safe electrical installations, ESD controls and ergonomic arrangement of work stations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authorities are the locally responsible German tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate tax, trade tax and domestic VAT registration, and the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). The principal electronic portal is ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de. The BZSt VAT identification number service is available at www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must register with the competent Finanzamt after commencement or preparation of business by submitting the electronic tax registration questionnaire through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the German tax number (Steuernummer) and registers the entity for corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, trade tax and, where applicable, VAT. The entity applies separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), normally using the tax number and commercial-register details. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in Germany must submit the applicable tax registration questionnaire to the responsible Finanzamt, provide constitutional documents, foreign tax-residence evidence, business activities, bank details, expected turnover, supply-chain information and evidence of German taxable transactions, and obtain a German tax number where required. It must apply for a USt-IdNr. if it needs to identify itself in intra-Community transactions. Non-EU businesses may be required to use the German VAT refund procedure or appoint an authorised tax representative where legally applicable. Registration does not itself create a permanent establishment; permanent-establishment and place-of-supply analysis remains necessary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a corporation and is subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the German Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG), solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election for a UG. The corporate-tax option under Section 1a KStG is principally available to eligible partnerships and does not convert a UG into a pass-through entity. A UG may convert, merge or otherwise reorganise under the German Transformation Act, subject to statutory conditions and tax-neutral reorganisation rules; these are not entity-classification elections. German anti-hybrid mismatch provisions, including Section 4k KStG and related provisions, may deny deductions or impose income inclusions for specified cross-border hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business making taxable supplies is exempt from German VAT registration. The domestic small-business regime under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply where the prior-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions. The small-business regime is an exemption from charging VAT, not a general exclusion from tax registration or recordkeeping obligations. Certain non-established suppliers may use EU One-Stop-Shop or import schemes instead of ordinary German registration where the relevant EU conditions are satisfied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union are generally VAT-exempt with the right to deduct related input VAT under Sections 4(1)(a) and 6 of the German VAT Act, provided the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the statutory export evidence is maintained. The exporter must establish both the non-EU destination and the exporter or customer status required by the relevant export category. Intra-Community supplies are generally VAT-exempt rather than technically zero-rated and require a valid customer VAT identification number, transport evidence and recapitulative-statement compliance. Services are not automatically export-zero-rated. B2B services generally follow the customer-location rule, with reverse charge potentially applying; B2C and special-category services require separate place-of-supply analysis. Input VAT refunds require valid supplier invoices, a direct business connection and absence of deduction restrictions. Medical monitoring equipment may also require customs classification, export-control screening and product-regulatory compliance, but those matters do not replace VAT evidence requirements.

  • Electronic export declaration filed through the German customs export system, including the Movement Reference Number and electronic exit confirmation where applicable
  • Commercial invoice identifying the German exporter, consignee, goods description, value, delivery terms, destination and relevant VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note or carrier confirmation
  • Proof of dispatch or receipt at the non-EU destination where required by the applicable export-evidence rules
  • Packing list and customs export records, including customs decision or export accompanying document where applicable
  • Purchase order, sales contract and customer correspondence supporting the export transaction
  • Evidence of payment or commercial settlement where relevant to audit substantiation
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number validation, transport declaration, transport documents and recapitulative statement; these documents support exemption rather than a third-country export zero rate
  • For services: contract, customer-location evidence, service-delivery records, customer VAT identification number where relevant, reverse-charge wording and evidence supporting the applicable place-of-supply rule

Germany does not generally operate real-time central VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital stamping. For domestic B2B supplies between German businesses, the recipient must be able to receive an electronic invoice from 1 January 2025. Issuance is subject to transitional rules: paper invoices and other non-structured formats may generally continue through 31 December 2026; issuers with prior-year domestic turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally continue until 31 December 2027; structured e-invoicing becomes generally required from 1 January 2028, subject to statutory exceptions. Compliant formats include XRechnung and other EN 16931-compliant structured invoices, including qualifying hybrid ZUGFeRD formats. EDI arrangements may qualify if they satisfy the statutory requirements. The invoice must contain required VAT particulars, be authentic, integral and legible, and be retained for the statutory retention period. Public-sector B2G e-invoicing requirements apply under applicable federal or Länder rules and may differ by contracting authority.

The Finanzamt may deny the VAT exemption or input-tax deduction, assess output VAT retrospectively, issue amended assessments, impose statutory interest and require repayment of refunds where export, transport, customer-status, invoice or place-of-supply evidence is incomplete or inaccurate. Incorrect VAT returns and invoices may trigger correction obligations, administrative penalties and, in serious cases, criminal liability for tax evasion under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code, including fines or imprisonment, with limitation periods that may extend to ten years for aggravated tax evasion. Intentional or reckless breaches of VAT invoicing, recordkeeping, declaration or cooperation duties may also result in regulatory fines. Fraudulent transactions may lead to joint-liability assessments, denial of input VAT, customs consequences and extended audit scrutiny. Voluntary disclosure rules are strictly limited and do not automatically cure fraudulent conduct or all third-party participation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully liberalised convertibility regime for both current and capital account transactions in accordance with EU free-movement-of-capital principles and the OECD Code of Liberalisation. No exchange-control permissions are required for inbound capital contributions, trade payments, dividend remittances, or intra-group financing. Profit remittances are free from exchange restrictions, but a dividend distribution by a German UG is subject to withholding tax of 20.5% (15% dividend tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced under a double-taxation treaty; treaty relief must be supported by a valid tax-residence certificate and, where applicable, a refund application to the German Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency and used freely. The only material compliance obligation is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV): residents must ensure that cross-border payments exceeding the monthly aggregate threshold of EUR 12,500 are reported, normally by the bank through the SWIFT/SEPA payment instructions, by the fifth banking day of the following month. No approval or conversion ratio applies to export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banks and payment institutions; the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) administers foreign-trade controls and cross-border statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for operational treasury, SEPA/SWIFT payments and FX cost optimisation when the EMI holds an EEA e-money licence under PSD2 and is registered under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). Medium for capital injections and documentary letters of credit because most EMIs do not offer LC issuance, full SWIFT sponsorship or direct access to TARGET2 liquidity. Low for offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA because such entities cannot lawfully accept German client funds or issue German IBANs without a BaFin licence or passporting notice.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder transfers EUR via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer if the shareholder is an EU resident) to the UG's designated operational or capital-contribution account. The payment must reference 'Kapitaleinlage / Contribution No. [X]' and, if required by the bank, an ISO 20022 purpose code such as 'CINV' (capital investment). The UG must provide the notarised contribution resolution and commercial-register extract so the bank can verify the receiving account and the payee. No Deutsche Bundesbank approval is required, but the receipt is captured in the bank's AWV cross-border statistics report when the amount exceeds the EUR 12,500 threshold; the bank must be instructed to allocate funds to the correct capital contribution ledger.For EU suppliers and service providers, use SEPA Credit Transfers in EUR with TARGET2 same-day clearing, referencing the invoice and order number. For non-EU and non-EUR disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 with full remittance information (invoice numbers, contract references, Incoterms, and ISIC scope). For high-value or high-risk component imports, issue or receive a commercial letter of credit under UCP600 using SWIFT MT700; settlement is made by MT103/MT202 after presentation of compliant documents (commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, inspection certificate). The UG must retain all trade documentation to satisfy bank due diligence and Bundesbank reporting obligations.Deliverable EUR/USD FX forward concluded under an ISDA/FX Master Agreement with a German or EEA bank; exposure must be commensurate with committed import/export invoices.European-style FX options (EUR/USD and EUR/CNH) for downside protection on USD or CNH procurement costs.Cross-currency swap (EUR/USD or EUR/CNH) for matching long-term USD capital injections or equipment lease liabilities.Offshore CNY non-deliverable forward (NDF) because deliverable onshore CNY may not be accessible from a UG account; counterparty must be a bank outside mainland China.FX swap (swap-in/swap-out) for short-term EUR/USD liquidity bridging without creating an open trade-position hedge.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for operational treasury, SEPA/SWIFT payments and FX cost optimisation when the EMI holds an EEA e-money licence under PSD2 and is registered under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). Medium for capital injections and documentary letters of credit because most EMIs do not offer LC issuance, full SWIFT sponsorship or direct access to TARGET2 liquidity. Low for offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA because such entities cannot lawfully accept German client funds or issue German IBANs without a BaFin licence or passporting notice.

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: fines up to €1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; administrative penalties

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for restitution, fines, and criminal prosecution including imprisonment if they direct or authorize the evasion
Non-Compliance with Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to 5% of the transaction value; potential retroactive transfer pricing adjustments

Director personal liability: Personal liability for corporate officers responsible for compliance; possible fines and disqualification

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 50% of the tax due plus interest; possible criminal sanctions

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the tax amount, penalties, and interest; potential imprisonment for willful misconduct

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal restitution obligations, fines, and criminal liability
