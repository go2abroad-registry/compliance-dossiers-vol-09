A virtual office registration in Germany is permissible only if the registered address is a physical place where legal process and statutory notices can be served and recorded mail is accepted. For an EPC light-rail constructor, a purely virtual/co-working address is acceptable for registration but a physical site office or project office in the target state may be required for contract tender awards and for commercial substance verification. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) and local tax office (Finanzamt) responsible for the registered seat of the entity; portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung, www.elster.de) for tax registration and filings.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German branch or a domestic UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must first complete commercial register registration (Handelsregister). The local tax office (Finanzamt) will then assign a tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax and trade tax. For VAT purposes, the entity must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER or paper form; the Finanzamt issues a VAT registration number (USt-IdNr.) for intra-community transactions, and the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) assigns the EU-wide VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). Foreign non-resident entities with taxable supplies in Germany must complete simplified registration through the competent Finanzamt or use the BZSt's special procedure for non-established taxable persons.

Foreign non-resident entities with taxable supplies in Germany must complete simplified registration through the competent Finanzamt or use the BZSt's special procedure for non-established taxable persons.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a general check-the-box regime for domestic entities; a UG is treated as a corporation subject to corporate income tax and trade tax. An entity may elect to form a partnership (Personengesellschaft) to obtain pass-through taxation, but a UG cannot elect pass-through status. Hybrid entity treatment may arise under the German CFC (Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung) rules and under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) implementation; certain hybrid mismatch elections are restricted under Sec. 4k EStG for OECD BEPS Action 2. For cross-border structures, an incorporated entity can be reclassified under the German 'qualifikationskonflikt' rules only when treaty relief is claimed; otherwise domestic entity-level taxation prevails.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Foreign entrepreneurs without fixed establishment in Germany: EUR 22,000 (EUR 0 for sellers of new means of transport or distance sellers if above EU remote-sales thresholds; small business domestic threshold: EUR 22,000 estimated turnover for the current year and EUR 50,000 prior year; mandatory registration for foreign businesses irrespective of threshold once any taxable service or distance sale is performed in Germany, subject to reverse charge and OSS special rules).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to non-EU destinations and certain intra-EU dispatches qualify for 0% VAT under Sec. 4 No. 1, Sec. 6 UStG if the entrepreneur proves that the goods have physically left the EU/Community territory within specified statutory time limits (generally 3 months for exports to non-EU countries, 6 months for intra-Community supplies). Services related to exports or offshore supplies, including commissioning, installation, and repair of movable property for export, may also be zero-rated if the recipient is an entrepreneur or the service directly benefits export goods; rail-transit EPC construction services physically performed in other countries are generally outside the scope of German VAT or zero-rated under Sec. 0 UStG if location rules establish foreign place of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / electronic export declaration via ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (Konnossement), including airway bill or CMR/rail consignment note as applicable
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and declared export value
  • Proof of export confirmation by customs (Ausfuhrbescheinigung) on the prescribed form (e.g., form 2.3 for EDP-based export evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (for proof of foreign consideration when required by tax office audit practice, especially for cross-border supplies between related parties or when anti-fraud verification demands payment evidence)

Germany has implemented mandatory e-invoicing for B2B domestic supplies from 1 January 2025 for taxpayers established in Germany, with phased obligations: full structured data exchange using XRechnung or ZUGFeRD; receiving e-invoices is mandatory from 2025, issuing obligations phase in by 2027 for businesses above turnover thresholds. Germany does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or tax authority digital stamping system for regular VAT; clearance is via periodic VAT returns submitted through ELSTER, with increasing use of e-invoice reporting (Meldedaten) to be transmitted for domestic B2B transactions from 2027/2028 under the Growth Opportunities Act.

False claims of export zero-rating are treated as tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under Sec. 370 AO and as VAT fraud under Sec. 26a/26b UStG and Sec. 73 AO; penalties include up to 10 years imprisonment for criminal evasion, monetary fines of up to EUR 50,000 per violation under administrative fine statutes, and immediate VAT assessment plus interest at 0.5% per month (6% annually) on underdeclared tax. Retrospective clawback of zero-rating is mandatory if the exporter fails to provide documentary proof within the statutory evidence period or if the goods are returned/diverted into the German/EU market; joint and several liability may be imposed on transporters, freight forwarders, or prior suppliers participating in an unethical VAT chain. Audit scrutiny is conducted through the Bundeszentralamt (BZSt) risk analysis and local Finanzamt export verification programs, with a standard six-year record retention period for tax documents and a ten-year period for real property developers, but export substantiation evidence must be kept for the duration of the assessment period and in practice for at least 10 years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible currency regime for both capital and current accounts under EU law and national FX regulations. The Euro is freely convertible with no restrictions on profit remittances for resident companies. Capital account transactions are permitted without prior approval except for real estate investments exceeding €500,000, which require Bundesbank notification. Profit repatriation faces no convertibility hurdles beyond standard KYC and AML compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under German law. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts. Statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank is required for individual shipments exceeding €12,500. For urban light rail EPC contracts, international client proceeds are received via SWIFT MT103 and disbursed directly to suppliers without forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are feasible through licensed German fintech providers such as Revolut Business or N26 Business for EUR settlements, but cross-border FX transfers to non-EU counterparties are subject to full KYC/AML review and may trigger licensing obligations under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG).
