Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) administered by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) at the company's registered office; corporate filings are submitted exclusively by an appointed German notary in electronic form.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG), specifically § 1 (formation), § 8 (application for registration), § 9a (false declarations), § 10 (registration content), and § 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt); supplemented by the German Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 8–12 for Commercial Register procedural rules, and the Federal Industrial Code (GewO) § 14 for trade registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory Model Protocol (Musterprotokoll); registered via the Commercial Register application (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) under Form HR B.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); local Finanzamt (e.g., Finanzamt für Körperschaften) for the tax number (Steuernummer) and corporate income tax/trade tax assessment; ELSTER electronic portal (www.elster.de) for filings; Hauptzollamt and ATLAS system for customs/EORI registration.

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders establishing a UG must: (1) notarize the articles of association and register the UG in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the Steuernummer, which covers corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT; (3) apply separately to BZSt via ELSTER or BZSt online services (form USt 1) for the USt-IdNr.; (4) obtain an EORI number from the Generaldirektion der Zöllne (ATLAS customs registration) before first export; (5) register for VAT as a taxable person regardless of actual turnover because the UG is a capital company with entity-level taxation; foreign non-resident ownership is permitted without a local managing director mandate beyond the UG's statutory Geschäftsführer requirement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a private limited-liability capital company treated as a separate taxable person under the Körperschaftsteuergesetz and Umsatzsteuergesetz; it is subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15%, solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of assessed corporate income tax, Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at a municipality-dependent effective rate of approximately 7%–17%, and statutory VAT at 19% on domestic supplies; the UG is not a pass-through vehicle and no partnership transparency applies. — Foreign shareholders establishing a UG must: (1) notarize the articles of association and register the UG in the commercial register (Handelsregister); (2) submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt to obtain the Steuernummer, which covers corporation tax, trade tax, and VAT; (3) apply separately to BZSt via ELSTER or BZSt online services (form USt 1) for the USt-IdNr.; (4) obtain an EORI number from the Generaldirektion der Zöllne (ATLAS customs registration) before first export; (5) register for VAT as a taxable person regardless of actual turnover because the UG is a capital company with entity-level taxation; foreign non-resident ownership is permitted without a local managing director mandate beyond the UG's statutory Geschäftsführer requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a private limited-liability capital company treated as a separate taxable person under the Körperschaftsteuergesetz and Umsatzsteuergesetz; it is subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15%, solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of assessed corporate income tax, Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at a municipality-dependent effective rate of approximately 7%–17%, and statutory VAT at 19% on domestic supplies; the UG is not a pass-through vehicle and no partnership transparency applies.

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; available statutory elections and mechanisms include: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §14 KStG for corporate income tax and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG for VAT, allowing consolidation of a German parent and the UG; (ii) small-entrepreneur election under §19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung) where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and expected current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000 (post-2025 thresholds); (iii) option to tax real property lettings under §9 UStG; (iv) trade-tax and VAT grouping via an Ergebnisabführungsvertrag (profit-and-loss pooling agreement); and (v) anti-hybrid aligned elections under §4k EStG that restrict interest deduction or royalty structures in hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No absolute turnover threshold for a resident UG: VAT registration is mandatory as soon as the UG performs any taxable supply in Germany; the simplified Kleinunternehmer exemption under §19 UStG is available only where the prior calendar-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and the current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; however, an export-oriented UG, such as one selling die-cast scale models to third-country customers, must register and obtain a USt-IdNr. to invoice exports at zero rate and to reclaim input VAT on export-related purchases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU third countries are exempt from VAT with full input VAT deduction under §4 Nr. 1 lit. a and §6 UStG; intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers are likewise exempt under §4 Nr. 1 lit. b and §6a UStG provided the customer's valid USt-IdNr. is confirmed and the goods physically leave Germany; eligibility requires verifiable physical movement of goods across the EU customs border within the statutory time frames, evidenced by an ATLAS export declaration, transport documents (CMR, Bill of Lading, Air Waybill), a commercial invoice with Incoterms, and, for EU supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number; the die-cast scale-model products (HS 9503 / subheading 9503.00) qualify for zero rating when the aforementioned documentary tests are met.

  • ATLAS Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing departure from Germany/EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice stating exporter, consignee, Incoterms, HS classification, and detailed product description
  • Packing list with quantities, weights, and package identification
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank credit confirmation demonstrating receipt of export proceeds
  • Valid USt-IdNr. of the German exporter and, for intra-EU supplies, the customer's validated EU VAT ID

From 1 January 2025, all domestic German B2B taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (e-invoicing) in formats XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.3 (EN 16931), or EDI; issuing obligations are phased — mandatory from 1 January 2027 for taxpayers whose prior-year turnover exceeded EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all taxable persons; Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime (unlike Italy or France), but invoices must satisfy authenticity, integrity, and readability requirements under §14 UStG; export invoices to third countries may remain in paper or PDF form if the movement-of-goods evidence is properly documented; customs declarations must be filed electronically via the ATLAS system with Movement Reference Numbers (MRN).

The German tax audit (Betriebsprüfung) applies strict documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating claims; missing, incomplete, or contradictory movement-of-goods evidence results in retrospective reversal of the zero rate and re-invoicing of 19% VAT, plus interest under §233a AO (0.15% per month) and late-payment surcharges under §240 AO; administrative fines may be imposed for deliberate documentary non-compliance; fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under §370 AO punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years or a monetary penalty; the limitation period for intentional tax evasion is extended to 10 years under §169 AO; additional clawback exposure arises under §25d UStG for joint and several liability where the supplier knew or should have known of a customer's tax evasion, and German customs authorities may pursue confiscation and penalty proceedings for false export declarations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag, resulting in a combined federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%; municipal Gewerbesteuer is separately levied at an effective rate typically between 14% and 17%, producing an aggregate headline tax burden of approximately 30% on German-source distributed profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, i.e. 26.375% on the gross dividend distributed by the German UG to a shareholder. Important UG-specific restriction: §5a(3) GmbHG requires the UG to retain 25% of annual net profit until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000; any legally distributable dividend remains fully subject to the 26.375% withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be submitted by 31 July of the year following the assessment period; where a tax advisor prepares the return, the deadline is extended to 28 February or 29 February in leap years of the following year. For a non-calendar fiscal year, the statutory deadline is the end of the seventh calendar month after the fiscal year end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 for the Local File: contemporaneous documentation is mandatory for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 in a fiscal year under the GAufzV. A Master File is required where the worldwide group turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Documentation must be prepared by the filing deadline and delivered to the German tax office within 60 days of a request, or 30 days for exceptional transactions.

Economic substance scope: Germany is not an Economic Substance Regulations jurisdiction; the German legal order applies the equivalent functional and anti-abuse rules. Under §42 AO, abusive transactions lacking economic or business purpose are disregarded; under §50d(3) EStG, treaty or directive relief is denied where the recipient lacks substance, personnel, premises, bank account control, and genuine business activity. The UG must in practice maintain its actual management seat in Germany, make operational decisions there, bear entrepreneurial risks, and have documentary evidence of functions, personnel, assets, premises, stock, logistics and e-commerce contracts to defend its German tax residence and transfer pricing positions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax declaration

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to five years or a fine under §370 AO; in aggravated cases involving large-scale evasion or abuse of authority, imprisonment of six months to ten years under §370(3) AO. The UG remains liable for the evaded tax, solidarity surcharge, trade tax and statutory late-payment interest, and business records may be seized.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are statutory representatives under §§34, 69 AO and are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes caused by intentional breach of tax duties. Criminal conviction triggers personal fines, travel bans, asset attachments and potential creditor recovery through veil-piercing where assets were commingled or the UG was operated as the director's private vehicle.
Transfer pricing deficiency and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Upward adjustment of taxable income under §1 AStG and §8(3) sentence 3 KStG; rejection of claimed deductions under §162(3) AO; mandatory surcharge of 5% of the adjusted income, at least EUR 5,000, under §162(4) AO where documentation is missing or untimely; plus trade tax reassessment, interest, and possible criminal prosecution if the documentation was falsified.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false intercompany invoices or manipulate transfer pricing are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax deficiency. They may be prosecuted as principals or abettors of tax evasion; civil claims by the company's creditors are available, and intentional misrepresentation destroys the limited-liability protection for the director's own assets.
Dividend withholding tax and profit-repatriation non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The UG is made liable under §44(5) EStG and §50d(1) EStG for the unwithheld tax, interest and late-payment surcharges; the recipient remains jointly liable; the tax office may assess the hidden distribution and deny the corresponding corporate deduction for the underlying payment.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under §§34, 69 AO for failure to withhold and pay the tax at source. Criminal tax evasion under §370 AO applies where the omission was intentional; travel bans and attachment orders may be issued in the criminal proceedings, and the director's private assets are directly exposed if he knowingly participated in unlawful profit extraction.
Abusive shell company and treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty and directive benefits under §50d(3) EStG, disregard of the abusive structure under §42 AO, re-attribution of profits to the actual business location, full corporate income tax and trade tax assessment, loss of withholding tax exemption, interest on unpaid taxes, and criminal liability where false declarations were submitted in the withholding tax procedure.

Director personal liability: Directors of an abusive shell cannot rely on the corporate veil for intentional wrongdoing; German courts may pierce the veil for capital inadequacy, commingling of assets, or fraud. The director may face personal civil restitution, asset tracing, travel restrictions, and imprisonment if treaty abuse is accompanied by false statements or attempted evasion of withholding taxes.
AML and beneficial-ownership reporting failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of up to EUR 1.5 million per violation under §56 GWG; criminal prosecution for money laundering under §261 StGB where assets derive from unlawful tax conduct; confiscation of proceeds under §73 StGB; and tax office authority to estimate income under §162 AO where incomplete or false records prevent verification.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for GWG reporting failures and may face imprisonment of up to five years for money laundering; the corporate veil is disregarded for intentional criminal participation, allowing criminal asset forfeiture, travel bans and personal civil damages against directors who knowingly use the UG as a financial conduit for undeclared revenue.
