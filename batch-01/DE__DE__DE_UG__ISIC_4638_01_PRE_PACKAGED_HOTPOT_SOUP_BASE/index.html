Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT IDs and the local tax office (Finanzamt) for income and trade tax administration; official portal: www.bzst.de / www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must complete the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) and submit it to the competent Finanzamt or BZSt, requesting both the regular tax number (Steuernummer) and, if applicable, the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). Non-resident businesses with no German establishment must appoint a fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter) and provide proof of business purpose, anticipated turnover, and German banking details; processing typically takes 4-8 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must complete the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) and submit it to the competent Finanzamt or BZSt, requesting both the regular tax number (Steuernummer) and, if applicable, the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). Non-resident businesses with no German establishment must appoint a fiscal representative (Fiskalvertreter) and provide proof of business purpose, anticipated turnover, and German banking details; processing typically takes 4-8 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not allow US-style check-the-box elections. An UG may elect tax group treatment (Organschaft) for corporate income tax and trade tax if a controlling parent holds more than 50% of voting rights and financial and organisational integration exists. For VAT purposes, an umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft (VAT group) election is available. Hybrid mismatch elections are restricted under ATAD implementation (§ 4k EStG) with no free-standing entity classification option.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for foreign exporters; VAT registration is required for any taxable supply in Germany. A small-entrepreneur exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) exists only for domestic turnover not exceeding EUR 22,000 in 2025, which is generally inapplicable to export distribution.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies physically exported to a non-EU country (or to a third-country customs procedure) qualify for VAT zero-rating under § 6 UStG, provided the goods are transported outside the EU within statutory time limits. Offshore services supplied to non-EU customers may also be zero-rated under § 8 UStG. Pre-packaged hotpot soup base bricks distributed through export must satisfy documentary evidence rules and invoicing requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Germany mandates structured e-invoicing for all domestic B2B supplies. Since 1 January 2025, all sellers must be able to receive conformant electronic invoices (EN 16931). Mandatory transmission obligations are phased in: from 1 January 2026 for enterprises with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2027 for all businesses. Accepted formats include XRechnung and ZUGFeRD. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system akin to Italy's SdI.

The Finanzamt may reverse zero rating if export evidence is missing, incomplete, or fraudulent, thereby assessing VAT at the standard 19% rate plus 6% annual penalty interest (§ 233a AO). Falsifying export documentation for VAT refunds constitutes tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) carrying penalties and imprisonment up to 10 years, plus confiscation of the amounts at issue. Retrospective clawback periods extend up to 10 years in cases of wilful evasion (§ 169 AO), with additional fines and obstruction penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. Germany imposes no general exchange controls, no prior foreign-exchange approval for inbound equity, no restriction on non-resident ownership of a UG, and no limitation on profit remittance, dividends, royalties, or trade settlements to related parties. German corporate taxes, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax apply at entity level; dividends paid to foreign shareholders are subject to German withholding tax at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (reduced under an applicable tax treaty). The main administrative requirement is the Bundesbank's external-trade statistics reporting (AWV) for material cross-border transactions over the relevant reporting threshold, as well as compliance with EU and German sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and declaration duties. There are no convertibility hurdles for USD, CNY, or other freely transferred currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not mandate the surrender of export proceeds to the central bank or any mandatory conversion ratio. A German exporter may keep foreign currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts, or settle in euro, and may freely convert or hedge exposure. Export receipts must be reflected in the exporter's accounting system and, when they exceed the statutory reporting threshold, reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV balance-of-payments statistics regime. There is no requirement to channel export proceeds through a designated German bank, but a German corporate bank relationship is required for an operational treasury and trade-finance account. Export of pre-packaged hotpot soup base is subject to EU food-safety, labelling, and customs-export formalities rather than to foreign-exchange surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the Eurosystem and responsible for balance-of-payments statistics and foreign trade reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV); monetary and foreign-exchange policy competences are shared with the European Central Bank and the German Ministry of Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) are legally usable for holding e-money, executing euro payments, and some limited foreign-exchange conversion. For this UG, the feasibility rating is MEDIUM. An EMI can be a secondary rail for rapid onboarding, multi-currency settlement, and payments to/from Asian suppliers and customers, and it must obtain full KYC/UBO documentation. However, an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, cannot grant overdrafts or trade loans, and funds held are not covered by statutory German deposit insurance. Many established German banks regard payments involving certain digital EMIs as higher AML risk and may debit-reject or freeze transactions. Consequently, an EMI should never be the sole treasury vehicle; it may only serve as a supplementary clearing rail after the UG holds its primary bank account with a marker-safe commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The preferred route is a SEPA or SWIFT MT103 credit to the UG's German IBAN, sent by the non-resident shareholder from a verified bank account in the name of the corporate shareholder. For formation or capital increase, funds should initially be credited to a notary-monitored blocked contribution account (Sonderkonto 2) so that the notary can certify full payment of the issued share capital; after registration or entry into commercial register, the funds are automatically transferred to the operational current account. The payment instruction must state the German purpose text 'Einlage Kapital' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' and a clear reference to the UG, trade-register number, and shareholder. For AWV statistics purposes the bank will require the statutory Bundesbank purpose category; the minimum supporting dossier is a shareholder capital contribution resolution, loan agreement if applicable, notarial certificate, and proof of the ultimate origin of funds to satisfy German AML and foreign-source-of-funds due diligence. For inbound equity from China, the Chinese parent company must have completed its own local outbound investment/filing procedure before remitting; this is a condition under Chinese law, not under German exchange control, but the German receiving bank may ask for the Chinese ODI certificate to evidence lawful source of funds.For export settlements, the UG should offer the following clearing rails: (i) open account based on contractual terms, settled by SWIFT MT103 or, within the euro area, SEPA Credit Transfer; (ii) documentary collection (DP/DA) with payment against shipping documents through the reimbursing bank; (iii) irrevocable commercial letter of credit established by the importer's bank, with SWIFT MT700 advice, negotiated through the UG's German presenting bank, with presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and any food-health certificate required by the destination country; (iv) bank payment obligation (BPO) in supply-chain finance platforms for larger repeated transactions.
