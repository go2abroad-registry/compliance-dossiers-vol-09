Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office; local Finanzamt (tax office) competent for the UG's registered seat; centralized electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); VAT identification number application portal: BZSt (www.bzst.de)

E-government filing portal

A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) must first be registered in the Handelsregister (commercial register), then file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of formation, from which it obtains the statutory Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; for cross-border activity, the entity must separately apply for an EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using Form V01 through ELSTER or BZSt; non-resident directors/shareholders may sign via apostilled power of attorney, and if the UG has no domestic seat or branch, a German tax representative may be required solely for VAT-relevant procedural acts; retention of tax filings, articles of association, and shareholder registers is mandatory under GoBD

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG is a fully taxable German corporate entity (Kapitalgesellschaft), resident in Germany by place of management and registered seat; it is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer at 15%), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag — abolished for most corporate taxpayers from 2025), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer; effective rate approximately 7%–17% depending on municipal Hebesatz), and VAT; there is no pass-through or fiscal transparency treatment for a UG under German law

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a 'check-the-box' pass-through election; the available legal mechanisms for a UG include: corporate income tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§ 14–19 KStG with a profit and loss pooling agreement, trade tax fiscal unity, VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under § 2 (2) Nr. 2 UStG, small business exemption (Kleinunternehmer) under § 19 UStG for domestic turnover not exceeding €22,000 in the preceding year and €50,000 expected in the current year, flat-rate agricultural forestry VAT under § 24 UStG, and for hybrid mismatch scenarios, tax-payer documentation and rebuttal elections under the German ATAD implementation (§ 4k EStG); no elective unilateral hybrid mismatch regime is permitted outside OECD/SEC conflict rules

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small business exemption threshold: €22,000 turnover in the previous year and €50,000 expected turnover in the current year; if exceeded, VAT registration is mandatory; for foreign suppliers of electronic, telecom, and digital services, the EU Seller Scheme OSS thresholds apply (€10,000 intra-EU distance sales aggregate), and where a non-resident makes taxable supplies in Germany exceeding the exemption threshold, VAT registration in Germany is mandatory; a UG engaged in grocery retail cannot use the small business exemption when exporting goods or selling to VAT-registered customers; imports from non-EU countries always trigger import VAT regardless of turnover thresholds

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods by a UG from Germany to non-EU third countries qualify as tax-exempt export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferung) under § 6 UStG, effectively zero-rated with full input VAT deduction, provided the goods physically depart the EU within a maximum three-month period (extendable to one year for justified cases) and the supplier retains documentary proof of export; in-store retail sales to international buyers are not automatically zero-rated; the UG must demonstrate through transport and customs documents that the goods were personally carried out of the EU or shipped by a forwarding agent; certain cross-border services to non-EU entrepreneurs are also free of German VAT under § 3a UStG and § 3b UStG if the recipient is a VAT-registered entrepreneur in a third territory; ordinary shelf sales to tourists remain taxable at 19%/7% unless the ATLAS-verified export trail exists

  • Electronic customs export declaration in ATLAS (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / Export Declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list containing HS codes and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical cross-border departure
  • Proof of export certificate or electronic export verification under § 10 UStG and § 11 UStDV
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank receipt evidencing repatriated export proceeds — while German law does not statutorily require FX remittance certificates, German tax courts treat repatriation evidence as persuasive corroboration of actual export delivery

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; from 1 January 2025, mandatory B2B electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) requires every German business to receive and archive structured e-invoices in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1/2.3 XML formats; the obligation for active transmission to B2B customers is phased — from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover above €800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all businesses; invoicing data must be transmitted through ELSTER/GEFRA-compliant interfaces, retained in compliant GoBD channels, and made available digitally during VAT audits; no blockchain stamping, API fiscal clearing, or fiscal device clearance mandate applies in Germany

Export zero-rating claims are high-risk audit items for the Finanzamt, Hauptzollamt (Main Customs Office), and BZSt; missing or inconsistent export documents trigger retroactive disallowance of the VAT exemption with reassessment of 19% VAT, plus default interest at 6% per annum from the original tax liability date; deliberate or reckless misrepresentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by fines or imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases; administrative penalty orders (Ordnungswidrigkeit) of up to €25,000 may be issued for procedural violations; the general assessment statute of limitation is five years but extends to ten years for evasion (§ 169 AO); because the import side is controlled by EU customs law, unfounded zero-rating can also trigger summary seizure of goods and customs smuggling charges under § 372 AO

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro convertibility is assured: Germany maintains no foreign-exchange control law, no capital-control approval, and no profit-remittance permit requirement. Both the current account (payment for imported Asian condiments, vegetables, freight, insurance and consultancy) and the capital account (equity injections, shareholder loans, disposal proceeds, dividend distributions and branch financing) are liberalized under Articles 63-65 TFEU and the AWV. A UG may remit dividends or repay shareholder loans to non-resident owners without central bank permission or currency allocation. The only genuine hurdles are tax-formal and documentary: a notarized shareholders' resolution must evidence distributable HGB book profit; a 26.375% German withholding tax (Abgeltungsteuer 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) generally applies to dividends paid to non-resident shareholders unless reduced under an applicable Germany-Asia double-tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; and any outward remittance exceeding EUR 25,000 must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German and EU law impose a 0% mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeline for surrender or repatriation of export proceeds; an exporter or importer may retain, convert, invest or spend foreign currency freely. The only time-bound statutory duty is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank: every cross-border payment received from or made to a non-resident in excess of EUR 25,000, or an aggregate series of related payments exceeding that threshold, must be submitted electronically through the Bundesbank 'International Payments / Zahlungsbilanzstatistik' portal no later than the fifth working day of the month following the payment month, using the official AWV payment-purpose code catalogue. For the Asian supermarket grocery model, supplier settlement wires should be coded under the goods-import category and any foreign shareholder capital tap under the capital-participation or shareholder-loan category.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, acting within the European System of Central Banks, with monetary issuance authority over the euro; foreign-trade and capital-movement oversight under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); AML/KYC supervision of banks and payment institutions by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); export-control sanctions screening co-supervised by the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) and cross-border statistical reporting administered solely by the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

MEDIUM feasibility, strictly as a supplementary rail. An EEA-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) passported into Germany under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may lawfully hold safeguarded client funds and execute multi-currency payments, but it cannot grant credit or overdrafts, may not pay interest on float, and must disclose its EMI status on all statements. An offshore non-EEA EMI cannot offer a German-credible IBAN, will trigger correspondent-bank friction, and is unsuitable for merchant acquiring, Handelsregister escrow-deposit certificates or Bundesbank statistical reporting. Wise, Airwallex and comparable EEA-passported EMIs may be deployed only as sub-ledger disbursement and low-cost FX conversion utilities; the primary treasury spine, capital escrow, and commercial-register deposit confirmation must remain with a fully licensed German credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder capital injection: remit via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's foreign bank to the UG's operational or escrow account at Commerzbank, Deutsche Bank or the chosen Landesbank, denominated in EUR to preserve clean statutory-capital book value and avoid FX uncertainty in the notarized capital-confirmation certificate. The wire's beneficiary line must show the UG's full commercial-register name and registered-office address; the remitter line must show the shareholder's legal name and IBAN/account number; the purpose-code field must carry the official Bundesbank AWV payment-purpose code for capital participation or shareholder loans, referencing the appropriate shareholders' resolution date. The receiving German bank will perform travel-rule data verification under Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, will screen the remitter against sanctions lists, and will automatically forward the statistical payment report to the Bundesbank when the payment exceeds EUR 25,000. The UG must contemporaneously preserve the notarized capital-contribution ledger extract, the bank's deposit confirmation (Gründerbestätigung), the Transparenzregister excerpt and the shareholder's source-of-funds bundle to satisfy any subsequent enhanced due-diligence review.Primary settlement rails: SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking for recurring supplier invoices under open-account terms; documentary commercial letters of credit issued as SWIFT MT700 through the UG's trade-finance desk and advised or confirmed through reputable Asian financial institutions for large sea-freight purchases; MT202 COV for correspondent-account settlement of LC proceeds; TARGET2 and SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated intra-EU supplier and logistics payments. Every outbound transaction must be tied to a UCP 600-compliant documentary package (commercial invoice, transport document, packing list, certificate of origin and, where applicable, phytosanitary certificate), and treasury must pre-register the euro-funding and FX conversion legs before each settlement date so that payments settle value-dated and no unhedged overnight currency exposure is accumulated.Bilateral deliverable outright FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH, EUR/JPY) concluded under the Deutsche Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte (DRV) or an ISDA master agreement with the house bankEUR/USD and EUR/CNH FX swaps combining a spot transaction with a matching forward reversal, used to finance import working-capital windows without drawing overdraft facilitiesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) cash-settled in EUR for INR, IDR, VND, PHP, TWD and KRW exposures, applicable when the vendor's onshore currency is non-internationalized and deliverable hedging is prohibited or impracticalEuropean vanilla FX options (EUR call / Asian-currency put and EUR put / Asian-currency call) to stabilize contingencies in perishable-grocery letter-of-credit pricing without requiring a firm future commitmentRolling settlement-day forward renewals executed on the bank's e-FX dealing platform, with each contract contemporaneously designated against a specific supplier invoice under a documented internal treasury hedging policy (non-speculative HGB net-open-position discipline)

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: MEDIUM feasibility, strictly as a supplementary rail. An EEA-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) passported into Germany under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may lawfully hold safeguarded client funds and execute multi-currency payments, but it cannot grant credit or overdrafts, may not pay interest on float, and must disclose its EMI status on all statements. An offshore non-EEA EMI cannot offer a German-credible IBAN, will trigger correspondent-bank friction, and is unsuitable for merchant acquiring, Handelsregister escrow-deposit certificates or Bundesbank statistical reporting. Wise, Airwallex and comparable EEA-passported EMIs may be deployed only as sub-ledger disbursement and low-cost FX conversion utilities; the primary treasury spine, capital escrow, and commercial-register deposit confirmation must remain with a fully licensed German credit institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for cross-border intercompany transactions exceeding EUR 250,000 per year (per transaction type); Master File required for group annual consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 100,000,000; CbC Report required for groups with consolidated revenue over EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate Enterprise Substance Rules, but the general anti-abuse rule under § 42 AO and § 1 AStG (arm's-length adjustment) require the UG to prove genuine management, physical store front, employee payroll, local inventory titles, and independent decision-making. A UG stripped of economic substance - using only a letterbox address, virtual office, and non-resident incorporated board - will be treated as a sham, with deemed constructive dividend and reallocation of profit under the sovereignty doctrine

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Revenue Understatement by Cash Sales

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO imprisonment up to 10 years or a fine up to €50,000; criminal surcharge of up to 10% of evaded tax; retrospective revocation of trade licence under § 35 GewO

Director personal liability: Geschäftsführer held personally liable for unreported VAT and CIT; civil restitution claim by tax office against personal assets; travel ban under § 132 StPo if flight risk exists; imprisonment for gross negligence or organised underestimation of income
Shell Company / Sham Establishment

Statutory sanction: Disregard of legal entity under § 42 AO; retroactive profit attribution to non-resident shareholder; 10-year limitation period for tax assessment; WHT recalculation on deemed dividend distributions; trade licence cancellation

Director personal liability: Director may be personally assessed for the full tax debt under § 34 AO (duty of compliance) if corporate assets are insufficient; disqualification from holding management office for 5 years; civil liability to creditors for improper insolvency
AML / Money Laundering via Retail Cash Structuring

Statutory sanction: FCA (FIU) administrative fine up to €100,000 per infringement under GwG; criminal money-laundering liability under § 261 StGB with imprisonment up to 5 years or fine; surrender of cash proceeds

Director personal liability: Director personally liable for non-compliance with AML record-keeping under § 6 GwG; travel ban and passport seizure; personal bankruptcy risk due to outstanding tax and laundering fines
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under § 1 AStG, adjustment of taxable income to arm's-length price; retroactive documentation penalty of €25,000 to €1,000,000 if contemporaneous records are unavailable; punitive interest of 0.5% per month; DTT mutual agreement procedure not available if gross negligence

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for additional corporate tax and trade tax adjustments if they knowingly approved the non-arm's-length payments; prohibition of future management; imprisonment up to 5 years if intent to evade is established
