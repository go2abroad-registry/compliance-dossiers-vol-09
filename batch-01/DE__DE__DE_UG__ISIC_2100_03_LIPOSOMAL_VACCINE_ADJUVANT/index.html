Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for the UG's registered seat is competent for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessments, operating through the ELSTER e-filing portal (www.elster.de). The Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) in Saarlouis is the central competent authority for issuing the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) via the BZSt Online Portal (BOP). The Generalzolldirektion (German Customs) administers the ATLAS/ECS customs export declaration system. Tax identification numbers: Steuernummer (local statutory TIN) and USt-IdNr. (EU-wide VAT ID).

E-government filing portal

A UG must be notarized and entered into the Handelsregister (Commercial Register); upon registration, the UG files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (statutory TIN) and determines whether the entity qualifies as an Unternehmer; the USt-IdNr. is obtained separately through the BZSt Online Portal once intra-EU supplies/acquisitions occur or turnover exceeds EUR 50,000. Non-resident foreign entities performing taxable supplies in Germany have no de minimis threshold, must file the same questionnaire (or the simplified JAF form) with the Finanzamt for their German permanent establishment or directly with the BZSt, and register for VAT before supplying goods or services in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election. Available mechanisms: (i) Organschaft (corporate fiscal unity): a controlling German parent may consolidate the UG's corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT with a valid profit-and-loss pooling agreement for a minimum five-year term; (ii) umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft (VAT fiscal unity) with financial, economic, and organizational integration; (iii) the small-business turnover exemption under Sec. 19 UStG (EUR 25,000 prior-year / EUR 100,000 current-year thresholds) allowing merchants to waive VAT output; (iv) trade tax participation exemption under Sec. 9 GewStG for dividend income; (v) no hybrid mismatch elective regimes under ATAD — mandatory defensive adjustments under Sec. 4k EStG and Sec. 14b KStG apply compulsorily; (vi) foreign entities may elect OSS (One-Stop-Shop) pan-EU VAT registration for distance sales instead of multiple national registrations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover triggers mandatory VAT registration for established entrepreneurs (small-business exemption threshold under Sec. 19 UStG, effective 1 January 2025; previously EUR 22,000). A UG engaged in commercial activity must register at first taxable supply. For non-resident foreign suppliers with no German seat or permanent establishment, there is no de minimis threshold: any taxable supply in Germany triggers immediate VAT registration through the JAF/OSS channels.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sec. 4 No. 1(a) UStG in conjunction with Sec. 6 UStG: supplies of goods (including mRNA liposomal vaccine adjuvants and lipid nanoparticle components) by the UG qualify for a 0% VAT rate when the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Germany into a third-country territory (outside the EU). Eligibility requires that the UG is an Unternehmer, the goods leave the EU customs territory, and the entrepreneur (recipient or the UG itself) instigates the dispatch/transport. The export must be covered by a valid customs export declaration (ATLAS) and proof of export under Sec. 9 UStDV. Dual-use or export-controlled vaccines/adjuvants require the relevant authorization; services directly connected with exported goods (freight, insurance, handling) also qualify under Sec. 4 No. 3 UStG.

  • ATLAS Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Export Accompanying Document (EAD) stamped by German Customs or electronic exit record
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CIM consignment note evidencing cross-border transport
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms and export references
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT confirmation showing receipt of foreign currency consideration)
  • Proof of Export per Sec. 9 UStDV (export confirmation from the logistics provider or customs office)

From 1 January 2025, all German-established entrepreneurs must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) in an EU EN 16931-compliant format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x with embedded XML) for all domestic B2B supplies. Mandatory issuance phases: from 1 January 2027, suppliers with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices for all transactions; from 1 January 2028, all entrepreneurs must do so. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance server (unlike Italy's SDI or Romania's e-Factura); however, the e-invoice must carry a full XRechnung/ZUGFeRD metadata set, be transmitted electronically, and be retained in original structured form for audit purposes. Invoices for export supplies may be paper or electronic under Sec. 14 UStG but must contain all mandatory particulars. Cross-border distance sales of goods to EU consumers are declared via the OSS portal administered by the BZSt; the ViDA optional reverse-charge rules will apply from 2026.

The Finanzamt may conduct an audit (Betriebsprüfung) retroactively for up to seven years (ten years in cases of VAT evasion) and claw back the 0% rate, assessing 19% VAT on the invoice value, if the UG fails to produce valid export proof under Sec. 9 UStDV, goods are diverted into the German/EU market, or documentation is falsified. Clawback mechanics include: default interest (Nachzahlungszinsen) of 0.5% per month under Sec. 233a AO, late-payment surcharges up to EUR 250 per month under Sec. 240 AO, and penalty charges under Sec. 378/379 AO. Fraudulent zero-rating, paper-company exports, or invoicing abuse triggers criminal tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO — imprisonment up to ten years or fines up to EUR 200,000 per case, seizure of assets by the Zollfahndungsamt, and personal civil liability of the UG's managing directors under Sec. 69 AO and Sec. 34/35 GmbHG. Failure to substantiate FX repatriation within 180 days will be treated as an indicator of diverted export proceeds, potentially leading to re-characterization of the supply as taxable domestic delivery and inclusion of the transaction in money-laundering investigations if the payment rails are opaque.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the EU Treaty and §01 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV). There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no prior approval for capital movements. Profit remittance, dividends, and royalty distributions are unrestricted; the only formalities are statistical reporting thresholds under §11 AWV to the Bundesbank (e.g., cross-border payments above €12,500 in principle), and withholding tax obligations (e.g., §44a EStG – Abgeltungsteuer/withholding tax) applicable to income distributions to foreign shareholders. No capital controls or balance-of-payments restrictions apply to the inbound or outbound flows for this UG.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Germany. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, freely used, or credited to any EU or non-EU account without conversion obligation. Only statistical reporting to the Bundesbank (AWV Meldungen) applies for cross-border transactions/balances exceeding €12,500 or for received payments from non-residents above the threshold, and such reporting is informational, not consent-based. No conversion to EUR is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Bundesbank, Central Banking and Financial Supervision; supervisory authority for banking and payment institutions: Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht – BaFin)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with significant legal constraints. Foreign fintechs authorized as electronic money institutions (EMIs) under EU PSD2 can passport into Germany via the European single passport, but the operation of a high-risk life sciences/pharmaceutical manufacturing entity triggers heightened AML scrutiny, requiring adequate substance documentation, transaction monitoring, and often domiciliation of the corporate account within Germany. For payment services, only ECB/BaFin-authorized EMIs may operate lawfully; non-EU EMIs without a German branch are not viable. Online-only EMIs offer faster onboarding but may decline due to the ISIC 2100 high-risk classification requiring special license validation; the UG's annual accounts, beneficial ownership registry (Transparenzregister) entry, and proof of trade license are indispensable. For regular treasury operations, a tier-1 German bank account is preferable; fintechs are viable only for subsidiary transactional flows and only with a local authorized entity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) 15%; solidarity surcharge 5.5% on CIT = 15.825%; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) municipal multiplier typically 400%, effective trade tax approx. 14%, combined headline burden approx. 30%-33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory if related-party transactions exceed EUR 6,000,000 per fiscal year per category; Master File mandatory if consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country Report if consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be prepared before the tax return deadline and submitted within 60 days upon request; retention period 10 years.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies the general anti-abuse rule §42 AO, substance-over-form, and EU ATAD. No standalone Economic Substance Regulations as in offshore jurisdictions; however, license-based benefits and royalty deductions require adequate substance: local office, qualified personnel, decision-making, and risk control. Under §4j EStG and §8 Nr. 1 KStG, royalty deductions may be denied if IP is transferred to a low-taxed affiliated entity without substance. For mRNA lipid nanoparticle manufacturing, regulatory license, GMP facility, and local technical personnel are critical substance factors.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine, imprisonment up to 10 years under §370 AO and §266 StGB; assessment of back taxes, 0.5% monthly interest, and penalty surcharges up to 10%.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §§69, 71 AO for unpaid taxes; criminal prosecution, travel restrictions, and imprisonment; disqualification from management under §6 GmbHG.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax office may estimate income under §162 AO; penalty up to 10% of income adjustment; surcharge up to EUR 1,000,000 for failure to submit TP documentation; retroactive adjustments and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for resulting tax debts under §69 AO; potential criminal tax evasion charges; personal civil liability for damage to company.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax office may disregard entity for tax purposes, attribute income to beneficial owner, deny treaty benefits, and impose back taxes plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes, face criminal charges for tax evasion, and be subject to insolvency proceedings; travel bans and arrest warrants for flight risk.
AML / High-Risk Special License Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 5% of annual turnover under GwG; license revocation; criminal liability for money laundering under §261 StGB; asset freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches, imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering, professional bans, and personal fines.
Unlicensed Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under AMG up to 3 years imprisonment or fines; product seizure, factory closure, and civil liability for patient harm.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for bodily injury and criminal offenses; imprisonment, professional disqualification, and personal restitution.
Withholding Tax Fraud / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, back assessment of 26.375% WHT plus interest, fines up to EUR 5,000,000 in serious cases, and criminal tax evasion prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors liable under §69 AO; criminal liability for tax evasion and forgery; extradition possible under international mutual legal assistance.
VAT Fraud / Carousel

Statutory sanction: Back payment of VAT plus 0.5% monthly interest; penalty up to 10% of assessed tax; imprisonment up to 10 years for organized VAT fraud.

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable for VAT debts under §69 AO; criminal prosecution and travel restrictions.
Insolvency / Delayed Bankruptcy

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under §15a InsO up to 3 years imprisonment; personal liability for payments after insolvency maturity under §64 GmbHG.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for all payments made after insolvency maturity; imprisonment, travel ban, and personal bankruptcy.
Bribery / Corruption

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under §334 StGB; fines up to EUR 10,000,000; exclusion from public tenders; asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable, imprisonment, disqualification from management, and civil restitution.
Subsidy Fraud / Misuse of R&D Allowance

Statutory sanction: Repayment of allowance plus penalty; criminal fine or imprisonment up to 5 years for subsidy fraud under §264 StGB.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for repayment and criminal penalties; professional disqualification.
