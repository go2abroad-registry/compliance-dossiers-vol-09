Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A UG may lawfully use a virtual office or business-center address as its registered address provided the address is a physical location in Germany, accessible for service of process during ordinary business hours, and not a mere post-office box. The registered address must correspond to a place where the company’s administration and communication are effectively maintained. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers; the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers tax registration and VAT returns. ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) is the official electronic tax portal.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must register with the competent Finanzamt, generally by electronically submitting the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The entity may apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.), generally through the Finanzamt or the BZSt application process. A foreign entity making taxable German supplies must register with the competent German Finanzamt; it generally cannot rely on the domestic small-business turnover threshold for supplies for which it is liable to German VAT. Registration documents and any required translations or authentication depend on the entity's place of establishment and circumstances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a separate corporation subject to German corporate income tax and trade tax; it is not eligible for a US-style check-the-box election to be treated as a pass-through entity. German tax classification follows legal form and applicable statutory rules. No general election allows the UG to opt out of entity-level taxation. Cross-border hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences in specified arrangements, but are not an elective entity-classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a German-established UG making taxable supplies in Germany. A non-established business making taxable German supplies generally must register from the first such supply unless a specific reverse-charge or other statutory exception applies. The German small-business scheme has thresholds of €25,000 prior-year turnover and €100,000 current-year turnover under rules effective from 2025, but eligibility and application depend on the supplier's establishment and statutory conditions; it does not generally eliminate a foreign supplier's registration obligation. Qualifying food products may be subject to the 7% reduced rate, depending on product classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying goods exported from Germany to a destination outside the EU may be VAT-exempt with input-tax deduction as an export delivery under German VAT law, provided the statutory conditions are met and the supplier retains prescribed evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate exemption requirements and are not exports to third countries. Services are not automatically zero-rated because a customer or payment is offshore; the place-of-supply rules, customer status, service type, and any applicable exemption or reverse-charge rule determine treatment.

  • Commercial invoice and sales contract or order identifying the goods, buyer, and transaction
  • Export customs declaration and evidence of release for export, including the relevant customs export reference or electronic exit confirmation where applicable
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route and Incoterms, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier confirmation
  • Supplier records linking the invoice and goods to the customs and transport evidence
  • No general inward-remittance certificate or foreign-exchange repatriation certificate is required under German VAT export rules

Germany has no general real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital tax-stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for relevant domestic B2B transactions. Issuing obligations are being phased in: transitional rules permit specified businesses to issue other invoice formats through 2026, with an extended transition for businesses meeting the statutory prior-year turnover condition through 2027. Structured invoices must meet applicable EU/German requirements; transmission through a tax-authority clearance API is not generally required. Separate rules apply to public-sector e-invoicing and excluded transaction categories.

Germany imposes no general statutory deadline for repatriating export proceeds as a condition of export VAT exemption. The supplier must substantiate the export and satisfy invoicing and record-keeping requirements. Unsupported exemption may be denied, resulting in VAT assessment, interest, and potentially correction of prior returns. Intentional or reckless tax evasion may attract criminal or administrative penalties under the German Fiscal Code; inaccurate VAT returns may also trigger late-payment consequences. The statutory limitation period may be extended in cases of tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full Euro convertibility for current account transactions and substantially unrestricted capital account movements. There are no exchange controls or prior approval requirements for profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital redemptions; however, statistical reporting duties apply under AWV Section 67 for cross-border payments, and banking counterparties will require tax residence certificates and documentary proof of underlying trade to satisfy anti-money-laundering due diligence. For non-EU shareholders, the Foreign Tax Act Section 11 may restrict certain royalty/interest deduction if the recipient is not entitled to full treaty benefits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or statutory timeline for export proceeds held in foreign currency. Export receipts may be retained in a foreign account or brought into Germany freely. Under AWV Section 67, any payment received from a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500, including export proceeds, must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the reporting entity within five banking days of the month in which the receivable arises. There is no minimum conversion ratio for EUR or any other currency.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank) and German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) / Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. Electronic money institutions and digital banks licensed under EU PSD2 can support EUR SEPA transfers, basic multi-currency balances, and SWIFT MT103 execution with lower setup costs and remote onboarding. However, they generally cannot provide documentary credit facilities, commercial LC issuance/confirmation, or committed treasury lines. Under German KWG, EMIs may not take deposits, meaning transaction-generated balances are not interest-bearing and not protected by statutory deposit insurance. Fintechs are viable for small operational payments but inadequate as the primary clearing rail for high-value trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For EU/EEA shareholders, use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer with reference 'Capital Contribution – Stammeinlage/Reserveansammlung'. For non-EU inbound flows, use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'CNCB' or 'SCAP' depending on whether it represents equity injection or capital reserve. The bank requires the UG´s IBAN in the beneficiary field, and the remittance must be accompanied by a notarised capital increase resolution, a contribution accounting report, and the latest UBO declaration. Under AWV, inbound remittances exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank by the German resident entity. No foreign exchange approval is needed, but banking reconciliation should separate investment capital from trade advances to avoid unintended tax or thin-capitalisation treatment.Preferred EU disbursement is SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) with purpose codes such as 'PURT' or 'GDDS' for goods.
