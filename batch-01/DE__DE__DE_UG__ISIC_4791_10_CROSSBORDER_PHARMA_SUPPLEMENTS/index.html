Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal tax administration is led by the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF). Tax registration and assessment are administered by the competent local tax office (Finanzamt); VAT identification numbers are issued by the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt). The principal electronic filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

After incorporation, the UG must submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt to obtain a tax number and register for applicable taxes, including VAT. It should separately apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) if required for intra-EU transactions. A foreign-established entity with German taxable activities must register with the Finanzamt assigned to its place of business or, if it has no German establishment, the Finanzamt designated for foreign taxpayers; it provides formation and ownership documents, business activity and address information, expected turnover, bank details, and evidence of taxable transactions. The Finanzamt may request certified translations or additional documents. A German UG ordinarily uses its German tax number and, where relevant, its USt-IdNr.; tax numbers and VAT IDs are distinct identifiers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A German UG is a corporation and is generally subject to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), the solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). It is not eligible to elect partnership or pass-through classification under a US-style check-the-box regime. German tax law has no general entity election that converts a UG into a transparent entity. Hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or require income inclusion in specified cross-border arrangements; they are statutory rules, not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a German-established UG from VAT registration for taxable business activities. The German small-business regime (Kleinunternehmerregelung) is a VAT exemption, not a general registration safe harbor: from 2025 it generally applies where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions and loss of the exemption when the current-year limit is exceeded. A non-established supplier making taxable German supplies generally has no domestic registration threshold. For qualifying intra-Community distance sales of goods and certain cross-border electronic services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply under the applicable conditions; the Union OSS can be used for eligible destination-country VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from the EU to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with the right to deduct input VAT (effectively zero-rated) if the statutory export-supply conditions are met and the supplier retains prescribed evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. The supplier must establish the required link between the supply and export and satisfy applicable invoicing and record-keeping rules. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine whether German VAT applies, and exemptions or other treatments must be assessed separately. EU cross-border B2C distance sales are generally taxed at destination, subject to applicable rules and OSS. Dietary supplements require separate product-compliance review; their VAT rate depends on the product's legal and factual classification and is not necessarily the standard rate.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, consideration, and applicable VAT treatment
  • Electronic export declaration and customs export reference (MRN), with customs-certified exit result or equivalent official evidence of exit from the EU
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the transaction, such as bill of lading, air waybill, courier tracking and delivery records, or road transport documentation
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and proof of payment where available, to substantiate the transaction and its connection to the exported goods

Germany has no general real-time VAT invoice clearance or digital-stamping system. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Issuance is being phased in: transitional rules generally permit paper invoices, or electronic invoices with recipient consent, through 2026, with specified extensions for some issuers; from 2028, structured e-invoices are generally required for domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory exceptions. A qualifying e-invoice must use a structured electronic format compliant with applicable standards (including EN 16931-compatible formats). E-invoicing rules do not replace VAT return, recapitulative statement, or record-keeping obligations. No general API pre-clearance is required.

Germany imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds, and an inward remittance certificate is not a general condition for export VAT exemption. If export evidence or other statutory conditions are insufficient, the Finanzamt may deny the exemption, assess output VAT retrospectively, charge statutory interest, and impose late-payment surcharges. Intentional or reckless VAT evasion may trigger tax-criminal or administrative proceedings, fines, and, for tax evasion, imprisonment under the German Fiscal Code. Input VAT refunds may be reduced or reclaimed where supporting evidence is deficient or the underlying transaction is false.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible currency under EU law, particularly the free movement of capital principle in Article 63 TFEU. There are no exchange control restrictions on current account transactions, capital account transactions, or profit remittances for compliant corporate entities. Foreign investors may inject capital and repatriate distributions without prior central bank approval, provided the underlying transaction is not subject to sanctions, anti-money laundering, tax, or foreign direct investment screening requirements. Dividend payments and loans to non-residents must be formalized, reported to German tax authorities as required, and routed through a valid German bank account; the Deutsche Bundesbank must be notified of individual cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 for statistical balance-of-payments purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

German law imposes no mandatory surrender or minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. Under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV), export receipts may be retained in any foreign currency, held in non-resident accounts, or used directly for cross-border settlement. The only statutory duty is statistical reporting: payments received from abroad exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the AWV reporting forms (Z1/Z4), generally by the 7th working day of the month following the month in which the payment is credited. There is no statutory timeline for converting export proceeds into EUR, and no obligation to convert into EUR or any prescribed currency.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank); foreign exchange statistical reporting and payment monitoring are administered by the Deutsche Bundesbank, while the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) is the competent authority for banking, fintech, and payment licensing under the German Banking Act (KWG) and the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditional feasibility; Medium overall viability. A European Economic Area (EEA) electronic money institution (EMI) may legally operate in Germany under the PSD2 passporting regime from its home-state regulator, but it cannot provide commercial letters of credit or traditional trade finance without a banking licence. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI is generally not viable as a primary treasury rail because it would require separate BaFin authorisation or a licensed sponsorship and is often restricted by card scheme rules for high-risk nutraceutical merchants. EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or other EEA-passported providers can be used for settlement/disbursement in EUR and certain foreign currencies, but they should be complemented by a tier-1 German commercial bank for guarded capital flows, FX, and LC operations. For this high-risk cross-border pharmaceutical supplements classification, many EMIs will reject or restrict the merchant category, so full underwriting viability remains low unless the entity obtains an e-commerce/pharma licence and provides complete supply-chain evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity contributions to the German UG must be paid to a German bank account in EUR via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer. The wire purpose must expressly identify the transaction as a capital contribution (Stammeinlage/Kapitalerhöhung) with the reference 'share capital contribution' and, where applicable, the company's HRB number. The bank will require a notarized shareholders' resolution, the capital contribution agreement, and a copy of the Handelsregister excerpt. For inbound payments above EUR 12,500, the receiving German bank will report the transaction to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV system, including the payment purpose code for cross-border capital transfers. For a UG, the legally prescribed minimum capital of EUR 1 is acceptable, but the bank's onboarding policy may require a higher substantive capital deposit or a shareholder loan agreement to address operational-risk concerns. All capital funds must be received from a bank account registered to the shareholder and cannot be routed through anonymized digital wallets or third-party intermediaries without producing detailed source-of-funds documentation.Outbound trade payments for cross-border supplements supply are executed through standard correspondent banking rails: SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary payments to foreign suppliers, SWIFT MT700/MT707 for documentary letters of credit, and SEPA Credit Transfer for EU/EEA settlements. For high-frequency disbursements to suppliers, EBICS-based bank interfaces or API payment files may be used to transmit batch transactions, with each payment carrying full beneficiary name, address, IBAN/BIC, invoice reference, customs commodity code (HS/CN), and AWV statistical purpose code. The bank will require supporting trade documents for any payment where the transaction appears inconsistent with a company's business profile; outbound payments exceeding EUR 12,500 are subject to monthly AWV reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank.
