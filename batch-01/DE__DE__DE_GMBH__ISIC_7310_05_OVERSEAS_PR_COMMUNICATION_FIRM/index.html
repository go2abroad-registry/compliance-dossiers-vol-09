Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT-ID and federal tax matters; municipal/local tax offices (Finanzämter) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and value-added tax administration; electronic filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de).

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign-owned German GmbH: (1) file the statutory tax-registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER; (2) obtain the local tax number (Steuernummer) for income tax, corporate tax and trade tax; (3) separately apply to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using official BZSt forms; (4) register for intra-Community statistical reporting (Intrastat) if applicable; (5) submit periodic VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) and annual VAT declaration electronically; foreign parent entities require a fiscal representative only in exceptional non-EEA cases, but a German GmbH acts as its own taxable person.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a separate legal person and a resident taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT; no pass-through eligibility for the GmbH as such. — For a foreign-owned German GmbH: (1) file the statutory tax-registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') with the competent local Finanzamt via ELSTER; (2) obtain the local tax number (Steuernummer) for income tax, corporate tax and trade tax; (3) separately apply to BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using official BZSt forms; (4) register for intra-Community statistical reporting (Intrastat) if applicable; (5) submit periodic VAT returns (monthly/quarterly) and annual VAT declaration electronically; foreign parent entities require a fiscal representative only in exceptional non-EEA cases, but a German GmbH acts as its own taxable person.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — the GmbH is a separate legal person and a resident taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT; no pass-through eligibility for the GmbH as such.

        
Available election forms
Germany does not recognise a general 'check-the-box' election comparable to US Subchapter K. Available elections include: (i) VAT consolidation (Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft) to treat a financially, economically and organisationally integrated subsidiary as one taxable person; (ii) corporate income tax group (Organschaft) for profit and loss pooling; (iii) hybrid mismatch elections under § 4k EStG (ATAD) to realign deduction/non-deduction treatment; (iv) trade tax profit allocation elections; and (v) limited option for real-estate VAT (§ 9 UStG) to make taxable supplies where otherwise exempt. No US-style check-the-box exists.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
For cross-border B2B services received in Germany by a non-established seller, no general turnover threshold applies (reverse-charge mechanism; registration obligation only in specific cases). For distance sales of goods into Germany the intra-Community distance-selling threshold is EUR 10,000 (increased to EUR 10,000 from 2021). For small German entrepreneurs, the § 19 UStG small-business exemption applies where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and the current-year projected turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000; exceeding either threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration. A foreign GmbH is generally taxable on all supplies in Germany without a de-minimis threshold.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods delivered to a non-EU destination qualify for exemption with zero-rating under § 4(1a) UStG if the goods have physically left the European Union and the supplier possesses proof of export (customs declaration and transport documents). Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers are exempt under § 4(1b) UStG, provided the purchaser holds a valid VAT ID and the supplier files an EC Sales List. Cross-border B2B services (including international public relations, media, and crisis communications) are not subject to German VAT where the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside Germany and the service is not land-related, tainting, or performed by physically present personnel; the place of supply is the recipient's jurisdiction under the general B2B rule, with the recipient responsible for reverse-charge in its own country. Input VAT incurred for the German GmbH's export-oriented / foreign-revenue operations remains fully deductible, but only if the zero-rating/exemption evidence requirements are satisfied.

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Since 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B invoices must be issued in a structured electronic format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) capable of machine-readable processing; invoices must be transmitted using compliant EDI or API-based channels. There is no central government clearance API for invoice approval, but VAT returns and annual declarations must be filed electronically via ELSTER using authenticated SOAP/REST APIs. The GoBD (Grundsätze zur ordnungsmäßigen Führung und Aufbewahrung von Büchern) and § 146a AO mandate digital bookkeeping, preservation of source documents, and electronic audit access for tax authorities.

      
Fraudulent or substantively defective zero-rating claims attract retrospective VAT assessment by the Finanzamt, denial of input VAT deduction, interest under § 233a AO at 0.5% per month, late-payment penalties under § 152 AO, and, in the case of intentional evasion or misrepresentation, criminal liability under § 370 AO with imprisonment up to 5 years and monetary fines; administrative fines under § 382 AO (customs violation) may apply where export documentation is incomplete or forged. Retroactive clawback covers the full original VAT amount plus accrued interest and penalties, without limitation period extension except in cases of tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Germany maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility of the euro for both current-account and capital-account transactions by virtue of Eurosystem membership and Article 63 TFEU on free movement of capital. No exchange-control authorization, licensing, or central-bank prior approval is required for trade and service payments, dividend remittances, interest, royalties, or shareholder-loan repayments. Profit repatriation is unrestricted as to amount and timing, subject only to German withholding tax on outbound dividends (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), which is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU parents holding at least 10% for at least 12 months, or to reduced treaty rates under Germany's double-taxation conventions. Practical frictions are limited to EU/UN sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering due diligence under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG), and mandatory Deutsche Bundesbank statistical reporting for any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. The German foreign-exchange regime imposes no surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeframe, or minimum retention ratio for export proceeds or service-export receipts. The GmbH may freely invoice in EUR or any foreign currency and retain receipts in offshore or domestic accounts. The only statutory compliance duty is the balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligation under Sections 67-69 AWV: all cross-border payments received from non-residents or made to non-residents must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank when the individual amount or the cumulative transaction series exceeds EUR 12,500, with the report filed electronically within seven calendar days of payment through the bank's reporting system or the Bundesbank's 'Allgemeines Meldeportal', using the applicable payment code for 'external services' or 'capital contribution'

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), operating in statutory cooperation with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), with prudential supervision of banks and financial intermediaries exercised by BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant wire-transfer protocol: equity contributions must be remitted in EUR via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer to the GmbH's dedicated Stammeinlage sub-account at a German full-license bank. The payment purpose (Verwendungszweck) must strictly read 'Einlage in das gezeichnete Kapital der [Name] GmbH' so that the unrestricted availability requirement under Section 8(2) GmbHG is satisfied, thereby enabling the managing directors to provide the statutory declaration on the capital contribution to the commercial register (Handelsregister). If the remittance originates from a non-resident payer and exceeds EUR 12,500, the receiving bank generates the AWV balance-of-payments statistical filing; the GmbH must verify that the Deutsche Bundesbank report uses the transaction type 'Einlage' (capital contribution). For shareholder loans (Gesellschafterdarlehen), the payment purpose must read 'Gesellschafterdarlehen gemäß Darlehensvertrag vom [date]', and the file must contain a signed and dated loan agreement, an interest-rate schedule, repayment milestones, and, where the loan is provided from abroad, subordination or intercreditor documentation to prevent tax reclassification as hidden equity under Section 42 AO and the debt-equity thin-cap rules. For capital injections exceeding material thresholds, a deposit-confirmation certificate (Einlagebestätigung) from the bank may be required by the notary and the commercial registerPreferred clearing rails for a cross-border PR and communications firm: SEPA Instant or SEPA standard credit transfer for EUR-denominated settlements within the European Union; SWIFT MT103 (international wire) for non-EUR supplier, subcontractor, and media-agency payments in USD, GBP, CHF, or APAC currencies; commercial letters of credit are generally not standard for advisory services and should be replaced by bank-payment undertakings or bank-verified IBAN/account confirmations where buyers request documentary security. Every outward payment must carry a purpose code aligned with the AWV: 'Remuneration for PR/consulting services under Contract No. X, Invoice Y' for service disbursements, 'Pass-through media buying under Agency Agreement No. Z' where the GmbH acts as an intermediary, and 'Dividend distribution for the financial year [FY]' for profit repatriation, the latter requiring approved annual financial statements, a managing-director distribution resolution, and the correct Kapitalertragsteuer withholding treatment with applicable treaty or EU-Directive relief, including the 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge where no relief is available. Outbound payments above EUR 12,500 are AWV-reportable within seven days; for dividend routing, a separate equity-clearance sub-ledger should be maintained and the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern filing must be evidenced by the bank before executionFX outright forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) with maturities of up to 24 months, documented under a German master agreement (Rahmenvertrag für Finanztermingeschäfte) or an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a German or EU-licensed bank counterpartyFX swaps (Währungsswaps) for rolling two-to-six-month liquidity rebalancing between EUR and USD/GBP, executed at the negotiated mid-market rate with full collateral/isda CSA marginingCross-currency swaps (Zins- und Währungsswaps) for matching long-term retainer and subcontractor obligations where the GmbH has a committed foreign-currency cash-flow profileCurrency options (Währungsoptionen) — permitted only where the GmbH documents the hedge objective and underlying exposure; options may be bought (premium-paid) but written options require an explicit Geschäftsführer resolution and treasury policy approval due to their non-linear risk profileNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — usable for settlement-risk-constrained currencies such as CNY offshore, KRW, INR, or USD/BRL where a regular deliverable forward is not available in the German interbank market, executed with a BaFin-supervised or equivalent EU/UK credit institution under CCP margining where applicable
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH as a secondary or ancillary clearing channel; MEDIUM-LOW as the sole or primary corporate treasury account. Legal constraints: EU-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs), e.g. Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, N26 Business, and bunq, are supervised by BaFin or another EEA regulator under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and the Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366; however, they are not 'credit institutions' under the German Banking Act (KWG) and therefore cannot grant credit, do not participate in the statutory German deposit guarantee scheme, and are not automatically recognized as a 'bank account' for the purpose of evidencing the 'free disposal' requirement of Section 8(2) GmbHG on statutory capital formation. German tax and insolvency practitioners advise that the Stammeinlage capital contribution be paid into a full-license commercial bank account. Offshore digital EMIs without an EEA license must not be used for payroll ordering, social-security contributions, or tax payments; foreign EMIs holding an EEA license may lawfully provide IBAN accounts to the German GmbH but should be limited to contingency reserves and small-ticket international retainer collections, with a documented treasury policy restricting their use

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
15% Koerperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritaetszuschlag (effective 15.825%); plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at 14–17%, yielding a combined effective tax rate between 30% and 33%.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding amount = 26.375% effective dividend withholding tax.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end; automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second following year when the return is prepared by a registered tax advisor; further discretionary extension possible.

      

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
Same statutory deadline as KSt 1: filing by 31 July of the following year, or extended to 28/29 February for professionally prepared returns.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 annual aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions (practical threshold; Germany formally imposes no fixed monetary trigger, but the tax office may require documentation at any level).

      
Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulation register, but §42 AO (abuse of law) and the substance-over-form principle allow the tax authorities to disregard a company lacking real staff, premises, and decision-making. A mere letter-box entity is denied treaty benefits and is subject to CFC taxation. For transfer pricing, the OECD Principal Purpose Test and the Multilateral Instrument rules apply, and the German tax auditors perform a functional and risk profile to ensure genuine economic substance.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)
        
Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 50,000 per charge in addition to the evaded tax; imprisonment of up to five years for controlling officers. The tax authority also imposes interest of 6% per annum on the evaded tax and can demand payment of the hidden tax amount plus penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for criminal fines and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced for recovery of the tax debt via Haftungsbescheid (§§69, 71 AO) if the director intentionally or negligently failed to pay taxes.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and intra-group attribution of income under the German anti-abuse rule (§42 AO); retroactive tax assessment; the entity may be disregarded and income taxed at shareholder level. Additionally, the company may lose its limited liability protection under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil in insolvency and tax proceedings.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face personal liability for unpaid taxes and social security contributions; courts may issue travel bans and asset freezes; severe cases lead to criminal charges for bankruptcy tax crimes (§§69, 70 StGB).

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering Breach
        
Statutory sanction: Fines of up to EUR 1 million for natural persons and EUR 5 million for legal entities, plus regulatory orders to discontinue operations; assets can be confiscated; repeat offenders face prosecution under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG).

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally subject to fines and can be imprisoned up to two years for negligent breaches and up to five years for intentional breaches. A liability order may be issued against the director's private assets if the company is insolvent.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 5% of the transfer pricing adjustment, with a minimum of EUR 5,000; also an interest surcharge of 6% per annum on the additional tax. The tax authority may estimate income and add a late filing penalty for the corporate income tax return.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or knowingly tolerate the absence of documentation are personally liable under §43 AO for the resulting tax arrears and penalties; criminal liability may arise if the documentation is falsified.

      

      
      

        Late Filing and Non-Payment of Withholding Taxes
        
Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty (Verspaetungszuschlag) of 0.25% per commenced month up to EUR 25,000; monthly late payment interest of 0.5% on arrears; enforcement fines may escalate to EUR 25,000 per unfiled return.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for tax debts if they fail to execute company payments after insolvency or when they knew of the unpaid taxes; a Haftungsbescheid can be issued against private assets, and the director may face professional disqualification.
