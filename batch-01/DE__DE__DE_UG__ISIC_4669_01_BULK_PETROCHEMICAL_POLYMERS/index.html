Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registergericht (Commercial Register Department) at the Amtsgericht (Local Court) of the registered office district; electronic filing via the XJustiz/Handelsregister portal

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG); core sections: §§ 1, 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), 7, 8, 10, 11, 35–41; supplemented by § 14 GewO (trade registration) and §§ 106–110 HGB (commercial register)

Formation / constitutional filings

Anmeldung zur Eintragung ins Handelsregister (Application for Entry in the Commercial Register), together with certified articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag or Musterprotokoll), list of managing directors, director consents, bank confirmation of paid-up capital, and power-of-attorney if any

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum subscribed capital for a UG is EUR 1.00 per founding shareholder; the full nominal capital must be paid in cash (contributions in kind are excluded) and placed in a German bank account at the free disposal of the managing director before registration; the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbH minimum).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate (Brandschutznachweis) for office and warehouse premises; structural load inspection (Standsicherheitsnachweis) for racking or silo storage systems; workplace safety clearance under the Arbeitsstättenverordnung (ArbStättV) and DGUV regulations by the BG RCI; electrical installation inspection (VDE) and occupational-safety confirmation from the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt before commencement of warehousing operations

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum subscribed capital for a UG is EUR 1.00 per founding shareholder; the full nominal capital must be paid in cash (contributions in kind are excluded) and placed in a German bank account at the free disposal of the managing director before registration; the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbH minimum).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT ID issuance and local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for income tax registration; official e-filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned German UG, submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt within one month after business commencement; the UG receives a local Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax. After the commercial register filing and business activity begin, apply to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr) using Form USt 1 A through ELSTER or the BZSt online portal. Non-resident suppliers without a German establishment may register directly for VAT purposes through the competent Finanzamt (government finance) or use the BZSt 'non-established business' procedure, providing incorporation docs, VAT certificate from home country, and proof of business activity. Also obtain an EORI number from the German customs administration (Generalzolldirektion) for export/import declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not offer a US-style 'check-the-box' election for a UG. The UG is a distinct tax-subject corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft). Available elections include: (1) 'Organschaft' (fiscal unity) for corporate income tax and VAT, allowing the UG to be consolidated with a German parent or controlled subsidiary; (2) for VAT, the election to treat closely-linked persons as a single entrepreneur; (3) specific hybrid mismatch elections required under the ATAD/CFC rules, which override contractual or classification choices. No entity pass-through election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic small businesses: EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and not exceeding EUR 50,000 projected current-year turnover (Kleinunternehmerregelung). For non-resident businesses, there is no registration threshold if taxable supplies are made in Germany; intra-Community distance-selling threshold from other EU countries is EUR 10,000 per calendar year. Import VAT applies regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third countries (non-EU) may be zero-rated under Section 4(1) no. 1 and Section 6 of the German VAT Act (UStG), provided the supplier is an entrepreneur, the goods are physically moved or dispatched by the supplier or on the supplier's account to a third-country destination, and the export is evidenced by proper proof (Ausfuhrnachweis). Intra-Community supplies of goods to an EU customer holding a valid EU VAT ID can also be zero-rated if the transport is documented. For the sale of virgin polyethylene/polypropylene granules to offshore entities, the transfer of title to a non-EU buyer, combined with customs export documentation and transport by common carrier, is sufficient; no zero-rating is available if the buyer is a German or other EU customer. Offshore B2B services may be zero-rated where the place of supply is in a third country, with the reverse-charge mechanism reported on the supplier's VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung / Zollanmeldung)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR for road transport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank payment confirmation / export proceeds receipt)
  • Commercial Invoice (commercial invoice with Incoterms and product description)
  • Packing List (packing list detailing weights and quantities)
  • Customs Exit Confirmation (customs exit proof from the exporting customs office / ATLAS status evidence)
  • EORI Number Registration Certificate
  • End-User Certificate or Supplier Country Customs Clearance where applicable

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2025, with transitional rules until 31 December 2027 for businesses with taxable turnover up to EUR 800,000, and until 31 December 2027 for combined simplified e-invoices. Mandatory formats: XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, or any equivalent PEPPOL-compliant format. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no API as a legal clearance interface, and no digital stamping regime. E-invoices must be transmitted in a structured electronic format via PEPPOL or operator networks; the recipient must be able to extract the required data. Non-resident suppliers must also issue e-invoices for domestic B2B taxable supplies.

Fraudulent or unjustified claims of export zero-rating trigger retrospective VAT assessment, denial of corresponding input VAT deductions, statutory interest (6% per annum accrued), late-payment surcharges under Sections 233a and 240 AO, and potential liability under Section 14c UStG if an invoice incorrectly shows VAT or improperly claims zero rating. Intentional or negligent misstatement may constitute tax evasion (Section 370 Abgabenordnung) punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to five years, and the tax authorities may require comprehensive export documentation audits; continuous false claims can lead to criminal prosecution, revocation of licenses, and joint and several liability for managing directors and authorised representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully convertible Euro currency regime with liberalized capital and current accounts. Profit remittances to foreign owners face no statutory hurdles for EU-resident entities; all transactions are subject to statistical reporting thresholds set by the Bundesbank under the Foreign Exchange Act.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. Companies must file statistical reports with the Deutsche Bundesbank for exports exceeding €12,500 within ten days of shipment. Conversion occurs at prevailing market rates with no prescribed ratios.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited feasibility for offshore digital EMIs due to EU-wide PSD2 and AMLD licensing requirements. Corporate fintech platforms are restricted; remote digital onboarding is permitted but requires enhanced due diligence and may be declined for high-risk commodity trading activities.
