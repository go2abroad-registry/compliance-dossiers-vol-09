Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) requires a stated share capital of at least EUR 1.00 per § 5a(1) GmbHG, fully paid-in in cash before Commercial Register registration; the law mandates that at least 25% of annual net profit be retained in a statutory reserve until the subscribed capital reaches the regular GmbH minimum of EUR 25,000. Share capital contributions must be transferred to a blocked corporate cash account (or notarial escrow account) and remain freely available to the Managing Director, evidenced by a banker's confirmation at registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutznachweis) approved by the Lower Building Authority and the local Fire Brigade expert (Sachverständiger für Brandschutz), structural load acceptance certificate for cold-room ceilings, racks, and freezer platforms, and a workplace clearance pursuant to the German Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung - ArbStättV), including a DGUV (German Social Accident Insurance) Vorschrift 1 risk assessment survey for steam, cryogenic, and mechanical hazards in the dim sum quick-freezing line.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for federation-wide VAT ID and withholding; local tax offices (Finanzamt) for entity income and trade tax assessment; official portals ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online applications (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

A UG must obtain a German tax number (Steuernummer) by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the local Finanzamt immediately after notarial incorporation and registration in the Commercial Register. The UG additionally applies to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG if it engages in cross-border transactions, using the BZSt portal or Finanzamt. For a foreign non-resident shareholder, the UG remains resident by virtue of its registered seat or place of management in Germany; the foreign parent must provide certified incorporation documents and a written power of attorney. A non-established foreign direct seller in Germany is obliged to register for VAT with the Finanzamt under §18 UStG regardless of turnover, and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative in cases under §22a UStG. All filings and subsequent correspondence are electronically submitted using the ELSTER account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide an OECD-style check-the-box or voluntary pass-through election; the UG is absolutely a body corporate subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available statutory elections include: (1) VAT and corporate income tax grouping through an Organschaft where financial, economic and organisational integration exists; (2) §9 UStG option to tax real estate; (3) loss carry-back/carry-forward elections under §10d EStG and §8 KStG; and (4) corporation tax group election under §6 KStG where stipulated. Hybrid mismatch classifications are not elective; anti-hybrid rules under ATAD and §4k EStG automatically recharacterise deductions or tax exempt income where divergent classifications by another jurisdiction would otherwise be exploited.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General mandatory VAT registration applies to every entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany without a monetary threshold. The only turnover-based exemption is the small business facility under §19 UStG: prior-year turnover below EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover expected below EUR 50,000. Non-established foreign entities must register for any taxable supply made within Germany regardless of turnover. For EU distance sales to German consumers, the cross-border OSS threshold is EUR 10,000 total across EU Member States; exceeding it triggers German VAT registration through the One-Stop-Shop or local registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of physical goods, including prefabricated frozen dim sum and dumplings, qualify for zero-rated VAT treatment under §4(1)(1) and §6 UStG when the goods are (i) actually transported to a non-EU destination, (ii) exported by the supplier as part of the entrepreneur's enterprise, and (iii) duly evidenced by commercial or customs proof of export. Delivery to an EU customer is generally exempt under §4(1)(b) UStG if the customer is taxable and holds a valid VAT ID; such intra-Community supply must be reported in EC Sales List and Intrastat where thresholds apply. Services connected to exported goods, or B2B services supplied to a non-EU business, may be zero-rated under §4(4)-§4(8) UStG where the recipient is established outside the EU and effective use criteria are satisfied. Domestic sales to German consumers remain subject to VAT, and may qualify for the reduced 7% rate for certain food products, but never for exports.

  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation evidencing the export sales proceeds
  • Commercial export invoice, packing list, and buyer's confirmation; additionally an Intra-Community supplier declaration or Intrastat declaration where applicable

Germany has phased in mandatory B2B electronic invoicing under the Growth Opportunities Act: from 1 January 2025 all businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices conforming to EN 16931 (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027 businesses with more than EUR 800,000 taxable turnover must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. There is currently no real-time fiscal clearance or compulsory digital stamping comparable to Italy's Sistema di Interscambio, but the tax administration is preparing a future real-time B2B transaction reporting system (EÜr) to be phased in from 2027-2028, requiring API-based transmission of transactional data to the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern. Until then, archiving, GoBD compliance, and electronic transmission through certified providers are mandatory for e-invoices.

Export zero-rating claims are intensely audited by Finanzamt and customs authorities. Failure to maintain or produce the legally required documentary proof (Beleg- und Buchnachweis), or deliberate false documentation, triggers retrospective VAT assessment (Nacherhebung) at 19% or the appropriate rate, with late interest accruing under §233a AO. Deliberate evasion constitutes tax fraud under §370 AO, carrying up to 10 years imprisonment; negligent tax shortfall is an administrative offence under §378 AO, subject to a fine up to EUR 50,000. Additional penalty surcharges of up to 10% of the assessed tax amount (minimum EUR 5,000) may be imposed under §§162, 164, and 164a AO. When proceeds are not repatriated within the contractual 180-day period, transfer pricing adjustments and CFC/anti-hybrid rules under §1 AStG may recharacterise the export sale, leading to imputed income, retrospective corporate tax and trade tax assessments, and limitation periods extended to 10 years for fraudulent concealment. Customs export irregularities may additionally trigger customs debt and administrative penalties under Union Customs Regulation (UZK) and the German Customs Administration Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully convertible euro regime with no exchange controls, no dual-rate system, no prior investment approval, and no frictional profit-repatriation restrictions. Current-account and capital-account movements are liberalized; dividends, loan repayments, and disposal proceeds of a UG may be remitted freely to non-residents, subject only to standard corporate-tax withholding or documentation obligations. The sole regulatory burden for a UG is statistical, not restrictive: cross-border payments received or made by a domestic company must be recorded in the Deutsche Bundesbank's balance-of-payments statistics when the transaction value exceeds EUR 12,500; this is a declaratory, post-event filing discharged by the domestic bank on behalf of the client.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any foreign currency, in domestic or offshore accounts, without a forced-sale obligation. For receipts from goods exports exceeding EUR 12,500, the German bank must report the transaction to the Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV statistics code for goods exports (Warenausfuhr), including the importer reference and contract number; the UG only needs to provide accurate payment purpose text. There is no time limit for holding export proceeds offshore and no minimum repatriation level.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main, German Federal Central Bank), with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as bank prudential supervisor; exchange-control duties are executed under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz — AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung — AWV).

Compliant routing: the foreign shareholder remits a high-value SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank clearing) in EUR to the UG's IBAN; the transfer must never be directed to a personal account. The purpose field must state 'Einlage auf das Stammkapital der UG' for equity or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' for shareholder loan; SWIFT/ISO 20022 may use Purpose Code ACCT or GDDS, which the receiving bank maps to the Deutsche Bundesbank's capital-transaction (Direktinvestition) statistical code. A newly founded UG opens a 'Gründungskonto' as a blocked-style founding account until the Handelsregister entry, then converts it to a regular operating account. Verification package: notarial incorporation deed, share register, Transparenzregister extract, and a source-of-funds file for the paying shareholder; for Chinese parents, include the ODI certificate and the bank remittance approval (SAFE registration for the Chinese outward wire). Use a German correspondent in the CNH hub (e.g., Commerzbank's correspondent through ICBC Singapore or HSBC Hong Kong) to obtain same-day value and avoid OFAC/NAT referencing issues.Primary rail pair: TT/Wire — SWIFT MT103 from the German bank to the dim sum supplier's bank, with field 70 containing the purchase contract number, invoice, HS 1902 class and customs declaration reference; and Commercial LC — irrevocable documentary credit (UCP600) issued by the German bank (Commerzbank or Deutsche Bank import-LC desk) in favour of the exporting dim sum manufacturer, settled against compliant presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, health certificate, certificate of origin and ISO 22000 certificate. For open-account top-up payments, keep all customs entries and warehouse receipts on file to satisfy transaction-monitoring; LC settlement can be in EUR or USD per the underlying contract. Maintain two correspondent banking relationships to prevent a single-corridor liquidity failure, and reconcile all settlements with the frozen-inventory ERP system.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD FX Forwards (Devisentermingeschäft) — bilaterally documented under the German Master Agreement for Financial Derivative Transactions (DRV) or an ISDA 1998 FX Annex; not subject to mandatory central clearing for a non-financial counterparty below the EMIR clearing threshold.EUR/CNH deliverable FX forwards and FX swaps — permitted for hedging CNH-denominated dim sum raw-material imports through a European bank counterparty.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on onshore CNY (EUR/CNY or USD/CNY) — permissible when the UG has no CNY deliverable account and only for genuine underlying import invoices; requires an ISDA NDF confirmation and EMIR reporting.Cross-currency swaps (CCS/XCCY) — permissible for hedging interest and principal exposure of a foreign-currency shareholder loan into EUR over a 2–5 year horizon.EUR/USD and EUR/CNH currency options (calls and puts) — permissible for hedging machinery capex and export receipts of the quick-freezing factory, with premiums payable in EUR.Commodity swaps are out of scope; only linear, unleveraged FX derivatives with fully collateralized EUR margin are permitted under German banking and EMIR rules for a UG.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-licensed EMIs and payment institutions (e.g., Wise Europe with Belgian licence, Revolut with Lithuanian licence, Airwallex with Maltese licence) used as ancillary multicurrency wallets with virtual IBANs and low-cost EUR/CNY/USD conversion; Low for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs, which legally cannot issue German IBANs or hold a BaFin passport and therefore cannot serve as a primary corporate banking rail. A German UG must maintain its primary liquidity and capital-subscription accounts at a full licensed credit institution; the EEA EMI is appropriate only for settlement sub-accounts downstream of the Tier-1 bank, and cannot provide import LC issuance or documentary trade settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions: mandatory Local File/Master File if annual turnover of controlled goods transactions exceeds EUR 5,000,000; service transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; other transactions EUR 500,000. Below these de minimis thresholds, simplified documentation acceptable

Economic substance scope: Germany applies substance-over-form doctrine (§ 41 AO) and ATAD minimum substance rules. A UG must maintain real business premises, personnel, equipment, and decision-making in Germany to be recognized as resident and for treaty benefits. Letterbox companies are disregarded and may be reclassified as transparent or taxable in the shareholder's residence

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment up to 5 years or fine (§ 370 AO); additional 10% penalty on evaded tax if criminal investigation initiated; reassessment plus interest; refund claims denied; customs and VAT liabilities triggered

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes under § 34, 69 AO; personal assets seizure; travel bans and surrender of passports; disqualification from management positions up to 5 years; extradition possible for aggravated cases
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under § 41 AO (substance over form); denial of treaty benefits and loss of DTA relief; retroactive 26.375% dividend withholding; trade tax add-back; administrative penalty up to EUR 50,000 for failure to meet substance requirements; registration cancellation

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil (Haftungsdurchgriff) under case law: shareholders/directors personally liable for company debts if capital materially undercapitalized and creditor harm; travel ban for fugitive non-resident directors; asset attachment in domestic and EU enforcement
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment under § 162 AO; penalty of 5% of the adjusted profit (minimum EUR 5,000) if documentation not supplied; increased to 10% if late; interest on deficiency accrues at 0.5% per month; assessment within 7 years

Director personal liability: Managing directors personally liable for the increased tax plus penalty if they were responsible for the transfer pricing policy and failed to provide documentation; criminal liability under § 370 AO for aiding tax evasion; restriction on leaving the country in severe assessment disputes
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Negligence

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 100,000 per contravention; up to EUR 1 million for repeated or systemic violations (§ 56 GwG); business registration revoked; confiscation of assets connected to suspicious transactions; referral to public prosecutor for money laundering under § 261 StGB

Director personal liability: Directors personally fined for failure to file beneficial ownership; criminal prosecution for money laundering if beneficial owner and directors had knowledge of illicit funds; travel ban and US/EU asset freezes in cross-border fraud cases; disqualification as director under § 6 GmbHG
