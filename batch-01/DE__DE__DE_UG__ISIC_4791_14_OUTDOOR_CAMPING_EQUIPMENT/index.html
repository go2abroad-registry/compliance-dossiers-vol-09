Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG has no statutory minimum capital requirement; a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00 is sufficient. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Germany applies no general exchange control restrictions, no mandatory approval for profit remittance, and no restrictions on dividend distributions by a UG (haftungsbeschränkt) to non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate tax withholding, a 26.375% dividend withholding tax rate for shareholders resident in third states, reduced to 0% or treaty rate where a double taxation treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies. Cross-border payments in excess of €12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, but this is a reporting requirement, not an authorization requirement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement. Exporters may freely retain foreign currency proceeds abroad or repatriate them at their discretion. However, all cross-border payments, including export receipts, must be recorded and reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for external transactions statistics if the amount exceeds €12,500 or equivalent. No minimum conversion ratio into euro is mandated; conversion decisions are purely commercial and may be managed through treasury hedging policy.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank and monetary authority) in coordination with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for banking supervision; Germany does not maintain a separate foreign exchange administration authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions are viable for low-risk e-commerce transaction flows, including internet retail of outdoor camping equipment. The entity must be licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or passport an EU home-member-state license into Germany. Offshore digital non-bank wallets based outside the EEA are not viable as primary treasury accounts because they lack SEPA direct access, do not qualify as bank accounts for corporate capital-contribution escrow purposes, and create elevated AML, sanctions, and tax-transparency exposure. A German UG should hold principal liquidity in a full-reserve commercial bank account and use EMIs only as auxiliary collection rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscription and loan capital contributions to the UG must be routed through SEPA instant or standard Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for euro-denominated payments, or via SWIFT MT103 for non-euro currency wires. The payment reference must clearly identify the contributing shareholder and the purpose of the capital measure, referencing the notarized capital increase resolution and commercial register filing number. For foreign currency inflows, Deutsche Bundesbank external transactions reporting (Wertpapier- und Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) requires lodgment of the transaction if the amount exceeds €12,500, stating the proper foreign trade and payment code. Verification must include the notarial deed or capital increase resolution, proof of identity of the remitter, the account ownership declaration of the UG, and a matching value-date credit confirmation. Funds must be held in an escrow account until the capital contribution is formally registered in the commercial register where minimum statutory capital is incorporated.Trade settlements should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA wholesale payments and via SEPA Credit Transfer for EU/EEA supplier payments. For high-value commercial imports of outdoor camping equipment from non-EU suppliers, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (Documentary Credit / UCP 600) routed through a German issuing bank and confirmed by a bank in the supplier's jurisdiction provides the preferred clearing rail. Additional rails include SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank cover payments and targeted instant payment systems. Payment earmarking must correspond to underlying import invoices, transport documents, and customs declarations. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (0.825% effective) + municipal Gewerbesteuer; at a typical hebesatz of 400% the combined statutory burden is approximately 29.83% before trade-tax deductibility adjustments.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that 25%, producing a final statutory dividend withholding burden of 26.375% (no church tax for corporate shareholders).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous local file mandatory when annual aggregate cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000; master file required when worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Administrative practice may also require simplified records for controlled transactions above EUR 500,000, and VAT/OSS evidence must align with TP models for online retail.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no named Economic Substance Regulations, but achievement of tax residence and treaty benefits depends on actual substance: registered seat and/or place of management in Germany (§ 11 AO), real business office, employees and management decisions taken in Germany. For an outdoor e-commerce UG, substance is demonstrated by local inventory management, warehouse contracts, marketing functions, customer service staff and bookkeeping in Germany. Absent substance, the UG may be disregarded under § 42 AO abuse doctrine, treaty benefits denied under § 50d, and income reallocated to the foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal punishment under § 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine of up to 360 daily rates; in aggravated cases (serious tax evasion involving cross-border transactions) 6 months to 10 years’ imprisonment. Civil tax assessment is recalculated with 6% annual late interest and the full tax debt plus solidarity surcharge is immediately collectible.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable under § 69 AO for taxes of the UG that they wilfully or grossly negligently fail to declare or remit. Criminal courts can order confiscation of assets, seizure of passports, travel bans and, on final conviction, imprisonment; directors may also be barred from managing companies.
Shell Corporation / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authority disregard under § 42 AO (abuse of legal forms) and denial of DTA relief under § 50d EStG; reallocation of income to the foreign beneficial owner triggers German corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT reassessments and additional withholding tax; administrative fines under trade registration regulations up to EUR 25,000 may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false substance statements are personally liable under criminal fraud provisions; personal tax liability may arise if the UG is reclassified as transparent for foreign tax purposes; seizure and travel bans can be ordered in summary criminal proceedings for insolvency or fraud.
VAT Fraud and Marketplace Liability

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution as tax fraud under § 370 AO with up to 5 years’ imprisonment; civil penalty up to EUR 30,000 under § 26a UStG; assessment of unpaid VAT plus administrative surcharge; the German tax authority may order full liability of the UG and revoke its VAT ID if returns are persistently absent.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid VAT if they knowingly fail to declare or if they use VAT receipts but do not remit; under § 69 AO liability includes negligent ignorance of VAT thresholds; criminal forfeiture of bank accounts and travel prohibitions are common preliminary measures.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Documentation surcharge under § 162(4) AO: 5% of the tax adjustment, at least EUR 5,000, increased up to 10% in severe or late-documentation cases; the tax authority may further estimate profits and impose a 6% per annum late-payment surcharge on the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Administrative fines for falsification of transfer pricing documents; if the failure is part of a criminal evasion scheme, managing directors face imprisonment under § 370 AO; civil liability for any resulting corporate tax debt remains if the director signed the declaration knowingly.
Profit Repatriation and Withholding Tax Neglect

Statutory sanction: The UG is liable for the unwithheld capital gains tax; the Finanzamt re-assesses the grossed-up dividend and imposes solidarity surcharge, late-payment interest at 6% per annum, and a late payment penalty. Criminal prosecution for evasion applies if there is intent to obtain the withholding tax advantage.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally jointly and severally liable under § 44(5) EStG for the full 26.375% withheld amount if distribution occurred without tax withholding; civil restitution in insolvency proceedings; court-ordered travel bans and passport seizure can be ordered for absconding risk.
