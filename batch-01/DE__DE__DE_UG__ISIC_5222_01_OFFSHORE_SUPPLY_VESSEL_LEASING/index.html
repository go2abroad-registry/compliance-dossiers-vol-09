Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt), Bonn, and locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) at the company's seat/place of management; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de). BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) and handles international tax matters; the Finanzamt issues Steuernummer and assesses corporate income/trade/VAT.

E-government filing portal

The UG must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt within roughly 4 weeks after formation/commencement of business. In the same procedure it applies for a Steuernummer (11-digit income tax/trade tax number), obtains a Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer account, and separately requests a VAT ID (USt-IdNr., format DE + 9 digits) from BZSt. Foreign entities without a German PE or place of management that only have taxable supplies in Germany must register with BZSt for a USt-IdNr. under Sec. 27a UStG and cannot use the Kleinunternehmer exemption; processing normally takes 4-6 weeks; representation by a German tax adviser (Steuerberater) is strongly recommended.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG is a German tax-resident capital company with unlimited corporate income tax liability under Sec. 1 KStG (worldwide income, with foreign tax credit or treaty exemption for foreign-source income). Headline federal tax: 15% corporation tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporation tax (combined 15.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at typically 7-17% depending on the municipality's Hebesatz (overall effective burden approx. 30-33% for a commercial enterprise). Not a pass-through; non-resident shareholders are allowed and profit distributions are subject to 26.375% German withholding tax (25% + 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reduced by treaty (e.g., 5% or 10% under DTTs with participation relief). Territorial scope: worldwide income of the resident UG, no territorial exemption, no tax-exempt zone status. — The UG must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt within roughly 4 weeks after formation/commencement of business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG is a German tax-resident capital company with unlimited corporate income tax liability under Sec. 1 KStG (worldwide income, with foreign tax credit or treaty exemption for foreign-source income). Headline federal tax: 15% corporation tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on corporation tax (combined 15.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at typically 7-17% depending on the municipality's Hebesatz (overall effective burden approx. 30-33% for a commercial enterprise). Not a pass-through; non-resident shareholders are allowed and profit distributions are subject to 26.375% German withholding tax (25% + 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reduced by treaty (e.g., 5% or 10% under DTTs with participation relief). Territorial scope: worldwide income of the resident UG, no territorial exemption, no tax-exempt zone status.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election. Relevant German elections include: (1) Organschaft (corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT fiscal unity) under Secs. 14-17 KStG and Sec. 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG between an UG and its controlling domestic parent; (2) extended trade-tax deduction (erweiterte Gewerbesteuerkürzung) for companies renting out real property under Sec. 9 Nr. 1 S. 2 GewStG; (3) exit-tax deferral election over 7 years under Sec. 6 AStG; (4) ATAD/hybrid-mismatch declarations and documentation (Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 8b KStG) disallowing deductions for hybrid mismatches with no elective opt-in; (5) entity classification is not elective for domestic German entities; a UG is always a taxable corporate entity subject to German domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

(a) Resident Kleinunternehmer (small-business) exemption: EUR 22,000 prior-year / EUR 50,000 current-year turnover (Sec. 19 UStG) - not available to foreign taxpayers or to companies claiming input VAT recovery; (b) intra-EU OSS distance-sales threshold: EUR 10,000 union-wide (one-stop-shop); (c) non-resident suppliers: no general VAT registration threshold - registration required before performing the first taxable supply in Germany (reverse charge applies for B2B services by foreign suppliers, but a German UG lessor must register and charge German VAT on domestic charter/supplies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The following positions may be zero-rated (tax-exempt with input VAT deduction): (1) Export of goods to third countries (Ausfuhrlieferung, Sec. 6 UStG) - e.g., outright sale of an offshore supply vessel to a non-EU buyer with export declaration; (2) charter/leasing of sea-going vessels used in commercial international shipping, including ancillary offshore logistics, under Sec. 8 (and Sec. 5 Nr. 2 UStG) if the vessel is a sea-going vessel ('Seegehendes Schiff') actually used in international traffic; offshore platform-supply voyages on the high seas may qualify per German and CJEU case law if the vessel is engaged in international commercial shipping (supply to continental-shelf installations outside the German territorial sea is commonly outside the scope or exempt; supplies to installations in the German territorial sea/EEZ may be taxable); (3) B2B services (vessel charter and leasing) supplied to a non-German entrepreneur may have the place of supply at the customer's establishment under Sec. 3a Abs. 2 UStG, thereby not being German-taxable, provided the customer is a third-country or EU business (reverse charge not applicable to third-country recipients). Full input VAT recovery is available. Substantiation must prove both the identity/status of the foreign customer and the actual export or international-use character.

  • Electronic export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) in the ATLAS customs system with a validated MRN (Movement Reference Number) or the SAD document via the single window, naming the UG as exporter
  • Bill of Lading (negotiable ocean B/L) or Sea Waybill evidencing carriage on a sea-going vessel from the German/EU port to the foreign destination
  • Charter-party agreement, vessel-lease contract or bareboat charter document describing the offshore supply vessel, its IMO number, flag and commercial international-use purpose
  • Certificate of Registry and flag-state ship documents proving sea-going vessel status (International Load Line Certificate / International Safety Management (ISM) certificate)
  • Commercial invoice (original) and, where applicable, the export customs entry (ATLAS Abmeldenachweis) or the Zollbestätigung (customs confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Zahlungsbeleg) or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in EUR or convertible foreign currency
  • EU Gelangensbestätigung (declaration of arrival) if the supply is an intra-EU movement (where not an export to a third country)
  • Offshore installation operator statement or voyage orders demonstrating operations on the high seas / outside German territorial waters for international traffic purposes

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Mandates: (a) B2G e-invoicing (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X XML following EN 16931) mandatory for all invoices to federal contracting authorities since 27 Nov 2020; (b) B2B e-invoicing transition: from 2025 German businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 Jan 2027 all resident businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue XRechnung/ZUGFeRD XML e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies; full B2B e-invoicing for all taxpayers from 1 Jan 2028; (c) cash-register fiscal data must be protected by a certified technical security system (TSE) and transmitted via certified software (GOBD/TSE); (d) no government API clearance or digital stamping portal exists; VAT returns are filed electronically via ELSTER (advance returns generally within 10 days; Dauerfristverlängerung possible).

German tax authorities scrutinize export zero-rating claims through the Finanzamt and customs post-clearance audits (Außenprüfung / Prüfung nach den §§ 209, 210 AO). Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating leads to retrospective VAT clawback for the supply plus default interest (0.15% per month under Sec. 233a AO), denial of input VAT deduction, and the statutory 4-year assessment period is extended to 10 years in case of evasion. Criminal exposure: tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung, Sec. 370 AO) punishes deliberate misdeclaration with up to 5 years' imprisonment (or 10 years in aggravated cases); negligent evasion (Sec. 378 AO) carries fines up to EUR 50,000; factually false export substantiation may also trigger sanctions under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and customs criminal law (Secs. 370/374 AO); directors/shareholders of the UG are personally liable for de-facto management, and the Finanzamt may impose administrative fines and order remedial disclosure. No statutory German FX-repatriation deadline is prescribed; the 180-day benchmark is the conventional contractual/audit assumption used for safeguarding export-proceeds evidence and transfer-pricing arm's-length payment terms.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital framework. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (5% on dividends under EU parent-subsidiary directive if applicable, otherwise 26.375% including solidarity surcharge, reduced by treaty). No exchange control approval is required for repatriation, but statistical reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 per month (AWV reporting).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments and holdings must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV §67-68) within 7 days of the transaction or month-end for balances. No minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking oversight; foreign exchange administration is primarily governed by the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV) administered by the Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German BaFin-regulated EMIs (e.g., PPRO, Solarisbank) can provide multi-currency IBANs and SWIFT connectivity, but they are not full-service banks for maritime escrow or complex trade finance. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise) are viable for ancillary liquidity but may face restrictions on maritime charter payments due to enhanced due diligence. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed under PSD2 and are subject to BaFin oversight; they cannot provide credit or LC services.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT (aggregate 0.825%) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); combined typical effective burden approx. 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375% aggregate statutory rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for the Local File; Master File required where consolidated group revenue is EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain an offshore-style ESR register, but applies §42 AO anti-abuse rules, substance-over-form doctrine, beneficial ownership principles, and ATAD CFC rules. The UG must demonstrate real local administration, vessel charter negotiation, operational control, and commercial personnel; lack of substance risks treaty relief denial and recharacterisation as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under §370 AO: imprisonment up to five years, up to ten years in aggravated cases; fines plus assessed tax and monthly default interest of 0.15%; seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under §69 AO for unpaid corporation tax, subject to criminal conviction, travel bans, asset freezes, and prohibition from managing commercial undertakings.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under §42 AO; denial of DTA relief; retroactive profit assessment; loss of limited liability protection; revocation of trade licence; potential civil liability for all corporate obligations.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors and beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and creditor claims; criminal liability for false declarations and money laundering if concealment is proven.
Transfer Pricing Breach

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may estimate offshore charter income under §162 AO; TP documentation penalty of at least EUR 5,000 plus 5% surcharge on the adjustment and additional late documentation penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for surcharges if they wilfully or negligently cause incomplete documentation; criminal liability arises if false or forged transfer pricing documents are submitted.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Breach of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG): fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; money laundering under §261 StGB: imprisonment up to five years; confiscation of vessels and bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal prosecution, personal fines, travel bans, freezing orders, disqualification from management, and extradition exposure.
