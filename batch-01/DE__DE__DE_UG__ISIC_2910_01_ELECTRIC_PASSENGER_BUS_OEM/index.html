Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the competent Local Court (Amtsgericht) for the registered office, with electronic filing through a German notary and nationwide publication in the Federal Gazette (Bundesanzeiger) and the Company Register (Unternehmensregister).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), as amended, particularly §§ 1, 2, 4a, 5, 5a, 7, 8, 9c, 10 and 35; supplemented by German Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 6, 8-11, 106, 107 and by the Notarization Act (Beurkundungsgesetz).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may fix a share capital of at least EUR 1, corresponding to the aggregate nominal value of all shares; in a cash formation every contribution must be fully paid into a bank account in the name of the UG and be at the free disposal of the managing director before registration in the Commercial Register. Non-cash contributions are permitted only if fully covered by assets and disclosed in the formation report. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profit in a statutory reserve until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers, customs and excise; locally competent tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment; official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

The UG is incorporated by notarial deed and registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The managing director then files the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt, which issues the corporate tax number (Steuernummer) and trade tax number (Gewerbesteuernummer). The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately from the BZSt via Form USt 1 TU. Foreign entities with a German permanent establishment or branch must file the same questionnaire with the local Finanzamt and obtain a VAT ID for intra-Community transactions; applications are processed electronically via ELSTER.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The UG is incorporated by notarial deed and registered in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The managing director then files the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt, which issues the corporate tax number (Steuernummer) and trade tax number (Gewerbesteuernummer). The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately from the BZSt via Form USt 1 TU. Foreign entities with a German permanent establishment or branch must file the same questionnaire with the local Finanzamt and obtain a VAT ID for intra-Community transactions; applications are processed electronically via ELSTER.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law provides no check-the-box or US-style pass-through election; classification follows the civil law legal form. Available elections: VAT fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) between a controlling parent and financially, economically and organisationally integrated subsidiaries; corporate income tax and trade tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) requiring a profit and loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); option under § 19 UStG for small businesses to waive the Kleinunternehmer exemption and charge VAT; and hybrid mismatch adjustments under § 4k EStG and the ATAD implementation rules, which disallow certain deductible payments in hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general monetary turnover threshold; all entrepreneurs are subject to VAT registration. The statutory small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer, § 19 UStG) applies where prior-year turnover is at or below EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is at or below EUR 100,000; above these amounts VAT registration and charging become mandatory, with an option to waive the exemption below the thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods to third-country territories are zero-rated under § 6 UStG, and intra-Community supplies to other EU Member States are zero-rated under § 6a UStG. Eligibility requires: the supplier must be an entrepreneur; the goods must physically leave German customs territory (export) or be dispatched/transported to another EU Member State (intra-Community supply); for intra-Community supplies, the recipient must hold a valid VAT identification number in another Member State; and the supplier must obtain and retain documentary evidence (Beleg- und Buchnachweise) per §§ 8-13 UStDV, including customs export declarations and transport documents.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged electronically via the ATLAS/ECS system
  • Export Accompanying Document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument, ABD)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the export order, incoterms and shipment terms
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, CMR waybill or Air Waybill) evidencing physical departure from German customs territory for exports or arrival in another EU Member State for intra-Community supplies
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or equivalent proof of payment received in the exporter's account

Staged B2B e-invoicing mandate under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025 all domestic B2B taxpayers must receive structured e-invoices in the EN 16931 format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); from 1 January 2027 businesses with taxable turnover above EUR 800,000 in 2026 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all businesses must issue e-invoices. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government API clearance, or digital stamping regime (no equivalent of the Italian Sistema di Interscambio).

Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO (Steuerhinterziehung), punishable with imprisonment of up to 10 years or fines; negligent tax shortfall under § 378 AO carries fines up to EUR 50,000. The tax office will retroactively disallow the 0% rate, assess 19% VAT on the supply, deny or claw back input VAT deduction (including under § 6a(4) UStG for deficient intra-Community evidence), and impose default interest of 0.15% per month under § 233a AO. False customs declarations also trigger penalties under Article 42 of the Union Customs Code and German customs administrative provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full euro convertibility across both capital and current account transactions in accordance with Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG). There are no exchange control approvals, licensing gates, or repatriation barriers on profit remittances, dividend distributions, interest, royalties, intercompany settlements, or trade payments. The only material frictions are statutory information duties: cross-border payments, claims, and receivables exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics, and all transactions must comply with EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules (GwG), and capitalization verification under the UG legal form.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory holding timeline, and no forced conversion ratio on export receipts. Exporters may freely retain, hold offshore, or convert foreign-currency receipts without limit or prior authorization. The sole compliance requirement for an EV bus OEM is the AWV statistical declaration to Deutsche Bundesbank for each cross-border export payment exceeding EUR 12,500, transmitted with the appropriate transaction purpose code within the statutory reporting window.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), acting in coordination with the European Central Bank for euro monetary policy; foreign trade and export control administration by the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA); banking conduct supervision by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional-Low for core treasury and Low for documentary credit operations. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI cannot passport into Germany under PSD2 and would require separate authorization under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); without that license it cannot issue a German IBAN, access TARGET2, or act as confirming bank for municipal letters of credit. EEA-licensed EMIs may provide SEPA-compliant euro rails and retail-grade FX conversion for sub-threshold operational payments, but the primary liquidity anchor must remain a German full-service commercial bank with correspondent banking, LC confirmation, and export financing capacity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from non-resident shareholders route via SWIFT MT103 in EUR and must be coded with the German balance-of-payments statistics purpose code for inward direct investment (establishment or acquisition of an enterprise), with the company's Commercial Register number quoted in the payment reference. Each credit exceeding EUR 12,500 triggers an AWV report to Deutsche Bundesbank. For a UG, a formal capital increase requires notarization and Commercial Register filing, so interim shareholder loans with written loan agreements and arm's-length interest are operationally faster for initial liquidity. All injections must be allocated to the German corporate account and supported by provenance evidence to satisfy GwG source-of-funds verification.Euro-denominated payments within the EEA settle via SEPA Instant or the TARGET2 (T2) large-value payment system; non-EEA raw-material and component procurement settles via SWIFT MT103 with corresponding banking fees. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) + 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax + trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rate (typically 14-17%), combined effective rate approx. 30%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the WHT, effective 26.375%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if cross-border related-party transactions exceed €5 million for goods or €500,000 for services. Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €100 million. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 60 days of request.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like UAE, but applies general anti-avoidance rules (Section 42 AO) and substance-over-form principles. A UG must have real economic activity (employees, premises, business decisions) to avoid being classified as a shell company. The Federal Fiscal Court requires substance for treaty benefits and to prevent abuse.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years in severe cases) and fines up to €50,000. Additional civil penalties up to 100% of evaded tax. Interest on unpaid tax at 0.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing officers can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they fail to file accurate returns or knowingly participate in evasion. Civil restitution, travel bans, and asset freezes may be imposed. In cases of gross negligence, personal liability extends to corporate debts.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, and imposition of penalty taxes. The company may be disregarded for tax purposes, leading to full taxation of profits at the shareholder level. Fines up to €25,000 for non-compliance with substance documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes if they knowingly operate a shell entity to evade tax. They may face criminal charges for aiding tax evasion and be subject to personal asset seizure.
AML / Money Laundering (Geldwäsche)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to €1 million for individuals; corporate fines up to €5 million. Administrative penalties for failure to implement AML compliance programs.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML breaches if they fail to appoint an AML officer, conduct due diligence, or report suspicious transactions. They may face imprisonment and travel bans.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, penalty of 5-10% of the adjustment amount (minimum €5,000), and interest on underpaid tax. In severe cases, criminal penalties for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant TP policies. They may also face criminal charges for tax evasion if intent is proven.
