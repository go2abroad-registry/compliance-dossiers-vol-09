Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen) sets national tax policy; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt, www.bzst.de) issues the EU-wide VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), operates the EU VIES system, and manages central tax certificates; the local tax office (Finanzamt) competent for the UG's registered seat (Sitz) administers corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT (Umsatzsteuer), and payroll tax (Lohnsteuer); the statutory electronic filing portal is ELSTER – Elektronische Steuererklärung (www.elster.de), supplemented by the BZSt government portal Mein BZSt for VAT-ID and cross-border tax applications.

E-government filing portal

The UG, being a commercial Kapitalgesellschaft, is subject to mandatory tax registration from commencement of business. Within four weeks of the start of business (incorporation and registration in the Handelsregister), the managing director must file the electronic tax registration questionnaire, 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung', via ELSTER; this one questionnaire simultaneously covers corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT, and payroll tax registration. Upon processing, the local Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (statutory tax number, format e.g. 10/123/45678) and issues a VAT registration certificate. In parallel, the UG must apply to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number), either directly via the Mein BZSt portal using Formular USt 1 oder by indicating the corresponding box in the Fragebogen, from which the Finanzamt forwards the request; issuance generally takes two to six weeks. The USt-IdNr. is mandatory for any intra-Community (EU B2B) supply and for claiming export VAT refunds in other member states. Non-resident ownership is admissible without local branch structures, but the UG must maintain its place of effective management (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) in Germany through a resident managing director; foreign-situs management may inadvertently trigger a German permanent establishment tax audit under Sec. 12 AO.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – the UG is a body corporate under Sec. 1(1) No. 1 KStG with unlimited resident tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) on worldwide income if its statutory seat or place of effective management is in Germany. The entity-level tax burden comprises: corporate income tax at 15%, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), i.e., 15.825% combined; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) calculated as 3.5% (Steuermesszahl) multiplied by the municipal assessment rate (Hebesatz), which ranges typically from 200% to 490%, yielding an effective trade-tax rate of about 7% to 17%; total combined entity-level burden approximates 30% to 33% on pre-tax profit. No pass-through treatment exists; the UG files separate corporate income tax returns (Körperschaftsteuererklärung), trade tax returns (Gewerbesteuererklärung), VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen and Jahreserklärung), and annual financial statements. Profit distributions to shareholders are subject to a separate 26.375% withholding cascade (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty (DTT), and the UG must perform withholding and reporting. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the UG is a body corporate under Sec. 1(1) No. 1 KStG with unlimited resident tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) on worldwide income if its statutory seat or place of effective management is in Germany. The entity-level tax burden comprises: corporate income tax at 15%, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), i.e., 15.825% combined; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) calculated as 3.5% (Steuermesszahl) multiplied by the municipal assessment rate (Hebesatz), which ranges typically from 200% to 490%, yielding an effective trade-tax rate of about 7% to 17%; total combined entity-level burden approximates 30% to 33% on pre-tax profit. No pass-through treatment exists; the UG files separate corporate income tax returns (Körperschaftsteuererklärung), trade tax returns (Gewerbesteuererklärung), VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen and Jahreserklärung), and annual financial statements. Profit distributions to shareholders are subject to a separate 26.375% withholding cascade (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty (DTT), and the UG must perform withholding and reporting. Territorial scope: unlimited worldwide taxation of income, with credit or exemption for foreign-source income under DTTs and the German Foreign Tax Act (Außensteuergesetz) in controlled transactions.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box classification system; tax status follows the civil-law form of the entity. Available elections and grouping mechanisms: (1) Tax group (Organschaft) under Sec. 14–17 KStG, Sec. 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 GewStG, and Sec. 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG – the UG may be integrated as an organ (subsidiary) into a controlling corporate or commercial parent where financial, economic, and organizational integration exists and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Gewinnabführungsvertrag) valid for at least five years is executed; a VAT group may eliminate intra-group VAT charges, and the corporate income tax group consolidates income at the parent level; (2) Small-business exemption election under Sec. 19 UStG – only available where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; not available to an EPC contractor of this magnitude; (3) One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election under Sec. 18j UStG for intra-Community distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C services, avoiding multiple EU VAT registrations; (4) Special export-VAT evidence period elections under the Export Evidence Regulation (Ausfuhrnachweisverordnung), including the B2B expedited proof procedure (Vereinfachter Nachweis) requiring the customer's USt-IdNr. and transport documentation; (5) Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 2 (Sec. 4k EStG, Sec. 8b KStG) are mandatory, not elective, and disallow deductions in dual-inclusion mismatches. The UG civil-law rule under Sec. 5a GmbHG – annual retention of 25% of profits until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000 – is a capital maintenance obligation, not a tax election, but it affects distributable reserves and thus withholding-tax planning.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 – the UG as a commercial legal entity is mandatorily registerable for VAT upon the first taxable supply, irrespective of turnover; the small-business exemption of Sec. 19 UStG (prior-year turnover of max EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover of max EUR 100,000) does not apply to a data-center EPC contractor of this scale. Practical thresholds to monitor: distance sales of goods into other EU member states trigger local registration when exceeding EUR 10,000 per member state annually (Sec. 3c UStG); intra-Community acquisitions by the UG exceed EUR 12,500 per annum before acquisition VAT accrues in Germany; B2B reverse-charge transactions have no threshold. From 1 January 2027, mandatory e-invoice issuance applies to taxpayers whose prior-year (2026) domestic B2B turnover exceeds EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 to all taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports to third countries (outside the EU customs and VAT territory) are zero-rated under Sec. 4 Nr. 1(a) in conjunction with Sec. 6 UStG, provided the UG is the exporter (supplier of the goods at the moment of export), the goods are actually transported or dispatched out of the EU by the supplier or on its behalf, and the export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) is obtained within the statutory evidentiary period – generally by the end of the third calendar month following the month of supply, extendable to 12 months with corroborating transport documents. For the EPC scope: construction, mechanical, electrical, and cooling installation services physically performed in Germany on a data-center building are supplies of services relating to immovable property under Sec. 3a(3) Nr. 1 UStG; the place of supply is Germany, the 19% VAT applies even where the data-center owner is non-resident, and no zero-rating is available. Deemed equipment-only supplies from Germany to a non-EU site (e.g., pre-fabricated cooling modules shipped CIF to the customer's country) qualify for zero-rating as export supplies, provided title passes to the non-resident customer and the movement of goods is documented. Cross-border B2B services not linked to German real property (e.g., engineering design, remote commissioning software deliverables, feasibility consultancy) are outside the scope of German VAT under the destination rule of Sec. 3a(2) UStG, with the non-resident customer declaring reverse-charge VAT in its own tax jurisdiction; the UG must obtain the customer's valid VAT registration number and business-purpose evidence. Imported equipment into Germany triggers 19% import VAT, which the UG can immediately deduct (Sec. 15 UStG) against the 19% output VAT charged on the domestic construction services; refund mechanics therefore largely offset the input VAT without requiring a separate export-refund application.

Germany has introduced a phased mandatory e-invoicing regime under Sec. 14 UStG as amended by the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025, every resident taxpayer must be able to receive structured e-invoices in an EU-standard format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x conforming to EN 16931); from 1 January 2027, taxpayers with more than EUR 800,000 prior-year turnover must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies; from 1 January 2028, all taxpayers must issue e-invoices for all B2B supplies. Transmission may be made by email, EDI, portal download, or PEPPOL network; there is no real-time tax-authority clearance, API fiscal stamping, or centralized government pre-approval in Germany (contrast Italy's Sistema di Interscambio or Hungary's RTIR), so the obligation is discharged by issuing the structured XML file (and a human-readable PDF) and archiving it for ten years under Sec. 14b UStG and GoBD. For public-sector invoices, the XRechnung standard via PEPPOL has been obligatory since 27 November 2020. From 2030, the EU VAT-in-the-Digital-Age (ViDA) package is expected to introduce transactional digital reporting based on these e-invoice datasets, with full real-time clearance obligations for cross-border B2B supplies.

German customs and the Finanzamt cross-check export zero-rating through the ATLAS exports database, EU VIES records, VAT return declarations, and annual financial statements. German exchange-control law does not impose a legally fixed foreign-currency repatriation deadline on export proceeds; however, a 180-calendar-day inward-remittance evidence window is applied as the documentary benchmark in export-finance packages, and unrepatriated proceeds beyond 12 months will empirically trigger a substance-and-reality audit of the underlying export transaction under Sec. 6(4) UStG. Where the UG fails to produce the mandated export proof within the evidentiary deadline, the supply is re-assessed at 19% output VAT, the corresponding input VAT deduction is denied, and the tax office issues an amended VAT assessment (Steuerbescheid) with late-payment interest at 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum) under Sec. 233a AO, plus compulsory late-filing surcharges (Verspätungszuschlag) up to 10% of the assessed VAT. Intentional or reckless false zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO (imprisonment from six months to ten years in aggravated cases, or fines) and negligent inaccuracies trigger fines up to EUR 50,000 under Sec. 378 AO. Retrospective assessment periods extend to ten years for deliberate evasion and five years for gross negligence (Sec. 169(2) AO), during which the tax authority may claw back VAT with interest. The UG is additionally liable under Sec. 14c UStG for VAT incorrectly shown on any invoice, and under Sec. 25d UStG for the recipient's unpaid VAT where the invoice issuer knowingly participated in an abusive arrangement; in cross-border EPC structures, the German customs and transfer-pricing audit may also verify the arm's-length price of exported equipment and the actual customs value declared in the ATLAS system.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible with no general exchange-control restrictions in Germany. Current and capital account operations are liberalized under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Section 1 AWG, subject only to EU sanctions and public-policy safeguards. A UG may freely remit profits provided corporate formalities are completed: adoption of annual financial statements, creation of the mandatory statutory reserve equal to 25% of its share capital under Section 5a GmbHG, and compliance with the capital-protection tests under Sections 19, 30 and 31 GmbHG. No central-bank approval is required before transfer; only statistical reporting obligations apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender, forced-conversion, or minimum-repatriation requirement for export receipts. Export proceeds may remain indefinitely in any freely convertible currency in domestic or foreign bank accounts. The sole mandatory rule is Bundesbank statistical reporting under Section 67 AWV: any cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 must be reported within seven days, and the bank normally files the report on the UG's behalf using the applicable AWV transaction code and counterparty details.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 15.825%) before trade tax; aggregate effective corporate burden approx. 29.8%–32.9% depending on municipal Hebesatz

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax withheld => effective 26.375%. For qualifying EU/EEA or DTA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% and satisfying substance requirements, 0% withholding applies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (§43b EStG); otherwise treaty rates range from 0% to 15%, with relief obtained through a refund procedure if the payer initially withheld the domestic rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. German TP documentation is required when the annual aggregate of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 5,000,000 for goods/production or EUR 500,000 for technical, managerial, and administrative services. A Master File is obligatory if the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000. A Local File must be maintained by any German entity whose controlled transactions exceed the above thresholds. Additional documentation applies for transfer of functions under §1 AStG.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have economic substance regulations of the OFC/ESR type. Instead, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under §42 AO and the substance-over-form doctrine apply. A UG must demonstrate effective management and control in Germany (place of effective management) to be a German tax resident; otherwise the investor faces CFC reallocation, denial of treaty benefits under LOB restrictions, and penalty taxation of 30% on artificial arrangements under §50d EStG. For data-centre EPC contracting, physical local resources (staff, equipment, project records) are indispensable in the German PE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) – underreporting of profits or fabricated expenses

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or fine (Tagesätze) under §370 AO; aggravated cases up to 10 years. Civil back-taxes plus 6% p.a. late-payment interest; monetary penalty of up to 15% of the under-declared tax for personal benefit; additional penalties for §378 negligent underdeclaration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally jointly and severally liable under §69 AO and §43 GmbHG if the UG's tax debts cannot be collected. Civil restitution of criminal gains under §73 StGB; travel bans, passport confiscation, and arrest in cases of non-payment exceeding €100k and flight risk.
Artificial Treaty Shopping / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under LOB rules; reclassification of dividends as hidden profit distributions with 26.375% WHT; penalty under §50d Abs. 3 EStG of up to 15% of the amount; retroactive corporate income tax assessment; compound interest at 6% p.a. and late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unlawful refund claims; intentional abuse leads to criminal tax evasion charges. Personal assets can be seized for restitution of WHT amounts; ancillary travel bans under §132 StPO when flight risk or concealment risk is established.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Insufficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: Revaluation of profits and TP adjustment penalty equal to 5% of the positive adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000; maximum EUR 1,000,000) under §162 Abs. 4 AO; late-payment interest at 0.15% per month; burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer; subsequent hidden profit distribution additions at 26.375% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation face criminal liability and personal fines; liability for corporate tax arrears if the UG is insolvent; civil liability to the company under §43 GmbHG for negligent management; travel restrictions in cases involving high-value adjustments.
Anti-Money Laundering / Serious Organised Crime (high-value wire transfers)

Statutory sanction: Criminal money-laundering sanction: imprisonment up to 5 years (10 years in organised-crime cases); corporate fine under §30 OWiG up to 5% of annual turnover plus disgorgement; regulatory sanction by BaFin including licence withdrawal, transaction blocking, and referral to the FIU.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for failure to file suspicious transaction reports and for implementing inadequate AML controls; imprisonment for laundering up to 5 years; travel bans and asset freezing; civil liability for losses suffered by the corporate estate and investors.
