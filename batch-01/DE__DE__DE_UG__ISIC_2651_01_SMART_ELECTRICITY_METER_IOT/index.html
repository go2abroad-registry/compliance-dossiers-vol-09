Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists for a domestic UG; the UG is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. Available elections include formation of a corporate tax and trade tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under the profit-and-loss pooling agreement, formation of a VAT fiscal unity (Umsatzsteuer-Organschaft), and, where hybrid mismatch rules apply, an election to align taxable periods or allocations under the German Implementation Act for ATAD and Sec. 4j EStG to avoid mismatch outcomes

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The general statutory registration rule: any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany is required to register for VAT; the small-business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG provides relief only where turnover in the preceding calendar year did not exceed EUR 22,000 and the current-year expectation does not exceed EUR 50,000. For foreign businesses supplying imported IoT hardware such as smart electricity meters, VAT registration is triggered when a taxable supply is made in Germany, without a separate monetary exemption threshold for the foreign applicant

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Sec. 6 UStG where the smart electricity meters are physically exported from Germany to a third-country destination such as Uganda, or to another EU Member State under intra-Community supply rules. The supplier must prove the goods left Germany/the EU and that the consignee is the acquirer; for offshore services and software-only IoT telemetry services, zero-rating may apply if the recipient of the electronically supplied service belongs in a third country and the service qualifies under the place-of-supply rules of Sec. 3a UStG

  • Electronically lodged customs export declaration in ATLAS (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument)
  • Commercial invoice with export VAT zero-rating declaration and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to the third-country buyer
  • Customs export certification from the exporting customs office
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Packing list and proof of consignment to the Uganda customer, where applicable

Domestic German B2B invoices must comply with the mandatory E-Rechnung requirements effective from 1 January 2025 under Sec. 14 UStG, requiring structured electronic formats such as XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or a standard electronic invoice XML schema; electronic transmission to the recipient and machine-readable XML content are mandatory, with no government real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no pre-clearance API; invoices must be retained in an unaltered, legible form for audit purposes

Fraudulent or improperly substantiated export zero-rating is treated as tax evasion under the German Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung): the tax office may retrospectively assess output VAT at 19% with interest of 0.5% per month from the date the VAT originally became chargeable, impose late-payment surcharges, and assert penalties under Sec. 370 AO of up to ten years' imprisonment for willful evasion; incorrect zero-rated invoices with a separate stated VAT amount may trigger liability under Sec. 14c UStG, and falsified export documentation gives rise to criminal prosecution, administrative fines, and clawback of the full unremitted VAT, generally within the standard four-year assessment period that is extended to ten years in cases of serious tax fraud

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full convertibility on both current-account and capital-account transactions, anchored by TFEU Articles 63-65 and the AWV principle of unrestricted capital movement. No prior licensing or central-bank approval applies to equity injections, dividend remittances, profit distributions, or divestment proceeds payable to non-resident shareholders. Practical friction is limited to documentary compliance: obtaining tax-residence certificates for withholding-tax treaty relief (25% German dividend withholding plus solidarity surcharge, reducible under an applicable DTA or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive) and including correct purpose codes on cross-border wires. There are no repatriation queues, no convertibility certificates, and no current-account restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency or converted freely at market rates. The sole administrative duty is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Section 67 AWV for cross-border payments (including export receipts) exceeding EUR 50,000 per non-resident counterparty per month, with the threshold raised from EUR 12,500 effective in 2023; the report is due no later than the seventh day of the month following the payment. In standard practice the account-holding bank files the payment report on the UG's behalf upon the client's written authorization, while the legal reporting duty remains with the UG as the resident corporate party.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Central Bank), operating within the Eurosystem framework headed by the European Central Bank (ECB); cross-border payment statistics and capital-movement administration are executed under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV), while export-control enforcement rests with the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is LOW for the primary treasury function and MEDIUM as an auxiliary payout channel. An EEA-authorized electronic-money institution passporting into Germany under PSD2, or a German-licensed payment institution under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), may lawfully execute payments, but it cannot accept deposits beyond e-money float, cannot grant credit, cannot hold fiduciary or escrow balances, and cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit. UK-authorized EMIs have lost the EEA passport after Brexit and would require a German branch license or an EU establishment. For this UG, an EMI is acceptable only for low-value EU operational invoice disbursements, not for inbound capital, milestone escrow collections, or export LC settlement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporation tax, producing an effective federal corporate tax burden of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and depends on the municipality; the combined corporate and trade tax burden is commonly approximately 23% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital-yield withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain exemption or refund relief under the Parent-Subsidiary regime, generally requiring at least 10% direct participation, a minimum one-year holding period for the full exemption mechanism, an eligible EU parent and satisfaction of anti-abuse and substance requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual corporation tax and trade tax returns are generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where a tax adviser is appointed, the statutory extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to temporary or special statutory rules. A non-calendar fiscal year uses the corresponding deadline measured from fiscal year-end. Tax prepayments are generally due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border controlled transactions, a Local File is generally required under German transfer-pricing documentation rules where the annual transaction volume exceeds EUR 6,000,000 for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing and royalties. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. Documentation may be requested by the tax authority and must generally be submitted within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single standalone regime labelled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is assessed through the arm's-length principle, the general anti-abuse rule under Section 42 AO, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, treaty anti-abuse provisions, principal-purpose concepts where applicable, and actual management and business functions. The UG should maintain German personnel or appropriately documented outsourced functions, premises or operational capacity where commercially required, decision-making evidence, bank and accounting controls, inventory and production records, and contemporaneous support for intra-group services, financing, intellectual property and risk allocation. A registered address without real activity, employees, control or decision-making authority is a material audit risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 AO may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. The company remains liable for unpaid tax, interest and related surcharges.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties under Section 69 AO, confiscation of proceeds, professional disqualification and civil claims. Travel restrictions may be imposed in enforcement or criminal proceedings where legally justified; imprisonment applies to convicted individuals, not the corporate entity.
Incorrect withholding tax and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: The German payer may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties. Intentional false reporting may constitute tax evasion; negligent failures may constitute tax offences or administrative violations depending on the facts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can be personally liable for withholding tax not remitted where the breach was intentional or grossly negligent. Criminal exposure can arise from knowingly fabricated treaty documentation or conduit arrangements.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, secondary adjustments, additional corporation tax and trade tax, interest, documentation penalties and possible tax-evasion prosecution may apply. Failure to provide usable documentation can permit estimation and adverse transfer-pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax losses caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties. Knowingly false Local Files, invoices or board records may support criminal liability.
Shell company and insufficient substance

Statutory sanction: Potential denial of treaty benefits, reallocation of profits, permanent-establishment findings, withholding tax assessments, Section 42 AO anti-abuse treatment, CFC consequences and penalties for inaccurate returns.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal tax liability and civil damages for deliberate or grossly negligent misuse of the entity. A sham arrangement may support criminal tax proceedings and asset-securing measures.
VAT fraud and missing VAT controls

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, repayment of input tax, interest, late-payment charges, administrative fines and tax-evasion prosecution may apply. Fraudulent invoice chains may also create criminal liability under applicable VAT provisions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable under Section 69 AO and may face criminal sanctions. Persistent VAT non-compliance can lead to enforcement, account attachment and insolvency consequences.
AML and beneficial-owner violations

Statutory sanction: Violations of the German Money Laundering Act may attract administrative fines, including enhanced fines for serious, repeated or systematic breaches and, for certain regulated entities, fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or higher statutory multiples of the economic benefit. Suspicious transactions may be reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit and accounts may be frozen.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be individually fined or prosecuted where they personally participated in or negligently permitted the breach. Money-laundering conduct can result in confiscation, asset seizure and imprisonment under the Criminal Code.
Insolvency and wrongful trading

Statutory sanction: The managing director must file without undue delay, generally no later than three weeks after illiquidity and six weeks after over-indebtedness, provided that a lawful restructuring remains possible. Delayed filing and prohibited payments may trigger criminal penalties, repayment claims and director liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may face imprisonment of up to three years or a fine for insolvency-filing violations, repayment claims for post-insolvency payments, civil damages, director disqualification and enforcement against personal assets.
UG capital and distribution violations

Statutory sanction: Unlawful distributions may require restitution, create balance-sheet and insolvency liability, and may constitute a criminal offence where assets are intentionally misappropriated or accounting records are falsified.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unlawful payments, creditor losses and tax consequences. Shareholders receiving unlawful distributions may also be required to repay them.
False accounting and document destruction

Statutory sanction: Tax estimates, denial of deductions, bookkeeping penalties, criminal accounting offences and tax-evasion charges may apply. Tax records generally require retention for periods prescribed by the German Commercial Code and tax law, with extended periods for certain accounting records.

Director personal liability: Directors and accounting personnel may face personal criminal liability, fines, imprisonment, civil restitution and confiscation. Directors can be liable for losses caused by failure to implement reasonable accounting and record-retention controls.
Unlawful cross-border payments and sanctions exposure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, customs assessments, seizure, transaction prohibitions, licence revocation and criminal prosecution may apply under German and EU sanctions, customs and export-control laws.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted personally for intentional or negligent violations, face fines or imprisonment, and become subject to asset seizure, business disqualification and travel restrictions where ordered by competent authorities.
