Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) — One-Stop Shop, Company Formation Department, in coordination with the Commercial Registry of the Ministry of Trade and Industry and the Egyptian Tax Authority.

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 as amended by Law No. 4 of 2018 (LLC formation, capital subscription, and managers' powers); Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, Chapter 2 (GAFI one-stop shop registration); Executive Regulations issued by Prime Ministerial Decree No. 1897 of 1981 as amended; Egyptian Tax Authority e-invoicing rules for newly formed companies.

Formation / constitutional filings

GAFI 'Company Establishment Request' form (Taseel) accompanied by the Incorporation Contract (Aqd Al-Taasis) and Articles of Association (Al-Nizam Al-Asasi), plus the founders' declaration; an executive summary is published in the GAFI electronic companies register.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no mandatory escrow for an LLC under Law 159/1981 as amended; founders freely set capital, which must be fully subscribed before registration, with at least 25% of each cash share paid in prior to incorporation (balance within five years); in-kind contributions are fully payable at incorporation and valued by the founders under legal liability.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (Shahadat Salamet Al-Hariq) from the General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) for the registered office; workplace clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 (Articles 202-226) requiring a health-and-safety file, fire extinguishers, exit signage, and electrical load inspection; Ministry of Manpower inspection clearance for premises above 50 m²; NASS social insurance registration for all staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no mandatory escrow for an LLC under Law 159/1981 as amended; founders freely set capital, which must be fully subscribed before registration, with at least 25% of each cash share paid in prior to incorporation (balance within five years); in-kind contributions are fully payable at incorporation and valued by the founders under legal liability.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official taxpayer portal at www.eta.gov.eg and e-invoicing/e-receipt clearance platform at invoices.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors first incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and obtain the commercial registration certificate; thereafter file the unified tax registration application (Form 1) with the ETA pursuant to the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 174 of 2022, submitting the incorporation deed, commercial register extract, registered address/lease evidence, notarized powers of attorney, and passport copies of all managers and shareholders; the ETA issues the Tax Identification Number and Tax Card; VAT registration is separately mandatory once taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 or may be applied voluntarily from EGP 100,000; the taxpayer must then onboard to the ETA e-invoice system, obtaining the company UUID, digital signing certificates, and API credentials before issuing compliant tax invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign investors first incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and obtain the commercial registration certificate; thereafter file the unified tax registration application (Form 1) with the ETA pursuant to the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 174 of 2022, submitting the incorporation deed, commercial register extract, registered address/lease evidence, notarized powers of attorney, and passport copies of all managers and shareholders; the ETA issues the Tax Identification Number and Tax Card; VAT registration is separately mandatory once taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 or may be applied voluntarily from EGP 100,000; the taxpayer must then onboard to the ETA e-invoice system, obtaining the company UUID, digital signing certificates, and API credentials before issuing compliant tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election exists under Egyptian law; the LLC is mandatorily taxed at entity level under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended, with limited elections available: (i) simplified small-business income tax regime for annual turnover below EGP 10 million; (ii) voluntary VAT registration for annual turnover between EGP 100,000 and EGP 500,000; (iii) VAT and tax incentive elections for investment zones and the Suez Canal Economic Zone; (iv) no hybrid mismatch election, with mixed-character financing instruments subject to recharacterization under the general anti-avoidance rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies (mandatory registration threshold); voluntary registration is available for annual supplies exceeding EGP 100,000; for imported electronically supplied digital services, the Egyptian resident recipient is required to self-assess VAT under reverse charge when the foreign supplier is not registered in Egypt

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods that physically leave Egypt as evidenced by customs declarations; to offshore business-to-business services rendered to non-resident customers where the economic benefit is actually consumed outside Egypt and consideration is received in freely convertible foreign currency transferred into an Egyptian bank account; and to digital remote services, including webtoon subscription and multilingual chapter-unlocking sales, supplied to non-resident subscribers, provided the supplier retains evidence of the customer's non-resident status, foreign consumption, and inward foreign-currency remittance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (bayan jumruki) validated by Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from Egypt
  • Commercial invoice expressly stating 0% VAT and bearing the ETA e-invoice QR code
  • Signed export contract or foreign purchase order identifying the non-resident buyer
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate confirming receipt of foreign-currency proceeds in an Egyptian bank account

Egypt imposes mandatory real-time fiscal clearance of B2B and B2C tax invoices through the ETA E-Invoice System; taxpayers must prepare structured XML/JSON invoices, submit them via certified ERP-to-ETA API integration or the ETA web portal, receive an ETA-validated UUID and Base64 QR code before each invoice is legally issued; the e-receipt mandate extends clearance obligations to B2C transactions; invoices lacking ETA validation are deemed legally defective and ineligible for input VAT deduction; cryptographic signing and digital stamping via the ETA-issued certificate are compulsory, and non-compliance triggers automated ETA risk flags, hard blockages, and administrative penalties.

ETA risk-based audit cross-references e-invoice data against customs export records, VAT filings, and bank FX remittance reports; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, absence of the required documentary bundle, or any false zero-rating declaration triggers retrospective clawback of the 14% VAT together with late-payment interest at double the statutory lending rate, administrative fines ranging from 30% to 100% of evaded tax and up to EGP 100,000, and criminal liability including imprisonment for tax evasion under Law No. 67 of 2016 as amplified by the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 174 of 2022.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a flexible exchange-rate regime (post-March 2024 unified float) administered by the CBE. Current-account transactions - including service royalties, intellectual property licensing, and digital webtoon syndication revenue - are fully convertible through licensed banks without pre-approval, subject to underlying contracts, commercial invoices, and CBE-mandated reporting forms. Capital-account inflows, such as foreign direct investment equity and shareholder loans, are convertible only after the receiving bank verifies the capital deposit and files an inward remittance report with the CBE; profit and dividend repatriation is permitted after the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) issues a tax clearance certificate, with dividend withholding tax of 10% for non-residents (0%-5% under applicable double taxation treaties). Practical hurdles include the available foreign-currency cover liquidity test applied by banks, documentary strictness of CBE outward remittance returns, and the prohibition of routing settlement through unlicensed fintech or virtual-asset corridors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE implementing regulations of Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020, the proceeds of invisible/service exports - including digital webtoon publishing revenue settled via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing - must be credited to a licensed Egyptian bank account within 3 months from the invoice due date (5 months for physical goods). There is no mandatory conversion ratio: 0% surrender into EGP is required, and the exporter may retain 100% of receipts in a foreign-currency retention account at its dealing bank. The dealing bank must receive the underlying platform payout statements and a signed export-proceeds undertaking; any overdue non-repatriated invoice is treated as a compliance breach carrying monetary penalties and suspension of treasury facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the exclusive foreign exchange regulator under Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020, supervising licensed dealer banks that execute all inward and outward remittances; the Egyptian Exchange operates the interbank FX matching system under CBE surveillance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-to-Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Payoneer, Wallter) cannot act as primary settlement banks because Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves electronic money issuance and cross-border payment processing to CBE-licensed entities. An offshore EMI may only serve as a downstream aggregator or pay-facilitator, with final treasury concentration into the Egyptian operational account via SWIFT MT103. The only fully compliant structure is a CBE-licensed local PSP or fintech (e.g., Paymob, Fawry) partnered with a Tier-1 bank as settlement agent; reliance on an unlicensed offshore EMI for liquidity management would trigger CBE AML notification and potential account restriction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the shareholder instigates a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for onward cover) from its home bank to the LLC's capital escrow account at a CBE-licensed Tier-1 bank. The transfer purpose is recorded under CBE balance-of-payments classification as Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Injection / Establishment of New Investment, and the beneficiary instruction must cite the Egyptian corporate tax number, the Commercial Registry extract, and the GAFI incorporation reference. On receipt, the bank issues a capital deposit certificate required for registration with GAFI and files the inward remittance report with the CBE within one working day; funds are released to the operational current account only after the commercial registry extract and initial tax card are produced. No surrender or conversion is applied to the capital account.Outbound settlement rails for the webtoon syndication treasury: (1) SWIFT MT103 credit transfer for non-documentary settlements, including foreign creator royalties, translation fees, content-delivery-network invoices, and treaty-reduced payments; (2) documentary Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600, sight or 30-90 day tenor) for physical merchandise, printed editions, and exhibition or syndication rights where goods move; (3) Bank Payment Obligation or escrow rails for milestone-based content licensing; and (4) local PSP payout rails (Paymob, Fawry) for domestic micro-payments to Egyptian creators. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 in aggregate annual controlled transactions triggers mandatory Local File and disclosure in the CIT return; a Master File is additionally required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 200,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting applies at ETA-decreed thresholds consistent with OECD guidance.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, Article 30 of Law No. 91 of 2005 empowers the ETA to disregard transactions and arrangements lacking commercial substance or actual economic implementation, and the Investment Law and Free Zones Law condition tax incentives on licensed premises, payroll, and real business activity. A webtoon IP-holding vehicle must demonstrably manage contracts, take business decisions, and bear risk in Egypt to preserve treaty benefits and free-zone exemptions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000, or the evaded tax amount if higher, pursuant to Article 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005; automatic addition of the deferred tax plus 2% monthly delay tax; inadmissibility of claimed deductions and reversal positions.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and authorized signatories who certified the return are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; criminal conviction is not limited to the corporate entity; officers face asset seizure, travel bans, and up to five years' imprisonment. Corporate veil-piercing is routinely applied against fake shareholder nominees.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Arrangements

Statutory sanction: The ETA invokes Article 30 of Law No. 91 of 2005 to disregard the arrangement, reallocate income to the Egyptian issuer, and assess full 22.5% CIT plus the 2% monthly delay tax. Transaction costs are denied; TP non-disclosure triggers an administrative penalty up to 100% of the re-determined additional tax, alongside mandatory master/local file production and CbC-related penalties.

Director personal liability: Founding directors and beneficial owners who engineered the artificial structure are personally assessable for the reinstated profits under the 'tax de facto manager' doctrine; Egyptian courts allow attachment of their personal foreign holdings; the Foreign Exchange and AML Unit may add freezing orders pending prosecution.
AML and Unlicensed Cross-Border Payment Abuse

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended by Law No. 22 of 2016): imprisonment for up to 15 years, fines up to double the laundered amount, confiscation of the digital accounts and cryptocurrency/token balances, and blacklisting of the entity from bank and PSP channels. Tax sanctions also add the 2% monthly delay tax and the 500-5,000 EGP fixed filing penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and finance managers are prosecutable as individuals for failing the beneficial-ownership and KYC protocols of payment rails; a judicial asset freeze, travel ban, and passport confiscation apply during investigation; civil restitution against individual officers compensates the treasury for the tax under-collection.
Unpaid Corporate Tax, Dividend Repatriation Non-Compliance, and Veil Piercing

Statutory sanction: Delay tax of 2% per month on the unreported WHT; fixed penalties; a discretionary penalty up to the amount of WHT evaded for repeated violation; the ETA may void the wind-up and reopen annual assessments for five years (Article 24-29 procedure); non-remitted WHT is a first-ranking treasury debt in liquidation.

Director personal liability: Under the collection arm of Law No. 91 of 2005, directors and liquidators who dispose of assets before paying tax are personally liable up to the value of assets disposed; board-level signatories face a travel ban on tax arrears exceeding the ETA threshold and imprisonment for misappropriation of withheld taxes. The corporate veil is pierced where the LLC was insolvent at the time of the dividend distribution or where capital was fictional at incorporation.
