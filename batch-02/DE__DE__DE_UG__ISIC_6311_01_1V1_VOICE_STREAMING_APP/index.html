Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register) maintained by the local court (Amtsgericht) of the registered office; filing is effected exclusively through a public notary (Notar) in electronic form pursuant to § 12 HGB

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG – Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung): §§ 1, 2, 2a, 4a, 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)), 7, 8, 10, 12, 35, 41; Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 12, 106 et seq. (electronic registration and shareholders list); Money Laundering Act (GwG) § 11 (beneficial owner declaration)

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Instrument of Constitution and Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag / Musterprotokoll nach § 2 Abs. 1a GmbHG), including the appointment of the managing director and the list of shareholders; submitted by the notary to the Commercial Register via the electronic register portal

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for the UG (haftungsbeschränkt); the Articles must stipulate a share capital of at least EUR 1.00 fully paid in cash (in-kind contributions are excluded under the model protocol, § 5a(2) GmbHG). The subscribed capital of EUR 2,000.00 must be paid into a German business payment account under the Company name and placed at the free disposal of the managing director, with bank confirmation furnished to the notary before registration. A statutory reserve of 25% of annual net profit must be accumulated until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.00 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG).

Funding & unlock path: No industry-specific civil defense or structural permit applies to an office-based software company. The Company must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act (ArbSchG), the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung), including fire extinguisher and escape route provisions of the lessor, and submit a hazard assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung). Formal fire safety and structural load certificates are covered by the landlord of the rented office space. If staff are hired, statutory accident insurance registration with the professional association (Berufsgenossenschaft) for administrative services is mandatory; no workplace used for the app service employees, however, requires a special clearance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for the UG (haftungsbeschränkt); the Articles must stipulate a share capital of at least EUR 1.00 fully paid in cash (in-kind contributions are excluded under the model protocol, § 5a(2) GmbHG). The subscribed capital of EUR 2,000.00 must be paid into a German business payment account under the Company name and placed at the free disposal of the managing director, with bank confirmation furnished to the notary before registration. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax administration is decentralized: the local Finanzamt (tax office) with jurisdiction over the UG's registered seat is the competent authority for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT returns; the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) is the competent authority for issuing the USt-IdNr (VAT ID), EU OSS filings, EORI numbers, and international information exchange; official electronic filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarial incorporation as UG (haftungsbeschränkt) and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Step 2: File the official 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' via ELSTER with the designated Finanzamt within one month of incorporation; the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer and determines registration facts. Step 3: Apply online to BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID). Step 4: For EU B2C digital services, register for the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme through BZSt. Step 5: Obtain an EORI number via eCustoms for export declarations. Non-resident owners do not create a permanent establishment solely by holding shares; all filings may be submitted directly or through a German tax advisor (Steuerberater).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG is a German corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft, GmbH variant) with unlimited tax liability (worldwide income) when statutory seat or place of effective management is in Germany; subject to 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge plus municipal trade tax (approximately 7–17%), producing an effective total income tax burden of about 30%; no pass-through or check-the-box flow-through regime is available.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the UG is inherently opaque. Available mechanisms: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under Sec. 14 KStG and Sec. 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, enabling corporate income tax and VAT pooling with a German parent; (2) Sec. 1a KStG corporate-tax option is available only to partnerships, not to a UG; (3) the small business exemption under Sec. 19 UStG may be waived by electing standard VAT taxation; (4) for intra-EU B2B supplies, the reverse-charge mechanism applies and must be accompanied by valid buyer VAT IDs; (5) hybrid mismatch restrictions under Sec. 4k EStG deny deductions to the extent that income is not symmetrically taxed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 annual turnover for the small entrepreneur exemption (Sec. 19 UStG), raised to €25,000 effective 1 January 2025; for B2C digital services to EU consumers there is no general registration threshold, but Sec. 3a Abs. 5 UStG provides a €10,000 annual intra-Community distance-sales threshold below which German VAT applies at 19%; non-resident entrepreneurs have no threshold and must register in Germany as soon as any taxable supply is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports (Sec. 4 Nr. 1a, Sec. 6 UStG) qualify for 0% German VAT when the goods physically leave the EU and the exporter holds an export declaration and proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis). For voice-streaming services: B2B supplies are localized at the customer's establishment under Sec. 3a Abs. 2 UStG and are outside German VAT, subject to reverse charge; B2C supplies to non-EU customers are localized outside the EU under Sec. 3a Abs. 1/3a UStG and are not taxable in Germany; B2C supplies to EU private consumers are taxable in the consumer's member state (via OSS or local registration) and cannot be zero-rated by the UG unless the €10,000 threshold applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung in ATLAS/AES with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (B/L or AWB) evidencing departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — replaced in Germany by bank credit advices and Deutsche Bundesbank AWV payment reports (no statutory certificate exists)
  • Apple IAP / Google Play Billing settlement statements and transaction logs evidencing merchant-of-record treatment and B2B/B2C classification
  • Electronic-services documentation: contracts, IP/geo-location logs, and Sec. 17a–17e UStDV Beleg- und Buchnachweis ledger records

Since 1 January 2025 domestic B2B suppliers must technically accept structured electronic invoices (E-Rechnungsverordnung 2024) in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1 format (Peppol BIS/SMP). Issuance obligations are phased: 2025–2027 with recipient consent; mandatory for turnover above €800,000 from 1 January 2028; mandatory for all taxpayers from 1 January 2029. There is no real-time fiscal clearance system, no digital stamping, and no tax-authority pre-approval of invoices; public-sector invoices are submitted via Peppol/XRechnung. ELSTER is the portal for VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and OSS returns.

Germany imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline or exchange controls, but an export proceeds retention period of 180 days is the accepted documentary standard; cross-border payments above €12,500 per month must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days for statistical purposes (AWV). Deficient export documentation triggers recapture of zero-rated VAT, assessment corrections under Secs. 164/165 AO, interest at 6% per annum under Sec. 233a AO, and late-payment surcharges of 1% per month under Sec. 240 AO. Tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment (up to 10 years in aggravated cases), fines, and full retrospective clawback; fraudulent export invoices additionally trigger liability of the invoice recipient under Sec. 25d UStG and criminal exposure under Sec. 26a/26b UStG. The VAT assessment limitation period is 5 years, extended to 10 years for evasion, and OSS/EU digital transactions are subject to data-driven enforcement by BZSt and joint audit groups.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted capital and current account convertibility pursuant to EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63–65 TFEU) and German foreign trade law (AWG). Euro capital flows are unlimited; non-resident shareholders may freely transfer dividends, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds. Only collection of statistical balance-of-payments data applies, no prior approval or exchange control exemption is required for profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in any currency or held offshore. A cross-border external economic reporting duty to Deutsche Bundesbank applies to export payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction or aggregated within a calendar month; such reports must be submitted electronically within five working days after the value date (AWV reporting).

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the national central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, operating within the European System of Central Banks and under the euro-area legal framework).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

European Economic Area (EEA) licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) are viable and may passport into Germany under PSD2 with full payment/treasury functionality. Offshore EMIs outside the EEA, including UK post-Brexit, are not permitted to serve German-domiciled businesses without a licensed German branch or an EEA-authorised agent; viability is Low for offshore EMIs and High for EU/EEA-regulated EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital contributions or shareholder loans must be routed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's personal account (or a corporate account in the same beneficial ownership chain) to the UG's blocked capital account or a specifically designated operational account. The remittance information must reference the notarial contribution resolution (Rückzahlung or Einlage) and must include the purpose code mandated by Deutsche Bundesbank's AWV external reporting schedule (e.g., 'D.10 – Direct investment and direct investment transactions'). Acceptable verification includes the notarised contribution deed, shareholder resolution, proof of initial capital payment (Einzahlungsbeleg), and a no-objection letter from the notary. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the dividend withholding amount, producing an effective statutory withholding burden of 26.375% on gross dividends paid by the UG to non-resident shareholders (§§ 43, 43a, 50 EStG). A 0% rate applies under § 43b EStG to qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the UG's capital for at least 12 months (EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive). Treaty rates vary by jurisdiction (e.g., 5% for qualifying direct holdings and 15% otherwise under the Germany-US DTA, Art. 10).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany: Local File documentation under § 90(3) AO is mandatory for all cross-border business transactions with related parties exceeding EUR 100,000 for goods deliveries and EUR 50,000 for non-good (service) transactions in a given tax year; the Master File (Stammdokumentation) is required where the worldwide group's consolidated revenues exceed EUR 750 million, with the UG being obligated to cooperate in producing it. Documentation must be submitted within 60 days of a tax office demand; transaction documentation must be contemporaneous.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the UAE/Caribbean but enforces equivalent substance requirements: (i) § 50d(3) EStG and BFH case law deny treaty relief and CFC immunity to foreign shareholders lacking real economic activity, staff and premises; (ii) § 42 AO (abuse of law) recharacterizes artificial or antedated contracts (e.g., appraisals for IP royalties) in accordance with economic reality; (iii) §§ 7-13 AStG CFC rules attribute passive income of a foreign controlled company to the German UG; (iv) the interest restriction rule under § 4h EStG limits net interest expense deductibility to 30% of EBITDA. A voice streaming UG must demonstrate commercial substance in Germany (employees, servers, marketing decisions, board minutes, separate bank accounts, proper transfer pricing documentation for any IP or service flows with group companies).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years or fines under § 370 AO; aggravated cases (commercial or large-scale evasion) up to 10 years' imprisonment; mandatory restitution of all evaded taxes, trade tax and solidarity surcharge plus default interest of 6% p.a.; forfeiture of the criminal profits (§ 73 StGB); entry in business and tax records; withdrawal of permits; fines under § 370(3) AO can be the equivalent of the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable as the persons responsible for tax accounting and filing (§ 34 AO, § 69 AO). They face: criminal prosecution with personal imprisonment; civil liability for tax losses vis-à-vis the tax office (§ 69 AO); liability for payments made after balance-sheet insolvency (§ 64 GmbHG); the managing director can be stripped of management rights and receive a travel ban or passport seizure (§§ 39, 326 AO). Restitution orders for unpaid taxes and interest are directly enforceable against individual assets.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA withholding relief under § 50d(3) EStG; re-computation of tax as if the abusive arrangement had not been concluded (§ 42 AO) with hidden profit distribution adjustments (§ 8(3) KStG); additional 15% withholding on any payments recharacterized as dividend; 6% interest on arrears; penalties up to 25% of the re-assessed tax; for cross-border round-tripping, classification as money laundering and serious tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly establish or maintain shell structures are personally liable under § 43 GmbHG for damage to the company, jointly liable for tax adjustments, subject to criminal tax fraud sanctions (up to 10 years) and risk being disqualified from serving as a managing director (professional ban). Personal creditor-claim exposure arises where the UG is found to be the alter ego; insolvency courts may impose restitution against the director personally for distributions out of capital (§ 31 GmbHG).
AML / Money Laundering (Geldwäschegesetz violations)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under § 56 GwG up to EUR 5 million or, for commercial operating entities, up to 10% of the annual turnover of the German legal entity; imprisonment for money laundering under § 261 StGB (up to 5 years; up to 10 years for professional/gang laundering); seizure and confiscation of funds; prohibition of operations by BaFin if the unlicensed payment activity is detected.

Director personal liability: Directors face: criminal proceedings under § 261 StGB with personal imprisonment; civil liability to the UG for failure to implement compliance systems under § 43 GmbHG; prohibition orders and travel bans; exclusion from management functions; insolvency-connected liability if AML fines render the UG insolvent and the director continued payments in insolvency (§ 64 GmbHG).
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Disclosure Penalties

Statutory sanction: Surcharge under § 162(3) and (4) AO: late submission of documentation attracts a surcharge of 6% of the resulting tax adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 1,000,000); if documentation is permanently missing or materially unfit, the tax authorities are entitled to draw adverse inferences and to apply presumption-based income adjustments pursuant to § 162(1) AO.

Director personal liability: The managing director bears the organizational duty to ensure documentation is prepared (§ 90(3) AO); his liability is direct if he fails to respond to the tax office's query. Sanctions include: personal fines, criminal proceedings for aiding tax evasion if the documentation was manipulated, liability for interest and penalties with the UG's assets, and in insolvency scenarios restoration claims by creditors and the insolvency administrator for the aggravated tax burden.
