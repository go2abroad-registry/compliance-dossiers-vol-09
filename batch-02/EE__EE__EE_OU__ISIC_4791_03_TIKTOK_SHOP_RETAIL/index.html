Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register maintained by the Registration Department of Tartu County Court and administered electronically through the e-Business Register portal.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), provisions governing private limited companies (osaühing), including §§ 135–148, together with the Commercial Register Act (Äriregistri seadus).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for entry of the private limited company in the Commercial Register, accompanied by the memorandum of association, articles of association, founder and management board details, registered office, and confirmation of share-capital contribution arrangements.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Estonia has no statutory aggregate minimum share-capital requirement for an OÜ. The articles must specify the share capital and share nominal values, and each share must be covered by a contribution in accordance with the Commercial Code. Immediate payment is generally required unless a legally permitted deferred-contribution arrangement is selected; no mandatory escrow account is imposed, although a bank account may be used to evidence monetary contributions.

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial clearance is required for a virtual retail business with no premises operated by the company. A company-operated warehouse or office must comply with applicable building and occupancy requirements, fire-safety rules supervised by the Estonian Rescue Board (Päästeamet), structural and electrical requirements, and occupational health and safety duties supervised by the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon).

Paid-in capital rule: Estonia has no statutory aggregate minimum share-capital requirement for an OÜ. The articles must specify the share capital and share nominal values, and each share must be covered by a contribution in accordance with the Commercial Code. Immediate payment is generally required unless a legally permitted deferred-contribution arrangement is selected; no mandatory escrow account is imposed, although a bank account may be used to evidence monetary contributions.

The chair is authorized to complete all incorporation filings.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ordinary online retail of lawful consumer products. Consumer and product-compliance supervision is principally conducted by the Consumer Protection and Technical Regulatory Authority (Tarbijakaitse ja Tehnilise Järelevalve Amet). Product-specific permits may be required for regulated goods.

Category: No general manufacturing or industrial operating licence is required for ordinary social-commerce and online retail activity. Separate approvals may apply to food, alcohol, tobacco, medicines, cosmetics, chemicals, financial products, or other regulated goods.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-MTA portal (e-mta.emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) electronically through the Estonian e-Business Register (e-äriregister). The company is automatically assigned an 8-digit Estonian Registry Code, which functions as the corporate ID and income tax registration number. VAT registration is effectuated by filing a standard application with the MTA through the e-MTA portal; the MTA then issues a separate VAT identification number (KMKR number). Non-resident companies without a local PE may register directly for VAT; in certain high-risk or non-EEA situations the MTA may require a local fiscal representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign investors incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) electronically through the Estonian e-Business Register (e-äriregister). The company is automatically assigned an 8-digit Estonian Registry Code, which functions as the corporate ID and income tax registration number. VAT registration is effectuated by filing a standard application with the MTA through the e-MTA portal; the MTA then issues a separate VAT identification number (KMKR number). Non-resident companies without a local PE may register directly for VAT; in certain high-risk or non-EEA situations the MTA may require a local fiscal representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box classification election exists; an OÜ is per se a corporate income tax taxpayer. Available statutory options include: (i) voluntary VAT group membership (käibemaksugrupp) for closely linked taxpayers; (ii) application of the reduced 14% CIT rate on regular profit distributions from taxed prior-period retained profits; and (iii) election of distribution timing (tax is triggered only upon profit distribution). Hybrid mismatch and reverse-hybrid rules under ATAD may apply, requiring any elective structuring to be assessed against Estonian domestic implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover for resident taxable persons; non-resident persons are generally required to register for VAT from their first taxable supply in Estonia, unless the supply falls under the Union OSS/IOSS special schemes; intra-Community distance sales of goods to Estonia may aggregate into the €10,000 EU-wide distance-selling threshold for EU-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU are zero-rated under the Estonian VAT Act when the seller transfers the right to dispose of the goods to the buyer and the goods are physically transported outside the EU within the statutory time limit. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number and subject to EC Sales List reporting are equally zero-rated. Certain services involving third-country customers, such as export-related ancillary services, services connected with immovable property outside Estonia, and cross-border services qualifying under the place-of-supply rules, are also eligible for the 0% rate. The taxpayer must hold positive documentary proof confirming the reality of the export or intra-EU movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / e-customs declaration) with the movement reference number (MRN) and successful electronic release from the Estonian Tax and Customs Board
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, AWB, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical dispatch from Estonian/EU territory
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods, the non-EU or EU customer, and the delivery terms (Incoterms)
  • Proof of payment or an Inward Remittance Certificate demonstrating receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Customer's VAT identification number and, for intra-EU supplies, a valid registration confirmation in VIES / EC Sales List

Estonia does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance mandate, and no government pre-clearance or digital stamping of invoices is imposed. E-invoicing is promoted through the Estonian e-Invoice standard, and public-sector entities are required to accept e-invoices. VAT returns and transaction-level data are filed electronically via the e-MTA portal, with API-based XML submission available for accredited software; continuous transaction controls are expected to evolve under the EU VAT in the Digital Age proposals but are not yet binding.

The MTA carries out post-export substantiation audits and may require production of all transport, payment, and customs records. If adequate export evidence is not present or the goods do not leave the EU within the legally permitted period, the zero rate is retrospectively reversed and VAT at 22% is assessed on the transaction value. Interest accrues at 0.06% per day until payment; additional late-payment surcharges, penalty fines, and criminal sanctions (up to imprisonment of three years) can be imposed for fraudulent export zero-rating or false declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and operates within the European Union single market and SEPA. The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general capital-control regime, no general approval requirement for foreign ownership, and no mandatory repatriation or conversion of foreign-currency receipts. Dividend and other profit distributions by an Estonian OÜ are generally not subject to a withholding tax under Estonian domestic law, subject to applicable anti-avoidance rules, treaty limitations, transfer-pricing requirements, withholding obligations for specific payment categories, and the Estonian distributed-profit tax regime. Banks may require evidence of source of funds, transaction purpose, beneficial ownership, tax residency, commercial substance, and supporting corporate approvals before processing unusual or high-value transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed deadline for converting or repatriating export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank contractual terms, sanctions screening, payment-services controls, accounting requirements, VAT and customs documentation, and applicable anti-money-laundering obligations. For cross-border goods transactions, the company should retain orders, invoices, platform settlement statements, customs records where applicable, shipping evidence, returns documentation, and reconciliation of gross sales, commissions, taxes, refunds, chargebacks, and net receipts.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia). Estonia has no separate foreign-exchange administration. Banking and payment-service supervision is conducted principally by the Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority), with anti-money-laundering supervision of relevant obliged entities conducted under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, SEPA transfers, multi-currency balances, and supplier payments, but not a substitute for a domestic bank account in every use case. Wise Europe SA and comparable providers are regulated payment institutions or electronic-money institutions rather than universal banks; customer funds are safeguarded rather than protected by ordinary bank-deposit insurance in the same manner as eligible bank deposits. Availability of local account details, TikTok Shop payout support, merchant-acquirer connectivity, chargeback handling, foreign-currency balances, and high-volume settlements depends on provider policy and jurisdiction. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services into the European Economic Area without the required authorisation, passporting, or local establishment. The OÜ must complete full corporate KYC, disclose beneficial owners and controllers, explain the social-commerce model, and maintain evidence supporting platform-originated receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions through a bank account held in the OÜ's legal name, preferably by SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments or by SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-SEPA currencies. Identify the payment as share capital, additional equity contribution, or shareholder loan, as applicable; use the correct corporate documentation and accounting treatment; and avoid unexplained third-party remitters. For a new OÜ, follow the Estonian Commercial Register and bank procedures for depositing share capital where the incorporation method requires evidence of payment. For shareholder loans, retain the executed loan agreement, maturity, interest, repayment terms, transfer-pricing support, lender ownership evidence, and source-of-funds documentation. Banks may request transaction-purpose information and supporting documents; Estonia does not generally use a universal statutory foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary commercial transfers. The company should nevertheless provide accurate remittance information, invoice or agreement references, and any bank-specific purpose classification.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers and beneficiaries. Use SWIFT cross-border transfers for non-SEPA payments, normally through the bank's ISO 20022 customer-credit-transfer channel; MT103 remains a commonly recognised legacy reference for a single-customer credit transfer, but message formats and availability depend on bank migration and correspondent arrangements. Use commercial letters of credit where supplier, country, documentary, or counterparty risk justifies them, with issuance or confirmation by an acceptable bank and documentary compliance under UCP 600; use documentary collections under URC 522 where appropriate. Match each payment to a purchase order, commercial invoice, shipping or fulfilment evidence, customs documentation where relevant, and sanctions screening. The OÜ should operate dual approval, beneficiary-change verification, segregation of duties, payment limits, daily liquidity reporting, and reconciliation of platform settlements, refunds, chargebacks, taxes, logistics costs, and supplier payments.Deliverable euro/foreign-currency forwards with an authorised bank or regulated investment firm, documented under the provider's master agreement and used to hedge identifiable commercial exposures.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies with convertibility or settlement restrictions, subject to the counterparty's regulatory permissions, local-market rules, applicable sanctions, collateral terms, and bank suitability assessment.Currency swaps or cross-currency swaps for longer-dated funding or recurring currency exposures, subject to counterparty credit approval, documented commercial purpose, valuation, collateral, and applicable EMIR reporting, clearing, risk-mitigation, and trading obligations.Spot foreign-exchange transactions and permitted options or option structures may be used where the authorised provider approves them and the company understands premium, valuation, liquidity, and close-out risks. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed retained profits; 20% on gross profit distributions (equivalently 20/80 of net distribution)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% domestic corporate income tax on gross dividends (20/80 of net); no separate shareholder-level WHT for resident and most non-resident shareholders; DTA-reduced to 5%/15% for qualifying beneficial owners and 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% holding, 1-year holding period)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

10th day of the month following the monthly tax period in which a taxable distribution/payment was made; calendar-year taxpayers with no monthly taxable events must file a zero-annual declaration by 30 April of the following year

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 annual aggregate controlled transactions with related non-residents for Local File; Master File and CbCR apply when group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000

Economic substance scope: Estonia is not an offshore regime; no formal Economic Substance Regulations are enacted, but EU ATAD (interest limitation, CFC, anti-abuse) and the Estonian general anti-avoidance rule require real business substance and key entrepreneurial functions/EIF in Estonia to access treaty benefits and avoid recharacterization of cross-border payments

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment up to 100% of underpaid tax, late interest 0.06% per day, a fine under the Taxation Act, and criminal fine or imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 7 years for aggravated commercial scale)

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for damages to the company; unpaid tax can be collected from personal assets if the board deliberately caused the non-payment through unlawful management acts; criminal conviction triggers disqualification and travel-ban enforcement
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under §146 of the Taxation Act (GAAR) and ATAD CFC rules; tax is re-imposed on the distributed profit as if the Estonia company had realized it; 20% distribution tax plus delayed interest and 20–60% GAAR penalty

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced where the OÜ was used for tax evasion; directors are liable to the extent of transferred assets; for repeat offenses, entry ban/Estonian travel ban and up to 5 years imprisonment
AML / Money Laundering through Social Commerce

Statutory sanction: AML penalties up to EUR 400,000 for the entity and up to EUR 80,000 for individuals under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; non-declaration of BO causes a cap of registration rights and per-diem fines

Director personal liability: Compliance officers, AML officers, and board members face criminal liability for intentional failure to report; the court may impose a travel ban, asset seizure, and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Money Laundering Act
