An OÜ may be registered with share capital fixed in the articles of association, including an amount below the default statutory €2,500, provided the board later manages capital in accordance with the Commercial Code's solvency rules. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet), e-MTA portal (e-maksuamet.ee / emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity may establish an Osaühing (OÜ) online through the e-Residency programme and register in the Commercial Register to obtain the Estonian registry code (business ID). The VAT ID (KMKR number) is obtained after compulsory VAT registration with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board, typically effected via e-MTA once taxable supplies exceed the VAT threshold or the entity voluntarily registers. A non-resident director and shareholder may file the registration digitally; a local contact may be required for operational compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial Deferral on Distributions

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; corporate income tax applies at the entity level only on distributions, deemed profit distributions, fringe benefits, gifts, and non-business expenses, with 0% tax on retained and reinvested profits. Standard distribution tax rate is 20/80 of net distribution (effectively 20% of gross), reducible to 14/86 for regular or 'more favourable' distributions if certain technical alternative is used. No hybrid mismatch election beyond EU ATAD-compliant statutory measures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable supplies in Estonia, beyond which VAT registration is compulsory; non-resident suppliers may also be liable for registration in respect of specific distance sales or B2C digital services according to EU One Stop Shop rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of wholesale polyethylene and polypropylene granules to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated where the supplier retains proof that the goods left Estonia within either the physical dispatch period or the mandatory time limit, generally by the end of the month following the supply or a longer administrative period, and the customer is the exporter. Intra-EU supplies of goods to a VAT-registered business customer are zero-rated when the purchaser's valid EU VAT ID is reported in VIES and transport to the other EU member state is evidenced. Related international services such as freight, transport, or incidental handling connected directly with export zero-rating are also eligible to the extent supported by the required documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD / EXA form through the Estonian customs system)
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight) or CMR consignment note (for road transport) or air waybill (for air freight)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit confirmation showing payment for the exported goods
  • Intra-EU EC Sales List and customer's valid VAT identification number (for intra-Community zero-rating)
  • Proof of the supplier's export or transport instruction and customs location or terminal receipt

Tax returns, VAT declarations, and EC Sales Lists are filed electronically through the e-MTA system. Estonia does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, cryptographic stamping, or continuous transaction controls for B2B invoices. E-invoicing operates under the EU e-invoicing framework and the Estonian B2B e-invoice infrastructure (e.g., e-invoice operators), but paper or PDF invoices remain legally acceptable unless contractually varied. Export invoices must be issued within the statutory period and transmitted to the customer; the tax authority may require electronic submission of listings and periodic summaries.

Export zero-rating is audited against the actual physical movement of goods and the exporter must retain all export evidence for at least seven years. Where documentary proof is missing, forged, or inconsistent, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board retrospectively claws back the zero-rated VAT, charges output VAT at 22% plus default interest (0.06% per day), and may apply a tax fraud penalty. Repeated or intentional violations may be prosecuted under the Penal Code, with fines up to EUR 200,000 for legal persons and criminal liability for responsible officers, including imprisonment up to five years for customs or tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full member of the euro area; the euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There is no Estonian Foreign Exchange Act, no exchange-control licensing regime, and no restriction on the transfer of capital, dividends, interest, or trade payments across the Estonian border. The only binding constraints are (i) European Union financial sanctions and targeted restrictive measures, (ii) Estonian AML/CFT reporting duties, and (iii) central bank balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is not subject to currency convertibility approval, but it is subject to the Estonian distributed-profits corporate income tax regime: regular distributions incur tax at the rate calculated on the net amount distributed (20/80 effective gross-up; reduced 14/86 rate may apply for certain regular distributions). Foreign shareholders may receive dividends in EUR or any freely convertible currency without separate central bank authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonian law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, repatriation, or domestic holding requirements on export proceeds received for wholesale distribution of petrochemical polymers. Export receivables may be collected in EUR, USD, or any convertible currency and may be held in any SEPA or SWIFT-connected account worldwide. Banking practice requires proper invoice and contract documentation for source-of-funds verification, but no statutory remittance timeline or conversion ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the national central bank and member of the Eurosystem, operating in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB). The Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) holds parallel regulatory competence for AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High for ancillary treasury operations, Low as the sole banking backbone. Estonian and EU-licensed fintech e-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut digital accounts passporting into Estonia) can provide multicurrency IBANs, fast EUR/USD conversion, and SEPA-compatible disbursement rails. However, for bulk petrochemical polymer wholesale distribution, the commercial model involves sizable trade flows, documentary letters of credit, and enhanced due-diligence scrutiny; most EMIs do not support SWIFT MT700 issuance, commercial LC reimbursement, or third-party trade settlement at the required volume. An EMI is viable as a secondary liquidity sweeper or for low-value incidental payments, but a full Tier-1 credit institution must remain the primary clearing counterparty. Estonian FSA licensing requires an EMI to safeguard client funds, but does not confer the balance-sheet strength or correspondent banking network required for large polymer trades.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are processed through standard SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit transfers to the OÜ's Estonian IBAN account maintained with a Tier-1 Estonian bank. No central bank permit or foreign-exchange approval is required. The payment reference and bank transfer details must indicate the legal basis of the transfer, preferably as 'Equity contribution per articles of association' or 'Shareholder loan facility'. The Estonian bank will complete balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Eesti Pank, and the transaction will be classified under the ECB balance-of-payments item for direct investment or other capital flows. To avoid delays, the relationship manager should receive advance notification, a board resolution, and a signed shareholder contribution agreement. For share capital subscribed before registration, the funds may be held in a temporary payment account or bank escrow account; for post-registration injections, the funds are credited directly to the operational account.Outbound trade settlements for polymer purchases are executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for open-account and cash-against-documents transactions; (ii) documentary letters of credit issued as SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707, governed by ICC UCP 600, with negotiating and confirming banks; (iii) SWIFT MT202 for interbank cover and reimbursement; and (iv) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for euro-denominated payments within the European Economic Area. USD-denominated polymer trades require USD correspondent banking chains and generally benefit from a commercial LC or LC discounting facility. All payment instructions must include the corresponding LC reference, commercial invoice number, Bill of Lading reference, and Incoterms. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of distributed profits, economically calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution. Retained and reinvested profits are generally taxed at 0% until distribution or another taxable profit-distribution event.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% dividend withholding tax for distributions by an Estonian resident company under the post-2024 regime. The Estonian company generally pays corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net dividend. Special recipient categories, transitional rules, anti-avoidance rules, and non-dividend payments must be reviewed separately.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory detailed local-file documentation generally applies to an Estonian resident company meeting at least two of the following group-size criteria: at least 250 employees, annual turnover exceeding EUR 50 million, or balance-sheet total exceeding EUR 43 million. A master file is generally relevant to an Estonian entity belonging to a multinational enterprise group meeting the applicable large-group criteria, including the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold for country-by-country reporting. Documentation can also be required for controlled transactions below these thresholds where the Tax and Customs Board requests support or the transaction is material or high-risk.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a single stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant through the general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership standards, permanent-establishment rules, controlled foreign company rules, EU anti-abuse principles, and the requirement that expenses and distributions have a genuine business purpose. A petrochemical trading OÜ should maintain real decision-making evidence, commercial contracts, inventory and logistics records, pricing authority, bank controls, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, and documentation showing why Estonia is the location of the relevant functions and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, statutory interest generally calculated daily, recovery costs, administrative or misdemeanour penalties, and criminal prosecution for intentional large-scale tax evasion. Criminal sanctions may include a fine or imprisonment for the responsible individual and a criminal fine for the legal person, subject to the Penal Code and the facts of the case.

Director personal liability: A director may incur personal tax liability where the company cannot pay and the director deliberately or through gross negligence breaches tax obligations under the Taxation Act. Criminal conviction may result in imprisonment, confiscation, prohibition from acting as a management-board member, and court-ordered compensation. Travel restrictions are exceptional judicial measures and are not automatic consequences of an assessment.
Undeclared profit distribution and private benefit

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax at 22/78 of the taxable net distribution or applicable fringe-benefit and employment taxes, together with interest, reassessment, and potential penalties for inaccurate reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the company, shareholders, creditors, or the tax authority for unlawful distributions, breach of fiduciary duties, insolvency-related misconduct, or grossly negligent tax non-compliance. Criminal liability may arise where concealment or fraud is intentional.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to arm's-length income or expense, possible secondary tax consequences, corporate income tax on a deemed distribution where relevant, interest, documentation penalties, and audit escalation. The burden of substantiating the pricing increases where the counterparty is in a low-tax jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors can be liable for deliberate or grossly negligent tax loss, false records, or breach of management duties. Lack of a formal local file does not by itself prove evasion, but fabricated comparables or knowingly non-commercial pricing materially increases civil and criminal risk.
Customs, excise, and import-value evasion

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duty, import VAT, interest, administrative penalties, seizure or forfeiture of goods, withdrawal of customs simplifications, and criminal prosecution for deliberate large-scale customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and customs representatives may be liable for deliberate or grossly negligent declarations, document falsification, or unpaid customs debt. Imprisonment and confiscation require criminal proceedings and a qualifying offence; travel bans are court-ordered rather than automatic.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-owner concealment

Statutory sanction: Enhanced due-diligence requirements, transaction refusal, reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit, supervisory fines, possible loss of banking facilities, and criminal sanctions for money laundering or deliberate facilitation. Criminal fines and imprisonment depend on the offence, value, intent, and aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or reckless failure where the statutory offence is satisfied. Civil recovery and confiscation may extend to proceeds and instrumentalities. A travel ban may be imposed by judicial order in criminal proceedings.
International sanctions and restricted-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, asset freezing, administrative or criminal sanctions under applicable Estonian, European Union, and United Nations sanctions legislation, cancellation of permits, confiscation, and substantial financial penalties. The applicable penalty depends on the specific sanctions regime and culpability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for intentional or negligent sanctions breaches, false declarations, or circumvention. Criminal proceedings can result in fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and management disqualification; travel restrictions require lawful judicial authorization.
Shell-company and permanent-establishment abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, transfer-pricing adjustment, reallocation of profits to the actual operating jurisdiction, Estonian or foreign permanent-establishment taxation, interest, penalties, and possible beneficial-owner or anti-abuse enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for misleading records and tax losses and criminal liability where documents or declarations are knowingly falsified. A lack of Estonian staff is not automatically unlawful, but fictitious substance and sham management materially increase enforcement risk.
Insolvency and unlawful asset extraction

Statutory sanction: Reversal of transactions, creditor claims, damages, insolvency proceedings, tax recovery, management disqualification, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent bankruptcy or unlawful asset dissipation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to compensate creditors or the estate and may face criminal fines or imprisonment where insolvency offences are established. Courts may restrict management activities and, where legally justified, impose procedural travel restrictions.
Accounting and record-retention failure

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment based on available information, denial of expense support, interest, administrative penalties, audit escalation, and possible criminal liability for intentional falsification or destruction of records.

Director personal liability: The management board is responsible for lawful accounting and financial reporting. Directors may be personally liable for resulting damage, tax loss, or creditor prejudice where the failure was intentional or grossly negligent.
