Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); electronic portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet) at https://e-mta.maksuamet.ee; English entry point: https://www.emta.ee/en

E-government filing portal

An Osaühing (OÜ) obtains its national tax registration number (Commercial Register registry code, which functions as the TIN) automatically upon incorporation in the e-Business Register, with no separate income-tax registration step; VAT payer registration is applied for in the VAT register through the e-MTA portal when calendar-year Estonian taxable turnover reaches EUR 40,000, or voluntarily from the first transaction, or immediately when a non-resident or distance-selling obligation arises (OSS/IOSS); the application is filed by the management board member or a duly authorised tax representative, generally takes 3 business days to process, and there is no minimum capital precondition for an OÜ; the statutory retention period for tax and accounting documentation is 7 years.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An Osaühing (OÜ) obtains its national tax registration number (Commercial Register registry code, which functions as the TIN) automatically upon incorporation in the e-Business Register, with no separate income-tax registration step; VAT payer registration is applied for in the VAT register through the e-MTA portal when calendar-year Estonian taxable turnover reaches EUR 40,000, or voluntarily from the first transaction, or immediately when a non-resident or distance-selling obligation arises (OSS/IOSS); the application is filed by the management board member or a duly authorised tax representative, generally takes 3 business days to process, and there is no minimum capital precondition for an OÜ; the statutory retention period for tax and accounting documentation is 7 years.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies a distribution-based corporate income tax: undistributed profits are taxed at 0% at the OÜ level and distributed profits are taxed at the entity level at 20/80 (20% effective), with an elective reduced rate of 14/86 (14% effective) for regular profit distributions meeting the statutory regularity conditions; no check-the-box or similar entity classification election exists under Estonian law; available elective mechanisms include voluntary VAT registration below threshold, EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) registration for cross-border and import distance sales, VAT refund procedures under Directive 2008/9/EC and the 13th Directive for non-established traders, and the ATAD-conformant hybrid-mismatch transparency rules applicable to cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of Estonian taxable turnover, excluding exports, intra-EU supplies and supplies declared under OSS/IOSS; the intra-EU distance-sales threshold applicable to an Estonian based e-commerce seller is EUR 10,000 per calendar year across the EU (OSS); import IOSS covers consignments up to EUR 150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods outside the European Union is zero-rated under Section 15(2) of the Estonian VAT Act where the goods are dispatched or transported from Estonia to a third country by the seller or on the seller's behalf and the seller possesses corroborative export evidence; intra-EU B2B dispatches to valid VAT-identified customers are also zero-rated, with EC sales list reporting; for cross-border e-commerce the MTA requires evidence that the buyer and delivery are located abroad (customer identity, foreign billing/consignment address, foreign payment instrument, shipment tracking, destination country customs release, and absence of any domestic use or posting within Estonia) and that connected ancillary services are supplied as a single economic export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/ECS record and MRN number) showing the goods exited the EU from Estonia
  • Transport documentation proving physical movement out of the EU (CMR consignment note, bill of lading, air waybill, or courier delivery proof with final destination confirmation)
  • Commercial invoice, order/contract, and any pro-forma or tax invoice referencing the export zero rate
  • Inward payment or external consideration evidence (SWIFT/IBAN credit advice, Stripe/PayPal settlement reports, or PSP payout statements) in support of the foreign-currency export price
  • Consignee and delivery verification files (delivery receipt, POD signature, customs release notices, and customer identity/VAT number where applicable)

Estonia does not operate a general real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for e-commerce B2B/B2C invoices; mandatory structured e-invoicing is imposed for suppliers of the public sector (PEPPOL BIS 3.0/EN 16931), while payment-side transaction data, PSP settlement files, bank account feeds, and cash register fiscalisation rules for physical point-of-sale retail provide the MTA with real-time or automated audit visibility; no API-based pre-approval or invoice certification is required.

The MTA audits export zero-rating claims by cross-checking customs exit messages (ECS), banking and PSP payment data, logistics/tracking histories, and EU administrative cooperation (Regulation (EU) No 904/2010) and may request the exporter to produce the full transaction file; where export evidence is insufficient or the goods did not actually leave the EU, the MTA retrospectively claws back the uncharged VAT by reassessing the supply at 22%, charging late-payment interest at 0.06% per day, and imposing procedural or supplementary administrative penalties under the Taxation Act; the general tax assessment period is 3 years from the end of the tax period, extended to 7 years in cases of knowingly false data or non-declaration; deliberate fraudulent zero-rating is indictable as tax fraud under the Penal Code, with fines, imprisonment of up to 5 years for aggravated offences, and confiscation of the resulting tax advantage.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia adopted the euro (EUR) on 1 January 2011. Both current-account transactions (e-commerce receivables, trade payments, platform settlements) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, dividends, intercompany loans, profit repatriation) are fully liberalized under Article 63 TFEU free movement of capital. There are no exchange controls, licensing permits, conversion certificates, or repatriation restrictions; no distinction is applied between resident and non-resident for the free use and conversion of EUR or other currencies. The only administrative obligation is the Eesti Pank balance-of-payments statistical reporting of individual cross-border payments exceeding the prevailing threshold of EUR 12,500 per transaction, filed by the resident bank on behalf of the account holder. Profit remittance by an OÜ to a non-resident shareholder attracts no Estonian withholding tax; corporate income tax at 20% on gross distributions (equal to 20/80 of net dividend) applies only at the distributing OÜ level, with a reduced 14% rate for regular dividend distributions from taxed retained profits. No hurdles exist for the outward remittance of e-commerce earnings in USD or other currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonian and European monetary law imposes no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds or online-sales settlement revenues. A resident OÜ may retain foreign-currency receipts in any EU/EEA or third-country account, may invoice in USD, CNY (offshore CNH), or any convertible currency, and is not obliged to convert incoming funds into EUR. For balance-of-payments statistics, resident enterprises must ensure that the account-holding bank reports individual cross-border payment flows above the Eesti Pank threshold (EUR 12,500 per transaction); reporting is informational and does not constitute a repatriation or surrender control.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) operates as the national central bank within the Eurosystem/ECB monetary framework. Prudential supervision of significant credit institutions is shared with the ECB under the Single Supervisory Mechanism; for non-significant banks, the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) is the licensing and conduct authority. AML/CFT supervision of banks is exercised by Finantsinspektsioon, with the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) handling suspicious transaction analysis.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility as secondary liquidity rails for receivables concentration and low-value supplier disbursements, but Medium-to-Low as the sole or primary treasury account. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions (authorized under the Estonian Payment Institutions and E-Money Institutions Act, Maksu- ja makseasutuste seadus) may open IBAN accounts by remote KYC using e-Residency or Smart-ID without physical presence, and can provide SWIFT/SEPA corridors for the e-commerce model. Legal constraints: (i) EMI funds are not covered by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme Act; (ii) high transaction velocity with Stripe/PayPal settlement flows triggers enhanced due diligence under the Estonian AML Act (RahaPTS); (iii) if the EMI is not passported in the EEA or relies on a third-party program manager, the Estonian-resident OÜ retains regulatory exposure to an unlicensed ledger; (iv) sanctions/OFAC-adjacent risk screening must be contractually guaranteed by the EMI. Recommended deployment: sweep target after Stripe/PayPal payout, plus an operational float for 3–5 days of working capital; the capital escrow and statutory ledger positions must remain in a supervised tier-1 Estonian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a new OÜ with no mandatory minimum capital (share capital may be EUR 1 since 2011), inbound equity funding is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's personal or corporate account to the OÜ's EUR operational account, using purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity-Related) in Field 70 or Field 77B; for EUR funding from within the EEA, a SEPA Credit Transfer with the same purpose code is acceptable. Where the single payment exceeds EUR 12,500, the recipient bank includes it in the Eesti Pank balance-of-payments report on behalf of the entity. The compliance verification bundle comprises: (i) signed shareholders' resolution specifying the cash contribution; (ii) bank SWIFT reference confirming value date; (iii) updated e-Äriregister extract showing the share-capital position (if the articles specify a nominal amount above EUR 1); and (iv) initial balance held in the operational account until the Stripe/PayPal merchant risk approval is obtained. No escrow arrangement or central-bank permit is required, and the injected capital is drawable immediately after account activation.Primary supplier payment rail: SWIFT MT103 or MT103 GPI over the correspondent banking network (typically via Commerzbank, Deutsche Bank, or an EU/US hub), with purpose codes in Field 70 categorized as 'TRAD' for commercial trade of goods, 'SCIR' for service supplier invoices, or 'SUPP' for general supplier obligations. For confirmed large purchase orders of maternity commodities exceeding approximately EUR 50,000, an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit is recommended, issued via SWIFT MT700 with presentation of commercial invoice, packing list, and pre-shipment inspection certificate from the supplier's factory. For intra-EU suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer are available with full ISO 20022 purpose-code transparency. For Chinese suppliers, the recommended settlement mechanism is CNH clearing through Hong Kong with the OÜ invoicing in EUR or USD, avoiding offshore NDF conversion on the CNY leg. All outbound disbursements must be reconciled against Stripe/PayPal settlement exports on a rolling T+3 basis; if monthly outward value exceeds EUR 150,000, the treasury must maintain a two-bank backup structure (primary: LHV; contingency: Swedbank or SEB) to mitigate correspondent-bank downtime.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/GBP FX Forwards (deliverable, tenor 1–12 months) supported by underlying commercial contracts; no current-account conversion restriction applies in Estonia.Offshore CNH/USD or EUR/CNH Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for hedging supplier costs invoiced in CNY, conditional on documented underlying trade transactions, supplier contracts, and shipping schedules.Cross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD XCS) for long-dated capital operations or term debt exceeding EUR 500,000, transacted under an ISDA 2002 master agreement with an Estonian or EU-licensed bank as the arranged counterparty.Vanilla FX Options (plain call/put and simple spreads) to protect a predefined conversion threshold on the USD-denominated settlement pool, subject to EMIR clearing and margin rules when traded with a financial counterparty.Internal Netting and Multi-Currency Liquidity Sweep between EUR and USD sub-ledgers to cash-match receivables and payables, avoiding speculative open positions on the unhedged residual treasury exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with each related party requiring a transfer pricing Local File; Master File required for a group with consolidated annual revenue exceeding EUR 50 million

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in offshore financial centres, but under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and domestic general anti-avoidance rules, the company must demonstrate real substance (office, staff, management operations) to obtain treaty benefits and to prevent recharacterization of retained profits as deemed dividends. The beneficial ownership requirement must be satisfied for any DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Undisclosed Distributions

Statutory sanction: Additional 20% corporate income tax on concealed distributions, a 50% fraud penalty on the underpaid tax, daily default interest of 0.06%, and criminal fines under the Estonian Penal Code if the amount exceeds EUR 20,000

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable with the company for underpaid tax; travel restriction orders are possible; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment up to seven years.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Profits may be deemed constructive dividends and taxed at 20% for the entire period; penalties for failure to file the beneficial ownership register (up to EUR 6,000); denial of EU treaty benefits to the circulated shell structure

Director personal liability: Directors and nominal shareholders can be held personally liable for the company's tax debts; the court may impose a business ban of up to 10 years, and criminal sanctions for corruption of the corporate form.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breach in E-commerce

Statutory sanction: AML fines up to EUR 5 million for legal persons under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; freezing of company bank and payment processing accounts; exclusion from e-Residency benefits

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may face criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to eight years; personal confiscation of illicit assets and restriction on engaging in commercial activities.
Failure to File Mandatory Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of up to EUR 2,000 per unfiled form; daily late payment interest of 0.06% on unpaid tax; tax authority may escalate to forced collection and revocation of tax location registration

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the tax arrears if the failure is found to be intentional or negligent; personal assets can be seized, and directors may be barred from acting as board members in any Estonian legal entity for up to five years.
