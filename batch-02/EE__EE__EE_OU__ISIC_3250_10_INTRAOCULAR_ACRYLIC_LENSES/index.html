Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-services portal e-MTA (https://e-mta.emta.ee); public guidance at https://www.emta.ee

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign founders (including e-Residents) incorporate the Osaühing electronically via the e-Business Registry (e-äriregister); the issued 8-digit registry code (registrikood) constitutes the permanent corporate TIN. Step 2: For VAT, submit the registration application through e-MTA immediately upon exceeding, or expecting to exceed, the €40,000 per-year taxable turnover threshold, or voluntarily before the threshold; the MTA assigns the VAT registration number (format EE + 9 digits) and enters the company in the taxable-persons register. Step 3: Non-EU suppliers without an establishment in Estonia or elsewhere in the EU must appoint an Estonian fiscal representative (maksuesindaja) for VAT; EU-established persons and resident OÜs apply directly. Step 4: Medical device licensure with the Estonian Health Board (Terviseamet) under EU MDR 2017/745 and ISO 13485 is a regulatory prerequisite but not a precondition to tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification elections; the OÜ is statutorily an entity-level taxpayer under the deferred profit-distribution system, and ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply mandatorily. Available tax elections: (i) voluntary VAT registration before crossing the €40,000 taxability threshold; (ii) VAT group treatment (single taxable person) for closely related persons upon application to the MTA; (iii) application of the reduced 14/86 rate to 'regular' profit distributions satisfying the Income Tax Act section 50 continuity test; (iv) registration in the EU One-Stop-Shop for distance sales of goods and B2C digital services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 per calendar year (EUR); compulsory registration from the day after the threshold is exceeded. Non-established suppliers must register from their first taxable supply in Estonia regardless of turnover. Separate EU distance-sales threshold of €10,000 per year applies to cross-border B2C supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT applies to intraocular acrylic lenses physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by the exporter of record, provided the goods leave the EU and the supplier holds contemporaneous documentary proof under Article 46 of EU Implementing Regulation No. 282/2011. Intra-EU B2B dispatches to a VAT-registered customer are also zero-rated with a valid EU VAT ID and the recapitulative statement (ECS). Offshore B2B services (contract lens finishing, engineering, or technical services for non-EU customers) are outside Estonian VAT scope as the place of supply is the customer's country. Monthly VAT refunds of excess input credits are available; the MTA may extend the statutory 30-day refund term where export documentation is incomplete.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX/SAD) bearing the accepted MRN issued by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board or another EU customs office
  • Transport document evidencing departure from the EU (CMR, Bill of Lading, or Air Waybill)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the exported intraocular lens batches
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT payment confirmation or letter-of-credit settlement evidence
  • EC sale listing (ECS) reference and customer's valid EU VAT identification number for intra-EU dispatches

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level government approval applies to B2B invoices. VAT-registered persons must file monthly electronic VAT returns (KMD) with the transaction-level annex (KMD INF) via e-MTA by the 20th day of the following month. Invoices to Estonian public-sector entities must be structured e-invoices (Peppol-compatible); voluntary B2B e-invoicing in XML/e-arve format is accepted. The MTA performs risk-based cross-matching of KMD INF partner data; no fiscal printers or physical seals are used.

Estonian law imposes no express statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is used as a prudent treasury and transfer-pricing control against deferred-receivable risk. Where export zero-rating is claimed without adequate proof, the MTA retroactively assesses standard-rate VAT (currently 22%; 20% for periods prior to 1 July 2024) plus default interest of 0.06% per calendar day. Under the Tax Procedure Act, false or misleading export documentation triggers an additional tax increase of up to 30% of the assessed tax, and intentional evasion of significant amounts is a criminal offence punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment. The MTA applies elevated scrutiny to high-value medical device exports, including verification of counterparty substance, banking records, and the physical customs export trail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia, as an EU member state, maintains full capital account convertibility with no restrictions on cross-border capital movements. Current account transactions, including profit remittances to non-resident shareholders, are likewise fully convertible under EU free movement rules with no regulatory hurdles or prior approval requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratios apply to export receipts in Estonia. Companies may retain export proceeds in foreign currency accounts without surrender. Conversion to local currency is required only for VAT declaration purposes or in non-EU trade settlements exceeding applicable thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasible under EU PSD2 and AMLD5 frameworks with regulated fintech providers offering corporate accounts. Offshore digital EMIs are permitted subject to strict KYC/AML verification and may be constrained for full liquidity management or correspondent banking access.
