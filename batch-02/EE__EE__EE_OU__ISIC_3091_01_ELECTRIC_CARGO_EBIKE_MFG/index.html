Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For an OÜ engaged in ISIC 3091 manufacturing, a virtual/registered-office service address is permissible as the legal seat for registry and tax correspondence, but it cannot replace a physical operational establishment in Estonia for commercial-substance purposes. The Estonian Tax and Customs Board requires a real Estonian business premises for claiming manufacturing input VAT, customs authorization, excise status, or certain SME subsidies. A mere virtual mailbox without staff, production equipment, or stock will not satisfy the place-of-effective-management test for tax residence. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet; MTA), including the e-MTA electronic tax portal at https://www.emta.ee/en and the Estonian Tax and Customs Board customs systems.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ obtains its legal entity registry code through the Estonian Business Register administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems. The registry code is generally used as the entity identifier for tax administration. The company must obtain e-MTA access through the management board, an authorised representative, or a certified service provider, and must register relevant tax obligations, payroll accounts, customs activities, and VAT status with the MTA. A foreign company without an Estonian registered entity may apply to the MTA for non-resident tax registration and a VAT identification number by submitting the prescribed application, constitutional and registration documents, proof of business activity, identification of representatives and beneficial owners, and evidence supporting the Estonian taxable activity. The MTA may request additional information and may require a tax representative or Estonian contact arrangements where applicable. An Estonian VAT identification number normally has the format EE followed by eight digits. VAT registration is separate from incorporation and is not automatically granted merely because an OÜ has been entered in the Business Register.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no general election to treat an OÜ as fiscally transparent. An OÜ is a separate legal person. Corporate income tax is generally imposed when profit is distributed or otherwise deemed distributed, rather than when accounting profit is earned or retained. Retained and reinvested profits are generally not subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the time of retention. Estonian resident companies may be affected by EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive hybrid-mismatch, interest-limitation, controlled-foreign-company and anti-abuse rules, but these are statutory qualification rules and not elective entity-classification regimes. A company may apply for VAT registration, VAT group treatment where statutory conditions are satisfied, and relevant customs authorisations; these elections do not change its corporate tax identity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply with the place of supply in Estonia during a calendar year. The threshold applies to the statutory taxable-turnover calculation and does not eliminate registration obligations arising from specific cross-border transactions, intra-Community acquisitions, imports, distance sales, or other special rules. The standard VAT rate is 24% from 1 July 2025; supplies made before that date may be subject to the rate applicable at the relevant tax point.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the statutory export conditions are satisfied and the exporter retains evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. The exporter should hold the commercial invoice, transport evidence, customs export declaration and electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs proof. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-identified customer in another EU Member State may also qualify for 0% VAT, subject to valid customer VAT identification, transport to another Member State, recapitulative reporting and supporting transport evidence. Services are not automatically export zero-rated. Services supplied to non-EU business customers may be outside the scope of Estonian VAT under place-of-supply rules, while services physically connected with Estonia or otherwise subject to Estonian place-of-supply rules may remain taxable. Input VAT may be recovered through the periodic VAT return to the extent incurred for taxable or zero-rated business activities and supported by valid VAT invoices and customs documents.

  • Commercial export invoice identifying the exporter, customer, goods, value, delivery terms and VAT treatment
  • Electronic export customs declaration accepted by customs
  • MRN or equivalent customs reference and evidence of exit from the European Union customs territory
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, CMR consignment note, air waybill, courier record or equivalent proof of dispatch
  • Packing list and product description sufficient to reconcile the goods with the customs declaration
  • Purchase order, sales contract or other commercial evidence of the export transaction
  • Evidence of receipt or delivery where relevant to the transport chain
  • Import customs documentation or import clearance evidence in the destination country where available
  • Proof of payment or bank records where requested for audit substantiation; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a statutory Estonian export zero-rating document
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification evidence, transport proof to another Member State and required EC Sales List reporting

Estonia does not generally require real-time pre-clearance or government clearance of ordinary domestic B2B invoices, and there is no universal continuous transaction control model for all taxpayers. VAT invoices may be issued electronically if the recipient accepts the electronic format and the invoice satisfies statutory authenticity, integrity and content requirements. E-invoice exchange is used extensively through certified operators and is required or operationally expected for specified public-sector transactions. VAT returns and related schedules are filed electronically through e-MTA, generally by the 20th day of the month following the taxable period. Customs declarations and export formalities are submitted electronically through the Estonian customs systems and the EU customs infrastructure. No general fiscal digital-stamping requirement applies to ordinary VAT invoices.

Estonia does not impose a general mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate to substantiate export zero-rating. If the exporter cannot prove that goods left the EU or otherwise fails the statutory conditions, the MTA may assess the unpaid VAT at the applicable rate, recover incorrectly refunded or deducted input VAT, impose statutory interest, and apply administrative penalties. Interest accrues on late tax liabilities at the statutory rate applicable during the relevant period. False invoices, fabricated customs evidence, deliberate misstatements or abusive VAT refund claims may result in fines for the taxpayer or responsible persons and, where the statutory elements and thresholds are met, criminal liability for tax fraud or other economic offences. The taxpayer must retain accounting, invoice, customs and transport records for the statutory retention period and must cooperate with MTA audits.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a Eurozone member (EUR adopted 1 January 2011). The euro is fully freely convertible with zero restrictions on both current-account transactions (trade in goods/services, royalties, dividends, interest) and capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, equity loans, real estate), in full compliance with Articles 63-65 TFEU. No exchange-control licences, no convertibility approvals, no residency-based limits, and no mandatory reporting of individual FX conversions apply. Profit remittance is entirely unrestricted; the only fiscal cost imposed at the source is the Estonian distributed-profit tax (20/80 gross-up, or 14/86 for regular recurring distributions), which is withheld and paid by the Estonian entity to the Tax and Customs Board (MTA). Offshore accounts of Estonian residents are permitted, provided all statutory accounting and tax-recognition rules are met and any statistical reporting requested by Eesti Pank for balance-of-payments purposes is submitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None in force. Estonia imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no compulsory conversion window, no repatriation timeline, and no minimum retention of proceeds in EUR. Export receipts may be held offshore in any currency, netted against import payables, or applied as trade-collateral under EU free-movement-of-capital safeguards. The only mandatory obligations are accounting recognition of revenue under Estonian GAAP/IFRS at the accrual date and application of arm's-length transfer-pricing rules if the receivables are due from related parties.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia); monetary policy and payment-system oversight delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Eurosystem, with prudential supervision shared with the European Banking Authority (EBA) and the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate-to-high for operational settlement accounts and retail collections, but materially lower for escrow, trade-finance, and treasury hedging functions. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Europe, Revolut Payments, Monese, Paysera) and payment institutions offer rapid remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, and SEPA/SWIFT-compatible output; however, under the Estonian Credit Institutions Act and PSD2, EMIs are not licensed credit institutions and therefore cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit (UCP600), cannot provide commercial loans or overdrafts, cannot act as issuing bank for banker's guarantees, and are widely treated as non-bank counterparties by institutional suppliers in the e-bike component supply chain. Recommended deployment: daily opex flows, low-value supplier invoices, customer card collections, and FX conversion below EUR 50,000 per transaction. All LC issuances, large inbound equity injections, and ISDA-documented hedging must be executed through a Tier-1 bank counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended rail: SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA injections or SEPA Credit Transfer (PACS.008) for EEA/EUR injections, credited to a EUR-denominated operational or share-capital account held with a Tier-1 Estonian bank. Payment reference must clearly state the legal purpose, e.g., 'SHARE CAPITAL INCREASE' or 'UNRESTRICTED SHAREHOLDER CONTRIBUTION (vaba omaniku sissemakse)' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN', accompanied by a board resolution of the parent entity and, where applicable, the Estonian e-Business Registry filing entry. As OÜ minimum share capital is zero, equity may alternatively be introduced as a contribution without a share-capital increase, recorded in equity reserves under Estonian GAAP. The receiving bank will request: (i) the underlying shareholders' resolution; (ii) source-of-funds evidence (six-month bank statement of the remitting entity); (iii) UBO declarations; and (iv) confirmation of the transaction for statistical/FIU purposes where required. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed/retained profits; 20% on gross profit distributions (calculated as 20/80 of the net distribution, equivalent to 25% of net dividend).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% profit-distribution tax imposed on the distributing OÜ; no separate gross-basis withholding tax is deducted from the non-resident shareholder. The effective burden is 20% of gross dividend / 25% of net cash dividend. Qualifying EU parent companies may obtain 0% source tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.
