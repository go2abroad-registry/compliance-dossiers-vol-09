Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) has no fixed statutory minimum capital, but the fully-paid cash share capital must be at least EUR 1 per share and every allotted share must be paid in full in cash before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited under Section 5a(2) GmbHG, and no escrow account is required if the paid capital is held at the free disposal of the managing director on a German bank account. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office, Bonn; locally competent Finanzamt for ongoing assessment of corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; statutory filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); VAT-ID, OSS/IOSS and non-established taxpayer registration: BZSt Online Portal (www.bzst.de); customs/export processing via ATLAS under the Generalzolldirektion (German Customs).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the UG(haftungsbeschränkt) articles and file with the commercial register (Handelsregister); Step 2: Register the trade business with the local Gewerbeamt and obtain the trade license; Step 3: File the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, submitted digitally via ELSTER; Step 4: Obtain the Steuernummer (federal tax number) and assigned VAT file plus corporate income tax/trade tax files from the Finanzamt; Step 5: Apply to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) required for intra-Community supplies and EU B2B invoicing; Step 6: Obtain an EORI number (prefix DE) from the Generalzolldirektion for customs export declarations; Step 7: Maintain a domestic bank account for export proceeds and corporate cash flows; non-resident licensors without a German establishment must register for German VAT directly with BZSt/the competent Finanzamt upon the first taxable supply in Germany and obtain a German VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with separate legal personality and is not a pass-through entity. It is subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) under §1 KStG: 15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, plus municipal trade tax at effective rates of approximately 7%–17%. Distributions to shareholders are separately taxed at shareholder level. No German territorial election is available and no tax-exempt zone applies to a resident UG; foreign-source royalties from film copyright licensing are included in taxable income with credit relief under applicable double tax treaties and §34c EStG.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime; entity classification follows mandatory civil-law characterization of the UG as a corporation. Available elections and regimes: (i) Organschaft fiscal unity for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT under §§14–19 KStG and §2(2) No.2 UStG, subject to profit-and-loss pooling and economic/financial/organizational integration; (ii) trade tax loss pooling and intra-group offset within a fiscal unity; (iii) Kleinunternehmer small-business VAT exemption election under §19 UStG if prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed €50,000 — generally unavailable for an active film licensing UG; (iv) mandatory anti-hybrid and reverse-hybrid adjustments under §4k EStG and §8b KStG for cross-border license structures (not elective); (v) no election to apply territorial taxation — worldwide taxation is the statutory default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€22,000 prior-year turnover and €50,000 current-year turnover for the §19 UStG Kleinunternehmer exemption; €10,000 EU-wide intra-Community distance-sales threshold for OSS registration; non-established companies must register for German VAT from the first taxable supply in Germany without any monetary threshold; the resident UG is registered regardless of these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to third-country destinations are zero-rated under §4 No.1a and §6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave Germany/EU territory and the supplier provides export confirmation (Ausfuhrnachweis) in accordance with §§8–13 UStDV. For international film copyright licensing, the place-of-supply rule in §3a UStG governs: a B2B license of intellectual property is deemed supplied where the customer is established; where the German UG grants distribution/licensing rights to a non-resident business customer, German VAT is outside the scope of charge, and zero-rating documentation is not required for the licensing fee itself. B2C digital film services to EU consumers must be declared under the OSS at the consumer's country VAT rate. Tangible media (e.g., physical masters or distribution carriers) shipped to non-EU territories require export proof and zero-rating support. The UG must retain the licensee's valid VAT ID, executed license agreement, usage/royalty reports and payment evidence to substantiate outside-scope or zero-rated treatment.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) transmitted electronically via ATLAS
  • Export accompanying document / electronic administrative document
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice describing the exported media and license terms
  • Export confirmation (Ausfuhrnachweis) prepared per §§8–13 UStDV
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank credit evidence for the license fees

Germany introduced a mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) regime: all established businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices from 1 January 2025; mandatory issuance for domestic B2B supplies applies from 1 January 2027 for enterprises with 2026 turnover exceeding €800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other enterprises. Accepted formats: XRechnung (UBL/UN/CEFACT) and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X; exchange may be conducted via Peppol or other compliant transmission channels; no real-time fiscal clearance, national e-invoice stamping or centralized government clearance process exists in Germany. Export declarations are transmitted electronically via ATLAS; OSS/IOSS returns are filed through the BZSt portal; invoice data and source documents must be retained in accordance with GoBD and made available to the Finanzamt on audit.

Finanzamt VAT audits (Umsatzsteuer-Sonderprüfung) scrutinize zero-rating and outside-scope positions; missing or late export evidence triggers reassessment at 19% VAT with monthly late-payment interest of 0.5% under §233a AO and a late-payment surcharge. Fraudulent or grossly negligent claims constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, with retrospective clawback for up to 10 years under §169 AO, fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 10 years in aggravated cases); negligent understatement is fined under §378 AO; managing directors face personal joint liability. Under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV §67), export-currency receivables must be collected within 12 months of export; the 180-day pipeline benchmark is the internal compliance review threshold, and late collection triggers Bundesbank reporting duties and, for reporting violations, fines of up to €30,000 under the AWG/AWV penalty framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU free movement of capital (TFEU Articles 63-65). No restrictions on profit remittance for a UG, provided the formalities of the German Commercial Code are observed. Profit distributions are subject to the solvency test under the Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG Section 30/31) and withholding taxes, but there are no FX permits, prior approval, or conversion requirements for legal profit transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds or other foreign currency receipts in Germany. However, any payment received by a German resident from a non-resident above EUR 12,500 (or the equivalent) must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border statistics under Section 67 AWV, normally via the ZKA payment reporting interface, within the applicable deadline; failure to report can cause administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank; competent authority for foreign exchange monitoring under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) and responsible for external sector statistics and capital account reporting)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Medium as a supplementary receiving and payment rail, Low as a primary clearing solution. Fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business) are legally permitted under PSD2/EMD as payment institutions or e-money institutions and can hold client funds, but they are not credit institutions and must not be treated as full treasury clearing banks. German corporate KYC and AWV reporting obligations require a domestic relationship with a supervised account-holding bank for documentary LC issuance and SEPA direct membership. Use EMIs only for small-value royalty collections and multi-currency receipt concentration, while all significant intercompany capital and trade flows must be cleared through a full bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The shareholder's inbound capital contribution must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA instant for EUR), annotated with purpose code 'K' (capital contribution) and the commercial register number of the UG. For amounts exceeding EUR 12,500, the UG must trigger a cross-border payment report to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV Section 67. Prior to assembly, the full capital contribution is credited to a bank account in the name of the UG or to an attorney escrow account. A certificate of no objection is required from the bank for the Commercial Register when the contribution is made in cash, unless no minimum capital is required for a UG; nevertheless, a bank receipt proving availability is mandatory for the notary.For the payment of license royalties and rights acquisition: (i) EUR payments within the EEA are cleared via SEPA CT (MT103 is still the reference message format when FX involved); (ii) non-EEA USD or GBP payments are cleared by SWIFT MT103 with a correctly documented purpose code; and (iii) when a commercial letter of credit is required for the purchase of film distribution rights, the bank should issue an irrevocable LC under UCP 600 (MT700) and settle by MT103 upon presentation of conforming documents. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, plus municipal trade tax generally producing an aggregate effective burden of approximately 29% to 33%, depending on the municipal assessment rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital income withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%, before exemptions, refunds, or treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, the annual return is generally due by 31 July of the following year. Where prepared by an authorized tax adviser, the extended deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions and tax authority requests. A fiscal year differing from the calendar year is measured from its fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual accounts must generally be prepared within three months for a large or medium-sized company and may be prepared within six months for a small company if consistent with orderly business practice. Publication or deposit is generally required within twelve months after the financial year-end; failure may lead to administrative fines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, German documentation obligations generally apply where the annual transaction volume with a related foreign party exceeds EUR 6,000,000 for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, and licensing. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year, subject to the applicable German documentation rules. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and produced promptly upon request; extraordinary transactions and restructurings require enhanced documentation.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separate general Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore ESR systems. Substance is nevertheless material under the German anti-abuse rule in section 42 of the Fiscal Code, treaty beneficial-ownership standards, section 50d(3) anti-treaty-shopping rules, controlled foreign company rules, transfer-pricing law, permanent-establishment analysis, and general anti-avoidance principles. A film-rights licensing entity should maintain real decision-making, qualified personnel or demonstrable outsourced capability, operational control over rights, commercially credible agreements, accounting records, bank activity, risk assumption, and evidence of actual exploitation and licensing functions. Mere contractual ownership, conduit payment flows, circular financing, or back-to-back sublicensing may be disregarded or denied relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code may result in a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, late-payment charges, and penalties remain recoverable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for culpable breach of tax duties, civil restitution, asset seizure, professional disqualification, and restrictions on holding office. Travel restrictions may be imposed only under applicable enforcement or criminal-procedure conditions; they are not automatic.
Incorrect or negligent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax understatement by negligence may constitute an administrative offense under the Fiscal Code and can lead to fines, corrective assessments, interest, late-payment charges, tax audits, and loss of procedural credibility.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where their culpable conduct caused tax loss or failure to withhold and remit tax. The company remains liable for the underlying tax and accessories.
Withholding tax failure

Statutory sanction: The UG may be assessed for the tax that should have been withheld, together with interest and penalties. Intentional failure may constitute tax evasion or aiding tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can incur personal liability under the Fiscal Code for failure to satisfy withholding obligations when payment priorities and liquidity permitted compliance. Criminal exposure may arise for deliberate concealment.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The Finanzamt may make income adjustments, impose additional corporate and trade tax, deny deductions, apply withholding tax, assess interest, and impose documentation-related penalties. Material documentation failures can attract substantial surcharges, commonly up to 10% of the income adjustment, subject to statutory minimums and maximums.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, tax-liability claims, insolvency-related liability, and criminal exposure where pricing was intentionally manipulated or records were falsified.
Shell company, treaty shopping, or conduit arrangement

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU withholding relief may be denied, the payment may be reassessed at domestic rates, and tax, interest, penalties, and anti-abuse consequences may apply under sections 42 and 50d(3) of the Fiscal Code and Income Tax Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may face tax recovery, civil restitution, criminal liability for intentional assistance, disqualification, and insolvency-related claims. A corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but personal liability may arise from statutory breach or unlawful conduct.
Money laundering and AML failure

Statutory sanction: Money laundering may result in fines or imprisonment, generally up to five years and in aggravated cases up to ten years. AML breaches can produce supervisory orders, administrative fines, reporting failures, and account restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, asset confiscation, civil claims, and management disqualification. Travel restrictions may apply in criminal proceedings where legally ordered.
Beneficial-ownership and transparency-register violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines may be imposed under the German AML framework, with higher exposure for intentional, repeated, or systematic violations. Banks and counterparties may suspend onboarding or payments.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are responsible for organizational compliance and may face personal administrative fines or liability for deliberate or negligent failures.
Permanent-establishment or place-of-management misstatement

Statutory sanction: Germany may assert corporate income tax, trade tax, withholding tax, payroll tax, and possibly VAT obligations, together with interest, penalties, audit adjustments, and dual-residence treaty proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid payroll or withholding taxes, negligent tax reporting, and insolvency losses caused by non-compliance.
Unauthorized profit distribution and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The payment may be reclassified as a hidden profit distribution, denied as a deduction, subjected to dividend withholding tax, and treated as a breach of capital-maintenance or insolvency rules.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may owe restitution to the UG, face shareholder and insolvency-administrator claims, and incur personal liability for unlawful payments, particularly after insolvency maturity.
Insolvency and delayed filing

Statutory sanction: Insolvency offenses may result in fines or imprisonment, repayment of prohibited payments, tax liability, director disqualification, and claims by the insolvency estate.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal restitution, creditor compensation, criminal prosecution, asset seizure, and restrictions on acting as managing director. Travel bans are possible only where ordered under applicable criminal or enforcement law.
Copyright and rights-chain misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil injunctions, damages, account of profits, contract termination, seizure or destruction of infringing materials, criminal copyright sanctions in serious cases, and tax reassessment may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for deliberate infringement, fraudulent representations, unlawful distributions, and losses caused by contracting without reasonable rights verification.
