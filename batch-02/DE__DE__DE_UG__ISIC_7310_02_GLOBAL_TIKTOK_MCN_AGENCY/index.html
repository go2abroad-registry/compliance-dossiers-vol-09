Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German Kapitalgesellschaft with independent legal personality. It is always tax-opaque in Germany and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Because its registered seat and/or place of management is in Germany, it has unlimited (worldwide) tax liability as a resident corporation from the date of registration. No tax-exempt-zone status applies. The UG remains subject to Körperschaftsteuer, Gewerbesteuer, and Umsatzsteuer regardless of the foreign residence of its shareholders.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists for a German UG; it is per se a corporation for German tax purposes. Available elections and mechanisms: (a) Small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung) if prior-year turnover does not exceed the statutory ceiling and current-year turnover is forecast below the ceiling; (b) cash-basis VAT accounting (Ist-Versteuerung) under § 20 UStG for qualifying small businesses; (c) tax group/fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§ 14 ff. KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, requiring a German corporate controlling shareholder, financial integration, and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag); (d) election by a US parent to treat the German UG as a disregarded entity under US check-the-box, which creates a hybrid entity that German anti-hybrid rules (§ 4k EStG) and the German Anti-Tax-Haven Act (Steueroasen-Abwehrgesetz) largely neutralize. There is no election to be classified as a partnership under German law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de-minimis registration threshold for a domestic UG: VAT registration is mandatory from commencement of an economic activity (§ 18 UStG). The only statutory turnover-based relief is the § 19 UStG small-business exemption, which operates in lieu of registration where prior-year turnover is at most EUR 22,000 (2024) / EUR 25,000 (2025) and current-year forecast turnover is at most EUR 50,000 (2024) / EUR 100,000 (2025); once the ceiling is exceeded, full VAT registration, invoicing, and reporting obligations arise. For cross-border intra-EU B2C digital and remote services, the OSS special scheme starts when intra-EU distance sales and remote services exceed EUR 10,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a German UG, two distinct mechanisms apply. First, physical goods exported from the EU are VAT-exempt with input VAT credit ('steuerfrei mit Vorsteuerabzug') under §§ 4 Nr. 1a, 6, and 13 UStG, which is functionally a 0% rate evidenced by customs documentation. Second, for MCN/talent-agency and creator-management services, the cross-border rule is not a zero rating but an 'outside the scope' characterization: under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG, B2B services supplied to a customer whose business seat is in another EU member state are subject to the customer-country reverse charge (no German VAT), and services supplied to a non-EU business customer are generally outside the scope of German VAT. The UG must prove (a) the customer's business status, (b) the customer's business seat and VAT registration number where applicable, and (c) the actual performance of the service. B2C services supplied by the UG to foreign end consumers remain German-taxable at 19% unless a specific statutory exception applies; MCN agency services do not fall under the § 3a Abs. 4 or Abs. 5 exceptions, so distance-to-consumer services do not qualify for zero rating. If the UG also sells branded merchandise, the goods export exemption applies and requires the § 9 UStDV export proof.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/electronic export declaration in ATLAS) and the resulting export proof under § 9 UStDV; required only for physical goods exports, with the alternative of a carrier/forwarder confirmation (Sammelausfuhrnachweis)
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (B/L), CMR waybill, or airway bill evidencing departure of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice containing the statutory VAT-exemption reference and, for services, a reverse-charge or no- VAT statement with the customer's valid VAT registration number
  • Buch- und Belegnachweis (books-and-records evidence) under § 17a UStDV for services: supply contract, performance documentation, and bank statements evidencing receipt of consideration
  • Inward remittance evidence: German VAT law does not mandate a formal 'inward remittance certificate'; the payment receipt functions as corroborating evidence of the exporter's claim and supports transfer-pricing documentation, but is not a statutory precondition for zero rating

Germany has not implemented real-time fiscal clearance, API tax stamping, or an Italian-style SDI authorization system. The German e-invoicing reform, implementing EU ViDA, is phased: from 1 January 2025, every domestic B2B supplier must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (European standard EN 16931, formats XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.x, EDI accepted); from 1 January 2027, suppliers with turnover above EUR 800,000 in the preceding year (2026) must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices. B2C invoicing remains unrestricted, but all invoices, including e-invoices without government clearance, must be archived immutably, completely, and readably for ten years under GoBD principles. For the UG as an exporter of services, the e-invoice must bear the reverse-charge notice for EU B2B customers and, for non-EU B2B customers, a statement that the place of supply is the customer's business seat.

Germany subjects export and cross-border service claims to close audit review, including EU-level data exchange and Eurofisc scrutiny. Where zero rating or outside-the-scope treatment is claimed without adequate proof, the Finanzamt retroactively assesses 19% German VAT plus interest of 0.5% per month (6% per annum) running from 15 months after the end of the relevant assessment year (§§ 233a, 240 AO), as well as late-payment surcharges under § 240 AO. Intentional misdeclaration constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO (imprisonment for up to 5 years, up to 10 years in aggravated cases, plus fines); negligent under-declaration is an administrative offense under § 378 AO (fine up to EUR 50,000). An improperly zero-rated invoice triggers liability for the stated VAT under § 14c UStG without an offsetting right. Claim windows extend four years (ten years in cases of evasion). In carousel or missing-trader fraud patterns, § 25d UStG may make the exporter jointly liable for input VAT wrongly deducted. For services, non-repatriation of the foreign receivable for more than 180 days is treated as a red flag for hidden profit distribution or shell structuring; although German law imposes no formal FX-repatriation deadline, audit practice expects actual collection and can recharacterize unreturned receivables as constructive dividends, triggering additional tax and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current and capital account convertibility under EU law, enshrined in Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The Euro (EUR) is freely convertible with no exchange controls or administrative restrictions. Profit remittance by an Unternehmergesellschaft (UG) to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject to submission of the annual financial statements and payment of the German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15%, solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, municipal rate typically 7-17% combined), and, where applicable, dividend withholding tax at 26.375% unless a tax treaty reduces or exempts the rate. No specific central bank approval is required for the transfer of dividends or capital returns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. There is no obligation to surrender foreign currency proceeds or time the repatriation of export earnings. However, every cross-border payment or receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent in foreign currency) must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance of payments statistics, typically through the domestic banking institution via the AWV reporting scheme within seven days of the end of the month in which the transaction occurred. For this TikTok MCN agency, platform-generated creator payouts in foreign currency, if any, must be included in such statistical reporting but do not require conversion or surrender.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) in coordination with the European Central Bank and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts are viable only if the EMI is licensed in the European Economic Area (EEA) and holds a valid passport or BaFin registration to provide payment services in Germany. Examples include Wise Europe SA (licensed in Belgium, passporting to Germany) and Airwallex (Europe) Limited. German law under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) prohibits unlicensed non-EEA EMIs from issuing German IBANs or providing payment services to German residents. A compliant offshore EMI such as Wise can be used as a secondary payment rail for TikTok creator payouts, but the primary corporate operating account must be held at a fully licensed German or EEA bank to maintain sturdy regulatory clearance for high-volume creator settlements. Feasibility rating: Medium-High, subject to proof of EEA authorization and due diligence documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the UG from non-resident shareholders must use SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT (for EUR) with the beneficiary account named 'Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)' and a clear reference 'Einlage/Kapitalerhöhung' plus the shareholder identification. The remitter must include the ISIN/LEI if available. German balance of payments reporting (AWV) via the receiving bank is mandatory for any single transaction above EUR 12,500/foreign currency equivalent. The appropriate German purpose code is 'Kapitalaufstockung' (Deutsche Bundesbank code range for direct investment equity). For SWIFT EPC purpose code, use 'CAPC' (Capital Payments) or 'CASH' only if the underlying remittance memo explicitly states 'capital contribution', ensuring the receiving bank records it as a direct capital payment and not a service payment to avoid unintended taxation. Wire verification is completed via matching the Handelsregister excerpt and the issuing bank's SWIFT confirmation.Outbound creator royalty settlements and trade disbursements are executed principally via SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA beneficiaries (e.g., creators outside the EEA) and via SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EEA-based creators. For high-value IP licensing or large consolidated creator payouts above USD/EUR 500,000, an irrevocable Letter of Credit (Commercial L/C, UCP 600) issued by a German bank may be used as settlement collateral if required by counterparties. All outbound transactions above EUR 12,500 must be reported by the bank under AWV statistics. For USD/EUR conversion, payments are cleared through the bank's continuous linked settlement (CLS) or conventional nostro-vostro clearing lanes, ensuring compliant FX execution and transparent fee disclosure. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts are viable only if the EMI is licensed in the European Economic Area (EEA) and holds a valid passport or BaFin registration to provide payment services in Germany. Examples include Wise Europe SA (licensed in Belgium, passporting to Germany) and Airwallex (Europe) Limited. German law under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) prohibits unlicensed non-EEA EMIs from issuing German IBANs or providing payment services to German residents. A compliant offshore EMI such as Wise can be used as a secondary payment rail for TikTok creator payouts, but the primary corporate operating account must be held at a fully licensed German or EEA bank to maintain sturdy regulatory clearance for high-volume creator settlements.
