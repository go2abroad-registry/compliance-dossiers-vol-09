Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); the competent local tax office (Finanzamt) for tax registration, tax numbers, corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT administration. Tax registration is generally submitted electronically through the ELSTER portal using the questionnaire for tax registration.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire to its competent Finanzamt after incorporation and commencement of business. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The entity or its authorized tax representative may separately apply to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr., generally through the BZSt application process or via the tax registration procedure. A foreign entity carrying on taxable activities in Germany must register with the competent German Finanzamt, appoint a German tax representative where required, provide constitutional documents, commercial-register evidence, management and ownership information, business descriptions, bank details, expected turnover, supply-chain information, and evidence of taxable transactions. Non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a fiscal representative for specific VAT refund or compliance procedures; Germany does not generally require a fiscal representative solely because the business is non-resident. VAT returns and related filings are submitted electronically through ELSTER unless an applicable exception applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a corporation and is generally subject to German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax at entity level. Germany has no US-style general check-the-box election for a UG. The corporate taxation election under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act is available to qualifying partnerships and is not an election available to a UG. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Section 4k of the Income Tax Act may deny deductions or impose corresponding limitations for imported mismatches, deduction/non-inclusion outcomes, or other arrangements involving related parties or structured arrangements. These rules do not convert a UG into a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a German business is exempt from VAT registration. A taxable person must register when conducting taxable transactions requiring German VAT compliance. The small-business regime (Kleinunternehmerregelung), applicable subject to statutory conditions from 1 January 2025, generally applies where domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year. The regime is an exemption from charging VAT, not a general rule eliminating all registration or reporting obligations. Foreign businesses making taxable supplies in Germany generally cannot rely on a domestic turnover threshold where German VAT is due. The reduced VAT rate is generally 7.0% for qualifying supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier or purchaser transports or dispatches the goods to the third country and the statutory export conditions are met. The supplier must retain objective evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory, normally through electronic customs exit confirmation or equivalent approved evidence. Intra-EU supplies are generally exempt with the right to deduct input VAT where the customer has a valid VAT identification number, the goods are transported to another EU Member State, and the supplier satisfies recapitulative statement, transport, invoice, and recordkeeping requirements. Services are not generally described as zero-rated exports. Business-to-business services are ordinarily supplied where the recipient is established under the place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of German VAT when supplied to a non-German business customer. Financial services, investment-management services, and portfolio-related services require transaction-specific analysis because exemptions, intermediary rules, and place-of-supply rules may apply. A multi-family office must separately assess whether its activities constitute exempt financial services, taxable advisory services, or regulated portfolio management.

  • Electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence proving departure from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and applicable packing list
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier delivery evidence
  • Purchase order, contract, and customer identity records
  • Evidence linking the exported goods to the supplier invoice and customs declaration
  • For intra-EU supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and recapitulative statement records
  • For offshore services: customer business-status evidence, contractual documentation, place-of-establishment evidence, invoices containing the applicable reverse-charge wording where required, and records supporting the place-of-supply conclusion

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system or mandatory digital tax stamping. From 1 January 2025, German-established businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance requirements are phased: businesses with prior-year domestic turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 generally must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from 1 January 2027; all affected domestic B2B suppliers generally must issue them from 1 January 2028. Permitted structured formats include XRechnung and other EN 16931-compliant formats, including qualifying hybrid formats such as ZUGFeRD. Transitional rules permit other invoice formats during the applicable transition periods. Electronic invoices must be retained in an auditable form; invoices may be transmitted through agreed electronic channels without submission to a central government clearance platform.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or input VAT recovery may be denied or clawed back where the taxpayer cannot substantiate export, transport, customer status, place of supply, or other statutory conditions. Incorrectly exempt invoices may result in tax liability under the German VAT invoice-liability rules, interest on underpaid tax, correction of VAT returns, repayment of refunds, and administrative penalties. Intentional or reckless false declarations may constitute tax evasion or tax understatement under the German Fiscal Code, with criminal or administrative consequences, fines, and, for serious intentional conduct, imprisonment. Late filing and late payment penalties may also apply. Tax authorities may impose additional documentation requirements, audit related entities and transactions, and deny input VAT deductions where invoices or business-purpose evidence are defective. Repeated non-compliance may lead to heightened audit frequency, security requirements, or restrictions concerning VAT refund procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions, anchored by European Union free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU. There are no exchange controls, no requirement for central-bank approval of equity inflows, loan repayment, profit repatriation, or foreign-currency balances. The euro is freely convertible against all major currencies. Profit remittance from a German UG is unrestricted as a matter of exchange control; the only constraints are tax-related, namely German corporate income tax, trade tax, and a 25% withholding tax on dividends subject to treaty reduction or parent-subsidiary exemption, plus contemporaneous transfer-pricing/substance requirements to prevent constructive profit shifting. A UG with cross-border family-office clients must keep complete intercompany documentation and arm s length fees for advisory, custody, and fiduciary services.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline in Germany. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained, credited, used, or converted freely. The only mandatory requirement is external-sector reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank: every cross-border payment received or made by a German resident, including export proceeds and import disbursements, in an amount exceeding EUR 12,500 or the equivalent must be reported on a monthly basis, generally by the seventh working day of the month following the value date, through the Bundesbank electronic reporting system. The report identifies the foreign counterparty, amount, currency, country, and transaction type. Non-compliance is an administrative offence; engaging a German Hausbank or treasury service provider to file the monthly AWV/Bundesbank report is recommended.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank), exercising foreign exchange statistical surveillance under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV); the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) supervises specific export-control licensing; the Federal Ministry of Finance retains overall monetary and regulatory policy authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low reliance for a multi-family office. EU/EEA digital payment institutions and electronic money institutions holding a PSD2 licence can provide IBAN accounts, SWIFT MT103 dispatch, and multicurrency wallets, but a German UG engaged in discretionary portfolio advisory is classified as a high-risk financial-sector client under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act and AMLD6. Licensed EMIs may support ancillary cross-border payment flows, but they cannot hold client securities, provide custodial portfolio services, or act as a substitute for a German custodian bank. An unlicensed offshore EMI or virtual-asset wallet claiming bank-like services to German residents would be illegal under the German Banking Act (KWG). Use a reputable EU EMI only as a segregated auxiliary cash-sweeping rail, not as the sole treasury clearing bank; require full beneficial-ownership discovery and jurisdiction-specific anti-money-laundering evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injections into the UG should be routed via SWIFT MT103 using the UG's own account at a German tier-1 bank, with the remittance information stating capital increase, capital reserve contribution, or equity injection in German: Einzahlung in das Stammkapital or Kapitalzurücklage. The ISO 20022 purpose code CAPB or EQUI should be selected where the remitting bank system requires a purpose code, and the German beneficiary bank must receive the corresponding notarised capital-increase resolution before crediting the account if the capital increase requires Commercial Register registration. In the required Deutsche Bundesbank external-economic-report file, the payment should be classified as direct-investment participation capital contribution, not as a trade receivable. For intercompany loans, use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code LOAN and include an executed written loan agreement with arm s-length interest rate, repayment schedule, and transfer-pricing documentation; a shareholder loan from a non-resident parent may be freely admitted without exchange-control permit but must be reported if exceeding EUR 12,500. Verification evidence should include the MT103 send confirmation, MT910 credit advice, bank compliance clearances, sanctions/UBO checks, and the underlying notarial and constitutional documents.For euro payments within the SEPA area, clearing should be executed through SEPA Credit Transfer with IBAN and BIC, including structured remittance of the invoice reference. For international payments outside the EEA or non-euro currencies, use SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary bank details and field 70 remittance data containing the invoice number, purchase order, and underlying trade purpose; if a correspondent bank cover payment is needed, MT202 COV may be used. For secured or documentary trade settlements, the German bank will issue a Commercial Letter of Credit under ICC UCP 600 transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT710 to the beneficiary bank, commonly confirmed by a London or Singapore bank; required documents include commercial invoice, bills of lading or airway bill, packing list, insurance certificate, and origin certificates. All outbound payments above EUR 12,500 must be captured in the monthly Bundesbank AWV balance-of-payments report. Treasury disbursements to foreign sub-advisers, family-service providers, and legal consultants should use SWIFT MT103 with purpose codes FEES or PROFIT rather than ambiguous family-gift codes; every payment should be supported by a valid service contract and invoiced according to German commercial tax rules.Outright foreign-exchange forward contracts against EUR and non-EUR currencies with a German or EU licensed bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) over emerging-market or restricted-convertibility currencies, cash-settled only in EUR, with underlying trade or family-office exposure documentation.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange swaps for tenor-matched cash-flow conversion between EUR and USD, GBP, CHF, CNY, or other major currencies.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps converting EUR floating-rate liabilities into a designated foreign currency, subject to EMIR clearing and margin rules.European-style currency options (call and put) for portfolio protection, executed with a German or EU counterparty under an ISDA CSA, capped by the UG s approved hedging-policy notional limits.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low reliance for a multi-family office. EU/EEA digital payment institutions and electronic money institutions holding a PSD2 licence can provide IBAN accounts, SWIFT MT103 dispatch, and multicurrency wallets, but a German UG engaged in discretionary portfolio advisory is classified as a high-risk financial-sector client under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act and AMLD6. Licensed EMIs may support ancillary cross-border payment flows, but they cannot hold client securities, provide custodial portfolio services, or act as a substitute for a German custodian bank. An unlicensed offshore EMI or virtual-asset wallet claiming bank-like services to German residents would be illegal under the German Banking Act (KWG). Use a reputable EU EMI only as a segregated auxiliary cash-sweeping rail, not as the sole treasury clearing bank; require full beneficial-ownership discovery and jurisdiction-specific anti-money-laundering evidence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 0.825% Solidaritätszuschlag (5.5% surcharge on 15%) plus municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, typically 14–17% in a standard 400%-Hebesatz municipality); aggregate effective corporate income tax burden approximately 29.9%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (statutory 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld tax). Treaty relief may reduce the rate to 15%, to 5% for qualifying substantial shareholdings (typically 10% voting rights), to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent is resident in another EU member state and satisfies the 12-month holding period, or to lower rates under German double taxation agreements such as the US (0%/5%/15%), China (5%/15%) or Switzerland (0%/5%/15%) treaties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory filing deadline is 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end (i.e. within 7 months after the tax year); where a recognised tax adviser prepares and transmits the return electronically, the deadline is automatically extended to 31 December; further discretionary extensions are rarely granted and only for extraordinary hardship.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Properly filed together with the corporate income tax return by 31 July of the following year; extended to 31 December when a tax adviser transmits the return electronically.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File (Aufzeichnungen) under § 90 Abs. 4 AO applies to cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 in sales or supply volume, or EUR 1,000,000 for intra-group service charges, in a given fiscal year; Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100,000,000; contemporaneous arm's length documentation of all significant intercompany transactions is recommended irrespective of thresholds due to the burden of proof reversal under § 162 Abs. 2 AO.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate 'Economic Substance Regulations' register of the Gulf type, but the doctrine is embedded in positive law: § 42 AO sets aside abusive structures lacking economic purpose; the controlled-foreign-company (CFC) rules of §§ 7–13 AStG attribute passive income to German shareholders in substance-less vehicles; and the German Banking Act (KWG) deems discretionary portfolio management a regulated financial service requiring a BaFin licence with genuine substance. A UG-MFO operating with zero local premises, no investment committee minutes, no discretionary decision records and no local staff triggers tax reallocation of its fees, revocation of any statutory licence, and criminal anti-money-laundering consequences. Demonstrating substance requires physical management in Germany, documented portfolio advisory decisions, segregation of client assets, and a staff directory linked to the KWG licence application.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Undeclared Foreign Custody Assets

Statutory sanction: Felony under § 370 AO: up to 5 years' imprisonment or a criminal fine; reassessed tax plus 6% p.a. interest, late-filing surcharge up to EUR 25,000; evidence of intentional concealment leads to seizure and publication.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax under §§ 69 and 71 AO irrespective of corporate veil protection; travel bans, passport seizure, freezing of personal assets, disgorgement of profits, and a management disqualification ban of up to 5 years under § 370 AO.
Unlicensed Financial Activity / AML Breach (Shell MFO)

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under § 54 KWG with imprisonment up to 3 years or fine; administrative fines under § 56 GwG up to EUR 5,000,000 per breach (and up to 10% of annual turnover for credit institutions); immediate cessation order and deregistration of the UG from the commercial register.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, civil restitution to defrauded clients, disqualification from acting as directors for 5 years; German enforcement courts may issue international travel bans and, under §§ 261/261a StGB, money-laundering imprisonment of up to 10 years for aggravated cases.
Sham Structure / Economic Substance Deficit

Statutory sanction: § 42 AO tax adjustment: income reallocated to the shareholders with retroactive effect; additional tax assessment plus 6% penalty interest; denial of expense deduction for the invoiced advisory fees; fines under § 379 AO up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the reallocated corporate tax; piercing of the corporate veil is routinely applied; travel bans and asset freezes under § 71 AO; imprisonment up to 5 years for criminal evasion linked to the sham structure.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / False DTA Relief Claims

Statutory sanction: Reversal of the burden of proof under § 162 AO: the tax office estimates income upwards; mandatory surcharge of 5% of the income adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000) for missing contemporaneous documentation; interest on underpaid tax at 6% p.a.; criminal classification under § 370 AO where the false certificate is knowingly used, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the tax estimate and surcharge; civil claims for restitution of the treaty relief fraudulently obtained; restraint on cross-border travel by enforcement courts; in aggravated cases, up to 5 years' imprisonment and permanent exclusion from managing a financial services entity.
