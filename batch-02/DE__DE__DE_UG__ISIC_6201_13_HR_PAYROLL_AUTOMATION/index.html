Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires a minimum share capital of EUR 1, fully paid in cash before registration and deposited in a dedicated escrow or trust account at a German credit institution; capital in-kind is prohibited under § 5a GmbHG, and the company must retain 25% of annual surplus until the stated capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required for the leased office premises under the local Building Regulation (Bauordnung), obtained by an authorized fire safety officer inspecting escape routes, smoke alarms, and structural fire load; structural load inspections are required for any server racks above 250 kg per square meter and for a separated occupational room; the workplace must be cleared under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) and occupational health regulation, including ergonomic assessments, emergency power measures, and a business physician appointment (Betriebsarzt) where payroll employees process data for high-volume customers. The public authority is the municipal building authorities and the local Fire Protection Association (Feuerwehr).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Germany - Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern - BZSt, www.bzst.de) is competent for the allocation of the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under Section 27a UStG, One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registrations, withholding tax clearance, and centrally administered tax matters. The local revenue office (Finanzamt) of the relevant Bundesland is competent for income tax, corporation tax, trade tax, payroll withholding tax, VAT assessment, and the issuance of the local tax number (Steuernummer). The unified national electronic filing portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de). The administrative framework is a unitary national tax system executed decentrally by state-level Finanzamter under the authority of the Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the articles of association of the Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschraenkt) and register the UG in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) upon contribution of the minimum capital (EUR 1 is sufficient). Step 2: File the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt through ELSTER, disclosing the legal form, shareholders, anticipated turnover, balance sheet projections, intended business activities, and wage tax data; this single questionnaire simultaneously registers the UG for corporation tax, trade tax, VAT, and payroll withholding tax (Lohnsteuer). Step 3: Obtain the tax number (Steuernummer) from the Finanzamt, which is used for all tax returns and assessments. Step 4: Apply to the BZSt for the electronic VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) using the dedicated BZSt online procedure or the paper form (Fragebogen zum Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr.); the USt-IdNr. is required for intra-Community B2B transactions and OSS registration. Step 5: Register the UG as an employer with the competent Finanzamt for payroll tax (this step is included in the Fragebogen) and obtain the Betriebsnummer (company number) from the Federal Employment Agency (Bundesagentur fuer Arbeit) for social security reporting. Step 6: If the UG has non-resident shareholders or a foreign place of management, file the supplementary questionnaire for non-resident entrepreneurs under Section 18(4c) UStG with the BZSt. Step 7: For EU cross-border digital sales to consumers, register with the German OSS operated by the BZSt. Step 8: Where the UG has no German registered office or permanent establishment, verify whether a permanent establishment is created by an economically supported German branch of an online payroll platform; if so, register with the competent Finanzamt of the permanent establishment's location. All filings are submitted electronically; paper filing is permitted only for certain administrative procedures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German domestic tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the UG is, however, treated as a per se corporation for US federal tax purposes under Treas. Reg. Section 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i)(A), and no entity classification election is available for a GmbH or UG in German domestic law. The relevant statutory elections and hybrid mechanisms available to a German UG are: (1) Corporate tax and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) - election to consolidate the UG with its German parent or German subsidiary under Section 14 KStG (requires a profit and loss transfer agreement with a minimum term of five years) and under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG (requires financial, economic, and organizational integration), so that intra-group transactions are disregarded for VAT and the group is taxed as one corporate taxpayer; (2) Small-business exemption election under Section 19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung) - available only if the prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and the estimated current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000 (effective 1 January 2025); the election is revocable and the exemption is mandatory unless the entrepreneur opts for VAT application; (3) Option to tax real estate under Section 9 UStG (option to treat a nontaxable supply of real property as taxable to secure input VAT deduction); (4) Hybrid mismatch notifications and elections under Section 4k EStG (ATAD II implementation), including the declaration of reverse hybrid entities and the election to apply the substitute rules where a foreign permanent establishment is treated as transparent; (5) Trade tax apportionment elections for multi-establishment UGs (Zerlegung) and (6) the wage tax individual tax card information procedure (elektronische Lohnsteuerabzugsmerkmale - ELStAM). No pass-through taxation is available for the UG itself: it is subject to corporate tax at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax (effective rates between approximately 7% and 17.15% depending on the municipal multiplier).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible under EU capital and payment liberalisation provisions; Germany maintains no exchange control restrictions on current or capital account transactions for residents or non-residents. Profit remittance by a UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires no prior central bank approval; the sole burdens are CIT/withholding tax compliance, i.e., 25% Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge and, if applicable, municipal trade tax deemed accrual, non-resident shareholder withholding under the Corporate Income Tax Act, and possible treaty rate reduction following a foreign tax clearance certificate (Freistellungsbescheinigung).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export revenues must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical purposes through the external trade statistics (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung and Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) when the amount exceeds EUR 12,500 or the threshold set by the Bundesbank in a calendar month; no requirement exists to convert such proceeds into euro.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank and Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI payment institutions may provide SEPA-compatible IBANs and multi-currency accounts, but they are not licensed as credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG) and do not extend deposit protection under the German Deposit Guarantee Act. Feasibility for payroll operations is conditional: an EMI can offer execution-only payment accounts for disbursement purposes, but a UG payroll service processing direct debits requires a genuine domestic bank relationship or a BaFin-authorised payment institution with a German branch because the absence of a German credit institution licence raises compliance counter-party risk and could impede SEPA Direct Debit schemes. Overall viability rating: Medium – permissible strictly as an ancillary liquidity rail; primary cash clearing must sit with a Tier-1 German bank to preserve creditor protection and GmbH capital maintenance rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital contributions for the UG must be paid by wire transfer into the German bank account as ordinary cash contribution. The shareholder directs a SWIFT payment from an account in the shareholder's name with the purpose code referencing the capital contribution. For a German resident euro contribution, use local SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit; for non-resident euro remittance, use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code GESELLSCHAFT (capital) or the recognised transaction purpose code 'CAPITAL_DOMESTIC_INVESTMENT' under Deutsche Bundesbank external trade statistics. The bank verifies the identity of the remitter, the contribution must be freely available to the managing director without adverse claims, and proof of payment must be preserved via the bank credit advice. For formation registration, the UG may need the registered capital to be paid in cash and to remain at the free disposal of the managing director; full payment after registration is permitted but must be completed in accordance with Section 5a GmbHG and the filing of the statutory confirmation with the Commercial Register.Outbound trade settlements for payroll automation services are executed through standard clearing rails: SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated payments within the EEA, SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA bank wires, and commercial letters of credit (documentary credit, UCP 600) where a buyer/client requires shipment or performance security. For routine employee salary disbursements, the preferred rail is domestic SEPA Direct Debit (SDD CORE) for payroll clients and SEPA Credit Transfers for suppliers. Cross-border payments from the UG to foreign contractors must include the correct transaction purpose code (e.g., INTRASERV, OTHERMISC) and documentary back-up to satisfy German balance-of-payments reporting obligations. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if related-party transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 for goods or EUR 500,000 for services; Master File required if group revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies substance abuse rules under §42 AO and anti-treaty-shopping provisions; no separate ESR, but substance is critical to avoid reclassification as a shell and to secure treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 50,000 per offense; additional penalty of 10% of understated tax; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participate; civil restitution, travel bans, and potential imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of income, retroactive taxation, and penalties up to EUR 25,000; potential revocation of limited liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for corporate tax debts if they fail to maintain substance; civil restitution and travel bans.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; criminal prosecution for money laundering; license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; imprisonment up to 5 years, travel bans, and confiscation of assets.
