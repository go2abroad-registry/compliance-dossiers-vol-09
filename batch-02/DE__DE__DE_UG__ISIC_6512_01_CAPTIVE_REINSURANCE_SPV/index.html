Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister Abteilung B (Commercial Register, Section B) at the Amtsgericht (Local Court) of the district of the registered office, e.g., Amtsgericht Charlottenburg for Berlin; electronic filing effected through the certifying Notar with the competent Registergericht.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), Sections 1 (formation), 2 (notarized articles), 3 (Musterprotokoll model protocol), 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft), 7-8 (registration application and accompanying documents), 10-11 (register entry and legal personality), 35-44 (managing directors); Commercial Code (HGB) Sections 8-10 (Handelsregister); Insurance Supervision Act (Versicherungsaufsichtsgesetz, VAG) Section 1(3) No. 2 (captive exemption) and Section 8 (authorization); Trades Notification Code (Gewerbeordnung) Section 14.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notariell beurkundeter Gesellschaftsvertrag (Notarized Articles of Association) or notarized Musterprotokoll (Model Protocol), together with the Handelsregister-Anmeldung (Application for Registration in the Commercial Register) and the notarially certified Geschäftsführerliste (List of Managing Directors).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG must be formed with a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00 (Section 5a(1) GmbHG). Unlike a GmbH, the full subscribed capital must be paid in cash and placed at the free disposal of the managing director before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited under Section 5a(2) GmbHG, and the 50% installment relief under Section 7(2) GmbHG does not apply. Payment is evidenced to the Registergericht by a bank confirmation (Bankbestätigung). The UG must retain 25% of its annual net profit in a statutory reserve until the registered share capital reaches EUR 25,000 (Section 5a(3) GmbHG). No formal escrow account is mandatory, but the notary may use a notary escrow (Notaranderkonto) to secure the contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate per Section 43 MBO (escape routes, fire alarm panels, extinguishers); structural load verification of office floors at ≥ 2.0 kN/m² imposed load; occupational clearance under the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV) with registered safety officer; registration with the Berufsgenossenschaft under DGUV Vorschrift 1; emergency lighting, alarm and evacuation drill records maintained.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG must be formed with a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00 (Section 5a(1) GmbHG). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.), central withholding and FATCA/CRS matters; local Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT administration. Electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a UG or a German permanent establishment must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt, either via ELSTER or in paper form, obtaining a local Steuernummer. Thereafter, the taxpayer applies separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under § 15 of the VAT ID Ordinance; a German tax representative or duly appointed managing director is required for filings, and non-EU-resident applicants may be required to appoint a fiscal representative for VAT purposes. Legal certainty on counterparty VAT IDs is verified through the BZSt/VIES confirmation system.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a UG or a German permanent establishment must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt, either via ELSTER or in paper form, obtaining a local Steuernummer. Thereafter, the taxpayer applies separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under § 15 of the VAT ID Ordinance; a German tax representative or duly appointed managing director is required for filings, and non-EU-resident applicants may be required to appoint a fiscal representative for VAT purposes. Legal certainty on counterparty VAT IDs is verified through the BZSt/VIES confirmation system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections relevant to a captive reinsurance UG: (1) corporate income tax and trade tax fiscal unity (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) under §§ 14, 17 KStG, permitting profit and loss pooling with a German parent; (2) VAT group (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG; (3) hybrid mismatch adjustments and corresponding taxpayer election/notification procedures under § 4k EStG implementing ATAD I/II; (4) application for a license waiver or simplified authorization as a captive reinsurer under § 8 VAG; and (5) no territorial tax-exempt-zone election is available for an onshore UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 (prior-year turnover) / EUR 50,000 (current-year turnover) small-business exemption under § 19 UStG; however, a foreign entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany is required to register for VAT without any threshold, and a captive reinsurer must register regardless of turnover where it conducts non-exempt or connected taxable transactions. Reinsurance and retrocession outputs are generally VAT-exempt under § 4 Nr. 10 UStG, but registration remains mandatory to comply with invoicing and input-VAT-position obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% export VAT is claimable under § 6 UStG where goods are actually exported to a third-country destination (or supplied intra-EU under § 4 Nr. 1 UStG), provided the exporter substantiates the export through customs and transport evidence under §§ 8-10 UStDV and ATLAS. Captive reinsurance and retrocession services are not zero-rated: they are exempt from VAT under § 4 Nr. 10 UStG irrespective of the recipient's location, and input VAT recovery is blocked under § 15 Abs. 2 UStG unless the captive reinsurer makes separate taxable supplies. Cross-border B2B services supplied to a non-resident entrepreneur fall outside the scope of German VAT under § 3a UStG with reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) confirmed by German Customs (Zoll)
  • Export accompanying document (EAD) and official customs exit confirmation for the third-country destination
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing cross-border transport
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Underlying reinsurance contract, risk slip or cover note evidencing the exported risk (captive reinsurance context)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign consideration where applicable

Mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing applies under the German E-Rechnung mandate: from 1 January 2025 all taxpayers must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 issuers with a prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue structured e-invoices; and from 1 January 2028 all issuers must comply. Accepted formats are XRechnung and ZUGFeRD 2.x, transmissible via e-mail, secure API, or portal. Germany has no government real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system (no Italian SDI-style matching); instead, VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen), annual VAT returns and recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung) must be transmitted electronically via ELSTER. GoBD-compliant digital record keeping and e-audit data export (GDPdU/ZUGFeRD) obligations apply.

Retrospective clawback of wrongly claimed export zero-rating and input VAT deductions arises under §§ 14c, 15 and 17 UStG, with a specific VAT penalty under § 26a UStG of up to EUR 50,000; late VAT carries monthly interest of 0.5% under § 233a AO. Fraudulent zero-rating or evasion is punishable under § 370 AO with imprisonment up to 10 years and fines up to EUR 500,000, while negligent evasion attracts fines under § 378 AO; GoBD record-keeping deficiencies trigger separate Ordnungswidrigkeit fines. For captive reinsurers, artificial structures may be recharacterized under § 42 AO, transfer pricing and hidden profit distribution reclassifications apply under § 8 Abs. 3 KStG with a 30% trade tax addback exposure. Germany imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a documentation and substance-audit reference, and prolonged failure to repatriate reinsurance profits may prompt controlled-foreign-company and substance challenges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU free movement of capital provisions. No exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. The Euro is fully convertible; no approval required for cross-border transfers. Reporting obligations exist for statistical purposes (e.g., to the Bundesbank for payments exceeding €12,500) but do not constitute approval hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters retain full discretion over the currency and timing of repatriation. No minimum conversion ratio or timeline is imposed. All proceeds may be held in foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad, subject to standard tax and reporting requirements.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and insurance supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) are viable for cross-border payment processing and multi-currency accounts, but they are not authorized to provide credit or hold client funds in the same manner as banks. For a captive reinsurance SPV requiring large reserve balances and complex treasury operations, EMIs face legal constraints on interest-bearing accounts and may have lower transaction limits. Enhanced due diligence is required for insurance-related entities. Offshore digital EMIs (non-EU) are generally not viable due to BaFin licensing requirements and lack of passporting rights.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for capital injection into the UG's German bank account: Use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'CAPT' (Capital Contribution) or 'GIFT' (if non-repayable). For equity contributions, the purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity) is appropriate. The transfer must reference the UG's name and account number. For amounts exceeding €12,500, the bank will report to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes. Verification includes providing the bank with a copy of the shareholders' resolution, the capital contribution agreement, and proof of source of funds (e.g., bank statements of the transferring entity).Clearing rails for trade settlements: For reinsurance premium payments and claims disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers. For larger or structured transactions, commercial letters of credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700 may be used, though less common in reinsurance. For intra-group transactions, use SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers. All outbound payments must be accompanied by appropriate purpose codes (e.g., 'INSUR' for insurance premiums, 'CLAI' for claims). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 group consolidated turnover triggers mandatory Master File and Local File under EU Transfer Pricing Documentation standards; Country-by-Country reporting arises at EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. Captive reinsurance SPVs are nonetheless required under § 90(3) AO to document intercompany premiums, loss reserves and risk allocation even below the monetary thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply separate Economic Substance Regulations of the type used in offshore financial centers; however, the abuse-of-law doctrine under § 42 AO, the economic-substance approach of German tax case law, and BaFin licensing and supervision under the Insurance Supervision Act (VAG) require a captive reinsurer to maintain real personnel, local management, adequate capital, and actual risk underwriting functions in Germany. A shell UG without these attributes is disregarded for tax and treaty purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO: up to 5 years' imprisonment (up to 10 years in especially severe cases) plus payment of all back taxes, late-filing surcharges and late-payment interest; administrative fine under § 378 AO for negligent tax shortfall of up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Managing directors assume personal liability under §§ 34 and 69 AO for unpaid corporate taxes if they intentionally or negligently fail to file and pay on time. Where tax evasion is criminal, directors may lose the right to act as managing directors, and public prosecutors may order travel bans, asset seizures and confiscation of the UG's assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may estimate income and impose a 10% TP adjustment surcharge under § 162(4) AO (minimum EUR 10,000), in addition to the tax undercharge, late interest and trade tax recomputation. In cases of intended deception, criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false TP documentation face personal criminal exposure for tax evasion; civil liability for the UG's tax deficiencies can result from breach of the duty of care under GmbH law. Repeated intentional TP fraud may lead to personal asset attachment, travel restrictions and removal as director.
Shell Company and AML License Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or, in serious commercial cases, up to 10% of annual turnover under VAG/GwG; compulsory cessation and forced liquidation of the reinsurer; loss of the commercial registration; in severe AML cases criminal money-laundering penalties under § 261 StGB trigger imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally fineable for administrative AML and VAG offenses and can be directly civilly liable for resulting creditor losses. Intentional use of the UG as an instrument of economic crime subjects directors, shareholders and beneficial owners to criminal indictment, possibly leading to travel bans, passport confiscation and jail terms; the corporate veil is pierced where asset mixing or fraudulent abuse has occurred.
