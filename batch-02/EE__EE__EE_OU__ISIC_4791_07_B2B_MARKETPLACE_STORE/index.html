Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Osaühing; founders may set a nominal share capital (commonly EUR 1–2,500). If the share capital is paid in cash before registration, it must be deposited into a temporary bank account held in the company's name or a notary's escrow account; confirmation of payment is filed with the application. No escrow account is required if the capital is payable after registration or contributed as a non-cash asset.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), operating the e-MTA (e-maksuamet / e-tax) digital portal.

E-government filing portal

A resident Osaühing (OÜ) is issued a registry code by the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) upon incorporation; this code constitutes the universal tax identification number. A non-resident foreign entity without an Estonian permanent establishment may procure an Estonian tax/VAT identification number by filing the prescribed VAT registration application through e-MTA. VAT registration must be completed before making any taxable supply with a place of supply in Estonia, unless the customer is liable under the reverse-charge mechanism or the supply is covered by the Union/Non-Union OSS or Import One-Stop Shop regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonian law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. An OÜ is by default a separate resident corporate taxpayer subject to the deferred corporate income tax system under which undistributed profits are not taxed and distributed profits are taxed at 20% or 25% as applicable. Elective mechanisms available include: (i) VAT group registration for closely affiliated taxable persons under section 7(1) of the Estonian VAT Act; (ii) the OSS/IOSS options for cross-border distance sales and electronically supplied services; and (iii) voluntary VAT registration when below the mandatory threshold. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are applied mandatorily rather than by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 for resident taxable persons based on calendar-year taxable turnover; no threshold applies to non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Estonia; EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies for cross-border distance sales and electronically supplied services routed through OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to goods dispatched or transported to a non-EU country by the supplier or on behalf of the supplier, provided the goods physically leave the European Union and the supplier possesses documentary proof of export. The export must generally be completed within three months of the supply, and the evidence must clearly substantiate the physical exit of the goods. Zero-rating also applies to internationally linked transport services, related ancillary services, and services directly connected with exported goods. Cross-border B2B services provided to non-EU customers are generally outside the scope of Estonian VAT rather than zero-rated. For marketplace or platform transactions, the actual exporter must hold the export evidence; an intermediary cannot apply zero-rating unless the underlying dispatch qualifies as an export supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EKS/export declaration containing a valid Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill or other transport document evidencing exit from the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation proving actual receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice issued by the selling OÜ
  • Purchase order or contract and supplementary dispatch evidence, such as warehouse release, packing list, and customs acceptance report

No real-time tax-authority clearance, continuous transaction controls, or mandatory digital stamping is implemented for general B2B invoicing in Estonia. E-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers selling to Estonian public contracting authorities through the centralized public sector e-invoicing network, which accepts EN 16931 and PEPPOL-compliant electronic invoices. For all other transactions, e-invoicing is voluntary but constitutes valid tax evidence. No statutory API-based fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement currently exists.

Estonia imposes full output VAT at 22% plus interest under the Taxation Act if zero-rating is not substantiated. The tax authority may reassess within the statutory limitation period and recover input VAT previously reclaimed, with late-payment interest equal to the Estonian weighted average central bank rate plus 8 percentage points. Knowingly or negligently presenting false export documents may trigger tax fraud penalties, increased tax assessments, and criminal sanctions under sections 346 to 348 of the Estonian Penal Code, including corporate fines and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment. Repeated misuse may further expose directors to personal liability and adverse public enforcement disclosure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is an EU and euro-area member. The euro is freely convertible, and Estonia has no general exchange-control regime restricting current-account payments, capital transfers, foreign ownership, or repatriation of lawful funds. Cross-border payments remain subject to EU and Estonian AML/CFT rules, targeted financial sanctions, bank screening, payment-services regulation, and documentation requirements. Dividends and other distributions must comply with Estonian company law, available distributable equity, solvency requirements, and applicable corporate income-tax rules. Estonia generally taxes company profits when distributed rather than when retained; this does not exempt recipients from withholding, residence-country tax, or reporting obligations where applicable. Banks may require evidence of the payment's commercial purpose, source of funds, beneficial ownership, and tax treatment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory deadline requires an Estonian company to repatriate export proceeds to Estonia, and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion or surrender ratio applies. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual, accounting, tax, AML/CFT, sanctions, and bank requirements. The company must maintain reliable transaction records and apply the applicable VAT and customs treatment; export proceeds do not themselves establish that a transaction qualifies as an export for VAT purposes.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia's national central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank determines euro-area monetary policy. The Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) supervises relevant financial-sector firms; it is not a foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment accounts and cross-border collections where the provider is appropriately authorised in the EEA and supports Estonian OÜ customers and the intended currencies. An EMI or payment institution account is not a bank deposit account and may not provide deposit-guarantee protection. Availability, safeguarding, permitted services, customer eligibility, and geographic restrictions vary by provider. Offshore digital EMIs are not a substitute for an Estonian bank account where a counterparty, registry-related process, payment provider, or bank specifically requires one. Verify the provider's home-state authorisation, passporting status, safeguarding arrangements, sanctions controls, and ability to support the company's B2B marketplace flows before onboarding.

20% of the gross dividend (computed as 20/80 of the net distribution) for distributions to non-resident shareholders; resident individuals may be eligible for the 14/86 reduced regular-dividend regime; treaty rates generally reduce the statutory 20% to 5% or 15%; parent-subsidiary directive may apply at 0% for qualifying EU parent companies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the 10th day of the month following the reporting month. A distribution in December is reported by 10 January; the final month of the fiscal year is included in the ordinary TSD cycle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with non-resident related parties; above this threshold contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory. Master File and CbC report thresholds follow OECD standards (group revenue ≥ EUR 750 million for CbC).

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but applies the OECD/G20 BEPS substance requirement, EU ATAD GAAR and CFC rules. Treaty relief and the 0% retained-profit regime require genuine management, physical presence, operational substance and real economic activity in Estonia. An e-residency OÜ that is a mere conduit/letterbox company is a target for recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Deficient Distribution Reporting

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the omitted profit distribution at 20/80; collection of unpaid tax plus late-payment interest at 0.06% per day; administrative fine up to €1,300; criminal tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and confiscation of proceeds (aggravated cases up to 7 years).

Director personal liability: Management-board members who knowingly cause the omission are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid CIT and withheld taxes; criminal conviction may involve travel bans, freezing orders, and imprisonment; the corporate veil may be pierced where the director is the sole shareholder and used the company for personal enrichment.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Increase of taxable income by the Estonian tax authority using the most appropriate TP method; resulting deemed distribution assessed at 20/80; late-payment interest; penalty for inaccurate submission up to 40% of underpaid tax in aggravated cases; denial of deduction of the excessive payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who negligently or intentionally signed false TP documentation may be held personally liable for resulting tax adjustments if the company becomes insolvent; criminal liability for false documentation in the context of tax fraud; management disqualification orders can be issued.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Estonian GAAR or treaty-LOB denial; all payments may be recharacterized as constructive profit distributions taxable at 20/80; treaty benefits are denied retrospectively; criminal or administrative penalties apply for sham arrangements; tax-authority decision may lead to immediate tax assessment on presumed distributions.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors involved in artificial structures can be jointly liable for the deemed distribution tax; travel bans and asset freeze orders are available in treaty-fraud investigations; imprisonment up to 5 years for abuse of legal forms.
Money Laundering and AML Violations through Marketplace Payments

Statutory sanction: Supervisory fine against the OÜ of up to €400,000 per violation or up to 10% of annual turnover in serious cases; suspension of payment-processing access; criminal money laundering penalties include imprisonment up to 7 years, confiscation, and publication of the enforcement measure.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers with knowledge or wilful blindness are personally criminally liable; travel bans and remand detention may be imposed; court may ban individuals from holding management positions in Estonian legal entities; civil restitution for third-party losses is recoverable from the directors.
