Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The local Registergericht (commercial register division of the competent Amtsgericht) for the company's registered seat; incorporation filings are transmitted electronically by a German notary.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), particularly sections 2–3 (formation and articles), 5a (UG), 7–8 (registration and filing documents), 10 (register entry), and 11 (pre-registration liability). Commercial-register procedure is governed by the Handelsregisterverordnung and the German Commercial Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or, if eligible, the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll), together with the electronically notarized Handelsregisteranmeldung and required supporting documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 44 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The entire stated capital must be paid in cash before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. The funds must be available to the company and evidenced to the managing director; no statutory escrow account is required. The UG must allocate 25% of annual net profit, adjusted for any loss carryforward, to the statutory reserve until share capital plus reserve reaches EUR 25,000. The USD filing-fee figure is an indicative conversion of the commonly applicable EUR 150 commercial-register fee; notarial charges and any applicable taxes are additional, and the payable amount should be confirmed with the notary and register court.

Funding & unlock path: No special fire, structural-load, or industrial-workplace clearance is ordinarily required solely for SaaS activities in compliant office premises. The operator must comply with applicable building-use, fire-safety, workplace health and safety, and accident-insurance requirements; the local building authority, fire authority, and competent statutory accident-insurance institution may apply if premises are altered, repurposed, or operated with relevant hazards.

Paid-in capital rule: A UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The entire stated capital must be paid in cash before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. The funds must be available to the company and evidenced to the managing director; no statutory escrow account is required. The UG must allocate 25% of annual net profit, adjusted for any loss carryforward, to the statutory reserve until share capital plus reserve reaches EUR 25,000. The USD filing-fee figure is an indicative conversion of the commonly applicable EUR 150 commercial-register fee; notarial charges and any applicable taxes are additional, and the payable amount should be confirmed with the notary and register court.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and federal tax administration; local Finanzamt (Local Tax Office) in the seat municipality for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT registration; electronic filing mandate: ELSTER portal (www.elster.de); customs/EORI jurisdiction: Generalzolldirektion (German General Directorate of Customs).

E-government filing portal

The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, thereby obtaining the Steuernummer (income tax/trade tax/VAT assessment number). Separately, the UG applies to the BZSt via form UST 1 (electronic through ELSTER or BZSt Online) for the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID), which is the operative identifier for B2B transactions, VIES validation, and intra-EU trade. If the UG performs exports to third countries or intra-EU freight, an EORI number must be registered with the Generalzolldirektion before filing the first export declaration. For foreign shareholders or foreign parent entities holding a German UG, no additional special authorization is required; a representative in Germany is recommended for service of process, and the UG must notify the Finanzamt of any changes to articles of association affecting tax classification. Non-resident digital service providers without a German establishment register directly with the BZSt under the simplified VAT procedure (Form UST 1 TG).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) and a resident corporation under §1 KStG, subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability on all income. Statutory rates: 15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate tax (0.825% effective), yielding a combined 15.825% federal income tax, plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at an effective rate between approximately 7% and 17% (base rate 3.5% × municipal Hebesatz, typically 200%-490%), for an aggregate total income tax burden of approximately 23%-33% depending on the city. Dividends received from domestic and foreign subsidiaries are 95% tax-exempt (participation exemption) where the UG holds at least 10% (subject to anti-hybrid rules); the UG is not a pass-through entity. Territorial_Scope: unlimited for resident corporations. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable to a standard German commercial seat; no special economic zone status attaches to LegalTech SaaS activities. — The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt within one month of business commencement, thereby obtaining the Steuernummer (income tax/trade tax/VAT assessment number). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The UG is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) and a resident corporation under §1 KStG, subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability on all income. Statutory rates: 15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate tax (0.825% effective), yielding a combined 15.825% federal income tax, plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at an effective rate between approximately 7% and 17% (base rate 3.5% × municipal Hebesatz, typically 200%-490%), for an aggregate total income tax burden of approximately 23%-33% depending on the city. Dividends received from domestic and foreign subsidiaries are 95% tax-exempt (participation exemption) where the UG holds at least 10% (subject to anti-hybrid rules); the UG is not a pass-through entity. Territorial_Scope: unlimited for resident corporations. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable to a standard German commercial seat; no special economic zone status attaches to LegalTech SaaS activities.

Available election forms

Germany does not offer a US-style check-the-box or pass-through election; the UG is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Statutory elections available to a UG include: (1) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §2(2) Nr. 2 UStG, allowing integration of a financially, organizationally, and economically linked subsidiary for consolidated VAT purposes; (2) waiver of VAT exemption on real estate letting under §9 UStG; (3) small-business exemption under §19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung) where previous-year turnover is ≤ €25,000 and current-year turnover is projected ≤ €100,000; (4) apportionment elections for multi-establishment trade tax allocation under §28–34 GewStG; and (5) for hybrid mismatch purposes, no domestic election exists, but the UG may align its tax classification with the OECD BEPS Action 2 and ATAD 2016/1164, specifically the German implementation in §4k EStG and §14 KStG, which disallow double deduction and deduction/non-inclusion outcomes without any elective carve-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€25,000 annual turnover in the preceding calendar year and projected €100,000 or less in the current calendar year (effective 1 January 2025 under the Growth Opportunities Act; previously €22,000/€50,000) — exceeding either limit disqualifies the small-business exemption and mandates full VAT registration. For cross-border digital SaaS supplies from Germany to consumers in other EU member states, the EU-wide OSS distance-sales threshold of €10,000 applies: below this aggregate, the UG charges German VAT; above it, the UG may use the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal in Germany to declare foreign VAT. For B2B services, no turnover threshold applies — the recipient's reverse-charge mechanism governs from the first euro. Foreign digital suppliers are subject to immediate German VAT nexus without a de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: 0% VAT under §4 Nr. 1, §6 UStG for supplies dispatched or transported to a third-country destination, provided the exporter retains the proof of export. Intra-EU supplies: 0% VAT under §6a UStG if the customer is a VIES-validated VAT-registered trader in another member state and goods physically move across the border. Services: cross-border B2B services — including LegalTech cloud document automation, e-signature authentication, and SaaS — do not carry German VAT at all because the place of supply is the customer's country (§3a(2) UStG); the UG must retain evidence of the customer's business status (valid VAT ID) and the B2B nature of the engagement. B2C digital services to third countries are also zero-rated/exempt (place of supply is the recipient's residence; outside the EU, German VAT does not apply). For export of goods combined with digital software/licensing, the UG must allocate consideration between the tangible and intangible components on an arm's-length basis, as customs valuation will scrutinize the software element separately from the physical goods.

  • Electronic customs export declaration filed in ATLAS/AES (Ausfuhranmeldung) with the Generalzolldirektion, evidencing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and completion of export
  • Commercial invoice, export accompanying document (EAD), packing list, and, where applicable, the Intrastat/Extrastat declaration evidence
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air), CMR Waybill (road), or postal/courier dispatch receipt demonstrating physical departure from EU customs territory
  • Proof of receipt by the non-resident customer: signed delivery confirmation, customs entry summary declaration at destination, or warehouse release note
  • For intra-EU zero-rating: the customer's VAT ID validated in the VIES database, plus the summary recapitulative statement (ZM) reporting the intra-community supply
  • Settlement evidence: bank transfer confirmation, SWIFT/remittance receipt, or foreign-currency credit note demonstrating consideration received from a non-German counterparty within the economic year, consistent with §1 AStG transfer-pricing documentation
  • Internal service agreement and risk assessment file documenting the B2B nature of digital SaaS supplies and exclusion of B2C classification

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or government API stamping system (no Italian-style SDI). Mandate timeline: from 1 January 2025, every VAT-registered German business must be able to receive structured e-invoices for domestic B2B supply; from 1 January 2027, businesses with annual turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all businesses must issue e-invoices. Accepted formats must be EN 16931-compliant: XRechnung (pure XML) or ZUGFeRD 2.8/3.x (PDF/XML hybrid). No biometric/digital stamp is required; no real-time clearance to the tax authority is imposed; however, all VAT returns, advance returns, and summaries are mandatory via ELSTER, and domestic B2B e-invoice data must be retained for 10 years under §§146–147 AO. The UG must also report cross-border supplies in the EU via the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung) through ELSTER.

German tax audits (Außenprüfung, §§193–207 AO) and customs audits will claw back zero-rated VAT with late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO) where the export document bundle is incomplete, internally inconsistent, or fails to prove physical removal from EU territory. Deliberate falsification of export declarations, misuse of a customer VAT ID, or knowing participation in missing-trader chains constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases) and compound monetary forfeiture; the fraud investigation authority (Steuerfahndung) may issue a retrospective assessment covering up to ten years (§169(2) AO) in cases of falsified documents. Additionally, §14c UStG imposes the full statutory 19% VAT on any invoice that improperly states German VAT, and the UG is exposed to joint and several liability for the tax debtor's unpaid VAT under §25d UStG where it knew or should have known of a supplier's tax evasion. The 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline aligns with the German arm's-length principle in §1 AStG: the Federal Ministry of Finance's administrative guidelines treat trade receivables not settled within approximately six months as not arm's-length, requiring a constructive adjustment of the taxable base (income correction, deemed dividend, or interest recharacterization) and, for a German UG, possible CFC recharacterization under §7–13 AStG of unrepatriated export proceeds into passive investment income subject to current taxation; the BFH requires the exporter to substantiate actual economic receipt within the annual accounts to preserve the integrity of zero-rating.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies unrestricted euro convertibility on both current and capital accounts under EU Treaty Articles 63-65 and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV). No exchange control approval is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation. Practical hurdles are documentary: accurate banking purpose codes, proper withholding tax documentation (e.g., dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, potentially reduced under a tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive), and auditable accounting records substantiating distributable profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be freely retained in EUR or foreign currency. The only statutory requirements are balance-of-payments statistical reports to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction, filed electronically under AWV reporting codes. Such reports are informational only and do not restrict the crediting or onward use of funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank and foreign trade payment statistics authority); Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for licensing and supervision of payment institutions, EMIs, and financial market participants.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, and only for operational payment routing, not for statutory capital escrow. An offshore digital EMI must be licensed in Germany or an EEA state and passported under PSD2 through BaFin registration; otherwise it cannot legally provide payment accounts to a German UG. The UG should verify the EMI's European IBAN, BaFin register entry, and protect client funds under the EMI safeguarding rules. Capital contributions and retained-earnings documentation should be held with a German licensed credit institution to satisfy statutory audit and treasury-verification requirements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT); municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) typically 14%–17% is added, producing a combined effective burden of approximately 30%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25.0% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax). Treaty relief may reduce or eliminate this rate, generally to 5% or 0% for substantial shareholdings, provided the parent is the beneficial owner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is required where cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 in a fiscal year; a Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and submitted within 30 days of a German tax audit request.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations filing, but substantial economic substance is a binding legal condition for corporate residence and treaty access under §10 AO (place of effective management) and §42 AO (general anti-abuse rule). The UG must maintain real local board decisions, a physical office, employees and accounting records; a letter-box company without German substance risks being taxed as a foreign-controlled domestic entity, losing treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, and facing penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a criminal fine under §370 AO; for particularly serious cases imprisonment between 6 months and 10 years. Tax authorities will reassess all understated taxes and impose interest under §233a AO at 1.8% p.a., plus late-payment and filing surcharges.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes attributable to breach of statutory duties under §§34, 69 AO and §64 GmbHG. Criminal conviction can trigger an order for civil restitution of the evaded amounts, a travel ban pending trial, and a prohibition from serving as a director in Germany.
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CIT and trade tax reclassification at the level of the controlling foreign shareholder; denial of treaty benefits under §42 AO and the Multilateral Instrument payor-beneficiary rules; risk of forced liquidation by the registry; fines up to EUR 150,000 for false Transparency-Register filings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for corporate tax arrears if the substance deficiency is accompanied by negligent insolvency or fraud. Travel bans and exit checks may be imposed to secure criminal prosecution and foreign-trade enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to taxable income, deemed-dividend or royalty secondary adjustments; penalty up to 5% of the audit-adjusted income, with a minimum penalty of EUR 5,000 under §162 AO; additional criminal exposure if the misstatement is intentional.

Director personal liability: A managing director who knowingly executed a non-arm's-length pricing model can be held personally liable for the resulting corporate tax shortfall and may face criminal restitution orders, travel restrictions and disqualification from holding office.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Dividend, Royalty or Interest WHT

Statutory sanction: The German tax authority assesses the full unwithheld amount against the UG as payer, plus late-payment interest at 0.15% per month, a late-payment surcharge under §240 AO and a late-filing surcharge under §152 AO; criminal prosecution under §§370, 266a StGB is possible.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable under §69 AO for failure to withhold and remit; if tax was deducted from the counterparty but intentionally not forwarded, the director faces criminal liability under §266a StGB for misappropriation of withheld taxes, including imprisonment and asset seizure.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Shell-Ledger Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 150,000 per infringement under the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG), up to 1% of annual turnover for serious institutional violations; the UG may be barred from making further distributions and the registry may strike off the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and the UBO are personally liable for unpaid fines and may be subject to civil restitution actions; in money-laundering cases under §261 StGB, directors face imprisonment, asset forfeiture and judicial travel bans.
