Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification numbers, tax certificates and cross-border tax data exchange; the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT and payroll tax administration; Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) as federal tax policy authority; Generalzolldirektion for customs/EORI matters. The statutory electronic filing portal is ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire, Section 138 AO) with the Finanzamt at its registered seat within one month of incorporation or commencement of business, generally via ELSTER. The Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) covering corporate income tax and trade tax. For cross-border activities, a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately from BZSt through its central online application interface; an EORI number is required for customs operations and is obtained from Generalzolldirektion. The questionnaire requires disclosure of directors, shareholders and ultimate beneficial owners; non-resident directors must ensure valid service-of-process arrangements in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German domestic law does not provide a check-the-box election for a UG; the UG is mandatorily treated as a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) and taxed at entity level on worldwide income (subject to tax treaty credits). Available elections include: (i) Organschaft (corporate tax and trade tax fiscal unity and VAT consolidation) under Sections 14-19 KStG, Sections 2-4 GewStG and Section 2 UStG, requiring a direct or indirect majority shareholding and a five-year enforceable profit-and-loss transfer agreement; (ii) foreign classification elections such as the US Form 8832 check-the-box, which are effective only for non-German tax systems and do not alter German entity-level status; (iii) the small-business VAT exemption under Section 19 UStG within the statutory turnover thresholds; and (iv) compliance declarations and adjustments under the anti-hybrid rules of Sections 4k and 4j EStG and Section 8b KStG where a hybrid mismatch has been identified. Section 1a KStG, which allows certain partnerships to elect corporate taxation, is inapplicable because the UG is already taxed at entity level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every entrepreneur is obliged to register for VAT independently of turnover; however, under Section 19 UStG a small-business exemption applies where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000 (thresholds increased with effect from 1 January 2025). Mandatory VAT registration also arises where an entity makes intra-Community acquisitions exceeding EUR 12,500 in a calendar year. For a PE buyout SPV, financing income and participation management are generally VAT-exempt without triggering registration, whereas taxable management or advisory fees do create registration and invoicing duties.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero-rate export exemption applies under Section 4(1) No. 1 in conjunction with Section 6 UStG where goods are actually dispatched or transported from German territory to a non-EU destination and the export declaration has been accepted by German customs with the exporter holding satisfactory documentary evidence (Ausfuhrnachweis) and book evidence. Intra-Community supplies are exemption-eligible where a valid customer VAT ID, transport proof and an EC Sales List are present. For this PE buyout SPV, typical income components such as dividends, capital gains from share disposals and loan interest are exempt from VAT without a zero-rating claim; management, advisory or acquisition services supplied to non-resident fund clients fall outside German VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (Section 3a(2) UStG) when the customer is established outside Germany and is a taxable person, or are reverse-charged where the customer holds a German VAT ID.

  • Customs export declaration filed in the ATLAS/AES system and the corresponding export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitpapier) with customs release confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sale/acquisition contract (SPA, term sheet) evidencing the value, parties and payment terms in EUR or the contract currency
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or CMR consignment note for road freight, evidencing physical dispatch to the third-country destination
  • Banking evidence of export proceeds, including an inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103 confirmation or a bank statement identifying the non-resident buyer
  • VAT registration certificate (VAT ID or tax number) and, where applicable, a customs power of attorney for the appointed German fiscal/customs representative

From 1 January 2025, all German domestic B2B taxpayers are required to accept structured electronic invoices in XRechnung, ZUGFeRD or EDI/EN16931-compatible XML formats. The obligation to issue electronic invoices starts on 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and on 1 January 2028 for all German B2B taxpayers. Germany has not implemented a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API reporting or digital-stamping regime; transaction-based reporting to a federal BZSt repository is expected to commence alongside the full e-invoicing obligation, with the technical specifications still being prepared by the BMF and BZSt.

Export documentation must be retained for ten years under Section 147 AO. Where zero-rating proof is incomplete, false or obtained by fraudulent documentation, the Finanzamt retrospectively denies the exemption, assesses VAT at 19%, and applies late-payment surcharges of 1% per commenced month under Section 240 AO plus interest under Section 233a AO. Negligent misdeclaration constitutes an administrative offence under Section 378 AO punishable by fines up to EUR 50,000; intentional tax evasion under Section 370 AO is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in particularly serious cases including gang fraud or large-sum commercial evasion, together with pecuniary sanctions. Customs false declarations further trigger penalty proceedings under Article 42 of the Union Customs Code and the German Customs Administration Act, and repeated export-fraud patterns can lead to suspension of the UG's VAT registration and disqualification of its German fiscal standing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Germany applies unrestricted capital movement within the EU and vis-à-vis third countries, subject only to EU sanctions, national security screening on foreign direct investment, and statistical reporting requirements. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, and liquidation proceeds may be transferred abroad without prior authorization, provided that tax clearance and anti-money-laundering verification are satisfied. The governing distinction is not current-account vs capital-account convertibility, but the accurate reporting of cross-border payments and transactions to the Deutsche Bundesbank for monetary statistics and balance-of-payments reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement. German exporters are not obliged to convert foreign-currency export receipts into euro, nor are they subject to any prescribed realization timeline. Foreign currency may be held in domestic or foreign bank accounts, used to pay trade obligations, or converted through authorized financial intermediaries. The only legal obligations are the AWV reporting thresholds for outward and inward payments (currently EUR 12,500 or equivalent) and accurate classification of the underlying transaction code in the Deutsche Bundesbank’s payment statistics system.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), acting in coordination with the Federal Ministry of Finance, the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), and the German Customs Administration (Zoll) for AML/CFT enforcement. Germany maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration because currency and payments convertibility are governed by the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital and by the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passporting into Germany under PSD2, subject to a full German banking-law compliance assessment and BaFin registration for cross-border services. Feasibility rating: Low for offshore third-country EMIs, which may not engage in payment services or e-money issuance from outside the EEA without a BaFin licence or a German/EU-licensed subsidiary or branch. A German PE buyout SPV should rely primarily on fully licensed EEA credit institutions for escrow and operational treasury functions, and may use an EEA EMI only for niche low-value payment flows, provided the SPV’s corporate treasury risks are not concentrated in a passported EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and shareholder-loan capital injections from EU or third-country investors should be executed by SWIFT MT103 or the SEPA cross-border euro credit transfer mechanism. The transfer must reference the UG’s German IBAN and include the customer-referenced purpose code assigned by the sending bank under EU payment statistics rules. For balance-of-payments reporting, the UG or its bank must provide the Deutsche Bundesbank with the correct AWV transaction code for cross-border payment statistics. Recommended routing: (i) investor funds are aggregated at a German escrow account, (ii) the transfer instruction states 'share capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan disbursement' as the transaction purpose, and (iii) supporting board resolutions and subscription statements are retained for the bank’s source-of-funds verification. The bank must complete the Bundesbank’s external economic reporting (Außenwirtschaftsstatistik) where the transaction value exceeds EUR 12,500. For non-EU inbound transfers, the UG must also comply with any sanctions screening and, if applicable, file the Bundesbank’s notification of direct investment in domestic companies.Permitted clearing rails for trade settlements are: (1) SWIFT MT103 individual credit transfers for direct supplier or seller payments; (2) Commercial letters of credit (MT700 series) opened by the UG’s German bank on behalf of the acquisition SPV or its operating subsidiary; and (3) domestic euro payments via SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer for German and EU counterparties. The bank will require an AWV transaction code on every outbound transfer, in particular for payment of the purchase price, repayment of intercompany loans, and payment of professional fees. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% CIT (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, average 14%); combined nominal effective rate approx. 30-33% depending on municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Germany applies 25% capital gains / dividend withholding tax (Abgeltungsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, yielding a combined statutory rate of 26.375%. Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, a qualifying corporate shareholder holding at least 10% for 12 continuous months can receive dividends free of withholding tax. Under numerous German tax treaties the rate is reduced to 5% or 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory local documentation under Section 90(3) AO is triggered where aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 5,000,000 or in significant transactions above lower thresholds per the 2023 Verwaltungsgrundsätze; Country-by-Country reporting applies for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not administer separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to DIFC / ADGM. Substance is nonetheless a statutory and treaty requirement: Section 50d(3) EStG blocks treaty benefits for companies lacking economic activity; Section 42 AO serves as GAAR against artificial shell arrangements. A PE buyout SPV must maintain office premises, decision-making records, independent management, bank accounts and arm's-length intercompany agreements to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Section 370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases; court fine plus full back taxes; late payment interest at 0.5% per month; publication of the criminal judgment.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable for taxes under Sections 34/69 AO. Conviction subjects directors to personal asset seizure, travel bans, mutual recognition warrants, and civil veil-piercing where fraud is established.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR under Section 42 AO recharacterizes the transaction; Section 50d(3) EStG denies DTA relief; a 5% surcharge on positive income adjustments under Section 162(4) AO may be imposed; asset confiscation in money-laundering-linked cases.

Director personal liability: Limited liability is eroded where the UG is undercapitalized and functions as a mere cloak; directors face personal tax liability, disqualification and civil liability for misrepresentation to investors and tax authorities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Mandatory income correction, interest accrual at 6% p.a., 5% penalty surcharge on the adjustment under Section 162(4) AO, and possible fine under Section 378 AO if negligence is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false transfer pricing documentation can be held criminally liable; the corporate veil may be pierced for fraudulent schemes; civil restitution claims by investors and creditor claw-backs are possible.
AML / Money Laundering (GwG / StGB)

Statutory sanction: Section 261 StGB: imprisonment up to 5 years, up to 10 years in organized-crime cases; financial administrative sanctions under the German AML Act up to EUR 1,000,000 or 5% of annual worldwide turnover; closure of business accounts.

Director personal liability: Statutory directors and AML compliance officers are individually criminally liable for knowingly violating due-diligence obligations. Directors face travel bans, freezing orders, personal confiscation and prohibition from serving as directors in future German entities.
