Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet), portal e-MTA (www.emta.ee); e-Residency digital identity accepted for remote tax administration.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without an Estonian TIN must first register with the e-Business Register (Äriregister) to acquire a company registry code, which constitutes the statutory TIN for an Osaühing. The registrar automatically communicates the registration to the Tax and Customs Board; the entity then activates e-MTA via its e-Residency digital identity, PIN, or Estonian ID card to finalise tax registration. Non-resident owners are permitted without minimum capital requirements, but a local contact person may be appointed. If the OÜ engages in VAT-taxable supplies above the threshold or starts an activity in Estonia with a permanent establishment, a separate VAT registration and employer registration may be triggered.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation with territorial profit-distribution deferral: the OÜ is not pass-through; undistributed profits are tax-free; tax arises on distributions, deemed distributions, and fringe benefits at a headline rate of 20% (22% for certain fringe benefits and gifts). Permanent establishment profits of a foreign company are taxable only if attributable to the Estonian PE. — Foreign entities without an Estonian TIN must first register with the e-Business Register (Äriregister) to acquire a company registry code, which constitutes the statutory TIN for an Osaühing. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with territorial profit-distribution deferral: the OÜ is not pass-through; undistributed profits are tax-free; tax arises on distributions, deemed distributions, and fringe benefits at a headline rate of 20% (22% for certain fringe benefits and gifts). Permanent establishment profits of a foreign company are taxable only if attributable to the Estonian PE.

Available election forms

Estonia applies a single corporate income tax system and does not offer US-style check-the-box elections. Relevant statutory election mechanisms are limited to (i) optional VAT registration below the €40,000 threshold, (ii) election under Income Tax Act §50 to apply prepayment of income tax on regular distributions, and (iii) special election for avoiding hybrid mismatches under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive as transposed by the Income Tax Act, including transparency alignments with the relevant EEA/OECD tax status of a partner or beneficiary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000: mandatory registration is required if the taxable person's taxable turnover (excluding zero-rated exports and certain intra-Community supplies) during a calendar year exceeds €40,000 at face value; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. Distance sales to Estonia trigger registration at a threshold of €10,000 under the EU One Stop Shop rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent VAT applies to (i) goods dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU (genuine export transactions), (ii) certain transport-related services directly connected to exports, (iii) goods entering a customs warehouse under customs suspensive arrangements, and (iv) intra-Community supplies of goods to a registered EU VAT number. Services to non-residents are zero-rated only if supplied to a business customer in another country or specifically enumerated as export services under the VAT Act. A grocery import/export hub must demonstrate that retail goods in imported Asian condiment or produce categories are either fully re-exported or sold as B2B intra-Community supplies; ordinary retail sales to Estonian consumers remain liable to 22% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-A form) accepted by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board
  • Commercial Invoice containing exporter, consignee, incoterm, commodity code, and gross/net weights
  • Bill of Lading or CMR consignment note (for maritime/road transport) evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Proof of receipt by the non-EU consignee (signed POD, customs stamp released by destination country)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of receipt of foreign currency proceeds in the Estonian OÜ bank account
  • Packing list, container manifest, or warehouse evacuation confirmation correlating with export declaration

Estonia does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. E-invoicing is permitted and widely used, but not universally mandatory in the private sector; the state requires public-sector suppliers to transmit structured invoices via the PEPPOL network. Taxpayers report VAT monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on turnover; e-MTA accepts VAT declarations via API-based XML submissions, and every registered trader must maintain and submit an e-form for member-statist and intrastat reporting as applicable. There is no non-resident physical stamping or burning rule for invoices, but all VAT invoices must satisfy standard VAT Act data fields and be issued within prescribed time limits.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board audits export zero-rating through risk-based criteria, cross-referencing customs declarations with logistics documents, insurance data, and bank receipts. If a prima facie export is not substantiated within the statutory documentary deadline or within the prescribed 180-day examination window set out in the task protocol, the Tax Board reverses the zero-rating and recovers 22% VAT from the supplier plus interest from the original due date. Intentional or negligent generation of false export documents attracts an administrative fine up to the amount of evaded VAT, additional tax arrears, and criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code for tax evasion involving large amounts, with imprisonment up to five years or extended monitoring for a criminal organisation. The statutory limitation period for tax assessments is three years, extended to seven years for tax evasion or deliberate concealment. Recovery can be enforced through tax enforcement (pantimine and seizure, including cross-border recovery under EU mutual assistance measures).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia applies a fully liberalised capital and currency regime as a member of the euro area. Both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible into euro under European Union internal market law, the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and Estonia's obligations under IMF Article VIII. There are no general exchange control permits, no central bank approval for currency conversion, and no restrictions on the purchase, holding, or sale of foreign exchange. Profit remittance is subject solely to the Estonian income tax system: a dividend distribution trigger gives rise to the standard 20/80 regular distribution liability at the corporate level, with no additional foreign-exchange approval or withholding requirement for outbound remittances to non-resident shareholders. The practical hurdles are therefore tax-compliance, beneficial-ownership verification, and banking due-diligence requirements rather than FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia does not impose any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement on export proceeds. Exporters may retain receivables in foreign currency, maintain multi-currency accounts with Estonian credit institutions, and convert to EUR at their commercial discretion. The only obligations are statistical in nature: credit institutions automatically report cross-border payments above €1,000 for balance-of-payments and international transactions statistics to Eesti Pank. No regulatory approval or customs-backed export-verification certificate is required for the free disposition of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem, in coordination with the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for banking conduct and AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment channel, Low as a primary treasury banking solution. An EEA-licensed electronic money institution (EMI) may passport into Estonia under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and provide e-money accounts, IBANs, and payment services without a banking licence, but such institutions are not credit institutions and cannot extend credit, issue capital-guarantee accounts, or offer commercial letters of credit. Offshore or third-country digital EMIs that hold no EEA passport are not authorised to provide payment services to Estonian entities on a standalone basis, and their use would create regulatory, clearing, and anti-money-laundering risk. For a supermarket treasury, an EMI may be viable only for low-value payables and employee expense payments, while the principal operating account and trade-disbursement rail should sit with a tier-1 credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The primary compliant route for inbound capital from a non-resident shareholder is an inbound international payment in EUR or foreign currency to the bank account of the Estonian OÜ using the SWIFT MT103 wire standard, with the payment reference explicitly stating 'Share capital contribution' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement' and any applicable legal document reference. The receiving Estonian credit institution collects the payment for Eesti Pank balance-of-payments statistics and will carry out source-of-funds verification; no prior exchange-control approval is needed. All corporate resolutions, updated share register, bank authority documents, and beneficial-ownership declaration must be available to the bank upon request. Where a capital escrow account is used before registration, the escrow agreement and bank-provided account opening documentation are required. For intra-EU EUR transfers, SEPA Credit Transfer can be used only for smaller amounts up to normal SEPA limits; above €100,000 or where same-day value is critical, T2/TARGET2 settlement through the correspondent bank is recommended.Outbound trade settlements for imported Asian condiments and vegetables should be cleared via the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value foreign-currency and non-SEPA supplier payments, providing irrevocable and auditable payment evidence; (2) T2/TARGET2 real-time gross settlement for euro payments to critical suppliers, ensuring immediate finality; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer for small euro-denominated recurring payments to EU-based import wholesalers; (4) commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by the Estonian bank in favour of the overseas supplier, either sight or usance, with corresponding bank confirmation where prudent; and (5) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for low-risk established suppliers.
