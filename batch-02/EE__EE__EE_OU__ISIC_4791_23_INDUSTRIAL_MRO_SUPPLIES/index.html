Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An OÜ may be formed with share capital of at least EUR 0.01 per shareholder, subject to the statutory minimum nominal value of a share. No mandatory escrow account applies. Contributions must generally be made before the registration application unless a legally permitted deferred-contribution arrangement is used; unpaid contributions remain the shareholders' liability and may restrict dividend distributions.

Funding & unlock path: No special clearance is required for ordinary office and catalog-retail operations beyond general building, fire-safety, and occupational-health duties. Commercial premises must comply with applicable fire-safety requirements overseen by the Rescue Board, including evacuation routes, fire equipment, and occupancy conditions. Any warehouse or facility conversion may require building approval, fire-safety documentation, structural load verification, and workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, with oversight by the Labour Inspectorate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); digital operating portal: e-MTA (emta.ee/e-maksuamet); e-Residency remote administration via eesti.ee e-services.

E-government filing portal

The TIN is the Commercial Register registry code issued by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Äriregister/RIK) upon incorporation of the OÜ. Procedure for foreign entities: (1) file incorporation through the e-Business Register (e-Residency digital-ID channel) and obtain the registry code/TIN; (2) activate e-MTA user accounts for statutory representatives using the e-Residency smart-ID or Mobile-ID; (3) apply for VAT registration via the e-MTA portal (standard form 'VAT register application') once turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed the statutory threshold or for voluntary registration; (4) non-resident entities with no Estonian permanent establishment may also submit a direct VAT registration application via e-MTA, with the VAT ID issued in the format 'EE' + 9-digit registry code; no Estonian local tax representative is legally required, although one may be appointed for service of process; (5) register for the Intrastat reporting system via e-MTA if intra-EU acquisitions/dispatches exceed the statistical threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the Estonian OÜ is the taxpayer for corporate income tax and VAT. Under the Estonian distribution-based corporate income tax regime, undistributed profits are not taxed at the entity level regardless of source; income tax arises only upon profit distribution, at 20% of the gross distribution (25% grossed-up on net profit) for ordinary distributions, with a reduced 14% gross rate (16.28% grossed-up) available for 'regular' dividends based on the average of prior three-year distributions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Estonian OÜ is the taxpayer for corporate income tax and VAT. Under the Estonian distribution-based corporate income tax regime, undistributed profits are not taxed at the entity level regardless of source; income tax arises only upon profit distribution, at 20% of the gross distribution (25% grossed-up on net profit) for ordinary distributions, with a reduced 14% gross rate (16.28% grossed-up) available for 'regular' dividends based on the average of prior three-year distributions. Non-resident owners are not subject to Estonian taxation on retained earnings. Participation-exempt foreign dividends received from a qualifying subsidiary are not re-taxed on onward distribution. Territorial scope: the distribution-based tax attaches to the profits of the Estonian resident company; no branch-level remittance tax applies to an Estonian PE of a foreign company beyond the same distribution tax rules.

Available election forms

Estonia does not apply the US check-the-box system; the relevant elections are statutory domestic mechanisms: (1) corporate group CIT election - a resident parent with at least 80% direct or indirect ownership in Estonian subsidiaries may elect group treatment so that intra-group dividends and transfers of assets are disregarded for distribution tax purposes; (2) VAT group election - two or more taxable persons established in Estonia may elect to register as a single VAT taxable person; (3) regular dividend rate election - the 14% reduced rate applies automatically to the extent distributions are covered by qualifying prior-year dividend history, and no separate filing is required beyond the dividend resolution; (4) voluntary VAT registration election available to exporters of zero-rated industrial MRO goods before the statutory EUR 40,000 threshold is reached; (5) OSS/IOSS election for cross-border distance sales of goods and intra-EU B2C supplies.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 (annual taxable turnover of goods and services per calendar year for an Estonian-resident supplier); EU distance sales of goods to final consumers in other Member States: EUR 10,000 union-wide threshold for OSS; below the threshold, supplies remain taxable at the supplier's home state rate; B2B domestic and intra-EU MRO supplies to registered business customers are generally excluded from the supplier's registration threshold through reverse charge or customer-VAT-number evidence.

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory since April 2019 for all invoices addressed to Estonian public-sector entities, in structured formats (iArve 3.0, EN 16931, Finvoice compatible); no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control database, or digital stamping regime is currently in force for B2B/B2C; e-MTA uses API-based connectivity for automated VAT return filing and prefilled risk-scoring from bank transaction and customs declaration data; businesses must issue invoices electronically within 7 calendar days of the supply and preserve registry data for 7 years; EU ViDA implementation is expected to mandate B2B e-invoicing and digital reporting for intra-EU cross-border transactions in the 2028-2030 horizon.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full euro-area member. The euro is legal tender and there are no exchange controls or capital movement restrictions under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Capital and current account transactions are fully convertible in euros or any freely convertible foreign currency. Profit remittances, dividends, liquidation proceeds, and return of equity contributions may be freely transferred abroad without prior central bank approval. Only EU-wide sanctions, anti-money-laundering (AML), and counter-financing-of-terrorism (CFT) verification requirements apply. Under the Estonian distributed-profit taxation model, profit is taxed at the moment of distribution as a dividend, requiring audited annual accounts, a shareholder distribution resolution, and tax filing with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, repatriation timeline, or foreign-currency retention limit. Exporters may retain receipts in any currency in a licensed commercial bank account. All transactions settle in euro via SEPA/TARGET2 or clear through corresponding SWIFT channels. Cross-border payments exceeding EUR 10,000 are reported to Eesti Pank for balance-of-payments and monetary statistics; this is an aggregated statistical obligation, not an approval or surrender mechanism.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting as the Republic of Estonia's central bank within the Eurosystem; foreign exchange surveillance and financial supervision coordinated with Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) for licensed credit institutions and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Monese, and Revolut Business, operating under the EU passporting regime without an Estonian branch. The EMI must hold an electronic-money license in its EEA home state and comply with the Estonian payment-services notification procedure. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot originate commercial credit; high-volume B2B MRO distribution may be temporarily restricted by some EMIs' internal counterparty policies; settlement through an EMI is not direct TARGET2 access, so a tier-1 commercial bank account is required for settlement finality, documentary LC issuance, and over-limit foreign currency clearing. For an Estonian OÜ engaged in electronic catalog retail, an EMI account is legally viable as a collection rail but not a full treasury substitute.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injection is routed by SWIFT MT103 (outside SEPA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EU/EEA) to the OÜ's EUR operating account. The remittance instruction must reference the written shareholder resolution, the amount of paid-in capital as registered in the Estonian e-Business Register (Äriregistri), and use the balance-of-payments transaction purpose code for direct investment/capital subscription. No Estonian exchange-control approval is required. Acceptance is accelerated when the receiving bank receives a board banking resolution, the registry confirmation of equity increase, the notarized or digitally-signed shareholder resolution, and an AML-source-of-funds questionnaire. For eurozone origination, SEPA provides same-day value; for non-EUR, the FX conversion price is the bank's spot rate plus disclosed margin.Outbound trade settlement for industrial MRO supplies uses the following rails: (a) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR suppliers in EU/EEA; (b) SWIFT MT103 single transfer or MT102 batch for non-EEA suppliers; (c) commercial documentary letter of credit (UCP600) where the supplier requires payment security for high-value MRO goods; (d) SWIFT MT202 for interbank cover where split settlement is required. Payment instructions must quote the Estonian balance-of-payments classification for goods traded by ISIC 4791 and the commercial invoice amount. Ownership-transfer documents (commercial invoice, packing list, airway bill or bill of lading, inspection certificates) must be preserved to evidence the underpinning transaction.
