Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register maintained by the Tartu County Court Registration Department and administered electronically through the Estonian e-Business Register portal.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), including sections 135-154 governing private limited companies, sections 138-140 governing formation and articles, section 145 governing application for entry, and sections 180-188 governing management and representation. The Commercial Register Act governs registry administration and electronic filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for entry of a private limited company in the Commercial Register, supported by the establishment resolution or agreement, articles of association, and beneficial ownership information.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An OÜ has no statutory minimum share capital requirement. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 0.01. Contributions may be monetary or non-monetary and must be fully made in accordance with the formation documents; deferred contribution arrangements are permitted only where legally documented and disclosed. The company has no general escrow-account requirement, although a bank or payment institution may require a temporary account before registration.

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial clearance is required for a home-office or ordinary online retail company. Any leased warehouse or fulfillment premises must satisfy building-use, fire-safety, evacuation, electrical, occupational-health, workplace-risk, and structural-load requirements. Fire-safety compliance is overseen through applicable Rescue Board requirements, while workplace safety is supervised by the Labour Inspectorate. Construction, change of use, storage of hazardous goods, or public-access premises may require additional approvals.

Paid-in capital rule: An OÜ has no statutory minimum share capital requirement. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 0.01. Contributions may be monetary or non-monetary and must be fully made in accordance with the formation documents; deferred contribution arrangements are permitted only where legally documented and disclosed. The company has no general escrow-account requirement, although a bank or payment institution may require a temporary account before registration.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The management board resolves that the company accept the subscriptions for all issued shares in accordance with the establishment agreement and articles of association, and record the subscribed share capital and contribution obligations in the company records. The board adopts the registered office stated in the Commercial Register application and authorizes submission of all incorporation filings through the e-Business Register. The board appoints [Name] and [Name] as management board members, each authorized to represent the company [jointly/individually] as registered. The board further authorizes opening operational bank and payment accounts and appoints the company accountant or accounting service provider.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No general industrial licensing board is required for ordinary internet retail through a Shopify DTC webstore. Product-specific regulators may apply depending on goods sold; the Consumer Protection and Technical Regulatory Authority is the principal market-surveillance authority.

Category: No general manufacturing or operating permit for retail sale via the internet. Separate permits, notifications, or registrations may apply to regulated products, including food, cosmetics, chemicals, alcohol, medicines, medical devices, or excise goods.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, ETCA) — national e-service portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee); corporate registry: Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) via e-äriregister.

E-government filing portal

A resident OÜ receives its statutory tax identifier automatically upon incorporation: the 8-digit registry code (registrikood) issued by the Commercial Register serves as the universal legal and tax identifier; the VAT registration number (EE prefix) is applied for separately in e-MTA. A non-resident entity without an Estonian permanent establishment must register for VAT before making taxable supplies in Estonia: file the VAT registration application in e-MTA (using the e-Residency digital ID or a power-of-attorney), attach constitutive documents and proof of economic activity, appoint a local VAT representative or agent when ETCA requires one, and obtain the EE-prefixed VAT number normally within five working days. There is no separate TIN issuance step for a foreign company; the VAT number and the commercial-registry code are the operative identifiers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A resident OÜ receives its statutory tax identifier automatically upon incorporation: the 8-digit registry code (registrikood) issued by the Commercial Register serves as the universal legal and tax identifier; the VAT registration number (EE prefix) is applied for separately in e-MTA. A non-resident entity without an Estonian permanent establishment must register for VAT before making taxable supplies in Estonia: file the VAT registration application in e-MTA (using the e-Residency digital ID or a power-of-attorney), attach constitutive documents and proof of economic activity, appoint a local VAT representative or agent when ETCA requires one, and obtain the EE-prefixed VAT number normally within five working days. There is no separate TIN issuance step for a foreign company; the VAT number and the commercial-registry code are the operative identifiers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Estonian Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus) imposes entity-level corporate income tax only when profits are distributed, with 0% tax on undistributed profit; this statutory deferral requires no election. Optional elections and regimes: (1) small-company pass-through — a private limited company in which natural persons hold more than 50% of the shares and which meets the statutory payment and size conditions may elect to be taxed as a partnership, with profits taxed directly to shareholders; (2) dividend rate election — distributions are taxed at the standard 20/80 gross-up (25% effective on the gross distribution) or at the reduced 14/86 gross-up for regular distributions satisfying the previous-year taxed-dividend rule; (3) VAT-group election under the Estonian VAT Act for Estonian-established related companies; (4) the reverse-charge and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) elections for cross-border goods. Estonia has no US-style check-the-box election; the ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act override any character-mismatch arrangements. Group consolidation is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year for domestic taxable supplies by an Estonian-established seller; EUR 10,000 union-wide annual turnover for intra-EU distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C electronic services (OSS threshold); no threshold for a non-established seller — VAT registration or use of the OSS/IOSS framework is due from the first euro of taxable supply in Estonia.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported by the Estonian seller from the EU to a third country are zero-rated for VAT if the exporter proves (a) dispatch and departure from the EU within the statutory export window, in practice within 3 months of the supply; (b) an accepted customs export declaration naming the Estonian seller as exporter or declarant; and (c) transport and destination evidence showing receipt outside the EU. Intra-EU business-to-business supplies of goods are zero-rated only where the customer's valid VIES VAT number is held and the recapitulative statement (KMDINF) is filed. Services ancillary to exported goods such as freight, handling, inspection and insurance are zero-rated. Digital services and electronic products supplied to non-EU consumers fall outside Estonian VAT under place-of-supply rules, with input VAT recoverable subject to refund rules. For imported consumer goods with a value not exceeding EUR 150, the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) regime may be used; otherwise VAT is collected at importation.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/ESAD) accepted and released by the Estonian customs office, stating the seller as exporter and the goods value, HS code and Incoterms
  • Transport document proving physical departure from the EU: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note, including the exit-office stamp or electronic customs release
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract identifying the third-country buyer, description, quantity, unit price and delivery terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT/MT103 advice, or bank statement evidencing receipt and, where applicable, conversion of the export proceeds
  • Destination-country import documentation or certificate of entry demonstrating arrival of the goods outside the EU
  • Proof of dispatch within the statutory export window, being goods departing the EU within 3 months from supply or the documented justification for any delay

Estonia does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-authorisation, or digital stamping regime. VAT reporting remains periodic through the e-MTA using monthly or quarterly KMD and KMDINF returns. The domestic e-invoicing mandate is phased: from 1 January 2025 every VAT-registered person must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 July 2025 domestic business-to-business invoices must be issued as e-invoices through Peppol/EN 16931 compliant service providers; business-to-government e-invoicing is already mandatory. e-MTA provides APIs for accredited accounting and ERP software, but no line-level clearance or continuous transaction control is imposed.

ETCA and Estonian Customs apply risk-based post-clearance controls and may suspend refunds pending verification of export files. Where the 0% rate is claimed without adequate or truthful proof, VAT is reassessed at 22% with statutory default interest from the due date; falsely declaring goods for export or fabricating documents constitutes tax and customs fraud, punishable by fines and imprisonment for up to 5 years for intentional evasion, and extends the reassessment limitation period. Input VAT refunds previously paid are clawed back up to the statutory assessment window, generally 3 years, extended in deliberate-concealment cases. Directors and beneficial owners may be held jointly and severally liable under the Taxation Act, and repeated abuse leads to loss of zero-rating privileges and customs compliance debarment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. Estonia is a euro-area member (euro since 1 January 2011) and applies the EU free movement of capital regime under TFEU Articles 63–66; no capital controls, exchange restrictions, or prior approval requirements exist for inbound equity, shareholder loans, trade settlements, or profit distributions. Dividends may be remitted freely and without Estonian withholding tax to any non-resident; the only tax burden is the 20% (14% regular payments) corporate income tax triggered at distribution. USD revenue received via Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe may be held or converted at commercial bank rates with no conversion obligation or surrender of proceeds to the central bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export/service receipts in Estonia. Entities are free to retain foreign currency in onshore or offshore accounts. The only formal obligations are: (i) banks' automatic balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Eesti Pank for cross-border transfers, (ii) EU Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 physical cash declaration at the border for movements of EUR 10,000 or more, and (iii) general AML customer-due-diligence and payee/payer travel-rule data under Regulation (EU) 2015/847.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the euro-area national central bank; banking conduct and AML/CFT supervision exercised by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority); financial intelligence for AML matters handled by the Rahapesu Andmebüroo (Estonian FIU) within the Police and Border Guard Board.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary liquidity rail. EEA-licensed digital EMIs and e-money institutions (e.g., Wise Europe AS with an Estonian EMI license, Monese, Payoneer via partner banks) provide multi-currency USD/GBP/EUR accounts, local receiving numbers, and faster remote onboarding for e-resident OÜs. Legal constraints under the E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC and the Estonian Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act: EMIs cannot extend credit, must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts, and are not covered by the Estonian deposit guarantee scheme. For statutory trade settlement, recurring supplier disbursements, and merchant-liquidity integrity, a primary account at a licensed credit institution (Swedbank/SEB/LHV) remains mandatory; the EMI is used for marketplace collection and FX diversification.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed or reinvested profits; 20% tax on profit distributions (applied as 20/80 on the net distributed amount, equivalent to 25% on the grossed-up pre-tax profit)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory income tax on the gross dividend amount (calculated as 20/80 of the net dividend, i.e., EUR 25 tax per EUR 100 net cash dividend); reduced 14% rate (14/86) applies to qualifying regular distributions; EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive provides a 0% rate for an EU parent company holding at least 10% of the OÜ for an uninterrupted 24-month period.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 50,000. Master File: mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Documentation must follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, be prepared in Estonian or English, and be delivered to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board within 60 days of a formal written request.

Economic substance scope: Estonia applies the EU ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule and case-law substance doctrines. An e-Residency OÜ operating a Shopify DTC brand must demonstrate genuine economic activity: real management decisions in Estonia, an Estonian bank account, contractual autonomy, inventory/price/currency risk assumption, and coherent operational substance. A letter-box OÜ with no employees and no local decision-making is exposed to MTA re-characterization, denial of treaty/directive benefits, deemed distribution adjustments, and imposition of 20/80 tax on profits artificially routed through the Estonian entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealed Dividend Distribution

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full distribution tax at 20/80, 0.06% daily arrears interest, administrative fines up to EUR 1,000 for repeated filing violations, substitute assessment by the MTA, and criminal prosecution under Penal Code section 344 for tax evasion with imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the controlling shareholder exposing the company to intentional non-declaration face joint and several civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans pending criminal investigation, disqualification from management, and imprisonment up to 5 years where corporate veil is pierced as a vehicle for fraud.
Shell Company / GAAR Artificiality

Statutory sanction: The MTA may disregard the artificial interposition, restrofitively recharacterize foreign service fees as constructive dividends, impose 20/80 income tax on the adjusted amount, add 0.06% daily interest, and levy administrative penalties for non-compliance with the substance and documentation obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham entity are exposed to veil-piercing claims requiring civil restitution of diverted funds, personal liability for the OÜ's tax debts if the company is insolvent, travel bans and extradition risk in cross-border fraud cases, and criminal sanctions for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing the taxable distribution base; 20/80 income tax on the adjusted amount; 0.06% daily late-payment interest; administrative fines up to EUR 1,000 for documentation failures; and criminal liability for deliberate fraudulent pricing that materially reduces Estonian tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the non-arm's length pricing are jointly liable for the resulting tax arrears and penalties if the company lacks funds; courts may impose civil recovery, disqualification, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for willful transfer pricing fraud.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Complicity

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act and Penal Code sections 392-394: freezing of bank and payment accounts, confiscation of criminally obtained property, criminal fines against the legal person, deregistration of the OÜ, and imprisonment up to 8 years for aggravated money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors implicated in the laundering scheme face personal asset freezing, travel bans, extradition proceedings, disqualification, and imprisonment up to 8 years; the corporate veil is pierced where the OÜ is used as an instrumentality for financial crime.
