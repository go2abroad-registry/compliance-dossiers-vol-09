Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID and cross-border matters; local tax office (Finanzamt) for income/corporate/trade tax registration; ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) for electronic filing and registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming a German UG must first register with the local Finanzamt using the legal-form questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung), obtain a national tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; then apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) via the German ELSTER portal or the official application portal for intra-Community transactions. Non-resident taxable entrepreneurs must apply to the local Finanzamt for VAT registration before commencing taxable supplies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity forming a German UG must first register with the local Finanzamt using the legal-form questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung), obtain a national tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; then apply separately to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) via the German ELSTER portal or the official application portal for intra-Community transactions. Non-resident taxable entrepreneurs must apply to the local Finanzamt for VAT registration before commencing taxable supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Corporate tax grouping (Organschaft) election allowing a UG to be treated as an income-tax-neutral part of an upstream controlling corporation for corporate and trade tax purposes, provided profit-and-loss transfer agreement and majority voting/control requirements are met; hybrid mismatch election and deduction/offset adjustments under Section 4k EStG and Section 12a KStG; no check-the-box election exists under German domestic law for resident corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 previous-year turnover and EUR 50,000 expected current-year turnover for small-entrepreneur exemption; non-resident entrepreneurs normally have no small-entrepreneur threshold and must register from the first taxable supply; intra-Community distance-sale registration threshold via OSS is EUR 10,000 cross-border turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export of goods applies under Section 6 UStG where the seller is verified as the exporter under the customs export declaration, the goods leave the EEA in the course of the supply, and the exporter keeps documentary proof of export; intra-Community supplies to another EU taxable person may be zero-rated under Section 4 No. 1(b) and Section 6a UStG with a valid VAT ID of the purchaser and transport evidence; B2B supplies of offshore/advertising intermediation services to non-resident taxation customers are generally outside the scope of German VAT under the reverse-charge/B2B place-of-supply rule, so no refund mechanism is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) via ATLAS/US through German customs
  • Transport evidence, e.g. CMR consignment note, Bill of Lading, air waybill, or shipment tracking record
  • Commercial export invoice with incoterms and export consignee identity
  • Frameworks used by Finanzamt to verify export chain: customs export document summary in the export application and the Export Accompanying Document
  • Payment evidence and inward-remittance confirmation where the tax office challenges actual cross-border payment

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory government API clearance; however, from 1 January 2025 domestic B2B e-invoicing is mandatory in principle with phased enforcement: all B2B suppliers must issue structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD standards) and recipients must be able to receive them; by 2027/2028 full processing obligations apply, with optional transmission via e-mail, EDI, or Peppol-compliant access points.

German tax offices routinely require fully documented export chains within 6 months and may deny zero-rating on audit if evidence is incomplete, resulting in VAT re-assessment at 19% plus default interest (currently 1.8% per annum on the incremental tax), late-filing penalties under Sections 152a AO, and liability for fraud penalties under Sections 369 and 370 AO including imprisonment up to 10 years for willful tax evasion; intentional misuse of zero-rated gaming monetization streams triggers retrospective clawbacks, promoter penalties, and joint-and-several liability for principals.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full convertibility for current and capital account transactions in accordance with EU Treaty provisions on the free movement of capital (Articles 63-66 TFEU). The Euro is the legal tender, and neither the Bundesbank nor BaFin imposes exchange control approvals, surrender requirements, or prior licensing for profit remittance. A German UG may remit dividends, royalties, interest or repayment of shareholder loans to non-residents without regulatory consent. The only structural hurdle is the 26.375% withholding tax on dividends and certain other income streams unless reduced or eliminated by an applicable double taxation treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. Automated filing obligations under the AWV, rather than convertibility limitations, constitute the principal administrative burden for cross-border treasury transactions exceeding EUR 12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Germany. Exporters may retain foreign currency freely, use it for payments, or convert it at market rates. The UG is merely required to report external receipts and payments over EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics, identifying the counterparty country, value date, purpose code and transaction nature. Such reporting does not impose repatriation deadlines or conversion amounts; it is a monitoring mechanism only.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) acting jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) and the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG). The Bundesbank is the competent authority for external sector statistics, cross-border payment reporting and the monitoring of capital flows; BaFin regulates banking, payment and financial services conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) may operate in Germany on a passport basis and provide multi-currency IBANs, payment routing, and cost-effective disbursement rails. However, an offshore EMI from a non-EEA jurisdiction is not an adequate primary treasury counterparty because it cannot offer a German IBAN, holds funds as electronic money rather than bank deposits, is not covered by the German Deposit Guarantee Scheme, and may be subject to correspondent banking de-risking. An offshore EMI may be used as a secondary collection channel for AppLovin or AdMob payouts after the UG has a stable relationship with a German or EU credit institution, but legal exposure arises if the EMI’s license is not valid for commercial cross-border collection in Germany. The UG should verify that the EMI is either BaFin-authorised or passported under ZAG with a fully filed agent notification before channelling material treasury balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions from non-resident shareholders should be settled by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's credit institution to the UG's German operational account in EUR. The wire must carry an accurate AWV purpose code—generally 'D' for capital contribution or 'N' for shareholder loan—together with the UG's registration and tax reference codes to permit automated Bundesbank statistical reporting. Before crediting, the German bank will verify the remitter's account name, the availability of a corroborating shareholder resolution, and the absence of sanctions hits. Where the equity contribution exceeds the registered share capital of the UG, the surplus must be allocated to the capital reserve (Kapitalrücklage) and documented in the annual financial statements. Foreign currency injections may be converted to EUR at the bank's mid-market rate with a spot confirmation retained; alternatively, the capital may remain in a foreign-currency sub-account, but for German GAAP reporting the functional currency is the Euro and a conversion formula must be disclosed.Outbound payments for trade settlements, licensing fees, ad-network intermediation fees, and contractor disbursements should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for immediate cross-border high-value transfer, or via SEPA Direct Debit for recurring EUR payments to counterparties within the Single Euro Payments Area. When purchasing advertising inventory, source code, or development services from abroad against performance milestones, the UG may use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit subject to ICC UCP 600 as a settlement rail, with the German bank acting as issuing bank and the beneficiary presenting service acceptances, invoicing and delivery certificates. The UG must retain evidence for each outbound payment and file the relevant AWV report when the payment equals or exceeds EUR 12,500; payments to related parties require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. For hybrid rails, the UG may also use real-time gross settlement through T2 and SEPA Instant for low-value, high-volume payouts to ad-network partners, while SWIFT MT103 remains the default for non-EEA counterparties.Plain vanilla EUR/USD and EUR/GBP forward contracts with a German credit institution under an ISDA or DRV master agreement, used to lock in ad-network revenue conversion rates for a horizon of up to 12 monthsNon-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts in emerging-market currencies such as INR, BRL, TRY, IDR, VND and MXN, settled in EUR, where the German bank cannot offer physical deliverability due to local currency restrictionsCross-currency swaps between EUR and USD or EUR and GBP for matching the currency of ad-network receivables with the operating expenses and distribution partner payments of the UGFX options including EUR/USD and EUR/GBP call options to hedge unexpected volume spikes and protect against adverse currency movement while retaining upside participation
