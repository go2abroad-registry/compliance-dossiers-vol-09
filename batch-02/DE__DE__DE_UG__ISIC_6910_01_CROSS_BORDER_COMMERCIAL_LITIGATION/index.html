Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt); local tax office (Finanzamt); online filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

A UG must first complete commercial registration in the German Trade Register. The managing director then files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' with the locally competent Finanzamt, which assigns the corporate tax number (Steuernummer). For VAT purposes, the UG must separately apply online to BZSt for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer). Foreign shareholders or foreign-resident directors must submit certified corporate documents, identification evidence, a declaration of the place of management, and appoint a domestic authorised recipient for service. A fiscal representative may be required where collection of VAT is at risk.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election. Tax classification follows mandatory legal-form rules. Available statutory elections are the corporate fiscal unity election (Organschaft) under §§ 14-17 KStG through a profit-and-loss transfer agreement, the VAT grouping election under § 2(2) UStG, and the small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG for turnovers below the statutory thresholds. Hybrid mismatch treatment under § 4k EStG and ATAD rules is mandatory, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for taxable persons; any entrepreneur is required to register before commencing taxable activity. The domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer) applies only where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000. Non-established foreign suppliers must register regardless of turnover whenever German VAT is chargeable unless the reverse-charge mechanism shifts liability to the domestic recipient.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to a non-EU destination qualify for zero-rating under § 4(1) No. 1a and § 6 UStG when the goods physically leave the Union customs territory and the exporter retains verifiable proof of exit. Cross-border commercial litigation, arbitration, and asset-tracing services are generally outside German VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule if the recipient is established outside Germany; for third-country non-business recipients, legal services are not taxable in Germany when the effective place of supply is outside Germany. The invoice must state the foreign recipient, the place of supply, and, where relevant, the client's VAT identification number.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) processed through ATLAS where physical goods are exported
  • Export confirmation (Ausfuhrnachweis) proving physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice or fee note referencing the specific service or goods sold
  • Transport documents where applicable (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR)
  • Contract or engagement letter showing the foreign client and place of performance
  • Inward remittance certificate or authenticated SWIFT/MT103 credit confirmation showing receipt of payment from abroad

Germany is implementing the E-Rechnung mandate under EN 16931. From 1 January 2025 every taxable person must be able to receive structured electronic invoices. From 1 January 2027 businesses with more than EUR 800,000 prior-year turnover must issue electronic invoices, and from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B supplies must be issued as structured electronic invoices. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, BZSt API validation, or digital stamping; the obligation is to transmit machine-readable XML invoices in the prescribed formats.

The tax authorities may retrospectively deny export zero-rating where documentation is incomplete, contradictory, or fraudulent. Output VAT becomes recoverable from the supplier plus late-payment interest under § 233a AO at 0.5% per month, and input VAT may be clawed back. Incorrectly issued export invoices may trigger liability under § 14c UStG. Deliberate misdeclaration may be prosecuted as tax evasion under § 370 AO with penalties of up to five years' imprisonment, unlimited criminal fines, and confiscation of economic benefit; negligence-based documentation failures carry separate fines under § 26 UStG and § 378 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies unrestricted current and capital account convertibility within EU free-movement-of-capital disciplines and IMF Article VIII obligations. There are no mandatory conversion saddles, no exchange licensing bar, and no profit-remittance veto for non-resident shareholders. Practical compliance for the UG consists of balance-of-payments reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank on transactions exceeding EUR 12,500, plus standard CRS/FATCA and tax withholding procedures on dividends or deemed profit distributions. The UG must first allocate 25% of adjusted annual profit to its statutory reserve (as required for an Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) before any cross-border remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or conversion obligation exists under German law. For the litigation business, client recoveries, damage awards and legal fees may be kept in foreign currency offshore. Mandatory reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank applies to all cross-border payments received from non-residents in excess of EUR 12,500; the resident recipient must report by the 7th day of the month following the payment month, using the central bank's electronic reporting channels. The AWV sets no conversion ratio, but every inbound payment must be classified under the 'Zahlungsbilanzstatistik' account categories.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) as central bank, acting jointly with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz) and the German Customs Administration (Generalzolldirektion) enforcing the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for core treasury, acceptable only as a supplementary collection rail. German and EEA-regulated EMIs can hold funds and provide IBANs under PSD2, but they cannot open a lawyer's statutory trust account (Anderkonto) recognized under the Bundesrechtsanwaltsordnung, and a IOLTA-equivalent cannot be hosted by an offshore EMI. Legal counterparties and German courts will normally reject settlement or trustee disbursements from a non-regulated EMI. A UG engaged in multi-jurisdictional litigation must maintain its principal clearing relationship at a licensed credit institution; offshore digital EMIs are constrained by cross-border marketing restrictions, lack of KYC consistency, and AWV-reporting intermediation weaknesses.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from non-resident owners must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the UG's German-designated account. The SWIFT field 70 must cite the corporate reference, the subject 'Gesellschaftereinlage' (capital contribution), and the tax/statutory purpose code so that the receiving bank can classify the transaction as a direct investment under the AWV. Before settlement, the UG must file the notarised capital-increase resolution with the commercial register, register all beneficial owners, and open a separate capital/escrow ledger if the funds are subject to court or client protection. Any transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank through the prescribed BBk electronic report by the 7th day of the following month, and the origin must be documented by bank statements, SWIFT sender references and UBO/source-of-wealth evidence.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements, expert witnesses, court fees, enforcement costs and client monies should be executed as SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary and reference information. For commercial litigation the dominant clearing rails are (a) outgoing wire transfers on the target payment rails 'IOLTA_Trust_Account' and 'Wire'; (b) attorney trust-account cheques/direct debits for domestic German payments; and (c) a commercial letter of credit only where a court requires a security bond or where a settlement agreement establishes a performance guarantee to an adverse party. Each outbound transfer must be tied to a matter number, a client trust-order document, and post-transfer AWV reporting where the payment exceeds EUR 12,500. Allocation between current-account and capital-account statistics must follow the true economic nature of the payment.Outright FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) with a physical settlement date, covered by an underlying litigation fee or client recovery exposure.FX swaps (Devisenswaps) used for temporary liquidity conversion without creating open speculative positions.Cross-currency swaps (Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps) strictly matching the maturity and cash-flow profile of a non-EUR arbitration claim or hedging obligation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with EEA/UK/US-regulated dealer banks only when the underlying Euro/foreign-exchange risk cannot be physically settled.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.825% (15% corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax amount; trade tax not included in this headline rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% statutory dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, i.e. 25% * 1.055 = 26.375%). Additional trade tax may apply on the dividend for the distributing entity in specific structures.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax return must be filed by 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end. Automatic extension to 31 December of the following calendar year applies when the return is prepared by a tax adviser (Steuerberater). Further administrative extensions are possible upon application to the local tax office (Finanzamt).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 for cross-border transactions with related parties; transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) must be prepared contemporaneously if the taxpayer is an enterprise with related-party cross-border transactions exceeding this threshold. Master File is required when the group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100 million and the German entity's transactions with related parties exceed EUR 5 million. Documentation must be submitted within 60 days upon request by the tax authorities. Penalties for failure to produce documentation include a surcharge of at least 5% of the adjusted profit, capped at EUR 1 million, and an estimation of taxable income in case of non-compliance.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the form found in UAE or other offshore jurisdictions. However, domestic anti-abuse rules under Sec. 42 AO (Abgabenordnung) and the general substance-over-form doctrine require that a German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft) maintain adequate economic substance: actual business premises, personnel, and management functions in Germany. The UG must conduct its business activities through its German-registered office and cannot be a mere letterbox company. For treaty benefits and EU Directive relief, the beneficial ownership test and the principal purpose test (PPT) under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) apply. German tax authorities may reject profit attribution to a German entity that lacks substance and reallocate income to the ultimate shareholder under Sec. 8a KStG (interest deduction restrictions) and Sec. 1 AStG (transfer pricing correction). Recent case law (e.g., BFH decision I R 54/17) confirms that a German entity must be substantively involved in the value-creating functions to claim income allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 50,000 for tax evasion by a legal entity under Sec. 370 AO read with Sec. 30 OWiG. The corporate entity may be subject to asset forfeiture. The administrative surcharge for tax evasion may reach 100% of the evaded tax. Additionally, the tax authority may impose a penalty of up to EUR 50,000 under Sec. 378 AO for negligent tax evasion. Interest on unpaid assessed tax accrues at 0.5% per month (Sec. 233a AO).

Director personal liability: Directors of the UG are personally liable for intentional or grossly negligent failure to meet tax obligations under Sec. 34 AO and Sec. 69 AO, including civil restitution of the tax loss. Criminal prosecution of directors under Sec. 370 AO may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary fine. In aggravated cases of commercial or gang-related tax evasion, imprisonment of up to ten years under Sec. 370a AO (repealed effective 2009 but still relevant for older offenses) or the general fraud provisions under Sec. 263 StGB; travel bans and surrender of passport may be ordered under Sec. 132 StPO to secure proceedings. Directors are also barred from serving as directors for up to five years upon conviction for tax evasion or bankruptcy-related offenses (Sec. 6 GmbHG, Sec. 76 InsO).
Shell Company / Missing Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may disregard the corporate entity and attribute its income to the ultimate shareholder (transparent treatment) under Sec. 42 AO; denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test; denial of loss deduction and input VAT recovery; retroactive tax assessment within 10 years (Sec. 169 AO) for cases of tax evasion and within 7 years for gross negligence. Trade tax and corporate income tax reassessment with interest and penalties up to EUR 1 million for missing transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for corporate tax debts if tax was evaded intentionally; the German Federal Fiscal Court (BFH) treats the managing director as a responsible representative under Sec. 69 AO. Criminal exposure for forming a criminal organization (Sec. 129 StGB) if the UG is used as a vehicle for organized tax fraud. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be ordered as part of criminal proceedings. The director may be disqualified from managing a GmbH/UG for up to five years under Sec. 6 GmbHG.
AML / Bankruptcy Irregularities (Insolvenzverschleppung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability for managing directors under Sec. 15a InsO (delay in filing for insolvency) punishable by imprisonment up to three years; under Sec. 283 StGB (bankruptcy fraud) up to five years; administrative fines up to EUR 1 million for AML violations under Sec. 56 GwG; loss of the corporate limited liability and the UG can be ordered to be dissolved by the register court (Amtsgericht).

Director personal liability: Directors are strictly liable to creditors for payments made after insolvency maturity (Sec. 64 GmbHG); civil restitution equal to the amount of prohibited payments; the corporate veil is pierced and the director must compensate the company's creditors. In cross-border matters, German courts may issue a European Arrest Warrant and international seizure orders. Directors face imprisonment of up to five years for bankruptcy fraud and up to ten years if the offense involves commercial gang activity; the UG's limited liability does not protect directors for intentional misconduct or gross negligence.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Verrechnungspreisdokumentation)

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under Sec. 1 AStG with a surcharge of 5% of the upward adjustment, minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 1 million per benchmarking deficiency. Additional tax interest at 0.5% per month if an adjustment is made for a past year.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate transfer pricing documentation or knowingly file incorrect tax returns are personally liable under Sec. 69 AO. Criminal exposure for tax evasion if the mispricing was intentional; the managing director may face imprisonment up to five years and civil liability for the corporate tax shortfall plus accrued interest.
