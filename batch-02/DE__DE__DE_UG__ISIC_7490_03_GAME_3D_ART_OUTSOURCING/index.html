Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT ID and central tax matters; local Finanzamt for income/corporation tax; electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned UG must be registered in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister) through a notary. Subsequently, using ELSTER, the UG must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt, obtaining the corporation tax and trade tax registration. A separate application to the BZSt (using the USt 1 TU form) is required for the VAT ID (USt-IdNr). The procedure is fully electronic; no minimum capital is required for the UG, but annual statutory reserve requirements apply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' style election exists. Statutory consolidation mechanisms include forming an Organschaft (tax group) for corporate and trade tax purposes, which permits Austrian-style full income consolidation when the controlling enterprise holds at least 75% of the subsidiary and imposes financial, economic, and organizational integration. A separate election allows VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Section 2(3) UStG using a monthly fixed-period option. Anti-hybrid elections under Section 4k EStG have been introduced as part of the German ATAD implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT registration is mandatory upon commencement of taxable entrepreneurial activity; the small-business exception (Kleinunternehmer, § 19 UStG) applies below EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 expected current-year turnover, but it waives output VAT and denies input VAT recovery; no separate economic threshold triggers registration beyond the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU are zero-rated under Section 6 UStG where the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and sufficient evidence is retained. For services, including 3D art outsourcing, supplied to non-resident entrepreneurs, the place of supply is the recipient's established location under Section 3a(2) UStG, placing the supply outside the scope of German VAT (equivalent to zero-rating). Eligibility requires the recipient to hold a valid VAT ID, the absence of German real-estate or on-site service nexus, and documentary proof of the recipient's entrepreneurial status and non-German place of establishment. The zero-rating/outside-scope treatment applies regardless of whether the art is transmitted electronically or on physical media.

  • Customs export declaration (ATLAS / Ausfuhranmeldung) for any physical media or tangible deliverables crossing the EU border
  • Bill of lading or airway bill (for physical consignments); CMR consignment note for road transport
  • Commercial invoice stating the international nature of the transaction and, for services, the reverse-charge reference under Sections 3a and 14(4) UStG
  • Executed contract or order confirmation evidencing the recipient's status as an entrepreneur and the non-German place of establishment
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT confirmation showing receipt of foreign currency within the applicable statutory deadlines
  • Recipient's VAT ID validated via VIES and a declaration from the recipient confirming business use of the services

From 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers in Germany must be capable of receiving structured electronic invoices (EN 16931) in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD formats. Mandatory issuing obligations phase in from 1 January 2027 for businesses with over EUR 800,000 turnover and become fully mandatory from 1 January 2028. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital API stamping; invoices are transmitted via the PEPPOL network or other compatible channels. VAT returns and recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung) are filed electronically via ELSTER. Intrastat dispatch threshold for Germany is EUR 400,000 (2025).

German tax authorities may retrospectively assess VAT and monthly interest at 0.5% per month where zero-rating is not substantiated to the statutory standard. The regular limitation period is four years, extending to ten years for tax evasion. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims trigger criminal liability under Section 370 AO (Steuerhinterziehung), punishable by fines and up to ten years' imprisonment, plus additional fines under Section 26a UStG for supplying false documents. Directors and managing officers assume personal liability under Sections 34 and 69 AO, and the zero-rated status is retroactively revoked, causing the VAT to become payable together with penalty surcharges and denial of input VAT credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions pursuant to Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is fully convertible; a UG may maintain multi-currency bank accounts and no authorization from the Bundesbank is required to send or receive foreign currency. Profit remittance is unrestricted from an exchange-control standpoint, but practical hurdles remain: German corporate law requires distributable profits evidenced by annual financial statements under the HGB and a managing-director/partner distribution resolution; tax law imposes a 25% capital-yield withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (i.e., 26.375% aggregate) unless a DTA or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces or eliminates the levy; and banks apply source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership checks before releasing high-value dividends to non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Germany. Export invoices denominated in USD, GBP, or other currencies may be credited to a foreign-currency account or converted to EUR at the company's instruction. The only binding rule is the external-statistics reporting duty under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV): a resident company receiving cross-border payments from non-residents totaling EUR 12,500 or more per counterparty/calendar month must file a Zahlungsempfänger report electronically with the Deutsche Bundesbank by the 7th calendar day of the following month. A comparable report (Zahler) applies to outbound payments. No central bank approval or conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), acting within the Eurosystem alongside the European Central Bank (ECB) and as the competent national authority for external-sector payment statistics and AWV reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a secondary, agile collection/payout rail for game-art milestone fees, but Low as the sole treasury banking layer. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer/Braintree, PayPal Business) can lawfully provide borderless payment accounts, but under the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) and PSD2 an EMI license is not a banking licence: it permits payment services and e-money issuance only, does not permit deposit-taking or credit products, and safeguarded funds are not covered by the statutory German deposit guarantee. Such accounts also may not offer a German DE IBAN if the EMI is outside the EEA; post-Brexit UK EMIs no longer passport automatically into Germany. For a UG, the use of non-German EMIs as the principal revenue account can impair AWV reporting, create substance/place-of-effective-management concerns with tax authorities, and complicate the KYC profile of subsequent domestic bank onboarding. Therefore, route final liquidity through a German bank account and use PayPal/EMIs only for receipt of low-value client payments with automatic sweeps to the DE IBAN.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A capital contribution from a non-resident shareholder into the UG should be sent by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT if within EEA) to the UG's German bank account in the exact legal name "Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)". The ordering party must be the shareholder's bank account; the beneficiary account must be the company's DE IBAN; the SWIFT field 70/Remittance Information must state "Einlage in das Stammkapital der UG / capital contribution pursuant to shareholders' resolution". When the receiving bank requires an ISO 20022 purpose code, use the equity/capital-transfer code accepted by that bank (often EQUI or a bank-specific capital-transfer code) and never characterise the receipt as goods, royalties, or a personal gift. The UG should retain the notarial/minutes evidence of the capital contribution, the pre-registration contribution confirmation (Übernahme), the current Handelsregister extract, and the bank's credit advice. Since the receipt is from a non-resident and for EUR 12,500 or more, the UG must submit an AWV Zahlungsempfänger report to the Bundesbank by the 7th calendar day of the following month. If the inbound amount is a shareholder loan instead of equity, a written subordinated loan agreement with arm's-length interest and appropriate tax substance must be kept to prevent recharacterisation as hidden equity.For service payments to subcontractors, freelance 3D artists, rigging specialists, and animation studios, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU/EEA beneficiaries). The payment reference should contain the purchase order/invoice number, the asset/service description, and the project name, enabling the bank and German tax authorities to map cash flow to trade deliverables. For high-value or purchase-order-driven contracts with overseas studios, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 through a German bank provides secure disbursement against presentation of required documents, including milestone acceptance certificates, asset delivery manifests, and commercial invoice. Use MT202 only for bank-to-bank commercially settled transfers, not for direct trade disbursements. Every outbound remittance from the UG to a non-resident that reaches or exceeds EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank as a Zahler report; settlement should never be split into artificial sub-threshold tranches solely to avoid reporting, as such structuring itself triggers AML and tax-evasion concerns.Plain-vanilla FX forward (Devisentermingeschäft) with a German or EEA credit institution, documented by an ISDA 2002/1998 master agreement or bank-specific forward contract, for hedging contracted USD/GBP receivables and payables.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) for currencies where physical delivery is impossible or highly impractical; in Germany NDFs are permissible when used as a genuine hedge of an underlying commercial exposure, though they are seldom necessary for EUR/USD in view of full convertibility.Cross-currency swap (FX swap / swap-points transaction) to convert scheduled USD cash flows into EUR or vice versa over matched maturities without creating an open speculative position.European-style FX options (Devisenoptionsgeschäft) where the UG has an approved bank credit/contingent line and the option is transparently linked to a specific milestone-based game-art contract; speculative option strategies require particular documentation and may be outside the UG's permitted business purpose.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a secondary, agile collection/payout rail for game-art milestone fees, but Low as the sole treasury banking layer. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer/Braintree, PayPal Business) can lawfully provide borderless payment accounts, but under the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) and PSD2 an EMI license is not a banking licence: it permits payment services and e-money issuance only, does not permit deposit-taking or credit products, and safeguarded funds are not covered by the statutory German deposit guarantee. Such accounts also may not offer a German DE IBAN if the EMI is outside the EEA; post-Brexit UK EMIs no longer passport automatically into Germany. For a UG, the use of non-German EMIs as the principal revenue account can impair AWV reporting, create substance/place-of-effective-management concerns with tax authorities, and complicate the KYC profile of subsequent domestic bank onboarding. Therefore, route final liquidity through a German bank account and use PayPal/EMIs only for receipt of low-value client payments with automatic sweeps to the DE IBAN.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.00% (Körperschaftsteuer or corporate income tax) plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag on CIT) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) with typical effective rate of 14%–17%; aggregate statutory corporate income burden commonly 30%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% dividend withholding tax under the German Income Tax Act plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax). If the recipient is a non-resident corporate shareholder, the German withholding tax is generally collected at source; the 5.5% solidarity surcharge is calculated on top of the 25% withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: required where the German UG enters cross-border controlled transactions with related enterprises exceeding EUR 5,000,000 for sale of goods or rendering of services, or EUR 500,000 for management/technical/administrative services. Master File: required when the global group's consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 100,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: mandatory in Germany when the multinational group's total consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but the interlocking effect of Section 42 AO (anti-avoidance), Section 1 AStG (German Transfer Pricing Act) and the Limitation on Benefits clauses in Germany's double tax treaties requires the German UG to demonstrate real and substantive business activity. For a game 3D art outsourcing studio, substance typically means: a German registered office, qualified art-director staff, actual decision-making on contracts, equipment/facilities, and auditable profit expectations. A letterbox UG without personnel, premises, or business risks is vulnerable to recharacterisation, income allocation to the foreign shareholder, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under Section 370 AO: imprisonment up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases; monetary fine following an administrative criminal procedure; automatic official tax assessment (Schätzung) and accrual of late-payment interest at 0.15% per month. Civil tax penalty may include forfeiture of the amount evaded.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) who signed or knowingly tolerated the false declaration is personally liable for the unpaid tax debt when the company is unable to pay; civil restitution and disgorgement of profits may be imposed; travel bans and freezing of private assets may be ordered by the Finanzamt under fiscal enforcement law; conviction leads to disqualification from serving as a director of any German company for a period of five to ten years.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Section 162(4) AO imposes a minimum adjustment surcharge of 5% on the increase in taxable income, with a presumption that pricing records were absent; the minimum penalty starts at EUR 5,000 and can reach EUR 10,000 per transaction block. The tax authority may apply a complete revaluation of the controlled transaction, and the adjustment income is subject to 30%–33% combined tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who instructed or approved the defective documentation are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency and penalty if the UG's assets are insufficient; in addition, deliberate intent to manipulate pricing may be prosecuted as criminal tax evasion and trigger director disqualification and asset seizure.
Shell Company / Abuse of Legal Form (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: Section 42 AO treats the sham arrangement as invalid for tax purposes; the Finanzamt reallocates all income to the actual economic owner. The shell UG's losses and carryforwards are disallowed, foreign tax credits are denied, treaty benefits are withdrawn under the DTA's Principal Purpose Test, and the company may be struck from the commercial register for lack of legally compliant corporate substance.

Director personal liability: The sole shareholder-director of the shell UG can be assessed directly as the taxable entrepreneur for the reallocated income; personal liability for the resulting taxes plus penalty interest is automatic; travel bans and administrative arrest are available as enforcement tools; criminal charges for tax evasion and for acting as a straw man may lead to imprisonment. A resident director who does not exercise actual management is liable for all corporate non-compliance.
AML and Corporate Register / Transparency Defects

Statutory sanction: Failure to file annual accounts triggers compulsory fine proceedings (Ordnungsgeld) of EUR 2,500 to EUR 25,000 per item per year; failure to file a Transparenzregister declaration is subject to fines up to EUR 200,000; serious AML violations carry imprisonment up to three years. The UG additionally loses access to simplified insolvency rules and suffers blocking of distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the company's non-filed reports; the administrative fines are issued directly against the acting director in his/her personal capacity. In AML-relevant breaches, directors are subject to individual criminal prosecution, disqualification from holding office, and travel bans under criminal court order; the corporation's veil is pierced and the director must satisfy the unpaid registration fines out of personal assets.
