Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure mailbox-only virtual office is not permissible as the registered office (Sitz) of a UG; §§ 4a and 4b GmbHG, applied by analogy, require a genuine physical address in Germany where business records are maintained and service of process can be effectively effected. A serviced-office or business-centre arrangement with a physical German address, service desk, landline, and an accepted authority to receive service is accepted in practice only if the UG maintains demonstrable commercial substance at that location. For a software-development entity such as a gaming anti-cheat middleware provider, tax authorities and registry courts may scrutinize a letterbox-only address where management functions are entirely exercised abroad. Business-centre arrangements are risk-acceptable only with proper service contracts and documented local management presence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification and central withholding tax matters; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax and trade tax assessment; national electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online Services (www.bzst.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors establishing a German UG (haftungsbeschränkt) must first notarise the articles of association and file for registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). Following registration, the competent local Finanzamt assigns a local tax number (Steuernummer) based on the corporate tax registration questionnaire. For VAT purposes, the UG must separately apply to BZSt for an EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using the official application form (USt 1 TU) filed electronically via ELSTER or BZSt Online Services. Foreign entities acting as managing directors or shareholders may need additional documentation, including certified export certificates of incorporation and evidence of legal existence, but no local fiscal representation is required for a resident UG.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime. A UG is compulsorily taxed as a corporations at entity level (Körperschaftsteuer plus Gewerbesteuer). Available statutory elections include forming an Organschaft (tax group) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT (fiscal unity) pursuant to §§14 ff. KStG and §2(2) Nr. 2 UStG, subject to profit-pooling and liability requirements. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are addressed through mandatory ATAD-implementing provisions (§4k EStG and §14b KStG); there is no elective hybrid entity classification for a UG. Certain limited elections exist for small businesses, e.g., the small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG, but this does not alter the entity tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for domestic entrepreneurs; any business activity triggers VAT registration regardless of turnover. Non-established EU sellers are subject to the €10,000 pan-EU distance-sale threshold, while non-EU sellers have no threshold for online marketplace and distance-sale obligations. A German UG may optionally apply the small-business exemption (§19 UStG) only if prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and expected current-year turnover is below €50,000; this is an exemption from VAT, not a registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating for goods applies under §4 Nr.1 and §6 UStG when the seller proves physical export to a third-country territory; equivalent zero-rating applies to intra-Community supplies (§4 Nr.1b and §6a UStG). For services (including gaming anti-cheat middleware and software licenses), German VAT is generally not chargeable when the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside Germany and the place of supply is the recipient's jurisdiction under §3a(2) UStG. If the recipient is a non-entrepreneur (B2C) in another Member State, the cross-border distance service rules or the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) mechanism apply. To claim zero-rating or the non-taxable status, the UG must obtain and retain documented evidence of the recipient's business identity, EU VAT ID (where applicable), and the contractual terms establishing the place of supply. Services directly related to exported goods may be zero-rated under §3(6a) UStG if bundled with confirming the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung) with accepted export accompanying document
  • Commercial invoice incorporating the exporter's VAT ID and delivery terms (Incoterms)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR International Consignment Note as transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment record evidencing receipt of consideration in foreign currency
  • Proof of transfer or dispatch (Gelangensbestätigung) for intra-Community supplies or customs-department certified export confirmation

Germany has mandated electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) for all domestic B2B supplies starting 1 January 2025. Invoices must be issued and transmitted in a structured electronic format (e.g., XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or a Peppol-compliant XML schema) unless a documented exception applies. There is no real-time tax clearance, API-based fiscal stamping, or government pre-clearance of invoices comparable to the Italian Sistema di Interscambio. E-invoices must be machine-readable and retained for 10 years under §§14b UStG and 147 AO. B2C electronic invoicing remains governed by standard rules with ease-of-invoicing certificates; no statutory fiscal seal is required.

The German tax authorities (Finanzamt and BZSt) audit export-zero-rating claims through extensive documentary scrutiny, cross-referencing ATLAS customs data, bank records, and company books. If the zero-rated supply is overturned, the UG becomes liable for the full statutory VAT plus late-payment interest at 0.5% per month under §233a AO. Deliberate or grossly negligent misrepresentation constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by up to five years' imprisonment or fines; the VAT liability is retrospectively clawed back for up to ten years. Frivolous claims can also trigger penalty surcharges under §§162, 164 AO, reversal of input VAT deductions, and in severe cases the appointment of a tax auditor or initiation of criminal tax investigation. Export documentation beyond 180 days may be challenged; failure to repatriate foreign sales proceeds within the statutory period may additionally trigger CFC issues or deemed taxable distributions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full euro convertibility on both current and capital accounts pursuant to TFEU Articles 63-66; there are no exchange controls, repatriation quotas, or advance approval requirements for dividends or profit remittances. The residual obligations are informational: mandatory balance-of-payments reporting to the Bundesbank and compliance with EU financial sanctions and beneficial-ownership disclosure rules; no material hurdle exists for a UG to receive or remit foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, forced conversion, or minimum retention ratio on export proceeds; the UG may freely hold or convert export receipts in EUR or foreign currency. Cross-border payments and receipts exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 7 days of the value date under Section 67 AWV, using the AWV statistical code corresponding to software/IP licensing and middleware royalty income.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank), operating jointly with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with material legal constraints: offshore non-EU EMIs cannot passport into Germany without a BaFin licence or a licensed German/EU partner under PSD2 and the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG); German-licensed fintech and EMI platforms such as N26 Business, Solaris, or Kontist can open remote accounts for a UG, but these accounts typically impose lower turnover thresholds, narrower transaction-purpose codes, and do not offer commercial documentary credit or escrow-grade clearing services required for high-value middleware licensing.
