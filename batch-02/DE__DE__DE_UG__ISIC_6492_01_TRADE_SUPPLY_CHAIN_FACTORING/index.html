Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht – Registergericht) in the district of the registered office, using the XJustiz/electronic legal-transactions portal; mandatory public publication via the German Federal Gazette (Bundesanzeiger).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG – Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung), in particular §§ 1, 3, 5(1)-(3), 5a, 6, 7(2), 8, 10, 35 and 39 GmbHG; Commercial Code (HGB) §§ 11, 12 and 106-108; Commercial Register Regulation (Handelsregisterverordnung).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially executed Articles of Association (Satzung) or statutory form template for a Unternehmergesellschaft (Musterprotokoll), together with the notarial application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister), filed electronically by the notary under § 12 HGB.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 2 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under § 5a GmbHG the UG has no statutory minimum share capital and may incorporate with a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00; each Geschäftsanteil must be stated in full euros. Where the stated capital is below EUR 25,000, the subscribed capital must in practice be paid up in full in cash before the registration application is filed, supported by a German bank deposit confirmation or a notary escrow account (Anderkonto); contributions in kind and deferred financing contributions are not admissible for this structure. The mandatory 25% retention of annual net profit must be accumulated as a statutory reserve under § 5a(3) GmbHG until the capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF); Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification and cross-border matters; local Finanzamt for assessment and TIN allocation; electronic filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the UG must file a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of business commencement; the Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer (corporate income tax and trade tax ID); the BZSt issues a separate VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) for intra-Community and export transactions; non-resident foreign entities without a permanent establishment may apply directly to the BZSt and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative or register for VAT through the EU one-stop-shop (OSS) where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Upon incorporation, the UG must file a tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of business commencement; the Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer (corporate income tax and trade tax ID); the BZSt issues a separate VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) for intra-Community and export transactions; non-resident foreign entities without a permanent establishment may apply directly to the BZSt and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative or register for VAT through the EU one-stop-shop (OSS) where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists for German corporations; the UG is per se liable to corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer 15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of CT) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer 7–17% depending on municipal multiplier). Elective mechanisms: tax and VAT group integration (Organschaft) under §§14–17 KStG and §2(2) Nr. 2 UStG; small-enterprise VAT election under §19 UStG if turnover thresholds are met; hybrid mismatch and CFC elections or attributions under §4k EStG and the German External Tax Relations Act (AStG) to neutralize double-dip or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration is mandatory for entrepreneurs exceeding EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover or EUR 50,000 current-year forecast (§19 UStG); intra-Community acquisition registration applies above EUR 500,000 per annum (§1a UStG); an active trade-finance and factoring business of a UG will normally exceed these thresholds and cannot rely on the small-business exemption for cross-border factoring supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods to non-EU destinations qualify for zero rating under §4 Nr. 1(a) and §6 UStG if the goods are physically removed from the German customs territory and transferred to an overseas third party, supported by ATLAS customs export confirmation; intra-EU dispatches are zero-rated under §6a UStG with a valid VAT ID and recapitulative statement; financial factoring services relating to export receivables are ordinarily outside the scope of German VAT or exempt under §4 Nr. 8 UStG; B2B services rendered to non-EU recipients are shifted to the recipient's jurisdiction under §3a(2) UStG and are thus not subject to German VAT.

  • Customs export declaration and ATLAS export confirmation (Ausfuhrnachweis)
  • Bill of Lading (maritime) / CMR waybill / Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order referencing the export transaction
  • VAT identification number and Intrastat or recapitulative statement evidence for intra-EU dispatches
  • Bank SWIFT MT103 or Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds within 180 days

Mandatory structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for all domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025, using XML formats compliant with EN 16931, XRechnung or ZUGFeRD; B2G invoices already require XRechnung; no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction controls are currently deployed in Germany; a B2B transaction reporting regime is expected in 2027–2028 under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz), aligning with ViDA e-reporting requirements.

Supplies missing statutory documentary proof are retrospectively reclassified as taxable domestic supplies, triggering VAT assessment at 19% plus interest of 0.5% per month (6% p.a.) from the original due date; deliberate or reckless false zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO with fines up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years, plus liability of the factoring entity for VAT fraud in trade-supply chains under §25d UStG and potential criminal liability of officers; refund claims may be refused, clawed back, or offset against outstanding assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates a fully convertible currency regime; both current and capital account transactions are liberalized under Article 63 TFEU and the euro is freely convertible without exchange-control approval. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to (i) the UG statutory reserve requirement under GmbHG section 5a(3), requiring retention of 25% of annual net profit until share capital plus reserves reach EUR 25,000; (ii) German dividend withholding tax of 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) on distributions to foreign shareholders, subject to applicable double-tax-treaty relief; and (iii) the AWV statistical reporting duty for any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender obligation and no minimum conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency. The only mandatory timetable is AWV sections 67-68: a German resident must report every payment received from, or made to, a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven calendar days via the electronic reporting portal, using the correct balance-of-payments reporting code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank, Frankfurt am Main) jointly with the European Central Bank under the Single Supervisory Mechanism; the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) issues the required factoring license under KWG section 32 and supervises AML compliance. The Bundesbank administers the mandatory balance-of-payments reporting system (AWV) covering all cross-border payments.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a secondary collection rail, Low as the primary treasury rail. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions may passport into Germany under the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) implementing PSD2 and may open EUR multi-currency IBANs; however, they cannot provide the BaFin-compliant client-money segregation required for factoring proceeds held in trust or escrow. Post-Brexit UK EMIs and non-EEA digital banks have no passporting rights and would require a BaFin branch license to offer payment services in Germany; reliance on such rails as principal clearing would jeopardize the factoring license. The UG's capital escrow account and all collection accounts must sit at a fully licensed German KWG credit institution; EMIs may be used only for small-ticket inbound collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inbound funding is executed as an SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder or institutional funder to the UG's German IBAN, with field 70 citing 'Kapitalanlage – Stammeinlage' for equity or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen' for shareholder loan. The UG must report any receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven calendar days using the direct-investment capital code. For factoring operations, a committed reverse-factoring credit facility from a German licensed bank is preferred over a foreign equity injection because it avoids thin-capitalization scrutiny, provides an audited funding line, and settles in EUR without FX conversion. Before the credit is made available, the bank collects the UBO declaration, source-of-funds evidence, and the BaFin license or pending-license certificate.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for non-EU supplier payments through the German bank's correspondent network; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer via TARGET2 for EUR-denominated EU collections and disbursements; (3) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 where the UG factors documentary export receivables, with proceeds payable against presentation of compliant transport and invoice documents; (4) SEPA Direct Debit for recurring reverse-factoring collections from German debtors; and (5) Bank Payment Obligations under URBPO for invoice-matched settlement with large German corporate factor clients.
