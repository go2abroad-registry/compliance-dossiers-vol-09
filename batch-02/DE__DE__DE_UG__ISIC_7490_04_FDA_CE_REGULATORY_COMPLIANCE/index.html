Default tax classification

19%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export rating applies to: (1) physical export of goods to third countries (§6 UStG) with proof of departure; (2) B2B consulting services supplied to non-German EU or third-country businesses where place of supply is at the recipient's establishment (§3a(2) UStG), evidenced by client VAT ID; (3) services directly connected to export goods (§7 UStG). Pure consulting services to foreign B2B clients are outside German VAT scope rather than zero-rated; B2C cross-border services remain taxable in Germany at 19%.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a full-convertibility jurisdiction. EU law (TFEU Article 63) mandates free movement of capital and payments; both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted for the UG, including profit remittance, dividend distribution, shareholder loan repayments and repatriation of liquidation proceeds. There is no licensing, prior approval or beneficial-ownership declaration required merely to send or receive foreign currency. The practical constraints are limited to: (i) the Deutsche Bundesbank AWV statistical reporting duty for any month in which cross-border payments received or made by the UG exceed EUR 50,000 (threshold raised from EUR 12,500 effective 1 July 2021), and (ii) tax compliance at payment level - dividend remittances by the UG attract German 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (effective 26.375%), subject to treaty relief or, for qualifying EU direct investors, potential refund procedures; consultancy service fees are generally free of remittance withholding tax. The UG may lawfully maintain foreign-currency accounts in Germany and abroad without any mandatory repatriation schedule or exchange-control clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion obligations exist under German law. All export and service proceeds in any foreign currency may be retained offshore or onshore in full. The only binding statutory duty is statistical: the UG must file a monthly AWV report with Deutsche Bundesbank when its total cross-border payments received or made within a calendar month exceed EUR 50,000, classifying each payment stream by real economic purpose (e.g., regulatory consulting fee, FDA filing fee reimbursement, capital contribution) using the Bundesbank balance-of-payments code scheme. No central-bank clearance form, no currency control form and no capital-restriction compliance filing is required for ordinary MedTech regulatory consulting activities.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Federal Republic of Germany) is the statutory central bank and the competent authority under Sections 67 to 70 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV) for balance-of-payments and cross-border payment statistics. BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht - German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) supervises corporate banks, payment institutions, electronic money institutions and merchant acquirers. Policy-making authority for foreign trade and payments regulation vests in the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and the Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF). Germany imposes no administrative foreign-exchange control clearance for standard commercial transactions because full convertibility is established.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) on the CIT, resulting in a minimum effective CIT rate of 15.825%; municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) applies separately at a rate between approximately 7% and 17%, yielding a combined normal corporate rate of roughly 30-33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory German withholding tax on dividends (Kapitalertragsteuer) is 25.0% plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld amount, yielding a combined rate of 26.375%. If an additional church tax (Kirchensteuer) applies (typically 8% or 9% of the dividend tax), the aggregate withholding may reach 28.6% - 29.0% depending on the federal state.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate tax return must be submitted by 31 July following the relevant fiscal year-end (unless due to preliminary VAT assessment, usually by 30 September of the same year); extension of the deadline can be granted upon application under § 109 AO up to an additional six months; statutory filing dates may be further postponed to 31 August or 30 April where the return is prepared by a tax advisor (§ 149, § 152 AO).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1 - Steuernummer Unique tax number assigned by the local tax office (Finanzamt) for income tax, trade tax, and VAT purposes. The UG must obtain a Steuernummer from the responsible Finanzamt for corporate income and trade tax; non-resident shareholders do not file this return but may need a separate tax number for German withholding tax certificates. Late filing triggers a coercive fine (Zwangsmittel) under § 329 AO of up to EUR 25,000 per pending return; in addition, a late-filing surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag) under § 152 AO applies at 0.25% per month on the assessed tax, capped at 10% of the tax amount, with a minimum of €25 and a maximum of €25,000. Provided no return is filed, the tax office can make an estimated assessment (Schaetzung) which is enforceable and subject to overvaluation penalties. Late payment attracts interest under § 233a AO at 0.5% per month (6% per annum) accruing from the due date up to 15 months after the end of the fiscal year, unless a self-assessed penalty is waived.
Box 14 - Wirtschaftsjahr Fiscal year start and end dates. For a UG, the fiscal year must correspond to the calendar year unless otherwise provided in the articles of association. Non-resident shareholders have no influence over the fiscal year determination for filing purposes; the UG must file based on its own approved commercial year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File (separate documentation per related-party transaction categories) is mandatory where the UG's annual sales revenue exceeds EUR 10 million in the preceding fiscal year and cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 1 million (for goods) or EUR 500,000 (for services) per year. A Master File must be maintained if the consolidated group turnover of the ultimate parent exceeds EUR 750 million. If the UG is a micro-enterprise with turnover below EUR 1 million, simplified documentation of reasonable arm's-length determinants suffices. Non-compliance with documentation obligations triggers a surcharge of up to 10% (minimum EUR 5,000) of the imputed income adjustment under § 162 AO, or €25,000 if intentionally omitted.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in the UAE; nevertheless, unwavering compliance with real substance is required under § 42 AO (abuse of legal forms) and under the treaty entitlement clause of § 50d EStG (Beneficial Ownership). A UG providing FDA 510(k) and CE mark consulting must demonstrate active business activity: own or rented office premises, qualified employees, a physical communication infrastructure, substantive discretionary decision-making within Germany, and fee income corresponding to actual services performed. Lack of such substance may result in denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of the UG as a sham or conduit, dual-imposition of income in the hands of the ultimate foreign shareholder, retrospective withholding tax assessment, and denial of input VAT deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO, imprisonment of up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases where the evaded amount exceeds EUR 100,000 or the offender acts in a gang-like manner); monetary penalty are set by court at up to EUR 15 million; additionally, the tax office assesses tax under the highest plausible estimate plus interest at 6% per annum and charges late-payment surcharges. For voluntary disclosure to avoid prosecution, the § 371 AO condition of full unreported income must be fulfilled before detection.

Director personal liability: Managing directors of the UG are personally liable under § 69 AO for intentional or negligent tax evasion, with civil restitution of the unpaid CIT, trade tax, and withholding tax plus interest. They face disqualification orders, administrative travel bans, and potential extradition under intergovernmental tax assistance agreements. In cases of deliberate tax fraud, directors are subject to criminal proceedings and, if convicted, can be sentenced to up to five years' imprisonment and a personal fine of up to 1.8 million EUR.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under § 42 AO, the tax authority may disregard the legal form of the UG, re-attribute the contracting income directly to the beneficial owner abroad, disallow all deductions (including costs of service), and impose a penalty of up to 10 years' back-taxation; if treaty relief is denied, 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% soli is applied on the deemed dividend distributions. Administrative fines under GwG (Anti-Money Laundering Act) may reach EUR 1 million for lack of disclosure of beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the entire tax debt if the tax office proves that the director failed to maintain substance documentation or knowingly permitted the company to operate as a letterbox. They are subject to civil restitution claims by tax authorities, withdrawal of residence permits and Schengen-area travel bans, and criminal conviction for tax evasion if complicit in the sham structure, with imprisonment of up to three years for aiding and abetting.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax office makes an upward profit correction under § 1 AStG, treating the difference as a constructive dividend, and may impose a documentation surcharge of 10% (min. €5,000) for non-completion, 20% (min. €10,000) for late delivery, and 25% (min. €25,000) for intentional failure; in addition, ordinary tax evasion penalties under § 370 AO may apply if the mispricing is deliberate. The statute of limitations for assessing such adjustments is raised to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve defective transfer pricing studies become personally liable for the resulting assessment, interest, and penalties. In deliberate schemes, directors can be imprisoned for up to five years for evasion of the German tax base; professional indemnity and director & officer liability insurance is void where gross negligence is proven.
Failure to Withhold Dividend Tax (Kapitalertragsteuer)

Statutory sanction: The UG is personally liable under § 50a, § 44, § 50c EStG for the unwithheld tax amount, and the tax authority may collect it from the UG plus late-payment interest (6% p.a.). Penalties for wrongful non-withholding are severe: a fine of up to EUR 25,000 per taxable event plus a default surcharge of 1% of the unpaid tax per month. The statute of limitation for collection is 10 years from year-end.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for the withheld but unpaid dividend tax if they fail to execute the withholding or fail to obtain the required treaty documentation. The same liability applies to dividend distributions by a UG under a parent-company directive (even if the shareholder is an EU parent) where formal approval has not been obtained by BZSt. This can result in personal civil enforcement against the director's private assets, a travel ban if the debt exceeds EUR 100,000, and criminal prosecution for embezzlement under § 266 StGB.
Anti-Money Laundering / Gatekeeper Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) §§ 2, 11, 56, failing to identify the client, not keeping the mandatory transparency register records, or not reporting suspicious transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) can be fined up to EUR 5 million (for enterprises) and EUR 2.5 million (for individuals). Repeat violations may result in prohibition of business operations and administrative asset seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the company's non-compliance under § 56 GwG if they knew or should have known of the deficiency. They can be sued for damages by third parties, face a ban from acting as a company director in Germany for up to five years, and may be criminally prosecuted for money laundering under § 261 StGB if they knowingly facilitate a transaction connected to predicate tax evasion or export-build regulations; imprisonment from one to ten years applies to serious money laundering cases.
