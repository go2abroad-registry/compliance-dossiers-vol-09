Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Osaühing may be incorporated with zero or any amount of share capital; the standard statutory reference of €2,500 is not a mandatory minimum for registration. If share capital is set below €2,500, shareholders remain jointly and severally liable for the company's obligations under § 140(2) of the Commercial Code until capital is increased to €2,500, and contributions must be monetary (no in-kind contributions). No escrow account is required at filing; paid-in capital is deposited into the company's bank account after entry in the register. Share capital must be denominated in EUR and fully paid before any distributions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) including automatic fire alarm and extinguishing system acceptance, evacuation plans, and annual fire audits; building structural load inspection and certificate of use (kasutusluba) from the local municipality; occupational health clearance from the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) covering the workplace risk assessment, cleanroom operator medical surveillance, noise and compressed air safety, and conformity of processing equipment with the Machinery Directive and low-voltage directives; electrical installation inspection and pressure equipment declaration by an accredited inspection body.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet), e-MTA online portal (https://e-mta.emta.ee; official website www.emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

For a resident Osaühing (OÜ), incorporate through the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) to obtain a registry code (registrikood) which is the statutory national taxpayer identification number. The registry code automatically establishes the entity's tax file with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. The OÜ must actively register for VAT via e-MTA from the beginning of the month following the month in which its taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 during a rolling 12-month period, or earlier if the entity makes a taxable import, intra-Community acquisition, or provides taxable services to Estonian persons under the reverse charge mechanism. Non-resident entities without an Estonian permanent establishment may apply directly to the MTA for an Estonian VAT identification number using the non-resident registration form and, where required by the MTA, appoint a local tax representative. Foreign shareholders will need e-Residency digital authentication or a notarized founding application, followed by the standard Commercial Register filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a resident OÜ is a separate taxpayer; corporate income tax is deferred until profit distribution, at 20/80 of the net distribution; retained profits are untaxed annually)

Available election forms

Estonia does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; an OÜ is per se subject to entity-level corporate income tax on distributions. The EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive hybrid mismatch rules are implemented domestically and may recharacterize payments involving hybrid entities. For VAT, closely linked taxable persons established in Estonia may elect to form a VAT group. A statutory reduced corporate income tax rate of 14/86 is available for regular distributions that meet the conditions of the Income Tax Act and is applied without elective entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 (annual taxable turnover exclusive of VAT; the obligation starts from the first day of the month following the month in which the 12-month cumulative turnover exceeds the threshold). Non-established foreign suppliers have no monetary threshold and must register from their first taxable supply in Estonia unless a special regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate may be claimed by the exporting OÜ where tangible surgical suture needle assemblies are sold for consideration and are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or a third party on its behalf from Estonia to a final destination outside the European Union. The goods must leave the EU in accordance with the Union Customs Code and the supplier must obtain and preserve acceptable proof of export in the form of an accepted customs export declaration and independent transport documents. Related services that are an integral part of the export transaction, such as freight, insurance, and inspection, qualify for zero-rating where they are provided directly to the export buyer and the underlying goods are exported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NCTS/ATLAS acceptance and EX movement reference)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / air waybill evidencing physical movement to a third-country destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent bank credit advice) evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Estonia does not operate a real-time transaction clearance or mandatory digital stamping system for invoices. Mandatory e-invoicing applies only to B2G transactions (public-sector buyers must receive PEPPOL/BIS 3.0 invoices with the Estonian national extension). The e-MTA portal is the statutory channel for filing VAT returns, recapitulative statements, and periodic sales lists; the MTA uses automated risk engines that cross-check customs export declarations, VAT return data, and bank records. Taxpayers must obtain an EORI number and use the NCTS/ATLAS customs export processes; electronic archive storage of invoices and source documents for seven years is mandatory.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board applies automated cross-matching of export declarations, VAT returns, and payment flows. If the 0% rate is applied without sufficient statutory export evidence, the MTA issues a retrospective VAT assessment at the standard rate of 22%, together with arrears interest at 0.06% per calendar day and a tax-default penalty of up to EUR 2,100 for a legal person. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating, including false customs declarations or false inward remittance documentation, may be prosecuted under the Penal Code as tax fraud, carrying penalties including imprisonment of up to five years, forfeiture of the economic benefit, and clawback of any VAT refunded. The general limitation period for assessment is three years from the taxable event, extended to seven years where intentional misconduct is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia maintains full capital account and current account convertibility as an EU and Eurozone member state. Profit remittances face no regulatory hurdles; dividends and retained earnings may be transferred abroad without restriction or approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required. Receipts may be credited directly to foreign currency accounts. Conversion to local currency is optional and subject to commercial discretion without statutory timeline or ratio.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained and reinvested profits; 20% CIT levied only upon profit distribution, calculated as 20/80 of the net distributed amount (equivalent to 20% of gross distribution). A reduced rate of 14/86 applies to regular profit distributions that meet the statutory three-year average distribution rule.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% of the gross distribution, structurally imposed as 20/80 of the net distributed amount; final, non-creditable withholding administered by the distributing OÜ. Reduced statutory rate of 14/86 (14% of gross) for regular distributions satisfying the three-year average rule.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 30 June of the year following the tax period where the tax period equals the calendar year. For a fiscal year ending on another date, due no later than the last day of the sixth month following the end of the fiscal year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly TSD due by the 10th day of the month following the reporting month. Quarterly advance CIT instalments due by the 10th day of the month following the reporting quarter (10 April, 10 July, 10 October, 10 January).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where annual cross-border controlled transactions exceed EUR 50,000 for services, intangibles, interest, and other non-goods transactions, or EUR 500,000 for goods transactions (Regulation No. 44 of 30 May 2016 of the Estonian Tax and Customs Board). Master File required where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report required where the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the type found in offshore financial centres. Nevertheless, substantive economic activity is decisive for corporate tax residence, permanent establishment attribution, and entitlement to treaty benefits under the Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test. An OÜ performing only nominal registered-agent services, with all board decisions and manufacturing functions conducted abroad, may be treated as a resident shell; its income can be re-attributed, treaty relief denied, and distributions recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - deliberate misdeclaration of taxable distributions

Statutory sanction: Penal Code Section 353 (Tax Fraud): criminal fine imposed on the OÜ as a legal person of 50 to 5,000 daily rates, each daily rate between EUR 100 and EUR 1,000, yielding a maximum legal-person fine of EUR 5,000,000; imprisonment of the natural-person director up to five years; mandatory surcharge of up to 30% of the concealed tax under Tax Code Section 129; accrued daily interest at 0.06% from the due date; publication of the tax arrears in the official public registry.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing applies where the OÜ is used as a mere instrumentality: the management board member becomes jointly and severally liable for the unpaid CIT and fraud fine. Criminal conviction carries disqualification from holding a management board position for up to three years, prohibition on leaving Estonia during the criminal proceedings, and confiscation of personal assets derived from the evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the OÜ as an artificial arrangement under the Estonian General Anti-Abuse Rule with retroactive effect: all purported distributions and related-party charges are aggregated into a deemed profit distribution taxed at 20/80; liability for unpaid tax, 30% fraud fine, and daily interest; denial of DTA benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; court-ordered compulsory dissolution where the company is found to have no lawful business purpose.

Director personal liability: Nominee and shadow directors are personally liable for the company's tax debts if the company lacks sufficient assets. Directors who sign false annual reports or e-TSB declarations incur criminal liability under Penal Code Section 282 for false documentation. Civil restitution to the tax authority extends to the director's personal property, with travel bans and extradition exposure under EU arrest-warrant procedures.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority discretion to adjust taxable profits to the arm's-length level and recharacterise the excess margin as a deemed distribution subject to 20/80 CIT; administrative fine up to EUR 10,000 for failure to produce the Local File; registrant-level penalty of 0.06% daily interest on the recomputed tax; secondary adjustment imposing withholding-tax liability on the offshore distributor as a constructive recipient of the distribution.

Director personal liability: The CFO and management board are jointly and severally liable for the TP adjustment if the company becomes insolvent. A deliberate TP arrangement to shift profit out of Estonia constitutes tax fraud under Penal Code Section 353, exposing directors to imprisonment up to five years, board disqualification, and claw-back of bonuses tied to under-reported profit.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Compliance Failure in the Distribution Chain

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act: supervisory penalty up to EUR 3,000,000 or 10% of annual worldwide turnover, whichever is higher; criminal money-laundering liability under Penal Code Section 394 with imprisonment of the directing mind up to eight years; legal-person fine up to EUR 5,000,000; asset-confiscation orders extending to the distributed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated AML compliance officer are subject to individual criminal prosecution for intentional ignorance of the beneficial-ownership requirement. Conviction triggers automatic removal from registered posts, a five-year professional disqualification, travel-ban and surrender-of-passport measures, and personal restitution to the company for the entire fines and penalties imposed.
