Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) assigns the domestic tax number and administers corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. ELSTER is the official electronic tax portal.

E-government filing portal

A newly established UG must submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, generally within one month after commencing business activity. The competent Finanzamt assigns a tax number. The UG may request a USt-IdNr. through the registration questionnaire or separately from the BZSt; the VAT ID is required for relevant intra-EU transactions and does not replace the tax number. A foreign taxable person without a German establishment generally applies to the competent German tax office for non-resident businesses. Registration obligations depend on the taxable supplies and applicable place-of-supply rules.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A newly established UG must submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, generally within one month after commencing business activity. The competent Finanzamt assigns a tax number. The UG may request a USt-IdNr. through the registration questionnaire or separately from the BZSt; the VAT ID is required for relevant intra-EU transactions and does not replace the tax number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a corporation subject to German corporate income tax and trade tax; it has no general check-the-box election to be treated as a partnership. The corporate-tax option under section 1a KStG is available to specified eligible partnerships, not to a UG. German anti-hybrid rules, including section 4k EStG and related provisions, may deny deductions or otherwise adjust outcomes; they are not entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for ordinary domestic taxable business activity: there is no general turnover threshold that exempts a business from VAT registration. The small-business scheme under section 19 UStG may exempt qualifying domestic supplies where the applicable prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; it is an exemption regime, not a general registration threshold. Separate EU-wide EUR 10,000 thresholds may apply to specified cross-border consumer sales and telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronic services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU may qualify as VAT-exempt supplies with input-tax deduction under sections 4 and 6 UStG, provided the statutory export and evidence requirements are met. A service supplied to a business customer outside Germany is generally not subject to German VAT where the place of supply is outside Germany under the applicable place-of-supply rules; this is not a blanket export zero rate. SaaS and other electronically supplied services to consumers require separate place-of-supply analysis, including EU VAT and OSS rules where applicable. Retain evidence of customer status, contractual scope, service delivery, and the relevant place of supply.

  • Customs export declaration and export accompanying/document reference data, including MRN where applicable
  • Customs confirmation of exit from the EU or other statutory export evidence
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and transport documents such as a bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier confirmation, as applicable
  • For services, customer contract, evidence of customer business status and location, service records, and payment evidence where relevant
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a general German statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption

Germany does not operate general real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital stamping. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Issuance requirements are being phased in: transitional rules generally permit paper or other electronic invoices through 2026, with an extension through 2027 for certain issuers whose prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 800,000. Thereafter, structured invoices complying with the applicable European standard and German requirements are generally required for domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory exceptions. No general tax-authority API clearance is required. Separate electronic invoicing rules apply to specified public-sector transactions.

Germany imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. A VAT exemption may be denied or reclaimed if the taxpayer cannot substantiate the statutory conditions; resulting VAT, interest, and potentially late-payment surcharges may be assessed. Intentional or reckless false claims may result in tax-evasion or tax-reduction liability under the Fiscal Code, including criminal or administrative penalties. The applicable consequences depend on the facts and culpability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany uses the euro and has no general exchange controls. The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A German UG may receive foreign investment, hold foreign-currency accounts, purchase foreign assets, pay foreign suppliers, and remit dividends or other permitted distributions without prior foreign-exchange approval. Distribution remains subject to German corporate-law solvency and capital-maintenance rules, annual accounts, distributable-profit requirements, withholding-tax treatment, beneficial-owner and anti-money-laundering checks, and any applicable tax-clearance or treaty documentation. Cross-border payments may be delayed by bank sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, payment-purpose review, or required statistical reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros. Exporters may retain foreign-currency proceeds in German or foreign accounts, subject to banking, tax, accounting, sanctions, and reporting obligations. Incoming SaaS, cybersecurity, licensing, and other service receipts should be supported by contracts, invoices, delivery evidence, and customer records. Cross-border payment and external-position reporting may apply under the AWV and Deutsche Bundesbank rules, generally including reportable resident-to-nonresident transactions above the applicable threshold, currently generally EUR 50,000 for many payment reports, subject to statutory exemptions, reporting categories, and changes in Bundesbank instructions. The UG should verify the current threshold and filing channel before each reporting period.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) supervises regulated banks, payment institutions, electronic-money institutions, and investment-services firms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, SEPA transfers, cards, multi-currency settlement, and selected international wires; not a substitute for a German commercial-bank relationship where a notarial share-capital payment confirmation, lender control, cash concentration, or full SWIFT capability is required. An offshore digital EMI may be unable to onboard a German UG or provide German IBANs, local safeguarding, or unrestricted correspondent access. A European Economic Area EMI or payment institution must operate under its home-state authorization and applicable passporting or German authorization regime. Client funds are generally safeguarded rather than covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme. The UG must verify the provider's register status, safeguarding model, fund-segregation arrangements, payment limits, correspondent banks, sanctions policy, data-processing terms, account-closure rights, and ability to produce transaction evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer/KST) federal corporate income tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on KST (aggregate 15.825%), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) assessed at a local multiplier (Hebesatz); typical combined effective corporate burden 30–33% depending on municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory Kapitalertragsteuer on dividend distributions, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the 25% (aggregate 26.375%). Non-resident shareholders may be exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when the corporate shareholder holds at least 10% of the UG's share capital for at least 12 months; treaty-reduced rates (typically 5% or 15%) apply for non-EU residents where a double tax agreement (DTA) exists.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR (euro). Mandatory transfer pricing documentation under GAufzV: Local File preparation is triggered when annual cross-border intercompany remuneration with a related party exceeds EUR 5,000,000 for goods transactions or EUR 2,000,000 for services transactions (including SaaS licensing, management fees, interest, and intercompany IT services). A Master File must be prepared when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000 per fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a standalone annual Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) filing regime comparable to offshore centres. However, § 42 AO (abuse of legal arrangements) and § 50d (3) EStG impose a de facto substance doctrine: to retain treaty and EU directive relief, the beneficial owner of dividends, interest, or royalties must demonstrate appropriate staff, premises, real business activity, and risk-bearing capacity. For a German UG operating as a cloud SaaS entity, actual economic substance (office, engineers, security analysts, sales contracts) is mandatory to avoid recharacterisation, denial of treaty relief, and retroactive WHT assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under § 370 AO: imprisonment of up to five years for standard evasion, or ten years in particularly severe cases; additional fines; mandatory assessment of underpaid tax and monetary interest; administrative penalty for late payment. The UG itself may be subjected to confiscation of economic benefit and disgorgement of unlawful profits.

Director personal liability: Managing directors (Geschäftsführer) are personally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for tax arrears left unpaid through culpable neglect; criminal conviction may result in restitution orders, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment. A director who participates in fraud can be prosecuted alongside the company.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under § 162 (3)-(4) AO, the tax authorities may estimate taxable income and impose a supplementary penalty: 25% of the upward adjustment where documentation is absent (minimum EUR 500), reduced to 10% for late but complete provision. Estimated additional tax plus interest may be assessed; audit may ripen into criminal investigation where facts suggest intent.

Director personal liability: Directors who obstruct audit or intentionally fail to maintain transfer pricing records may be held personally liable for the resulting the tax plus penalty, and may face regulatory investigation for breach of tax compliance duties under § 69 AO.
Withholding Tax Evasion / Improper Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Under § 44 (5) EStG the UG is obliged to account for the un-withheld tax; the BZSt may reverse treaty benefits under § 50d (3) EStG, retroactively applying 26.375% WHT; penalties under §§ 378/379 AO add up to EUR 50,000; interest under § 233a AO accrues from the distribution date.

Director personal liability: The managing director who signed the dividend resolution or instruction to pay with knowledge of tax evasion is jointly liable for unpaid WHT and penalties; travel bans and personal fines may be imposed during criminal proceedings.
Shell-Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty/directive relief under § 50d (3) EStG and § 42 AO; recharacterisation of transactions; retroactive taxation at statutory WHT rates; aggressive tax arrangement disclosure penalties under § 138j AO (DAC6) up to EUR 50,000 per omission; potential criminal tax evasion charges.

Director personal liability: Directors of the abusive UG may be personally liable for additional taxes, fines, and interest; if conviction for tax evasion, imprisonment and disgorgement are possible; the same individuals may be disqualified from serving as company directors in Germany.
AML / Payment Rails Violation

Statutory sanction: Enforcement fines under § 56 GwG up to EUR 100,000 per breach (or up to 5% of annual turnover for serious infringements); criminal prosecution for money laundering under § 261 StGB with imprisonment of three to five years; asset seizure and forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are subject to personal fines and criminal liability if they participate in, tolerate, or fail to reasonably prevent laundering through the SaaS platform; travel bans and confiscation orders are routinely imposed in German AML prosecutions.
