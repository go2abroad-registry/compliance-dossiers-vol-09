Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies for an Osaühing; share capital may be entered at EUR 1 or any higher amount stated in the articles. A temporary bank escrow account is not mandatory for the minimum formation route, but where capital is actually paid in cash, it must be held in a separate euro bank account or a notary's escrow account until the company is registered.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from Estonian Rescue Board (Päästeamet) confirming compliance with fire safety design requirements, fire extinguisher placement and access routes; structural load inspection certificate from the municipality's building inspectorate for warehouses; workplace clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act including risk assessment, first-aid training, employee health surveillance and a written work environment procedure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); principal portal: e-MTA, with entity registration through the Estonian e-Business Register.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ receives a Commercial Register code upon incorporation; this code is generally used as the entity tax identification number. The company must create e-MTA access, appoint authorized representatives, and register for VAT if statutory conditions are met. A foreign entity without an Estonian legal entity must register with the MTA as a non-resident taxable person when it carries out taxable transactions requiring Estonian registration. The VAT application is filed electronically through e-MTA or by an authorized representative and should include the foreign entity certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, proof of business activity, contracts, transaction forecasts, management and contact details, bank information, and evidence of an Estonian fixed establishment where relevant. The resulting Estonian VAT number has the format EE plus the assigned registration number. An EORI number must be obtained separately for customs activity involving imports or exports in the European Union.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no United States-style check-the-box election. An OÜ is generally treated as a resident corporate entity if incorporated in Estonia or managed from Estonia. Estonian corporate income tax is generally imposed when profits are distributed or otherwise treated as distributed rather than when profits are earned and retained. Eligible distributions may benefit from participation-exemption, parent-subsidiary, treaty, or European Union directive rules, subject to statutory conditions. Estonia applies anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, including restrictions for deduction, non-inclusion, imported mismatch, and reverse-hybrid outcomes. These rules are statutory and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply with the place of supply in Estonia during a calendar year. A foreign taxable person may be required to register from the first relevant Estonian taxable transaction where the domestic threshold does not apply, including transactions involving an Estonian fixed establishment, unless a statutory reverse-charge or other exception applies. Voluntary registration may be available before the threshold is reached.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT if the goods leave the EU customs territory and the supplier can substantiate the export with customs and transport evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% VAT where the customer is VAT-identified in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies recapitulative statement, transport, and invoice requirements. Services are generally outside Estonian VAT or subject to 0% treatment where the place-of-supply rules locate the transaction outside Estonia, including qualifying B2B services supplied to a customer established outside Estonia. Export zero-rating does not apply merely because the customer is foreign; the applicable place-of-supply, transport, customer-status, and evidence requirements must be met. Clinical diagnostic reagents are ordinarily subject to the standard rate unless a specific statutory medical-product exemption or reduced-rate rule applies to the particular product and transaction.

  • Electronic customs export declaration and customs MRN or equivalent export record
  • Customs exit confirmation or electronic proof that the goods left the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, supplier, customer, value, VAT treatment, and delivery terms
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, CMR, air waybill, or courier record, as applicable
  • Sales contract, purchase order, delivery evidence, and proof of receipt where available
  • Evidence of payment or bank records where relevant to transaction substantiation; an inward remittance certificate is not generally a separate statutory Estonian export document
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, and applicable recapitulative statement

Estonia does not generally operate a universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or continuous transaction reporting system for ordinary domestic B2B transactions. VAT-registered businesses must issue compliant VAT invoices, maintain accounting records, submit VAT returns and annexes electronically through e-MTA, and retain supporting documentation. Electronic invoicing is required or operationally expected for certain public-sector transactions and must comply with applicable European and Estonian e-invoice standards. There is no general requirement for pre-issuance digital stamping or submission of every invoice to the MTA. Customs declarations for exports and imports are filed electronically through the EU customs systems, with customs acceptance and exit records serving as evidence of customs status.

Estonia has no general foreign-exchange control or statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days. The supplier must nevertheless retain reliable evidence of export, transport, customer status, contractual substance, and payment. If 0% VAT is unsupported or incorrectly applied, the MTA may reassess output VAT at the applicable rate, deny input-VAT recovery connected with fictitious or improperly documented transactions, impose statutory interest on underpaid tax, and apply administrative penalties for reporting or documentation failures. Interest accrues on tax arrears under the applicable statutory rate. Deliberate false invoicing, concealment, sham exports, customs fraud, or materially fraudulent VAT claims may result in fines or criminal liability under Estonian law, together with recovery of unpaid VAT, interest, and unjustified refunds. Customs authorities may also recover import or export-related duties and impose sanctions under the Union Customs Code and Estonian implementing law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro. The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Estonia has no general exchange controls, no approval requirement for ordinary inbound or outbound foreign-currency payments, and no general restriction on dividend, interest, royalty, loan, or capital remittances to non-residents. Banks may require evidence of the legal purpose, beneficial ownership, source of funds, sanctions status, tax compliance, and underlying commercial transaction. Dividend distributions remain subject to Estonian corporate-law solvency and distribution rules and to Estonian taxation applicable at the time of distribution. The company must separately assess withholding tax, beneficial-ownership, transfer-pricing, permanent-establishment, and anti-avoidance rules in the recipient or counterparty jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or deposited with a domestic bank. Export proceeds may remain in foreign currency and may be held in domestic or foreign accounts, subject to bank terms, sanctions controls, accounting records, customs and VAT documentation, and applicable anti-money-laundering requirements. Foreign-currency balances must be recorded and remeasured under the applicable Estonian accounting framework.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank, the central bank of Estonia; banking supervision is conducted within the Single Supervisory Mechanism by the European Central Bank for significant institutions and by the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for other supervised institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment accounts, SEPA transfers, foreign-currency collection, and routine supplier payments, provided the provider is an authorised European Economic Area payment institution or electronic-money institution and permits Estonian OÜs in this activity. An EMI account is not a bank deposit account and may not provide credit, deposit-guarantee protection, documentary trade finance, or the full range of treasury products. Offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs require verification of licensing, safeguarding, correspondent access, sanctions screening, data protection, account portability, and acceptance by counterparties and customs-related service providers. Fintech reliance should not replace a domestic bank account where capital contributions, regulated trade finance, local payroll, tax payments, or bank-issued guarantees are required.
