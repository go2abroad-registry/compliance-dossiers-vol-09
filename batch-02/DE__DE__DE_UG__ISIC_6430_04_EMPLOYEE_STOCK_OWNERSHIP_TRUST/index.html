Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Germany: Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office) is the central authority for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and cross-border exchanges; all routine assessments, Steuernummer assignment, VAT advance returns and corporate tax audits are administered by the locally competent Finanzamt (Tax Office) at the UG's registered seat; statutory e-filing runs exclusively through the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) and BZSt portal (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the UG's articles of association, appoint the managing director (Geschäftsführer), and register the UG in the Handelsregister (Commercial Register) at the local Amtsgericht; legal existence commences upon registration. Step 2: The Finanzamt automatically assigns the Steuernummer after the notarial filing and issues the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (§138 AO tax-registration questionnaire), which the UG must complete and return within one month of commencing business. Step 3: Apply to BZSt for the VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG via ELSTER/Form USt 1 TU; the VAT ID is compulsory for intra-EU invoices and VIES confirmations. Step 4: Register with ELSTER for digital SafeID certificates covering mandatory e-filing of VAT, corporate income tax, wage tax and annual returns; obtain an employer number (Betriebsnummer) from the Bundesagentur für Arbeit for payroll/ESOP administration. Step 5: Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the UG without German tax residency; a German registered office and a local managing director or authorized agent remain mandatory for valid service of process and tax correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a German Kapitalgesellschaft and a fully separate taxpayer subject to Körperschaftsteuer (15%), solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate income tax) and Gewerbesteuer (municipal trade tax, effective rate typically 12.6%–17.2% dependent on the municipal Hebesatz); profits are taxed at entity level with no pass-through entitlement; residence-based worldwide taxation applies to German-resident UGs with foreign tax credit under double-taxation treaties; Germany is a unitary national tax jurisdiction with no sub-state income-tax differentiation.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections: (i) Organschaft tax-group election (§§14–17 KStG, §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 UStG) requiring a 75%+ controlling interest and an executed profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Gewinnabführungsvertrag), consolidating corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT into the parent/occupying enterprise; (ii) small-business VAT exemption (§19 UStG Kleinunternehmerregelung) where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 100,000, with renunciation of input VAT recovery; (iii) cash-basis VAT accounting (§20 UStG Ist-Versteuerung) for prior-year turnover up to EUR 800,000; (iv) the mandatory UG reserve rule under §5a GmbHG requiring appropriation of 25% of annual net profit until subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000, after which conversion to a GmbH may occur. Hybrid-mismatch restrictions under §4k EStG (ATAD 2) disallow deductions for payments treated as deductible in the counterparty jurisdiction; no elective hybrid instrument is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

As of 1 January 2025, the small-business exemption threshold (§19 UStG) is EUR 25,000 of taxable turnover in the preceding calendar year and EUR 100,000 expected in the current calendar year; exceeding either threshold makes VAT registration unconditional. Because the UG is by statutory form a commercial entrepreneur (Formkaufmann), it is obliged to register and file German VAT returns upon exceeding the EUR 25,000 prior-year threshold; below-threshold UGs may voluntarily elect regular taxation under §19 Abs.2 UStG to recover input VAT on ESOP-plan and trust-administration costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to: (i) export supplies of goods to third-country destinations (§4 Nr.1, §6 UStG) evidenced by the accepted customs export declaration; (ii) intra-EU dispatches (§4 Nr.1b, §6a UStG) where the customer's VAT ID is validated in VIES and the goods physically leave German territory; and (iii) B2B services supplied to entrepreneurs established outside Germany (§3a Abs.2 UStG), including cross-border ESOP share-plan administration and trust services, whose place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction so that German VAT is not chargeable; input VAT attributable to such outward supplies is recoverable via the ordinary VAT return or, for non-established traders, the EU 13th Directive refund procedure.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged electronically in the ATLAS system, including the valid Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument)
  • Proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) under §§8–10 UStDV: the customs-stamped export declaration or accepted alternative evidence (e.g., ATLAS register excerpt, carrier's customs attestation)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or air waybill evidencing dispatch from the EU and delivery to the foreign customer
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export/ESOP service contract identifying the UG as supplier and stating the incoterms and value
  • Inward remittance certificate: bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 confirming receipt of the export proceeds on a German bank account
  • For intra-EU dispatch: validated customer VAT ID (VIES output), signed delivery notes and transport records satisfying Article 138 of the VAT Directive

Electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) in EU-standard EN 16931 formats (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD) is mandatory for domestic B2B transactions from 1 January 2025: every business must be able to receive structured e-invoices from that date; mandatory issuance applies from 1 January 2027 for enterprises with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other businesses. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system (no API-based state clearance akin to Italy); compliance is evidenced instead through the ATLAS customs system, ELSTER electronic filings and GoBD archiving rules (10-year retention of invoice originals). Violations of invoice format and content requirements are administrative offences under §26a UStG subject to fines of up to EUR 5,000 and may additionally result in forfeiture of the input VAT deduction.

The Finanzamt will retroactively assess German VAT at 19% (or 7% where applicable) plus statutory interest of 0.15% per month (1.8% p.a.) under §233a AO if the Ausfuhrnachweis or intra-EU dispatch evidence is not produced on audit; intentional misapplication of export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or fines (up to ten years in aggravated cases), while §26a UStG imposes additional fines for wrongful intra-EU zero-rating; German customs and BZSt cross-check ATLAS MRN export data against VAT returns, transactional listings (§18f UStG) and payment flows. Germany imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day window is the customary audit benchmark for accepting export proceeds, and failure to receive payment within that period may trigger reversal of the export treatment with reassessment of VAT, interest and, in fraudulent cases, criminal penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany grants unrestricted de jure convertibility for both current and capital account transactions, grounded in Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital) and Section 1 of the AWV. No exchange-control approval, declaration license, or mandatory channeling is required for inbound equity, ESOP trust contributions, or outbound profit/dividend/repurchase remittances in EUR or any freely convertible currency. The sole statutory burden is the Bundesbank external-payment statistical reporting threshold: payments to or from non-residents exceeding EUR 50,000 per month must be reported on form Z5/Z5M (cross-border payment report), and direct investment positions exceeding EUR 12,500 require AWV balance-of-payments position reporting. Remittance of UG distributable profit is only subject to German withholding tax (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge, unless a double-tax treaty reduction is applied) and is not blocked by FX or liquidity regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The Federal Republic imposes no surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio to EUR, and no minimum holding period on export or service receipts. A German UG may freely invoice in EUR or foreign currency under Section 1(3) AWV and may retain export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts either at a German bank or at a foreign bank without any repatriation obligation. Only the monthly statistical reporting duty (AWV Z5 report for payments above EUR 50,000) and the abridged yearly position reporting for assets exceeding EUR 12,500 apply.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action; FX and external-payments statistical surveillance is exercised by Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passported into Germany under PSD2 (e.g., Solaris SE, N26 Bank, Pliant, Modulr Germany) are viable for sub-ledger, payment-initiation, and card rails, with a feasibility rating of Medium-High and legal status as electronic money payment accounts rather than full bank accounts. Non-EEA offshore EMIs (post-Brexit UK, Singapore, Hong Kong) are not viable as a primary treasury bank for a German UG: they cannot hold the UG's corporate account in Germany without a BaFin branch license or EU passport, and any pass-through of the ESOP trust's monies by an unlicensed intermediary would violate Section 32(1) KWG (prohibition of unlicensed deposit-taking) and breach Geschäftsführer fiduciary duties under Section 43(1) GmbHG. Offshore EMIs may be used only as auxiliary FX/expense cards, never as the legal holder of statutory trust monies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound ESOP funding from the non-resident parent or setting company must be routed via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA originators or via SEPA Credit Transfer (single euro payments area, ≤ EUR 100,000 instant via SEPA Instant) for EEA originators into the UG's dedicated EUR IBAN at a Bundesbank-licensed primary bank. The remittance information must carry the ISO 20022 purpose code 'TREA' (treasury operations) or 'SUPP' (supplementary transfers) with the explicit AWV statistical transaction key for capital inflows (Kapitalverkehr – Direktinvestitionen) and the composite key 'DE__DE__DE_UG__ISIC_6430_04_EMPLOYEE_STOCK_OWNERSHIP_TRUST' as the reference. No cash, bearer instruments, cryptocurrency, or third-party merchant cards may be used for the injection; the bank will verify the notarized share-subscription resolution and the trust declaration before releasing the funds from the capital escrow account to the operating sub-account.Outbound trade and employee settlement disbursements execute through (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (regular or instantaneous) for EEA/EUR-denominated employee and broker payments; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR and non-EEA payments, e.g., repurchase of shares of employees residing outside the EEA or settlement with international custodians; (iii) TARGET2 large-value EUR netting where a single repurchase settlement exceeds EUR 500,000; and (iv) commercial letters of credit (documentary LCs, UCP 600) only where the UG is contractually obliged to acquire cross-border goods or securities-linked deliverables against a presentation of title documents. For pure equity transfers (book-entry shares) the standard clearing route is the securities transfer engine (e.g., Clearstream settlement or a custodian's restricted-share ledger) paired with an MT103 payment instruction containing the settlement reference; the UG must never disburse by bearer cheque, cash withdrawal, or SEPA direct debit for outbound trade.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/GBP FX forwards (deliverable or non-deliverable, maturity ≤ 12 months) executed solely with ECB-regulated counterparties and matched to committed ESOP repurchase or option-exercise cash flows.FX swaps (spot-forward pairing) converting surplus USD/GBP trust liquidity into EUR for immediate payroll and share-settlement obligations, subject to an ISDA/FX master agreement and a board-approved hedging policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted-currency jurisdictions (CNY, INR, BRL) limited strictly to the notional amount of non-resident employees' realized option proceeds, with no speculative leverage.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) between EUR and USD where the UG takes on an external capital-raising loan to fund share purchases and must align its currency and interest-rate profiles; no embedded options, no exotic structures, and no netting of employees' personal positions through the UG hedge book.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passported into Germany under PSD2 (e.g., Solaris SE, N26 Bank, Pliant, Modulr Germany) are viable for sub-ledger, payment-initiation, and card rails, with a feasibility rating of Medium-High and legal status as electronic money payment accounts rather than full bank accounts. Non-EEA offshore EMIs (post-Brexit UK, Singapore, Hong Kong) are not viable as a primary treasury bank for a German UG: they cannot hold the UG's corporate account in Germany without a BaFin branch license or EU passport, and any pass-through of the ESOP trust's monies by an unlicensed intermediary would violate Section 32(1) KWG (prohibition of unlicensed deposit-taking) and breach Geschäftsführer fiduciary duties under Section 43(1) GmbHG. Offshore EMIs may be used only as auxiliary FX/expense cards, never as the legal holder of statutory trust monies.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the assessed CIT, producing a 15.825% federal headline burden before trade tax; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) varies between roughly 7% and 17% depending on the local Hebesatz, bringing the combined effective corporate profit tax burden in major cities to approximately 30%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital income tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, i.e. 26.375% combined statutory rate. Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, the rate is 0% when the non-resident corporate shareholder holds at least 10% of the UG's capital and satisfies the statutory holding period; many German tax treaties reduce the rate to 5% or 15% for portfolio shareholdings and 0% for direct substantial shareholdings.

Due by 31 July of the calendar year following the tax year for self-filers; where the UG is represented by a tax adviser, the deadline is automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second following calendar year. A null return must still be filed for a dormant UG.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border related-party transactions must be documented when the annual transaction volume for a transaction category exceeds EUR 100,000; a master file and local file are generally prepared for significant German taxpayers with cross-border intragroup turnover, and a country-by-country report filing is mandatory for groups with consolidated group revenue above EUR 750,000,000. The German tax authorities may request TP documentation even below these thresholds where a specific audit indication exists.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a standalone named ESR code, but economic substance is a central legal requirement. A UG must have a real registered office, actual local management, personnel, accounting, bank accounts and contractual functions commensurate with its ESOP trustee activity. The general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 AO, treaty-shopping safeguards in Section 50d EStG, and commercial-law trust principles allow the tax authorities to disregard a 'letterbox' UG or reallocate income to the economic owner if the ESOP trust functions are carried out abroad. A UG that only holds legal title without business management in Germany is likely to be denied DTA benefits and may be treated as tax-transparent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a criminal fine under Section 370 AO; in aggravated cases up to ten years. The tax claim remains fully due, plus monthly late payment surcharges of 1% and interest of 0.5% per month.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face criminal prosecution and personal civil liability under Sections 34 and 69 AO for unpaid corporate taxes when the failure lawfully to declare taxes is due to conscious dereliction of duty. Courts may impose a travel ban, seize personal assets and, after repeated offenses, prohibit the individual from holding management functions in German companies.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits and parent-subsidiary relief; re-assessment of the UG's profits as those of the foreign beneficial owner; application of Section 42 AO to disregard the corporate vehicle; administrative fines under regulatory law for false corporate registrations. Failure to maintain commercial substance may also trigger dissolution under German corporate law by the registry court.

Director personal liability: The limited liability shield is not absolute: directors may be personally liable for taxes and social contributions where the UG is classified as a sham or where the director allowed the company to continue while insolvent. In serious cases involving cross-border structuring, the director can be subjected to asset seizure, prohibition from managing any German company and extradition procedures for fiscal crimes.
AML / Money Laundering in Trust Administration

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for natural persons and up to 10% of annual turnover for legal entities under the GwG; imprisonment up to five years under Section 261 StGB for money laundering. Supervisory orders by the German Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) or competent FIU can freeze accounts and revoke authorisation to operate trust services.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally or negligently fail to discharge AML obligations are personally liable for administrative fines and civil damages caused to the trust beneficiaries. The court can issue professional disqualification orders preventing further management of financial or trust companies and may seize the director's personal assets in connection with laundering offenses.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Income re-attribution under Section 1 AStG at the arm's length price; documentation surcharge of 5% of the adjusted income increase under Section 162(4) AO, with a minimum penalty of EUR 5,000; additional hidden dividend distribution treatment may trigger withholding taxes on deemed distributions and solidarity surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the additional taxes, interest and penalties if they knowingly signed incorrect transfer pricing returns or caused the UG to enter into non-arm's length agreements for the benefit of foreign shareholders. The insolvency court may claim compensation from directors who dissipated corporate assets through tax-motivated transfer pricing adjustments.
