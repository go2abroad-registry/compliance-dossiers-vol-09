Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) administered by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) in the district of the registered office, with electronic filing through a German notary (Notar) using the electronic register procedure.

Governing statute

German Act on Limited Liability Companies (GmbH-Gesetz, GmbHG), particularly § 1 (formation), § 2 (form of articles of association), § 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), § 7 (application for registration), § 8 (content of application), § 10 (legal effect of registration), §§ 35-44 (managing directors), and § 56 (list of shareholders), together with the German Commercial Code (HGB) provisions on the Commercial Register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified incorporation deed (Errichtungsurkunde) incorporating the articles of association or the statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll), together with the electronic application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) filed by the notary.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 37 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG has no statutory minimum share capital; the registered share capital may be EUR 1.00, but the contribution must be fully paid in cash and be at the free disposal of the managing director (GmbHG § 5a(2), § 7(2)). The contribution is credited to a corporate bank account in escrow-style trust until registration. At least 25% of annual net profit must be transferred to a statutory reserve until the registered capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5a(3)).

Funding & unlock path: A fire safety certification (Brandschutznachweis) is obtained from the municipal fire protection authority covering emergency exits, fire alarm systems, and extinguisher placement; the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) requires a structural load assessment, safe electrical installations verified by the operator, unobstructed escape routes, first-aid equipment, and a workplace risk assessment; the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt is the inspecting authority, and occupational safety evidence is retained on site.

Paid-in capital rule: UG has no statutory minimum share capital; the registered share capital may be EUR 1.00, but the contribution must be fully paid in cash and be at the free disposal of the managing director (GmbHG § 5a(2), § 7(2)). The contribution is credited to a corporate bank account in escrow-style trust until registration. At least 25% of annual net profit must be transferred to a statutory reserve until the registered capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5a(3)).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is applicable to ISIC 8220 office-based data labeling; the municipal Trade Office (Gewerbeamt) remains the sole registration authority, and the Federal Immission Control Agency (Umweltbundesamt) is relevant only for an incidental notification if heating, ventilation, or backup power installations exceed non-significant thresholds.

Category: No operating permit is required; the company operates under the trade registration certificate (Gewerbeanmeldung). If the activity is limited to office-based data annotation and IT services, no permit under the Federal Immission Control Ordinance (4. BImSchV) or other sectoral licensing statutes (e.g., GewO § 34) is triggered.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) and local Tax Office (Finanzamt); filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity registers with the competent local Finanzamt using the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire), obtains a local Steuernummer, and applies to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Electronic registration is routed through ELSTER or the BZSt online portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entity registers with the competent local Finanzamt using the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire), obtains a local Steuernummer, and applies to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). Electronic registration is routed through ELSTER or the BZSt online portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election. A UG is by default subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available elections include VAT group treatment (Organschaft) and, for income tax, corporate tax group (steuerliche Organschaft) if profit and loss transfer agreements exist. Hybrid mismatch elections are restricted under the German implementation of ATAD / EU anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for taxable entrepreneurs; small-business exemption applies when prior-year turnover ≤ €22,000 and current-year turnover ≤ €50,000. For non-resident entities supplying B2B services, VAT registration is mandatory without turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods under Section 6 UStG if the goods leave Germany to a third-country or EU destination and documentary evidence is maintained. For cross-border B2B services, including data labeling and AI training services supplied to non-resident recipients, the place of supply is in the recipient's jurisdiction; the supply is not subject to German VAT and is instead reverse-charged or taxed abroad. OECD / EU B2B place-of-supply rules govern the zero/VAT-free treatment of offshore services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement or equivalent transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Zahlungsbestätigung / Bankbeleg for export proceeds)

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2B domestic supplies from January 2027 (with phased enforcement), using formats such as XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.0, or EDI; from January 2025, all domestic B2B invoice recipients must be able to receive structured e-invoices. There is no continuous transaction control or central government clearance API requirement.

Export zero-rating and cross-border VAT-free treatment are subject to strict documentary audit scrutiny. Missing or fraudulent export evidence triggers retroactive VAT assessment, interest, and penalties under Sections 370–374 AO, including fines up to the amount of evaded tax, imprisonment up to 10 years, and joint liability of legal representatives. False or manipulated export documentation is treated as tax evasion and may result in criminal conviction and exclusion from zero-rating benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current and capital account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act/German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance. The euro is freely convertible, and no exchange-control approval or prior permit is required for inbound capital subscriptions, service receipts, profit distributions or repayment of shareholder loans. Profit remittance by a UG to a foreign shareholder is not subject to any administrative transfer ceiling; however, statutory dividend withholding tax of 25% plus surcharge applies at source, with treaty relief available upon certified proof of tax residence and beneficial ownership. Regulatory scrutiny focuses solely on AML/KYC verification, EU sanctions compliance, and cross-border payment statistics reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Germany. Cross-border payments and receipts exceeding EUR 12,500 in equivalent value must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank on a post-transaction statistical basis under the reporting duties contained in sections 59-60 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance. Such reporting is administrative, not restrictive, and does not require repatriation, mandatory sale, or approval by the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility of fintech EMI onboarding is High for lightweight receivables and disbursements, but such institutions should be used only as a supplementary clearing rail and never as the exclusive banking backbone. EU-licensed e-money institutions such as Wise are generally permitted to onboard a German UG remotely and provide euro IBANs and multi-currency settlement; however, they do not offer regulated credit or committed FX/treasury services, may classify data-labeling mass-payout flows as high-volume payroll-like activity, and may suspend accounts pending enhanced due diligence on each beneficiary. Legal constraints under PSD2 and German KWG require that any deposit-taking, lending or systematic credit facility remain with a credit institution; therefore, the UG must maintain a primary BaFin-supervised commercial bank account for statutory capital protection and long-term corporate credit history.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections into the UG treasury account are executed by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR from the foreign shareholder to the UG's account at a German credit institution. The payment instruction must include the exact corporate legal name and stated purpose 'Capital Contribution to DE_UG', supported by a certified shareholder resolution and, if required by the receiving bank, a copy of the contribution notice required under the German Act on the Reduction of the Cost of Shareholder Support. The receiving bank must verify origin-of-funds through the shareholder's bank confirmation and passport/UBO documentation. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 7.0–17.5% — aggregate statutory corporate burden approximately 29.8%–32.3%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that withholding, producing an effective statutory rate of 26.375%

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

31 July following the fiscal year-end; tax-adviser-prepared returns are ordinarily extended to 31 December, with possible further administrative deferral; late-payment interest accrues after 15 months at 6% per annum

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory timeline as KSt 1 A: 31 July following the fiscal year-end with tax-adviser extension; local municipal multiplier (Hebesatz) determines final trade tax burden

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory for cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 6.55 million for goods or EUR 2.05 million for services per fiscal year; Master File documentation applies when group revenue exceeds EUR 100 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the GCC/UAE Economic Substance Regulations; instead, German tax law enforces substance through § 42 AO (general anti-abuse rule), § 39 AO (substance over form), § 50d EStG (anti-treaty-shopping), and the ATAD general anti-abuse rule. A UG claiming treaty benefits and EU directive relief must maintain genuine economic substance: real office, employees, functional decision-making authority, and operational risk control

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, extended to 10 years in especially serious cases, plus monetary fines, confiscation orders, full back-tax collection, late-payment interest and public disclosure of the enforcement measure

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for taxes not paid due to intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties; personal civil restitution to the tax authority applies; criminal conviction can impose professional disqualification and travel-bans
Shell Company / Sham Structure

Statutory sanction: Under § 39 AO, the entity is disregarded and income re-attributed to the economic owner; treaty and EU-directive relief denied; commercial-register deregistration; administrative fines up to EUR 50,000 per episode and cancellation of VAT registration

Director personal liability: Directors face personal indemnity for under-withheld corporate tax and trade tax, civil liability for false commercial-register declarations, and criminal exposure under §§ 283 StGB when sham structures are part of insolvency concealment; travel-bans and deportation-restrictions can be ordered
AML / Money Laundering Through Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Criminal money-laundering under § 261 StGB: imprisonment up to 5 years; administrative fines under § 56 GwG up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; payment-account freezing, revocation of payment services and public enforcement notices

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for deficient AML controls and failure to report suspicious transactions; civil damages to regulators and affected customers; severe cases lead to personal asset freezes, travel restrictions, and disqualification from serving as managing directors
Failure to File and Pay Corporate Taxes

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessments (Schätzung) with adverse surcharge rates; late-filing penalties up to EUR 25,000; interest at 6% per annum; enforcement through seizure, insolvency petition and preferential tax claims in insolvency

Director personal liability: Personal director liability under §§ 69, 71 AO for unpaid wage tax and social-security contributions; imprisonment up to 5 years for aggravated evasion; travel-bans, asset freezes and professional disqualification are available
