Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister (Commercial Register, Section B / HRB) kept by the local Amtsgericht (Local Court) — for Berlin, Amtsgericht Charlottenburg; electronic filing exclusively through the certifying German civil-law notary via the EGVP/XJustiz e-justice portal.

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG) — German Limited Liability Companies Act; the Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) is governed as a statutory variant under § 5a GmbHG; formation requirements under §§ 1-2 GmbHG; registration procedure under §§ 7-10 GmbHG; simplified Model Protocol under § 2a GmbHG.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarial deed of incorporation (Errichtungsniederschrift) with Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory Model Protocol (Musterprotokoll pursuant to § 2a GmbHG), together with the formal application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) submitted to the Handelsregister.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG(haftungsbeschränkt) requires a minimum stated share capital of EUR 1.00 (at least one business share of EUR 1.00); the contribution must be made in cash, must be fully paid before registration, and must remain at the free disposal of the Managing Director — evidenced by a bank credit confirmation (Kontonachweis) attached to the registration application. Contributions in kind are not permissible under the simplified Model Protocol. Post-formation, 25% of annual net profit must be retained in a statutory legal reserve (gesetzliche Rücklage) until the reserve equals 25% of the registered share capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: certification of the office premises by the local building authority (Bauaufsichtsbehörde) or an approved fire safety expert, confirming escape routes, fire alarm systems per EN 14604, fire extinguishers, and compliance with the Musterbauordnung and state building codes. Structural load inspections: the tenant must obtain the landlord's written confirmation of floor-load capacity for office use and any server equipment carried by the structure; heavier IT infrastructure requires a structural engineer's check. Occupational clearance: mandatory workplace risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung) under §§ 4, 5 Arbeitsschutzgesetz, ergonomic screen-workstation compliance under ArbStättV, registration with the employers' liability insurance association (BG) upon first employee hire, and, for physical premises, inspection by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt; civil-defense zoning obligations are not triggered as the site does not constitute a hazardous installation (Störfallbetrieb).

Paid-in capital rule: The UG(haftungsbeschränkt) requires a minimum stated share capital of EUR 1.00 (at least one business share of EUR 1.00); the contribution must be made in cash, must be fully paid before registration, and must remain at the free disposal of the Managing Director — evidenced by a bank credit confirmation (Kontonachweis) attached to the registration application. Contributions in kind are not permissible under the simplified Model Protocol. Post-formation, 25% of annual net profit must be retained in a statutory legal reserve (gesetzliche Rücklage) until the reserve equals 25% of the registered share capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office — www.bzst.de, with local Finanzämter administering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Domestic UG: (1) notarized incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), plus municipal Gewerbeanmeldung; (2) file the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung electronically via ELSTER within four weeks of formation, whereupon the competent Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; (3) apply separately to BZSt on Form USt 1 C (via ELSTER) for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (German VAT ID) used for intra-EU B2B transactions and OSS declarations. Non-resident foreign entities: submit the Fragebogen through ELSTER directly to BZSt, which allocates the case to the competent Finanzamt and issues a Steuernummer; a German VAT ID is obtainable from BZSt where the foreign entity performs German taxable supplies. There is no general turnover threshold for locally established entities — registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply; cross-border B2C digital suppliers may instead elect the Union-OSS scheme below the EUR 10,000 annual intra-EU threshold, which displaces local German VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Domestic UG: (1) notarized incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), plus municipal Gewerbeanmeldung; (2) file the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung electronically via ELSTER within four weeks of formation, whereupon the competent Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer covering corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; (3) apply separately to BZSt on Form USt 1 C (via ELSTER) for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (German VAT ID) used for intra-EU B2B transactions and OSS declarations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity election exists in German tax law; an UG is a body corporate taxed at entity level under Körperschaftsteuergesetz and Gewerbesteuergesetz. Available statutory elections: Organschaft (fiscal unity) consolidation for corporate income tax and trade tax under §14 KStG / §2 Abs.2 GewStG; Kleinunternehmer small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 (2025 rules); cash-basis VAT accounting (Ist-Versteuerung) under §20 UStG; OSS registration election for intra-EU distance sales and B2C electronic services under §18j UStG; VAT entity grouping for financially integrated enterprises under §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 UStG. Hybrid-mismatch relief is mandatory under §4k EStG (ATAD implementation) with no elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory turnover threshold for German-established businesses — registration is required from the first taxable supply. Non-resident suppliers of B2C digital and distance-selling services: EUR 10,000 Union-wide annual threshold under §18 Abs.4a UStG, above which German VAT registration or OSS is compulsory. Domestic small-business relief: prior-year turnover ceiling of EUR 25,000 (§19 UStG, effective 1 January 2025).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: zero-rated under §4 Nr.1 lit.a and §6 UStG where tangible goods are physically dispatched or transported by the supplier (or for the supplier's account) from German/EU customs territory to a non-EU destination; the supplier must hold documentary evidence of the actual exit, the goods must be destined for the customer's business use and leave the EU unchanged. Intra-EU supplies: zero-rated as intra-Community supplies under §6a UStG when customer VAT ID and EC Sales List reporting are valid. Cross-border services: B2B digital EdTech services are outside German VAT scope when the recipient is established outside Germany (place of supply at recipient's seat, §3a Abs.2 UStG), substantiated by the customer's valid foreign VAT ID and supporting contract; B2C electronically supplied language-learning subscriptions supplied to consumers outside the EU are outside German VAT scope under §3a Abs.3 Nr.5 UStG, evidenced by customer residence, payment-billing country (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing) and IP-location data; B2C supplies to EU consumers are declared via OSS at destination-state rates.

  • Commercial Invoice (Handelsrechnung) and delivery note (Lieferschein)
  • Transport documentation — Bill of Lading (Konnossement), CMR waybill or Air Waybill (AWB) showing the goods' final non-EU destination
  • Bank payment evidence and inward remittance certificate (Zahlungseingangsbeleg) demonstrating FX repatriation
  • For zero-rated intra-EU supplies: EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and the buyer's valid VAT identification number

Mandatory phased domestic B2B e-invoicing: from 1 January 2025 every German entrepreneur must receive structured machine-readable invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD or EDI-compatible formats) for domestic supplies; from 1 January 2027 taxpayers with turnover above EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 all other taxpayers, must issue such e-invoices through an approved transmission channel. Germany operates no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime comparable to the Italian SDI; transaction-level transaction reporting to the tax administration is planned from 2027 in alignment with the EU ViDA initiative. Public-sector invoices must comply with the XRechnung standard; API-based submission to authorities runs exclusively through ELSTER (§87a AO).

Insufficient or false export documentation triggers retrospective cancellation of the zero-rating and reassessment of German VAT at 19% (§6, §18 UStG), disallowance of corresponding input VAT recovery, and late-payment interest at 0.15% per month (§233a AO). Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating constitutes tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under §370 AO, sanctionable by fines up to EUR 50,000 or imprisonment up to five years, with elevated penalties for gang-based or large-scale export fraud; negligent cases fall under §378 AO (fines up to EUR 50,000). Ancillary consequences include personal liability of the tax representative (§71 AO), prohibition from public procurement, and mandatory blacklisting in the BZSt risk database. BZSt and German Customs (Zoll) jointly operate risk-based audit programs targeting speculative exporters and platform-based digital merchants; BMF administrative guidance requires two independent, non-contradictory corroborating documents for every zero-rated export, including the customs declaration and payment-repatriation proof.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro (EUR) enjoys full and unrestricted convertibility in both current and capital account transactions by virtue of Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital) and the EU Capital Requirements Regulation framework. Germany imposes no exchange-control authorization requirement for inbound equity injections, outbound profit distributions, dividend remittances, or royalty payments. The only residual obligation is statistical: any cross-border payment or receivable exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven business days for balance-of-payments statistics (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik). Profit repatriation is obstacle-free provided the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) observes corporate-law capital maintenance rules (Kapitalerhaltung) under the GmbHG, which prohibit distributions that erode the registered statutory capital base.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies to export receipts. Germany abolished all exchange-control surrender obligations in the course of full capital liberalization; export proceeds (whether in EUR or foreign currency) may be freely retained onshore or offshore. The sole statutory requirements are (i) the AWV Section 67 statistical declaration for receipts above EUR 12,500 and (ii) standard German commercial invoicing documentation. No central-bank approval is required for keeping foreign currency accounts.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank) in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin – Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht). The governing legal framework comprises the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV, Chapter 3, Sections 62–68) for cross-border statistical declarations and the Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz – GwG) for banking compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability as a secondary operational liquidity corridor for USD-zone receivable collection and cross-border disbursement, but expressly Low viability as the exclusive treasury anchor or as the statutory capital-deposit vehicle. Licensed electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) operate under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) holding e-money in safeguarded accounts; they are not statutory deposit-taking credit institutions and therefore do not benefit from the German deposit-guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz, EUR 100,000 statutory protection) unless integrated via a fully licensed EU banking partner. German notaries and the commercial register (Handelsregister) require the UG's initial capital formation (Stammeinlage) to be verifiable through a credit institution; a stand-alone EMI license is legally insufficient for the Section 8(1) GmbHG notarized capital confirmation. Recommended architecture: (i) a German onshore bank account for capital injection and statutory treasury; (ii) an EMI account (USD-denominated) as a settlement collection rail for Apple/Google App Store payouts requiring multi-currency funneling.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the UG's initial capital (Stammeinlage) and any subsequent capital increase (Kapitalerhöhung): the wire must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EEA) exclusively from a regulated credit institution directly to the UG's German account titled in the company's own precise legal name (including the 'i.G.' suffix before registration). The payment reference must include the notary file number (URN) and the statutory formula 'Einlage zur UG (haftungsbeschränkt)'; the notary's capital-confirmation certificate under Section 8(1) GmbHG is released only after the incoming amount is 'at the free disposal' of the managing director, a condition ordinarily satisfied after SEPA/instant-TARGET2 settlement. For non-resident corporate shareholders, the routing must additionally carry: (i) the MT103 originator's full legal address and sector; (ii) a board resolution of the subscriber approving the subscription; (iii) a sworn declaration under the German Banking Act confirming that the deposit is genuine equity and not a disguised loan; and (iv) an AWV balance-of-payments report filed with the Bundesbank within seven business days of the credit clearing if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500.Outbound clearing rails for the EdTech subscription business: (a) SEPA Credit Transfer (TARGET2, T+1) for all EEA counterparties, including Apple/Google EU settlement accounts which accept EUR IBAN collections; (b) SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 pain.001 message for non-EEA royalty, licensing, subcontracting, and marketing disbursements, using standardized purpose codes: 'SOFT' (software services) for tutoring-platform fees, 'TRSD' (licenses) where intellectual-property rights are the economic underlying, and 'COMM' (commissions) for market-development partners; (c) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700, UCP600) are not a principal rail for SaaS subscription flow but remain permissible for large enterprise training contracts, executed as irrevocable sight LCs with a clean delivery certificate referencing 'delivery of interactive language-learning software license keys'. Apple App Store and Google Play Billing settlement receipts arrive as periodic mass-payout SEPA/EDI batch files referenced by merchant ID; treasury must reconcile these batch references against platform payout statements to clear receivables efficiently.EUR/USD deliverable outright forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) with maturities up to 12 months, permissible under GmbHG Section 43 fiduciary standards provided the notional volume is matched to projected USD-denominated Apple/Google store payout exposure and is non-speculative.EUR/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for bank lines lacking a physical EUR/USD deliverable market, executed through BaFin-regulated EU investment firms under the same corporate-liability doctrine.EUR/USD cross-currency swaps (Devisenswaps) for tenor-matched treasury management of the capital account, limited to a 24-month projected cash-flow collar.EUR/USD plain-vanilla purchased currency options (protective puts, no written options) to establish a floor on large enterprise licensing disbursements denominated in USD.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability as a secondary operational liquidity corridor for USD-zone receivable collection and cross-border disbursement, but expressly Low viability as the exclusive treasury anchor or as the statutory capital-deposit vehicle. Licensed electronic-money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) operate under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) holding e-money in safeguarded accounts; they are not statutory deposit-taking credit institutions and therefore do not benefit from the German deposit-guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherungsgesetz, EUR 100,000 statutory protection) unless integrated via a fully licensed EU banking partner. German notaries and the commercial register (Handelsregister) require the UG's initial capital formation (Stammeinlage) to be verifiable through a credit institution; a stand-alone EMI license is legally insufficient for the Section 8(1) GmbHG notarized capital confirmation. Recommended architecture: (i) a German onshore bank account for capital injection and statutory treasury; (ii) an EMI account (USD-denominated) as a settlement collection rail for Apple/Google App Store payouts requiring multi-currency funneling.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

30.0% typical aggregate effective load (Corporate Income Tax 15.0% + Solidarity Surcharge 5.5% on CIT = 0.825% + average Gewerbesteuer/Trade Tax ~14.175%; municipal Hebesatz variation drives the exact trade tax component).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the WHT, yielding a combined 26.375% statutory rate. Qualifying EU corporate shareholders can claim the Parent-Subsidiary Directive at 0% upon a 12-month minimum holding period and ≥10% shareholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 100,000 for cross-border goods trade; EUR 50,000 for services; EUR 30,000 for intangible/licence and interest-related transactions with foreign related parties (GAufzV). Failure to maintain contemporaneous Local File/Master File may result in penalty of up to EUR 10,000 plus a 5% surcharge on transfer-pricing corrections made by the tax auditor.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a UAE-style standalone 'Economic Substance Regulations' code, but substance requirements are embedded in German general anti-avoidance law and DTA beneficial-ownership clauses. A UG that exists solely as a letterbox or conduit fails §42 AO, the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, and the German concept of 'wirtschaftliche Betrachtungsweise'. To be respected, the UG must maintain actual local management, a registered office, strategic decision-making in Germany, substantive EdTech operational activity (staff, infrastructure, contracts, risk assumption) and, in line with §8c KStG, sufficient economic participation for loss/credit utilisation. This is especially acute when Apple/Google App Store revenues are booked in a low-tax jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under §370 AO, imprisonment of up to 5 years for ordinary evasion, or 6 months to 10 years for particularly serious cases (massive amounts, customs/transfer-pricing manipulation, abuse of office). Monetary fines are supplementary and calculated from the evaded tax amount.

Director personal liability: GmbH/UG statutes allow direct criminal prosecution of the managing director. The corporate veil is pierced for deliberate tax fraud: the director is personally liable for evaded taxes, late interests, and penalties, and may be subjected to preventive travel bans or surrender of passport in serious cases. Civil liability extends to restitution to the tax authority under §§31, 823 BGB and §64 GmbHG for losses caused to creditors.
Shell Company / Letterbox Entity (Briefkastenfirma / Abzweigung)

Statutory sanction: The tax office may disregard the entity under §42 AO, deny treaty benefits under the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test, reallocate income to the foreign owner, and impose back taxes plus interest under §233a AO; if discovered after formal loss/credit utilisation, the UG may be retroactively liquidated for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: A managing director who orchestrates a letterbox UG is exposed to personal liability for the entire amount of back taxes, trade tax, withholding tax, and applicable penalties. German insolvency law may support a claim for 'Existenzvernichtungshaftung' (destruction-of-existence liability) if the director denudes the UG of assets while maintaining an external display of solvency. Repeated or sophisticated planning can result in criminal tax evasion charges and extradition-compatible travel restrictions.
Withholding Tax and Dividend/Interest Leakage

Statutory sanction: The UG is personally liable for the withheld amounts (§44 Abs. 5 EStG); late payment triggers 0.5% monthly interest and a late-payment surcharge. The tax office can assess the full WHT at the corporate level.

Director personal liability: The managing director is directly liable for unremitted WHT under §69 AO, even if the UG is subsequently insolvent. Intentional failure to withhold is treated as tax evasion, leading to prison sentences and court-ordered restitution. Directors may also face AML reporting duties under the GwG when distributing funds to high-risk jurisdictions.
AML / Transaction Monitoring Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Violation of the German Anti-Money-Laundering Act (GwG) exposes the UG and responsible persons to fines up to EUR 100,000 per violation for ordinary negligence and up to EUR 1 million or more for serious organisational failures; if funds are used to launder proceeds of crime, criminal penalties under §261 StGB apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or recklessly process illicit subscription revenue as part of a structured cross-border scheme can be imprisoned and fined; they are personally liable for forfeiture of goods/assets under §73 StGB and for damage claims by the financial intelligence unit (FIU). In egregious cases, a travel ban and freezing of personal bank accounts protect the investigation and enforce restitution.
