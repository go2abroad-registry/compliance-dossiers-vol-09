Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); digital portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet) at https://www.emta.ee, customs e-services at https://e-ts.ee

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Eesti Osaühing (OÜ), the natural person shareholder/board member must obtain an Estonian digital ID or e-Residency card, then incorporate via the Estonian Commercial Register online portal (ariregister.ee). Upon completion the Commercial Register issues a registry code (Registrikood) which serves as the national tax identification number. For VAT registration, the entity must file Form KÄ via e-MTA under the e-Residency authenticated account. Non-established foreign entrepreneurs without a permanent establishment are required to appoint a fiscal representative when taxable supplies to Estonian private individuals are made; where reverse-charge applies, VAT registration may be unnecessary. The tax ID for VAT purposes is prefixed 'EE' followed by the 9-digit registry code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

There is no check-the-box regime. Estonian OÜ is statutorily treated as a body corporate subject to entity-level income tax, but under the Estonian corporate income tax system the tax liability arises only on distributions, deemed distributions, and non-business expenses at a rate of 20/80 of the gross distribution (effective 25% on net cash distributed); retained and reinvested profits are taxed at 0%. No optional flow-through election is permitted. Hybrid mismatch treatment follows ATAD and Estonian law, with no unilateral entity election. Advance pricing agreements and specific refund procedures for non-residents are available through e-MTA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident entrepreneurs, mandatory VAT registration arises when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 per calendar year. Non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies to non-VAT customers in Estonia are required to register without a threshold; supplies to Estonian VAT-registered customers are generally subject to domestic reverse charge, permitting the non-resident to be exempt from VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of deep-frozen meat and pelagic fish outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT where the goods are physically dispatched from Estonia to a non-EU destination within the statutory timeframe, title passes, the exporter retains documentation proving departure from the EU, and the customs export declaration is accepted by the customs office. The exporter must be the seller and must hold evidence of both dispatch and transport, and in practice payment evidence such as SWIFT/LC credit advice is retained to establish the commercial reality.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / IE-523) legitimized by Estonian customs
  • Bill of Lading or CMR consignment note or air waybill proving physical departure from EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, veterinary health certificate and EU export certificate for meat/pelagic fish
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/Bank payment confirmation evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds

Estonia does not operate a real-time transactional fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for standard B2B invoices. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G invoices conforming to the European norm EN 16931; private sector e-invoicing is voluntary but widely accepted. VAT returns, customs declarations, and periodic listings are filed electronically through e-MTA and the national customs ETS system. There is no API stamping mandate for ordinary commercial invoices in Estonia.

Where the Estonian Tax and Customs Board determines that zero-rating was claimed without sufficient proof of export or on fraudulent documentation, the 0% rate is revoked and VAT at 22% is assessed from the date of the original supply, with late interest accruing. The assessment may extend to the full invoice value and connected corporate income tax distributions. Under the Estonian Taxation Act, intentional tax evasion carries fines and imprisonment under the Penal Code, including penalties for serious tax fraud of up to seven years' imprisonment and disqualification from holding management positions. Reclamations of falsely claimed refunds are subject to collection with enforcement remedies under e-MTA authority.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has been a euro-area member since 1 January 2011; the euro is fully convertible. There are zero restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Articles 63-66. No exchange control permits, prior approvals, or repatriation consents are required for import settlements, shareholder capital injections, or profit remittances. The only cost layer on profit repatriation is the Estonian distribution tax: corporate income tax is levied at 20% on the gross distributed dividend (effective 20/80 of the net amount), with a reduced 14% for regular thin distributions; this is a corporate-level tax, not a convertibility or remittance barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Estonia imposes no statutory time limit, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or import proceeds. All receipts and payments may be freely denominated in EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency. The sole compliance duty is statistical: credit institutions and, in certain direct-reporting cases, the corporate itself must report cross-border transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 to Eesti Pank for balance-of-payments statistical compilation. Customs declarations (import of frozen beef/seafood) are filed electronically with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) under the Union Customs Code.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the national central bank of the Republic of Estonia and member of the Eurosystem; monetary policy is set by the European Central Bank (ECB) for the euro area. Financial supervision and AML/CFT oversight of credit institutions is conducted by the Estonian Financial Supervisory Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) in coordination with Eesti Pank, while the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) within the Police and Border Guard Board operates as the central FIU.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions may passport payment services into Estonia under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and the EMD2 (Directive 2009/110/EC) regime; such entities are viable for SEPA instant credit transfer and SWIFT MT103 disbursement routing, and for multi-currency shadow accounts. However, EMIs cannot legally issue documentary letters of credit, provide regulated lending facilities, or accept deposits, so they cannot replace a Tier-1 bank for an import working-capital structure. Fully offshore, non-EEA EMIs (e.g., Caribbean or Asia-Pacific licensed PSPs) are not viable for the operational treasury of an Estonian licensed importer; Estonian correspondent banks routinely reject or freeze funds routed through unregulated offshore payment intermediaries, and such routing triggers enhanced due diligence and potential Finantsinspektsioon AML scrutiny.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital contributions and subordinated shareholder loans enter the Estonian OÜ via SWIFT MT103 in EUR or USD or via SEPA Credit Transfer with a statistical purpose code recorded for Eesti Pank BoP reporting (threshold EUR 12,500). Recommended SWIFT field 70 purpose code: 'GAGE' (investment/equity capital injection) or the EBA payment purpose code 'EQUI' for SEPA messages; for USD, the remitting bank uses MT103 through the Estonian bank's USD correspondent (typically Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas, JP Morgan Chase, or Bank of America New York). No prior Bank of Estonia approval or registration is required for the injection, but the corporate must file the capital increase (if equity) in the e-Business Register and, for shareholder loans exceeding arm's-length thresholds, maintain a signed loan agreement, fixed repayment schedule, and benchmark-based interest-rate policy for Estonian transfer-pricing documentation. All payment advices referencing the import licence number (VTA approval) and the commercial contract number facilitate smooth clearing.Outbound settlement of frozen beef and seafood import trade is executed primarily through: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for advance payments, pro-forma invoice settlements, and balances under roughly EUR 50,000 per shipment; (2) SWIFT MT700 documentary letter of credit (sight or 30-90 day usance) for bulk carcass and pelagic-fish cargoes, with documents including commercial invoice, full set of clean-on-board bills of lading, VTA-recognized health/catch certificates, packing list, and certificate of origin; (3) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for small round-trip amounts to EEA suppliers and for payment of customs duties and import VAT to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (MTA) revenue account using the MTA external payment reference. All outbound USD and CNY wires are routed through the bank's SWIFT correspondent network; funds are not cleared as direct payments without full screening. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD and Annex 7 must be submitted by the 10th day of the month following the taxable month. For a 31 December fiscal year-end, the December TSD is due by 10 January; any profit distribution declared in the annual financial statements is generally declared in the TSD for the month of the general meeting resolving the distribution.

Deadline: · Frequency:
