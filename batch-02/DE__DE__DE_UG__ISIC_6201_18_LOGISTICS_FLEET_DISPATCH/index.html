Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) in the district of the registered office; filings are lodged electronically by the notary through the state electronic register network (XJustiz / handelsregister.de).

Governing statute

GmbH Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), sections 1, 2, 3, 5a, 7, 8, 10 and 35; German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB), sections 8 through 16; Commercial Register Regulation; Notarization Act (Beurkundungsgesetz).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified incorporation deed (Gründungsprotokoll) comprising the Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), and the application for entry into the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister) submitted electronically through the notary.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 34 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) requires nominal share capital of at least EUR 1, fully paid in cash before filing; contributions in kind are prohibited under section 5a(2) GmbHG. The full subscribed capital must be deposited into a German business bank account in the company's name and be freely disposable at the managing director's disposal, evidenced by a bank confirmation. Under section 5a(3) GmbHG, 25% of annual net profits must be retained as a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the leased office must comply with the applicable state building code (Landesbauordnung), including emergency exits, fire alarm, and landlord's fire protection documentation; occupational clearance: the company must register with the employers' liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft), comply with the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) on lighting, ventilation, sanitation, and risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung). No structural load inspection is required for an office-only tenant.

Paid-in capital rule: An Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) requires nominal share capital of at least EUR 1, fully paid in cash before filing; contributions in kind are prohibited under section 5a(2) GmbHG. The full subscribed capital must be deposited into a German business bank account in the company's name and be freely disposable at the managing director's disposal, evidenced by a bank confirmation. Under section 5a(3) GmbHG, 25% of annual net profits must be retained as a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local Finanzamt (Tax Office) designated by the registered seat of the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt); central electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt, Bonn) is the competent authority for the issuance of the EU VAT Identification Number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and for VIES cross-border data exchange.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Notarized execution of the articles of association (Musterprotokoll or individual deed) and appointment of the managing director; the share capital of the UG must be fully paid in, with a statutory minimum of EUR 1 per shareholder. Step 2 – Registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) through the notary; legal personality of the UG commences upon registration, which takes approximately 2–6 weeks depending on the local court. Step 3 – Filing of the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Questionnaire for Statutory Tax Registration) with the competent local Finanzamt, submitted electronically via ELSTER or in paper form; this questionnaire registers the UG for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT, and triggers the assignment of a national tax number (Steuernummer). Step 4 – Separate electronic application to the BZSt for the EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr) for cross-border B2B supplies; the EU VAT ID is required for VIES confirmation and for the reverse-charge mechanism in EU trade. Step 5 – Ongoing statutory obligations commence immediately: monthly or quarterly VAT pre-registration returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung) due by the 10th day after the reporting period, wage tax declarations where employees are engaged, annual corporate income tax returns, trade tax returns, and annual financial statements to be filed and published in the Commercial Register. Non-resident shareholders and foreign managing directors must provide certified identity and address documentation, apostilled corporate evidence, and, where applicable, proof of a German bank account for tax refunds; no minimum capital is required beyond EUR 1 per share, but profit-retention rules require 25% of annual net profit to be reserved until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a German private limited liability company with full legal personality. It is classified as a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to (i) corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at a uniform national rate of 15%, (ii) solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of the corporate income tax, and (iii) municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate of approximately 7%–17% depending on the local multiplier (Hebesatz of 200%–490%). The combined statutory burden in major German business centers (e.g., Berlin at 410%, Munich at 490%) ranges from approximately 29.8% to 32.9%. The UG is subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability on its income by reason of its statutory seat and place of effective management in Germany. There is no pass-through or tax-transparent election for a UG under German law; the entity pays tax on its own profits, and shareholder distributions are subject to a final withholding tax of 25% plus solidarity surcharge (26.375% total). The tax classification is statutory and does not depend on the nationality or residence of the shareholders; non-resident shareholding is permitted without altering the entity-level taxation regime. This classification is not connected to any tax-exempt zone or special economic zone regime.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime; entity classification as a taxable corporation is mandatory and cannot be overridden. Available statutory elections and structuring mechanisms include: (i) VAT group election (Organschaft) under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG, whereby the UG may be included in a German VAT group of a controlling parent and thereby consolidate VAT liabilities; (ii) corporate income tax and trade tax group (Organschaft) under Sections 14–19 KStG, requiring a profit-and-loss pooling agreement, majority voting rights, and financial integration for the entire fiscal year; (iii) small-enterprise exemption under Section 19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung), available only where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000, which is practically disadvantageous for an export-oriented UG because it blocks input VAT recovery; (iv) election to apply the cash-based accounting method (Einnahmen-Überschuss-Rechnung) where turnover does not exceed EUR 800,000 per year; (v) investment deduction (Investitionsabzugsbetrag) and accelerated depreciation elections under Sections 7g EStG for movable fixed assets such as telematics hardware and fleet-dispatch servers; (vi) partial income and trade tax elections for private assets that are not relevant to a UG; and (vii) mandatory application of German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 4k EStG and 14 KStG in line with the ATAD framework, with a waiver only where treaty-consistent alternative treatment can be evidenced. No 'deemed dividend' or thin-capitalization election exists beyond the statutory interest limitation rule of Section 4h EStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 — VAT registration becomes mandatory where the entrepreneur's total turnover in the preceding calendar year exceeded EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is expected to exceed EUR 50,000 (Section 19 UStG, thresholds in effect since 1 January 2025; previously EUR 17,500 / EUR 50,000). Below these thresholds, the entrepreneur may elect the small-business exemption, which relieves from VAT charging but simultaneously denies input VAT deduction. For a UG engaged in cross-border logistics-fleet SaaS, immediate VAT registration and USt-IdNr procurement is the commercially necessary default because the small-business exemption would block the deduction of development, hosting, and infrastructure input VAT and would impair the reverse-charge administration applicable to B2B supplies to EU clients.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The UG may claim German VAT zero-rating or the non-application of German VAT in four distinct scenarios: (1) Export supplies of goods to non-EU destinations, zero-rated under Section 6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the export is evidenced; (2) intra-Community B2B supplies of goods, exempt under Section 6a UStG, provided the customer's valid EU VAT ID is disclosed and confirmed through VIES and the goods are dispatched to another Member State; (3) cross-border B2B services, including SaaS, telematics licenses, and fleet-optimization subscriptions, where the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction under Section 3a UStG and German VAT is not chargeable, with reverse charge applying in the customer's EU Member State or local VAT in third-country jurisdictions; (4) B2C electronically supplied services to non-EU customers under Section 3a(5) UStG, which are not subject to German VAT. For a logistics-fleet SaaS UG, the qualifying revenue stream is Category 3/4; incidental hardware or SIM-card dispatches qualify under Categories 1 and 2 with mandatory customs and transport documentation. Claiming zero-rating requires that the UG retains reliable commercial documentation demonstrating the foreign customer's identity, the cross-border character of the supply, and the economic performance outside Germany.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS/AES electronic export filing with Master Reference Number) for physical goods shipments; for pure SaaS delivery, no customs filing is applicable and the substantive evidence is the master subscription agreement, usage logs, and technical delivery records
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or carrier tracking proof evidencing physical dispatch of hardware or goods from German/EU territory
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, ISO currency, and the statutory zero-rating annotation ('steuerfrei gem. §6 UStG' or 'Reverse Charge – liability of the recipient')
  • VIES certificate or printed validation of the customer's EU VAT ID for intra-Community B2B supplies; for third-country customers, official tax resident certificates (Ansässigkeitsbescheinigung) or Certificate of Incorporation
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice showing receipt of the foreign-currency consideration in the UG's German bank account, with matching currency conversion records

Germany has a statutory B2B e-invoicing mandate without real-time fiscal clearance. Since 1 January 2025, all German entrepreneurs must be technically able to receive structured electronic invoices in the formats XRechnung, ZUGFeRD (version 2.x XML or embedded XML), or EDI. Since 1 January 2027, German enterprises with total turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices to domestic B2B customers; as of 1 January 2028, all German B2B issuers are required to issue structured e-invoices. The e-invoice data are exchanged bilaterally between trading parties; there is no central government clearance portal, digital stamping requirement, or prior API approval in Germany. Tax authorities receive aggregated periodical VAT data through the electronic Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung filed via ELSTER by the 10th day after each monthly or quarterly reporting period. EU intra-Community B2B supplies must additionally be reported in a recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung, ZM) filed monthly via ELSTER, with cross-validation of customer VAT IDs through VIES. A real-time transaction reporting system for domestic B2B supplies is under legislative design (EU ViDA framework and OECD SAF-T) with expected operationalization after 2030.

German tax audits (Außenprüfung/Betriebsprüfung under Section 193 AO) subject export zero-rating and cross-border service invoices to rigorous documentary verification; every claimed zero-rated supply is cross-checked against customs records, transport documentation, recipient VAT IDs, and payment flows. Where the required evidence is incomplete, the tax authorities retroactively assess German VAT at 19% on the previously zero-rated turnover and impose default interest of 0.5% per month under Section 233a AO, in addition to late-payment penalties under Section 240 AO. Deliberate or grossly negligent misdeclaration constitutes VAT evasion under Section 370 AO, punishable with imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, or up to 10 years in especially severe cases involving substantial sums, plus monetary fines; fraudulent VAT refund claims may also trigger asset seizure and expedited enforcement. The managing director bears personal liability for unpaid VAT under Sections 69 and 34 AO read in conjunction with Section 34 GmbHG where payment or declaration obligations are breached. If the foreign consideration is not received within the 180-day customary period, the tax administration may treat the transaction as an adjustment of consideration under Section 17 UStG, requiring the UG to issue a corrective invoice and repay the foreign VAT input claimed; non-repatriation of export proceeds may additionally trigger reporting duties under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV) to the Deutsche Bundesbank. All books, invoices, and export proofs must be retained for 10 years under Section 147 AO; under assessment periods for tax evasion extend to 10 years, so a clawback assessment can reach a full decade after the relevant supply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is an IMF Article VIII member and an EU Member State applying Articles 63–66 TFEU; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control approvals, capital-account restrictions, or profit-remittance permits applicable to the UG. Inbound equity contributions, shareholder loans, SaaS subscription receipts, and outbound dividends/loan repayments may be freely converted and repatriated. The only constraints are (i) German dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% combined, treaty-reduced for qualifying non-resident shareholders), (ii) the UG-specific statutory reserve obligation under § 5a GmbHG requiring retention of 25% of annual net profit until the registered capital reaches €25,000, and (iii) purely statistical AWV reporting for single cross-border payments of €12,500 or more. A UG with non-resident ownership faces no conversion hurdle, but German banks will require full economic-substance documentation for the inbound capital route.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds, including SaaS license fees and telematics subscription income. The UG may retain 100% of export earnings in EUR, USD, or any permitted foreign currency without time limitation. The sole compliance requirement is statistical: every export-related receipt or cross-border payment from a non-resident of €12,500 or more must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 7 calendar days via the AWV balance-of-payments portal (Form Z1/Z5, using the service-export/software-license purpose code). No Bundesbank authorization or BaFin approval is required to hold or convert foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: The Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany) is the competent foreign-exchange and cross-border payments statistics authority, operating under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht – BaFin) supervises banking conduct, anti-money-laundering compliance, and FX derivative market conduct. For a resident UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), the Bundesbank receives all cross-border payment reports, while BaFin licenses the commercial banks and payment institutions used by the UG; no separate central-bank license is required for the UG itself.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Koerperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% Solidaritaetszuschlag on the CIT amount (0.825 percentage points), plus municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at rates of approximately 7% to 17%, producing a combined statutory effective rate of roughly 22.8% to 32.8% depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate statutory dividend withholding rate of 26.375% on gross dividends paid by the UG to non-resident shareholders. For German-resident corporate shareholders benefiting from the Section 8b KStG participation exemption, a refund or exemption certificate may neutralise the effective corporate tax burden; however, the distributing UG must generally withhold and remit unless a valid exemption certificate or treaty relief is documented.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally 31 July of the year following the taxation year. If the return is prepared by a licensed tax adviser, the statutory automatic extension runs to 28/29 February of the second following year; further extension may be granted by the responsible Finanzamt.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same deadline regime as the corporate income tax return: 31 July after the assessment year, extended to 28/29 February of the second following year when prepared by a tax adviser.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany has no statutory de-minimis euro threshold for Local File and Master File preparation; documentation obligations arise whenever material cross-border controlled transactions with related enterprises exist under Sections 90(3) AO and 1 AStG. A mandatory Country-by-Country Report is required where the consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. In practice, the tax audit authority may demand documentation for any fiscal year in which related-party transactions, including SaaS licensing, telematics route data fees, intercompany royalties and fleet service charges, exceed EUR 500,000 per related party.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations scorecard. Instead, economic substance is tested through statutory anti-abuse principles: actual place of effective management under Section 10 AO, real staff, physical premises, decision-making, substantive contracts and operational assets. An UG that operates only as a letterbox or fails to demonstrate its own logistics-fleet-dispatch software development, licensing and data-processing activity can be re-characterised under Section 42 AO, denied treaty benefits and subjected to full audit reconstruction of income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Underreported Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Section 370 AO: imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine; in particularly serious cases imprisonment of up to ten years. Evaded tax is assessed plus Hinterziehungszinsen of 0.5% per month for the underpayment period, generally capped at ten years. Civil administrative late-payment surcharges are also imposed.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschaeftsfuehrer) is personally liable under Sections 34 and 71 AO for corporate tax obligations he or she controls. Personal criminal liability attaches to directors who sign false tax declarations; courts impose restitution orders, travel bans, seizure of assets and imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced where the director strips assets to avoid payment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax office can disregard the corporate form under Section 42 AO, apply a substance-over-form reconstruction, disallow agreed transfer prices, deny treaty benefits and assess penalties for materially incorrect declarations. Administrative fines and penalty charges of up to 10% of adjusted income may be added.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for deficiencies arising from misuse of corporate form, particularly where the UG has been used as a sham to shift profits to tax havens. Personal liability orders can include restitution, freezing orders under Sections 111b ff. StPO, travel bans and disqualification from serving as director.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Section 162 AO, the tax auditor may estimate taxable income to the UG's detriment. If documentation duties under Section 90(3) AO are grossly violated, a penalty surcharge of 5% of the adjusted income amount (minimum EUR 5,000) is imposed; severe or intentional cases trigger additional criminal liability under Sections 370 and 378 AO.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for taxes resulting from inadequate transfer-pricing documentation where they intentionally or negligently fail to maintain records. Sanctions include administrative fines, personal enforcement orders against private assets and, in egregious cases, criminal prosecution.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violations of the GwG attract administrative fines of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover, and money laundering under Section 261 StGB is punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years; confiscation of assets is mandatory for organised laundering. Breaches of beneficial ownership reporting to the Transparenzregister also trigger fines.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable if they knew or deliberately ignored that the UG was used to launder assets. Personal sanctions include imprisonment, travel bans and permanent disqualification from corporate management. German courts apply source-of-funds verification duties and can pierce the veil against the director and de facto shadow shareholder.
