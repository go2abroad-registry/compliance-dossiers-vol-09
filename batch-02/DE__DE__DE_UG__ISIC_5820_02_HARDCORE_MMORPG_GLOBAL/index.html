Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Republic of Germany – Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) (central VAT-ID and OSS administration; www.bzst.de) acting with the local Finanzamt competent for the UG's registered seat; electronic filing and VAT-ID application via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Within four weeks of incorporation, the UG must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt, obtaining a Steuernummer (local tax number). With the Handelsregisterauszug and the tax registration confirmation, the UG then applies to BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr./VAT ID). Non-resident direct sellers must register through ELSTER's Grundinformationsblatt or the BZSt OSS portal, using a fiscal representative if the Finanzamt requires one; VAT registration is effective from the first taxable supply. The UG should simultaneously register for the EU OSS Union scheme and, where relevant, for Einfuhr-Umsatzsteuer deferral.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in German law. Available elections: (1) Organschaft (corporate income tax and VAT fiscal unity) under §14 KStG and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG; (2) Kleinunternehmer-Regelung under §19 UStG (VAT relief where prior-year turnover ≤ €25,000 and current-year expected turnover ≤ €100,000); (3) OSS special scheme election without threshold, replacing individual EU VAT registrations; (4) reverse-charge election for qualifying intra-EU B2B supplies. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not elective; Germany applies ATAD hybrid rules as mandatory law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies to a resident UG or to non-resident suppliers of B2C electronic services (registration is mandatory from the first taxable download or app-store sale). Optional small-business relief under §19 UStG applies up to €25,000 prior-year / €100,000 expected current-year turnover (2025 reform). For intra-EU distance sales of goods and B2C digital services under the Union OSS, the German domestic threshold is €10,000 per annum before the sale becomes taxable in the consumer's EU Member State.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of goods (e.g., collector editions or merchandise dispatched outside the EU) qualifies for VAT exemption under §4 Nr. 1a UStG where the goods are taken to a third-country destination with a validated customs export declaration. Intra-EU dispatches to B2B customers are tax-free under §6a UStG upon confirmation of the customer's valid VAT ID and filing of the recapitulative statement. Digital MMORPG services and in-app purchases are not 'goods': their export treatment arises from place-of-supply rules — B2B services to non-EU operators fall outside German VAT (§3a Abs. 2 UStG); B2C digital downloads to non-EU consumers fall outside German VAT (§3a Abs. 3 UStG); EU B2C sales via Apple/Google are VAT-accounted by the platforms as deemed suppliers; EU B2B supplies are reverse-charged. Contemporaneous documentation of customer location (IP address, mobile country code, payment BIN, residence certificate) is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS electronic declaration with ECTN/MMT exit confirmation)
  • International transport document (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR)
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit evidence (Bundesbank Z5 report where value exceeds €12,500)

From 1 January 2025, electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) is mandatory for all domestic B2B supplies; structured e-invoices must be issued in XRechnung (EN16931 XML) or ZUGFeRD hybrid format and transmitted through the federal PEPPOL/BZSt infrastructure. Germany currently has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime comparable to Italy or Romania; however, under the EU ViDA initiative, near-real-time transaction-based digital reporting to BZSt is being implemented incrementally (expected 2028–2030). For app-store sales, Apple App Store and Google Play act as deemed taxable suppliers under §3 Abs. 3 UStG, assuming responsibility for VAT collection, clearance, and e-invoicing/reporting on B2C digital supplies; the UG must reconcile platform VAT statements with local records.

Fraudulent zero-rating or unsubstantiated outside-scope treatment constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO (imprisonment up to 10 years and criminal fines) and VAT fraud under §§26a–26c UStG. Missing export or location proof results in retrospective imposition of German VAT at 19%, denial of input VAT deduction, interest under §233a AO (0.5% per month) and late-payment surcharges under §240 AO (1% per month). Additional penalties apply for failure to file or incorrect OSS recapitulative statements (Verspätungszuschlag up to €2,500 per BZSt notice). Criminal and civil liability extends to managing directors; in deemed-supplier scenarios, Apple/Google may be held jointly and severally liable for unaccounted VAT on German-channel B2C sales.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the TFEU free-movement-of-capital provisions and the IMF Articles of Agreement. There are no FX surrender requirements, no exchange-control approvals, and no administrative distinction between current and capital account convertibility in the German legal system. Profit remittance from a German UG is lawful and generally does not require central bank authorization; however, dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders may be subject to German withholding tax under Section 50a EStG, with available relief under double tax treaties, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or the EU Interest and Royalties Directive, where applicable. Practical hurdles arise only from KYC/AML source-of-funds verification, sanctions screening, financial-statement solvency checks, and the statutory transparency and tax-compliance obligations of the UG.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no specific timeline requiring export proceeds to be converted into EUR or repatriated. Exporters are free to hold, invoice, and receive foreign currency through German and foreign bank accounts. Nevertheless, under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), residents must report cross-border payments and receivables to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes where the aggregate value per calendar month exceeds EUR 12,500. Such reporting is purely statistical and does not constitute an FX control or surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and monetary authority of the Federal Republic of Germany, Frankfurt am Main), acting in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) for licensing, prudential supervision, and anti-money-laundering enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary payment and settlement conduit, but Low as the sole treasury banking relationship. An offshore non-EU electronic-money institution cannot lawfully provide payment services in Germany without a European passport or a BaFin/ZAG license, and a German banking relationship is therefore necessary for statutory reporting and regulatory credibility. EU-licensed EMIs, including German BaFin-licensed or passported E-money institutions, can support remote onboarding through video KYC, issue German/EU IBANs, and provide useful operational rails for app-store payouts, vendor settlements, and low-value gaming transactions. However, Tier-1 commercial banks remain the preferred counterparty for capital escrow, liquidity concentration, AWV statistical reporting, and possible future credit facilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital and shareholder loan injections into the German UG should be routed as follows: EUR transfers from EU shareholders via SEPACredit Transfer using the UG’s German IBAN; non-EUR transfers via SWIFT MT103 with the UG as ultimate beneficiary, full ordering-party and beneficiary bank details, and a clear purpose reference indicating either equity contribution, share capital increase, or shareholder loan. German banks will require the capital payment to be credited to the UG’s capital escrow or operational account after completion of KYC, board authorization, and source-of-funds checks. All cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 in a calendar month must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV, and the bank may rely upon the UG to provide the required statistical categorization. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax (0.825 percentage points), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at a municipal multiplier that typically yields an effective combined load of approximately 29.8% to 32.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

German statutory dividend withholding tax is 25.0% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, giving a total of 26.375% on gross distributions. Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, a qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 25% for 12 months may obtain a 0% rate; various DTA provisions reduce the rate to 15%, 10%, 5% or 0% depending on shareholding and treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory German thresholds under the GAufzV: EUR 5,000,000 for cross-border transactions in goods and EUR 500,000 for cross-border services, including digital services and licensing, trigger mandatory contemporaneous documentation. A Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. Local File and Master File obligations apply when requested by the German tax authority, generally within 60 days for normal cases and 30 days for extraordinary transactions.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those of offshore financial centres. Nonetheless, economic substance is legally decisive for: (i) beneficial ownership under DTAs and Section 50d(3) EStG; (ii) the General Anti-Abuse Rule in Section 42 AO; (iii) German CFC rules under the AStG; and (iv) anti-hybrid and ATAD rules. A German UG holding only IP, without employees, office premises, board meetings in Germany, or operating substance, can be recharacterised as a shell company whose DTA relief is denied and whose income is reattributed to the actual controlling foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – deliberate under-declaration of revenue or artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Up to five years' imprisonment, or up to ten years for aggravated evasion, under Section 370 AO; fines, immediate reassessment, late payment interest at 0.5% per month, and seizure of assets. Negligent tax short-payment may be fined under Section 378 AO up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) is personally liable for non-withheld and unpaid corporate taxes under Sections 69 and 34 AO, including civil restitution, garnishment of private assets, travel bans, and imprisonment; corporate veil-piercing is applied where the director deliberately or grossly negligently caused non-payment; Section 64 GmbHG imposes personal liability for payments made at a time of insolvency.
Shell Company / treaty-shopping structure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under Section 50d(3) EStG; disregard of arrangement under Section 42 AO; CFC income recharacterisation under the AStG; reassessment plus 6% annual interest; additional trade tax and corporate income tax with penalties. If accompanied by false declarations, criminal prosecution for tax evasion may also be brought.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for resulting German corporate tax, trade tax and withholding tax; courts may pierce the UG's limited liability if the corporate form is used for fraudulent or abusive purposes; personal assets can be attached and foreign travel restricted where tax crimes are charged.
Transfer Pricing documentation failure or non-arm's-length royalty/interest payments

Statutory sanction: Under Section 162(4) AO, a surcharge of 5% of the adjusted income increase, with a minimum EUR 5,000 and maximum EUR 250,000, unless the taxpayer proves the adjustment was not caused by poor documentation; the tax authority may issue a rebuttable presumption that the taxpayer's reported income is understated; additional penalties under Section 90(3) AO apply.

Director personal liability: Director liability extends to any additional tax and surcharge if the director signed the non-compliant transfer pricing documentation or knowingly instructed false intercompany pricing; criminal prosecution may follow where the documentation is fabricated; civil liability under Section 43 GmbHG requires reimbursement of any corporate loss caused by breach of duty.
AML / unauthorised payment services

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed payment services constitute a criminal offence under the ZAG, punishable by imprisonment up to five years and fines; BaFin may issue cease-and-desist orders, appoint special inspectors, and impose administrative fines; in addition, profits from unlicensed activities are subject to disgorgement under Section 73 StGB.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal responsibility for operating without the required licence and for failing to report suspicious transactions under the GwG; personal fines and forfeiture of personal profits can be imposed; court may impose a travel ban and order restitution to affected customers; the UG's limited liability does not shield directors from knowingly unlawful payment operations.
