Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires a minimum stated share capital of EUR 1.00, which must be fully paid in cash to a German business bank account before registration and must remain in the free disposal of the managing director; no escrow or blocked-account mandate applies. Until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, the company must retain at least 25% of annual net profits as a legal reserve pursuant to Section 5a(3) GmbHG.

Funding & unlock path: Fire brigade certified acceptance (Brandschutzabnahme) of server rooms and evacuation routes; static structural load inspection certificate for raised floors and server racks issued by the building authority (Statiker-Nachweis); occupational workplace clearance under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung, ArbStättV), including emergency lighting, emergency exits, workstations for shift trading, and occupational medical examinations where required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT ID and Federal Tax Certificate; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) competent for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessments; electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) plus BZSt online services (www.bzst.de)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the UG's articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag/Musterprotokoll) and file with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister); Step 2: Upon registration, complete the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER (Formulare 15a/15b) and submit to the locally competent Finanzamt within one month of business commencement; Step 3: Receive the statutory tax number (Steuernummer) from the Finanzamt for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT (Umsatzsteuer); Step 4: Apply separately to the BZSt for the EU VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer – USt-IdNr.) via the official online application (Datenübermittlung an das BZSt through ELSTER); Step 5: Register the UG's bank accounts, opening balance sheet, and managing director (Geschäftsführer) in the respective fiscal records; estimated completion of all registrations: 4–10 weeks from notarization

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime in Germany; a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is mandatorily taxable as a corporation at entity level (Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge plus trade tax). Available elections: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) election under § 14 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG whereby a 100% (or ≥75% for corporate income tax after 2021) shareholding UG can be absorbed into the controlling parent for corporate income tax, trade tax, and/or VAT; (2) small-entrepreneur scheme under § 19 UStG for turnover below EUR 22,000 (previous year) and EUR 50,000 (current year estimate), optional waiver; (3) the revised EU-ATAD hybrid mismatch rules (§ 4k EStG, § 2b KStG) may oblige the UG to align its classification where a foreign hybrid entity or permanent establishment mismatch exists; (4) trade tax apportionment election for a UG's permanent establishments in other municipalities (§ 28 ff. GewStG); (5) loss carry-forward election under § 8d KStG (loss-free continuation of business) versus forfeiture under § 8c KStG on 50%+ share transfer

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany applies a universal VAT registration duty for entrepreneurs: no monetary threshold for registration of a UG; however, the small-entrepreneur exemption (§ 19 UStG) applies only where turnover (excluding VAT on exempt and capital goods) did not exceed EUR 22,000 in the preceding calendar year and is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000 in the current year, in which case the UG may apply to the Finanzamt for exemption; the UG may nevertheless irrevocably opt into standard VAT registration for up to 5 years (not applicable to most exempt financial services); from 1 January 2025 the previous-year threshold rises to EUR 25,000 and the current-year limit to EUR 100,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

HFT trading services are generally VAT-exempt (not zero-rated) under § 4 Nr. 8 UStG where the UG acts on its own account or carries out dealings in securities, derivatives, options, futures, and other financial instruments; where the UG performs offshore B2B services to non-EU clients (e.g., market-making agency services, liquidity provision or algorithmic order execution at the client's risk), the place of supply under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG is the business location of the recipient, so the service is outside the scope of German VAT and effectively 0% charged with full input-VAT deduction if the UG is a VAT-registered entrepreneur; for export zero-rating of goods (§ 6 UStG) and intra-community supplies (§ 6a UStG) the UG must prove (1) the recipient's VAT ID, (2) movement of goods to another EU member state or to a destination outside the EU, and (3) entrepreneur-to-entrepreneur (B2B) character; for supplementary services (advisory, software licensing, data services) rendered to foreign recipients, the B2B place-of-supply rule under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG likewise excludes German VAT; refund of foreign input VAT is claimable via § 18 Abs. 9 UStG for EU refunds (BZSt application through the electronic portal by 30 September of the following year) and by § 18 Abs. 9a UStG (third-country refund) where reciprocity exists

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung/ABD) printed from the German ATLAS export system confirming the export release (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or equivalent electronic transport title) evidencing dispatch outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice complying with § 14 UStG and § 14a UStG (including the UG's VAT ID and recipient's VAT ID where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103/FX reconciliation statement) evidencing foreign-currency receipt to the UG's € account
  • Intra-community supply evidence: CMR/transport contract signed by consignee, monthly dispatch list, and the customer's valid VAT ID as registered in the VIES database
  • For financial services: a written service agreement and execution confirmation evidencing the counterparty's identity, location, and B2B capacity for place-of-supply determination

Mandatory electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025 every German VAT-registered entrepreneur must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x XML format) for domestic B2B supplies; from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices via the E-Rechnung portal or certified software; from 1 January 2028 all businesses must issue e-invoices. Germany does NOT operate real-time transaction-level fiscal clearance (no API-based continuous transaction controls), but B2B transaction data must be reported via mandatory annual/deferred VAT returns (Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldung) submitted electronically to ELSTER by the 10th day following each calendar month or quarter; additionally intra-community supplies must be reported in the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung – ZM) to BZSt by the 25th day after each calendar quarter (or monthly where optional); no digital stamping or SAF-T real-time documentary controls are required

Export zero-rating and non-rendering of German VAT for financial services are subject to strict documentary scrutiny by the Finanzamt upon substantive tax audit (Außenprüfung). Claim to 0% or full exception is denied retroactively if the UG fails to demonstrate the evidential chain; where the 0% claim is unfounded, the UG is liable under § 14c UStG to pay the statutory VAT on the invoice amount, and the corporate income tax and trade tax base are retroactively adjusted, with late-payment interest of 0.15% per month (§ 233a AO); forklift penalties: (1) Steuerhinterziehung (tax evasion) under § 370 AO for deliberate misuse of the export/offshore evidence carrying imprisonment up to 5 years or fines and retrospective clawback for up to 10 years; (2) recklessness or fraud under § 370a AO may extend imprisonment up to 10 years; (3) administrative penalty for breach of e-invoicing duties up to EUR 5,000 per invoice under § 26a UStG; (4) personal, joint, and several liability of the managing director (Geschäftsführer) under § 69 AO and § 34 AO for unpaid VAT where the UG becomes insolvent; (5) the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern may deny or claw back refunds under § 48 Abs. 2 BordergO, and the UG's export-VAT registration can be blocked on non-compliance; all export and offshore records must be preserved for 10 years per § 147 AO

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility pursuant to Article 63 TFEU; the EUR is freely convertible with no exchange control regime. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and repatriation of capital from a UG are unrestricted and require no central bank approval; the only frictions are the 25% withholding tax on dividends (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and, where applicable, church tax) and the statistical cross-border payment declaration obligations under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV). Non-resident ownership of a UG is explicitly permitted; capital injections enjoy unencumbered convertibility. Limited residual restrictions exist solely through EU/UN sanctions enforcement, WTO/OECD compliance, and sectoral investment screening under AWV Sections 55-60, none of which materially impedes an algorithmic market-making treasury.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Germany imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no conversion ratio for export proceeds or trading receipts; foreign currency may be held indefinitely. The only binding obligations are (a) the AWV Section 67-69 statistical report to Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days for any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 received by or on behalf of a German resident company, and (b) transaction-level reporting under MiFIR/EMIR where the UG holds a BaFin license; neither constitutes a surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Eurosystem national central bank) jointly with the European Central Bank (ECB); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) holds complementary regulatory authority over licensed investment firms and payment institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG), the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG), and the Securities Trading Act (WpHG/MiFIR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low. BaFin-licensed EEA electronic money institutions (e.g., Pliant, N26 Business, Kontist) can provide IBANs and multi-currency ledger accounts under ZAG, but they impose velocity caps, offer no direct T2/TARGET2 or Fedwire membership, lack MT103 commercial payment issuance, and cannot provide segregated clearing or intraday credit. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs are legally prohibitive without a MiFID/ZAG passport and will be rejected by German compliance. For ultra-low-latency algorithmic market-making, a full credit institution or prime-brokerage clearing relationship is mandatory; EMIs are viable only for non-critical operating expense disbursement.
