UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal framework is the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), particularly sections 1, 3, 10, 11, 12, 15, 19, 20, 21, 23, 43 and 45; the German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB), particularly section 18 on company names; the German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), particularly sections 5a, 7 and 8; the Act on the Central Register of Beneficial Owners (Transparenzregister- und Finanzinformationsgesetz); the Transparency Register Ordinance (Transparenzregisterverordnung, TrDüV); the EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives as implemented in German law; Regulation (EU) 2015/847 on payer information; Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 on cash controls; Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 and related EU AML reforms as they become applicable; the EU Sanctions Regulations; the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG); and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). A UG must report its beneficial owners to the German Transparency Register unless an applicable statutory exemption applies. A beneficial owner is generally the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the entity, including a person holding more than 25 percent of capital or voting rights or exercising control by comparable means. If no natural person can be identified, the legal representatives or managing directors are recorded as notional beneficial owners.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A UG must have a domestic business address suitable for service of documents under GmbHG section 8 and the Act on Proceedings in Family Matters and in Matters of Non-Contentious Jurisdiction (FamFG), particularly sections 10 and 15 concerning service and register proceedings. A virtual office may be acceptable if it provides a genuine German address, reliable receipt and forwarding of official correspondence, access to company records, and a lawful basis for use. A mere mail-drop, unstaffed address, or address shared without operational control creates registration, banking, tax, and AML risk. For developer infrastructure, database, API monitoring, and cloud-tool activities, no sector-specific operating licence is generally required, but the company should maintain demonstrable management, accounting, customer-contract, data-protection, information-security, and server or hosting arrangements. A virtual office is therefore legally possible but commercially and AML-wise weaker than a staffed office.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Unternehmensregister at https://www.unternehmensregister.de and the electronic commercial-register system at https://www.handelsregisterbekanntmachungen.de; Germany has no single nationwide statutory name-reservation portal. Pre-clearance is normally obtained informally from the competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (Industrie- und Handelskammer, IHK) and the competent local register court (Registergericht).

Name reservation / lock period

Germany does not provide a generally applicable statutory nationwide name-reservation period. Obtain written or email pre-clearance from the relevant IHK before notarization, but treat it as non-binding. Submit the incorporation deed and registration application through a German notary to the competent local Registergericht. If the court issues an interim objection, respond within the stated deadline with amended wording, evidence of distinctiveness, proof of lawful use of sensitive terminology, or an IHK opinion. If registration is refused, request the formal refusal order and file an immediate complaint (sofortige Beschwerde) with the competent register court within the statutory period stated in the order, generally one month under the FamFG framework. The complaint should identify the challenged finding, provide legal and factual grounds, attach the IHK opinion and supporting evidence, and propose an alternative name where appropriate. A final adverse decision may be escalated to the competent Higher Regional Court (Oberlandesgericht) where legally available. Name protection and unfair-competition remedies under HGB sections 18 to 37 and the German Trademark Act remain separate from commercial-register approval.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister des zuständigen Amtsgerichts (Commercial Register of the competent Local Court) at the registered office of the company; nationwide access via the common registry portal www.handelsregister.de.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG), principally §§ 1 (formation and legal personality), 2 (notarial form of the Articles of Association), 3 (content of the Articles), 5 (share capital), 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)), 6 (managing directors), 7 (application for registration), 8 (contents of the application), 10 (entry in the Commercial Register), and 39 (list of managing directors); implementing rules under the Handelsregisterverordnung (HRV) and the Gerichtskostengesetz (GKG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarielle Beurkundung des Gesellschaftsvertrags (notarized Deed of Incorporation including the Articles of Association) followed by the electronically certified Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister (Application for Registration in the Commercial Register), submitted exclusively by and through the notary.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Germany — Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) and EU-level matters; local Finanzamt for TIN (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessment; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online portal.

E-government filing portal

For a UG founded by non-residents: (1) notarize the articles of association and register the UG in the commercial register (Handelsregister) via the local court; (2) obtain trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) at the local Gewerbeamt; (3) file the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt to obtain the Steuernummer (TIN); (4) apply to BZSt for the VAT ID for intra-EU and cross-border invoicing; a designated German authorized representative is required for non-resident shareholders.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a limited-liability corporation taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income (Körperschaftsteuer 15% + solidarity surcharge 5.5% thereof, Gewerbesteuer municipal rate ~14–17%); no pass-through eligibility. — For a UG founded by non-residents: (1) notarize the articles of association and register the UG in the commercial register (Handelsregister) via the local court; (2) obtain trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) at the local Gewerbeamt; (3) file the official tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt to obtain the Steuernummer (TIN); (4) apply to BZSt for the VAT ID for intra-EU and cross-border invoicing; a designated German authorized representative is required for non-resident shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a limited-liability corporation taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income (Körperschaftsteuer 15% + solidarity surcharge 5.5% thereof, Gewerbesteuer municipal rate ~14–17%); no pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box regime. Statutory elections include: Organschaft (corporate tax/VAT fiscal unity under §§ 14 ff. KStG and § 2 UStG) with profit-pooling and single VAT taxpayer status; option to tax real estate under § 9 UStG; small-business relief under § 19 UStG within turnover thresholds; hybrid-mismatch restrictions apply under § 4k EStG (ATAD), disallowing elective hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for any economically active entrepreneur; small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer, § 19 UStG) only if prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year projection ≤ EUR 50,000; intra-Community acquisition threshold EUR 500,000 and EU distance-selling/OSS threshold EUR 10,000 trigger registration; exporters of digital B2C services must register/use OSS above EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies (§ 6 UStG) and intra-Community supplies (§ 6a UStG) are tax-free with input VAT credit when goods leave the EU or are dispatched to another Member State and the supplier holds commercial and transport evidence; cross-border B2B cloud/database/API services to non-EU customers fall outside German VAT (reverse charge, § 3a UStG); B2C digital services to EU consumers are taxed at destination via OSS. Zero-rating requires an export declaration, transport proof (Belegnachweis) and, for intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid USt-IdNr. plus EC Sales List (Zusammenfassende Meldung).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS) with MRN
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR Consignment Note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT bank receipt evidencing FX repatriation
  • Delivery and transport-chain evidence (destruction-of-export-proof records)
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer's valid EU VAT ID and EC Sales List records

Mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing since 1 Jan 2025 (structured XRechnung/ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, transmitted via Peppol, API or portal; hybrid paper/PDF transitional rules until 2027); no general real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in Germany; cash-register operators must use a certified Technical Security Element (TSE) with goBD-compliant data recording.

Finanzamt audits may retroactively deny zero-rating and input-VAT recovery for missing export substantiation (reasonable-evidence standard), triggering interest at 0.5% per month (§ 233a AO) and late-payment penalty up to 1% per month (§ 240 AO, capped at EUR 50,000); false or fraudulent zero-rating qualifies as tax evasion under § 370 AO (fines to EUR 50,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years, 10 years in aggravated cases) and § 14c UStG shifts VAT liability to the invoice issuer; regular VAT assessment limitation is 4 years, extending to 10 years for evasion, permitting retrospective clawback and criminal referral.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility; the euro is freely convertible and there are no exchange-control approvals, no restrictions on foreign-currency account holding, and no mandatory euro conversion for resident or non-resident actors. Profit remittance barriers are purely tax-driven, not FX-driven: dividend distributions by a UG to non-resident shareholders attract 25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate), normally reduced under an applicable double tax treaty (e.g., US 0%/5%, China 10%, UK 10% with holding conditions) either through a Freistellungsbescheinigung or a refund application. Current-account flows (SaaS subscriptions, cloud infrastructure fees, professional services) face no convertibility obstacle; the only statutory friction is the mandatory Deutsche Bundesbank statistical declaration for cross-border payments of EUR 12,500 or more per transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no minimum retention in Germany, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be held in any currency in any domestic or foreign account. The sole binding requirement is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank: every inbound cross-border payment of EUR 12,500 or more must be reported on the AWV Z1/Z2 declaration within seven calendar days, including the underlying purpose code (e.g., Warenausfuhr, Software-Dienstleistung, Lizenzgebühren). Failure to report constitutes an administrative offence (Bußgeld up to EUR 30,000) under AWV Section 48.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) acts as the central bank and external-sector statistical authority under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV); financial market supervision and AML conduct are vested in the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin); export and import payment surveillance rests with the Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle (BAFA). The legal regime comprises the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV), the Geldwäschegesetz (GwG), and EU free-movement-of-capital provisions (TFEU Art. 63).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for EU/EEA-licensed digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business, Pliant, Currencycloud) as secondary treasury rails, but they cannot replace a German bank account for statutory, tax, or escrow purposes. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI does not hold a banking licence and cannot accept deposits or grant credit, so client funds are not protected under the German Deposit Guarantee Act; (ii) an EEA EMI passporting into Germany must file a notification with BaFin; (iii) cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 still trigger Bundesbank AWV reporting irrespective of the intermediary; (iv) German tax authorities and trade counterparties view a foreign IBAN as a book-tax red flag for substance, and payment flows must preserve a clear German operational nexus. Mercury ACH is not directly legally viable for a standalone UG: Mercury requires a US entity (Delaware LLC or C-Corp), a US registered agent, an EIN, and beneficial-ownership filing under the Corporate Transparency Act; the UG should instead open a dedicated USD account at a German multi-currency bank or, if justified, establish a US holdco/sub. Recommended architecture: German bank as the primary account for all tax, escrow, and EU settlements; Stripe Billing (Ireland or Amsterdam processing entity) settled into the German EUR IBAN; EMIs used only for foreign-currency vendor payouts below EUR 12,500 per transaction, with monthly aggregation monitoring.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant trajectory: (i) Equity contributions - the UG's notarized articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) fix the share capital; the contribution is paid to a dedicated German bank account with an 'Auszahlungssperre' (blocked for free disposal); the bank issues the statutory confirmation (Bankbescheinigung) to the German notary; the commercial register entry is then filed.
