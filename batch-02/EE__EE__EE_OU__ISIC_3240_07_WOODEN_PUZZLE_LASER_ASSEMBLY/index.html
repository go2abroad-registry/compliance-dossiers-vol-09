Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Maksu- ja Tolliamet (MTA; Estonian Tax and Customs Board) – official portal: e-MTA (https://emta.ee); commercial registry interface: e-Business Register (https://ariregister.rik.ee); e-Residency digital identity accepted for all filings.

        
E-government filing portal
An Osaühing (OÜ) receives its statutory tax identifier automatically upon incorporation: the 8-digit registry code (registrikood) serves as the corporate TIN for income tax, VAT, and customs purposes; no separate domestic TIN certificate is issued. The e-Business Register entry is conclusive proof of tax identification. VAT registration (KMKR number) is applied for by the board member through e-MTA using a digital ID or e-Residency digital card; a non-resident foreign company files Form K and may be required to appoint a local tax representative. Registration is immediate via electronic filing; physical presence is not required for EU/EEA residents.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: Estonia operates a deferred entity-level corporate income tax system – undistributed profits are taxed at 0% regardless of territorial source; tax liability arises only upon profit distribution, deemed dividends, and certain fringe benefits (20/80 gross-up of net benefit). Non-resident shareholders face withholding tax on dividends (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions for EU/EEA parents; otherwise 20/80 on the gross payment). Territorial scope: taxation attaches to the distributing Estonian entity, not to the shareholder's jurisdiction. CFC rules (Income Tax Act § 52) allocate passive income of low-taxed foreign subsidiaries to the Estonian parent. — An Osaühing (OÜ) receives its statutory tax identifier automatically upon incorporation: the 8-digit registry code (registrikood) serves as the corporate TIN for income tax, VAT, and customs purposes; no separate domestic TIN certificate is issued. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation: Estonia operates a deferred entity-level corporate income tax system – undistributed profits are taxed at 0% regardless of territorial source; tax liability arises only upon profit distribution, deemed dividends, and certain fringe benefits (20/80 gross-up of net benefit). Non-resident shareholders face withholding tax on dividends (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions for EU/EEA parents; otherwise 20/80 on the gross payment). Territorial scope: taxation attaches to the distributing Estonian entity, not to the shareholder's jurisdiction. CFC rules (Income Tax Act § 52) allocate passive income of low-taxed foreign subsidiaries to the Estonian parent.

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box system exists in Estonian law; entity classification is statutory. Available elections include: (i) regular dividend election – payments made at least once in the three preceding years and within 12 months of the balance-sheet date qualify for the reduced 14% rate (taxable base equal to 14/86 of the net dividend), whereas non-regular distributions are taxed at 20/80 of net; (ii) VAT group election under VAT Act § 7(3) allowing two or more Estonian taxable persons to form a single VAT group; (iii) voluntary VAT registration from the first euro of taxable turnover; (iv) the EU Parent-Subsidiary zero-withholding election for qualifying dividends; (v) ATAD hybrid-mismatch adjustments are mandatory and do not require a statutory election. No elective pass-through status is available for an OÜ.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
22%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
€40,000 per calendar year of taxable domestic supplies of goods and services (VAT Act § 3(1)); €10,000 per calendar year for intra-EU distance sales of goods and intra-Community acquisitions under the EU distance-selling framework (VAT Act § 4(2)); voluntary registration for turnover below these thresholds is permitted. Foreign companies without Estonian establishment require registration for Estonian VAT on B2C supplies exceeding the threshold or on importation.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
0% VAT applies to: (i) export of goods physically dispatched or transported out of the EU (VAT Act § 15(1)) – applies to precision laser-cut wooden puzzle sets delivered to third-country customers; (ii) intra-EU supplies of goods to a taxable person registered for VAT in another Member State (VAT Act § 16), evidenced by a valid EU VAT ID obtained through VIES validation and movement of goods to that Member State; (iii) services directly connected to exported goods, including transport and handling; (iv) processing work on movable goods if the resulting goods are exported. The exporter must be VAT-registered and retain evidence that dispatch occurred within 90 days of the supply; failure to substantiate export converts the supply to taxable at 22%. Toy manufacturing (ISIC 3240, HS 9503) does not require a statutory license for export.

No real-time continuous-transaction-control (CTC) clearing regime: Estonia does not impose digital stamping, state clearance APIs, or pre-approval for invoices. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies – invoices to Estonian public sector entities must be structured XML (e-invoice 3.x / UBL) submitted through e-MTA or the PEPPOL network. B2B e-invoicing is not mandatory; however, XML e-invoices are the MTA-preferred audit format. B2C retail sales are fiscalized through MTA-certified online cash registers (elektrooniline kassa) with fiscal memory chips; data are stored locally without real-time transmission to MTA. VAT returns are filed electronically via e-MTA; filing is monthly by the 20th day following the VAT period (annual returns permitted for small taxable persons with annual turnover below €40,000).

      
MTA export audits carry a five-year limitation period, extended to ten years where VAT fraud is suspected. Estonian law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is retained as the standard due-diligence benchmark for documentary evidence of export proceeds (MTA may accept contractual credit terms in excess of 180 days where adequately documented). Failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers: reassessment of VAT at 22% on the full supply value; default interest at 0.06% per day (circa 21.9% per annum); disallowance of corresponding input VAT credits; administrative penalty of up to €3,200 under the Tax Act; and criminal tax-fraud liability under Penal Code § 393 (fine or imprisonment up to 5 years for the responsible board member). Retrospective clawback extends to all input VAT previously recovered on costs attributable to the unsubstantiated zero-rated exports, with the burden of proving physical export or intra-EU movement resting on the taxable person.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Estonia uses the euro and has no general exchange controls. Current-account payments, trade settlements, service fees, royalties, salaries, and dividend distributions may generally be paid cross-border without quota, licensing, or mandatory conversion. Capital-account movements, including capital contributions, intercompany loans, acquisitions, and distributions, are generally permitted but remain subject to bank KYC, beneficial-owner verification, source-of-funds evidence, sanctions screening, corporate authorization, accounting support, and applicable EU investment-screening or anti-money-laundering controls. An OÜ may distribute profits only on the basis of properly approved and distributable financial results, subject to solvency and corporate-law requirements. Estonia generally taxes distributed profits at company level rather than imposing a conventional annual corporate-income tax on retained earnings; the standard distributed-profit tax rate is 22/78 of the net distribution from 2025, subject to applicable exemptions and anti-abuse rules. Dividend withholding, treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, and beneficial-ownership limitations must be reviewed separately for the recipient and payment chain.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Estonia has no statutory mandatory surrender requirement, no prescribed conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring an exporter to convert or repatriate export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in euros or other currencies, subject to the company bank's account terms, payment-service rules, sanctions controls, accounting records, VAT and customs documentation, and tax reporting. Banks may request commercial invoices, customs declarations, transport evidence, customer contracts, and an explanation of transaction purpose before crediting or releasing funds.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), within the Eurosystem; foreign-exchange activity is primarily governed by European Union law, Estonian AML legislation, payment-services law, sanctions law, and commercial banking compliance requirements. The Financial Intelligence Unit is the competent AML and suspicious-transaction authority.

Suitable EEA electronic-money institutions and payment institutions may onboard an Estonian OÜ remotely, but they are not banks, generally do not provide deposit-guarantee protection, and must safeguard customer funds under the applicable payment-services regime. Offshore institutions outside the EEA may face correspondent-bank, licensing, sanctions, data, and de-risking restrictions and may not be acceptable for regulated payment activity in Estonia. A fintech account should not be the sole treasury account where payroll, tax, customs, refunds, or substantial supplier payments require a stable domestic or EEA banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Route shareholder capital through the OÜ's account at an EEA bank or regulated EEA payment institution from an account held in the documented shareholder's or lender's name. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments and SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 equivalent messaging for non-SEPA currencies. Identify the transfer as share-capital contribution, additional paid-in capital, shareholder loan, or other legally supported funding; do not use an ambiguous payment reference. Maintain the shareholders' or board resolution, subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, originating-account proof, ownership information, and accounting entry. Estonia does not use a universal statutory FX purpose-code regime comparable to certain controlled-currency jurisdictions; the bank may nevertheless require an internal purpose classification, invoice, contract, loan documentation, or commercial-register evidence. Confirm whether the payment is intended for registered capital, an unregistered capital contribution, a loan, or operating revenue before execution. Screen all parties, banks, jurisdictions, and payment narratives against EU and applicable international sanctions. Large or unusual transfers may be held pending enhanced due diligence and should not be split to avoid controls.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers and SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 cross-border payments for non-SEPA transactions. Use documentary Commercial Letters of Credit, standby letters of credit, documentary collections, bank guarantees, or open-account payments according to counterparty risk and bank availability. A commercial LC should specify applicant, beneficiary, issuing and advising banks, currency, amount, expiry, shipment terms, Incoterms, documents required, discrepancy rules, partial-shipment and transshipment provisions, sanctions clauses, and governing rules such as UCP 600 where selected. Match invoices, purchase orders, customs and transport documents, goods descriptions, beneficiary names, and payment amounts. Maintain evidence for accounting, VAT, customs, transfer pricing, sanctions screening, and fraud monitoring. Estonia and the euro area provide access to SEPA and TARGET services through participating institutions; SWIFT availability depends on the selected bank and correspondent network. Avoid payments to personal accounts, unexplained third parties, sanctioned banks, or jurisdictions inconsistent with the documented supply chain.Deliverable EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/SEK, EUR/PLN, and other bank-offered forward exchange contracts for documented forecast or committed commercial exposures, subject to bank credit limits, appropriateness assessment, confirmations, collateral or margin terms, and EMIR reporting or clearing requirements where applicable.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies or jurisdictions where physical delivery is restricted or unavailable, subject to counterparty eligibility, local-market legality, EMIR and MiFID II requirements, valuation, collateral, and settlement controls.Foreign-currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for documented liquidity or funding exposures, subject to ISDA or equivalent documentation, counterparty credit limits, collateral arrangements, valuation controls, EMIR obligations, and accounting treatment under the applicable financial-reporting framework.Spot foreign-exchange transactions and forward-starting hedges executed through an authorized EEA bank or regulated investment firm for genuine operating, trade, or financing exposures; speculative activity, mismatched leverage, and hedging without documented exposure should be prohibited under the treasury policy.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for payment collection and operating flows, including SEPA transfers, card acquiring, marketplace receipts, and EUR treasury. Suitable EEA electronic-money institutions and payment institutions may onboard an Estonian OÜ remotely, but they are not banks, generally do not provide deposit-guarantee protection, and must safeguard customer funds under the applicable payment-services regime. Offshore institutions outside the EEA may face correspondent-bank, licensing, sanctions, data, and de-risking restrictions and may not be acceptable for regulated payment activity in Estonia. Stripe and Amazon Pay onboarding remains dependent on merchant-country eligibility, beneficial-owner verification, product and consumer-safety review, chargeback controls, VAT data, and settlement-account acceptance. A fintech account should not be the sole treasury account where payroll, tax, customs, refunds, or substantial supplier payments require a stable domestic or EEA banking relationship.
