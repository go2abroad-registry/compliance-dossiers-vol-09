Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Statutory minimum share capital for an OÜ is EUR 2,500 (ÄS § 135). Estonia permits zero-capital formation: no paid-up contribution is required at the moment of incorporation, and no escrow or bank-segregated capital account is mandatory. The founders subscribe the full nominal capital in the Articles of Association; the management board may call the payments as needed for solvency, and the subscribed capital must be traceable and effectively contributed by the end of the first financial year or upon board demand.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, MTA) under the Estonian Ministry of Finance; exclusive digital filing and VAT registration via the e-MTA portal (https://e-mta.emta.ee); corporate registration via the e-Business Registry (https://ariregister.rik.ee).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entrepreneur incorporates an OÜ through the e-Business Registry (available 24/7 using an e-Residency digital ID or a notarised application). The Commercial Registry automatically assigns the Estonian registration code (ärregistri kood), which constitutes the statutory Estonian TIN, and transmits the entry electronically to the MTA; the MTA opens the taxpayer file without any separate TIN application. To become a VAT trader, the OÜ files the KMR VAT-registration application in e-MTA and is issued an Estonian VAT identification number (KMKR) effective from the requested registration date. Non-resident foreign entities without an Estonian seat that make taxable supplies in Estonia must register directly with MTA under VAT Act § 19(4) from the first day of supply unless the €40,000 threshold or the cross-border OSS election applies; foreign entities may also voluntarily VAT-register before exceeding the threshold.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections include: (1) the periodic-distribution election under the Corporate Income Tax Act (TMS) §§ 15–16, allowing a reduced 14/86 rate (14% of gross dividends) on dividends distributed regularly from retained earnings up to the average of the prior three tax years' distributions; (2) voluntary pre-threshold VAT registration under VAT Act § 19; (3) the Union and Non-Union OSS scheme elections for distance sales of goods and electronic services crossing the €10,000 EU threshold; (4) hybrid-mismatch neutralisation under the ATAD 2016/1164 transposition in Estonian tax law, which applies automatically rather than by elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover (VAT Act § 19(2)) for supplies within Estonia; EUR 10,000 annual cross-border distance-sales/electronic-services turnover for intra-Union and non-Union one-stop-shop schemes; VAT registration is mandatory retroactively from the day the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT is available under VAT Act § 23 for exports of fast-fashion goods physically dispatched to third countries, provided the goods leave the EU within 90 days and an accepted customs export declaration (SAD/EX with MRN) exists; under VAT Act § 24 for intra-Community supplies to a customer holding a valid, VIES-verified EU VAT number, with the EC Sales List and transport evidence completed within the VAT return period; ancillary freight, packing and international logistics services connected to exported goods qualify as export-related services. Physical distance sales to consumers in third countries are treated as exports; e-commerce payment proceeds collected via Klarna BNPL and Shopify Payments do not alter the zero-rating analysis when the goods cross the EU external border.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document EX/SAD with MRN number and electronic customs acceptance)
  • Bill of Lading (sea or air) or CMR / international waybill corroborating that the goods left Estonia and the EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate in convertible currency evidencing the foreign buyer's payment settlement
  • Sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list and dispatch/fulfilment-centre records for the consignment
  • KMD INF export schedule electronically submitted to MTA with the VAT return and, where applicable, the EC Sales List for intra-Community zero-ratings

Estonia does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or mandatory digital stamping on invoices. Invoices issued to Estonian public-sector contracting authorities must be electronic in a structured, Peppol-compatible format. VAT returns (KMD and the KMD INF annex listing intra-Community and export supplies) must be filed electronically via e-MTA; automated risk-scoring, e-audit and cross-referencing with customs declarations, transport and bank transaction data constitute the fiscal control layer, facilitated by X-tee API integrations. No prior tax-authority invoice approval or QR-stamp regime is in force.

MTA conducts post-clearance audits using customs export data, VIES, bank FX records and e-commerce platform data. Deficient or late documentation triggers retrospective reclassification of the supply as taxable at 22% VAT, plus 0.06% daily late-payment interest and, in negligent cases, an administrative surcharge. Intentional fraudulent zero-rating constitutes tax fraud under Penal Code § 403, punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years for responsible managers; the MTA may simultaneously revoke VAT registration, disallow future zero-ratings, confiscate unjustified refunds, and publish sanction decisions in its e-audit register.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has full euro currency convertibility for both current and capital account operations. As a eurozone member state, Estonia applies the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU) and the EU Capital Requirements Regulation. There are no exchange controls, no residence-based convertibility restrictions, and no separate domestic FX authorization regime. Profit remittances, dividends, capital returns, and loan repayments are freely transferable in euro or any other currency, subject only to standard anti-money laundering (AML) verification and corporate tax compliance rules for distributions. The only material limitations arise from EU/UN sanctions, tax transparency directives, and the requirement for payment service providers to conduct bona fide KYC checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratios. Exporters, including e-commerce retailers, are free to retain, hold, or transfer export receipts in any currency and are not required to repatriate or convert EUR-denominated or foreign-currency proceeds within any timeline. This is consistent with EU capital liberalization and the euro regime. Reporting obligations are limited to statistical balance-of-payments granularity, as required by ECB Regulation (EU) No 184/2005 (as amended), through Eesti Pank data collection, but no treasury control or forced conversion is applied.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), within the Eurosystem and under the supervisory framework of the European Central Bank and the Estonian Financial Supervisory Authority (Finantsinspektsioon)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMIs is Medium. Estonia, as an EU member, permits the use of licensed EU/EEA e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) for corporate treasury operations under the PSD2 and the E-Money Directive, subject to passporting notifications. Offshore digital EMIs from non-EEA jurisdictions (e.g., UK, Singapore, the US) may execute SWIFT/SEPA payments to Estonian OÜs, but they cannot hold Estonian accounts without a physical or registered EEA entity unless directly authorized in Estonia. Legal constraints include AML/CFT equivalence assessments, the restriction that e-money accounts cannot provide credit or interest, and the need for the OÜ to maintain its underlying commercial settlement flows through an EU-authorized banking partner for lower counterparty risk. For an e-commerce fast-fashion retailer, a combination of an EU-licensed EMI (e.g., for low-value disbursements) and a tier-1 Estonian bank account is viable; however, relying solely on offshore EMI rails is disfavored by institutional investors and tax authorities in Estonia.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection of shareholder capital into the Estonian OÜ must flow through standard SEPA/SWIFT secured rails with transparent remittance instructions. The remitter must use the eurozone SEPA Credit Transfer for euro inflows originating in the EU/EEA, or a SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA/cross-border transfers. In the MT103 field 70 (Remittance Information), the payment should clearly reference 'Capital increase of OÜ [name], registry code ...'. To satisfy the banks' FX and AML controls, the sender must attach evidence of corporate resolution approving the capital contribution, the updated share register, and investor due-diligence packs. There is no mandatory government approval for equity capital injection; however, banks may require the use of a dedicated capital escrow or internal capital account until verification. For statistical reporting, the inbound flow is recorded as 'direct investment – equity capital' in Eesti Pank's balance-of-payments reporting and must be submitted by the resident entity via the bank's statutory reporting channels within the prescribed calendar timetable.Outbound disbursements for fast-fashion trade settlements should be routed through three primary clearing rails. First, for EU/EEA suppliers and logistics providers, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in euro for immediate delivery (single euro payments area standard, IBAN-based). Second, for non-EU/EEA suppliers in Asia, use SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) with complete 1B field completion and remittance details as per the trade invoice; the transfer must be accompanied by underlying commercial contracts and transportation documents if above EUR 10,000 or at the bank's CDD threshold. Third, for large bulk garment orders (USD 50,000 or more), use a documentary letter of credit (LC) issued via SWIFT MT700/MT710 and confirmed by the supplier's bank; the LC should be an irrevocable, at-sight or usance LC subject to UCP 600. For the fast-fashion business, supplier payments for small recurring sample orders are best executed using MT101/MT103 batches through treasury management systems, while LC usage is reserved to substantial purchase contracts. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMIs is Medium. Estonia, as an EU member, permits the use of licensed EU/EEA e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) for corporate treasury operations under the PSD2 and the E-Money Directive, subject to passporting notifications. Offshore digital EMIs from non-EEA jurisdictions (e.g., UK, Singapore, the US) may execute SWIFT/SEPA payments to Estonian OÜs, but they cannot hold Estonian accounts without a physical or registered EEA entity unless directly authorized in Estonia. Legal constraints include AML/CFT equivalence assessments, the restriction that e-money accounts cannot provide credit or interest, and the need for the OÜ to maintain its underlying commercial settlement flows through an EU-authorized banking partner for lower counterparty risk. For an e-commerce fast-fashion retailer, a combination of an EU-licensed EMI (e.g., for low-value disbursements) and a tier-1 Estonian bank account is viable; however, relying solely on offshore EMI rails is disfavored by institutional investors and tax authorities in Estonia.
