Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

German Commercial Register (Handelsregister) through the competent local register court (Registergericht); electronic publication and company-register information are available through the Unternehmensregister at https://www.unternehmensregister.de and register notices through https://www.handelsregisterbekanntmachungen.de. Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no uniform statutory nationwide company-name reservation period or automatic 30-day reservation mechanism. Obtain a preliminary written name assessment from the competent IHK and conduct searches in the Handelsregister, Unternehmensregister, German Patent and Trade Mark Office database, EUIPO, domain registries, and relevant business databases. If the register court rejects the name, request the written decision and legal grounds, identify the specific objection, and submit an amended filing or a formal objection/remedy within the period stated in the decision. Depending on the procedural act, an immediate complaint (Beschwerde) under the German Court and Notary Costs Act and applicable FamFG procedure may be available. Coordinate the remedy with the filing notary; do not use a rejected or materially confusing name while review is pending.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the competent local district court (Amtsgericht) for the company's registered office, with filings submitted electronically through a German notary.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), particularly Sections 1-3 (formation and articles), Section 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft), Sections 7-8 (registration requirements), Sections 10-11 (registration and legal effect), Section 13 (separate legal personality), Section 35 (management representation), and Section 42a (annual accounts and profit allocation). The German Commercial Code (Handelsgesetzbuch, HGB) applies to commercial register and accounting matters.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised deed of formation (Gründungsurkunde) incorporating the articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), followed by the electronic application for registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister). The model protocol (Musterprotokoll) may be used where its statutory conditions are satisfied.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 83 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) may be formed with stated share capital below EUR 25,000, with a statutory minimum of EUR 1. The subscribed cash contribution must be paid in full before registration; contributions in kind are not permitted at formation under Section 5a GmbHG. No statutory escrow account is required, but the capital is normally paid into a company bank account after notarisation and before the managing director confirms availability to the notary. The company must retain 25% of annual net profit, reduced by loss carryforwards, as a statutory reserve until capital reaches EUR 25,000, subject to the statutory conversion rules.

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial clearance is ordinarily required for a standard office. The premises must comply with applicable building, fire-safety, escape-route, electrical, workplace, and occupational-health requirements. A building authority or fire-protection authority may require occupancy approval, fire-safety documentation, structural load evidence, or inspections for a new, altered, or high-occupancy premises. The employer must conduct workplace risk assessments and comply with the German Occupational Safety and Health Act and related rules if employees are engaged.

Paid-in capital rule: An Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) may be formed with stated share capital below EUR 25,000, with a statutory minimum of EUR 1. The subscribed cash contribution must be paid in full before registration; contributions in kind are not permitted at formation under Section 5a GmbHG. No statutory escrow account is required, but the capital is normally paid into a company bank account after notarisation and before the managing director confirms availability to the notary. The company must retain 25% of annual net profit, reduced by loss carryforwards, as a statutory reserve until capital reaches EUR 25,000, subject to the statutory conversion rules.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is ordinarily required for a single-family office UG investing only its own family assets. The local Gewerbeamt is the relevant authority for any required trade registration. BaFin is the competent authority for potentially regulated banking, investment-services, asset-management, payment, or collective-investment activities.

Category: No manufacturing or industrial operating licence is ordinarily required. A BaFin authorisation under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz), Securities Institutions Act (Wertpapierinstitutsgesetz), or Capital Investment Code (Kapitalanlagegesetzbuch) may be required if the business provides regulated services, manages third-party assets, operates an investment fund, or conducts another regulated financial activity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German state tax administration through the competent local Finanzamt, with the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) responsible for issuing German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.). Tax registrations and filings are generally administered through the ELSTER electronic tax portal. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer); the BZSt issues the EU VAT identification number.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a capital company and is mandatorily subject to German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and solidarity surcharge on taxable income; it does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election. It is generally also subject to municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Partnerships may elect corporate taxation under Section 1a of the German Corporate Income Tax Act, but that election is not available to a UG and does not alter the UG's classification. A VAT small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply automatically if statutory turnover limits are satisfied, with an option to waive the exemption and elect ordinary VAT treatment; the waiver generally binds the taxpayer for the statutory minimum period. VAT grouping (Organschaft) may arise where its financial, economic, and organizational conditions are met, but it is not a general check-the-box election. German anti-hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not provide an elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business with ordinary taxable German supplies is exempt from VAT registration. The German small-business regime under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply, for tax periods from 2025, where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year domestic turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions. The regime is an exemption from charging VAT rather than a universal registration safe harbor. Foreign businesses with German taxable supplies may require registration from the first relevant transaction. The reduced VAT rate is generally 7% for specified goods and services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify as VAT-exempt with input-tax deduction under the German export exemption rules if the goods are transported or dispatched to the third country and the supplier satisfies documentary and accounting requirements. The supplier must establish export status, generally through customs exit confirmation or approved alternative evidence, and retain a clear link between the invoice, goods, transport, customs declaration, and customer. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for the separate VAT exemption for intra-Community supplies only if the purchaser's valid EU VAT identification number, transport to another Member State, and required reporting and documentary conditions are established. Services supplied to business customers generally follow the place-of-supply rule and may be outside German VAT, with reverse charge potentially applying; this is not technically a German 0% export rate. Services to private customers and special services require separate place-of-supply analysis. Financial services, investment management, and family-office activities may be VAT-exempt or taxable depending on the precise service, contractual function, and statutory exemption conditions; input VAT recovery may therefore be restricted.

  • Customs exit confirmation or other legally accepted proof that the goods left the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and evidence identifying the supplier, purchaser, goods, value, destination, and transaction date
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or freight-forwarder confirmation
  • Packing list and delivery records where relevant to identify the exported goods
  • Proof of receipt or alternative transport evidence where required by the applicable intra-EU or export documentation rules
  • Accounting records linking the exempt supply to the customs and transport evidence
  • Customer and VAT identification evidence for intra-EU supplies, including valid purchaser VAT ID and required recapitulative-statement data
  • For offshore services, contracts, customer-status evidence, place-of-establishment evidence, service descriptions, reverse-charge wording where applicable, and records supporting the place-of-supply analysis
  • Bank payment records may support commercial substantiation but are not, by themselves, a statutory substitute for customs exit evidence

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or continuous transaction-control system, and invoices are not generally subject to prior digital stamping by the tax authority. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices complying with the applicable German and European standard, principally EN 16931. For domestic business-to-business supplies, mandatory issuance of structured e-invoices is phased in: during 2025 and 2026 paper and other electronic invoices generally remain permitted subject to transitional rules; issuers with prior-year domestic turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 generally must issue structured e-invoices from 2027; mandatory structured e-invoicing is generally extended to all in-scope domestic B2B transactions from 2028. Exemptions and special rules apply to specified transactions, small-value invoices, and certain transport tickets. XML-based formats such as XRechnung and hybrid formats such as ZUGFeRD may be used if the structured data requirements are satisfied. VAT return and recapitulative-statement filings remain electronic through ELSTER where applicable.

Germany has no general foreign-exchange control or statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days. Export VAT exemption or input-tax recovery may be denied or clawed back if the supplier cannot substantiate export, transport, customer status, or the correct place of supply. The taxpayer may be required to correct VAT returns, repay improperly refunded or deducted input VAT, and pay statutory interest and other payment-related charges. Incorrect invoices may require correction and repayment of tax shown separately. Tax authorities may impose VAT assessments, late-filing penalties, administrative fines, and record-retention or cooperation sanctions. Intentional or reckless fraudulent claims may constitute tax evasion under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code, punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, and in particularly serious cases by imprisonment of six months to ten years. Criminal and civil recovery can extend to unpaid VAT, interest, and related tax liabilities. Export and intra-EU evidence should be retained in accordance with German tax-record retention rules, generally ten years for accounting records and eight years for specified business records under current rules, subject to the applicable document category and legislative changes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has exercised full capital and current account convertibility since 1959 under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; obligations of Art. 63 TFEU free movement of capital apply. No exchange control authorization, permit, or central bank approval is required for cross-border fund movements, currency conversion, or profit remittance by a resident UG. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to German withholding tax at 26.375% (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), reduced under DTT or refundable in specific cases; no Bundesbank clearance or quota applies to remittance. Statistical reporting under AWV is mandatory for aggregate cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month; the reporting burden is administrative only and does not curtail convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Germany abolished mandatory surrender of export proceeds and all mandatory conversion ratios in 1959. Export receipts may be retained freely in EUR or foreign currency with no statutory holding period. A pure statistical filing obligation applies under Deutsche Bundesbank balance-of-payments reporting: residents must report aggregate receipts from non-residents above EUR 12,500 per calendar month (AWV Sec. 68–70, form Z1/Z3) within seven days after the month end; no conversion ratio, timing mandate, or repatriation percentage exists.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), Frankfurt am Main; supervisory coordination with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) for KWG/GwG banking conduct and AML enforcement; external sector statistics enforced by Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: inbound family or private-bank funds transfer to the UG's German credit institution via SWIFT MT103 in EUR; where the originator holds foreign currency, the sending bank executes conversion and the purpose code must state 'Gesellschaftereinlage (capital contribution)' or 'Darlehen an die UG (shareholder loan)'. Supporting verification: notarized capital increase resolution, contribution confirmation by German notary for cash contributions, updated Handelsregister excerpt, and originator's bank statement proving lawful source of funds. All payments from non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500 aggregate per calendar month must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV within seven days after month-end (statistical code for direct-investment equity inflows); the bank will additionally screen against sanctions and require confirmation that the funds do not represent return of criminal proceeds. The UG should classify the injection as pure equity or documented shareholder loan to avoid bank-side recharacterization as a deposit-taking transaction under CRR/KWG.Clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV for cross-border investment disbursements, PE capital calls, and family-office payments; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for EU beneficiary disbursements; (3) commercial and standby Letters of Credit under UCP 600 / ISP98 for acquisition-linked trade settlements, issued by the house bank; (4) securities settlement through TARGET2-Securities (T2S) via the bank's custody arm; FX conversion executed on SWIFT CONV or settled through CLS where ISDA master agreements apply. Outbound payments must bear a precise Verwendungszweck mapping to the underlying SFO transaction, must pass OFAC/EU sanctions screening, and must be aggregated for AWV statistical reporting when cumulative monthly flows exceed EUR 12,500. Supporting file: fund subscription forms, investment management agreement, invoices or closing statements, and custodian confirmation of securities booking.Plain-vanilla FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) with German or EU counterparty banks under an FX/ISDA master agreement; own-account hedging by the UG requires no BaFin license and is a permitted non-banking treasury activity.FX swaps (FX swap/rollover) for short-term EUR/USD, EUR/CHF, and EUR/GBP working-capital mismatches, settled via CLS or bilateral SWIFT confirmation.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS/CCIRS) under ISDA 2002 with CSA/VM collateralization, permitted for long-duration hedging of foreign-currency-denominated family portfolio assets.Vanilla purchased European-style currency options (zero-premium structures excluded or treated with caution) to cap conversion cost while preserving participation in favourable moves.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) technically permissible under the free-convertibility regime but generally unavailable in the German onshore market for liquid EUR crosses; where an offshore EMTN or illiquid emerging-market currency exposure arises, NDFs executed with an EU-authorized bank are acceptable as a documented hedging overlay, subject to AWV statistical reporting and board-approved treasury policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a supplementary rail only; Low as a primary treasury anchor. BaFin-licensed or PSD2-passported EU electronic money institutions (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Bunq, Qonto, Airwallex) may provision IBANs, SWIFT MT103 execution, and FX conversion without a full banking license. However, a UG structured as a Single Family Office engaged in investment holding, custody, and high-value private banking wires triggers expectations of credit-institution services (securities custody, investment intermediation, lending); such services exceed the permitted scope of an EMI under ZAG and are not within a pure payment institution's passport. The central operating account and securities custody must remain at a CRR credit institution; EMIs may be used solely for disbursement convenience, with strict transaction-purpose labeling to prevent de-risking closures.
