Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an OÜ; where the proposed share capital is below EUR 2,500, the full subscribed capital must be paid into a bank account opened in the company's name before registration (bank certificate appended); where capital is EUR 2,500 or more, at least one quarter (but not less than EUR 2,500) must be paid before registration, with the balance due within ten years. No escrow account requirement exists under Estonian law; payment is evidenced by a bank confirmation certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: warehouse and office premises must possess a valid building usage permit (kasutusluba) and, where statutory thresholds apply, a fire safety declaration filed with the Rescue Board (Päästeamet); structural safety and occupancy load verified per the Building Code. Occupational: the employer must perform a documented risk assessment, implement workplace health and safety measures under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus), arrange health surveillance where required, and maintain verified first-aid and evacuation equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an OÜ; where the proposed share capital is below EUR 2,500, the full subscribed capital must be paid into a bank account opened in the company's name before registration (bank certificate appended); where capital is EUR 2,500 or more, at least one quarter (but not less than EUR 2,500) must be paid before registration, with the balance due within ten years. No escrow account requirement exists under Estonian law; payment is evidenced by a bank confirmation certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian OÜ is a fully tax-resident juridical person under the Income Tax Act; profits are exempt from corporate income tax while retained/undistributed; corporate income tax of 20% (20/80 of net) applies upon profit distribution, liquidation proceeds, fringe benefits, gifts, and deemed benefits; regular dividends may qualify for the reduced 14% (14/86) rate. Territorial scope: worldwide income of a resident OÜ is within the net of the distribution-based tax; Estonia-source income of non-residents is separately taxable at source. No check-the-box pass-through regime is available for OÜ).

Available election forms

Estonia does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is statutory. Available statutory elections: (a) reduced 14% dividend tax election for 'regular' distributions computed against the average taxed distributed profits of the preceding three tax years; (b) voluntary VAT registration below the EUR 40,000 annual threshold; (c) EU VAT One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for cross-border B2B services, B2C distance sales of goods, and non-established supplier schemes; (d) Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) election for goods imports of €150 or less; (e) VAT group registration election for closely connected Estonian taxable persons; (f) ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically, with no elective relief for hybrids.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for Estonian-established businesses (mandatory threshold); cross-border EU B2C distance sales and electronically supplied services become taxable in the destination member state once the EU-wide annual threshold of EUR 10,000 is exceeded, triggering OSS registration; non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold and must register before the first taxable supply in Estonia; IOSS registration required for eligible imports of goods up to €150 in consignments.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the seller or a third party on its behalf within 3 months from the supply date, with MTA-proof of export; (ii) intra-Community supplies to EU customers holding a valid VAT identification number, with the goods dispatched to another Member State and Intrastat reporting obligations observed; (iii) online B2C distance sales of cosmetics to consumers in other EU member states where the €10,000 OSS threshold is exceeded, declared under OSS at the destination country VAT rate (or Estonian VAT at 22% below threshold); (iv) services directly linked to export cargo operations and cross-border services under place-of-supply rules. Substantiation requires continuous documentary evidence (export declaration, transport paperwork, purchase/order documents, payment records) demonstrating goods left Estonia within the statutory period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX) with MRN number and customs office certification
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR / international courier consignment note proving third-country physical delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment and customer identity
  • Supplementary: commercial invoice, packing list, destination-country import customs declaration, logistics tracking history, VIES validation confirmation for intra-EU B2B sales, and OSS/IOSS records where applicable

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or government pre-clearance API authorization is currently in force for domestic B2B/B2C invoices in Estonia. Invoices must be issued by the 7th day following the supply. E-invoices (including XML/UBL and in the public sector) are mandatory for invoicing all Estonian public-sector entities; broader B2B mandatory e-invoicing has been publicly announced in line with EU ViDA and is expected to phase in from 2027-2028. All VAT returns (KMD, KMD INF, OSS/IOSS declarations) must be filed electronically via e-MTA using X-Road authenticated credentials; there is no paper filing alternative for VAT.

Estonia's MTA applies retrospective audit scrutiny to all zero-rated export claims; insufficient or inconsistent shipment/proof documentation invalidates the 0% rate and triggers full 22% VAT clawback with statutory late-payment interest of 0.06% per day (compounded) plus tax non-compliance fines up to €2,400 per violation (higher on repeated offences); deliberate fraudulent zero-rating (e.g., fabricated customs declarations or bogus courier proof) constitutes criminal tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code (Karistusseadustik § 385–386), punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and extension of the assessment period to up to 6 years. Estonia imposes no hard statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export turnover; the 180-day benchmark reflects prudent internal compliance practice for preserving certified remittance evidence to defend export zero-rating and transfer-pricing positions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a member of the euro area; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Estonian law imposes no exchange controls, no administrative approval for cross-border capital transfers, and no restriction on profit remittance, dividends, or loan repayments to non-resident shareholders, subject to standard EU anti-money laundering (AML), sanctions, and tax reporting obligations. Retained profits of a resident OÜ are tax-deferred until distribution; upon distribution, the standard 20/80 dividend tax is levied at the payor level, but this does not constitute a convertibility or remittance restriction. For non-EU/EEA counterparities, enhanced due diligence may apply, but legal convertibility is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in Estonia. Exporters are not obliged to repatriate or convert foreign currency receipts within any timeline; they may hold export proceeds in foreign currency accounts (e.g., USD, GBP) at any licensed credit institution or payment institution, provided that transactions comply with EU sanctions regulations and the Foreign Exchange Law framework (as implemented under the European Union treaties). For intra-EU trade settlements in euro, proceeds arrive via SEPA and are immediately available without any conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; foreign exchange administration delegated to the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for enforcement of the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (Rahapesu ja terrorismi rahastamise tõkestamise seadus).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe, Revolut Ltd (Irish EMI) and Payoneer are authorized by EU/EEA regulators and can operate in Estonia under the EU passporting regime. They offer euro IBANs and cross-border payment rails, but are not credit institutions and do not provide licensed lending or investment services. For the low-risk cosmetics e-commerce SCOPE_ID, an EMI account is viable for day-to-day treasury and supplier payments, but for holding regulatory capital, tax authority interactions, and robust banking relationships, a tier-1 local commercial bank account is strongly recommended. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the shareholder (registered share capital or shareholder loan) must be routed via SWIFT MT103/SEPA credit transfer to the OÜ’s Estonian IBAN. For initial registered capital, the payment must reference the shareholder’s Bank Transaction Code 'SHAR' and if required by the bank, the e-Business Registry payment reference (e.g., 'Issue of share capital'). No specific 'purpose code' exists in Estonia because the euro is the domestic currency; however, for SWIFT transfers from outside the EU, use Standard Settlement Instructions (SSIs) from the receiving bank. Verification: a bank confirmation or SWIFT MT103 copy noting the exact IBAN of the OÜ’s capital escrow account and the UBO’s name; prior notification to the bank with the share capital increase form (koosseisu nimekiri) and the RIKS decision. Dividend payments to foreign shareholders are processed via the same MT103 with the purpose code 'DIV' (when required by intermediate banks), but Estonia does not require an explicit FX permit. For large capital injections (above €100k), the bank may request additional source-of-wealth documentation but cannot obstruct the legal payment.Primary clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SEPA Instant Credit for EU supplier payments below €100k in EUR – delivers near-rail credit; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EU suppliers (e.g., South Korea, US, UAE) – message must include the beneficiary’s IBAN/account number, SWIFT/BIC, and invoice reference; (3) Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) via the issuing bank (e.g., LHV or Swedbank) for high-value inventory purchases; L/Cs are confirmed by the beneficiary bank, with terms typically 30-90 days after sight. The OÜ may use debit-credit card rails (Shopify Payments, PayPal) for DTC sales, but outbound supplier payments should be made via direct bank transfer to maintain full auditability and avoid cross-border interchange fees. For foreign currency payments (USD, GBP, CNY), the Estonian bank will execute an internal FX conversion at the wholesale interbank rate plus a margin; no separate FX license is needed. Estonia participates in TARGET2 and T2S for large-value payments, allowing same-day settlement of EUR transfers to any EU country.Plain Vanilla FX Forwards (deliverable in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY) – offered by Estonian Tier-1 banks with a minimum notion of typically €25,000 and a physical delivery requirement; recorded under EMIR (EU) 648/2012 and subject to mandatory reporting to a Trade Repository.Foreign Exchange Swaps (FX Swaps) – simultaneous spot and forward transaction, permitted for currency risk management for trade payables/receivables; requires a signed ISDA or ETF master agreement with the bank.Currency Options (EU-style or American-style) – available on request; require premium payment and are subject to bank’s credit limit approval.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permissible only for currencies with restricted convertibility (e.g., RUB, CNY if non-repatriable); however, since the EUR is the functional currency and Estonian law imposes no capital controls, the use of NDFs is not necessary for standard cross-border trade; to document hedging rationale, the OÜ may enter into an NDF for exotic currency exposure under an ISDA agreement, provided the bank has a dedicated middle-office function.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe, Revolut Ltd (Irish EMI) and Payoneer are authorized by EU/EEA regulators and can operate in Estonia under the EU passporting regime. They offer euro IBANs and cross-border payment rails, but are not credit institutions and do not provide licensed lending or investment services. For the low-risk cosmetics e-commerce SCOPE_ID, an EMI account is viable for day-to-day treasury and supplier payments, but for holding regulatory capital, tax authority interactions, and robust banking relationships, a tier-1 local commercial bank account is strongly recommended. Legal constraints: EMIs must comply with Estonian AML regulations and the e-Residency digital identity verification rules; they generally accept remote onboarding but may reject high-risk jurisdictions or require additional documentation.
