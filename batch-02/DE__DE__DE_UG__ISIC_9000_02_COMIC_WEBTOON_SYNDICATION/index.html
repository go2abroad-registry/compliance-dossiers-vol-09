Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Unternehmergesellschaft requires minimum registered share capital of EUR 1; in standard practice a nominal capital of EUR 1,000 or more is subscribed and every subscribed cash contribution must be at the free disposal of the managing director before registration. No statutory escrow account is mandatory, but cash contributions are held in a dedicated German bank account opened for the pre-formation company. A statutory reserve of 25% of annual net profit must be retained until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for national VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.), OSS registrations and foreign entrepreneur VAT refunds; local Finanzamt (tax office) for the Steuernummer (tax number), corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) and VAT (Umsatzsteuer) assessments. Electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de); BZSt portal: www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Within one month of commercial registration (notarial deed and entry in the Handelsregister), the UG must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER to the locally competent Finanzamt. The questionnaire registers the UG for VAT, corporate income tax and trade tax; the Steuernummer is assigned within approximately 2–6 weeks. Thereafter, the UG applies separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) under §27a UStG, which is mandatory for intra-Community supplies and OSS use; processing takes 3–6 weeks. Non-resident shareholders or managing directors must appoint a domestic authorized representative for service of process; the UG must maintain a registered seat and address in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election; a UG is mandatorily a corporation taxed at entity level (15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge and trade tax). Statutory elections available: (1) Small-entrepreneur VAT exemption under §19 UStG where prior-year turnover ≤ €22,000 (until 2023) / €25,000 (from 2024) and current-year turnover ≤ €50,000 / €100,000 (from 2025), with forfeiture of input VAT recovery; (2) VAT grouping (Organschaft) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG requiring financial, economic and organizational integration into a German controlling company; (3) Corporate income tax and trade tax grouping (ertragsteuerliche Organschaft) under §§14 ff. KStG with profit and loss pooling agreement. (4) Irrevocable OSS registration election under §18j UStG for EU B2C digital services once the €10,000 annual threshold is exceeded; (5) Hybrid mismatch adjustment elections/disclosures under §4k EStG for reverse-hybrid and structured arrangements. All elections require explicit filing with the Finanzamt and cannot be applied retroactively.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for VAT registration: any entrepreneurial activity triggers a Steuernummer and VAT compliance obligations. Reduced exposure: §19 UStG small-business exemption (prior-year turnover ≤ €22,000/€25,000 and current-year ≤ €50,000/€100,000 threshold as phased by the Wachstumschancengesetz). For cross-border B2C digital supplies within the EU (webtoon chapter sales), the German place of supply continues to apply until annual EU-wide distance sales of digital services exceed €10,000 (exclusive of VAT); above this threshold, mandatory OSS registration in Germany applies for all EU B2C digital services, with VAT payable at destination-country rates (standard 15–27%).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is granted for B2C digital services (webtoon chapter unlocking) supplied to customers resident outside the EU, under §4 Nr. 1 Buchst. b UStG in conjunction with §3a Abs. 2 UStG (place of supply at the customer's habitual residence) and §6a UStG for intra-EU supplies. B2B digital supplies to non-German entrepreneurs are shifted to the recipient under reverse charge. Substantiation requires two independent, non-contradictory items of customer-location evidence under EU Implementing Regulation 282/2011 Art. 24f (IP address geolocation, bank country, billing address, mobile country code, fixed-line location, SIM card). Where Apple IAP or Google Play Billing is the sole payment rail, the platform is generally the deemed supplier for VAT (app store model); the UG's supply to Apple/Google qualifies as zero-rated B2B export if the platform VAT ID or establishment is outside Germany and the UG holds the relevant contracts and evidence. For direct subscription sales outside app stores, the UG must issue invoices, collect destination-country VAT via OSS (EU) or zero-rate non-EU supplies, and retain the complete location-evidence file for 10 years.

  • Substitute evidence package: auditable customer-location evidence (two non-contradictory criteria per EU Reg. 282/2011 Art. 24f).
  • Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console monthly sales, payout and commission statements.

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API fiscalization or digital stamping regime operates in Germany. Mandatory structured electronic invoicing applies to domestic B2B supplies in phasing: from 1 January 2025 all German businesses must be able to receive XRechnung (EN 16931) or ZUGFeRD 2.x invoices; from 1 January 2027 issuance of e-invoices is mandatory for taxpayers with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000; from 1 January 2028 all taxpayers must issue e-invoices. Invoices are transmitted peer-to-peer (no central database), with VAT returns filed electronically via ELSTER (monthly/quarterly advance returns and annual return). B2C supplies via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing: the platform operators (Apple/Google) act as deemed suppliers of the underlying digital content and handle consumer VAT; the UG's invoicing to Apple/Google is B2B and, where cross-border, subject to reverse charge, with e-invoice obligations applying from 2027.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims or fabricated customer-location evidence constitute tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and fines, with a 10-year limitation period for intentional evasion; retrospective VAT assessments at 19% plus late-payment interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO), denial of input VAT recovery, and penalty surcharges under §§26, 26a UStG apply. The Finanzamt may impose security deposits and provisional VAT assessments (§§18e, 18f UStG) where location evidence is inadequate. If platform proceeds are not repatriated within contractually agreed periods (standard 180 days), transfer-pricing adjustments under §1 AStG and potential disallowance of business expenses apply, including 6% p.a. interest on adjustments; foreign currency balances remain fully taxable. Cross-border audit coordination among EU tax authorities via DAC 1, DAC 7 and the OSS single window permits automated exchange of platform sales data and VAT numbers; clawbacks follow automatically for mismatches between platform-reported transactions and the UG's zero-rated export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is an EU member and fully participates in the EU single market. There is no exchange-control licensing for current-account transactions and no general restriction on capital-account operations; Art. 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital and payments. Inward equity for a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) and onward profit remittance are freely convertible; the only restrictions are (i) EU/UN/CFSP sanctions and (ii) transaction-reporting under AWV. Remittance hurdles are therefore tax/commercial rather than FX: the UG must first satisfy the statutory profit-retention rule requiring 25% of annual net profit to be reserved until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000 under §5a(3) GmbHG; dividend distributions are subject to German withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (total 26.375%), reduced or eliminated under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable DTT if the recipient is the beneficial owner and holds a qualifying shareholding; onward transfer into a foreign operating bank account is unrestricted and no central bank approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio in Germany. Export/service receipts, including app-store proceeds from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, may be retained in a freely convertible foreign currency or credited to a foreign correspondent account. The only statutory timeline is the Bundesbank reporting obligation under §67 AWV: any cross-border payment in excess of EUR 12,500 must be reported within five business days after the payment is received or credited to an account. The report must identify the customer, counterparty, amount, currency, payment purpose and, where relevant, the underlying trade classification; no conversion is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank), with BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) as the overarching financial, AML/CTF and payments supervision body

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for offshore digital EMIs. An offshore non-EU EMI has no legal basis to provide a German IBAN or hold settlement funds for a German UG; it would violate the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) if used for money-transfer-like activity. A fully EU-licensed EMI with a passported licence may offer an account, but most fintech platforms lack tier-1 treasury infrastructure for FX hedging, commercial LC, corporate loans and statutory reserve reporting. Therefore, for a webtoon syndication UG with recurring Apple/Google settlement flows, only EU-authorised EMIs are feasible, and only as a supplementary disbursement channel, not as the primary corporate bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an equity injection into the UG, use a SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008/pacs.009) remittance with the app-side sender exactly matching the registered shareholder name, payment purpose field 'EQUI' (equity contribution), and a clear reference to the notarial share-transfer or subscription agreement. If the contribution originates from an EU/EEA member state, a SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR is equally valid, provided the remittance information identifies 'Einlage / equity contribution' and the serial number of the notarised document. Any inbound cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 must be captured in the Deutsche Bundesbank AWV reporting system within five business days, and the bank should receive an updated AWV report code for 'Investitionen'. For each injection the bank will also require proof that the paying account belongs to the same ultimate beneficial owner or a direct creditor; a loan-capital book-in-route is treated as debt and carries a different SWIFT purpose code (e.g., 'LIQU' or 'LOAN') and, if applicable, a low-capital commitment ruling for trade tax purposes.For recurring EUR disbursements to Apple, Google, content licensors, artists and revenue-share partners inside the EEA, use SEPA Credit Transfer (pacs.008) with full remittance reference including the invoice, statement of intercompany split and the standard 'ROYA' (royalty) or 'TRAD' (trade settlement) purpose code. For non-EEA counterparties in USD, GBP, CHF or JPY, use SWIFT MT103 with structured remittance information identifying the underlying licence or syndication contract; if the transaction requires documentary security, an irrevocable SWIFT MT700 Commercial Letter of Credit can be arranged, but it is rarely used for digital webtoon transactions and only makes sense when physical merchandising rights or significant advance payments are syndicated. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Medium for offshore digital EMIs. An offshore non-EU EMI has no legal basis to provide a German IBAN or hold settlement funds for a German UG; it would violate the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) if used for money-transfer-like activity. A fully EU-licensed EMI with a passported licence may offer an account, but most fintech platforms lack tier-1 treasury infrastructure for FX hedging, commercial LC, corporate loans and statutory reserve reporting. Therefore, for a webtoon syndication UG with recurring Apple/Google settlement flows, only EU-authorised EMIs are feasible, and only as a supplementary disbursement channel, not as the primary corporate bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge (5.5% of KSt) plus Gewerbesteuer at municipal rate, typically 14%–17%; aggregate range approximately 29.8%–32.8%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, aggregate 26.375%; EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may reduce to 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders (≥10% shareholding for ≥12 months); Germany’s tax treaties generally provide 5%–15% rates depending on shareholding and treaty wording.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return due by 31 July of the year following the tax year; automatic extension to 28 February of the second following year where a tax adviser prepares the return. Prepayments due quarterly on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File when annual cross-border controlled goods transactions exceed €5,000,000 or services exceed €500,000 under GAufzV § 4; Master File when consolidated group revenue exceeds €100,000,000; Country-by-Country Report at consolidated group revenue above €750,000,000. German tax authorities may still demand documentation under § 90 AO for smaller transactions if profit is materially affected.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply a UAE-style ESR code; economic substance is enforced through § 42 AO (abuse of law), § 50d EStG (beneficial ownership, anti-treaty-shopping), CFC legislation, and established case law. A UG must demonstrate real management, premises, personnel, bank accounts and substantive business functions; a letterbox company will be denied treaty benefits and its transactions may be recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Digital Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO: imprisonment up to five years; aggravated cases up to ten years; fines under § 376 AO; interest on evaded tax at 0.5% per month; reassessment and confiscation of hidden proceeds.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO for taxes not paid due to intentional or grossly negligent breach of duty; conviction may result in asset seizure, travel ban, disqualification from managing companies and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Income estimation and upward correction under § 162 AO; 5% documentation surcharge on the transfer-pricing adjustment, minimum €5,000; additional late-documentation surcharges; penal interest under § 233a AO; possible criminal prosecution as tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors must ensure contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is prepared in Germany; failure to do so creates personal Haftung under § 69 AO for resulting tax and penalty liabilities, and knowingly false documentation exposes directors to criminal liability.
Withholding Tax Default on Profit Repatriation and Royalties

Statutory sanction: The German payer is liable for unwithheld tax under §§ 50a, 73 AO; late-payment interest accrues; administrative fines under § 378 AO apply for negligent under-deduction; intentional conduct triggers § 370 AO criminal prosecution and potential imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instruct or execute payments without statutory withholding become personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax; travel bans and arrest orders may be issued in serious tax enforcement cases.
