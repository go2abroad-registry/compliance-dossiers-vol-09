Governing statute

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The subscribed capital must be paid in full in cash before registration; contributions in kind are not permitted at formation. The capital must be freely available to the managing director after registration. At least 25% of annual surplus, reduced by any annual loss carried forward, must be allocated to a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000. There is no single fixed state fee in USD; court and notarial fees are generally calculated under the German Court and Notarial Costs Act based on the transaction value.

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial clearance is normally required for an office-based software business. The leased premises must satisfy applicable building-use, fire-safety and occupational-health requirements. The landlord or competent building authority should confirm permitted office use, escape routes, fire protection and structural load limits. If employees are engaged, comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Act, workplace ordinance, accident-insurance rules and required risk assessments. Server rooms, batteries, generators or customer-facing premises may require additional fire, electrical, structural or building approvals.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local German Finanzamt (tax office) with jurisdiction over the UG's registered seat; federal e-filing portal ELSTER (www.elster.de) for all statutory filings; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, www.bzst.de) for the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), OSS registration and EU-wide tax data exchange; Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF) issues binding decrees on VAT, e-invoicing and export proof requirements.

E-government filing portal

Immediately after commercial registration, the UG submits the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire, Form 14/15) via ELSTER or in hard copy to the competent Finanzamt, which assigns the 11-digit Steuernummer covering corporate income tax, trade tax and payroll tax. The UG separately applies to BZSt on Form USt 1 TU for its USt-IdNr. (VAT ID). Non-resident shareholders should appoint a German fiscal representative for VAT disputes if no permanent establishment exists. A certificate of tax residence (Ansässigkeitsbescheinigung) can be issued on request for foreign authorities, and the UG must quote its VAT ID, tax number and EORI number on all invoices and customs documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election. A UG may qualify for transparent partnership taxation under the restrictive Section 1a KStG option if all shareholders are natural persons. Hybrid mismatch countermeasures are applied under Section 4j EStG and Sections 10 ff. AStG (ATAD implementation). Corporate group consolidation (Organschaft) is available via a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. Foreign entity classifications, such as US Forms 8832, do not modify German default taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small-business exemption (Sec. 19 UStG): EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and maximum EUR 50,000 forecast current-year turnover. Intra-EU OSS threshold for distance sales and digital services: EUR 10,000 aggregate across all EU member states per calendar year. A German-resident UG is generally required to register for VAT from the first day of taxable operations; B2B reverse-charge supplies do not trigger German registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies under Sec. 6 UStG to goods physically exported to third countries, evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport proof. Intra-Community zero rating under Sec. 6a UStG requires a valid EU customer VAT ID, physical dispatch to another member state, and an entry in the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung). B2B offshore software and hosting services are outside German VAT by reverse charge upon receipt of the customer's valid VAT ID. B2C digital subscriptions are never export zero-rated; they are taxed in the consumer's EU country via OSS. The UG must retain complete export evidence for 10 years; an Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation substantiating foreign payment should be obtained within 180 days of export.

  • Commercial invoice in German or English bearing the UG's VAT ID, Steuernummer and mandatory e-invoice fields
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) or intra-Community delivery confirmation (Gelangensbestätigung)

Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies commenced 1 January 2025: taxpayers must receive e-invoices in XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x formats, with phased transmission duties for issuers by enterprise size until 2028. Transmission occurs via PEPPOL/EDI networks or portals; no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping applies to ordinary online sales. Physical B2C point-of-sale cash registers require a certified TSE (Technische Sicherheitseinrichtung) and a receipt issuance obligation (Belegausgabepflicht). The EU ViDA reform is expected to introduce harmonised real-time transaction reporting by 2030.

Tax auditorates routinely subject export zero-rating to focused audits; missing or false export proof triggers reassessment at 19% VAT plus default interest at 6% per annum under Sec. 233a AO, with assessment periods extended to 5 years for negligence and up to 10 years for intentional misdeclaration. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating invokes criminal prosecution under Sec. 370 AO, carrying imprisonment up to 5 years (aggravated cases up to 10 years), fines, forfeiture of economic benefits, and retrospective clawback of input VAT previously deducted. Managing directors bear personal liability under Sec. 34/69 AO for unpaid VAT and penalty surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a Eurozone member with fully liberalised current and capital account convertibility. There is no exchange-control licensing, no mandatory government approval for foreign-currency borrowing, lending, dividend remittance, or repatriation of capital, and no differentiation between resident and non-resident individuals for UG ownership. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) may freely hold multi-currency accounts, invoice in any currency, and distribute profits to non-resident shareholders, subject only to (i) statutory accounting and creditors' protection rules, (ii) 26.375% German withholding tax on dividends, including solidarity surcharge and, where applicable, church tax, reduced or exempted under an applicable double tax treaty, and (iii) statistical reporting duties to the Deutsche Bundesbank. Profit remittance is therefore procedurally unrestricted, but requires a formal shareholder distribution resolution and must be validated against the UG's distributable retained earnings under GmbH Act rules applicable by analogy to UGs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, repatriation, conversion, or blocking period for export proceeds in Germany. The UG may retain export earnings in EUR or any foreign currency, with any domestic or foreign bank, for an unlimited period. The AWV imposes only statistical reporting obligations: cross-border receipts and payments for services and capital flows exceeding EUR 12,500 (or the equivalent in foreign currency) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the electronic reporting portal within the prescribed reporting window (commonly the fifth working day of the month following settlement), and external assets of EUR 500,000 or more per foreign country must be reported on an annual stock basis under the external-positions statistics (Bestandserhebung). These are information requirements; none restricts the freedom to convert or transfer funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main), acting as the sole administrator of German external-sector statistics and payment-balance reporting; macro-prudential oversight and banking supervision are shared with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin); foreign exchange and capital-movement policy is governed by the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), subject to European Union Treaty obligations on free movement of capital (Art. 63 TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability as a secondary treasury rail, but not as the sole primary banking venue. Fintech and electronic-money institutions such as Pliant (Germany, licensed via Solaris SE), Qonto (France, passporting into Germany), and Revolut Business (Lithuanian EMI passport) offer instant multi-currency IBANs, low-cost USD/GBP corridors, and API connectivity that materially accelerate Stripe/Paddle payout reconciliation for a no-code website builder. Legal constraint: these institutions are payment institutions or e-money institutions under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG), not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz, KWG). Client funds are safeguarded, not fully covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme, and the EMI is not permitted to take deposits for lending. The mandatory treasury design is therefore a hub-and-spoke model: the UG's ultimate liquidity and salary/corporate-tax payments must settle through a Tier-1 German bank account, while EMI accounts are used for currency conversion, acquisition of sub-suppliers, and as a receiving facility for non-EUR payout streams, with automatic nightly sweeps to the German master account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be routed as a clean SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer from the non-resident shareholder's account to the UG's German EUR IBAN, with the beneficiary reference entering the AWV purpose code for capital contribution (e.g., 'Einlage in das Unternehmensvermögen' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen'). The UG must ensure that the receiving bank has a valid AWV statistical reporting mandate: any receipt from non-EEA residence that exceeds EUR 12,500, or its foreign-currency equivalent, has to be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the bank's electronic external-transaction reporting channel (Zahlungsmeldung) within the applicable statutory deadline, normally the fifth working day of the month following settlement. For a shareholder loan, the parties should execute a written loan agreement with arm's-length interest, and the UG must report the loan's principal receipt and subsequent interest payments under the appropriate balance-of-payments service codes. The routing must avoid mixing capital contributions with commercial revenue; a separate capital reserve account or a clear SWIFT reference 'CKT/053' is recommended for clean audit trails, and any capital increase exceeding EUR 25,000 requires a notarial alteration of the articles of association before bank acceptance.Outbound disbursements for the no-code website-builder operation should observe a three-rail clearance policy: (i) for EEA suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer is the default rail, using ISO 20022 purpose codes such as 'SUPP' for supplier payments and 'ROYA' for royalty/licensing fees; (ii) for non-EEA suppliers and enterprise clients requiring documentary certainty, SWIFT MT103 is executed through the Tier-1 bank with the correct beneficiary name, IBAN or account number, and SWIFT BIC, and the message must carry the proper purpose code (e.g., 'SERV' for services, 'LIC' for licensing) to support German balance-of-payments statistics and to avoid intermediary bank return fees; and (iii) for high-value or contractually required transactions, including buy-side of sub-licences or escrowed performance guarantees demanded by large corporate clients, the UG may use irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 or standby letters of credit issued by its German house bank. Since the primary commercially accepted rails are Stripe Billing and Paddle, the treasury blueprint must require a daily automated reconciliation between the acquirer payout reports, the multi-currency EMI balances, and the German operating account, and must enforce the rule that foreign-currency balances are converted to EUR within a target haircut of 0.2% to avoid residual currency drift; where the UG sells to end-customers in USD, the Stripe or Paddle payout can be routed in USD to a USD-denominated account at the Tier-1 bank (or an EMI) and subsequently sold spot to EUR. For payroll and tax payments, the UG must always settle from the German entity in EUR via SEPA to the tax office and statutory payroll providers, never in foreign currency.Deliverable FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, executed under an ISDA or DRV master agreement with the German relationship bank; suitable for locking future Stripe/Paddle USD payout conversion rates up to 12 months forward.FX swaps (Währungsswaps) used for intra-month liquidity smoothing and rollovers of forward positions; permitted under German law without special foreign-exchange approval and subject only to EMIR reporting requirements where applicable.European-style vanilla currency options (EUR/USD puts and calls) providing asymmetric protection on significant non-EUR revenue items without eliminating upside; the UG must fund option premiums in EUR and must not enter naked positions.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for invoicing or settlement currencies that are non-convertible or illiquid in the onshore market, or for currencies where the bank cannot deliver legal tender (e.g., certain emerging-market currencies); the UG should request NDFs solely as a contingent overlay and must ensure the NDF refers to an ISDA-backed official fixing; for the primary EUR/USD exposure, NDFs are unnecessary and deliverable forwards are preferred.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CIRS) as a permitted instrument to hedge economic risk on a substantial foreign-currency shareholder loan or a future intercompany financing arrangement; the UG, being a small non-financial counterparty under EMIR, benefits from the clearing threshold exemption, but must still complete mandatory trade reporting through its counterparty bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability as a secondary treasury rail, but not as the sole primary banking venue. Fintech and electronic-money institutions such as Pliant (Germany, licensed via Solaris SE), Qonto (France, passporting into Germany), and Revolut Business (Lithuanian EMI passport) offer instant multi-currency IBANs, low-cost USD/GBP corridors, and API connectivity that materially accelerate Stripe/Paddle payout reconciliation for a no-code website builder. Legal constraint: these institutions are payment institutions or e-money institutions under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG), not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz, KWG). Client funds are safeguarded, not fully covered by the German statutory deposit-guarantee scheme, and the EMI is not permitted to take deposits for lending. The mandatory treasury design is therefore a hub-and-spoke model: the UG's ultimate liquidity and salary/corporate-tax payments must settle through a Tier-1 German bank account, while EMI accounts are used for currency conversion, acquisition of sub-suppliers, and as a receiving facility for non-EUR payout streams, with automatic nightly sweeps to the German master account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.000% Körperschaftsteuer + 0.825% Solidaritätszuschlag (5.5% of 15%) = 15.825% before municipal trade tax; typical combined effective burden: 29.825% (Berlin, Hebesatz 400%) to 32.825% (Munich, Hebesatz 490%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer + 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding amount); the WHT is final for non-resident shareholders; an additional German income tax assessment is not made if the shareholder is a non-resident individual (§ 32d EStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 — sum of ordinary cross-border controlled transactions in the fiscal year (disclosure threshold for Local File, 'Aufzeichnungspflicht' § 90(3) AO); extraordinary transactions (außergewöhnliche Geschäftsvorfälle, e.g. sale of the IP of the no-code platform) — documentation threshold EUR 100,000. Master File mandatory where the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Submission deadlines: 60 days after request (30 days for extraordinary transactions); automatic income presumption under § 162(3) AO if documentation incomplete.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate Economic Substance Regulation file like the UAE; substance is the decisive criterion in several doctrines: (1) § 42 AO abuse-of-form recharacterisation of a UG without real economic activity; (2) § 50d(3) EStG treaty-shopping denial if the non-resident shareholder lacks substance; (3) ATAD/hybrid rules and § 4j EStG denying deductions for payments to tax-haven companies without substance; (4) minimum taxation rules under which a UG with no payroll, no German office, and no decision-making in Germany can be disregarded as a pure letterbox company — its income is re-attributed to the ultimate owners with 15–30% interest plus penalties. A valid substance package for the no-code business: a rent-paid German registered office, at least 1–3 payroll employees in Germany, German-language boards/minutes, code repository and DevOps logs showing German leadership, German IBAN for Stripe/Paddle settlement, and robust TP documentation for any licence/royalty streams.
