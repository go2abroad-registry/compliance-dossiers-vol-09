Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) with jurisdiction over the UG's registered seat, responsible for assigning the Steuernummer (statutory tax number) and assessing corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Federal Central Tax Office, www.bzst.de) is the competent authority for issuing the EU-wide VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). All statutory filings, including the tax registration questionnaire, VAT advance returns, and annual tax returns, are submitted electronically via the 'Mein ELSTER' portal (www.elster.de) operated by the German federal states' fiscal administration.

E-government filing portal

1) Complete commercial registration at the local Handelsregister (Commercial Register) and file the Gewerbeanmeldung (trade registration) at the local trade office, obtaining the trade licence confirmation. 2) Within one month of commencing business, transmit the electronic tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt, disclosing the UG's shareholders, intended activity (ISIC 8299, NAICS 561431 - commercial mail receiving and digital scanning), opening balance sheet, and forecast turnover. 3) The Finanzamt issues the unified Steuernummer, covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT accounting, by written assessment notice (Erfassungsschreiben), normally within 2-8 weeks. 4) Apply separately to the BZSt for the EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) using the electronic application form; the USt-IdNr. is mandatory for cross-border B2B invoicing and VIES verification. 5) Open a German business bank account and complete ELSTER organisational registration, obtaining the authentication certificate for secure XML transmissions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a resident capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to unlimited German tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) under § 1(1) KStG. Profits bear 15% Körperschaftsteuer, plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate tax (0.825% effective), plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at a 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%-490%), yielding an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 23%-33%. The UG has no pass-through eligibility; shareholder distributions attract 25% German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus solidarity surcharge, with relief available under applicable double-tax treaties. — 1) Complete commercial registration at the local Handelsregister (Commercial Register) and file the Gewerbeanmeldung (trade registration) at the local trade office, obtaining the trade licence confirmation. 2) Within one month of commencing business, transmit the electronic tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt, disclosing the UG's shareholders, intended activity (ISIC 8299, NAICS 561431 - commercial mail receiving and digital scanning), opening balance sheet, and forecast turnover. 3) The Finanzamt issues the unified Steuernummer, covering corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT accounting, by written assessment notice (Erfassungsschreiben), normally within 2-8 weeks. 4) Apply separately to the BZSt for the EU VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) using the electronic application form; the USt-IdNr. is mandatory for cross-border B2B invoicing and VIES verification. 5) Open a German business bank account and complete ELSTER organisational registration, obtaining the authentication certificate for secure XML transmissions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a resident capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to unlimited German tax liability (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) under § 1(1) KStG. Profits bear 15% Körperschaftsteuer, plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the corporate tax (0.825% effective), plus municipal Gewerbesteuer at a 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%-490%), yielding an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 23%-33%. The UG has no pass-through eligibility; shareholder distributions attract 25% German withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus solidarity surcharge, with relief available under applicable double-tax treaties.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections for a UG: (1) corporate Organschaft (tax group) under § 14 KStG by concluding a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Gewinnabführungsvertrag) with a financially and organisationally integrated parent, consolidating income tax and trade tax within the group; (2) VAT Organschaft under § 2(2) No. 2 UStG, merging the UG's separate VAT registration into the controlling entrepreneur; (3) trade-tax consolidation aligned with the corporate Organschaft; (4) election of the small-business VAT exemption (§ 19 UStG) at €25,000 prior-year and €100,000 current-year turnover thresholds (2025), generally disadvantageous for a B2B service provider requiring input VAT recovery; (5) optional reverse-charge treatment for specified German domestic supplies under § 13b UStG; and (6) application of the statutory ATAD II hybrid-mismatch override under § 4k EStG, which permits no elective neutralization - hybrid outcomes are resolved in favour of the German tax result.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold triggers compulsory VAT registration; any entrepreneurial activity under § 2 UStG obliges the UG to register with the Finanzamt within one month and to file VAT returns. The only statutory relief is the Kleinunternehmer exemption under § 19 UStG, available where prior-year turnover does not exceed €25,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed €100,000 (2025 values); this relief is commercially unsuitable for a UG issuing B2B invoices and claiming input VAT refunds. A non-EU foreign entrepreneur is required to register for German VAT from the very first taxable supply in Germany, without any de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to exports delivered to third-country (non-EU) territories under § 6 UStG where the UG proves physical removal of the goods from Germany and completion of the customs export procedure through the ATLAS electronic customs declaration; intra-EU B2B dispatch is instead treated as an exempt intra-Community supply requiring the customer's VIES-valid VAT ID, with the supplier filing a recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung). Services: the UG's mail-scanning and virtual-office services are business-support services supplied to entrepreneurs, the place of supply of which is the customer's establishment under § 3a(2) UStG; where the recipient is an entrepreneur established outside Germany, the service falls outside the scope of German VAT, evidenced by invoicing with the customer's valid foreign VAT ID and the statutory indication of reverse charge or foreign place of supply. Services supplied to non-entrepreneurs (B2C) established in third countries remain subject to 19% German VAT under § 3a(1) UStG, with no zero-rating relief available for this service category.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ATLAS Ausfuhranmeldung) together with the electronic customs release confirmation (Ausfuhrnachweis/status message) proving the export procedure closed in the third-country destination
  • Transport and dispatch documentation (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or postal/courier dispatch certificate for forwarded mail) evidencing the physical departure from German territory
  • Inward payment evidence (bank transfer statement, SEPA/SWIFT credit advice, or accounts-receivable ledger extracts) substantiating the foreign-source remittance, serving as the commercial and documentary proof (handels- und belegmäßiger Nachweis)

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing ('E-Rechnung') under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025, every B2B supplier must be able to receive e-invoices in EN 16931-compliant structured formats (XRechnung XML or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X hybrid); from 1 January 2027, all suppliers with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue e-invoices; and from 1 January 2028, all B2B domestic suppliers must issue e-invoices. Germany does not operate a centralized real-time clearance authority or digital stamping regime comparable to Italy's SDI or Brazil's NF-e; fiscal control is effected through ELSTER system-to-system transmission of monthly VAT advance returns (Voranmeldungen) due electronically by the 10th day of the following month, with an optional permanent extension (Dauerfristverlängerung) conditioned on a special advance payment. Invoices not issued in the mandated structured electronic format do not entitle the recipient to input VAT deduction under § 14 UStG.

German export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) must be obtained and retained within the statutory period, documentary and commercial evidence generally being required within 60 days from the supply under § 10 UStDV; on audit, the Finanzamt verifies the ATLAS export messages, transport documents, and payment flows, and where zero-rating is denied because substantiation is incomplete or fraudulent, output VAT is retroactively assessed, input VAT previously deducted is clawed back, and monthly late-payment interest of 0.5% accrues under § 233a AO. Wilful or reckless misrepresentation constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or by fines (up to ten years in aggravated cases), and due-diligence violations attract summary fines of up to €5,000 under § 26a UStG; the tax authority may further impose enforcement penalties (Zwangsgeld) and reopen past assessment periods where refund claims have been obtained on the strength of false documentation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany as a euro-area member maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility pursuant to Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There is no administrative approval for inbound equity, service-export receipts, or outbound dividend repatriation; the applicable regime is notification and statistical supervision. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than exchange-control: a UG is subject to 15 percent corporate income tax plus 5.5 percent solidarity surcharge (15.825 percent combined) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective 7 to 17 percent depending on the municipal Hebesatz), and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders attract German withholding tax at 26.375 percent (25 percent dividend tax plus 5.5 percent solidarity surcharge), which may be reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No minimum holding period, conversion certificate, or prior authorization restricts the transfer of export earnings or the foreign-currency bank balances of the UG.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no fixed conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. Export earnings may be retained indefinitely in EUR or third-country currencies at domestic or foreign banks. The only binding obligation is statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank under Sections 67 ff. AWV: any cross-border payment or claim arising with non-residents that equals or exceeds EUR 12,500 per individual transaction must be captured in the receiving bank's external-sector report (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik) or be reported directly by the UG via the Bundesbank payment statistics portal. The UG must therefore provide precise SWIFT purpose codes and supporting invoices upon request; failure to report can attract administrative fines under Section 19 AWG.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (national central bank and external-sector statistics authority) with the European Central Bank for euro-area monetary policy and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) together with German Customs (Zoll) as enforcement authorities under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for receivable collection and disbursement execution, Low as the sole statutory and tax treasury venue. German and EEA-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) admitted under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may legally be used by a UG for SEPA and card settlement, and non-EEA licensed EMIs may offer cross-border multi-currency wallets; however, EMI accounts are not full bank accounts: e-money must be safeguarded in segregated accounts, deposits do not constitute bank deposits protected under deposit-guarantee schemes, and the UG's obligation to maintain a stable German-domiciled account for share-capital verification, trade-tax assessment, and Bundesbank reporting remains subject to stricter scrutiny. EMIs that merely provide mail-forwarding compatibility without a German establishment create an additional substance gap in a CMRA and ISIC 8299 model and are therefore not recommended as a substitute for a tier-1 German account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound equity from a non-resident shareholder uses a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for intercompany transfers) to the UG's verified German account in EUR, with the beneficiary field showing the UG legal name and IBAN, and a precise remittance reference, for example /REC/ Gesellschaftereinlage auf Stammkapital - Barkapital. The UG and the remitting bank must provide the statistical purpose code for direct investment (Direktinvestition: Kapitaleinlage von Anteilseignern) so that the receiving bank can fulfil its Deutsche Bundesbank payment-reporting duty if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Verification by the onboarding bank comprises: notarised shareholders' resolution or capital-increase deed, list of subscriber shares, confirmation of the new share capital from the commercial registry, proof of currency conversion to EUR if transferred in foreign currency, and a statement that funds originate from a licensed bank account and are not third-party money. If a shareholder loan is used, the contract, interest rate aligned with Section 4h EStG interest-barrier rules, and intercompany documentation must accompany the transfer.For Eurozone settlement, the UG should use SEPA Credit Transfers (pain.001.001.09) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfers (Echtzeitüberweisung) for urgency; for cross-border EUR and non-EUR payments, SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with gpi tracking are the standard rail. Physical trade settlement is generally not needed for a services business, but where the UG procures scanning hardware or software from abroad, a UCP 600-compliant documentary letter of credit with a confirming German bank or an SWIFT MT700 or MT760 (standby LC) can be used. All outbound payments to non-residents above EUR 12,500 must be identifiable for Bundesbank statistical reporting through the bank's automatic filings, and the UG must supply invoices, purpose codes and AWV-compliant payment instructions. For group cash sweeps, a cash-pooling arrangement in euros with same-day value is permissible, provided it is documented as a true cash-pool agreement and does not trigger transfer-pricing or constructive-distribution issues.Outright FX forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) transacted under the German Master Agreement for Financial Derivatives Transactions (Deutscher Rahmenvertrag fuer Finanztermingeschaefte, DRV) with a licensed credit institutionNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with restricted settlement or inconvertibility risk, settled in EUR cash difference under the bank's ISDA or NDF master termsCross-currency swaps (CCS) for converting long-term EUR liabilities into USD, GBP, or CHF exposure and aligning interest-rate streamsFX swaps (spot-forward combinations) for short-term intercompany liquidity bridging and working-capital managementPurchased plain-vanilla FX options (call or put, or zero-cost collar structures written for hedging purposes) consistent with the UG's documented foreign-exchange risk policy

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for receivable collection and disbursement execution, Low as the sole statutory and tax treasury venue. German and EEA-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) admitted under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) may legally be used by a UG for SEPA and card settlement, and non-EEA licensed EMIs may offer cross-border multi-currency wallets; however, EMI accounts are not full bank accounts: e-money must be safeguarded in segregated accounts, deposits do not constitute bank deposits protected under deposit-guarantee schemes, and the UG's obligation to maintain a stable German-domiciled account for share-capital verification, trade-tax assessment, and Bundesbank reporting remains subject to stricter scrutiny. EMIs that merely provide mail-forwarding compatibility without a German establishment create an additional substance gap in a CMRA and ISIC 8299 model and are therefore not recommended as a substitute for a tier-1 German account.
