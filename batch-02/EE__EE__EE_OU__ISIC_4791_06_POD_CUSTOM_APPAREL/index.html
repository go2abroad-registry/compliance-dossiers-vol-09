Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus — RIK), operating the Estonian Commercial Register (Eesti äriregister) under the Ministry of Justice; electronic filings submitted exclusively through the e-Business Register portal at ariregister.rik.ee.

Governing statute

Commercial Code of the Republic of Estonia (Äriseadustik), in force since 1 September 1995, principally §§135-145 (formation of Osaühing), §136(1) (minimum share capital, as amended by the 2024 capital reform to EUR 1.00), §142 (memorandum of association), §144 (registration application), §145 (management board), §149 (signature rights and shareholders' register), and §596 (registry fines); supplemented by the General Part of the Economic Activities Code Act (MSEÜS), the Accounting Act (Raamatupidamise seadus), and the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration in the Commercial Register (kandeavaldus) for a private limited company — Form 'OÜ asutamine' — with appended Memorandum of Association (asutamisleping) and Articles of Association (põhikiri), all digitally signed with qualified Estonian e-identity certificates.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 5 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Share capital of an OÜ must be at least EUR 2,500.00 under the standard regime; from 1 January 2024 the reduced-capital regime permits incorporation with a fully paid share capital of EUR 1.00, provided that the entity's net assets at formation are at least equal to the share capital and that shareholder distributions are prohibited until the accumulated net assets reach EUR 2,500.00. The subscribed capital must be fully paid into a bank account in the company's name before the registration application is submitted; there is no statutory escrow or notary-controlled capital deposit, but non-monetary contributions must be valued and disclosed in the memorandum.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); official national portal: www.emta.ee; e-services portal: e-MTA at e-mta.emta.ee.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities can obtain an Estonian tax identity through two routes. Route 1 (establishment route): incorporate an Osaühing (OÜ) in the Estonian Business Register (Äriregister) via the e-Residency digital portal or via a notary; the 8-digit registry code issued by the Commercial Register is the legal-entity TIN and is used for income tax, social tax, VAT, and statistical filings. Route 2 (direct non-established registration route): a foreign company not established in Estonia but required to charge Estonian VAT must file a non-resident VAT registration application with MTA via the e-MTA portal before making taxable supplies in Estonia, attaching proof of foreign corporate registration and VAT ID, constitutional documents, bank account confirmation, a local address for service, and, if required by MTA, an authorised representative or fiscal representative in Estonia. In both routes, MTA verifies the application and issues an Estonian VAT identification number in the format EE followed by 9 digits; a separate EORI number should be obtained from MTA for customs export declarations. Registration is mandatory when taxable turnover in Estonia reaches EUR 40,000 in a calendar year or, for non-established persons, immediately upon the first taxable supply not covered by reverse charge, Union OSS, or IOSS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not have a US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through regime. An OÜ is per se a separate legal person and an entity-level taxpayer under Estonian law; undistributed profits are not subject to corporate income tax, but taxes are imposed on distributions and certain deemed benefits. Available elections include: (i) VAT group treatment for closely-linked Estonian taxable persons, subject to MTA approval; (ii) Union OSS, Non-Union OSS, and IOSS elections for cross-border distance sales and electronic services to EU consumers; (iii) the reduced regular-dividend taxation rate of 14% for qualifying distributions under the Estonian Income Tax Act; and (iv) mandatory application of EU anti-hybrid and ATAD rules with no elective opt-out. There is no scope to classify an OÜ as a disregarded entity or branch for Estonian income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for persons established in Estonia. Non-established foreign taxable persons supplying taxable goods or services in Estonia are required to register from the first taxable supply unless the supply is covered by reverse charge, Union OSS, Non-Union OSS, or IOSS. For intra-EU cross-border distance sales of goods to non-taxable EU consumers, an EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies before the place of supply moves to the destination member state; Union OSS may be elected in Estonia to declare and pay that destination-state VAT. For low-value imports of goods to EU consumers not exceeding EUR 150, the Import One-Stop Shop may be used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical print-on-demand apparel qualifies for Estonian export zero-rating when title passes to a non-EU customer and the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union customs territory from Estonia by the supplier, the customer, or a carrier acting on either’s behalf, provided that complete and mutually consistent export evidence is obtained and retained within the statutory VAT return deadline. Services performed for a non-EU customer and directly related to the exported goods, such as printing or logistics services, may also be zero-rated under export-service rules. Electronically supplied digital design services supplied to non-EU resident customers are outside the Estonian VAT place-of-supply rules and are not subject to Estonian VAT; they are not treated as export zero-rating. Supplies to EU consumers are subject to the EU distance-selling and OSS/IOSS rules and do not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/EAD) accepted by MTA or the customs office of exit, with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and proof of export validation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or combined transport document) evidencing that the goods physically left the European Union to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/payment-provider statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds in the OÜ's bank account
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, POD production and dispatch records, courier labels, and fulfillment-provider reports evidencing the complete order and delivery chain

Estonia does not require real-time pre-clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping of standard VAT invoices. Invoices to the Estonian public sector must be submitted as structured e-invoices using the PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 format. B2B and B2C e-invoicing is permitted with recipient consent. For retail cash-register transactions, including face-to-face card payments, certified software must transmit sales data to MTA in real time through the eKassa fiscalisation service, using digitally signed transaction records. Online payments through Stripe or PayPal do not trigger eKassa cash-register transmission, but the OÜ must issue compliant VAT invoices and report sales through periodic VAT returns. All VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, OSS returns, Intrastat declarations, and customs export declarations are submitted electronically through e-MTA, supported by X-Road APIs and qualified electronic signatures.

Estonian Tax and Customs Board auditors apply risk-based scrutiny to export zero-rating claims, particularly in e-commerce and POD retail. Missing, contradictory, late, or fabricated export documentation will result in denial of the 0% rate, reassessment with output VAT at the standard 22% rate, late-payment interest at 0.06% per day, and administrative penalties on the incorrect VAT declaration. Deliberate use of forged or collusive export evidence, circular payments, or fictitious customs trails is treated as tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code, with consequences including fines, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud. MTA may retrospectively reopen affected VAT periods within the statutory limitation period, claw back related input VAT credits, and extend assessment to connected transactions in the same fraudulent chain. Repatriation of export sales proceeds must generally be evidenced within 180 days from shipment; failure to meet that deadline without adequate explanation will materially weaken the evidentiary position and expose the zero-rating to reversal.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has full capital and current account convertibility as a euro-area member state. The euro is the sole legal tender; there are no exchange control restrictions on cross-border payments, profit repatriation, dividends, or liquidation proceeds for resident or non-resident shareholders. Estonia applies EU Treaty freedoms, and the Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik) requires only standard corporate formalities for distributions (audited or reviewed financials, solvency test, and board resolution). No prior central bank approval, conversion quota, or repatriation tax clearance is required for profit remittance; the only hurdle is the corporate income tax on distributions at a rate of 20/80 of the net distribution (i.e., 20% on gross distributed profit), plus possible withholding on non-resident payments if royalties or interest are paid, though dividends to non-resident companies may be exempt under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Since Estonia is part of the euro area and maintains no foreign exchange control law, all export receipts in EUR or any foreign currency may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted freely. There are no statutory timelines for repatriation or conversion ratios; invoicing and settlement terms are contractual. For EU statistical reporting, cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 10,000 must be reported to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) via the balance of payments (BoP) statistical reporting system, but this is not a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), as the central bank of Estonia and member of the Eurosystem and European System of Central Banks; in matters of foreign exchange regulation, the Estonian Ministry of Finance and the Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) operate within the EU Capital Markets Union framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for licensed EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions operating in Estonia under the EU PSD2 regime (e.g., Wise Europe SA, N26, Revolut, Monese, Holvi, or similar EMI-licensed providers). Estonian OÜs may open EMI business accounts fully remotely without physical presence in Estonia. EMI accounts may be used for operational settlements and cross-border payments, but are generally not suitable as primary treasury concentration accounts for significant escrow, large trade finance, or LC operations. Legal constraint: EMI must hold an e-money or payment institution license under the EEA passport and must perform actual AML/KYC of the Estonian entity; non-EEA offshore digital EMIs are not viable for a licensed Estonian operating company due to inability to provide reliable IBAN settlement within SEPA and higher correspondent banking friction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to an Estonian OÜ should be structured as a loan, equity contribution, or share capital payment from the shareholder. The compliant wire routing is via SWIFT MT103 over TARGET2/SEPA or via SWIFT with a transparent purpose code: for equity contributions, use IASG (EU statistical purpose classification) or ECB BoP code '2800 Capital contributions' / '3100 Shares and other equity'; for shareholder loans, use code '2810 Loan with related company' or '4800 Change in liabilities between residents and non-residents'. The bank must see the shareholders' resolution, share subscription agreement, and loan agreement to substantiate the purpose code. The receiving Estonian bank will process the credit into a business operational account; amounts exceeding EUR 10,000 must be reported to Maksu- ja Tolliamet based on the BoP report. No central bank approval is required; however, if the injection exceeds a material threshold (typically EUR 50,000 or domestic bank discretion), the bank will request source of wealth, source of funds, and a tax opinion on financing structure. Dividends and profit distributions remain taxable at 20% on gross distribution (or 14% on regular distributions under certain Estonian rules), so injections should avoid mislabeling as revenue.Outbound trade disbursements for POD retail (payments to manufacturers, print shops, logistics providers, and refunds) should be executed by SWIFT MT103 (SEPA/ISO 20022 equivalent for EUR within EEA) directly from the Estonian OÜ's operational account. Commercial letters of credit are permissible but not typical for POD low-value retail trade; LC would be used only for bulk procurement of apparel inventory from non-EEA suppliers. The recommended settlement rails are: (1) Bank-to-bank SEPA credit transfer in EUR for European suppliers; (2) SWIFT MT103 with purpose codes 'Purchase of goods (trade)' or '2000 Payment for goods and services' for third-country suppliers; (3) Commercial LC (SWIFT MT700) with INCOTERMS (e.g., FOB or EXW) for high-value batch procurement, supported by bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, and inspection certificate; and (4) PSP settlement (Stripe/PayPal) to the OÜ's banking platform following standard payout cycles. The banking treasury blueprint should maintain segregated accounts: a primary operational account for receipts/disbursements and a disbursement sub-account to preserve clear audit trails for Estonian Tax and Customs Board VAT and corporate tax filings.
