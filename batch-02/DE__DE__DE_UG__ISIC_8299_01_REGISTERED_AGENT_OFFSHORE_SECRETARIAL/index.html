Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG has no statutory minimum capital; the minimum share capital is EUR 1.00 (at least EUR 1.00 per subscriber). The subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash and placed at the free disposal of the managing directors before registration; no legally mandated escrow account exists, but bank confirmation of free availability is required. Statutory retention rule: 25% of annual net profit must be allocated to a statutory reserve until the issued capital reaches EUR 25,000.00.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection by the local fire protection office (Brandschutzdienststelle / Berufsfeuerwehr) confirming escape routes, fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, and a signed fire-safety checklist; structural load and workplace clearance certificate from a certified engineer verifying floor loading, ventilation, lighting, and ergonomics per ArbStättV (Workplace Ordinance); occupational health clearance by a company physician (Betriebsarzt) with first-aid equipment under ArbSchG; if mail-processing staff exceed 250 person-hours per week, statutory workplace health monitoring and welfare documentation apply.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG has no statutory minimum capital; the minimum share capital is EUR 1.00 (at least EUR 1.00 per subscriber). The subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash and placed at the free disposal of the managing directors before registration; no legally mandated escrow account exists, but bank confirmation of free availability is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern - BZSt) for VAT identification and certain central procedures; local tax office (Finanzamt) for income, corporate, trade, and payroll tax registration; official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a German-resident UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), the legal representative submits the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) to the locally competent Finanzamt, through ELSTER or in writing, before commencing business. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (local tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. For the EU-wide VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer), the applicant applies separately to the BZSt using the relevant form or via ELSTER after the Finanzamt has assigned the local tax number. Foreign-owned and non-resident entities may appoint a German tax agent or fiscal representative and follow the same questionnaire procedure, providing the shareholding structure, registered office address, intended activities, and expected turnover. The VAT ID is normally issued within a matter of weeks once the local tax number exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The UG is a domestic tax-resident corporation when its statutory seat or place of management is in Germany; it is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), solidarity surcharge (Soli), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at entity level, with no pass-through or S-corporation analog. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The UG is a domestic tax-resident corporation when its statutory seat or place of management is in Germany; it is subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer, 15%), solidarity surcharge (Soli), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at entity level, with no pass-through or S-corporation analog. It is separately taxable from shareholders; distributed dividends are subject to German withholding tax unless treaty relief or ABAD benefits apply.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in German tax law. Available statutory elections include: (1) fiscal unity election ('Organschaft') for corporate income tax and VAT where a controlling company is financially, economically, and organisationally integrated with the UG, combined with a profit-transfer agreement for corporate tax purposes; (2) waiver of the small-business VAT exemption under §19 UStG; (3) election to form a VAT group for closely related persons under German law. Hybrid mismatch rules under §4k EStG apply mandatorily to German entities; there is no elective hybrid election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 (previous-year turnover) and EUR 100,000 (current-year forecast) — small-enterprise exemption threshold under §19 UStG; no general VAT registration threshold applies to German-established businesses, and non-resident remote sellers may be required to register from the first euro of taxable sales unless using the EU One-Stop-Shop or Import-One-Stop-Shop arrangements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply is zero-rated under §4 No. 1 and §6 UStG where the supplier is the exporter, the goods are moved by the supplier or by a purchasing third party out of the customs territory of the European Union, and the export is evidenced in accordance with §§8-9 UStG and the Export Verification Regulation (Exportnachweisverordnung). Services supplied to non-EU B2B customers are generally outside the scope of German VAT or zero-rated under reverse-charge place-of-supply rules if the recipient is an entrepreneur in a third country; offshore company secretarial and registered-agent services fall under B2B place-of-supply rules and may be subject to German VAT if the recipient is not a qualifying entrepreneur or where services are physically performed in Germany and connected with German real estate. The taxpayer must prove, through documentary evidence, that the goods left the European Union and that the customer was the actual exporter; the credibility, consistency, and commercial coherence of the documentation are decisive for acceptance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument / ATLAS export declaration with MRN - Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (CMR, airway bill, sea waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing departure from the EU)
  • Commercial invoice, export contract, and freight documentation confirming the parties, goods description, quantities, and movement terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment receipt evidencing the foreign customer's payment in accordance with the documentary chain (required by commercial and AML practices; not a standalone German VAT statutory condition when other export proof is complete)

Germany does not currently impose real-time fiscal clearance, government-authorised API stamping, or digital stamping. Since 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD, CII/UBL format); mandatory issuance is phased — from 1 January 2027 for enterprises with annual turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all other domestic B2B suppliers. E-invoices must be transmitted electronically via a mutually agreed channel; there is no central government clearance API at present. Paper invoices remain acceptable in specified exception cases (e.g., certain exempt supplies, small-value invoices below EUR 250, transport tickets, and foreign-issued invoices).

German tax authorities apply strict documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Missing or inconsistent evidence leads to reclassification of the supply as taxable domestic supply, triggering VAT reassessment, interest under §233a AO, and late-payment surcharges. Intentional or reckless false statements constitute VAT evasion under §370 AO and fraud under §263 StGB, with penalties including unlimited fines, imprisonment up to ten years for serious evasion, and retrospective clawback of zero-rating and related input VAT deductions. The burden of proof lies with the exporter; all export evidence must be retained for at least ten years and produced upon request during external audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible in Germany. Neither current account nor capital account transactions require exchange-control admission under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV). Profit remittance and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject to standard corporate-law limitations (UG must retain 25% of annual net profit until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000) and to 26.375% German withholding taxation (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge), which may be reduced under applicable double-tax treaties. Every cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must be statistically reported to Deutsche Bundesbank within five trading days via the Z-category (Z1–Z4) reporting framework; the client bank usually files the report, but the UG treasury must verify the statistical purpose code and preserve corroborating trade documents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation timeline, or minimum holding period for export proceeds or cross-border service receipts. EUR and foreign-currency receivables may be retained freely onshore or offshore. The only compliance obligation is statistical: receipts from non-EEA customers exceeding EUR 12,500 must be declared to Deutsche Bundesbank through the AZP electronic portal, normally submitted by the receiving bank on the corporate customer's behalf.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (monetary authority and administrator of AWV balance-of-payments statistics) in conjunction with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as prudential and anti-money-laundering supervisor for banks, payment institutions, and e-money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary treasury banking; Medium as an ancillary payments corridor. Only BaFin-licensed or EU-passported e-money institutions (for example Solarisbank/Penta, Qonto, Airwallex, Wise Business) may legally provide SEPA-clearing accounts. Post-Brexit UK, UAE, Hong Kong, and US EMIs without a German or EU passport cannot clear through TARGET2/SEPA and are unsuitable as primary rails. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Rail: SWIFT MT103/MT210 for non-EEA capital contributions and client fee inflow; SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA-originated fees. Equity injections must reference the shareholder resolution and the Commercial Register share-capital evidence; the bank may require earmarking as a share-capital contribution. All non-EEA amounts above EUR 12,500 must be AWV-reported with the relevant statistical purpose code: Z2/6200 for corporate-secretarial service fees and Z4 capital-category for equity inflows. The bank files the report; the UG treasury must retain the contract, invoice, service-delivery evidence, and Transparenzregister documentation for at least eight years.For EEA disbursements to corporate registries, notaries, and professional counterparties, use SEPA Credit Transfer. Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) are legally permissible but economically unsuitable for professional-service disbursements, and are reserved only for trade-finance-backed purchases of goods.
