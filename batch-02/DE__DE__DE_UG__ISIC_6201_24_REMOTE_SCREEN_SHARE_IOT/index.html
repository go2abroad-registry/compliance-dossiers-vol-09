Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID; Finanzamt (Local Tax Office) for tax number and corporate income tax; Bundesanzeiger for publication. Portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) and Mein ELSTER; BZSt VAT ID query portal.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register with the local Finanzamt via ELSTER or paper form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire). Required documents: certified copy of commercial register extract (Handelsregisterauszug), articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag), proof of managing director identity, and bank details. Upon registration, the Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer (tax number) for direct taxes and, upon separate application to BZSt, a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.) for cross-border VAT. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment may register for VAT via a fiscal representative if required. Processing time typically 2-6 weeks. UG (haftungsbeschränkt) must also register with IHK/HWK and Handelsregister before tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election available in Germany. UG is taxed as a corporation (Körperschaftsteuer) by default and is subject to trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) unless exempt. No hybrid mismatch elections. Option for transparent taxation (Transparenzoption) under §1a KStG for partnerships only, not applicable to UG. VAT group (Organschaft) possible under strict conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for foreign entities; domestic entities: EUR 22,000 (prior year) and EUR 50,000 (current year) for small business exemption (§19 UStG). For intra-EU acquisitions, EUR 12,500 threshold for acquisition VAT. Remote sales to German consumers: EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU countries are zero-rated under §4 Nr. 1a UStG and §6 UStG. Cross-border services (e.g., software services) supplied to non-business customers outside the EU may be zero-rated under §3a(2) UStG. For B2B services within the EU, reverse charge applies under §13b UStG, resulting in 0% German VAT. Offshore services must be proven that the place of supply is outside Germany.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with MRN
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation of foreign payment)
  • Commercial invoice referencing export
  • Proof of exit (Ausgangsvermerk) from customs

Germany mandates e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for B2B domestic transactions from 1 January 2025 for receipt, with phased issuance requirements (2027/2028). No real-time clearance required; invoices must comply with EN 16931 and XRechnung/ZUGFeRD formats. VAT returns via ELSTER; no API for real-time clearance. Digital stamping not required.

Fraudulent export zero-rating leads to retrospective VAT assessment plus late payment interest (6% per annum). Penalties under §370 AO (tax evasion) up to 10 years imprisonment and fines. Clawback of input VAT deductions; additional penalties up to 100% of evaded tax. Customs penalties under Zollkodex. Tax audit (Betriebsprüfung) may extend 4-10 years. Non-compliance with documentation results in denial of zero-rating and full 19% VAT liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany forms part of the eurozone and applies full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU free-movement-of-capital rules and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz). There are no exchange controls, no prior administrative authorisation for foreign-currency remittances, and no limitation on repatriation of profits. A UG may remit dividends to non-resident shareholders through any bank account, subject to statutory distribution formalities and, where applicable, German dividend withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge; effective 26.375%) before treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief is assessed. Foreign-currency proceeds may be held in original currency or converted to EUR freely. The only material hurdle is the Bundesbank's balance-of-payments reporting obligation for individual cross-border payments exceeding €12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender regime, no minimum conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriation or conversion of export proceeds. Export receivables of a German UG may be held indefinitely in foreign currency and reused for operating or treasury purposes. For balance-of-payments statistics under Section 67 of the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung), residents must report cross-border receipts and payments from non-residents exceeding €12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank electronically, generally by the end of the month following the transaction month. No central bank approval or conversion direction applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank and foreign exchange statistical authority), operating jointly with the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision, anti-money-laundering compliance, and payment institution authorisation under the German Banking Act (Kreditwesengesetz) and the German Anti-Money-Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an ancillary collection and disbursement rail; Low as a primary treasury bank. A UG may lawfully use EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions such as Stripe, Paddle, Wise, or Airwallex for collection of subscription, licensing, and IoT telemetry fees, provided the EMI holds a valid licence passported into Germany under PSD2 and complies with BaFin and Bundesbank reporting rules. Offshore non-EEA EMIs are not legally acceptable as a full banking substitute because they do not hold a BaFin/KWG banking or BaFin-licensed e-money authorisation, cannot provide insured or protected client-money accounts under German trust-account principles, and may create AML, correspondent-banking, and substance risk. The prudent treasury design is to use Paddle and Stripe as the front-end merchant/settlement rails, then periodically sweep cleared proceeds to a tier-1 German commercial bank account in the name of the UG, preserving a clear audit trail for the Bundesbank, tax authorities, and issuing bank due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the UG must be routed as a clear SWIFT MT103 corporate payment to the UG's German bank escrow or operational account. Field 70/Remittance Information should state the purpose in explicit terms, for example 'CAPITAL INJECTION - SUBSCRIPTION OF SHARES' or 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION PER NOTARIAL DEED DATED [DATE]', and the bank memo should reference the ISO 20022 purpose code associated with capital transfers. For statistical compliance, the transaction must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank where the amount exceeds €12,500; no conversion approval is required for EUR. The bank will verify the notarial deed, the commercial register extract, the UBO declaration, the source-of-funds documentation, and the internal approval resolution. Until the UG is registered in the Handelsregister, the capital should be parked in a segregated escrow/trust account; after registration, the account is released to the UG as ordinary current-account liquidity.For operating and trade disbursements, the UG should use SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for EEA payments and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA and/or foreign-currency payments, with full invoice references and remittance information. For enterprise software sales, commercial letters of credit are generally unsuitable; where contractual security is required, a SWIFT MT700 standby letter of credit or bank guarantee is preferred. Recurring SaaS royalties and reseller commissions should be settled through SWIFT MT103/SEPA CT with purpose codes such as 'ROYALTIES' or 'LICENSE FEES', supported by invoices, delivery telemetry, and licensing reports.
