Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); operational portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet) at https://www.emta.ee and joint e-services for corporate and value-added tax filings.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of an Osaühing (OÜ) in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri), tax registration is effectuated automatically and the company receives a national resident registration code (registry code) which serves as its statutory tax identification number. Non-resident founders may use the e-Residency digital ID to sign incorporation and registration forms electronically without physical presence. A non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in Estonia that engages in taxable supplies in Estonia must apply separately to the MTA for Estonian VAT identification through the e-MTA portal, either on a voluntary basis or mandatorily once the domestic taxable turnover threshold is exceeded, and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative or tax trustee approved by the MTA. All TIN and VAT ID applications require authenticated digital signatures, proof of legal existence abroad, and documented appointment of a contact person, with MTA processing generally completed within 3 to 10 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - Estonia applies a unique distribution-based corporate income tax system (deferred tax model): an OÜ is taxed at the entity level only on profit distributions and other deemed distributions (fringe benefits, gifts, non-business expenses and transfer pricing adjustments) at a headline rate of 20/80 of the net distribution (equal to 20% of the grossed-up taxable base), while retained and reinvested earnings remain statutorily tax-free; the standard rate is effectively 25% calculated on the grossed-up distribution base; regular dividend distributions to Estonian resident individuals may benefit from a reduced 14/86 rate where the dividend-receiving individual is a resident natural person, subject to prior distributed profit rules. Interest, royalties and management fees paid to affiliated non-residents may be recharacterized as deemed distributions under the thin capitalization and controlled-foreign-company provisions. The OÜ's fiscal domicile is Estonia, and worldwide business income is within the scope of Estonian taxation only upon distribution, except for the special regime applicable to permanent establishments and the CFC anti-deferral rules; the applicable tax classification is therefore Entity_Level_Taxation with territorial and distribution-based scope. — Upon incorporation of an Osaühing (OÜ) in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri), tax registration is effectuated automatically and the company receives a national resident registration code (registry code) which serves as its statutory tax identification number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - Estonia applies a unique distribution-based corporate income tax system (deferred tax model): an OÜ is taxed at the entity level only on profit distributions and other deemed distributions (fringe benefits, gifts, non-business expenses and transfer pricing adjustments) at a headline rate of 20/80 of the net distribution (equal to 20% of the grossed-up taxable base), while retained and reinvested earnings remain statutorily tax-free; the standard rate is effectively 25% calculated on the grossed-up distribution base; regular dividend distributions to Estonian resident individuals may benefit from a reduced 14/86 rate where the dividend-receiving individual is a resident natural person, subject to prior distributed profit rules. Interest, royalties and management fees paid to affiliated non-residents may be recharacterized as deemed distributions under the thin capitalization and controlled-foreign-company provisions. The OÜ's fiscal domicile is Estonia, and worldwide business income is within the scope of Estonian taxation only upon distribution, except for the special regime applicable to permanent establishments and the CFC anti-deferral rules; the applicable tax classification is therefore Entity_Level_Taxation with territorial and distribution-based scope.

Available election forms

Estonia has no generic US-style check-the-box election; instead, the following statutory elections and filings are available: (i) election to apply the reduced 14/86 withholding/dividend rate for regular recurring dividends paid to Estonian resident individuals; (ii) VAT group registration election under VAT Act § 9, allowing closely economically and organizationally integrated legal persons to be jointly registered as one VAT group; (iii) voluntary VAT registration election for suppliers below the threshold; (iv) election for the special flat-rate income tax regime for sole proprietors (not available to an OÜ but relevant for branch structures); (v) election to apply the tonnage tax regime for eligible shipping companies; (vi) ATAD hybrid mismatch neutralization declarations filed with the MTA to apply the primary and secondary (reporting) rules through the annual CIT return mechanism; and (vii) written advance ruling and binding tariff-information applications to secure the tax characterization of cross-border transactions. Treaty relief for non-resident shareholders is obtained by filing the standard Estonian refund/relief application and, where applicable, a certificate of tax residence from the shareholder's home state.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic VAT registration mandatory from 1 January 2024 onwards when the cumulative taxable supply of goods and services in Estonia exceeds EUR 40,000 in a calendar year; a separate EU distance-selling registration threshold of EUR 10,000 applies to intra-Community distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C electronically supplied services (One-Stop-Shop OSS scheme), with the EUR 40,000 threshold not applying to distance sales, imported goods or supplies made by non-Establishment foreign persons, for which registration is required from the first transaction absent OSS election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export VAT treatment applies under VAT Act § 25 where goods are dispatched or transported from Estonian customs territory (including from an OÜ's bonded or tax-suspended warehouse) to a third country or third territory and the supplier retains documentary proof that the goods left the European Union within the statutory time limits (generally within 90 days from the time of supply for the transport/export arrangement). The supply of cosplay prop costumes, theatrical apparel, synthetic wigs and licensed costume robes qualifies as an export of goods provided the buyer is a non-EU customer, the goods physically cross the EU external border under customs supervision, and the export is evidenced by the customs export declaration. Exported services that are directly related to exported movable goods (e.g., design, tailoring, finishing and freight to a third-country customer) may also be zero-rated or treated as outside the scope of Estonian VAT where the recipient is established outside the EU and the services physically and legally attach to the goods; pure intellectual property and licensing royalties are subject to reverse-charge or place-of-supply rules and are not automatically zero-rated. The 0% rate can be claimed on VAT returns with full supporting documentation retained for at least seven years.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD / EX1) accepted by Estonian Customs (MTA Customs Department) containing the EX registration number, tariff commodity code (HS/CN 9505 or 6115/6004 as applicable for apparel and textile props), statistical value, and the actual export date;
  • Transport evidence: bill of lading (ocean), air waybill (air), or CMR/consignment note (road/rail) showing the consignor, consignee and third-country destination and confirming physical departure from EU territory;
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Estonian OÜ to the non-EU customer stating the 0% VAT rate, Incoterms, unit value, and a declaration of export eligibility;
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank confirmation from the Estonian credit institution evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds from the export customer on terms consistent with the invoice;
  • Purchase order, signed contract and internal export ledger records demonstrating the commercial substance of the cosplay prop apparel supply;
  • Customs power of attorney and forwarding agent instructions, if export clearance is handled by a licensed customs broker.

Estonia does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping or API-based tax clearance mandate on domestic business-to-business invoices; e-invoicing in the European standard (EN 16931 / UBL / Factur-X) is legally accepted and mandatory for suppliers of public-sector contracting authorities under Regulation (EU) 2014/55 and the Estonian Public Procurement Act; the MTA operates a centralized e-return system through the e-MTA portal requiring periodic VAT returns and aggregated sales/purchase ledgers, with the new SAF-T based electronic TSD (toimepäeva deklaratsioon) reporting package gradually being introduced for structured transactional data; taxpayers with e-invoicing capabilities are expected to transfer sales and purchase invoice microdata via API or file upload, and the forthcoming EU ViDA framework will phase in mandatory standard-based e-invoicing for cross-border B2B transactions after 2030.

The MTA subjects export zero-rating claims to automated risk-based scrutiny through its analysis engine, comparing customs declarations, VAT returns and banking inflows; where proof of physical export, correct destination, or commercial validity is incomplete, inaccurate or fraudulent, the tax authority may reassess the supply at the 22% standard VAT rate, increase the net tax liability, and issue a supplementary VAT assessment with tax default interest calculated on a daily basis at the statutory arrears rate (currently 0.06% per day, i.e. approximately 21.9% per annum) accruing from the original due date. Fraudulent export zero-rating, falsified export documentation, fictitious foreign purchasers, or use of shell invoices to obtain unwarranted refunds constitutes tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code §§ 389, 397-1 and 399, punishable by fines up to EUR 1,200,000, up to eight years' imprisonment for natural persons, and prohibition on business activity; the MTA may additionally impose a non-declaration penalty of 0.5% to 5% of the unpaid tax, deny refunds and set them off against arrears, and extend the standard five-year limitation period to ten years where concealment is proven. Directors of the OÜ incur personal secondary liability for deliberately unpaid taxes, and clawback may extend to connected group entities under unjust enrichment and controlled-foreign-company rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes. Estonia imposes no exchange controls or central bank authorisation requirements on cross-border flows, owing to EU free movement of capital. Profit remittance as dividends is legally unrestricted, but the distribution itself triggers Estonian corporate income tax under the taxation-of-distributions system at the standard rate of 20% of the gross distribution, reduced to 14% for regular and qualified smaller distributions. No conversion taxes, surrender duties, or central bank approval hurdles apply, although anti-money laundering (AML), tax-residence, and bank KYC checks may affect the practical speed of execution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds or to convert them into euro. Exporters may retain receivables in any currency, maintain foreign-currency trading accounts, and convert as they see fit at commercial rates. There is no mandatory repatriation deadline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement to obtain export proceeds clearance from Eesti Pank. Euro-denominated receipts settle through the SEPA and TARGET2 systems, while non-euro receipts are settled by SWIFT or correspondent banking channels.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the central bank of Estonia and member of the Eurosystem, acting as the national monetary authority and payment systems overseer; prudential banking supervision is consolidated within Finantsinspektsioon (the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) under the joint supervisory framework with Eesti Pank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorised by Eesti Pank or another EU regulator under PSD2 passporting rights; such EMIs can provide EUR IBAN accounts, SEPA/SEPA Instant clearing, and multi-currency wallets for an Estonian OÜ. Feasibility rating: Low for offshore digital EMIs not licensed in the EEA; they are not recognised as regulated credit or payment institutions in Estonia, lack access to the SEPA scheme as direct participants, and may carry material AML and investor-protection risks. For treasury-grade operations, the OÜ should route primary liquidity through a tier-1 commercial bank, using an EU-licensed EMI strictly as a post-check and final-settlement tool of last resort.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is for the shareholder to transfer EUR from its own bank account, in the shareholder's legal name, to the OÜ's operational or capital escrow account at a licensed Estonian bank. For equity injections, the bank will require a signed board resolution approving the share issuance, an updated Estonian Business Register extract confirming registration of the capital increase, and a source-of-funds explanation. The SEPA ISO 20022 payment instruction should carry a purpose code designating equity capital contribution or a loan; for SWIFT MT103, the field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHARE CAPITAL' as applicable. No foreign investment authorisation is required in Estonia; however, transaction monitoring may trigger a beneficial-ownership check before funds are available for disbursement.Routine supplier settlements in EUR within the EEA settle via SEPA Credit Transfer; same-day liquidity is available through SEPA Instant if the counterparty bank participates. For non-EUR suppliers, SWIFT MT103 with standard cost-accounting codes is the clearing rail of choice. For large or first-time cross-border procurement, especially of synthetic wig materials or specialised costume hardware from Asia, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (MT700) issued by the OÜ's bank can protect both buyer and seller; the LC must be aligned with the incoterms and underlying trade invoices.
