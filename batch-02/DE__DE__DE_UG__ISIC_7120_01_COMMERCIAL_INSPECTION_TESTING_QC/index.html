Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister Abteilung B (Commercial Register, Section B) maintained by the local court (Amtsgericht) at the registered office district, in cooperation with a certified German notary; accessed through the electronic register portal (Handelsregisterportal / elektronisches Registerverfahren).

Governing statute

Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbHG – German Limited Liability Companies Act), supplemented by the Handelsgesetzbuch (HGB) and the Beurkundungsgesetz; key sections: §§ 1 (purpose), 2–3 (notarized Articles of Association), 4a (registered office), 5 (Stammkapital), 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft – UG), 6–8 (managing directors, application for registration), 10 (registration in the Commercial Register), 35 (representation by managing directors).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag / Musterprotokoll für die Unternehmergesellschaft), together with the notarized application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) pursuant to § 7 GmbHG, the shareholder list (Gesellschafterliste) under § 8 GmbHG, the managing director appointment and specimen signature (Unterschriftsprobe).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG (haftungsbeschränkt) has no statutory minimum nominal share capital; the subscribed Stammkapital must be at least EUR 1.00 and fully paid before registration, being at the free disposal of the managing director (§ 8(2) GmbHG). Market practice requires a bank confirmation (Geschäftskonto eröffnet as 'UG i.G.') with an initial deposit of approx. EUR 1,000–3,000 to ensure solvency and the bank's acceptance. Mandatory statutory reserve: 25% of annual net profits must be retained until the issued share capital reaches EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG), which is applied from the date of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutzabnahme) by the Untere Bauaufsichtsbehörde confirming compliance with the state building code (MBO), including escape routes, fire-extinguishing equipment, and the fire-safety concept of the office/laboratory premises; structural load inspection certificate (Standsicherheitsnachweis) for installed test benches and heavy equipment; workplace clearance under the Arbeitsstättenverordnung (ArbStättV), with required room depth, ventilation, lighting (≥ 500 lux in laboratory area) and sanitary facilities; occupational health and safety audit by the insurance carrier Berufsgenossenschaft Handel und Warenlogistik (BGHW), including accident-prevention regulations, first-aid and hazardous-substance exposure assessment; mandatory appointment of a workplace-safety officer per § 11 ASiG.

Paid-in capital rule: UG (haftungsbeschränkt) has no statutory minimum nominal share capital; the subscribed Stammkapital must be at least EUR 1.00 and fully paid before registration, being at the free disposal of the managing director (§ 8(2) GmbHG). Market practice requires a bank confirmation (Geschäftskonto eröffnet as 'UG i.G.') with an initial deposit of approx. EUR 1,000–3,000 to ensure solvency and the bank's acceptance. Mandatory statutory reserve: 25% of annual net profits must be retained until the issued share capital reaches EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG), which is applied from the date of incorporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the value added tax identification number (USt-IdNr) and international withholding relief; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) with jurisdiction over the registered seat for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT assessment, and payroll tax; electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for all statutory returns; municipal trade office (Gewerbeamt/Ordnungsamt) for trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) that triggers trade tax assessment.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize the articles of association and register the UG in the commercial register (Handelsregister), obtaining a registered seat in Germany. Step 2: Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) to the competent Finanzamt within 4 weeks after business commencement, disclosing shareholders, managing directors, forecast turnover, and planned outbound services; the Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. Step 3: Apply separately to BZSt for a USt-IdNr via ELSTER or the BZSt online portal (mandatory for EU cross-border invoices, VIES validation, and reverse-charge references); processing typically takes 3–8 weeks. Step 4: File the Gewerbeanmeldung with the municipal trade office (requirement and basis for the trade tax contribution with the local Hebesatz). Step 5: Obtain the mandatory Wirtschafts-Identifikationsnummer (W-IdNr) under the Company Digitalization Act, issued by the Federal Statistical Office via the commercial register. Non-resident shareholders are permitted without restriction; if no managing director is tax resident in Germany, the tax office may require appointment of a domestic authorized recipient (Zustellungsbevollmächtigter) for procedural documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (mandatory for the UG, which is a capital company; no pass-through or check-the-box eligibility). The UG is a resident taxpayer with unlimited worldwide corporate income tax liability under Section 1 Körperschaftsteuergesetz (KStG) whenever its registered seat or place of effective management is in Germany. Corporate income tax: 15% of taxable income; solidarity surcharge: 5.5% of the corporate income tax (0.825% effective); trade tax: 3.5% of taxable trade income multiplied by the municipal collection rate (Hebesatz), normally 300%–490%, producing effective trade tax rates of approximately 10.5%–17.15%, so the aggregate statutory tax burden is approximately 28%–32% of pre-tax profit. Distributions to foreign shareholders are subject to withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) of 25% plus solidarity surcharge (26.375% total) unless reduced under a double taxation treaty (DTT), accessible by filing a withholding relief application at BZSt. Territorial_Scope: Worldwide_Unlimited (foreign-source business income is fully taxable; DTTs commonly provide foreign tax credit or exemption; Section 8b KStG grants a 95% participation exemption on dividends and capital gains from qualifying foreign subsidiaries; German CFC rules in Sections 7–13 Außensteuergesetz apply to passive foreign income). The UG is not eligible for the Section 34a EStG retained-earnings privilege, which applies only to sole proprietorships and partnerships.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box election; classification of the UG is statutory per KStG and the UG is per se a body corporate. Available elections and options: (1) Tax-consolidation Organschaft election under Sections 14–17 KStG and Section 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag), a qualified voting majority of at least 75%, and financial, economic, and organizational integration, allowing group loss-offset for corporate income tax and, for VAT, treatment as a single taxable person; (2) Small-enterprise VAT exemption election under Section 19 UStG if prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is expected not to exceed EUR 50,000 — economically adverse for a QC agency because input VAT recovery is excluded and zero-rating of exports becomes inefficient; (3) Openly disclosed reserve election for capital companies is not available, but the UG may retain profits to build the legal reserve toward EUR 25,000 GmbH capital; (4) Reverse-charge shifting under Section 13b UStG for imported services and works received from non-German suppliers; (5) DTT-derived shareholder election to claim reduced dividend withholding tax through a BZSt exemption certificate (Freistellungsbescheinigung) under Section 50d EStG; (6) Elective application of the German GAAP simplification for micro-sized GmbH/UG entities in the commercial balance sheet, insofar as the corporate tax balance sheet follows commercial accounts; (7) For foreign-parented UGs, election under German transfer-pricing documentation thresholds: simultaneous documentation (zeitnahe Dokumentation) is required for cross-border transactions with related parties exceeding EUR 10 million per transaction type (or EUR 1 million for financial transactions) in fiscal years beginning after 31 December 2023.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a fully liberalized euro-area jurisdiction: both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, and there are no exchange-control approvals, surrender requirements, or frictions on profit remittance. Dividend distributions by a UG to a non-resident shareholder may be executed freely; tax is withheld at 25% plus solidarity surcharge (approximately 26.375%) subject to any applicable double-taxation treaty, but no FX license or central-bank conversion order is required. The only practical regulatory hurdles are (i) the statutorily required balance-of-payments statistical reporting for cross-border payments above EUR 12,500, (ii) bank KYC/AML documentation, and (iii) EU/UN sanctions screening on beneficiary jurisdictions and persons.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German law imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory EUR-conversion ratio on export proceeds. An exporter may keep its foreign-currency receipts in any bank, in any currency, and use them to settle foreign obligations. The mandatory compliance duty is the Bundesbank monthly reporting under sections 59 to 67 AWV: each inbound or outbound cross-border payment (or related series of payments) that exceeds EUR 12,500 must be reported using the Bundesbank's electronic reporting channels (typically by the tenth day of the month following the payment); reporting codes must reflect the underlying commercial-inspection service and not a generic goods code.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank), administering the foreign-trade and balance-of-payments statistical reporting regime under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); banking conduct supervision for FX and payment services is exercised by the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), with EU-level payment-system oversight by the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rated Medium-High as an operating rail for multi-currency balances, platform-related settlements, and fast supplier disbursements. Wise Europe and equivalent digital EMIs are authorised as Electronic Money Institutions under the EU Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and passport into Germany under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2); they cannot, however, act as the UG's statutory capital-destination account because German notarial practice requires confirmation from a KWG-authorised credit institution (Vollbank) before issuing the capital-deposit certificate. Funds held at an EMI are safeguarded in segregated accounts, not insured by the German statutory deposit guarantee scheme (Einlagensicherung). For the UG's treasury architecture, the EMI must be a complementary execution lane subordinated to the primary fully licensed German bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal burden of 15.825% before municipal trade tax. Municipal trade tax generally adds approximately 7% to 21%, depending on the municipality; the national average total burden is approximately 30% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital gains withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic rate of 26.375%. A German corporation receiving dividends may generally claim a participation exemption subject to statutory conditions. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain a 0% withholding result under the EU Parent-Subsidiary regime if the minimum holding, holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse requirements are satisfied. Treaties commonly reduce dividend withholding to 5% for qualifying substantial corporate holdings and 15% for portfolio holdings, but the applicable treaty must be checked individually.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax and trade tax returns are generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Where a German tax adviser prepares the return, an extended deadline generally applies under the applicable annual administrative deadline rules, subject to statutory exceptions, audit orders, and special extensions. Tax prepayments are due quarterly on the statutory dates. Filing deadlines may be extended only by the tax authority or under an applicable statutory extension.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous documentation generally applies to cross-border related-party transactions. A simplified threshold commonly used for detailed local-file documentation is annual cross-border transaction volume exceeding EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for services and other transactions. A local file may nevertheless be required below those amounts if the tax authority requests documentation or the facts present heightened risk. A master file is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational enterprise group with consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 100 million in the preceding fiscal year, subject to applicable German rules. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups meeting the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no single standalone regime labelled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is assessed through the arm's-length principle, German transfer-pricing rules, treaty beneficial-ownership standards, anti-treaty-shopping provisions, general anti-avoidance rules, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, principal-purpose and abuse doctrines, and business-purpose requirements. The UG should maintain real German management, qualified personnel, operational premises, decision-making records, customer and supplier contracts, functional-risk analysis, bank activity, and evidence that the inspection and quality-control services are actually performed. A mere registered address, nominee management, pass-through invoicing, circular funds, or undocumented related-party charges creates material denial, withholding, recharacterization, and penalty risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax must be assessed and recovered, with interest and administrative penalties. Voluntary disclosure relief is highly formalistic and may fail after discovery or investigation begins.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability for culpable failure to remit taxes, civil damages, asset seizure, professional disqualification, and confiscation. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from criminal proceedings, arrest warrants, or bail conditions.
Failure to Remit Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: The UG may be assessed for the unwithheld amount, interest, late-payment surcharges, and tax penalties. Intentional withholding-tax evasion can constitute a criminal offense.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can incur personal liability for tax payment failures caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of their duties and may face criminal investigation, civil recovery, and asset enforcement.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make income adjustments, deny deductions, impose additional corporate income tax and trade tax, charge interest, and impose documentation-related penalties generally ranging from a percentage of the adjustment or transaction amount, subject to statutory minimums and maximums.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil liability for avoidable tax losses, shareholder claims, insolvency-related claims, and criminal exposure if the pricing was intentionally manipulated or supported by false records.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Treaty or EU exemptions may be denied, income may be recharacterized, withholding tax may be assessed retroactively, and anti-abuse provisions may apply. Authorities may also challenge beneficial ownership and impose interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil tax claims, criminal exposure where deception or intentional evasion is established, asset seizure, insolvency liability, and disqualification. Travel bans are not an ordinary automatic sanction but may follow criminal procedural measures.
VAT Fraud and Carousel Transactions

Statutory sanction: Input VAT may be denied, output VAT assessed, refunds recovered, interest imposed, and criminal VAT-fraud penalties applied. Serious cases can result in imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT and damages where supervisory duties were breached and may face prosecution, searches, seizure, asset freezing, and insolvency claims.
Books, Records, and Filing Failure

Statutory sanction: Possible late-filing surcharges, coercive fines, estimated assessments, tax interest, bookkeeping penalties, administrative fines, and enforcement costs. Non-filing of annual accounts can result in regulatory fines imposed through the Federal Gazette process.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for tax and insolvency losses caused by non-compliance, and persistent breaches may support removal, disqualification, or criminal proceedings where statutory offenses are met.
AML and Beneficial-Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines can be substantial and may be increased for serious, repeated, or systemic violations. Suspicious transactions may be frozen or reported, and criminal money-laundering liability may arise where knowledge or intent is established.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal administrative or criminal liability, confiscation, asset freezing, reputational consequences, and restrictions on management activities.
Unlicensed or Misrepresented Inspection Activity

Statutory sanction: Possible prohibition orders, administrative fines, withdrawal of authorization, civil damages, contract invalidity, customer claims, and criminal liability for fraud or document offenses where reports or certifications are falsified.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tort and contractual liability, criminal prosecution for intentional misconduct, professional disqualification, and insolvency claims. The exact licensing requirement depends on whether the activity is ordinary commercial quality control or a regulated statutory conformity-assessment function.
Payroll, Social Security, and Worker Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, employer and employee social-security contributions, interest, surcharges, administrative fines, and criminal penalties may apply. Intentional non-payment of employee social-security contributions is a serious criminal offense.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unpaid employee social-security contributions and wage taxes and may face criminal prosecution, asset enforcement, and insolvency proceedings.
Insolvency and Asset-Stripping

Statutory sanction: Potential civil repayment claims, insolvency administrator recovery, fines, and imprisonment for insolvency offenses, fraudulent delay, or breach of accounting duties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for payments made after insolvency, late filing losses, tax and social-security breaches, and creditor damage. Courts may impose management bans; travel restrictions may be ordered only in applicable criminal or enforcement circumstances.
