Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA). Official portal: e-MTA at www.emta.ee, authenticated via ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID or e-Residency Digi-ID over the X-Road data exchange layer. The corporate tax identification number (TIN) of an OÜ is its Commercial Register (Äriregister) registry code; VAT identification numbers use the prefix EE plus 9 digits (e.g., EE100456789).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the OÜ online through the e-Business Register (fully available to e-Residents); the registry code issued at incorporation automatically constitutes the statutory TIN and must be quoted on invoices, import declarations and all e-MTA filings. Step 2: Complete the initial tax-registration notification (place of management, ISIC activity code, banking details, beneficial ownership filing) through e-MTA; no separate TIN certificate is needed for resident electronic companies. Step 3: Apply for VAT registration via e-MTA under the VAT Act (käibemaksuseadus) within five working days after the registration obligation arises; domestic taxable turnover triggers registration at EUR 40,000 per calendar year, with retroactive effect from the date liability began; a non-resident supplier without an Estonian seat or permanent establishment must register before its first taxable supply (no threshold), appoint a document-service representative and, at MTA discretion, post VAT security. Step 4: Register for any applicable Union OSS, Non-Union OSS or Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) schemes and for a PEPPOL e-invoice address for B2G sales. All corporate tax forms (e.g., TSD annual income tax declaration, KMD monthly VAT return, KMD INF VAT/sales list) are filed electronically; the VAT certificate and EU VIES confirmation are issued electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An OÜ is autonomously taxable under Estonia's deferred/distribution-based corporate income tax: 0% income tax on retained and reinvested profits; 20% income tax on gross profit distributions (equivalent to 20/80, i.e., 25% of the net dividend actually paid); reduced 14% rate for regular periodic distributions of qualifying profits; deemed distributions include fringe benefits, gifts, representations and non-business expenses. Foreign business income may qualify for participation exemption under Income Tax Act § 53; outbound interest, royalties and management fees to related non-residents carry a 20% withholding (subject to EU Interest/Royalty Directive and treaty relief). No pass-through or transparent taxation is available for an OÜ.

Available election forms

Estonia has no check-the-box election. Available statutory elections: (1) reduced 14% CIT on regular distributions (Income Tax Act § 50); (2) VAT group registration for Estonian affiliated companies; (3) Union OSS election for EU distance sales and B2C e-services; (4) Non-Union OSS election for e-services by third-country suppliers; (5) Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) election for remote sales of low-value imported goods not exceeding EUR 150; (6) participation-exemption election for foreign PE dividend and branch profits; (7) simplified reverse-charge and refund arrangements for non-established taxable persons. ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (2019/2020 implementation) deny benefits from double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion arrangements, but the distribution-based CIT inherently limits such mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic established suppliers: EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable supplies (periodically adjusted by Parliament). Intra-EU distance sales and B2C e-services: EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold for OSS users. Non-resident suppliers with no Estonian seat or permanent establishment: no threshold, registration is due before the first taxable supply; e-commerce marketplace deemed-supplier rules apply to non-EU sellers of physical goods into the EU. Import VAT is payable on all business imports without threshold; IOSS may be used for consignments not exceeding EUR 150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to: (i) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or a third party on its behalf within 90 days of supply; (ii) intra-EU B2B supplies to a VAT-registered customer with a valid VIES-confirmed EU VAT ID; and (iii) directly related freight, handling and inspection services. Zero-rating is conditional on documentary proof of physical export (customs MRN, transport documents, warehouse and payment records). Digital e-services supplied to non-EU consumers fall outside the Estonian place of supply and are not charged with Estonian VAT (customer-location proof required). Estonia imposes no exchange-control or statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects banking, AML and transfer-pricing documentation practice rather than a legal repayment period. Excess input VAT on export purchases is refundable through the monthly KMD return.

  • Customs export declaration (MRN) or electronic export summary declaration evidencing release and physical exit of goods from the EU
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note with destination address and proof of delivery/electronic POD
  • Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent: SWIFT MT103/bank credit advice, or Stripe/PayPal payout settlement statements evidencing collection of export sales proceeds

No real-time tax clearance, continuous transaction control or digital stamping regime applies in Estonia as of 2025. Mandatory electronic reporting applies: monthly VAT returns (KMD) and EU sales lists (KMD INF / VIES) must be filed by the 20th day of the following month through e-MTA; customs declarations are lodged electronically via the Estonian customs declaration system (ECS/ICS). B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for all invoices to Estonian public-sector bodies under the EU e-invoicing standard (EN 16931, PEPPOL network). e-MTA publishes X-Road/API interfaces for machine-to-machine submissions; taxpayers must preserve structured e-invoice files and underlying audit trails. A national real-time-economy e-invoice rollout is in preparation but is not yet mandatory for B2B/B2C transactions.

MTA conducts retrospective export-audit verifications under a general tax-assessment limitation of three years, extended to five years or more in cases of concealment or material misstatements. Where zero-rating is disallowed, the OÜ is reassessed 22% output VAT on the full sales value, plus late-payment interest at 0.06% per calendar day, tax-default penalties under the Taxation Act, and revocation of the VAT certificate. Fraudulent export schemes (fictitious MRNs, phantom courier consignments, circular EU routing, false VIES customer identities) are treated as tax evasion under Penal Code § 403, punishable by criminal fine (daily-rate system) and/or imprisonment up to three years, increased to up to five years for substantial amounts or organized-group conduct, plus retrospective clawback of refunded input VAT and interest. Cross-border evidence is exchanged through Regulation (EU) 904/2010 VAT administrative cooperation and customs mutual-assistance channels.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible: within Estonia there are no exchange-control restrictions on current-account transactions (goods, services, income) or capital-account transactions (foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, portfolio investment, repatriation) — a direct application of Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. Neither Eesti Pank nor any Estonian agency requires prior authorization, registration, or post-transaction reporting for inbound capital injections, outbound supplier payments, or dividend distributions. Since 1 January 2011 Estonia is a full Eurosystem member; EUR is the sole legal tender and there is no domestic foreign-exchange market regulation. The only profit-remittance friction is fiscal: the OÜ, not the foreign shareholder, bears Estonian distribution tax at 20/80 of the net dividend (14/86 for certain regular distributions to Estonian residents and individuals; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive participation exemption for EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10%), and no additional withholding tax is imposed at the recipient level. The consumer-electronics e-commerce model therefore faces zero legal convertibility hurdles; residual friction is limited to bank-level KYC de-risking of cross-border marketplace flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender, conversion, or repatriation rules apply. Estonian law imposes no statutory timeline for the repatriation of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR, and no obligation to route foreign-currency receipts through a domestic bank. Because the euro is a fully convertible reserve currency, the OÜ may invoice in EUR, USD, GBP, or CNH and retain the resulting foreign-currency balances indefinitely in multi-currency accounts at Estonian banks or EU/EEA passported EMIs without notification to Eesti Pank. The binding obligations are accounting and evidentiary: export and marketplace proceeds must be recorded at ECB reference exchange rates under the Accounting Act (Raamatupidamise seadus), retained with underlying documentation (commercial invoices, customs declarations CN22/CN23/SAD, and PSP settlement reports) for the 7-year statutory retention period, and reflected in the entity-level distribution tax base of the Income Tax Act when profits are distributed.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — the Eurosystem central bank acting under the Bank of Estonia Act and the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; it executes euro-area monetary policy, maintains T2 settlement membership, and holds the reserve accounts of Estonian credit institutions. Prudential supervision of banks, payment institutions, and e-money institutions is delegated to Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) under the Financial Supervision Authority Act. AML/CFT supervision and financial-intelligence functions rest with the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (RahaPTS). Estonia maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration, and the Ministry of Finance exercises no exchange-control authority.
