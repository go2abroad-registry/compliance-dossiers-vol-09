Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG must have a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1; no statutory escrow or blocked-account arrangement is prescribed, but cash contributions must be fully paid and at the managing director's free disposal before the Commercial Register application is submitted. Under § 5a(3) GmbHG, 25% of annual net profits must be retained annually until the registered capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and structural clearance for the leased office: workplace fire-safety inspection and escape-route certification under the local building regulations and the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung); structural load confirmation for high-density patent files and archive rooms. Occupational-safety clearance is evidenced by a workplace risk assessment under § 5 ArbSchG and registration of the employer with the responsible Berufsgenossenschaft before staff begin work.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG must have a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1; no statutory escrow or blocked-account arrangement is prescribed, but cash contributions must be fully paid and at the managing director's free disposal before the Commercial Register application is submitted. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) and locally competent tax office (Finanzamt); official portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online Portal (www.bzst.de)

E-government filing portal

Upon notarial incorporation of the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) and registration in the Commercial Registry (Handelsregister), the UG is automatically allocated a national tax number (Steuernummer) by the locally competent Finanzamt. A VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) must be applied for separately from the BZSt using the official form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr' (Form USt 1 TU). Non-resident owners do not alter the registration procedure, but a non-resident UG must appoint a domestic tax representative for VAT procedures if it has no fixed establishment in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a German private limited company with legal personality, subject to unlimited corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% plus solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) of 5.5% of the corporate income tax, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) dependent on the municipal rate (Hebebericht public rates). There is no pass-through or flow-through classification for German tax purposes.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists in German law. The UG is mandatorily opaque and individually taxed. Hybrid mismatch determinations under the German anti-hybrid rules (§ 4k EStG) require alignment of tax classification of foreign entities and permanent establishments but do not permit a UG to elect pass-through status. Elections are limited to choice of taxation year, trade tax allocation, and grouped accounting for VAT purposes (Organschaft) if the controlling company holds financial and operational integration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a domestic German entrepreneur (UG), the small-enterprise relief applies only when the previous year's turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and the forecast current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000; however, a UG carrying out export zero-rated supplies or intra-Community acquisitions is required to register without any threshold. Foreign entrepreneurs, regardless of turnover, are mandatory registrants when making taxable supplies in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods under § 6 UStG applies where the supply is unequivocally dispatched to a non-EU destination (or into a German free-port/free-zone regime) and the exporter is the supplier or a forwarding agent. The evidentiary standard is the customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) accepted by customs authorities. Cross-border services rendered by the patent prosecution agency to a non-EU client will fall under the B2B general rule (place of supply at the customer's seat, outside Germany); if supplied to non-EU private individuals, such services are zero-rated provided the recipient is not habitually resident in Germany. Supporting evidence includes the contract, business profile of the client, bank payment documents, and any records proving the place of establishment of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) validated by customs (ABD acknowledgement)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export to non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice categorizing the supply as zero-rated export with reference to § 6 UStG
  • Bank payment instruction or Inward Remittance Certificate (imported currency receipt) proving the foreign payment
  • Any transport and insurance documents corroborating the export trajectory

German B2B electronic invoicing mandate applies in stages: from 1 January 2025, every German business must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027, sellers with more than EUR 800,000 turnover in 2026 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all B2B transactions must be e-invoiced. Accepted formats are XRechnung, ZUGFeRD and EDI. Germany does not employ real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous control frameworks (CTC); invoices need to be transmitted electronically and archived in a tamper-proof manner for 10 years.

German tax authorities subject export zero-rating to substantive, documentary audits. Failure to substantiate the export within the prescribed period results in retrospective imposition of VAT at the 19% standard rate, together with tax interest at 0.5% per month (6% p.a. under § 233a AO). Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or fines; the tax liability may be imposed on the UG and, in aggravated cases, extended to any participating shareholder or director under personal liability provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital account convertibility under EU regulations and national FX law. Current account transactions, including professional services and IP-related fees, are freely convertible. Profit remittances face no significant hurdles beyond standard corporate tax compliance and beneficial ownership reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service exports such as PCT patent applications and trademark registrations do not require mandatory surrender of proceeds. Receipts must be collected and reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the payment statistics reporting system. Conversion occurs at prevailing market rates.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the corporate income tax, resulting in 15.825%, plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) levied at the statutory base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (commonly 340%–490%). The combined effective corporate tax burden is approximately 30%–33% depending on the municipality. Trade tax is not deductible as a business expense for corporate income tax purposes.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, resulting in an effective rate of 26.375% on gross dividends paid to non-resident shareholders. A UG may distribute dividends only after the mandatory annual 25% profit retention until share capital reaches EUR 25,000 (Sec. 5a GmbHG). For non-resident corporate shareholders within the EU/EEA holding at least 10% of the shares for at least 12 months, the withholding tax is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (implemented in Sec. 43b EStG).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed by 31 July of the calendar year following the fiscal year-end (Sec. 149 AO). Where the return is prepared and signed by a German tax advisor (Steuerberater), the deadline is automatically extended to 28/29 February of the second calendar year following the fiscal year-end. The annual financial statements, schedule of shareholdings, and tax reconciliation statements must be attached.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The trade tax return is due on the same date as the corporate income tax return: 31 July following the fiscal year-end, extended to 28/29 February of the second following year when prepared by a tax advisor. The UG is subject to trade tax as a commercial enterprise by legal form under Sec. 2 Abs. 2 GewStG. Advance trade tax payments are due on 10 February, 10 May, 10 August, and 10 November of each year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR – Master File: required where the worldwide consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 100 million per fiscal year (Sec. 5 GAufzV). Local File: required where the aggregate value of cross-border transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 5 million per transaction category per fiscal year (Sec. 5 GAufzV). Country-by-Country Report: required for groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million (Sec. 138f AO). A patent prosecution agency typically falls below these thresholds, but contemporaneous documentation remains mandatory when arm's length testing is needed.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate Economic Substance Regulations but enforces substance requirements through the OECD principal purpose test (Sec. 8c AStG and DTA case law), Sec. 42 AO general anti-abuse rule, and Sec. 50d Abs. 3 EStG, which denies treaty benefits to entities without economic substance. A UG holding patent prosecution activities must demonstrate actual substance through German business premises, locally employed qualified staff, independent bank accounts, managing director residing in the EU/EEA who effectively exercises executive authority, actual decision-making in Germany, and an active trade with genuine commercial risk. Letterbox companies without substance will be disregarded for treaty purposes, classified as tax avoidance vehicles, and their income may be attributed to the foreign shareholder, triggering hidden profit distribution adjustments and CFC taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years; aggravated cases under Sec. 370 Abs. 4 AO provide imprisonment of 1 to 10 years; court-imposed fine in addition to the criminal sanction; the tax claims survive the conviction with an additional interest surcharge; statute of limitation for criminal prosecution is 10 years.

Director personal liability: The managing director is liable under Sec. 69 AO for the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties when the company's inability to pay results from his culpable violation of duties. Directors can face travel bans, asset freezing orders, revocation of residence rights, and under the EU arrest warrant, extradition in serious cross-border cases.
Non-filing or late filing of tax returns

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessment with an arbitrarily high tax basis (Hinzuschätzung) under Sec. 162 AO; late filing surcharge up to 25% of the assessed tax, minimum EUR 25 per month; monthly interest of 0.15% on arrears; late payment surcharge of 1% per month up to 10%; in repeated cases, administrative fine of up to EUR 25,000 may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the estimated tax shortfall, interest, and surcharges if their intentional negligence caused the non-filing; the tax office may issue a personal liability order and enforce detention or travel restrictions for the managing director.
Shell company and lack of economic substance / treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under Sec. 50d Abs. 3 EStG and the OECD principal purpose test; recharacterization of income as hidden profit distribution (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung) under Sec. 8 Abs. 3 KStG; reclassification of the UG as tax transparent or as a sham under Sec. 42 AO; reallocation of income under the German transfer pricing rules Sec. 1 AStG with a penalty surcharge of 10% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: De facto director liability extends to the foreign beneficial owner who exercises actual control; the owners face civil claims for the repayment of distributions, joint and several liability for the adjusted corporate tax, and criminal exposure for breach of Sec. 266 StGB (unfaithful management) with imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax office may adjust income under Sec. 162 AO and levy a penalty of 10% of the upward income adjustment (minimum EUR 5,000, maximum EUR 100,000) under Sec. 162 Abs. 4 AO; the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer for the business operations he failed to document; additional default interest runs at 1.8% per annum; criminal tax evasion proceedings are opened if intent is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate documentation are personally liable for the adjusted tax amount, the surcharge, and interest if the company is unable to pay; professional negligence in supervision of the transfer pricing function may constitute a breach of the German Corporate Governance obligations and trigger liability claims by the company's creditors.
AML/CFT and licensing compliance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the statutory license and of the UG's commercial registration; administrative fines under Sec. 56 GwG of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal sanctions for money laundering under Sec. 261 StGB (imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years, or 6 months to 10 years for commercial gang-related laundering).

Director personal liability: Managing directors and de facto managers are personally subject to criminal liability for non-compliance with AML duties; the corporate criminal law provisions (Sec. 30 OWiG) impose fines on the company, while the competent authority may issue a ban on holding managerial positions, and foreign owners may be extradited for laundering proceeds of foreign predicate offenses.
