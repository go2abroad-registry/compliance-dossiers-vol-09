Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-MTA portal: https://emta.ee

E-government filing portal

Foreign founders filing an OÜ through the Estonian e-Business Register obtain a registry code automatically, which functions as the TIN. For VAT, registration is filed with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board using form PVR through e-MTA using an e-Residency card or EU digital identity. VAT registration becomes effective from the requested date, typically when taxable turnover in Estonia exceeds EUR 40,000 per calendar year or on a voluntary basis.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: resident OÜ is a separate taxable person; undistributed profits taxed at 0%; profit distributions are taxed at 20% on the net distribution (20/80 gross-up). Non-resident shareholders may be subject to withholding tax unless participation exemption or treaty relief applies. — Foreign founders filing an OÜ through the Estonian e-Business Register obtain a registry code automatically, which functions as the TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: resident OÜ is a separate taxable person; undistributed profits taxed at 0%; profit distributions are taxed at 20% on the net distribution (20/80 gross-up). Non-resident shareholders may be subject to withholding tax unless participation exemption or treaty relief applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in Estonia. The OÜ is conclusively taxed as a corporate entity for VAT and income tax. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 2 are codified; limited elective treatment arises under participation-exemption dividend rules (0% withholding on qualifying intercorporate distributions) and through advance dividend declarations permitted under the Estonian Income Tax Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover in Estonia; for EU distance sales of goods by an Estonian supplier, the special EU threshold is EUR 10,000 (Union distance-selling scheme)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically sold to non-EU customers qualify for 0% VAT if the exporter proves that the goods were dispatched/transported from Estonia to a third country within 90 days of the supply, evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration containing a Movement Reference Number (MRN). Services supplied by an Estonian OÜ to a non-EU entrepreneur or to a business customer outside Estonia are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside Estonia/EU. In automated dropshipping, each sale must be substantiated as an export or distance sale; supplies of goods that end up delivered to EU customers are not zero-rated unless subject to the Union OSS scheme.

  • Customs Export Declaration (MRN) and export report
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial Invoice with incoterms and declared value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving receipt of payment from the non-EU customer
  • Purchase order / contract
  • Packing list

Estonia does not operate mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory API-based digital stamping, or e-invoice clearance for B2B. E-invoicing is permitted and encouraged in UBL 2.1 / RDW format via e-MTA; public-sector e-invoicing is mandatory. VAT returns, OSS declarations, and Intrastat filings are submitted electronically through e-MTA, with no current pre-clearance mandate prior to invoice issuance.

Zero-rating is strictly audited by the ETCB. Unsupportable export claims trigger retrospective VAT assessment for up to three years (five years in cases of gross negligence or intentional concealment), default interest at 0.06% per day, and a tax-fraud penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax. Criminal liability under Penal Code § 346 may apply, including imprisonment up to five years for fraudulent VAT refund claims or customs document false statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general restrictions on converting or transferring funds for current-account or capital-account transactions. An Estonian OÜ may generally receive and make cross-border payments, hold foreign-currency accounts, contribute or repay capital, and remit dividends or other lawful distributions without a foreign-exchange permit. Banks and payment providers must apply customer due diligence, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, and source-of-funds checks. Dividends and other distributions remain subject to Estonian company-law requirements and applicable tax; since 2025, distributed profits are generally taxed at the company level at 22/78 of the net distribution, subject to applicable statutory rules and exceptions. Non-resident owners should separately assess tax residence, permanent-establishment, withholding, and controlled-foreign-company consequences in relevant jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline or mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio applies to an Estonian OÜ. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or held abroad, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, contractual, and bank-reporting requirements. VAT, customs, and statistical reporting obligations may apply according to the transaction and goods movement; they do not constitute a general foreign-exchange surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s national central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. Eesti Pank does not operate a general foreign-exchange licensing or capital-control regime. Prudential supervision of banks is conducted within the Single Supervisory Mechanism by the European Central Bank for significant institutions and by the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for other supervised institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to high for payment operations where the provider is authorized in the EU or EEA, serves Estonian companies, and supports the required currencies, counterparties, and payment corridors. An electronic-money institution (EMI) account is not a bank deposit and may not provide deposit-guarantee protection. An offshore or non-EEA digital EMI has no automatic right to provide regulated services in Estonia; verify its authorization, local or passporting permissions, safeguarding arrangements, withdrawal rights, and sanctions and correspondent-bank coverage. Stripe and PayPal may be used subject to their onboarding, prohibited-business, reserve, chargeback, and settlement terms; they do not replace a bank account or determine the company’s tax treatment.

0% shareholder-level withholding tax; the distributing OÜ bears corporate income tax at 20% or 14%, which is the final source tax (no additional withholding on dividends)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions with non-residents triggers mandatory Local File/Master File under EMTA transfer pricing guidelines; domestic related-party transactions may require documentation when exceeding €25,000

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework; however, substance is embedded in the place-of-effective-management residency rule (Income Tax Act § 6) and EU ATAD CFC rules. An OÜ managed and directed from abroad may be deemed resident in the state of effective management, eroding Estonian 0% retained-profit benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty payment under the Taxation Act up to €1,600; daily penalty interest at 0.06%; criminal fine up to 200 daily rates; imprisonment up to 3 years under Penal Code § 351 for substantial tax evasion

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false declarations or concealed distributions are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and interest; civil restitution orders; temporary travel bans and asset freezes; imprisonment up to 3 years if criminal thresholds are met
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of underreported profit as deemed dividend distribution subject to 20% corporate income tax; transfer pricing penalty up to €1,600; five-year correction notices; daily penalty interest on additional CIT assessed

Director personal liability: Board members are personally liable for the tax difference if they caused or approved the mispricing; prohibition from serving as director in serious cases; civil restitution to the company and tax authorities
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may reattribute place of management to the jurisdiction where directors reside, stripping Estonian tax residency; accumulated profits are treated as distributed and taxed at 20%; anti-abuse penalty up to €3,200

Director personal liability: Directors resident abroad may face taxation in their own country on sheltered retained profits; in Estonia, directors may be personally liable for unpaid tax if bad faith is proven and may be forcibly removed from the register
Anti-Money Laundering / KYC Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian AML Act (RahaPesus ja Terrorismi Rahastamise Tõkestamise Seadus), administrative penalties for obliged entities up to €5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; for non-obliged entities, fines up to €1,000,000 plus mandatory corrective internal-control directives

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined under the AML Act; courts may impose a directorial ban of up to 5 years; assets may be frozen; imprisonment up to 5 years where criminal intent or organized-crime involvement is established
