Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register maintained by the Registration Department of Tartu County Court, accessed through the Estonian e-Business Register.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), principally Sections 135-146 concerning the private limited company, founders, foundation agreement, articles of association, share contributions, application for registration, supporting documents, and registration; Sections 180-181 concerning management board duties and representation. The State Fees Act governs registry charges.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for entry of a private limited company in the Commercial Register, accompanied by the foundation agreement or foundation resolution, articles of association, management board details, beneficial ownership information, and confirmation of share contribution arrangements.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No aggregate statutory minimum share capital applies to an OÜ. Each shareholder's share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 0.01. The contribution obligation must be recorded in the foundation documents and fulfilled in accordance with the Commercial Code. A monetary contribution is paid to the company's account or, where legally permitted for the selected formation method, may be deferred subject to the founders' and management board's liability. The official registry fee is EUR 265, approximately USD 287 at an indicative EUR/USD rate; the payable amount is legally denominated in euros.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet / MTA); e-services portal at e-MTA (e-mta.ee) and state portal ecsti.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or registering a branch/PE in Estonia obtains an Estonian registry code (äriregistri kood) via the Commercial Register (e-äriregister), which doubles as the tax identification number for VAT, corporate income tax, and withholding tax purposes. A non-resident without a permanent establishment may register directly for VAT through Form F (VAT registration application) via the MTA e-service if making taxable supplies in Estonia or acquiring distance sales; the VAT registration number is issued as EE + 9 digits. Physical persons obtain a personal identification code (isikukood); non-residents engaged in taxable activities may apply for a simplified Estonian tax number through the MTA. Taxable persons must file an economic activity notice (majandustegevusteade) where required, and a fiscal representative may be mandated for non-resident taxable persons without a permanent establishment in Estonia.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing or registering a branch/PE in Estonia obtains an Estonian registry code (äriregistri kood) via the Commercial Register (e-äriregister), which doubles as the tax identification number for VAT, corporate income tax, and withholding tax purposes. A non-resident without a permanent establishment may register directly for VAT through Form F (VAT registration application) via the MTA e-service if making taxable supplies in Estonia or acquiring distance sales; the VAT registration number is issued as EE + 9 digits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia applies a distribution-based corporate income tax system: undistributed profits are not taxed, and CFC/exit-tax rules apply on deemed distributions and transfers; no general check-the-box or full pass-through election is available for OÜ. An OÜ may elect to apply the regular corporate income tax rate of 20/80 on taxable profit under limited conditions (e.g., thin capitalization or non-business distributions), but the default regime is taxation only on distributions at 20/80 (effective 20%). A non-resident company may elect to be treated as having a permanent establishment for VAT grouping purposes under special VAT grouping rules (VAT group registration), but this is not a tax classification election. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are implemented, and taxpayers may elect to apply the Estonian general anti-abuse rule position for hybrid instruments; no analogous 'check-the-box' entity classification system exists in domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 in taxable turnover (supplies subject to VAT, excluding zero-rated and reverse-charged supplies) within a 12-month period for resident taxable persons; non-residents making taxable supplies in Estonia must register regardless of threshold, and distance sales of goods into Estonia from other EU member states trigger registration above EUR 36,000 per year unless OSS is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated exports apply to the supply of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU, provided the supplier proves export within the statutory time limit (generally 3 months from supply; extended to 6 months for goods shipped by the buyer with a transport service order, and up to 12 months for exports under consignment stock under special provisions). Services related to goods exported to non-EU countries (e.g., freight, handling, loading) are zero-rated if directly linked to the export and the service recipient is a non-resident. Also zero-rated are substantial services performed on movable property acquired from a non-EU country, services to non-residents in certain B2B situations, and supplies under the distance sales and marketplace rules if outside Estonia's VAT territory. The exporter must hold documentary evidence of physical export from the EU Customs territory and the recipient's identity and address.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX, including the Export Decision/EX1 or electronic export declaration lodged in ECS) with customs office of exit stamp or electronic 'Amber' status confirmation
  • Commercial Invoice (sales contract/packing list) matching the export entry number and consignee references
  • Transport documents: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR/CIM consignment note signed by carrier, or postal shipment receipt
  • Proof of departure of goods from the EU: carrier confirmation, customs status 'Export completed' (elik ECS 'Exportation' status), or port/airport loading evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit confirmation or bank statement evidencing the export sales proceeds received from the non-EU buyer/payee)
  • Power of attorney/export authorization and any mandatory export licenses for controlled or dual-use goods (not applicable for silicone fidget toys unless embedded electronic components trigger controls)

Estonia does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance mandate for B2B domestic invoices; mandatory e-invoicing requirements apply to B2G invoices (public-sector e-invoicing under European Standard EN 16931, submitted through the Peppol network or the national portal). B2B e-invoicing is optional but Peppol-based delivery is recognized, and all invoices must be issued within 7 days of supply. Since 2025, Estonia has announced a roadmap toward mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic transactions, but no API-based pre-clearance or digital stamping regime is currently required; VAT returns must be filed electronically through the e-MTA portal no later than the 20th day of the month following the tax period, with SAF-T-like and periodic summary data exchange between EU member states.

The MTA may reject the zero rate and assess 20% VAT plus late-payment interest (0.06% per day from the due date) if the exporter fails to substantiate the export within the statutory period, files false customs declarations, or does not repatriate payments within the commercial deadline; administrative fines up to EUR 2,000 per false invoice, criminal liability for VAT fraud involving tax evaded over EUR 10,000 (punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years), reflection of the EU Regulation 904/2010 exchange system, VAT registration cancellation, and retrospective clawback for up to 3 years (extended to 10 years for VAT fraud) with joint and several liability of managing board members under the Estonian Taxation Act.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is an EU member state and eurozone participant. Both capital account and current account transactions are fully convertible under EU free movement rules. No capital controls, profit repatriation restrictions, or remittance hurdles exist for resident or non-resident entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds required. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts without statutory timeline or conversion ratios. Voluntary surrender available but not obligatory.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs face EU passporting and Estonian AML constraints; local banks remain preferred for corporate treasury due to correspondent relationships and full banking license requirements.
