Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister), operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Äriregistri Keskus) under the Ministry of Justice.

Governing statute

Commercial Code of Estonia (Äriseadustik), Chapter 12 on private limited companies (Osaühing) sections 135-171; registration procedure under sections 31-39 of the Commercial Code and the e-Business Register online filing rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregistri avaldus, U-Code form), filed electronically in the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) and signed with a qualified digital ID, Smart-ID, Mobile-ID, or an e-Residency digital ID card.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; an OÜ may be registered with zero share capital. If the articles set a positive share capital, the subscribed capital must be paid to the company's temporary bank account before registration only if the articles require full payment before entry. No escrow account is otherwise required under Estonian law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the leased office must have a valid fire safety declaration or fire certificate filed with the Rescue Board, fire extinguisher checks, evacuation procedure and staff fire training per the Emergency Preparedness Act. Structural load: the premises must have a lawful occupancy permit (kasutusluba) from the local municipal building authority confirming use as office space. Occupational: the workplace must be registered with the Labour Inspectorate, a risk assessment must be completed, workstation ergonomics and first aid provisions must be in place, and any employees undertaking crew-facing functions must have the required STCW maritime safety awareness certificates.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital; an OÜ may be registered with zero share capital. If the articles set a positive share capital, the subscribed capital must be paid to the company's temporary bank account before registration only if the articles require full payment before entry. No escrow account is otherwise required under Estonian law.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) — central portal e-MTA (e-maksuamet / emta.ee), with company registry filings coordinated through the e-Business Register (Äriregister) and X-Road data exchange.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity may establish a resident OÜ (no statutory minimum capital applies) or a branch in the Commercial Register via the e-Business Register; the registry code automatically issued upon incorporation is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and also serves as the basis for the EE-prefixed VAT number. Non-resident foreign companies that do not incorporate in Estonia may obtain a foreign-person tax code (välismaalase maksukood) from MTA. For VAT, a non-established foreign supplier must apply for VAT registration in the register of taxable persons through e-MTA before commencing taxable supplies in Estonia, with no turnover threshold applicable to non-established persons; an e-Resident ship-crew recruitment agency can complete all steps entirely digitally without physical presence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: Estonian OÜ is taxed at entity level under a deferred, distribution-based corporate income tax system — 0% on retained and reinvested profits (Income Tax Act §§ 50–51), with corporate income tax at 20/80 (i.e., 20% of gross distribution) arising only when profits are distributed via dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, or non-business disbursements. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Estonian OÜ is taxed at entity level under a deferred, distribution-based corporate income tax system — 0% on retained and reinvested profits (Income Tax Act §§ 50–51), with corporate income tax at 20/80 (i.e., 20% of gross distribution) arising only when profits are distributed via dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, or non-business disbursements. Estonia applies a territorial remittance-based scope for resident companies, and the OÜ is not a pass-through entity.

Available election forms

No IRC-style check-the-box regime is available. Available statutory elections include: (i) regular dividend taxation at 20/80 under ITA §50 versus the preferential rate of 14/86 for regular distributions and the §51 additional low-taxed dividend election; (ii) VAT grouping under VAT Act §16, small-trader deregistration election, and OSS/IOSS special schemes for cross-border B2C distance sales; (iii) election to apply the special shipping-tax or tonnage-tax rules only if the company operates qualifying maritime transport, not for crew-recruitment-only activity; (iv) group dividend notices under ITA §50-1(14) enabling parent-level attribution; (v) hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by ATAD implementation, with limited taxpayer-inconsistent characterization adjustments available to avoid double deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 taxable turnover per calendar year for Estonian-established taxable persons; foreign persons not established in Estonia are subject to mandatory registration before their first taxable supply in Estonia with no threshold, except where the OSS/IOSS procedure covers B2C distance sales and intra-EU cross-border goods supplies (the €10,000 pan-EU distance-sales threshold referred to Estonia as the member state of consumption); crew-recruitment B2B services supplied to foreign shipowners or non-established vessel operators are generally outside Estonian VAT scope and therefore do not count toward the turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT if the goods are physically dispatched from Estonian customs territory to a destination outside the EU/EEA and the exporter holds the customs export declaration accepted by the customs office (EX1 / SAD via the Estonian customs system) and the goods leave the territory within 3 months of the supply. Crew-recruitment agency services supplied to a non-established shipowner or foreign vessel operator are outside Estonian VAT scope under the B2B place-of-supply rule (VAT Act §12) rather than zero-rated, with input VAT refundable through the periodic VAT return or the EU refund procedure; services directly connected with sea-going vessels used in international traffic, including supply of stores and fittings, are zero-rated under VAT Act §22(5); intra-Community supplies of goods require the buyer's valid VAT identification number in VIES to apply the 0% EC-supply rate.

  • Accepted customs export declaration (SAD / EX1 electronic customs declaration with Estonian customs export movement reference number)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill evidencing physical departure from EU customs territory with terminal or customs-office stamp
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT/MT103 or bank credit advice) showing full export consideration received, or the zero-rated invoice cross-referencing the export refund claim
  • Export invoice record bearing the explicit statement 'Export — 0% VAT (VAT Act §22)' and the relevant Incoterms
  • Proof-of-export register summary / advance cargo information (ICS) with transport-contract appendices

No mandatory nationwide real-time fiscal e-invoice clearance or digital fiscal stamping applies to standard B2B supplies. Mandatory e-invoicing applies to invoicing the Estonian public sector (B2G) per EU Directive 2014/55/EU via PEPPOL. Businesses must issue an invoice within 7 calendar days of the supply (VAT Act §34). VAT returns, intrastat returns, and customs declarations are filed electronically through e-MTA using authenticated API or X-Road interfaces; excise-sensitive goods require pre-registration in the electronic excise system and may be subject to authorised economic operator digital seals; fully electronic archiving of invoices without real-time CRM/API integration remains compliant.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating/export claims are re-characterized as domestic taxable supplies at 22% with retrospective clawback, default-interest surcharge (currently 0.06% per day as published by MTA), and fines under the Taxation Act of up to 300% of the evaded tax. Conduct involving false documentation, fictitious export, or sham crew-supply chains triggers criminal liability under Penal Code §387 (tax fraud, up to 5 years' imprisonment) and withdrawal of e-MTA user access; repeat violations lead to compulsory deregistration from the VAT register and potential director or management personal liability for unpaid VAT. The documented export and remittance evidence set must exist before the relevant VAT-return filing deadline; if funds are not repatriated within the agreed commercial terms (reference default 180 days) or transport documents are incomplete, the 0% claim is invalidated on audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full euro-area member with complete current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no foreign-exchange controls, no discriminatory transaction taxes, and no approval requirements for import/export of capital, profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or seafarer wage payments. Non-EUR proceeds from crew-management invoices may be held abroad or converted freely. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted under monetary law; only the Estonian Corporate Income Tax Act distribution tax applies as the fiscal condition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio exists for Estonian export receipts. A crew-recruitment agency may invoice in EUR, USD, NOK or any agreed currency and may retain the resulting foreign-currency balance offshore, provided that the underlying transaction is correctly booked and the bank/AML record-keeping requirements are met.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) acting within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; no separate Estonian foreign-exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Passported EU/EEA e-money institutions (EMIs) may lawfully offer multi-currency IBAN payment accounts in Estonia and are viable for crew payroll and travel-card rails because Estonia recognises passporting under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2). However, a pure offshore EMI domiciled outside the EEA cannot lawfully serve as the operating bank without an EEA establishment or a sponsored arrangement; even for EEA EMIs, viability is High only for auxiliary payment flows and Medium for the primary treasury account, since an EMI is not a credit institution, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, may restrict high-value MT103 cover, and is subject to safeguarding rules but not deposit insurance and core lending.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonia has no fixed statutory EUR threshold mandating Local File or Master File; the MTA may require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation during any audit where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 or where officers are located in a low-tax jurisdiction. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for ultimate parent entities with group consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations comparable to the UAE revised ESR framework. However, substance is critical in applying tax treaties, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and anti-abuse provisions. An Estonian OÜ acting as a ship crew recruitment agency must maintain real management premises, personnel records, board decision procedures, recruitment contracts, accounting records, and payroll execution in Estonia. A letterbox company lacking substance is vulnerable to CFC recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and application of Estonian source taxation on gross service fees.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Deemed Distribution Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Deemed distribution tax at 20/80 of the net economic benefit, plus penalty tax up to 20% of the amount due and daily late interest of 0.06%. Administrative fines under the Tax Procedure Act can reach EUR 32,000; criminal tax evasion carries a fine or custodial sentence up to 8 years.

Director personal liability: Board members are jointly and severally liable for tax deficiencies caused by intentional or grossly negligent conduct; travel bans and asset seizures can be imposed on delinquent directors; disqualification from serving on Estonian business boards follows a criminal conviction.
Artificial Transfer Pricing on Crew Management Fees

Statutory sanction: MTA re-adjusts income to arm's length under Income Tax Act §50; the adjustment is recharacterized as a deemed distribution subject to 20/80 tax; late filing penalties apply to amended declarations; fines escalate to EUR 5,000 per incorrect TSD and 20% penalty tax on the underestimated tax amount.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for the additional tax and penalties where the pricing scheme was knowingly implemented; a director may face criminal prosecution for intentional tax understatement and be prohibited from holding board office for up to 3 years.
Letterbox / Shell Company Misuse

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may disregard the taxpayer entity, treat the vessel crew services as an Estonian source trade, and tax the full gross revenue at 20% (or higher applicable withholding) with penalties; the Registrar can compulsorily strike off the company, and an administrative fine of EUR 400 to EUR 10,000 may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for the company's obligations if dissolution occurs without proper liquidation; personal bankruptcy and travel restrictions are possible; e-Residency status and digital ID permissions can be revoked for abuse of the program.
AML Enforcement and Illegal Crew Payroll

Statutory sanction: Back assessment of social tax at 33%, unemployment insurance, funded pension contributions, withholding tax at 20/80, and healthcare-related surcharges. Financial supervision fines reach EUR 100,000 under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act for reporting failures.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for unremitted payroll taxes; criminal money laundering penalties include imprisonment up to 12 years, forfeiture of assets, blanket travel bans precluding escape, and lifetime prohibition from acting as managers of credit institutions or money service businesses.
