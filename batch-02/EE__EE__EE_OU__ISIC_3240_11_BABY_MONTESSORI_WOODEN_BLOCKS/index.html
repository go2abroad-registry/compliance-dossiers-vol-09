Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered-office address in Estonia is mandatory for every OÜ. The address need not be the company's production site, and a contracted virtual-office address provided by a licensed Estonian corporate-services provider is permissible for the formal registered seat, as long as the provider is a regulated service provider and the mailbox receives official mail. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Osaühing; founders may fix share capital at any amount not less than €1 (subscribed in the articles of association). Cash contributions must be paid to a temporary escrow/bank account opened in the company's name before registration; the funds are released to the company upon registration. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); electronic portal: e-MTA at https://e-mta.emta.ee; official public website: www.emta.ee

E-government filing portal

An Osaühing (OÜ) obtains its registry code (8-digit tax identification number, format 1XXXXXXX) automatically upon incorporation through the e-Business Register (e-äriregister). For VAT purposes, a separate VAT number (format EE100XXXXXX) is then applied for via the e-MTA portal once the company is established or first makes taxable supplies. Foreign founders may execute the entire procedure using the Estonian e-Residency digital ID with no minimum capital and no physical presence requirement. Non-resident OÜ shareholders and non-established suppliers may register with MTA through an authorised representative; non-residents must register before the first taxable supply because no domestic turnover threshold applies to them.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Estonia applies a deferred distribution-based corporate income tax: retained profits are taxed at 0%, whereas a 20/80 (effective 25%) tax is imposed on standard profit distributions and a 14/86 (effective 16.28%) rate on regular distributions. Foreign branches of an OÜ benefit from territoriality (exempt profit) unless located in a low-tax jurisdiction. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Estonia applies a deferred distribution-based corporate income tax: retained profits are taxed at 0%, whereas a 20/80 (effective 25%) tax is imposed on standard profit distributions and a 14/86 (effective 16.28%) rate on regular distributions. Foreign branches of an OÜ benefit from territoriality (exempt profit) unless located in a low-tax jurisdiction. The entity is not pass-through eligible.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; tax status is fixed by the legal form in the Commercial Register.; VAT small-enterprise exemption election: permitted for Estonian-established persons until annual taxable turnover reaches EUR 40,000.; VAT voluntary registration election: an OÜ may register for VAT before reaching the statutory EUR 40,000 threshold.; Reduced-rate regular dividend election (14/86) under the Estonian Income Tax Act for recurring profit distributions meeting statutory conditions.; VAT group registration election for affiliated entities (consolidated VAT group via e-MTA).; ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch rules fully transposed; no elective entity classification available for hybrid instruments; advance pricing agreements with MTA available for transfer pricing certainty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year for taxable supplies by persons established in Estonia; no registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers; EUR 10,000 annual threshold for EU distance sales of goods under the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS); non-EU e-commerce deliveries may be declared through the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to: (1) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported from Estonia to non-EU destinations, provided the goods leave the EU within the statutory proof period and transfer of ownership has occurred; (2) EC intra-Community supplies to registered EU VAT numbers when the buyer is established in another Member State and VAT identification is validated; (3) directly related logistics services charged to foreign clients for export goods. MTA requires documented evidence that the risk of loss and control passed to the buyer and that physical exit from the EU is verifiable. For Montessori wooden blocks sold via Amazon Pay and Stripe, PSD2 payment evidence is considered relevant corroboration but not a substitute for customs and transport proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX-1) accepted by MTA customs with movement reference number (MRN) confirming export from the EU
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR (road), or Air Waybill (air) showing the consignee and non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or irrevocable SWIFT MT103 credit confirmation evidencing payment of the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Incoterms contract demonstrating transfer of control and risk
  • Export customs status proof (e-MTA exit confirmation or ECS message) and insurance documents where applicable

Business-to-government (B2G) e-invoicing is mandatory for public sector contracts and is submitted as XML/e-Invoice through e-MTA; VAT returns are filed electronically via e-MTA by the 20th day of the month following the tax period (monthly, or quarterly under the small-enterprise scheme); there is currently no mandatory real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance or digital stamping for B2B transactions; all export invoices and supporting documents must be preserved for 7 years.

There is no Estonian exchange-control statute, but MTA treats a 180-day repatriation of proceeds as the documentary benchmark for good-faith exports; failure to substantiate zero-rating triggers reassessment at 22% VAT with statutory late interest of 0.06% per day, plus a supplementary tax fine of up to 25% of the underpaid amount. Deliberate or fraudulent export misdeclaration exposes the OÜ, its officers, and beneficial owners to criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code (Karistusseadustik) section 390, punishable by a pecuniary sentence or imprisonment up to 5 years, together with retrospective clawback of untaxed VAT, customs offences in relation to false export declarations, and enhanced AML scrutiny under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act for proceeds channelled through Amazon Pay or Stripe payment rails.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Estonian legal tender is the euro (EUR). As a member of the European Union and the euro area, Estonia maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU Articles 63–66). No restrictions exist on inward or outward capital movements, profit remittances, dividends, or repayment of shareholder loans. The only compliance layers are EU sanctions, AML/CFT reporting thresholds, and statistical reporting to Eesti Pank for balance-of-payments purposes. Profit transfer to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted and subject to applicable withholding tax regimes (e.g., 20/80 distribution withholding tax on dividends when not covered by participation exemption) but no FX conversion barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines in Estonia. Export receipts may remain in EUR or any freely convertible currency in resident or non-resident accounts. Only EU/EEA anti-money-laundering record-keeping and Eesti Pank balance-of-payments reporting obligations apply when the transaction value exceeds the statistical threshold (€12,500 for cross-border payments and positions).

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) within the Eurosystem / European Central Bank for monetary policy; Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority) for financial conduct, AML/CFT supervision, and licensing of credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High with legal constraints. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) holding an e-money license under the Second Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and PSD2 (EU) 2015/2366 may operate cross-border in Estonia under passporting. Estonian OÜ may lawfully hold treasury balances with EMIs such as Monese, Payhawk, or other EEA-authorized fintechs, provided the EMI's parent-supervisory authority is within the EEA and the account service includes IBAN SEPA transaction capability. Constraints: EMIs cannot provide commercial credit products (e.g., overdrafts or trade finance loans) unless separately licensed as a credit institution; EMIs are not covered by the Estonian deposit guarantee scheme (because they are not credit institutions); high-risk sectors or product categories (toy manufacturing is low-risk) may still be refused because the EMI's risk appetite is narrower than a Tier-1 bank. Large-volume cross-border trade settlements should be concentrated in full-service commercial banks, not EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan funding from non-resident investors must be wired via SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA or high-value transfers, or via SEPA CT if the sender is located in the European Economic Area. The payment must contain (i) the OÜ's legal name and unique registry code (Estonian register code 1xxxxx); (ii) the account IBAN; (iii) a purpose code aligned with Estonian balance-of-payments statistics: for equity capital injections use 'E' for additional paid-in capital, 'CF' for capital transfer, or 'INVEST'; reference the board resolution and share subscription agreement. The bank will request supporting documents for FX and BoP reporting: audited balance sheet or net asset calculation, base capital contribution decision, proof of identity of the investor, and a source-of-funds declaration. Because Estonia has no capital controls, no central bank approval is required; but Eesti Pank requires statistical reporting for amounts above €12,500, and the bank will submit the BoP form automatically. If the investor is in a high-risk third country, additional Enhanced Due Diligence will apply: proof of corporate existence, UBO certificates, sanctions screening. Optimal routing is a single MT103 from the investor's corporate account to the OÜ's operational account at a major Estonian bank.For payment to suppliers and manufacturers (e.g., raw beechwood, non-toxic dyes, packaging), use SEPA CT for EU/EEA suppliers (no extra costs, EUR, IBAN-to-IBAN) and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA suppliers. For high-value consignments, use an irrevocable commercial documentary letter of credit (LC) issued by the Estonian bank through SWIFT MT700/MT720 to the beneficiary bank, especially when transacting with Asia or non-OECD jurisdictions. For open-account imports, bank-grade SWIFT MT103 with matched invoice and shipping documents (B/L, packing list, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate) will fulfill the bank's 'know-your-transaction' requirement. Export collections from Amazon/PayPal/Stripe arrive via Marketplace clearing rails (ACH/payout batch) and should be swept periodically to the primary operating account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50,000 (total value of cross-border related-party transactions in a tax year)

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but requires genuine economic substance and commercial rationale; anti-abuse rules and CFC provisions apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €1.2 million; criminal prosecution; additional tax plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; reclassification of income; penalty up to €100,000

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for tax debts; potential criminal charges for fraud
AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million; suspension of operations; revocation of license

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fines; imprisonment up to 3 years for willful non-compliance
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalty up to 50% of the additional tax assessed; interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if gross negligence is proven
