Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital since the 2011 amendment; a zero-capital Osaühing may be registered with a stated capital of EUR 0. Where any share capital is subscribed, the entire contribution must be paid into the company's dedicated settlement bank account or, in notarial formation, a notary escrow account before the registration application is filed; the payment certificate is attached to the application. No separate escrow deposit with the Registry is required, and funds become freely available to the company upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace compliance: the Osaühing must carry out an occupational health and safety risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus) and, for any office or warehouse premises, submit a fire-safety business declaration to the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) information system; structural load inspections are required only where physical storage exceeds the building's design loading, and are not applicable to a pure procurement/trading operation with a standard office lease. No civil defense or emergency clearance is required for the scope of cross-border trading without on-site dangerous-goods storage.

Competent tax authority

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian OÜ is a separate CIT taxpayer under the distribution-based regime: undistributed profits are taxed at 0% per annum; tax at 20/80 (effective 25%) is triggered on gross dividend/distribution) with a 14/86 regular reduced rate available; no territorial pass-through election for the OÜ.

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification election; the OÜ is statutorily an entity-level taxpayer. Elections available: (i) the 12-month regular-distribution calculation period and the minimum-distribution test for the 14/86 rate under Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus) §50(2); (ii) option under VAT Act to tax immovable-property transactions and relinquish exemptions; (iii) group VAT registration election for Estonian-option VAT groups; (iv) mandatory ATAD/anti-hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically and cannot be waived; (v) for e-Residency companies, choice of standard articles of association and dividend calendar affects CIT timing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover (calendar year) for local taxable persons; non-residents without a fixed establishment must register from the first taxable supply unless reverse-charge applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available under KMS §31(1) for physical export of battery-grade lithium carbonate powder (HS 2836.91) to a destination outside the EU where: (i) the goods are dispatched/transported out of the EU customs territory by the supplier or by a foreign buyer with no Estonian establishment; (ii) the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of the supply date; (iii) the supplier holds authentic documentary evidence of exit. Services ancillary to the export (e.g., international freight, customs clearance) are likewise zero-rated under KMS §30(2). Claims must be substantiated with the export customs declaration and transport documentation.

  • Customs export declaration lodged as SAD/electronic e-Export message through the MTA national customs system (Export Control System), MRN number and exit confirmation
  • Commercial invoice with HS Code 2836.91, battery-grade purity specification, quantity, FOB/CIF Incoterms and supplier/destination data
  • Packing list showing gross/net weights, container numbers and seals
  • Transport document proving physical exit: Bill of Lading (maritime), CMR (road) or Air Waybill (air), including third-party carrier evidence of arrival outside the EU
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank transfer confirmation evidencing actual payment from the foreign buyer and the absence of undisclosed offsetting arrangements

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate or digital stamping/API clearance system is currently imposed in Estonia. Mandatory B2G e-invoicing applies through the PEPPOL BIS 3.0 national network (e-arve standard) implementing Directive 2014/55/EU. B2B e-invoicing is permitted but not compulsory; all VAT invoices should be issued within 7 days of supply or in line with the KMS, and data transparency is achieved via e-MTA electronic filing and the X-Road interoperability platform.

MTA may audit export zero-rating claims within the general 6-year limitation period and will claw back any VAT zero-rating not supported by the required exit and payment evidence; the assessed VAT (22%) falls due immediately, plus late-payment interest at 0.06% per day under the Taxation Act (MKS) §131. False or fraudulent documentation triggers administrative penalties up to EUR 1,200 under MKS §155, criminal tax evasion liability under Penal Code (Karistusseadustik) §§396-1 (imprisonment up to 5 years for natural persons, criminal fine for legal persons), and customs fines under the Customs Act. The OÜ and its e-Residency directors remain jointly liable within the company's assets.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has adopted the euro as its sole legal tender since 1 January 2011. There are no foreign-exchange control restrictions on current or capital account movements, reflecting full currency convertibility under the EU Treaty provisions for free movement of capital and payments. Residents and non-residents may freely open and operate euro and foreign-currency accounts with licensed banks. Outbound profit remittance is subject only to the Estonian corporate income tax on distributions (generally 20% or 14%-0% for regular profit distributions, depending on the distribution regime), not to exchange-control approval or withholding tax at source. Restrictions arise exclusively from EU autonomous sanctions, targeted financial sanctions, anti-money-laundering/Counter-Terrorist Financing statutes, and the obligation to provide accurate balance-of-payments statistical reports to Eesti Pank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory repatriation regime in Estonia. Exporters are not required to convert export receipts into euro, nor are they subject to any minimum conversion ratio or statutory collection timeline. Foreign-exchange proceeds may be retained on accounts with authorised credit institutions or used directly for trade settlements, subject to standard AML/KYC monitoring and, where applicable, balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Eesti Pank. Supporting trade documentation must be maintained to verify the underlying commercial transaction.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the Estonian central bank and member of the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions have partial viability as additional liquidity rails. They can provide SEPA/euro IBAN accounts and SWIFT-based foreign-currency settlement through PSD2 passporting or an Estonian branch licence. Feasibility is rated Medium-High for EU-regulated EMIs but Low for unregulated or offshore non-EEA digital banks. Many EMIs are cautious with wholesalers of commodities and metals and apply conservative speed-bump limits or decline business sectors involving high-value cross-border trade unless the customer provides full commercial transparency. EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot provide deposit-taking, overdraft, or documentary letters of credit; they remain useful only for pass-through settlement and FX conversion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity and intercompany loan injections into an Estonian OÜ are subject to no exchange-control approval but must be made through a licensed credit institution via SEPA if in euro or via SWIFT MT103 if in USD/CNY or another currency. Payment purpose codes should correspond to Eesti Pank balance-of-payments classification, typically Direct Investment - Equity Capital or Shareholder Loan. The wire message must reference the corporate registration code of the Estonian entity and the underlying board resolution or loan agreement. Banks will perform source-of-funds verification before accepting unusually large capital injections. The Estonian Commercial Register expects share capital contributions to be made to the company's bank account and declared in the registration file; for loan capital, a written shareholder loan agreement in the company's records is critical. No central-bank pre-approval or visa-like capital-control filing is required.Operational disbursements for procurement of battery-grade lithium carbonate are cleared through standard cross-border rails. For ordinary advance payments and pre-payments, SWIFT MT103 wire transfers are the primary rail; for bulk commodity settlements, documentary letters of credit issued by the Estonian bank via SWIFT MT700 and confirmed by the beneficiary's bank provide secure two-party clearing. MT202 messages may be used for interbank funding but not for direct customer reconciliation. EUR payments clear within TARGET2/Single Euro Payments Area; USD payments clear through the US correspondent banking system (Fedwire/CHIPS); CNY payments can be cleared through CIPS or an off-shore CNH corresponding bank. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed profits; 20% on gross profit distributions (20/80 of the net amount paid by the distributing OÜ); 14% reduced rate on regular distributions qualifying under the three-year average rule (14/86 of the net amount).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20/80 of the net distributed amount (20% of gross distribution) levied on the distributing OÜ as the final Estonian source tax; no separate additional dividend withholding tax at the shareholder level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

10th day of the calendar month following the month of the taxable distribution or payment, with simultaneous payment of the assessed tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions with related parties. Master File requirement: MNE group consolidated revenue of at least EUR 50 million. Country-by-Country report threshold: EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has not introduced Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, substance is tested through the place of effective management doctrine, the requirement of genuine Estonian governance, and general anti-avoidance rules. An OÜ that relies solely on e-Residency without actual business premises, local board decision-making, or employment substance is exposed to residence recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, and invocation of Estonian GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deemed Profit Distribution Mismanagement

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment at 20/80 or 14/86; penalty interest of 0.06% per day; tax misdemeanour fine up to EUR 6,400; criminal penalties for intentional evasion under Penal Code section 386, including imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax where evasion results from deliberate concealment, with civil restitution, asset-preservation orders, travel bans on tax debts exceeding EUR 10,000, disqualification from managing companies, and criminal liability in case of fraud.
Shell Entity / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entity as foreign tax resident and denial of Estonia's distribution-based tax regime; compulsory strike-off and dissolution proceedings by the Commercial Register; fines for false annual report declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for company debts where the company trades while insolvent or where the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent misuse; exposure includes civil restitution, court-imposed management prohibition, and extradition risk in cross-border sham-entity cases.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment; additional 20/80 distribution tax on the adjusted deemed profit; penalty interest at 0.06% per day; procedural fines up to EUR 2,600 per documentation request; possible recovery of withholding tax on deemed royalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns containing false transfer pricing disclosures face fines and management disqualification; fraudulent transfer arrangements causing insolvency trigger super-individual liability actions in Estonian courts.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Estonian Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act up to EUR 2.5 million or 10% of annual worldwide turnover for legal persons; freezing of accounts; prohibition on business activities; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors incur personal fines, management prohibitions, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 5 years under Penal Code section 394 for money laundering if they knowingly facilitate or ignore red-flag transactions; civil restitution is available to injured parties and the state.
