Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A fully virtual registered office without any physical presence is generally permitted for an OÜ engaged in the sale of dental clear aligner sheets, provided that the company maintains a registered address in Estonia (which may be a legal service provider's address or a law firm's office) for official receipt of correspondence and legal service of process. However, for this specific industry, the Estonian Health Board (Terviseamet) and the local municipal authority will require a physical place of business, storage, and/or production site if the company physically handles aligner sheets within Estonian territory, including any laboratory or direct-to-consumer fulfillment activity. A virtual office is therefore permissible as the registered seat, but the operational substance (manufacturing, quality control, distribution, and complaint handling) must be either (i) physically located in Estonia, in which case the registered address must reflect that actual operational site, or (ii) fully outsourced to a third-country contract manufacturer, in which case the Estonian entity is a pure holding/marketing company and the virtual office is acceptable subject to economic substance reporting to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board. The Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (RAB) does not classify a virtual-office-based OÜ as inherently high risk, but the AML risk tier for this entity is Low_Risk_Standard only if the business plan demonstrates genuine commercial activity and transparent bank account usage.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under Article 63 TFEU; Estonia maintains no exchange controls, no prior licensing for investments or loans, and no restrictions on profit remittance or external financial flows. The sole operative impediment for a dental-aligner OÜ is the entity-level corporate income tax triggered upon distribution: retained and reinvested profits are untaxed, while dividends and deemed profit distributions are taxed at 20/80 of the net dividend, with a 14/86 reduced rate available when the statutory recurring-distribution test is satisfied. The distribution tax is levied on the OÜ itself and is generally not subject to further withholding at the non-resident shareholder level; a 0% regime applies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive to qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for 12 months. Because Estonian law does not impose exchange-control approvals, the only compliance duties are the bank-automated monthly balance-of-payments reporting and standard anti-money-laundering assessment for high-value transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist in Estonia. Export proceeds from dental clear aligner sheets may be retained indefinitely, un-converted, in USD, CNY, GBP or any trade currency in accounts of the OÜ; there is no statutory conversion ratio, surrender deadline, or central-bank collection mechanism. Practical obligations are limited to: (i) EUR-denominated bookkeeping under the Estonian Accounting Act; (ii) traceable supporting documentation for each export receipt (commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, customs declaration) to satisfy bank AML reviews; and (iii) automatic reporting of cross-border payment flows by the servicing bank to Eesti Pank for balance-of-payments statistics, typically at EUR 12,500 per transaction or monthly cumulative thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the national central bank within the Eurosystem, jointly responsible with the European Central Bank for monetary policy, payment and settlement system oversight, and balance-of-payments statistics. Prudential supervision and AML/CFT surveillance over credit institutions are exercised by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority), with significant banks falling under the ECB's Single Supervisory Mechanism. The Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) administers tax collection but holds no exchange-control mandate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium rating: a supplementary treasury rail, not a standalone substitute for a tier-1 credit institution. EEA-licensed digital EMIs passporting into Estonia under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Monese, Revolut) may open accounts for the OÜ and provide SEPA Instant Credit, card acquiring, and multi-currency IBANs without a physical branch. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution, may not grant overdrafts or issue commercial letters of credit, and its safeguarded client funds are not covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund's EUR 100,000 deposit guarantee; (ii) concentration of treasury through an offshore, non-EEA EMI (e.g., US or Singapore-based) triggers increased KYC scrutiny, loses domestic SEPA direct membership, and incurs correspondent-bank fees; (iii) where Stripe is already the primary platform collections rail, tier-1 banks view a further routing of liquidity through an offshore EMI as a concentration red flag unless monthly reconciliations and licensed-entity evidence are provided. Onboarding is unrestricted under Estonian law, but the EMI must apply the same customer due diligence, and the OÜ must maintain the core bank relationship for tax, trade, and deposit-safety reasons.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity should be routed as a high-value SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer to the OÜ's account at its tier-1 Estonian bank, with the reference field carrying the balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment equity capital, under the Bank of Estonia transactional nomenclature, and the bank-specific purpose tag such as 'Kapitali sissemakse' rather than a goods/services code. The bank requires, before clearing, the share-subscription minutes or an equity-contribution resolution approved by the board, the updated Äriregister extract, and a UBO source-of-funds statement. Where the OÜ is established under the current Estonian Commercial Code regime with no minimum statutory share capital, the subscribed capital amount must nevertheless be recorded accurately and, if a monetary contribution is made, credited to the dedicated capital sub-account and registered in the Commercial Register after receipt. A shareholder loan is a permissible alternative inbound channel, provided it is documented under written loan terms and priced on an arm's-length basis for thin-capitalization and tax-deduction purposes; no FX conversion or authorisation from Eesti Pank is required.Outbound settlements are split by corridor and value. EU and EEA suppliers of thermoplastic polymer raw materials are paid via SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR, including SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) for urgent clears; non-EEA vendors in the United States or China are settled via SWIFT MT103 with the correct trade purpose code, invoice reference, and ISBP-aligned documentary support. Large procurement transactions of thermoforming machinery are best supported by a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700, UCP 600) issued by the Swedish, SEB or LHV trade desk, with the beneficiary negotiating at a confirming bank; this substitutes bank acceptance for vendor credit risk. On the receivables side, Stripe card rails settle in EUR to the operational account for platform orders, while B2B orthodontic clinics remit by SEPA Direct Debit or direct MT103; PEPPOL-aligned e-invoicing facilitates EU B2B and public-sector settlements. EEA-licensed digital EMIs passporting into Estonia under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Monese, Revolut) may open accounts for the OÜ and provide SEPA Instant Credit, card acquiring, and multi-currency IBANs without a physical branch. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution, may not grant overdrafts or issue commercial letters of credit, and its safeguarded client funds are not covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund's EUR 100,000 deposit guarantee; (ii) concentration of treasury through an offshore, non-EEA EMI (e.g., US or Singapore-based) triggers increased KYC scrutiny, loses domestic SEPA direct membership, and incurs correspondent-bank fees; (iii) where Stripe is already the primary platform collections rail, tier-1 banks view a further routing of liquidity through an offshore EMI as a concentration red flag unless monthly reconciliations and licensed-entity evidence are provided. Onboarding is unrestricted under Estonian law, but the EMI must apply the same customer due diligence, and the OÜ must maintain the core bank relationship for tax, trade, and deposit-safety reasons.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution. Undistributed profits are generally not subject to Estonian corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ to a non-resident shareholder. The OÜ generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the distributed profit. Dividends paid to Estonian resident individual shareholders can be subject to separate rules, and the applicable treatment must be checked for the distribution date and shareholder status.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Estonian transfer-pricing documentation is generally linked to the statutory size thresholds: annual turnover of at least EUR 50 million, balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43 million, or an average of at least 250 employees, together with specified related-party transactions. Documentation can also be required for transactions with persons in low-tax jurisdictions and in other prescribed circumstances. For multinational groups, the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold is relevant to country-by-country reporting. The local file and master file must be prepared in accordance with Estonian rules and OECD principles where applicable; there is no universal EUR transaction-value threshold that replaces the statutory tests.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the regimes of certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, Estonian anti-avoidance rules, the general anti-abuse principle, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, interest-limitation rules, treaty anti-abuse standards, and EU anti-hybrid rules apply. The OÜ should maintain real decision-making capacity, appropriate directors, commercial premises or operational infrastructure where warranted, personnel or outsourced functions with demonstrable control, operational records, bank-account control, inventory and quality systems, and evidence that aligner manufacturing and management decisions occur where represented. e-Residency alone is not tax residence, economic substance, or a permanent-establishment safe harbor.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and concealed distributions

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid corporate income tax, statutory late-payment interest, recovery costs, administrative fines, and potential criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion or submission of materially false information.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax liability where they intentionally or negligently cause the company to fail to pay tax, subject to statutory conditions. Criminal proceedings may result in fines or imprisonment. Civil damages, enforcement measures, and restrictions on management may also arise; travel bans are not automatic and require a separate legal basis.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and related-party mispricing

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment based on arm's-length pricing, late-payment interest, documentation and filing penalties, and possible denial of unsupported deductions or treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for losses caused by intentional or negligent breaches of management duties. Criminal liability requires proof of a separate offense, such as intentional tax evasion, false accounting, or fraud; ordinary TP error does not automatically create imprisonment.
Permanent establishment and undeclared Estonian activity

Statutory sanction: Additional corporate tax, payroll taxes, VAT, social taxes, interest, penalties, registration obligations, and possible tax assessments in the affected jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate or grossly negligent non-compliance under applicable tax and company-law rules. Cross-border enforcement, asset restraint, and travel restrictions depend on the relevant jurisdiction and judicial process.
VAT fraud and intra-EU trade misreporting

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, repayment of improperly refunded VAT, late-payment interest, administrative penalties, loss of VAT registration or deduction rights, and criminal exposure for fraudulent conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax or civil liability in cases of intentional or culpable conduct and criminal sanctions for fraud. A company shield does not protect directors from liability for their own unlawful acts.
Medical-device and product-safety non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Market-surveillance orders, corrective action, withdrawal or recall, prohibition of supply, administrative fines, product liability claims, and potential criminal sanctions for serious or intentional breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal criminal or civil exposure where they knowingly place unsafe or non-compliant devices on the market. Corporate limited liability does not immunize individuals from product-safety offenses or personal negligence.
AML, beneficial-ownership, and sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines, transaction blocking, account termination, confiscation or freezing of assets, reporting obligations, and criminal sanctions for money laundering or sanctions evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may face personal fines, criminal prosecution, imprisonment, and asset confiscation. Travel restrictions may be imposed by a competent authority or court where legally justified; they are not an automatic consequence of an AML finding.
False accounting and unsupported manufacturing records

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting and reporting penalties, civil claims, loss of financing or regulatory approvals, and criminal liability for falsification, fraud, or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and accounting personnel may be personally liable for intentional false reporting, damages, and criminal penalties, including imprisonment where the statutory elements are met.
Improper profit repatriation and shareholder financing

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as a taxable distribution or benefit, corporate income tax, withholding tax where applicable, interest, penalties, denial of deductions, and possible debt-equity reclassification.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unlawful distributions, insolvency-related payments, breach of fiduciary duties, and resulting creditor or shareholder losses. Personal imprisonment requires proof of a separate criminal offense.
Insolvency and creditor-prejudice conduct

Statutory sanction: Avoidance of transactions, restitution, insolvency proceedings, director disqualification, damages, and criminal sanctions for prohibited insolvency conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for creditor losses and unpaid obligations caused by a breach of insolvency duties. Courts may impose management restrictions and, for criminal conduct, fines or imprisonment.
