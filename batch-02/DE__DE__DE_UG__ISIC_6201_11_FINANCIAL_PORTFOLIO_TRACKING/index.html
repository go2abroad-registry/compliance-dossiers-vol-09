Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent local Amtsgericht acting as Commercial Register court (Handelsregister), determined by the company's registered office; incorporation filings are submitted electronically by a German notary.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), particularly sections 2 (notarial form), 3 (articles), 5a (UG), 7 (registration prerequisites), 8 (registration application), 10 (register entry), and 11 (pre-registration liability). Commercial Register procedures are governed by the Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung, HRV).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for entry in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister), electronically filed by the notarizing notary, together with the notarized articles of association or the notarized simplified-procedure formation protocol.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG may be formed with stated share capital of at least EUR 1. The full nominal amount of each share must be paid in cash before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. No statutory escrow account is required. The company must retain one quarter of its annual net profit, after any loss carryforward, in the statutory reserve until the reserve and share capital reach the GmbH minimum capital threshold. Notarial and court fees are separately payable and vary with the transaction and applicable fee schedules; no fixed USD state filing fee applies.

Funding & unlock path: No sector-specific fire, structural-load, or industrial workplace clearance applies to ordinary office or cloud-software operations. Premises must comply with applicable building-use, fire-safety, workplace, and occupational health and safety requirements. Any fit-out, change of use, server facility, or material structural alteration may require approval or inspection by the competent local building, fire, and occupational safety authorities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT ID and customs matters; local tax office (Finanzamt) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and payroll tax registration; national e-filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned UG: after notarial incorporation, the managing director files the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) with the locally competent Finanzamt via ELSTER or in paper form. The Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (corporate tax number) and requests an Umgründung/TIN as needed. Separately, the VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-ID) is applied for electronically from BZSt (usually within 2-6 weeks). If the UG engages in cross-border goods movement or customs procedures, it must obtain an EORI number from the Generalzolldirektion. All tax number changes, branch registrations, and VAT registration updates are made through the same Finanzamt/BZSt channels.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under German tax law; a UG is irrevocably classified as a capital company subject to corporate income tax, trade tax, and solidarity surcharge (abolished for most periods). Available elections include: (i) Organschaft (corporate group and VAT consolidation) where a parent holds the majority of voting rights and controls the UG; (ii) VAT option to tax otherwise exempt real estate transactions under section 9 UStG; (iii) transitional Kleinunternehmer (small business) VAT code election if turnover thresholds are met; (iv) anti-hybrid mismatch elections are not elective — German law applies mandatory rules under section 4k EStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration of resident commercial companies; any economic activity triggers Finanzamt registration and VAT compliance. A small-business (Kleinunternehmer) exemption may apply under section 19 UStG if the previous calendar year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and the current year's projected turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; however, a UG formed as a commercial capital company is generally ineligible to apply the exemption in practice because its statutory purpose is commercial and shareholders usually project taxable turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A German exporter may zero-rate export of goods under sections 4(1)(1) and 6 UStG if the goods physically leave the EU in the name of the supplier. The prerequisite is proof of dispatch or transport to a destination outside the EU (e.g., Gelangensbestätigung, customs export declaration, transport documents). For B2B services supplied to a non-EU customer (including offshore financial software and portfolio tracking SaaS), the place of supply is the customer's country, so the supply is outside German VAT scope (reverse-charge or no German VAT) provided the recipient is an entrepreneur and the benefit occurs in that jurisdiction. Zero-rating/out-of-scope treatment also requires that adequate commercial accounting records identify the foreign recipient, the service nature, payment flow, and residency evidence.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / Export Accompanying Document)
  • Commercial invoice in German or English
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Gelangensbestätigung (proof of arrival) or Ausfuhr-/Abholbestätigung
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment evidence for export proceeds (where applicable by bank review)

Germany imposes mandatory B2B e-invoicing: from 1 January 2025, all resident taxpayers must be able to receive electronic invoices formatted as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X; from 1 January 2027, issuers with annual turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue such e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all B2B taxable supplies are mandated. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, API pre-clearance, or digital stamping requirement in Germany. Invoices must be secured for ten years; invoice data must be submitted periodically via VAT return (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung) through ELSTER.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment as German domestic VAT (likely 19% or 16% in force during transition periods), plus interest under section 233a AO running from the original return date. Misstatements can attract penalties under sections 240 and 378 AO, and intentional evasion constitutes criminal tax evasion under section 370 AO punishable by up to five years' imprisonment or fine. The tax office may disallow input VAT deductions, impose late payment surcharges (1% per month up to 25%), and issue retroactive assessments within four years for simple negligence, seven years for serious neglect, and ten years for intentional evasion. Additional liability may extend to corporate officers under section 69 AO for breach of statutory duties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany grants full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions within the European Economic Area and against most third countries. EUR is the domestic legal tender; there is no general exchange control permit for profit remittances or dividend distribution from a German UG. Legal hurdles are limited to corporate law (balance-sheet solvency and profit availability) and tax law. Profit distributions to a non-resident shareholder may be subject to German withholding tax on dividends (Kapitalertragsteuer plus solidarity surcharge, generally 26.375%, subject to treaty relief or EU parent-subsidiary directive benefits) but not to FX conversion certification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Germany. A German resident exporter is permitted to retain export proceeds indefinitely in any foreign currency and is not required to convert them into EUR. The only material compliance duty is statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border cash payments and payments received from non-residents when the individual payment exceeds EUR 12,500; such reporting is informational only and does not impose any conversion ratio or mandatory repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, as Germany's central bank and the monetary authority of the euro area; with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as the competent banking and financial services supervisor. Foreign exchange and cross-border payment reporting are administered by the Deutsche Bundesbank under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV), while capital movement restrictions are governed by EU free-movement-of-capital principles.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium legal viability for cross-border digital EMIs that hold a valid European Economic Area electronic money institution licence and that passport into Germany through the BaFin register. Offshore unlicensed EMIs are not viable for core treasury use because they cannot provide a German IBAN, are outside BaFin/MaRisk oversight, and attract severe AML/CFT audit and bank-correspondent rechallenge risk. An EEA-licensed EMI may be used as a second-layer disbursement rail, but the primary treasury account should remain at a German prompt-payment and statutory deposit-protection institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be executed by wire transfer from the shareholder's own account through SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer, using an IBAN of the UG's German bank account. The wire narrative should state 'Kapitalerhöhung UG' for equity recapitalisation or 'Darlehen Gesellschafter' for a shareholder loan, and must not be commingled with trade receivables. The receiving German bank will assign the applicable Deutsche Bundesbank statistical payment code on its AWV reporting; the resident beneficiary must ensure that any cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 is reported to the Bundesbank within seven calendar days of receipt. Verification should include the notarial capital-increase certificate, the updated shareholder register, a board resolution approving the capital measure, and the contributing shareholder's UBO and tax-residence documentation.For software-licence royalties, subcontractor fees, cloud-service purchases, and app-store operational expenses, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA payments and SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA EUR payments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 (cross-border related-party transactions) or EUR 1,000,000 (domestic transactions) triggers documentation. Master File/CbC reporting if group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: No standalone ESR; Germany applies § 42 AO general anti-abuse rules and § 50d(3) EStG. An UG lacking business substance, staff, and operational risk while being part of a shell invoicing scheme will be recharacterized and denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under § 42 AO; income allocated to actual beneficial owner; assessment plus interest; criminal § 370 AO imprisonment up to 5 years or fine.

Director personal liability: Personal enforcement via § 69/§ 71 AO; Haftungsbescheid; civil restitution; travel ban if criminal investigation is opened and flight risk exists.
Verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung (Hidden Profit Distribution)

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as deemed dividend; 26.375% WHT assessed retroactively; interest under § 233a; penalties up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: MD liable for unwithheld WHT under §§ 44, 71 AO; criminal sanction under § 369-370 AO; personal joint liability covers shortfall.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Surcharge under § 162(4) AO of 10% of the positive adjustment (minimum EUR 10,000; maximum EUR 1,000,000), plus late interest.

Director personal liability: Directors have a duty of proper accounting; failure may lead to § 34/69 AO personal liability; if evasion is deliberate, imprisonment and confiscation of benefit.
AML / UBO Non-Compliance in Financial Flows

Statutory sanction: Fines under GwG up to EUR 1 million (serious cases up to 10% of annual turnover); statutory registration embargo and loss of tax benefits if undeclared beneficial owner; criminal money-laundering charges under StGB § 261.

Director personal liability: Director's personal responsibility under § 80 GwG; supervisory failures give rise to personal fines and damage claims; in cross-border cases, provisional extradition arrest may be imposed.
