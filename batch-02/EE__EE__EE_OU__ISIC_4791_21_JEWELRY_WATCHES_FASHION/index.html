Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Estonian e-Business Register (e-Äriregister): https://ariregister.rik.ee/

Name reservation / lock period

Estonia does not generally operate a separate statutory company-name reservation period comparable to a 30-day reservation system. The applicant should search the e-Business Register, trademark databases, domain records, and relevant regulated-sector registers, then submit the incorporation application promptly. If the registrar rejects the name or the registration application, obtain the written decision and legal grounds, correct the name or supporting evidence, and resubmit through the e-Business Register where permitted. A registrar decision may be challenged through the statutory court or administrative-review route stated in the decision, generally by filing a reasoned appeal within the applicable statutory deadline. The appeal should identify the challenged decision, demonstrate name distinctiveness and legality, attach registry and trademark evidence, and include certified translations where relevant. The exact deadline and forum must be taken from the rejection decision and the applicable Commercial Register Act procedure; do not assume a 30-day period.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, 'MTA'); official online portal: e-MTA (e-teenused) at www.emta.ee; incorporation and TIN issuance through the Commercial Register (Äriregister) e-notary service, including e-Residency remote authentication for non-resident founders.

E-government filing portal

The OÜ's nine-digit Commercial Register registry code is the statutory TIN. After incorporation, the e-Residency digital-ID holder or authorized local manager activates the company in the e-MTA portal and submits the VAT registration application (Form K). Non-resident owners must also file beneficial-ownership declarations with the Commercial Register. A foreign entity without an Estonian registered office that engages in taxable Estonian supplies must register for VAT with the MTA before commencing activity and is generally required to appoint a local fiscal representative; alternatively, non-established suppliers may use the EU OSS/IOSS channels to avoid domestic VAT registration for distance sales.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation under Estonia's deferred/distributive corporate income tax (CIT): the OÜ is the taxpayer and CIT at 20% (20/80 of net distribution) arises only upon distribution or deemed distribution of profits, including dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, non-business expenses, and transfer-pricing adjustments. Retained and reinvested earnings are untaxed indefinitely. A resident OÜ is taxed on distributions from all sources on a worldwide-income-distribution basis, with credit for foreign withholding taxes; dividends benefiting from the EU parent-subsidiary exemption are excluded when conditions are met. — The OÜ's nine-digit Commercial Register registry code is the statutory TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation under Estonia's deferred/distributive corporate income tax (CIT): the OÜ is the taxpayer and CIT at 20% (20/80 of net distribution) arises only upon distribution or deemed distribution of profits, including dividends, fringe benefits, gifts, non-business expenses, and transfer-pricing adjustments. Retained and reinvested earnings are untaxed indefinitely. A resident OÜ is taxed on distributions from all sources on a worldwide-income-distribution basis, with credit for foreign withholding taxes; dividends benefiting from the EU parent-subsidiary exemption are excluded when conditions are met.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists; all OÜs are automatically entity-level CIT taxpayers. Available statutory elections include: (i) full management control over distribution timing, which defers the CIT event; (ii) the reduced 14/86 withholding rate for 'regular dividends' up to the statutory three-year average distribution base paid to resident individuals; (iii) depreciation method elections (straight-line vs. declining-balance) for eligible fixed assets; (iv) inventory valuation and write-off elections under the Income Tax Act; (v) ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch rules apply non-electively, but taxpayers may rely on the statutory carve-outs for non-structured arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover per calendar year for resident or established taxable persons, triggering mandatory VAT registration from the first euro of excess turnover (deregistration applies if the EUR 40,000 ceiling will not be reached); the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies to cross-border distance sales of jewelry and watches to consumers in other Member States, above which OSS or destination-state registration is required; non-established foreign suppliers have no de minimis and must register before the first taxable Estonian supply unless the transaction is covered by OSS/IOSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies where: (i) goods are physically dispatched or transported by the seller or on the seller's behalf from Estonian or other EU customs territory to a destination outside the EU, with departure within the statutory three-month window (or an MTA-approved extension); (ii) intra-EU B2B dispatches to a buyer holding a valid VAT identification number in another Member State, supported by EC Sales List entries; and (iii) certain ancillary services directly connected to exported goods, such as international transport and loading. Online B2C distance sales of fashion jewelry and fine timepieces to EU consumers do not qualify for zero-rating: below the EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold the Estonian 22% VAT applies; above the threshold, or at the seller's option, OSS returns at destination Member State rates. Third-country imports of consignments up to EUR 150 are declared under IOSS at the point of sale, and marketplace deemed-supplier rules may shift VAT collection liability to platforms facilitating the sale.

  • Customs export declaration (Export Accompanying Document/EAD or EXS electronic export record) evidencing the goods' exit from EU customs territory and the non-EU destination.
  • Transport document — bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or airway bill — naming the consignor and consignee and proving dispatch to the foreign buyer within the statutory export period.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract or webstore order record, and payment settlement evidence (Stripe/PayPal or SWIFT listings) identifying the non-resident customer and the transaction price.
  • Corroborating VAT records for intra-EU dispatches (VAT ID/VIES confirmations, EC Sales List and Intrastat returns) and, for third-country exports, warehouse or carrier movement logs supporting the outbound journey.

Estonia does not operate a real-time state clearance or digital stamping system (no SdI-style mandate). Statutory B2B e-invoicing is phased in: from 1 January 2025, taxable persons with annual turnover exceeding EUR 10 million must issue e-invoices to domestic B2B customers; from 1 January 2026 all other taxable persons are obliged to do so. E-invoices must be EN 16931/PEPPOL-compliant and exchanged through registered e-invoice operators or compliant ERP/PEPPOL access points; buyers must confirm e-invoice receipt capability. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, Intrastat, and OSS/IOSS filings are submitted electronically via e-MTA by the 20th of the following month; although no transaction-level clearance trigger exists, PSP payment data from Stripe and PayPal is reportable under EU CESOP rules for fraud-risk matching and cross-border e-commerce audit targeting.

Estonia imposes no standalone foreign-exchange repatriation filing, but the 180-day benchmark anchors the exporter's documentary integrity horizon. MTA audits require the full export documentation bundle; missing, contradictory, or untimely departure evidence causes the supply to be recharacterized as domestic and assessed at 22%, with a retrospective clawback plus late-payment interest of 0.06% per day (approximately 21.9% per annum) from the original due date. Fabricated exports, circular or phoenix-trading schemes, and export-invoice fraud trigger tax-fraud proceedings under Penal Code Section 403, with fines and imprisonment of up to five years for large-scale evasion; assessment limitation periods are extended for deliberate concealment, and company managers and VAT agents may incur joint and several liability. The MTA cross-matches CESOP PSP data, customs exit records, and e-commerce refund patterns, focusing on jewelry/watch distance-selling networks and related-party reinvoicing arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts under the EU legal order (Articles 63-66 TFEU); euro adoption since 1 January 2011; no exchange-control statutes, no prior-authorization for inbound or outbound capital movements, no limits on dividend remittance or trade settlement; EUR transfers within SEPA are unrestricted; USD/GBP conversions occur at market rates without registration or declaration; the 20/80 effective rate on distributed profits and the 14/86 rate on regular distributions are tax frictions only, not FX convertibility obstacles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonian law imposes zero export-proceeds surrender, zero mandatory conversion ratio, and zero repatriation deadline. A jewelry merchant may retain USD/GBP in multi-currency accounts, match receipts directly against supplier invoices, or convert to EUR at the prevailing commercial rate without notification to Eesti Pank.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; prudential supervision by Finantsinspektsioon (Financial Supervision Authority); AML/CFT oversight by the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) under the Police and Border Guard Board.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File arise when cross-border controlled transactions with related parties exceed EUR 250,000 in aggregate during a tax period; additional Country-by-Country reporting obligation arises where the multinational group's consolidated turnover is at least EUR 750 million

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no formal Economic Substance Regulations checklist for a standard OÜ, but general anti-avoidance rules and the OECD Principal Purpose Test require genuine business substance and local decision-making; an OÜ that lacks employees, operational activities, or business functions in Estonia, particularly in the e-Residency context, may be treated as a shell and denied any treaty or EU-law benefit

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Hidden or Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment equal to the profit-distribution tax on the hidden distribution, plus interest at 0.06% per day, monetary penalty of up to 2.5 times the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud where the amount exceeds the criminal threshold; fines in court may reach EUR 8 million for legal persons in severe cases

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in hiding distributions is jointly liable for the underpaid tax; Estonian courts can pierce the corporate veil, impose civil damages equal to the tax shortfall, issue travel bans, and sentence individuals to up to four years' imprisonment for tax fraud
Shell Company / e-Residency Misuse

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as a non-resident PE or sham, retroactive taxation of income at standard rates, denial of tax treaty benefits, and administrative fine up to EUR 5,000; criminal charges for false filings may follow

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax under the Tax Code when control was exercised in bad faith; travel bans and disqualification from serving as a director are imposed by court order
AML and Sanctions Compliance – High-Value Online Retail

Statutory sanction: AML supervisory fines of up to EUR 3.2 million for a corporate entity; revocation of the e-money/payment authorisation of the payment processor arrangement; penal interest on any associated undeclared income; criminal liability for money laundering up to EUR 1.6 million fine and imprisonment of the managerial team

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal administrative fines up to EUR 160,000, direct civil claims from the Financial Intelligence Unit for damages, and up to six years' imprisonment for willful AML violations
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Correction of the taxable base and additional distribution tax of 20% on the adjusted amount, late-payment interest, and a transfer-pricing penalty of up to 50% of the additional tax assessed; denial of expense deductions if documentation is not produced

Director personal liability: The director who signs a materially incorrect transfer-pricing certificate is subject to a personal fine up to EUR 6,400; in severe deliberate cases, joint and several civil liability and criminal prosecution for tax fraud up to four years' imprisonment apply
