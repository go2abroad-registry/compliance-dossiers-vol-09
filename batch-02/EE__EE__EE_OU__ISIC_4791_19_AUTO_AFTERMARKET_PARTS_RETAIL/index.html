Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Business Register (Eesti Äriregister), operationally administered by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus, RIK) within the administrative area of the Ministry of Justice; registrations are effected electronically through the e-Business Register portal (e-äriregister) at ariregister.rik.ee.

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Ariseadustik), Chapter 4 on private limited companies (Osaühing, OÜ), Sections 135-192, in particular Sections 136 (share capital), 138-141 (foundation, application and entry) and 148-150 (management board, representation); supplemented by the registry provisions of the General Part of the Civil Code Act (Tsiviilseadustiku uldosa seadus), the State Fees Act (Riigilõivuseadus) and eIDAS Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 on electronic signatures.

Formation / constitutional filings

Standardised incorporation application (Kandevadlus) submitted electronically, together with the digitally signed foundation resolution (Asutamisotsus), Articles of Association (Põhikiri), consent of the management board member (Juhatuse liikme nõusolek) and the shareholders' register (Osanike nimekiri).

No statutory minimum paid-up capital: the former EUR 2,500 floor was abolished by the Commercial Code amendment effective 1 January 2024, and an OÜ may be registered with zero share capital. No escrow or blocked bank account is required for e-Residency online incorporation; if any capital is payable post-registration, the foundation decision fixes the payment date and the receivable is recorded in the shareholders' register. An OÜ exceeding the turnover, balance-sheet or employee thresholds of the Auditors Activities Act is subject to mandatory audit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, EMTA); e-MTA portal at emta.ee

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders may incorporate an OÜ through the Estonian e-Business Register, including using e-Residency digital identity; the company receives an 8-digit Estonian business registry code (TIN-equivalent). A foreign non-established company must separately apply to EMTA through e-MTA for VAT registration before making taxable supplies in Estonia, submitting registry extracts, proof of foreign VAT status, and appointing an Estonian tax representative when required. EMTA then issues an Estonian VAT identification number in the format EE + 9 digits.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign shareholders may incorporate an OÜ through the Estonian e-Business Register, including using e-Residency digital identity; the company receives an 8-digit Estonian business registry code (TIN-equivalent). A foreign non-established company must separately apply to EMTA through e-MTA for VAT registration before making taxable supplies in Estonia, submitting registry extracts, proof of foreign VAT status, and appointing an Estonian tax representative when required. EMTA then issues an Estonian VAT identification number in the format EE + 9 digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Estonia. An OÜ is automatically treated as a separate resident legal entity. Tax elections are limited to timing of profit distributions under the Estonian corporate income tax system, VAT group registration, and certain group arrangements; hybrid mismatch adjustments and CFC rules under ATAD are mandatory rather than elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover in Estonia during a consecutive 12-month period; however, a foreign non-established seller generally has no threshold and must register before the first taxable domestic supply, subject to EU distance-selling/OSS rules for cross-border B2C supplies above EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories from Estonia to non-EU destinations qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union and documentary evidence proves their departure from Estonia. The exporter must hold a valid export declaration, transport documentation, and payment evidence, and the consideration should be received from the non-resident buyer within the statutory or commercially required foreign-exchange repatriation period.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/ECS export movement) showing EE export and final third-country destination
  • Bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill evidencing movement outside the European Union
  • Inward remittance certificate, bank statement, or matching payment evidence reflecting receipt of export proceeds

Estonia does not operate a universal real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Registered taxpayers must file VAT returns electronically through e-MTA, and structured XML e-invoices are standard for B2B and mandatory for B2G sales. EMTA supports API-based data exchange and machine-readable e-invoice transmission, but there is no continuous transaction control reporting requirement.

EMTA may retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT and associated input VAT if export proof is incomplete or not retained, with tax arrears interest of 0.06% per day and an additional tax evasion penalty of up to 30%, or 50% for repeat violations. Fraudulent or fictitious export documentation may trigger criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code, including fines and imprisonment for up to five years, director-level liability, and extension of the assessment period to five years for intentional evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area and EEA/EU member with fully liberalised capital movements under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There is no foreign-exchange-control statute, no capital-account vs current-account approval regime, and no convertibility restriction on profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, share capital returns, or inward equity injections. The only practical remittance prerequisites are corporate and tax formalities: a valid shareholder/board decision, audited annual accounts if statutory thresholds are exceeded, and application of the Estonian Income Tax Act at the moment of distribution, with possible mitigation under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive provisions or an applicable double tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. An auto-parts e-commerce exporter may retain export receipts in any currency, hold them in multi-currency bank accounts, use them directly for foreign suppliers, or convert them freely without central-bank permission. Continuing obligations are limited to ordinary trade documentation, statistical reporting (Intrastat/customs thresholds), transfer-pricing and accounting records, and bank-side balance-of-payments reporting to Eesti Pank for large cross-border payment flows.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia); AML/CFT supervision is exercised by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority) and Rahapesu Andmebüroo (Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-to-High only as an ancillary clearing rail. A PSD2-licensed Estonian e-money institution or an EEA-passported EMI may lawfully hold client funds in safeguarded accounts, operate SEPA payment accounts, issue corporate cards, and provide FX conversion for operational balances. The legal constraint is that an e-money institution is not a credit institution and cannot take deposits, hold the corporate treasury as a deposit, issue commercial letters of credit, or serve as the primary custodian of share capital and escrow amounts. Consequently, EMIs are viable for payment routing and marketplace payouts, but the main treasury backbone must remain a licensed commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: an inbound SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer in EUR from the shareholder's personal or corporate account with the remitter name matching the shareholder; beneficiary details matching the OÜ's Commercial Registry name and designated Estonian bank account; and a remittance narrative clearly stating 'equity contribution' or 'share capital deposit' with the bank-assigned treasury purpose category for capital movements. The onboarding bank may require a board resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and a completed Eesti Pank balance-of-payments questionnaire; the bank files statistical cross-border flow reports on the client's behalf. For shareholder loan funding, a written loan agreement must be executed and the payment marked 'shareholder loan disbursement' to avoid reclassification as a distributable equity item.Primary clearing rail is SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant in EUR for EU/EEA suppliers, logistics providers, and warehouse payments; SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers are used for non-EEA and non-EUR counterparties in the US, UK, China, and emerging sourcing markets, with FX conversion executed at the market mid-rate. For high-value container purchases or bulk inventory orders, the clearing structure is supplemented by UCP600-compliant commercial letters of credit issued by an Estonian commercial bank, with invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and customs entry verification. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% corporate income tax on distributed profits, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution from 1 January 2025. Undistributed and reinvested profits are generally not subject to Estonian corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% Estonian withholding tax is generally imposed on dividends paid by an Estonian resident OÜ to a resident or non-resident shareholder. The distributing OÜ generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the net distribution. A EUR 78 net dividend normally produces EUR 22 of Estonian corporate income tax, subject to applicable exemption rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD and applicable annexes are generally due by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the payment or taxable event occurred. The annual report is generally due within six months after the end of the financial year, subject to the Commercial Code rules and the entity's reporting period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonia uses the euro. Local File documentation is generally relevant for qualifying taxpayers, including a company meeting at least one prescribed size criterion such as annual sales revenue of at least EUR 50 million, balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43 million or average number of employees of at least 250, as well as certain regulated, listed or otherwise specifically covered taxpayers. Master File obligations generally apply to qualifying Estonian entities within multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million, subject to Estonian implementing rules. Even below formal thresholds, related-party transactions must be priced at arm's length and sufficiently documented.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the UAE ESR framework. Substance remains relevant under Estonian transfer-pricing rules, tax-residence and permanent-establishment principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, general anti-avoidance rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules and treaty principal-purpose tests. An OÜ conducting online automotive-parts retail should maintain genuine commercial activity, appropriate management and decision-making, accurate inventory and logistics records, Estonia-linked accounting and banking controls where commercially appropriate, arm's-length related-party contracts, and evidence that Stripe, PayPal, warehousing, fulfilment, marketing and intellectual-property arrangements reflect actual functions and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, statutory interest, administrative penalties and recovery costs. Serious intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offence under the Penal Code and may result in a fine or imprisonment; a legal person may receive a criminal monetary punishment within the statutory limits.

Director personal liability: A management-board member may incur personal liability where the member intentionally or through gross negligence causes the company's tax debt or fails to perform statutory tax duties. Criminal liability may attach to the responsible individual. Civil recovery, enforcement restrictions and a prohibition on leaving Estonia may arise only where ordered or permitted under the relevant enforcement or criminal-procedure rules; none is automatic.
VAT carousel, undeclared distance sales and payment-platform mismatch

Statutory sanction: Back-assessed VAT, interest, penalties, denial of input-tax deductions, possible joint-liability measures and investigation for tax fraud where concealment is intentional. VAT obligations may also arise under EU distance-selling and One Stop Shop rules.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal tax-debt liability for intentional or grossly negligent conduct and criminal exposure for fraudulent VAT arrangements. Payment processors, banks and tax authorities may suspend accounts or conduct enhanced verification.
Disguised dividend and unlawful profit distribution

Statutory sanction: 22/78 corporate income tax, possible fringe-benefit or non-business-expense taxation, interest, penalties, repayment claims under the Commercial Code and potential invalidity or clawback of unlawful distributions.

Director personal liability: Management-board members may be required to compensate the company or creditors for unlawful distributions and may be jointly liable where statutory distribution restrictions were breached. Deliberate asset stripping may constitute a criminal offence.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and related-party extraction

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to arm's-length profit, additional corporate income tax or other applicable tax, interest, documentation penalties and possible secondary tax consequences. Treaty benefits may be denied under beneficial-ownership or principal-purpose rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for knowingly approving non-arm's-length arrangements, inadequate records or fraudulent invoices. Repeated intentional conduct may support criminal tax-evasion proceedings.
Shell company, beneficial ownership and AML failure

Statutory sanction: Suspension or termination of banking and payment services, reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit, administrative penalties, account restrictions, confiscation or freezing measures, and criminal sanctions for money laundering or related offences.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and beneficial owners may face criminal prosecution, asset seizure, confiscation and imprisonment. Travel restrictions may be imposed by competent authorities in criminal proceedings or enforcement, but are not an automatic consequence of e-residency or ordinary tax non-compliance.
Accounting, annual-report and corporate-governance failure

Statutory sanction: Official precepts, fines, forced filing or deletion-related measures, tax reassessment, creditor claims and potential insolvency proceedings. Failure to file annual reports can lead to a warning, penalty and eventual deletion from the Commercial Register under applicable procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for damages caused by failure to maintain accounting, failure to file for bankruptcy when required, or continuation of payments while insolvent. Serious concealment of insolvency or creditor prejudice may result in criminal liability.
Unlicensed or non-compliant online retail conduct

Statutory sanction: Product withdrawal, sales prohibition, administrative fines, consumer claims, trademark or copyright damages, customs measures and possible criminal liability for counterfeit or unsafe goods. Ordinary online retail of lawful replacement parts generally requires no special corporate tax licence, but product-specific obligations remain applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate unlawful trading, counterfeit distribution, consumer deception or failure to respond to known safety risks. Criminal sanctions may include fines or imprisonment depending on the offence and scale.
