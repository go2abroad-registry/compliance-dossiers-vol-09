Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authorities are the locally responsible Finanzamt for corporate income tax, trade tax administration, and VAT registration; the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office, BZSt) issues the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). Electronic tax filings and tax questionnaires are generally submitted through the ELSTER platform (www.elster.de). The German tax number (Steuernummer) is assigned by the Finanzamt. Germany does not generally issue a separate corporate TIN equivalent to the personal Steuer-ID; the Steuernummer and, where applicable, the USt-IdNr. are the principal business tax identifiers.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must be registered with the competent local Finanzamt after incorporation. The procedure generally comprises: (1) incorporation and commercial-register registration; (2) submission of the electronic Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung through ELSTER; (3) provision of the notarized formation documents, commercial-register details, registered office, business activities, beneficial-owner information, bank details, expected turnover, expected profit, and requested tax treatments; (4) issuance of the Steuernummer by the Finanzamt; and (5) separate application for the USt-IdNr. to the BZSt, normally through the BZSt online application or through the tax-registration process. A foreign company without German residence must apply to the Finanzamt responsible for its German permanent establishment, fixed establishment, taxable supplies, or other German tax nexus. VAT registration is required before conducting taxable transactions for which German VAT is due, subject to applicable exemptions and the small-business regime. Non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a German or EU tax representative for specific VAT procedures, including certain refund or import arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a capital company and is subject to entity-level German corporate income tax. Germany has no general U.S.-style check-the-box election for a UG. The UG cannot elect transparent partnership taxation merely by filing a classification form. Relevant elections and regimes may include VAT treatment as a small business under Section 19 UStG, subject to statutory turnover limits and waiver rules; participation in a tax consolidation group (Organschaft) where all legal and economic conditions are satisfied; and, for qualifying partnerships rather than a UG, an election under Section 1a KStG for corporate-income-tax treatment. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 4k EStG and related provisions operate by law and do not provide a general taxpayer election to neutralize a mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business making taxable supplies is exempt from German VAT registration. The small-business exemption under Section 19 UStG may apply if the business turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to the statutory rules applicable from 1 January 2025. The exemption is not a general VAT registration safe harbor: businesses must register where required for taxable transactions, intra-Community transactions, imports, reverse-charge obligations, or other German VAT liabilities. A business using the Section 19 exemption generally cannot deduct input VAT and must comply with the applicable invoicing and record requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under Section 4 No. 1(a) in conjunction with Section 6 UStG, provided the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country, the exporter or its customer bears the required transport relationship, and the exporter maintains documentary evidence of export. The evidence normally includes customs export data and transport or delivery evidence linking the goods to the third-country destination. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State may qualify for exemption under Section 4 No. 1(b) and Section 6a UStG, subject to valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative-list reporting, and other conditions. Executive search and headhunting services are generally services rather than exported goods. For B2B services, the place of supply is generally the customer’s business establishment under Section 3a(2) UStG; a service supplied to a business established outside Germany may therefore be outside the scope of German VAT, subject to customer-status and place-of-supply evidence. This is not technically a German 0% export rate. Services supplied to German customers generally remain taxable at 19%, unless a specific exemption applies. Services supplied to private customers require separate place-of-supply analysis and may remain subject to German VAT.

  • Export declaration lodged through the German or EU customs system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and electronic exit confirmation, where applicable
  • Commercial invoice, service or sales contract, packing list, and transport documentation
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport document, where applicable
  • Proof that the goods physically left the European Union and evidence linking the goods to the relevant invoice and customer
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and correct recapitulative-statement reporting

Germany does not currently operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or transaction-reporting system. From 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic business-to-business transactions, subject to statutory exclusions and transitional rules. The mandatory German e-invoice formats include recognized structured formats such as XRechnung and ZUGFeRD, provided the invoice contains the required structured data. For issuing domestic B2B e-invoices, transitional rules generally permit other invoice formats until 31 December 2026; businesses with preceding-year turnover not exceeding EUR 800,000 may generally use the transition until 31 December 2027. From 1 January 2028, the mandatory structured e-invoicing rules generally apply to in-scope domestic B2B supplies. Public-sector invoicing may be subject to federal or state-specific XRechnung and portal requirements. There is no general digital VAT stamp, mandatory pre-clearance API, or real-time invoice approval requirement for ordinary private-sector invoices.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement that export proceeds be received through a designated bank within 180 days. The taxpayer must substantiate the VAT treatment through reliable accounting, customs, transport, customer-status, and place-of-supply evidence. If export exemption or an outside-scope treatment is incorrectly claimed, the unpaid German VAT may be assessed retrospectively, generally together with interest under Section 233a AO, late-payment charges, and potentially penalties or late-filing surcharges. Input VAT previously refunded or deducted may be clawed back with interest. Intentional or reckless false declarations may constitute tax evasion under Section 370 AO or tax understatement under Section 378 AO, with criminal or administrative consequences. Incorrect invoices may create invoice-tax liability under Section 14c UStG, requiring correction and repayment even where the amount was not otherwise due. Customs violations may trigger customs duty recovery, import VAT recovery, administrative fines, and criminal liability under applicable customs and tax provisions. Fraudulent or knowingly unsupported claims can also lead to heightened tax audits, denial of input-tax recovery, and liability of responsible officers or persons involved in the filing.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full capital-account and current-account convertibility under TFEU Article 63 and AWG Sections 1–7. There are no exchange-control approvals, forced conversion rules, surrender quotas, or central-bank licensing requirements applicable to retainer fees, executive-search service receipts, or profit distributions to non-resident shareholders of a UG. The only profit-remittance hurdle is fiscal: a UG distributing profits triggers 25% German capital-yield tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, aggregating 26.375%; treaty relief or, for a qualifying EU corporate shareholder satisfying the Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, a 0% rate may apply. Cross-border service fees remitted from Germany to a non-resident headhunting firm are not subject to German withholding tax absent a German permanent establishment; German VAT reverse-charge on the German recipient applies under Section 13b UStG for B2B services received from foreign suppliers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no earmarking requirement on export proceeds, service receipts, or retainer income. A German UG may freely hold and reinvest foreign currency balances. The sole statutory duty is statistical: German residents must report receivables from non-residents and inbound cross-border payments to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics within five working days where a transaction exceeds EUR 12,500; bank-mediated SEPA/SWIFT credits are reported automatically by the receiving bank, and transactions at or below the threshold are exempt. No sector-specific export-surrender timeline applies to ISIC 7830 executive-search services.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank). The operative legal framework is the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV), under which the Deutsche Bundesbank executes balance-of-payments statistics and cross-border payment reporting; administrative licensing and protective-measure authority rests with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK), while AML/CTF supervision of account-opening banks is performed by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (0.825% effective) and municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax at an average aggregate rate of 28–34%; typical combined statutory load approx. 30%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375% if distributed to an individual shareholder. If the beneficiary is a German corporate shareholder meeting §8b KStG requirements, the dividend is 95% tax-exempt and no additional withholding applies. For non-resident corporate shareholders, the 26.375% statutory rate applies unless a double tax treaty (e.g., Germany–USA or Germany–China) reduces the rate to 0%, 5%, or 15% depending on participation and substance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For calendar-year taxpayers: 31 July following the tax year; if a tax advisor prepares the return, the deadline extends to 31 August. For off-calendar fiscal years: the last day of the seventh month after the close of the fiscal year (normally four months, unless extension granted).

Non-resident shareholders owning a UG are not required to file this return personally; the UG files in its own name. For a permanent establishment of a non-resident, only German-source income is entered. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) of 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 50 and capped at EUR 25,000; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per month on unpaid amounts; interest on back taxes at 0.5% per month from the 15th month after the tax year (Basiszins + 6 percentage points).
Box 30 (Solidarity Surcharge) Computed as 5.5% of the assessed corporate income tax for the period. Manager applies the same rate to taxable German-source income of the PE. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) of 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 50 and capped at EUR 25,000; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per month on unpaid amounts; interest on back taxes at 0.5% per month from the 15th month after the tax year (Basiszins + 6 percentage points).
Box 45 (Prepaid Tax) List quarterly estimated Körperschaftsteuer and solidarity prepayments already made during the tax year. Only prepayments made with respect to German-source income are creditable. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag) of 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 50 and capped at EUR 25,000; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag) of 1% per month on unpaid amounts; interest on back taxes at 0.5% per month from the 15th month after the tax year (Basiszins + 6 percentage points).
Box 12 (Gewerbeertrag) Trade earnings equal to taxable commercial income plus add-backs under §8 and minus deductions under §9 GewStG. Non-resident UG engaged in German executive-search business through a German PE must file; enter only German PE income. Verspätungszuschlag up to EUR 25,000, Säumniszuschlag 1% monthly, and municipal penalty of 10% of the trade tax for untimely filing.
Box 30 (Allowance) Apply the EUR 24,500 allowance for corporations under §11 GewStG. Allowance applies to PEs as well. Verspätungszuschlag up to EUR 25,000, Säumniszuschlag 1% monthly, and municipal penalty of 10% of the trade tax for untimely filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany does not specify a single statutory threshold; contemporaneous Local File documentation is expected once cross-border related-party transactions (goods or services) exceed EUR 500,000 in annual volume. Master File documentation is required if the worldwide group has annual consolidated sales in excess of EUR 100 million; transfer prices must be set at arm’s length in all cases.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no codified ESR of the Caribbean/UAE type. However, substance is enforced through §42 AO (abuse of structures), §50d EStG (treaty-sheltering restrictions) and the CFC rules of §§7–13 AStG. A UG must demonstrate real physical presence (office space, management decisions in Germany), at least one German-resident managing director, and a verifiable business purpose for headhunting/executive search services; otherwise tax treaty benefits may be denied, income re-attributed to the controlling shareholder, and penalty interest applied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under §370 AO, imprisonment up to 5 years (up to 10 years for aggravated cases) and/or a criminal fine up to EUR 10 million; mandatory additional interest at 0.5% per month on the evaded tax and administrative penalty of 10% of the assessed tax increase.

Director personal liability: Director owes restitution of tax shortfalls under §69 AO; assets may be attached to satisfy tax debt; criminal trial can trigger travel bans and passport seizure; a conviction disqualifies the director under GDPR and professional registration requirements.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under §162(4) AO, mandatory penalty of 10% of the adjusted profit increase, minimum EUR 2,500 and maximum EUR 250,000; tax assessment is made using unfavourable estimation; interest on tax differences accrues at 6% p.a.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the penalty if it arises from wilful or grossly negligent failure; civil liability for damages to the company; in the context of tax offense, directors may face imprisonment.
Shell Company/Briefkastenfirma (Treaty Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Under §42 AO the structure is disregarded; dividends/royalties reclassified as German-source income; withholding taxes are recomputed at 26.375% or 15.825%; penalties for incorrect statements up to EUR 50,000; criminal consequences if intent is present.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for all additional corporate tax and penalties; treaty relief is denied; civil courts may pierce the veil; directors may be investigated for tax evasion; travel can be restricted.
AML/Compliance Failure (GwG)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under §56 GwG of up to EUR 100,000 for private individuals and up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal entities; revocation of the statutory employment-licence; prohibition orders; publication of adverse administrative decision.

Director personal liability: Director is subject to monetary penalty, disqualification from managing license-regulated business, and imprisonment up to one year for intentional violations; civil liability for damages caused by failure to report.
