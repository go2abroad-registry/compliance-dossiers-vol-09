Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for an Estonian OÜ; founders may set share capital at a nominal level not below EUR 1 or may initially record unpaid capital as a receivable claim of shareholders. There is no escrow account requirement; any contribution made before registration is deposited into the company's bank account and documented by the bank, but this is not a precondition to submission.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate and fire inspection clearance from the Estonian Rescue Board (Päästeamet) for the warehouse and administrative premises; structural load inspection certificate from a certified civil engineer confirming floor and racking capacity for bulk cement clinker storage; workplace certificate and occupational health and safety clearance from the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) under the Occupational Health and Safety Act; if the premises are within a port area, an additional port safe-access clearance and stevedoring operation permit from the Port Authority may be required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for an Estonian OÜ; founders may set share capital at a nominal level not below EUR 1 or may initially record unpaid capital as a receivable claim of shareholders. There is no escrow account requirement; any contribution made before registration is deposited into the company's bank account and documented by the bank, but this is not a precondition to submission.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a eurozone member using the euro as legal tender. Full capital and current account convertibility is guaranteed under EU law and the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act (Välisvaluutaseadus). There are no exchange controls, no restrictions on profit repatriation, and no prior approval requirements for dividends or capital outflows. Foreign investors may own 100% of an OÜ, and all legal tender obligations may be settled in EUR. Non-resident bank accounts and cross-border payments operate freely, subject only to EU sanctions screening and AML/CTF requirements. The euro is freely convertible both within the EU and globally.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds in Estonia. Export receipts may be retained in any convertible currency or converted to EUR at market rates. No statutory timeline requires repatriation into Estonia; however, transfer-pricing and substance rules require that profits be justified by actual economic activity. For customs and balance-of-payments reporting, entities must declare cross-border goods movements through the Estonian Customs and report direct investment or settlement data to Eesti Pank for statistics, but no conversion ratio or forced sale applies.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High for EUR and multi-currency payment accounts provided by licensed EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passporting into Estonia. Suitable for fast inbound/outbound Electronic Fund Transfers, FX conversion, and virtual IBAN solutions. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide full trade-finance guarantees; they are not substitutes for a licensed bank for LC, documentary collections, or large bulk transactions. Use as a supplementary liquidity rail, but primary treasury banking should be via tier-1 credit institutions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the gross amount of distributed or otherwise taxable profits, collected through the 22/78 mechanism; retained profits are generally not subject to corporate income tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on ordinary dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder. The Estonian company instead pays corporate income tax on the distribution, generally at 22/78 of the net amount distributed. Transitional treatment may apply to dividends taxed under the former 14/86 regime, including potential 7% withholding on certain onward payments to natural persons.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 500,000 is a key transaction-value threshold under Estonian transfer-pricing documentation rules for certain persons; separate size-based criteria apply. Detailed documentation requirements generally apply to specified large taxpayers, including entities meeting criteria such as net turnover exceeding EUR 50 million, total assets exceeding EUR 43 million, or more than 250 employees, and to qualifying groups subject to the applicable rules. Group-level Master File requirements may apply to multinational groups meeting the relevant consolidated revenue threshold, including the EUR 750 million threshold under country-by-country reporting rules. Verify the current Estonian regulation and the taxpayer’s status; documentation may be requested even where a threshold is not met.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have a general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. An OÜ must nevertheless conduct genuine business activity, maintain reliable accounting and supporting records, and substantiate the commercial purpose and arm’s-length nature of related-party dealings. Applicable rules include transfer pricing, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation, general anti-avoidance principles, and beneficial-ownership requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and materially false reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, recovery of unpaid tax and statutory interest, administrative fines, and potential criminal liability for tax fraud under the Penal Code. Criminal penalties depend on the offense and facts and may include a pecuniary punishment or imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability for their own knowing participation and civil or tax liability where statutory grounds for recovery from a responsible person are established. Limited liability does not protect a director from personal wrongdoing.
Undocumented or non-arm’s-length related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board may adjust taxable amounts, assess additional tax and interest, and impose applicable reporting or documentation penalties. A recharacterized value transfer may be treated as a taxable distribution or other taxable payment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own intentional misconduct and may face recovery actions where statutory responsible-person conditions are met; lack of documentation materially increases audit and adjustment risk.
Fictitious residence, treaty abuse, or concealed beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: Denial or recovery of treaty relief, withholding-tax reassessment, interest, administrative penalties, and potential criminal proceedings for deliberate fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible persons may incur personal liability for knowingly false claims or participation in fraud; ordinary shareholder status alone does not create personal liability.
Unlawful profit extraction or disguised distributions

Statutory sanction: The amount may be treated as a taxable distribution, fringe benefit, gift, donation, or non-business expense, with tax, interest, and penalties. Company-law restrictions may also require repayment of unlawful distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore unlawful distributions or compensate the company under the Commercial Code and may incur tax or criminal liability where statutory conditions are met. Travel restrictions or imprisonment are not automatic consequences of an ordinary tax assessment.
AML, sanctions, and trade-control violations

Statutory sanction: Potential payment freezing, transaction prohibition, administrative or criminal penalties, confiscation, and restrictions under applicable Estonian, European Union, and other applicable sanctions laws. AML reporting and customer-due-diligence obligations depend on whether the company falls within a regulated category or specific statutory duty.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal administrative or criminal liability for their own participation, authorization, or legally relevant failure to act. Corporate limited liability does not shield individual offenders.
