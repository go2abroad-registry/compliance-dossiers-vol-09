Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and central withholding matters; local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT assessment, and registration. Primary e-filing portal: ELSTER (elektronische Steuererklärung) at https://www.elster.de; BZSt portal: https://www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Register the UG with the local Finanzamt (for the registered office jurisdiction) using Form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (questionnaire for tax registration) submitted electronically via ELSTER or in person. Completion of the questionnaire obtains the local Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT; after the Finanzamt forwards the data, BZSt issues the USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number). A foreign parent or non-resident shareholder must appoint a domestic authorized representative and disclose all shareholders/directors; written power of attorney and certified incorporation documents are generally required. For cross-border services, the foreign entity may also need a German VAT registration if it supplies B2C services or e-services to non-entrepreneurs in Germany, or for domestic reverse-charge supplies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the UG (a limited-liability company under German law) is a resident corporation with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income; subject to 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag, effectively 0.825% on top of corporate tax), and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, typically 7–17% range depending on municipal rate; for a German company with permanent establishment only German-source trade tax. No Territorial_Scope election is available; no Tax_Exempt_Zone applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime; Germany follows a closed corporate tax system. The UG cannot elect pass-through or partnership status. Available elections: (1) Körperschaftsteuer-Organschaft – fiscal unity election under Secs. 14, 17 KStG between a German parent and the UG, enabling profit/loss pooling and eliminating sub-level tax on qualifying profit transfers; (2) VAT Organschaft (organschaftliche Eingliederung) – fixed-establishment VAT grouping election under Sec. 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, treating the UG and parent as one taxable person with the parent responsible for VAT; (3) si triggering hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD/German Section 4k EStG and Section 8b KStG for cross-border payments; no unilateral check-the-box for foreign LLC; any entity classification is governed by German tax treaty law and domestic HGB accounting rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 (preceding calendar year gross turnover) and EUR 50,000 forecast current-year; under Sec. 19 UStG, if previous year did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current year will not exceed EUR 50,000, a domestic entrepreneur is exempt from VAT except in cases of mandatory registration. However, a UG providing electronic services or e-invoicing B2B supplies is normally not eligible for the small-business exemption for non-domestic entrepreneurs; a non-EU/foreign supplier supplying e-services to German non-entrepreneurs generally requires VAT registration from the first euro unless the OSS procedure covers it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Zero-rating applies to export deliveries under Sec. 6 UStG if the physical goods depart from Germany to a third-country territory (incl. outside the EU) or are delivered to an EU customer in another Member State and qualify as intra-Community supply (Sec. 4 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 and Nr. 2 UStG); the supplier must retain documentary proof of removal from Germany. Services: Under place-of-supply rules, German VAT is not chargeable if the recipient is an entrepreneur in another country (B2B Sec. 3a Abs. 2 UStG) for electronic, SaaS, EMR/cloud, and software services; if received by a non-entrepreneur in a third country, the supply may be deemed outside the scope of German VAT; medical-sector SaaS invoiced to overseas medical clinics generally falls outside German VAT where the clinic's business establishment is abroad. Official zero-rating (0% VAT) is used for physical exports and EU intra-community deliveries; for cross-border services the invoice may show 'no German VAT – place of supply in customer's jurisdiction' and be reported under OSS or via invoice as reverse-charge where applicable. The exporter must verify the customer's valid VAT ID using the VIES or BZSt database for EU supplies.

  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement) or Air Waybill (Luftfrachtbrief)
  • Commercial Invoice (Rechnung) with explicit zero-rating legal basis (Sec. 6 UStG or intra-Community supply reference)

Germany introduced mandatory B2B e-invoicing under the Growth Opportunities Act ((fortune) Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025 every taxable person established in Germany must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X); from 1 January 2027 domestic B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices in structured data formats. There is no real-time per-invoice clearance or digital-stamping requirement; XML validation is for auditability, not fiscal pre-approval. API integration with ERP systems is standard; Peppol interoperability applies for government suppliers and is increasingly adopted. For exports, a standard commercial invoice in paper/PDF remains acceptable, but it must contain VAT ID and EU customer VAT ID for EU B2B supply; there is no electronic customs stamp on the invoice; the customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) is filed in ATLAS/ECS e-customs and serves as the primary clearance evidence.

Fraudulent or improperly substantiated export zero-rating triggers VAT assessment in the amount of gross invoice value, plus statutory late-payment interest (Sec. 233a AO – 0.5% per month after 15 months post-year-end); criminal penalties under Sec. 370 AO for tax evasion up to 10 years imprisonment (or fine); Sec. 26 UStG/§369 AO permits seizure of goods where export proof fails. Administrative denial or clawback applies when the required customs/invoicing/fx repatriation evidence is missing, irregular, or false; repeated violations may result in termination of VAT registration status and joint-and-several liability of shareholders/directors under Sec. 69 AO, 71 AO. The Finanzamt retains a five-year audit limitation period (10 years in cases of gross negligence/intent).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is an International Monetary Fund Article VIII member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility and no exchange-control apparatus; the euro is freely convertible for all commercial, financial, capital, and profit-repatriation transactions. Capital mobility is constitutionally protected at EU level by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), so a German UG may freely receive equity injections, shareholder loans, SaaS subscription receipts, and intra-group funding from any jurisdiction without FX licences, prior approvals, or repatriation quotas. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is legally unlimited but tax-friction applies: dividends suffer German withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%), reducible under double-tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the shareholder is an EU parent; no statutory foreign-exchange conversion or central-bank notification is required for distributions. The UG must only maintain AWV statistical reporting for each external transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, and must comply with standard German tax filing, commercial-law balance-sheet, and thin-capitalisation documentation rules when shareholder funding is structured as debt.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export proceeds in Germany; the Federal Republic abolished all foreign-exchange surrender and repatriation obligations under the 1961 full-liberalisation policy and continues unrestricted treatment under the AWG/AWV. A UG exporting EMR software and clinical workflow licences may retain all foreign-currency receivables, convert them at any time through a German bank, or keep them in multi-currency accounts indefinitely. The only mandatory compliance duty is statistical: every cross-border receipt or payment (including export proceeds, Stripe payouts originating abroad, vendor disbursements, and capital movements) equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 or the countervalue must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank via the Z5 payment-statistics form by the 7th calendar day of the month following the transaction, per Section 67 AWV; non-compliance is an administrative regulatory offence (Ordnungswidrigkeit). The treasury system must therefore automate Z5 generation and filing for every qualifying inbound and outbound wire, but no portion of export proceeds is required to be exchanged into EUR, deposited domestically, or held for a minimum period.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Bundesbank, Frankfurt am Main), acting as the German foreign-exchange and external-payments administration authority under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV); export-control licensing and enforcement is jointly vested in the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) and the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA); prudential supervision of banks and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) and, for significant institutions, the European Central Bank within the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High for operational speed, Medium for legal and risk robustness. A German registered UG may lawfully open and operate an account with a BaFin-licensed German electronic money institution (e.g., Solaris, Pliant, N26 Business) or an EEA-licensed EMI passporting into Germany under the second Payment Services Directive (PSD2) (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business). These EMIs provide rapid remote onboarding, virtual IBANs, SWIFT MT103-equivalent rails, embedded multi-currency wallets, and often lower FX spreads, which is attractive for cross-border clinic subscription collections. However, for the EMR/HealthTech SaaS treasury the legal constraints are material: (i) an EMI is not a full credit institution under the German Banking Act (KWG), so it cannot book loans, overdrafts, Commercial Letters of Credit, or true FX hedging margin facilities; (ii) most EMIs deploy algorithm-driven KYC that flags HealthTech/medical dataflows, high-volume recurring filings, or high-risk-source jurisdictions and may freeze onboarding without human review; (iii) the UG remains fully responsible under GDPR Article 28 and the German Health Data Protection framework for any routing that transmits protected medical metadata through an EMI payment chain, creating a sub-processor exposure that must be covered by an Article 28 Data Processing Agreement; (iv) AWV Z5 and tax-reporting obligations remain with the UG even when the collection account is held abroad or at an EMI; and (v) a non-German EMI lacking a German branch or registered agent may complicate BaFin consumer/corporate-protection enforcement. Recommended blueprint: use an EMI only as a secondary collection rail for low-ticket cross-border SaaS invoices where tier-1 banks are slow, maintain the primary operating, escrow, and FX accounts at Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank, and execute a GDPR-compliant data-processing addendum plus a German registered-office proof before any EMI wallet is credited.

Recommended blueprint: use an EMI only as a secondary collection rail for low-ticket cross-border SaaS invoices where tier-1 banks are slow, maintain the primary operating, escrow, and FX accounts at Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank, and execute a GDPR-compliant data-processing addendum plus a German registered-office proof before any EMI wallet is credited.

The UG receives shareholder equity as a pure EUR transfer using SEPA Credit Transfer where the shareholder holds an EU/EEA account, or SWIFT MT103 where the shareholder remits from outside the Single Euro Payments Area; the structured ISO 20022 purpose code must be 'EQUI' (Equity) and the plain-text remittance field must state 'Kapitaleinzahlung gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag' with reference to the notarised formation deed or capital-increase resolution. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

German corporation tax is 15% of taxable income. A solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporation tax produces an effective federal burden of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax is additional and generally produces an effective burden of approximately 7% to 21%, depending on the municipality. The combined burden for a typical municipality is approximately 29% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic German dividend withholding rate is 25%, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing a combined rate of 26.375%. Domestic participation exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany, and applicable tax treaties may reduce or eliminate the charge subject to ownership, holding-period, substance, anti-abuse and documentation requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border controlled transactions, a German Local File is generally required when the annual transaction volume exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties and similar dealings. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, where the statutory cross-border transaction conditions apply. Documentation may also be required for extraordinary transactions irrespective of ordinary thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the General Anti-Abuse Rule in Section 42 AO, anti-treaty-shopping rules in Section 50d(3) EStG, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership principles, transfer-pricing rules and treaty principal-purpose standards. A German UG should maintain real decision-making, adequate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, commercially justified premises and systems, proper books, independent governance, and evidence that its EMR SaaS functions and risks are actually performed in Germany. A mailbox entity, circular financing, artificial royalty chain or conduit structure creates material denial-of-deduction and treaty-relief risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is generally punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may attract imprisonment from six months to ten years. The tax, interest and possible monetary penalty remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for taxes not assessed or not paid because of intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax duties under Section 69 AO. Criminal prosecution may result in fines, imprisonment, professional disqualification and, in serious cases, asset-seizure measures. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial or enforcement measures.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make income adjustments, deny deductions, reclassify payments as hidden profit distributions, impose late-documentation penalties and assess corporation tax, solidarity surcharge, trade tax and interest. Documentation failures may result in estimation and surcharge penalties under the German transfer-pricing rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax liability for culpable breaches of filing, withholding or payment duties. Intentional manipulation can support tax-evasion proceedings. Corporate limited liability does not protect a director from statutory tax or criminal liability.
Withholding-tax failure and unlawful profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: The UG may be liable for the unwithheld tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties. Improper treaty-relief claims may be denied retroactively, with recovery from the payer and, where applicable, criminal tax consequences.

Director personal liability: Managing directors can be personally liable for withholding-tax losses caused by intentional or grossly negligent failure to withhold or remit. Criminal exposure arises where the failure is deliberate or supported by false documentation.
VAT, invoicing and digital-service compliance

Statutory sanction: Additional VAT, interest, late-filing surcharges and administrative fines may apply. Deliberate VAT fraud may constitute tax evasion with imprisonment or criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for VAT losses caused by culpable conduct and may face criminal proceedings for deliberate false VAT returns or fraudulent invoicing.
Payroll tax and social-security non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Authorities may assess wage tax, social-security contributions, interest, surcharges and administrative fines. Intentional withholding or non-payment of employee social-security contributions may constitute a criminal offense under Section 266a StGB.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unpaid payroll taxes and social-security contributions and may face criminal fines or imprisonment, particularly for intentional non-payment.
Money laundering, beneficial ownership and AML

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German AML Act can reach EUR 1 million or, for certain serious, repeated or systematic breaches, the higher statutory multiple of the economic benefit or the applicable percentage of annual turnover. Corrective measures, public naming and criminal money-laundering liability may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face personal administrative or criminal liability. Money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine, with aggravated cases carrying higher imprisonment ranges. Asset freezing and confiscation are possible; travel bans are case-specific rather than automatic.
Insolvency delay and unlawful payments

Statutory sanction: The managing director may face insolvency-related criminal penalties, repayment claims, civil damages, director disqualification and confiscation. German insolvency law generally requires timely filing when the company is unable to pay or over-indebted, subject to the statutory filing periods and exceptions.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, unpaid taxes and creditor losses. Imprisonment or fines may apply for insolvency offenses. Travel restrictions may be imposed by a court or enforcement authority in specific proceedings but are not an automatic consequence.
Data protection, medical software and licensing

Statutory sanction: GDPR administrative fines may reach EUR 20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, for the relevant infringements. Sector regulators may impose product restrictions, withdrawal orders, corrective measures and additional fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability, regulatory orders and, where separately criminalized, criminal exposure. Limited liability does not protect directors from their own intentional or negligent misconduct or from statutory organizational duties.
