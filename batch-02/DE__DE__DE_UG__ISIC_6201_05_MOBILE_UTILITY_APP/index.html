Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for federal tax matters and VAT identification; local Finanzamt (tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT registration; ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) for electronic filing and registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity establishing a German UG, the incorporator must register the company with the commercial register (Handelsregister). The local Finanzamt then assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) based on the commercial register entry and questionnaires submitted via ELSTER. VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is issued by BZSt upon application using the official form after the company is tax-registered. Non-resident business founders may make the application through a German fiscal representative or legal counsel; the UG cannot obtain a VAT ID before its Steuernummer is issued. Registration deadlines: one month after commencing commercial activity (§138 AO).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign entity establishing a German UG, the incorporator must register the company with the commercial register (Handelsregister). The local Finanzamt then assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) based on the commercial register entry and questionnaires submitted via ELSTER. VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is issued by BZSt upon application using the official form after the company is tax-registered. Non-resident business founders may make the application through a German fiscal representative or legal counsel; the UG cannot obtain a VAT ID before its Steuernummer is issued. Registration deadlines: one month after commencing commercial activity (§138 AO).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not permit check-the-box classification for UG entities; all UGs are taxed as corporations (Körperschaftsteuer) with entity-level corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax. Available elections include: (i) application of the Kleinunternehmeruregelung under §19 UStG to waive VAT on turnover below statutory thresholds; (ii) option for regular taxation under §9 UStG; (iii) formation of a fiscal unity (Organschaft) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT; (iv) transfer pricing documentation elections and thin-capitalization safe harbours; (v) cross-border hybrid mismatch elections where consistent with German Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive implementation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies to all entrepreneurs from inception, without turnover floor for domestic established businesses. However, the small-business exemption (§19 UStG) is available where the prior-year turnover did not exceed €25,000 and the current-year estimated turnover does not exceed €100,000. For non-resident suppliers of digital services or distance sales, a VAT registration obligation is triggered by any taxable supply in Germany unless the annual pan-European distance-sales threshold of €10,000 is exceeded; below this threshold, the supplier may alternatively use the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or Mini One-Stop-Shop (MOSS) scheme without full German registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A UG may zero-rate its supplies if the transaction qualifies as (i) export of goods to a non-EU country (§6 UStG), (ii) intra-Community supply to another EU business with a valid VAT ID (§4 Nr. 1 UStG, §6b UStG), (iii) certain offshore services connected to goods or performing directly outside the EU (§7 UStG), or (iv) deemed export supplies. For mobile utility apps, the sale of software and related services to non-EU end users is generally outside the scope of German VAT (not zero-rated but not subject to German VAT) if the customer is not a German business. To claim zero-rating for intra-Community supplies, the buyer must provide a valid EU VAT ID and the supplier must file a recapitulative statement. For export of goods, the goods must physically leave the EU within a reasonable period evidenced by customs or transport documentation.

  • Export declaration (Ausfuhrbegleitpapier) or slightly simplified export customs declaration through ATLAS
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical movement from Germany to non-EU destination
  • Certificate of arrival / customs clearance proof from the destination country (where available)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer record showing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • VAT ID validation confirmation for intra-Community supplies (VIES validation result)
  • Intra-Community supply recapitulative statement and transaction log

Germany mandates that all B2B domestic invoices must be issued as structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) to ensure receipt and machine-readability from 1 January 2025. A phased implementation applies for large and small businesses. The furnishing of VAT returns and periodic recapitulative statements must be transmitted electronically via the ELSTER portal or certified tax software APIs. Germany operates a deferred digital transaction reporting environment rather than real-time clearance; mandatory electronic clearance will be phased in through the EU ViDA regime by 2028. There is no current digital stamping or real-time fiscal clearance requirement for ordinary B2C mobile utility app sales via Apple IAP or Google Play Billing, but platform operators (Apple, Google) act as deemed suppliers for VAT when selling through app stores, with VAT collected by the platform operator under the deemed supplier model and the UG as the underlying developer.

Fraudulent or unjustified zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment plus interest of 0.5% per month (6% annual) from the initial tax liability date. Under German tax law, incorrect VAT treatment may constitute a tax evasion offence under §370 AO punishable with imprisonment of up to five years, fines, and forfeiture of profits; negligent underdeclaration is subject to administrative penalties of up to €50,000. The tax office may retrospectively clawback zero-rated relief where the exporter cannot substantiate timely physical export or provision of qualifying services, and may impose a late-filing surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed VAT. The UG remains jointly and severally liable with its officers for intentionally or negligently confirmed false declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Section 1(2) Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG). A UG may freely convert EUR into USD or any other currency, hold foreign-currency balances onshore or offshore, and remit dividends, interest, royalties, and loan repayments without regulatory approval. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are exclusively fiscal and documentary: (i) the UG is subject to the GmbH capital-maintenance rule (Sections 30-31 GmbHG) and must retain 25% of annual profits until stated share capital reaches EUR 25,000 (Section 5a GmbHG); (ii) dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders attract 26.375% German withholding tax (25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced under a double-taxation treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; (iii) each cross-border payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank within five working days for balance-of-payments statistics, but this is purely informational and never suspends payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None exist. German law imposes no surrender obligation, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or App-Store settlement proceeds. A UG may indefinitely retain USD, GBP, or other foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts. The sole obligation is statistical: inbound settlements from Apple Distribution International (Ireland), Google Ireland entities, or other non-resident payers exceeding EUR 12,500 must be captured in Bundesbank external-sector statistics (Zahlungsmeldung under Section 67 AWV), normally filed by the receiving bank within five working days.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) is the central bank and the competent authority for external-sector statistics and cross-border payment reporting under Sections 67-70 of the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV, German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance). The Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) is the policy authority; BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) supervises banking and payment institutions. Germany is an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction: no central-bank licence, FX authorization, or exchange-control permit is required for ordinary cross-border payments by a UG.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as an auxiliary settlement account; Low as a sole primary treasury account. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (Wise Europe SA, Revolut UAB, Qonto, Pliant, Monese) may lawfully provide IBAN accounts to a German UG under PSD2 EU passporting. Practical constraints: (i) Finanzamt and German social-security institutions operate most efficiently with a German IBAN for SEPA direct-debit mandates; Regulation (EU) 260/2012 prohibits formal IBAN discrimination, but several public-sector counterparties still apply an operational German-IBAN preference; (ii) funds safeguarded at an EMI are not covered by German statutory deposit insurance (Einlagensicherung); (iii) AWV balance-of-payments reporting for payments exceeding EUR 12,500 is handled within the German banking circuit, whereas an EU-passported EMI in Belgium or Lithuania reports outside the Bundesbank circuit, adding friction; (iv) EMIs generally do not grant commercial credit and do not execute derivative or FX-forward hedging requiring ISDA documentation. Recommended architecture: a Tier-1 German bank account for taxes, payroll, corporate substance, and statutory reporting, complemented by an EMI account for App-Store collections, multi-currency balances, and small developer disbursements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% national corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge; aggregate headline 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is imposed additionally at a location-specific rate, typically 12%–17% of trade profits, giving a combined effective rate of roughly 29%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains tax (KapESt) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, totalling 26.375%; no church tax; trade tax can be credited for corporate shareholders under certain conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory filing threshold triggering a local file / master file in Germany; documentation must be produced on request. Practical triggers: transactions with foreign related parties exceeding EUR 1 million for goods or EUR 100,000 for services; entities with annual turnover below EUR 6 million may still be required to file if cross-border dealings occur. Non-compliance with the documentation timeline can lead to a penalty of up to EUR 200,000 (§162 AO) plus a 20% surcharge to the extent tax would have also been adjusted.

Economic substance scope: No separate German ESR, but the general anti-abuse rule §42 AO requires an economic justification for all transactions; letterbox companies are disregarded for tax purposes. A UG must possess real substance—office, managed activities, decision-making capacity, and personnel—if it seeks treaty benefits or app-store revenue attribution. In the app-store model, the UG must own IP and exercise entrepreneurial functions in Germany (attribution of income over §1 OECD transfer pricing).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Up to 5 years imprisonment (up to 10 years for especially serious cases) + fine of up to EUR 50,000 (criminal fine); automatic interest at 0.15% per month and up to EUR 25,000 late filing surcharge; the tax claim remains fully enforceable.

Director personal liability: Managing director may be individually prosecuted if the act was committed with knowledge; prohibited from serving as company manager for 5 years; civil claim for restitution against company under §69 AO for tax debts; travel ban/interpol red notice possible when flight risk.
Shell Company / Letterbox Company (Briefkastenfirma)

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities will disregard the legal form under §42 AO; the company loses its tax treaty and VAT status; transactions are recharacterized as direct receipts of the foreign owner; penalties for false VAT invoice claim (input VAT denied) and potential money laundering investigation.

Director personal liability: Civil liability to creditors and tax office under §69 AO; in insolvency, director is liable for not filing for insolvency in a stuck situation; possible criminal fraud conviction if the 'shell' facilitated VAT fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (Substance Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Correction of income to hypothetical arm's length amount, imposition of a 6% 'contractual interest gap' (actually interest on additional tax at 0.15%/month from the statutory filing date), documentation penalty up to EUR 200,000, and potential criminal tax evasion if intentional.

Director personal liability: Director will be personally liable if the TP documentation is prepared with false statements; exclusion from management due to lack of reliability.
AML / UBO Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines under the German Money Laundering Act (GwG): up to EUR 100,000 for non-filing; up to EUR 1 million for grave violations (money laundering, concealment under §10, §56 GwG); prohibition from exercising entrepreneurial activity by competent authority.

Director personal liability: Board member personally liable to pay overdue taxes due to lack of proper monitoring; criminal imprisonment if money laundering is proven (up to 5 years).
Withholding Tax Non-Remittance / Conduct on Dividends

Statutory sanction: The distribution is taxable; the company as withholding agent is liable for the tax (and surcharge) under §42d EStG; penalty for non-payment and interest; restitution to the tax office; the foreign shareholder may be denied treaty relief; possible criminal tax evasion if the failure was negligent.

Director personal liability: Director retains personal bankruptcy-like liability under §69 AO due to failure to discharge the company's duty to withhold; personal claim by tax authority; if willful, criminal proceedings.
