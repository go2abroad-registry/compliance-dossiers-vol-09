Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German corporation and is not eligible for a United States-style check-the-box election. It is generally subject to German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax at entity level. Available statutory choices include electing regular VAT taxation instead of the small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act, where the statutory conditions apply; applying for cash-basis VAT accounting under Section 20 of the German VAT Act where eligible; and, subject to strict legal and economic requirements, participating in a German tax consolidation group (Organschaft). German anti-hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences, but they do not provide a general elective hybrid classification. A transparent partnership classification cannot be elected for a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business carrying out taxable supplies is automatically exempt from VAT registration. The small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act may apply where total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to the statutory calculation rules and effective-date provisions. A business may waive the exemption and elect regular VAT treatment; the election generally binds the taxpayer for the statutory minimum period. The reduced VAT rate is 7% for specifically listed supplies. Cross-border transactions, reverse-charge services, intra-Community supplies, imports, and transactions involving German fixed establishments can create registration or reporting obligations independently of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax deduction under the German VAT Act if the goods are transported or dispatched to a non-EU destination and the supplier retains prescribed documentary or accounting proof of export. The supplier must verify the purchaser, destination, transport route, and export evidence. Services supplied to a business customer are generally taxable where the customer is established under the B2B place-of-supply rule; services supplied to a customer established outside Germany may therefore be outside the scope of German VAT rather than technically zero-rated. Evidence of customer business status, establishment, contractual scope, and place of use is required. Services connected with German immovable property or otherwise subject to special place-of-supply rules may remain taxable in Germany. Intra-Community supplies of goods are generally VAT-exempt in Germany only where the statutory conditions, valid customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and recapitulative statement requirements are satisfied.

  • Export customs declaration or electronic export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument), including the export confirmation generated by the customs export system where applicable
  • Commercial invoice, contract, purchase order, packing list, and transport documentation identifying the goods, supplier, purchaser, destination, and transport route
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport evidence, as applicable
  • Customs exit confirmation or other legally accepted proof that the goods physically left the European Union
  • Evidence of the customer's non-EU destination and, where relevant, evidence of payment or commercial settlement
  • For services, customer VAT or business-status evidence, contractual documentation, place-of-supply analysis, and records supporting non-German establishment or use
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory German VAT export document; bank records may nevertheless support the commercial substance and payment trail

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system or mandatory government pre-approval of ordinary invoices. Since 1 January 2025, German businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices complying with the German and EU e-invoicing framework for domestic business-to-business transactions. Issuance obligations are subject to transitional rules: paper and other invoices remain permitted for specified periods, with mandatory structured e-invoice issuance for most domestic B2B supplies phased in from 2027 and generally applying from 2028, subject to turnover and other statutory conditions. A compliant e-invoice must contain structured data, typically in an EN 16931-compliant format such as XRechnung or an accepted hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD. Invoices must be archived in an auditable manner for the statutory retention period. No general digital tax stamp or real-time API clearance applies to ordinary German VAT invoices.

Germany has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement that export proceeds be routed through a German bank. Export VAT exemption or input-tax recovery can be denied or clawed back if the supplier cannot substantiate the export, the non-EU destination, the customer's status, the transport, or the applicable place-of-supply rule. Unpaid VAT may be assessed retrospectively, generally with statutory interest under the German tax rules, and incorrect VAT returns may trigger late-payment surcharges and other administrative consequences. Intentional or reckless false declarations, fabricated export evidence, concealed taxable turnover, or fraudulent input-tax claims can result in tax evasion or tax fraud proceedings, criminal penalties, civil tax liability, recovery of improperly refunded tax, and possible liability of responsible directors. Customs violations may additionally lead to customs debt, administrative fines, seizure, and criminal prosecution. The normal German tax assessment limitation period is generally four years, extended to five years for reckless tax reduction and ten years for intentional tax evasion, subject to statutory suspension and special rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible. Germany generally permits current-account and capital-account transactions, including foreign investment, dividends, loan repayments, and transfers of profits, subject to applicable corporate, tax, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and reporting rules. There is no general requirement for prior exchange-control approval to remit profits. A UG may distribute profits only in accordance with German company law, including preparation and approval of annual accounts, allocation to the statutory reserve under GmbHG section 5a(3), preservation of stated capital, and any applicable solvency and shareholder-resolution requirements. Dividends to non-residents may be subject to German withholding tax, treaty relief or refund procedures, and beneficial-ownership documentation. Cross-border payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV) is statistical, not an authorization or surrender requirement. Since 1 January 2025, the general reporting threshold for relevant cross-border payments is an amount exceeding EUR 50,000 per payment, subject to statutory exemptions and transaction-specific rules; the responsible resident party should confirm the applicable reporting obligation and deadline with the Bundesbank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio or surrender requirement. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to contract terms, sanctions, tax, accounting, and applicable AWV reporting obligations. Banks may request commercial invoices, contracts, shipping or service-delivery evidence, and payment-purpose information for compliance.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy; Germany has no general exchange-control authority imposing routine currency conversion or capital-account approval requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium to high for routine euro and supported-currency collections and payments, subject to the provider's country, entity, activity, and risk acceptance. Wise Europe SA and other appropriately authorized EU payment or electronic-money institutions may provide payment accounts or e-money services; these are not necessarily bank deposits and may not support UG formation-capital deposits or all corporate banking functions. Verify the provider's authorization and safeguarding disclosures, permitted currencies, account-holder name, German IBAN availability, SEPA and SWIFT coverage, limits, and support for tax, payroll, cash, lending, and account-confirmation needs. An offshore EMI does not remove German AML, tax, accounting, sanctions, or AWV obligations and may decline or restrict the account. The business must separately assess the professional authorization restrictions applicable to tax advice and bookkeeping services under the Steuerberatungsgesetz.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For subscribed share capital, remit funds from the shareholder's verified account to the designated formation account or company account using a clear reference such as 'share capital contribution' and the UG's formation or registered name. Retain the subscription and bank evidence and ensure the contribution is properly recorded in the company's accounts; avoid unrelated third-party funding or commingling. For shareholder loans, execute and retain a written loan agreement and record principal, interest, maturity, and repayment terms. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT for international or non-SEPA transfers; provide accurate beneficiary details, IBAN/BIC, payer information, and a truthful payment narrative. Germany has no universal statutory FX purpose-code system comparable to some controlled-currency jurisdictions. Banks may request purpose and supporting documents. Assess AWV reporting for relevant cross-border payments exceeding the applicable threshold, currently generally EUR 50,000 per payment from 1 January 2025, subject to exemptions, and submit required reports to the Bundesbank within the applicable deadline. Do not treat the statistical report as payment authorization.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro-area and SEPA payments; use SWIFT customer credit transfers, commonly documented through MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA cross-border payments. Agree fees and currency with the counterparty, validate beneficiary bank instructions independently, and retain the invoice, service or delivery evidence, contract, approval, and payment confirmation. Commercial documentary letters of credit may be arranged through a bank for genuine goods-trade transactions, subject to bank credit approval and documentary conditions; they are generally not a routine settlement rail for virtual CFO services. Apply sanctions screening, fraud controls, tax and withholding analysis, and AWV reporting where applicable. Record foreign-currency transactions and resulting exchange differences under applicable German accounting and tax rules.Deliverable forward contracts with a bank or authorized provider for documented commercial exposures, subject to counterparty onboarding, suitability or appropriateness requirements where applicable, and confirmation of the company's authority and purpose.Non-deliverable forwards may be available for restricted or non-deliverable currencies, subject to the counterparty's product eligibility, jurisdictional restrictions, and documentation of the underlying exposure; they are not a substitute for compliance with local controls in the relevant currency jurisdiction.Foreign-exchange swaps for documented liquidity management or conversion needs, subject to bank approval, contractual documentation, valuation, collateral, and counterparty-risk controls.The company should adopt a board-approved treasury policy specifying permitted instruments, hedge limits, counterparties, approval authority, exposure measurement, accounting treatment, and prohibition on speculative dealing. Derivative transactions may trigger MiFID II, EMIR, reporting, risk-mitigation, or clearing obligations depending on the instrument, counterparty, and applicable thresholds; confirm classification and exemptions with the bank or qualified adviser.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to high for routine euro and supported-currency collections and payments, subject to the provider's country, entity, activity, and risk acceptance. Wise Europe SA and other appropriately authorized EU payment or electronic-money institutions may provide payment accounts or e-money services; these are not necessarily bank deposits and may not support UG formation-capital deposits or all corporate banking functions. Verify the provider's authorization and safeguarding disclosures, permitted currencies, account-holder name, German IBAN availability, SEPA and SWIFT coverage, limits, and support for tax, payroll, cash, lending, and account-confirmation needs. An offshore EMI does not remove German AML, tax, accounting, sanctions, or AWV obligations and may decline or restrict the account. The business must separately assess the professional authorization restrictions applicable to tax advice and bookkeeping services under the Steuerberatungsgesetz.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax due and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of 14-17%; aggregate effective rate approx. 30%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the tax (total 26.375%); if recipient is EU/EEA corporate shareholder with >=10% participation, effective domestic withholding reduced to 5% deemed base under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, resulting in 1.31875% tax; treaty relief may reduce the rate to 0-15% depending on DTA

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 for goods/services and EUR 500,000 for intangible/royalty transactions; Master File required when consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: No standalone ESR law, but Germany mandates economic substance through anti-abuse rules (§42 AO), license barrier royalty restrictions (§4j EStG), CFC rules (§7-14 AStG), MLI/PPT provisions, and tax treaty benefit denial where no real economic substance exists; directors must prove actual management, office, staff, and transactional substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 10 years under §370 AO, monetary fine equal to the evaded amount plus interest (0.5% monthly), mandatory publication of tax evasion judgment, seizure of assets/bank accounts, nullification of tax settlements; a fine up to EUR 50,000 and loss of business license

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable under §69 AO for negligent failure to file returns or incorrect disclosure; civil restitution to the tax office; criminal conviction resulting in travel ban and passport revocation; directors may be imprisoned if taxes are wilfully unpaid, and the corporate veil is pierced in case of capital fraud or missing substance
Shell Company / Abusive Arrangement (Briefkasten-UG)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief and refund of withholding taxes; reclassification of income to beneficial owner; imposition of secondary tax liability; administrative penalty under §72 AO up to EUR 25,000; mandatory liquidation order by commercial register; revocation of permitted activity and exclusion from EU market

Director personal liability: Director/shareholder held jointly and severally for unpaid corporation tax and trade tax if assets were extracted; possible criminal charge for evading withholding taxes and for false declarations; travel restrictions and surrender of passport pending tax investigation; imprisonment for fraudulent concealment of assets
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance (Geldwäschegesetz)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EUR 200,000 for natural persons and up to EUR 1,000,000 for corporate entities under GwG; criminal penalty for concealment of beneficial owners up to 5 years imprisonment; prohibition from obtaining licenses to provide virtual CFO services; disclosure to FIU (Financial Intelligence Unit); contract termination by payment rails (Stripe, Wise) and freezing of funds

Director personal liability: Directors individually liable for AML breaches; statutory disqualification from holding management positions in German companies; seizure of digital assets and bank accounts; travel bans where asset movement is suspected; extradition risk for cross-border obstruction of tax enforcement
