Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Business Register (Eesti Äriregister) operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK), Tartu; filing is accepted through the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) online portal with digital ID authentication.

Governing statute

Commercial Code of Estonia (Äriseadustik, RT I 1995, 26, 355), as amended: Section 135 (private limited company definition), Sections 136-140 (share capital, shares and payment), Sections 142-150 (management board), Section 152 (registration application); supplemented by the General Part of the Civil Code Act and the State Fees Act (Riigilõivuseadus) for filing tariffs.

Formation / constitutional filings

Registration application (registreerimisavaldus) in the e-Business Register, executed with qualified digital signatures and accompanied by the foundation agreement, articles of association (põhikiri), and consents of management board members.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 45 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No meaningful statutory minimum: the Commercial Code sets a technical floor of EUR 0.01 for the share capital of an OÜ; contributions need not be paid before filing and may be settled in money or non-cash assets within one year after registration (ÄS section 140(1)). No escrow account is required; the registry does not verify payment prior to entry. If a higher share capital is declared, the registry may request proof if the amount appears abusive, but normal e-commerce fulfilment capital of EUR 1-2,500 is accepted without escrow.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) covering escape routes, fire alarm system, extinguishers and fire-fighting rules under the Fire Protection Act; structural load clearance integrated into the municipal building operation permit (kasutusluba) confirming that racking and loading-dock loads are within design limits; workplace clearance by the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon) based on the workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, including fork-lift safety, dock guarding, PPE and emergency plans for the 3PL cross-dock site.

Paid-in capital rule: No meaningful statutory minimum: the Commercial Code sets a technical floor of EUR 0.01 for the share capital of an OÜ; contributions need not be paid before filing and may be settled in money or non-cash assets within one year after registration (ÄS section 140(1)). No escrow account is required; the registry does not verify payment prior to entry. If a higher share capital is declared, the registry may request proof if the amount appears abusive, but normal e-commerce fulfilment capital of EUR 1-2,500 is accepted without escrow.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) the Foundation Agreement and Articles of Association submitted for registration are hereby adopted in final form; (2) the founder's subscription for shares is accepted, and the Management Board is authorised to take all acts needed to perfect share payments, the declared share capital being lawfully adequate for the company's stated objects; (3) the registered office of the company is fixed at [address], Tallinn, Estonia, as stated in the registry application; (4) [Name, personal code] is appointed member of the Management Board with sole right of signature; (5) the Management Board is authorised to open bank accounts, retain professional advisers, procure warehouse premises and equipment, and file all statutory registrations and tax notifications required for commencing cross-dock order-fulfilment operations.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No general industrial/operating licence is mandated for warehousing and storage under EMTAK 5210 in Estonia; the relevant reference authorities are the Local Government building authority (premises use), the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (customs/excise arrangements if offering bonded warehouse services), and the Rescue Board (fire safety). An optional Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) or customs warehouse authorisation under Article 240 of Regulation (EU) No 952/2013 may be applied for voluntarily at the Tax and Customs Board.

Category: Building operation permit (kasutusluba) authorising the premises for warehouse/logistics use; optional customs warehouse authorisation (luba, UCC Art. 240) and excise warehouse licence (if excise goods are stored).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet) - e-MTA online portal (www.emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain statutory TIN/VAT ID by registering on the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) for a registry code, or, when acting as a foreign undertaking without local incorporation, by filing VAT registration application via the e-MTA portal. The application must include proof of foreign registration, constitutional documents, and a designated local representative for tax procedures. An e-Residency digital ID may be used to complete the filing online.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities obtain statutory TIN/VAT ID by registering on the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) for a registry code, or, when acting as a foreign undertaking without local incorporation, by filing VAT registration application via the e-MTA portal. The application must include proof of foreign registration, constitutional documents, and a designated local representative for tax procedures. An e-Residency digital ID may be used to complete the filing online.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available. The default regime taxes OÜ as a separate entity; retained profits are not subject to income tax until distribution. Statutory elections include (i) applying the reduced 14% corporate income tax rate on regular dividends distributed out of up to 7% of the entity's equity (for EU/EEA corporate recipients), (ii) forming a VAT group with closely linked Estonian entities, and (iii) choosing instalment declaration of dividend distributions. Hybrid mismatch regulations are mandatory under ATAD and do not provide elective avoidance mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 (annual taxable turnover for Estonian-established businesses); non-resident businesses registering for Estonian VAT are subject to no threshold and must register before making any taxable supply in Estonia

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to (i) goods physically exported from Estonian territory to a destination outside the EU, and (ii) certain intermediate services (e.g., transport, warehousing, cross-dock handling, and logistics) supplied directly in connection with such export or performed for a non-EU customer, provided the place of supply is Estonia and the underlying goods leave the EU. Proof of export must be obtained and retained by the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank transfer/transaction evidence)
  • Warehouse / inventory dispatch records for cross-dock fulfillment

Estonia does not implement a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. E-invoicing via the e-MTA interoperable systems is accepted and encouraged; VAT returns are submitted electronically through API and e-MTA portals by the statutory filing date (up to the 20th day of the month following the tax period). No continuous transaction control mechanism or pre-clearance of individual invoices is currently imposed.

Estonian Tax and Customs Board may retrospectively deny zero-rating where the exporter lacks complete and timely export evidence; VAT becomes payable with late interest accruing at 0.06% per day. Non-compliance fines range up to EUR 1,200 for minor offenses and up to EUR 25,600 for significant violations; intentional fraudulent zero-rating claims may trigger criminal prosecution carrying imprisonment and limitation extension to seven years. Customs audit reviews also scrutinize the validity of the exporter's customs status and the existence of an actual physical export flow.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general restrictions on converting or transferring funds for current or capital transactions. A resident OÜ may generally receive capital, pay suppliers, hold foreign-currency accounts, and remit dividends or other lawful payments cross-border without a currency-conversion approval or profit-repatriation permit. Banks and payment providers may require evidence of the transaction's purpose, beneficial ownership, source of funds, tax treatment, and sanctions compliance. Dividends are distributable only from legally available profits and subject to Estonian company-law, solvency, accounting, and applicable tax requirements. Estonia generally taxes distributed profits at company level; the applicable rate and treatment should be verified for the distribution period. EU financial sanctions, AML/CFT controls, reporting obligations, and any restrictions applicable to a counterparty or destination remain binding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory deadline requires an Estonian exporter to repatriate export proceeds to Estonia, and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion or surrender ratio applies. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to banking terms, accounting, tax, sanctions, and contractual requirements. VAT and customs reporting, proof of export, and accurate recognition of revenue remain applicable. For cross-border e-commerce fulfillment, maintain contracts, invoices, shipment and customs records, warehouse evidence, and payment reconciliations to substantiate the underlying trade.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia's national central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. Estonia has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine exchange controls; relevant payment-services, prudential, sanctions, and AML/CFT supervision is divided among competent Estonian and EU authorities, including the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority and the Financial Intelligence Unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium to High for supplementary payments and multi-currency collections, subject to provider underwriting, supported-country rules, and transaction profile. Airwallex and other appropriately licensed EU or EEA payment institutions or electronic money institutions may provide payment accounts and payment services, but an EMI account is not necessarily a bank deposit and may not support every Estonian corporate function, local cash service, credit product, or statutory capital procedure. Verify the provider's current home-state authorization and passporting status in the relevant official register, safeguarding arrangements, account-holder eligibility, and permitted use. Estonia's euro-area membership supports SEPA euro payments; international or non-euro transfers may use SWIFT or provider-specific rails. Do not describe an EMI balance as a protected bank deposit unless the applicable deposit-guarantee regime expressly covers it.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Receive equity contributions or shareholder loans through a transfer from an account in the investor's or lender's name where practicable. Provide accurate legal names, registration details, IBAN or other beneficiary account details, BIC where required, and a concise payment reference identifying the transaction as an equity contribution, share subscription, or shareholder loan. Estonia has no general FX approval or mandatory capital-inflow conversion requirement; do not use an invented statutory purpose code. Supply the bank with the subscription or loan documentation, corporate approvals, ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, and a reconciliation to the company's accounts. Box 2 Social tax on employee wages and benefits Not applicable for non-resident employees unless they are Estonian tax residents or work in Estonia; otherwise leave blank. Late filing penalty of €200 per month (max €2,000) plus interest at 0.06% per day on unpaid tax; late payment penalty of 0.06% per day.
Box 3 Withholding tax on payments to non-residents (royalties, interest, etc.) Report any Estonian-source royalties or interest paid to non-residents; apply treaty relief if TRC provided. Late filing penalty of €200 per month (max €2,000) plus interest at 0.06% per day on unpaid tax; late payment penalty of 0.06% per day.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50,000 (for goods) and €20,000 (for services) per controlled transaction; if total controlled transactions exceed €1,000,000, a full Local File is required.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not have specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. The entity must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., office, employees, decision-making) in Estonia to avoid being classified as a shell company and to benefit from the 0% retained earnings regime.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €1,000,000 for the company; additional tax plus interest at 0.06% per day; criminal prosecution for intentional evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly participated in evasion; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years for criminal tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of retained earnings as deemed distributions, triggering 20% tax; penalties up to €32,000; potential denial of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for the tax shortfall if they failed to ensure substance; possible disqualification as director.
AML / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €5,000,000 for the company; confiscation of assets; criminal prosecution for money laundering (imprisonment up to 8 years).

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment, if they knowingly facilitated money laundering; travel bans and asset freezes.
