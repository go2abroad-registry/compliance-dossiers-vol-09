Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); tax administration and VAT services are available through the e-MTA portal.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ is assigned a registry code upon entry in the Estonian Commercial Register; this code is used as its domestic tax identification number. Apply for VAT registration through e-MTA or the prescribed application process. A person whose taxable turnover in Estonia exceeds EUR 40,000 from the beginning of a calendar year must generally apply within three working days of exceeding the threshold. A foreign taxable person making supplies in Estonia may be required to register from its first taxable supply, subject to statutory exceptions, including applicable reverse-charge treatment. A non-EU applicant may be required to appoint a fiscal representative; the MTA may waive this requirement for qualifying applicants. The MTA issues the VAT identification number after registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An Estonian OÜ is generally taxed as a separate legal entity, with corporate income tax arising principally when profits are distributed or treated as distributed. Estonia has no U.S.-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no general election to treat it as a pass-through entity. EU cross-border hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or require inclusion in defined cases; they are anti-avoidance rules, not elective entity-classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of qualifying taxable turnover in Estonia in a calendar year. The threshold does not generally protect a foreign taxable person from registration where that person makes a taxable supply in Estonia; statutory exceptions and reverse-charge rules must be assessed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may qualify for the 0% VAT rate where the goods are dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU and the supplier can substantiate the export and its connection to the supply. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer or payment is offshore: treatment depends on place-of-supply rules and any applicable exemption or special zero-rate provision. Supplies to another EU Member State are intra-Community supplies, not exports, and require separate conditions and evidence. Input VAT recovery is subject to statutory deduction rules and supporting documentation.

  • Customs export declaration and evidence of exit from the EU, such as validated customs-system export results
  • Commercial invoice and contract or purchase order identifying the goods, parties, and supply
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR, or carrier confirmation
  • Proof of payment or remittance where relevant to substantiate the transaction; Estonia does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory export zero-rating document

Estonia does not generally require real-time tax-authority clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory clearance through an authority API for domestic VAT invoices. E-invoices are used in public-sector transactions, and since 1 July 2025 a buyer that has registered to receive e-invoices may require a seller to issue invoices in the prescribed e-invoice format under applicable rules. VAT invoices must satisfy statutory content and retention requirements; VAT returns and related filings are submitted electronically through e-MTA.

Estonia imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. A taxpayer claiming zero-rating must retain adequate transaction, transport, and customs evidence and cooperate with MTA verification. Unsupported or ineligible zero-rating can result in reassessment of VAT, statutory interest on tax arrears, and applicable tax-proceeding penalties. Intentional tax fraud may also attract criminal liability under Estonian law. No export-specific 180-day repatriation rule applies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility. Estonia is a Eurozone member and EU Member State, so capital movements are fully liberalized under TFEU Article 63 and the EU Capital Movement Directive. The national Foreign Exchange Act has been repealed; no exchange control approval is required for inbound equity injections, loan capital, dividend repatriation, or trade settlements. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject only to standard Estonian withholding tax on dividends (20% standard; 14% if paid on a regular basis with underlying 7% corporate tax) and no separate foreign-exchange license is needed. ECB Balance of Payments statistical reporting obligations apply at the bank level for cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion regime. Estonian exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in EUR or any foreign currency, with no mandated timeline for conversion. Export proceeds can be held onshore in multicurrency current accounts or used directly for supplier settlements via SWIFT. The only obligation is transactional reporting for external statistics (ECB BPM6 classification), handled automatically by the resident bank via the e-statistical reporting system (REP) to Eesti Pank.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) within the Eurosystem, European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and payment system oversight, and the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for banking conduct

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is High for ancillary treasury rails but Medium as a sole primary bank account. EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized under PSD2 may passport into Estonia; examples include Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, and the Estonian-licensed EMI Enty. They support SWIFT/SEPA wires, virtual IBANs, and multicurrency balances. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot grant credit, are not covered by the Estonian Deposit Guarantee Fund (deposits are safeguarded, not insured), and Finantsinspektsioon imposes enhanced AML scrutiny on cross-border merchants. Recommended architecture: hold primary liquidity at LHV or Swedbank and use one EMI for cross-border collection from distributors, then sweep to the licensed bank account.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained and reinvested profits; 20% standard rate on gross distributed profits; 14% reduced rate for regular qualifying profit distributions (20/80 and 14/86 on net payout)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

No separate dividend withholding tax exists in Estonia; the distributing OÜ is charged CIT at 20% of the gross distribution (or 14% for qualifying regular dividends). Economically, the exporter does not withhold but bears the tax before remittance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD must be filed by the 10th day of the month following the tax period (e.g., by 10 February for January taxable events), unless extended by emergency orders.

Box 1 (Standard-distribution CIT) Gross amount of profit distributions paid during the month that do not qualify for the reduced rate; calculate 20% tax (formula: 20/80 of net amount paid). Non-resident shareholders are not to be listed as taxpayers; report the company's own CIT liability on the distribution, regardless of the recipient's country. No additional withholding is to be mechanically applied. Late filing: maximum coercion fine up to EUR 5,000 under the Taxation Act; late payment interest accrues at 0.06% per day on unpaid tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual cross-border controlled transactions triggers mandatory declaration in the TSD controlled-transaction annex; the Estonian Tax and Customs Board may demand a full Local File above EUR 1,000,000 per related party or aggregate EUR 2,500,000. Master File/CbCR is compulsory for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 in accordance with OECD BEPS reporting.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations akin to UAE/BVI. However, the Estonian tax administration applies a functional and factual substance test in transfer pricing audits, CFC enquiries and e-Residency tax residence analyses. An OÜ manufacturing and calibrating multi-parameter ECG monitors must demonstrate: (i) board decisions in Estonia; (ii) qualified clinical/engineering personnel in Estonia; (iii) physical calibration assets and inventory in Estonia; and (iv) assumption of production risk. Lack of substance can cause the company to become tax-resident in the shareholder's state, invalidate treaty benefits, or render distributions taxable under CFC rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False TSD Declaration

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 7,500 per company or criminal tax fraud imprisonment of up to 5 years; aggravated cases involving large amounts or organized groups carry up to 10 years' imprisonment, confiscation of assets and tax arrears with daily penalty interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TSDs are jointly and severally liable for the tax arrears under the taxation and tort rules; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes and disqualification orders for up to 3 years; criminal conviction leads to direct imprisonment.
Prohibited Profit Distribution / Capital Adequacy Violation

Statutory sanction: Board members are required to return the outflow or compensate creditors; administrative penalty under the Commercial Code and possible criminal liability for fraudulent balance sheet; the tax authority may disregard the distribution and reclassify it as a taxable transaction.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced by statute: management board members who authorized the distribution are personally and severally liable for the amount repaid to the company and for creditors' losses; travel bans can be ordered during insolvency proceedings, and criminal insolvency offenses may lead to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Hidden Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Mandatory tax adjustment to the arm's length benchmarked profit; additional 20% CIT on the deemed distribution plus 0.06% daily penalty interest; penalties for incorrect tax returns under the Taxation Act; where a stateless or low-tax affiliate is involved, CFC and DAC6 aggressive-arrangement reporting obligations may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally engineered the mispricing are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax if the company cannot pay; in insolvency, courts can order contribution, travel bars and criminal proceedings for fraudulent transfer of assets.
e-Residency Shell Company / AML Abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act of up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons (or 5% of total turnover); criminal money laundering penalties up to 8 years' imprisonment; revocation of e-Residency status is imposed administratively by the Police and Border Guard Board.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors who knowingly exercise control through shells are treated as principals; civil forfeiture of the transferred assets, personal restitution, travel bans and extradition may follow; directors can also be disqualified from acting as directors of an OÜ for up to 5 years.
