Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-services portal e-MTA (e-maksuamet) and state portal eesti.ee; commercial register serviced by the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) for the registry code that functions as the statutory tax number.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the OÜ via the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) using e-Residency digital ID to obtain a unique registry code (ärregistri kood), which is the default tax number for CIT, VAT, and social tax; no separate CIT account is required. Step 2: Apply for VAT registration in e-MTA within 5 working days from the day on which taxable turnover exceeded or is objectively expected to exceed EUR 40,000 over a rolling 12-month period; VAT registration is retroactive to the date the threshold was exceeded. Step 3: A non-resident entrepreneur with no seat and no permanent establishment in Estonia must appoint an Estonian VAT representative (jointly and severally liable for VAT) before or together with the VAT registration application; the representative must be entered in the tax register. Step 4: MTA issues a VAT registration certificate with the KMKR number (format EE followed by the existing registry code) and assigns the filing frequency; registration may be refused or annulled where the applicant has no permanent establishment, no genuine economic activity, or material tax arrears. Step 5: Submit VAT returns (form KMD), Intrastat declarations, and OSS/IOSS returns through e-MTA APIs or web e-services; e-Residency digital identity enables fully remote incorporation, signing, and ongoing tax correspondence without physical presence in Estonia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — Estonia applies a deferred, distribution-based corporate income tax: undistributed profits of the OÜ are taxed at 0% until distribution. Distributions and deemed distributions (fringe benefits, non-business expenses, gifts) are taxed at 20/80 of the net amount, i.e., 25.0% effective on the gross distribution. Regular profit distributions meeting the statutory history and cap tests are taxed at 14/86, i.e., 14.0% effective, limited to the average taxable profit of the three preceding tax years. Profits attributable to a permanent establishment of a non-resident are taxed at 20/80 upon repatriation. The OÜ is a resident company with unlimited tax liability under the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus); it is not a pass-through entity under Estonian, EU, or US check-the-box concepts, but profits of foreign PEs of the OÜ are exempt under the territorial participation exemption where distributive criteria are met.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Estonian law; the OÜ is always an entity-level corporate taxpayer. Available elections include: (a) voluntary VAT registration before reaching the legal threshold; (b) VAT grouping of closely linked taxable persons under the VatS, upon joint application to the MTA; (c) group taxation election allowing an EEA-registered parent company to form a consolidated Estonian tax group, deferring distribution tax on intra-group distributions; (d) the regular distribution election that applies the reduced 14/86 rate to usual profit distributions declared at least once in each of the three preceding tax years and capped at the prior three-year average; (e) Union OSS and Import OSS/IOSS elections for cross-border distance sales and low-value import consignments, avoiding multiple VAT registrations in other Member States; and (f) the second-hand goods margin scheme election under the VatS for vintage luxury resale, which taxes only the margin while the ordinary invoice-based VAT method remains an available contractual alternative.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supplies of goods and services over a rolling 12-month period triggers compulsory VAT registration from the day the threshold is reached; intra-Community distance sales and cross-border B2C digital services are subject to the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold with the Union OSS available above that level; imports of goods in consignments not exceeding EUR 150 in value may use the Import One-Stop Shop, and marketplace deemed-supplier rules apply to facilitated import consignments; non-established foreign operators have no minimum threshold and may be registered from the first taxable transaction, including at the MTA's own initiative.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically dispatched from Estonia to a destination outside the EU qualify for the 0% export rate where the taxable person holds a customs export declaration (SAD/EXA) and proves that the goods left EU customs territory within the statutory period, generally three months from the date of supply (VatS export rules); the export must be reported in the KMD VAT return. Services directly related to exported goods (freight, storage, packaging, inspection, commission) are zero-rated or fall under the recipient's reverse charge. Intra-EU supplies of goods to a taxable person in another Member State are zero-rated where the customer holds a valid VAT registration number confirmed in VIES and the dispatch is documented. For vintage luxury goods acquired and resold by the OÜ, the second-hand goods margin scheme taxes only the margin (sale price less purchase price) and remains compatible with export zero-rating, provided the margin is separately recorded. Directly attributable input VAT on exports is refundable, with MTA refund periods of about 30-60 days for zero-rated exporters under normal conditions.

  • Bill of Lading (maritime), Air Waybill (air), or CMR/CIM consignment note (road/rail) in the exporter's name showing the consignee, the destination outside the EU, and the dispatch and exit dates
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or authenticated bank statement evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the non-resident buyer within the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list describing the luxury goods, quantities, declared value, and country of origin

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or transaction-level pre-approval regime exists in Estonia for ordinary B2B/B2C sales; an invoice must be issued within 7 calendar days of the supply (or of payment if earlier), and invoices must be retained for 7 years. E-invoicing is mandatory only for supplies to state and municipal authorities, using the EN 16931/UBL standard via PEPPOL or the central public-sector e-invoice portal. VAT returns (form KMD), Intrastat listings, OSS declarations, import declarations, and SAF-T-style audit files are submitted digitally through e-MTA using REST/API or web forms; MTA has the right to request real-time API visibility into sales registers, e-commerce platform transaction data, and payment data under the EU e-commerce VAT compliance package.

Estonian foreign exchange law is fully liberalized and imposes no general statutory surrender deadline; nevertheless, MTA applies a 180-day evidentiary benchmark for receipt of export proceeds. Where the exporter fails to prove the physical exit of the goods within the statutory period or lacks the required documents, the MTA retrospectively recharacterizes the supply as a domestic sale at 20% VAT, imposes late-payment interest at 0.06% per day, and levies statutory penalty payments for false or incomplete declarations, with increased penalties for repeated violations and criminal liability under Penal Code sections 384-385 for deliberate tax fraud (pecuniary punishment or imprisonment of up to five years for large-scale evasion). MTA algorithms cross-match customs exit declarations, transport data, bank statements, and VAT return lines; unwarranted zero-rating claims are clawed back under reassessment limitation periods of 3 years, extended to 6 years for gross negligence and 10 years for concealed non-declaration. Directors, beneficial owners, and VAT representatives may be held personally liable, the company may be placed on the MTA public risk list, and the VAT registration may be revoked with immediate effect; fraudulent export chains, circular dispatches, and margin-scheme misuse are also prosecuted under the anti-money-laundering framework.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Estonia is a euro-area Member State and applies the free movement of capital under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Both current-account and capital-account payments are unrestricted. Profit remittance has no foreign-exchange hurdle; the only material commercial hurdle is to demonstrate distributable reserves and to settle the applicable Estonian distributed-profit corporate income tax before the dividend is paid. A bank may also request the board resolution, financial statements, and tax filing evidence for a dividend or loan-repayment outflow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists. Statutory timeline: N/A; export and marketplace receipts are immediately free for retention, reinvestment, or settlement. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. The OÜ may hold export proceeds in EUR or any foreign currency without central-bank approval, subject only to normal bookkeeping and VAT/income tax documentation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is the national central bank and part of the Eurosystem; euro monetary policy is decided by the European Central Bank. Domestic prudential and market-conduct supervision for banks, payment institutions, and e-money institutions is exercised under the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority framework within Eesti Pank. There is no separate Estonian foreign-exchange licensing authority, and no currency-conversion permit is required for lawful cross-border payments.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on distributed profit (gross basis, equivalent to 20/80 on net distribution); 0% on retained and reinvested profit

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax at source. Estate does not impose a separate withholding tax on dividends; the economic burden is the 20% profit tax paid by EE_OU on distribution (20/80 of net dividend, which equals 20% of the gross distribution).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000. Estonian transfer pricing documentation is required where the annual aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions conducted by EE_OU exceeds EUR 50,000. The Master File and the Local File requirements apply to groups exceeding the OECD Country-by-Country threshold of EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue; a lower simplified documentation threshold may apply to loans, interest, royalties and management fees.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, Estonian courts, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board, the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test require genuine economic substance where a company is resident in Estonia. For an OÜ operating vintage luxury resale online, substance is demonstrated by physical or recognized virtual office, documented board decisions taken in Estonia, Estonian bank account or regulated payment gateway under contract, local administrative or part-time staffing, operational inventory risk, authenticity-assessment procedures, and complete accounting records kept in Estonia. An e-Residency digital identity alone is never economic substance. A company nominally resident but directed and managed abroad can be reassessed by the beneficial owner's jurisdiction as a tax resident there, and MTA may disregard the artificial Estonian seat, recapturing the benefit of the 0% CIT deferral.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Misdeclaration of Distributions

Statutory sanction: Estonian Tax and Customs Board assesses the missed deemed distribution at 20% CIT plus 0.06% daily interest. Tax fraud prosecution under the Criminal Code may result in a fine of up to EUR 400,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years; the court may additionally order confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false TSD declarations is personally exposed to criminal prosecution, civil recovery, a management-ban order, travel restrictions and, in serious cases, custodial detention pending trial. Corporate veil-piercing allows MTA to collect unpaid tax from the director's personal assets where the company is a mere instrumentality.
Shell Company - E-Residency Abuse

Statutory sanction: MTA applies the general anti-abuse rule and may treat the company as a sham, recasting accumulated profits as taxable distributions at 20%. The Commercial Register may dissolve the company for lack of genuine business purpose. In the shareholder's home jurisdiction, CFC rules may tax the undistributed profit immediately. Combined penalties and interest may exceed 50% of the accumulated surplus.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors risk personal liability for the company's resultant tax debt. The Commercial Register may apply a coercive penalty, and under criminal procedure travel bans and seizure of passports are available where the individual intends to leave without settling MTA claims.
AML / Counterfeit Money-Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit may impose a legal-person fine up to EUR 4,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act. Payment service providers may freeze Stripe and Escrow_Pay accounts. The OÜ's VAT number and authorization to trade may be suspended, and luxury goods are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers involved in laundering proceeds through consignment-based resale are personally punishable with imprisonment of up to 8 years, fines, restitution orders, and confiscation of personal assets. International travel restrictions and euro-arrest warrants may be issued.
VAT and Customs Fraud - Cross-Border E-Commerce

Statutory sanction: MTA assesses VAT and late interest at 0.06% per day. Customs proceedings can impose penalties up to EUR 320,000 and forfeiture of goods. If fraud is established, prison terms of up to 5 years and additional criminal fines are available.

Director personal liability: A director participating in false customs valuations or cross-border VAT evasion is personally liable as an offender; MTA may assert financial liability over the director's assets, while the Criminal Code supports detention, travel bans, and prohibition on management.
Transfer Pricing Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: MTA adjusts the taxable base under the arm's-length principle and recharacterizes the excess fee as a deemed profit distribution subject to 20% CIT. Penalties and daily interest are charged on the difference; if the scheme is considered fraudulent profit-stripping, criminal penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused approval of the non-arm's-length fee may be jointly liable for the additional CIT, interest, and penalties. A court may impose a director disqualification for repeated transfer-pricing fraud and order personal restitution where the parent is insolvent or beyond Estonian jurisdiction.
