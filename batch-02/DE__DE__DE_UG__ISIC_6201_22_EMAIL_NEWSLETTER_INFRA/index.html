Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG requires an initial share capital of at least EUR 1.00 paid in cash or in kind at fair value; contributions must be fully paid and at the free disposal of the managing directors; no escrow account is mandated for cash contributions. The company must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG §5a(3)).

Funding & unlock path: Workplace safety certification is required for any office premises, including fire safety provisions, emergency exits, and first-aid equipment, in accordance with the Arbeitsschutzgesetz and DGUV regulations. Occupational health clearance is evidenced by a risk assessment and involvement of the company doctor (Betriebsarzt) if staff exceed statutory thresholds.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office (for VAT ID, federal withholding, EU OSS); local Finanzamt (district tax office) for corporate income, trade tax, and VAT assessment; official electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

After notarization of the articles of association and commercial register entry (Handelsregister), the UG must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire) via ELSTER with the competent local Finanzamt. The Finanzamt thereafter issues the Tax Number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. For cross-border transactions, a separate VAT Identification Number (USt-IdNr.) is applied for online from BZSt; foreign parent entities must also evidence their status; non-resident shareholders do not require specific licensing. Registration deadlines are generally one month after business commencement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The UG is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and solidarity surcharge. Available elections include: corporate fiscal unity (Organschaft) via profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Unternehmensvertrag) for income tax and trade tax; VAT fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) election; VAT small-business exemption election under Sec. 19 UStG; and reduced 7% VAT rate where statutory conditions are met (not applicable to email newsletter SaaS standard services).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 annual turnover for the small-business exemption (Sec. 19 UStG, effective 2025); no registration threshold for non-resident suppliers of digital/B2C services to German customers - registration effective from the first euro (Union OSS procedure mandatory; intra-Community distance-sales threshold EUR 10,000 applies Union-wide)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the email newsletter infrastructure SaaS (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 518210), German VAT is not chargeable if the place of supply is outside Germany: for B2B services, the customer's location governs (Sec. 3a UStG) with the customer's valid EU or non-EU VAT ID; for B2C digital services to non-EU consumers, the customer's residence/place of establishment governs, and such services fall outside the EU VAT scope. Intra-EU B2B digital services are reverse-charged and B2C intra-EU digital services are reportable via the OSS scheme. Export of goods, if any ancillary, qualifies for zero-rating under Sec. 6 UStG subject to evidence of physical export.

  • Valid customer VAT ID or equivalent foreign tax registration number verified via VIES/OECD exchange where applicable
  • Executed service contract and itemized invoice evidencing place of supply and customer business status
  • Transactional technical logs (IP geolocation, email/DNS routing records, platform account registration data) evidencing customer location
  • Bank statements and payment records (Stripe Billing and credit-card settlement reports) evidencing actual consideration and FX repatriation
  • Accounting ledger records and ELSTER/BZSt-based zero-rating or reverse-charge confirmations

Phased domestic B2B electronic invoicing mandate (E-Rechnung): from 1 January 2025 every German-resident business must be able to receive structured XML invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD format) and, from 1 January 2027 (issuers with turnover above EUR 800,000) and 1 January 2028 (all issuers), must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies. There is no real-time fiscal clearance/stamping engine comparable to the French or Italian models; clearance occurs indirectly through ELSTER tax returns, the EU OSS portal, and annual E-Bilanz (electronic tax balance sheet) transmission. GoBD data-retention and audit-track rules also apply to accounting records.

Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment with interest of 0.5% per month (Hinterziehungszinsen), late-payment surcharge up to 1% per month (maximum five years but at least EUR 25), and, where fraud or recklessness is proven, criminal penalties under Sec. 370 AO of up to five years' imprisonment (aggravated cases: six months to ten years) plus fines. Additionally, the invoice issuer may owe the VAT under Sec. 25d UStG, and the responsible managing director can be held personally liable in restitution proceedings for unpaid tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a euro-area member state, guarantees full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the euro under TFEU Articles 63–66 and Directive 88/361/EEC, implemented by the AWG/AWV. No exchange-control licence, prior authorisation, rationing, dual-exchange-rate mechanism, minimum holding period or import/export cover requirement applies to a Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) (UG). Inward equity contributions, outward trade disbursements, profit repatriation and dividend distributions are unrestricted under FX law; the operative constraints are corporate and fiscal only: (i) under GmbHG § 5a(3), the UG must appropriate 25% of the annual surplus (after offsetting prior-year losses) to a statutory reserve until the subscribed share capital plus accumulated reserves reach EUR 25,000; (ii) under GmbHG §§ 30–31, distributions are lawful only to the extent net assets do not fall below the subscribed capital, i.e., the UG cannot distribute 'capital-protection' assets to shareholders; (iii) dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder attract German withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (aggregate 26.375%, plus church tax where the shareholder is a natural person), reducible under the applicable double-tax treaty (typically a 5%–15% treaty rate for corporate shareholders holding a qualifying participation) provided the beneficial-ownership and substance conditions are met; (iv) royalties for software/SaaS licensing paid by the UG to an EU/EEA corporate recipient may be exempt from German withholding under the EU Interest & Royalties Directive, whereas payments to non-EEA recipients may fall under § 50a EStG withholding and require professional determination. There is no FX hurdle to paying director salaries, settling foreign cloud invoices, or converting USD receipts at market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. German law imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation timeline or minimum holding period for export receipts; the UG may lawfully retain 100% of USD-denominated SaaS subscription proceeds in a foreign-currency account or convert them to EUR at market rates. The only mandatory discipline is statistical reporting under AWV § 67: every German resident, including a UG, must report to the Deutsche Bundesbank any payment received from, or made to, a non-resident within five business days when the individual transaction exceeds EUR 12,500. The report must state the counterparty, amount, currency and the standardized purpose code from the Bundesbank's external-statistics classification; for the email-infrastructure SaaS scope, the applicable code category is computer/data-processing services (Dienstleistungen). Card settlements batched and remitted by a payment processor (Stripe) through an EU entity are not individually reportable where the immediate settlement payer is the processor's EU establishment, but the UG must be able to evidence from processor settlement reports that the aggregated flows correspond to cross-border SaaS exports; any direct SWIFT receipt from a foreign merchant exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported separately. Failure to report is an administrative fine; it is not a currency-convertibility restriction.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German central bank, headquartered in Frankfurt am Main), acting through its nine Hauptverwaltungen (regional offices) as the monetary, financial-statistics and external-payments authority; Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as the prudential licensing and supervisory authority for banks and payment institutions under the Kreditwesengesetz (KWG) and the Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG); and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action together with the Federal Ministry of Finance as custodians of the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG) and the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV). The Bundesbank does not operate an exchange-control approval regime: its role is statistical monitoring, balance-of-payments compilation and oversight of payment and securities settlement systems.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 0.825% solidarity surcharge = 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) assessed at 7–17% depending on municipality, yielding an indicative combined effective rate of approximately 30–33% for ordinary active trade in major German cities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375%; under most German DTAs a corporate shareholder holding at least 10% may claim reduction to 5%, while portfolio dividends are often reduced to 15%.

Within seven months from the fiscal year-end: for a calendar-year UG, the annual return is due by 31 July of the following year; representation by a professional tax adviser triggers a blanket extension under current administrative practice to 30 April of the second subsequent tax year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 for annual cross-border controlled transactions in goods and EUR 500,000 for controlled services; a German Master File and Local File must be prepared for the UG where the multinational group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000, triggering country-by-country reporting obligations.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply sector-neutral Economic Substance Regulations, but substance is enforced through the registered seat and place-of-management tests (§§11-12 AO), permanent establishment principles, controlled foreign company rules (§§7-13 AStG), the general anti-abuse doctrine (§42 AO) and treaty-benefit limitation. A UG operating an email newsletter infrastructure business must show actual German management, payroll, office premises, local bank accounts, service-delivery functions and real commercial risk; otherwise income can be reattributed, treaty relief denied and CFC taxation triggered at shareholder level.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under §370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine; particularly serious cases carry 6 months to 10 years; additionally, all under-assessed taxes fall due with late-payment interest and confiscation of the economic benefit.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal criminal liability, confiscation of personal assets, passport seizure, travel bans, identity-card detention, civil restitution to the tax authority, and limitation of liability is pierced where the UG is used as a sham vehicle.
Negligent Late Filing and Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Late-payment interest at 0.15% per month, late-filing surcharges up to EUR 25,000, coercive fines up to EUR 25,000, enforced seizure of bank accounts and receivables, and in repeated or structured cases criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for withheld wage tax and withholding taxes under §§35 and 69 AO; continuing payments after insolvency creates liability under insolvency law; courts may issue travel bans and passport confiscation during enforcement or criminal investigation.
Transfer Pricing and Economic Substance Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under §162 AO, imposition of a documentation surcharge up to EUR 1,000,000, recharacterization of payments as constructive dividends, denial of treaty relief, CFC reattribution of income, plus 6% or higher annual interest on the resulting tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors bear the burden of proving arm's length substance; where documentation cannot be produced, tax authorities may assess directly against the director in enforcement, and suspected fraudulent documentation exposes the director to criminal sanctions including travel bans, attachment of assets and imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML and Payment Rail Misuse

Statutory sanction: Conviction for money laundering under §261 StGB: imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years; commercial or gang-related cases carry 6 months to 10 years; asset forfeiture and fines equal to the full economic benefit obtained through the unlawful scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly using the UG as a laundering vehicle lose the limited-liability shield, face civil restitution to defrauded creditors, passport confiscation, travel bans, extradition proceedings, and both the managing director and shadow director may be imprisoned.
