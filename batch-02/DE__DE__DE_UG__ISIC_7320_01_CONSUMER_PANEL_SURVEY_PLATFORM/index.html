Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister), Abteilung B, maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) in the district of the registered office; the electronic application must be submitted exclusively by a German notary public.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz, GmbHG), in particular §§ 1, 2, 4b, 5, 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft), 7, 8, 10, 35; Companies Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung – HRV); Notarisation Act (Beurkundungsgesetz – BeurkG) § 40a; Company Law Digitalisation Act (DiRUG 2021) for online notarisation and electronic register filing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of the Unternehmergesellschaft (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister) together with the notarised Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or Model Protocol (Musterprotokoll), the certified list of shareholders and managing directors, the managing director's specimen signature, and the bank confirmation of the fully paid-up share capital.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 32 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG has no €25,000 statutory minimum and only requires a nominal share capital of at least EUR 1.00, but a working capital of EUR 1,000–3,000 is commercially recommended. All cash contributions must be fully paid in before registration and must be at the free disposal of the managing director; no escrow account (Sperrkonto) is mandatory for cash payments. Under § 5a(3) GmbHG, 25% of annual net profit must be retained in a statutory reserve until the capital reaches €25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Office workplace compliance under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung): fire-safety clearance covering escape routes, fire extinguishers, smoke detectors and an emergency evacuation plan; structural load inspection only if the registered office exceeds standard floor-load thresholds, which is generally unnecessary for a typical office of up to 500 m²; occupational health and safety risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung), ergonomic workstations, first-aid provision, and mandatory notification to the statutory accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft Handel und Warenlogistik).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF) as policy authority; Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt, www.bzst.de) as central agency issuing USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) and administering the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) procedure; local Tax Office (Finanzamt) responsible for tax assessment and allocation of the Steuernummer; electronic filing and registration portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned entrepreneurs incorporate the UG by executing Articles of Association before a Notar and filing for entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) — minimum capital of EUR 1 is permissible without a statutory minimum-capital requirement. Within one month of business commencement, the UG must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent local Finanzamt via the ELSTER portal, which automatically allocates the Steuernummer (income and payroll tax number) and determines VAT treatment. The UG then applies separately to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) using the online application form (USt 1 A); issuance typically requires 2–4 weeks. Non-resident ownership is unrestricted; a non-resident Geschäftsführer (managing director) must be named and may serve under EU/EEA residence rules, and the registered office must be maintained in Germany for all tax correspondence.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) per se, subject to: (i) corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% of worldwide taxable income, (ii) solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of corporate income tax (net 0.825% aggregate), and (iii) trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) levied by the municipality at a multiplier between 200% and 490% applied to the 3.5% base rate, yielding a typical effective rate of 14–17%. Combined effective company-level taxation approximates 30–32% of profits. Territorial scope: unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht (unlimited tax liability) under § 1 KStG on global income, with foreign tax credit under tax treaties or § 34c EStG. Distributions to shareholders attract 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus solidarity surcharge, being reduced under applicable double-tax treaties. No tax-exempt-zone status applies in Germany. Under § 5a GmbHG, the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000. — Foreign-owned entrepreneurs incorporate the UG by executing Articles of Association before a Notar and filing for entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) — minimum capital of EUR 1 is permissible without a statutory minimum-capital requirement. The UG then applies separately to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (VAT ID) using the online application form (USt 1 A); issuance typically requires 2–4 weeks. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) per se, subject to: (i) corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15% of worldwide taxable income, (ii) solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of corporate income tax (net 0.825% aggregate), and (iii) trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) levied by the municipality at a multiplier between 200% and 490% applied to the 3.5% base rate, yielding a typical effective rate of 14–17%. Combined effective company-level taxation approximates 30–32% of profits. Territorial scope: unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht (unlimited tax liability) under § 1 KStG on global income, with foreign tax credit under tax treaties or § 34c EStG. Distributions to shareholders attract 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus solidarity surcharge, being reduced under applicable double-tax treaties. No tax-exempt-zone status applies in Germany. Under § 5a GmbHG, the UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; the UG is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available elections include: (1) VAT small-business exemption under § 19 UStG where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 50,000, requested within the tax registration questionnaire; (2) waiver of VAT exemption under § 9 UStG for real-estate transactions; (3) formation of an Organschaft (VAT and corporate tax group) under § 2(2) No. 2 UStG and § 14 KStG, consolidating turnover, input VAT, and income tax with a qualifying controlling parent; (4) no partnership-option (check-the-box-like) election under § 1a KStG, which applies exclusively to partnerships, not to the UG; and (5) German anti-hybrid rules under §§ 4k, 4j EStG disallowing deductions for hybrid mismatch arrangements. Once capital reaches EUR 25,000, the UG may elect to convert into a standard GmbH.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic entrepreneurs must register for VAT from the first euro of business activity (no de-minimis VAT registration threshold for active entrepreneurs). A small-business (Kleinunternehmer) VAT exemption applies only where previous-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year expected turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000. For cross-border supplies: distance sales of goods to non-registered EU consumers are subject to the EUR 10,000 union-wide threshold, above which destination-country VAT applies via the OSS or local registration; for electronically supplied services (including consumer-panel survey SaaS), B2C supplies are always taxed in the customer's EU member state from the first euro, so the UG must charge the destination-country VAT rate and remit via the OSS (or register locally). For B2B cross-border services, no threshold applies — place of supply is the customer's country and reverse charge applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods to third countries (outside the EU) are zero-rated under § 6 UStG upon proof of export. Services are treated as not subject to German VAT where the place of supply is outside Germany: (1) B2B services to foreign entrepreneurs under § 3a(2) UStG — place of supply is the customer's establishment, the German supplier invoices without VAT, reverse charge applies in the customer's country, and the supplier must hold documentary proof of the customer's valid foreign VAT ID (VIES-verified for EU customers); (2) B2C electronically supplied services to non-EU consumers (including the consumer-panel survey platform's paid subscriptions and research reports) fall outside German VAT under § 3a(3) UStG; and (3) B2C digital services to EU consumers are taxed in the destination member state at the applicable local VAT rate, remitted through the OSS procedure. Because ISIC 7320 market research and consumer polling is an unregulated commercial activity, no statutory license is required for the zero-rating claim. The supplier may request a binding advance ruling (verbindliche Auskunft) from the Finanzamt for materially significant cross-border contracts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) — ATLAS/ICS electronic declaration acknowledgement with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) under the Union Customs Code
  • Bill of Lading (Konnossement), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR consignment note, or postal/courier receipt evidencing physical departure of exported goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice reconciling the foreign-currency consideration received from the overseas customer with the zero-rated invoice
  • Commercial invoice and signed service agreement demonstrating the cross-border B2B/B2C supply relationship and the place-of-supply determination
  • Customer's valid foreign VAT registration certificate or VIES confirmation of the customer's foreign VAT ID for B2B services
  • Digital evidence of customer residence for B2C electronically supplied services (IP address geolocation, billing country, payment instrument country, telecom subscriber data) under § 3a(3) UStG and the EU VAT digital-services rules
  • Handover protocol of the delivered research output (download receipts, dated e-mail confirmations, server access logs) evidencing completion of the market-research supply

Germany operates no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamp, or mandatory API-based tax clearance for standard invoices (unlike Italy or Spain). However, a statutory e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) mandate applies from 1 January 2025 for all domestic B2B supplies: issuers must transmit structured electronic invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1+ formats via PEPPOL or similar networks) and buyers must be able to receive such invoices; full structured e-invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions becomes obligatory by 1 January 2027. Every invoice under § 14 UStG must contain mandatory fields (full name/address of supplier and customer, supplier's VAT ID or Steuernummer, sequential invoice number, date of supply, quantity and nature of goods/services, net amount, applicable VAT rate and grossed-up VAT amount). Cash-register systems, if any, must use a certified Technical Security Element (TSE) under the Kassensicherungsverordnung. For the online market-research platform with PayPal Mass Payout and Wise settlement rails, B2C invoices must be issued within 6 months under § 33 UStDV. Digitized records must be retained under GoBD principles: 10 years for invoices and accounting records, 8 years for other business documents. The EU 'VAT in the Digital Age' (ViDA) package is expected to introduce transaction-based e-reporting for B2B supplies by 2030.

German tax authorities apply strict documentary-evidence standards: if the export declaration, transport evidence, or service-evidence bundle is incomplete or inconclusive within the statutory retention period, the Finanzamt retrospectively reverses the zero rating, assesses 19% VAT, and imposes interest under § 233a AO at 6% per annum on the unpaid tax. Deliberate or reckless false claims constitute criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO (imprisonment up to 10 years or fines); negligent conduct triggers fines under § 378 AO of up to EUR 50,000 per act. Unjustified VAT advantages or refunds are separately penalized under § 26c UStG with fines up to EUR 10,000 or 10% of the unjustified benefit. Incorrect declarations in the OSS return cause re-assessment by the destination member states, possible exclusion from the OSS procedure, and interest charges. The Umsatzsteuer-Nachschau (§ 27b UStG) authorizes unannounced on-site VAT spot checks. Note: German law prescribes no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is embedded herein as the standardized documentary/audit expectation for inward-remittance evidence to be matched to the zero-rated supply. Non-repatriation beyond that benchmark may, in transfer-pricing analysis, trigger an adjustment of the cross-border transaction under § 1 AStG with deemed interest income and corresponding withholding obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; both current and capital account transactions are free from exchange-control authorisation. Profit remittance, dividends, and repayment of shareholder loans are unrestricted. Large statistical notification duties apply for payments to/from non-residents under §67 AWV (monthly if the individual amount exceeds EUR 12,500) and external positions reporting under §68 AWV for balances above EUR 25,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement on export proceeds, whether in euro or foreign currency. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances freely, but for balance-of-payments statistics every cross-border transaction exceeding EUR 12,500 (or month-end external receivables above EUR 25,000) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the resident bank or directly via the 'Allgemeines Meldeportal'. There is no timeline for conversion into EUR.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank) with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as financial supervisory authority; exchange control is administered under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for BaFin-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs), provided the EMI holds an active licence per §1a ZAG or an EU/EEA passport and the UG satisfies corporate and beneficial-ownership due diligence. Offshore digital EMIs without a German/EU authorisation are not acceptable as primary treasury rails; they may be used only as auxiliary payout venues after enhanced due diligence (EDD) and are constrained by the German prohibition on accepting third-party funds without a licence. Legal binder: the UG must maintain at least one SEPA-able account in its own name and record all banking relationships in the transparency register.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001.001.09) for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR payments to the UG's German bank account, labelled with purpose code 'CAP-IN' or OCI-structure 'KAP-EINZ' in the remittance information. The associated Deutsche Bundesbank external position report must be filed as Z3 or Z4 (monthly) if the injection exceeds EUR 12,500, using classification 'E 1 - Direktinvestitionen'. The shareholder must instruct the remitting bank with the UG's full legal name (UG, haftungsbeschränkt), BIC/IBAN, and a resolution reference. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality. The combined burden is commonly approximately 30%, but is not a single nationwide rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic withholding rate of 26.375%, before any church tax and subject to exemptions, relief, and treaty reductions. A domestic exemption or refund may be available to qualifying corporate recipients, including qualifying EU parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to statutory conditions and anti-abuse rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany has no single threshold that universally triggers both a Local File and a Master File. Under the Foreign Tax Act and implementing regulations, transaction-specific records are generally required for cross-border related-party transactions. Contemporaneous documentation is subject to specific thresholds, including aggregate consideration exceeding EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions in a fiscal year. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 100 million in the preceding fiscal year. Documentation must be produced within the applicable statutory period upon request; special rules apply to extraordinary business transactions and reporting.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a general-purpose Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. German tax law nevertheless applies substance-sensitive rules, including beneficial ownership, anti-abuse provisions, the general anti-avoidance rule in section 42 AO, treaty anti-abuse and principal-purpose standards, and transfer-pricing rules requiring arm's-length outcomes consistent with functions performed, assets used, and risks controlled. A consumer-panel research platform should substantiate its personnel, decision-making, contractual allocation of functions and risks, ownership or licensing of data and software, and pricing of related-party services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax filings

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Reckless tax understatement may constitute an administrative offence under section 378 AO and may result in a fine. Back taxes, interest, and related surcharges remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal criminal or administrative liability where the statutory elements are met. They may also be liable for tax liabilities under section 69 AO for culpable breach of tax duties. Imprisonment is possible following criminal conviction. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction; enforcement measures depend on separate statutory grounds.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income and impose tax and interest. Under section 162 AO and section 90 AO, documentation failures can result in estimation and statutory surcharges; the surcharge can generally be 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, subject to statutory minimums and maximums, with separate rules for late or incomplete documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for culpable tax-duty breaches and may face criminal or administrative proceedings if the conduct involves intentional or reckless tax evasion or understatement. A pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create personal criminal liability.
Unlawful dividend distribution and capital maintenance

Statutory sanction: A prohibited payment may be recoverable from the recipient under the GmbH Act provisions applicable to a UG. The company may also face insolvency-related recovery and liability consequences. A distribution does not become tax-free merely because it is unlawful under company law.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be liable to the company for payments made in breach of capital-maintenance duties and may incur insolvency-related liability. Criminal liability requires the elements of a separate offence; imprisonment and travel restrictions are not automatic consequences.
Insolvency delay and creditor-prejudicing payments

Statutory sanction: Failure to file within the periods prescribed by section 15a of the Insolvency Code may be a criminal offence, punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to three years, or up to five years where committed intentionally in the circumstances specified by law. Insolvency offences and avoidance rules may also apply to asset transfers and payments.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal civil liability for prohibited payments, criminal liability for qualifying insolvency offences, and claims by the insolvency administrator. Any arrest, travel restriction, or imprisonment requires separate statutory grounds and applicable legal process.
Money laundering and beneficial-ownership non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under section 261 of the Criminal Code can result in a fine or imprisonment, with higher penalties for aggravated cases. Breaches of applicable reporting or transparency obligations may result in administrative fines and other enforcement measures.

Director personal liability: Individuals may be personally liable for their own conduct or culpable breaches of applicable duties. Corporate liability or fines may arise under applicable administrative-offence rules. Imprisonment or travel restrictions are not automatic and require the relevant statutory basis and process.
Shell-company, sham-residence, or treaty-abuse arrangements

Statutory sanction: Relief may be denied or withdrawn, withholding tax and interest may be assessed, and tax anti-abuse rules may apply. Intentional false statements may additionally constitute tax evasion or another offence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly make or authorize false filings or breach tax duties. Denial of treaty relief alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil or establish criminal liability.
