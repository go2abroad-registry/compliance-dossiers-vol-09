Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister beim Amtsgericht (Commercial Register of the Local Court) in the district of the registered office, filed electronically through the Common Register Portal of the German federal states (Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder).

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbH-Gesetz, GmbHG), especially § 1 (purposes), § 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), § 7 (application contents), § 8 (statement of contributions), § 9c (court examination), §§ 10–12 (registration and publication); complementary provisions of the Commercial Code (HGB) regarding the Commercial Register; Notarisation Act (BeurkG) and Regulation on Electronic Commercial Register Procedure (EHUG).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised Application for Entry in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) together with the Articles of Association or Model Protocol (Musterprotokoll), shareholder list (Gesellschafterliste), and managing director's declarations.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires a minimum nominal share capital of EUR 1.00, though a higher capital of generally at least EUR 1,000 is commercially standard practice. The issued capital must be fully paid in cash before registration and must remain at the free disposal of the managing director (§§ 5a(1), 7(2), 8(2) GmbHG). A statutory escrow (Sperrkonto) is not expressly required by law, but a capital-deposit confirmation (Einzahlungsbestätigung) issued by a credit institution is the customary evidentiary document submitted to the Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises must satisfy structural and fire-safety provisions of the local Bauordnung, including a valid fire-safety inspection and, where the building code requires, a certificate of occupancy (Nutzungsgenehmigung); the workplace must comply with the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung), Occupational Safety and Health Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz), DGUV V3 electrical-equipment inspection, and mandatory ergonomic and fire-protection training for all staff.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires a minimum nominal share capital of EUR 1.00, though a higher capital of generally at least EUR 1,000 is commercially standard practice. The issued capital must be fully paid in cash before registration and must remain at the free disposal of the managing director (§§ 5a(1), 7(2), 8(2) GmbHG). A statutory escrow (Sperrkonto) is not expressly required by law, but a capital-deposit confirmation (Einzahlungsbestätigung) issued by a credit institution is the customary evidentiary document submitted to the Commercial Register.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) for tax policy; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) as the central federal tax authority and administrator of statutory VAT identification numbers; local Finanzamt as the assessing authority for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT; ELSTER (https://www.elster.de) as the official electronic tax filing portal.

E-government filing portal

A UG must register with the competent local Finanzamt using the official 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (registration questionnaire). Required attachments include notarized articles of association, commercial register excerpt, register of managing directors, shareholding information, anticipated turnover, and bank details. The Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. The statutory VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is separately applied for through BZSt after business formation; BZSt issues the VAT ID after identity and business verification. A foreign entity without a German establishment or permanent representative uses the BZSt form for non-established taxable persons and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative in Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG is a German-resident capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with unlimited worldwide tax liability if its registered office or place of management is in Germany. Profits are subject to corporate income tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 5.5%, and local trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); no pass-through treatment is available. Shareholder distributions are separately taxed.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide check-the-box elections for UGs. The principal statutory election is the creation of an Organschaft (fiscal unity) for VAT under §2(2) no. 2 UStG and for corporate income tax and trade tax under §8 KStG and §7 GewStG, requiring financial, economic and organizational integration plus a profit-and-loss pooling agreement. Hybrid mismatch rules under §4k EStG apply automatically and do not provide elective status; taxpayers may, however, choose between tax credit and exemption under applicable double tax treaty provisions via §26 KStG and related administrative guidance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For resident small enterprises under §19 UStG: exemption applies where previous-year turnover did not exceed €22,000 (€25,000 from 2025) and current-year expected turnover does not exceed €50,000 (€100,000 from 2025). For non-resident service suppliers and cross-border B2B services, there is no general VAT registration threshold; registration is required from the first taxable supply with a German place of supply unless the recipient is liable under reverse charge. For distance sales of goods and imports, separate thresholds and one-stop-shop regimes apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For offshore B2B engineering, CAD and BIM services, the place of supply is outside Germany when the recipient is a taxable person established in another EU Member State or in a third country (§3a(2) UStG). Such supplies are outside the scope of German VAT (effectively 0%) and do not require German export zero-rating. To substantiate the treatment the supplier must demonstrate the recipient's business status and location, retain the underlying contract and deliverables, and issue an invoice stating the statutory basis (§3a(2) UStG). For physical exports of goods, 0% applies under §6 UStG if goods leave Germany in a customs export procedure, with proof via customs documentation; intra-Community supplies are zero-rated under §6a UStG subject to valid EU VAT ID of the customer and EC Sales List reporting.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) where goods are physically exported; not applicable for pure offshore BIM/CAD services
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill together with transport evidence supporting physical export; not applicable for pure services, but retained where the contract includes bundled deliverables
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank MT103, or credit advice evidencing cross-border receipt of the contract value, together with the service contract and invoice detailing the BIM/CAD scope and non-resident recipient

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. A mandatory e-invoicing obligation for domestic B2B supplies has applied since 1 January 2025: invoices must be issued in a structured, electronic format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x). Until 1 January 2027, transitional rules permit issuance of paper or PDF invoices with customer consent; thereafter structured e-invoicing is compulsory. VAT returns are filed periodically through ELSTER in aggregated XML summaries; transaction-level real-time reporting is not yet in force in Germany pending the EU VAT in the Digital Age legislation.

Input VAT deductions or export zero-rating claimed without adequate documentary proof are subject to retrospective correction and recovery by the Finanzamt. False zero-rating or intentional failure to evidence the place of supply constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by fines and up to five years' imprisonment; negligent underdeclaration is subject to administrative penalty under §378 AO. Outstanding VAT accrues interest at 6% per annum after the due date (§233a AO), late payment surcharges apply at 1% per commenced month under §240 AO, and the assessment limitation period extends from four years to ten years in cases of tax evasion. Any reclaimed input VAT is clawed back with statutory interest and penalties.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal corporate income tax burden of 15.825%. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and varies by municipality; the combined corporate and trade tax burden commonly approximates 30% but is not a uniform national rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate is generally 25% plus the 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing a total rate of 26.375%, before any church tax where applicable. A reduced rate or exemption may apply under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if all substantive and procedural requirements are met.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, returns are generally due by 31 July of the following year when prepared without a tax adviser. Where prepared by a tax adviser, the general statutory deadline is the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory exceptions, special extensions, and any applicable administrative deadline relief. The deadline is calculated from the end of the fiscal year for a non-calendar fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, German documentation rules generally require transaction-specific records where annual transaction volumes exceed EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including many services. A master file is generally required where the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Documentation deadlines, content, and thresholds are subject to current statutory rules and exceptions.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no general standalone regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, treaty beneficial-ownership and anti-abuse rules, the principal-purpose test where applicable, and domestic anti-avoidance provisions. The UG should evidence actual decision-making, personnel, premises, functions, assets, risks, and commercial rationale consistent with its reported income. For offshore BIM or engineering services, contracts, project records, personnel time, deliverables, IP ownership, and allocation of development and operational costs should support the actual arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax declarations

Statutory sanction: Under section 370 of the Fiscal Code, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine; particularly serious cases may attract imprisonment from six months to ten years. Unpaid tax, interest, and applicable surcharges remain recoverable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal liability where they intentionally participate or otherwise satisfy the statutory elements. They may also incur civil liability for tax losses caused by culpable breach of tax duties, subject to the statutory conditions. Criminal conviction does not automatically pierce the corporate veil; travel restrictions require a separate legal basis.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance and inadequate records

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, and assess additional tax and interest. Under the Fiscal Code, failures to prepare or submit required transfer-pricing documentation may result in statutory surcharges, including percentage-based surcharges subject to minimum and maximum amounts, and estimation of taxable income where records are inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for culpable breaches of tax obligations and criminal exposure if the conduct also constitutes intentional tax evasion. Personal liability is not automatic solely because a transfer-pricing adjustment is made.
Withholding-tax, payroll, and VAT non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The UG may owe unpaid tax, late-payment surcharges, interest, penalties, and enforcement costs. Failure to remit taxes withheld or required to be withheld may constitute a criminal offence depending on intent and circumstances.

Director personal liability: A managing director may be personally liable for tax debts where statutory conditions for culpable breach of tax duties are met, including failure to ensure payment of taxes due. Criminal liability requires proof of the applicable offence; civil liability and criminal liability are distinct.
Shell-company, sham-transaction, or treaty-abuse conduct

Statutory sanction: Treaty or directive relief may be denied, deductions or benefits may be disallowed, and tax may be reassessed with interest and applicable penalties. Intentional false reporting may also constitute tax evasion under the Fiscal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional participation in tax offences or culpable breach of statutory duties. German law does not impose automatic veil-piercing, travel bans, or imprisonment merely because an entity is characterized as a shell.
Insolvency and unlawful distributions

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include repayment claims, civil damages, insolvency-related liability, and criminal penalties where the statutory elements of an offence are met. The UG's statutory reserve obligation must also be observed: generally one quarter of annual net profit, reduced by any loss carryforward, is allocated to the legal reserve until the required share capital is reached.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal civil liability for prohibited payments, culpable delay in insolvency filing, or breach of duties, and criminal liability for specified insolvency offences. Liability is fact-dependent and does not arise solely from the UG's limited-liability form or insolvency.
