Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires stated share capital of at least €1.00; each cash contribution must be paid in full before registration and placed at the free disposal of the managing director in a dedicated company bank account. No escrow is mandatorily required, although the managing director must submit a written declaration of free disposal to the Commercial Register. Until the share capital reaches €25,000, at least one quarter of the annual net profit must be transferred to a statutory reserve.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification of the leased office by the responsible building authority or accredited expert, including escape route plan and emergency lighting; structural load inspection of the office floor to certify safe occupancy; occupational health and safety clearance by the State Labour Inspectorate (Gewerbeaufsicht), covering ergonomics, electrical equipment safety, and first-aid provisions.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG (haftungsbeschränkt) requires stated share capital of at least €1.00; each cash contribution must be paid in full before registration and placed at the free disposal of the managing director in a dedicated company bank account. No escrow is mandatorily required, although the managing director must submit a written declaration of free disposal to the Commercial Register. Until the share capital reaches €25,000, at least one quarter of the annual net profit must be transferred to a statutory reserve.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility under the EU free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU). Current account transactions, including dividends, interest, royalties, and management fees, may be freely converted and remitted without exchange-control permits. Capital account items such as equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, acquisitions, and capital repatriation are likewise unrestricted. There are no limitations on holding foreign-currency accounts and no requirement to use onshore counterparties. Practical hurdles are limited to tax withholding, substantive beneficial-ownership documentation, anti-money-laundering/KYC compliance, and statistical reporting obligations for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500. For a SPAC UG, no FX approval is required for offshore capital accumulation or future cross-border acquisition; however, investment-control screening under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) may apply if the acquisition target operates in a sensitive industry.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts in any designated account. The only statutory requirement is balance-of-payments reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500; the relevant report is due no later than the last calendar day of the month following the month in which the payment was received. There is no forced sale, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no obligation to route export receipts through a centralized government account. For the SPAC UG, export-proceeds rules are not applicable due to the absence of trading activity, but the same reporting threshold applies to inbound escrowed contributions, interest earnings, and future acquisition-related wires.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, responsible for foreign-exchange statistics and balance-of-payments reporting administration)
