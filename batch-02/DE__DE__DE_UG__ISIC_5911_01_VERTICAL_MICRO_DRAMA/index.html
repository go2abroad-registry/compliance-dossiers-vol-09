Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) is competent for EU VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr. under § 27a UStG), OSS/IOSS registrations and VIES/SETAX confirmations; the locally competent Finanzamt (tax office) issues the Steuernummer (income/corporate tax TIN) and administers Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldungen and annual VAT returns. Primary portals: ELSTER (www.elster.de) for all tax filings and the BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de) for VAT-ID and One-Stop-Shop registration.

E-government filing portal

After notarial incorporation and entry in the Handelsregister, the UG must file the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) with the competent Finanzamt within one month of commencing business (§ 138 AO), normally via ELSTER. The questionnaire discloses shareholders and their foreign TINs, place of management, anticipated turnover, German bank account and managing directors; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer and automatically forwards data to BZSt, which electronically issues the USt-IdNr. (EU VAT ID) within approximately 2–6 weeks. Non-resident founders and managing directors must provide their home-country tax identification numbers and may appoint a certified German tax agent to obtain ELSTER company certificates. No fee applies; the VAT ID is confirmed free of charge through VIES, and a paper VAT certificate is available on request.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) under § 5a GmbHG) is a German corporation subject to unlimited German tax liability on worldwide income: Körperschaftsteuer 15%, Solidaritätszuschlag 0.825%, Gewerbesteuer approximately 14–17% depending on municipal multiplier, producing an aggregate effective income tax rate of roughly 30–33%. No pass-through, flow-through or US-style S-corporation election is available. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) under § 5a GmbHG) is a German corporation subject to unlimited German tax liability on worldwide income: Körperschaftsteuer 15%, Solidaritätszuschlag 0.825%, Gewerbesteuer approximately 14–17% depending on municipal multiplier, producing an aggregate effective income tax rate of roughly 30–33%. No pass-through, flow-through or US-style S-corporation election is available. Territorial scope is worldwide (unlimited) because both registered seat and place of effective management are presumed to be in Germany; no tax-free zones or domestic offshore regimes apply to a UG.

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box or elective-entity-classification regime; a UG is per se a taxable corporation. Available statutory elections relevant to the entity: (1) Organschaft — VAT consolidation under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG and corporate tax group under §§ 14–19 KStG, requiring financial, economic and organisational integration plus a profit-and-loss pooling agreement; (2) Kleinunternehmerregelung small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG; (3) Ist-Versteuerung cash-based VAT accounting election under § 20 UStG where previous-year turnover does not exceed €800,000; (4) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registration via BZSt for EU B2C digital services. German anti-hybrid rules (§ 4k EStG and ATAD implementation) apply mandatorily and cannot be overridden by elective arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG, effective 1 January 2025): turnover ≤ €25,000 in the preceding calendar year and projected ≤ €100,000 in the current calendar year; mandatory VAT registration applies when either limit is exceeded. A distinct €10,000 annual EU-wide B2C threshold applies to cross-border electronically supplied services (§ 3a Abs. 5 UStG): once exceeded, streaming micro-drama subscriptions sold to consumers in other Member States are taxed at the destination-state VAT rate and may be declared via the BZSt OSS. A globally active vertical-micro-drama UG will in practice exceed the €100,000 projection and must be VAT-registered; registration is also commercially necessary to preserve input VAT deduction (Vorsteuerabzug).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies under § 4 Nr. 1a in conjunction with § 6 UStG only where physical goods are dispatched to a non-EU third country and the UG, acting as principal rather than agent, holds contemporaneous Ausfuhrnachweis (proof of export). Services: no explicit export zero-rating rule exists for intangibles; instead German place-of-supply rules exclude German VAT from export-type service transactions — (i) B2B licensing and broadcast-right sales of vertical micro-dramas to non-EU customers are taxable in the customer's country under § 3a Abs. 2 UStG and Article 44 VAT Directive; (ii) B2C digital streaming sold to non-EU consumers is taxable in the consumer's country under § 3a Abs. 3 UStG; (iii) B2C digital streaming to German consumers attracts 19% German VAT, while sales to other EU consumers are declared through the OSS at the applicable destination-state rate, subject to the €10,000 small-remote-seller rule. For app-store monetisation through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, where the store operator qualifies as deemed supplier under § 3 Abs. 3 UStG, the UG receives net-of-VAT settlements and must retain platform tax statements evidencing VAT discharge; where the UG remains the VAT principal, output VAT must be declared on EU B2C revenue.

Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, API polling or digital stamping. The B2B e-invoicing mandate (E-Rechnung) applies to domestic supplies: from 1 January 2025 every supplier must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD 2.x or PEPPOL BIS) and issue them upon recipient request; issuance becomes obligatory for enterprises with 2026 turnover above €800,000 from 1 January 2027 and for all domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2028, exchanged via PEPPOL or agreed EDI routes. OSS cross-border invoices and B2C app-store sales are not subject to the German e-invoice format mandate; platform invoices follow the applicable platform and destination-state rules.

Fraudulent zero-rating or false export-proof claims constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or fines; negligent misdeclarations attract fines up to €50,000 under § 378 AO. Where export proof is missing at audit, the supply is recharacterised as taxable at 19% with interest at 6% per annum from the statutory VAT due date (§ 233a AO), and input VAT adjustments may be imposed. The authorities may retroactively assess for up to ten years in concealment cases (§ 169 AO); the managing director faces personal liability under §§ 34 and 69 AO. Export documentation must be retained for ten years (§ 147 AO) under the Vollbelegprinzip strict-documentation doctrine. Although Germany imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for export proceeds, prolonged non-collection beyond twelve months may trigger arm's-length scrutiny under § 1 AStG and recharacterisation as a hidden profit distribution under § 8 Abs. 3 S. 2 KStG.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-66. There is no substantive distinction between capital and current account remittance for German domestic law; profit distributions, loan repayments, and capital repatriations may be executed freely in EUR or convertible foreign currency through any regulated bank. Non-resident investors may hold and transfer capital in Germany without prior FX approval. The only legal obligations relate to statistical reporting for cross-border payments and the application of EU sanctions restrictions where applicable. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders may be subject to German dividend withholding tax and, where relevant, relief under an applicable double tax treaty; this is a tax compliance event, not an FX convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Federal Republic of Germany imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation obligation for export proceeds. Companies are free to hold export receipts in foreign currency accounts with domestic or foreign banks. There is no statutory timeline for converting foreign currency export proceeds into EUR and no minimum conversion ratio. The only statutory requirement is reporting under Section 4 AWV: any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 or the foreign currency equivalent must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes. The report is filed through the participating bank or directly via the Bundesbank electronic reporting system. Failure to file correctly may attract administrative pecuniary penalties, but it does not affect convertibility or lawful use of funds.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany), responsible for the monetary and foreign exchange framework, with regulatory oversight exercised jointly with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises prudential conduct of banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for integration of an EU/EEA-licensed e-money institution as a supplemental payment rail; Low for any offshore, third-country, or UK-only EMI as a primary or concentration account. Legal constraints: under the Second Payment Services Directive (EU 2015/2366), a valid e-money license from an EEA member state is passportable into Germany, but a UK EMI post-Brexit no longer holds an automatic EU passport. An offshore EMI must hold a BaFin license or an EEA passport to maintain a German business account. Even where permitted, funds held at an EMI are e-money rather than bank deposits and are not protected by the German statutory deposit guarantee. Therefore, an offshore EMI is acceptable only for ancillary disbursement routing, while all material treasury liquidity and capital escrow balances must sit at a Tier-1 credit institution subject to German banking and resolution rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders must follow a fully compliant banking path. The investor must transmit funds only from its own duly KYC-verified account through a wire protocol accepted by Deutsche Bundesbank reporting, normally SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR/SEPA and SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR transactions inside the SEPA zone. Payment instructions must clearly state the corporate purpose, such as Contribution in cash against issuance of UG shares or Subordinated shareholder loan, and include the bank required AWV statistical purpose code for direct investment or capital transfer, conventionally referred to as Code V1 (Gründung beziehungsweise Erhöhung des Kapitals) for equity capital contributions. The receiving German bank will perform a cross-check against the notarised corporate resolution, the register of shareholders, and the deposited articles of association. Where the aggregate cross-border payment exceeds EUR 12,500, the UG or remitter is legally obliged to file the corresponding Bundesbank foreign payments statistics report. If the capital originates from a trust, foundation, or coded recipient, the bank will demand the full underlying source-of-funds file and will block funds absent complete transparency. After authentication, the amount is credited to the capital escrow account and thereafter transferred to the operational account only after the UG or GmbH capital increase is registered in the commercial register.Outbound disbursements for production expenses, license fees, profit-sharing and service payments should be structured through regulated clearing rails. For EUR payments to beneficiaries within the SEPA region, use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer. For remittances in foreign currency or to non-SEPA beneficiaries, use SWIFT MT103, preferably with Global Payments Innovation (GPI) tracking. For high-value commissioned micro-drama production contracts requiring bank assurance, use a Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit) routed through the Tier-1 German bank and the beneficiary's confirming bank. All outbound payments must be matched to an executed contract, a valid VAT or commercial invoice, and, where required, the AWV statistic code for services, royalties, or trade settlement. Cash surrogates, cryptocurrency, unsupported peer-to-peer transfers, or payment systems outside the regulated banking network are not acceptable disbursement rails for this treasury blueprint.Deliverable FX forwards against EUR for major liquid currencies including USD, GBP, CNY, and JPYFX swap (swap de change / foreign exchange swap) transactions for short-term liquidity rebalancingCross-currency swaps to convert structural USD or emerging-market streaming revenue streams into EUR over matched tenorsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies subject to convertibility restrictions or where physical EUR delivery is impossiblePlain vanilla foreign exchange options and option-based collars or participating forwards transacted under ISDA 2002 or equivalent master agreements with BaFin-licensed or EEA passporting counterparties

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for integration of an EU/EEA-licensed e-money institution as a supplemental payment rail; Low for any offshore, third-country, or UK-only EMI as a primary or concentration account. Legal constraints: under the Second Payment Services Directive (EU 2015/2366), a valid e-money license from an EEA member state is passportable into Germany, but a UK EMI post-Brexit no longer holds an automatic EU passport. An offshore EMI must hold a BaFin license or an EEA passport to maintain a German business account. Even where permitted, funds held at an EMI are e-money rather than bank deposits and are not protected by the German statutory deposit guarantee. Therefore, an offshore EMI is acceptable only for ancillary disbursement routing, while all material treasury liquidity and capital escrow balances must sit at a Tier-1 credit institution subject to German banking and resolution rules.
