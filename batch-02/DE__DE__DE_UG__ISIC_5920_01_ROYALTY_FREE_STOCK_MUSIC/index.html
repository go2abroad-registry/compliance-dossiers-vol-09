Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) and local Finanzamt (tax office); official e-filing portal: ELSTER (https://www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must: (1) file Gewerbeanmeldung with local trade office if establishing a permanent establishment; (2) complete Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) with the local Finanzamt to obtain Steuernummer (tax number); (3) apply for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) via BZSt for intra-Community supplies and export transactions; (4) if no permanent establishment, register directly for VAT through BZSt using the special foreign-entity registration procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: German-resident UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability (Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, municipal rate varying from approx. 7% to 17%); worldwide income is taxable absent treaty relief; no territorial election or tax-exempt zone applies. — Foreign entity must: (1) file Gewerbeanmeldung with local trade office if establishing a permanent establishment; (2) complete Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) with the local Finanzamt to obtain Steuernummer (tax number); (3) apply for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) via BZSt for intra-Community supplies and export transactions; (4) if no permanent establishment, register directly for VAT through BZSt using the special foreign-entity registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: German-resident UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is subject to unlimited corporate income tax liability (Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, municipal rate varying from approx. 7% to 17%); worldwide income is taxable absent treaty relief; no territorial election or tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under German law; statutory consolidation elections include Organschaft for corporate income tax (§14 KStG) and VAT grouping (§2a UStG); hybrid mismatch elections and disclosures are governed by the ATAD Implementation Act; no special classification election exists for German legal forms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business (Kleinunternehmer) registration threshold: €22,000 annual turnover (2025); intra-Community distance selling and OSS threshold: €10,000 for EU-wide distance sales; no monetary exemption for foreign suppliers of digital or electronic services — any taxable supply triggers German VAT registration/OSS obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export supplies of goods to third-country destinations under §4(1)(1) UStG and to certain B2B services supplied to foreign entrepreneurs under §4(1)(1) and §4(1)(2) UStG, provided the place of supply is outside Germany and documentary proof of export/foreign-recipient status is maintained; royalty-free stock music licensing is typically treated as a service supplied by reference to the recipient's business establishment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the EU free movement of capital provisions (Article 63 TFEU). Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (5% on dividends under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if applicable, otherwise 26.375% including solidarity surcharge, reduced by tax treaties). No exchange control approvals are required for cross-border payments, but reporting obligations apply for statistical purposes (AWV reporting to the Bundesbank for transactions exceeding €12,500).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances freely. However, all cross-border payments and receipts must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics if the amount exceeds €12,500, within five days of the transaction. No conversion ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in coordination with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking and FX oversight; the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and eurozone payment systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. German-licensed EMIs (e.g., PPRO, Solarisbank, or N26 Business) can provide multi-currency accounts and faster onboarding, but they are subject to BaFin supervision and may have stricter KYC for cross-border flows. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK-based) are less viable post-Brexit due to loss of EU passporting; they require German licensing or partnership with a German bank. For a low-risk digital music licensing business, a German EMI is acceptable, but traditional banks offer more robust correspondent banking relationships for SWIFT and LC operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporation tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporation tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) applies separately at a rate determined by the municipality, with a statutory assessment rate multiplier of at least 200%. A common aggregate burden is approximately 29% to 33%, depending on the municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) on dividends, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. Church tax may apply to individual recipients where relevant. For a qualifying corporate EU parent, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption implemented in Germany may reduce withholding to 0%, generally subject to minimum participation, minimum holding-period, anti-abuse, and substance requirements. Domestic relief may also apply to qualifying corporate recipients under German participation rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporation tax and trade tax returns are generally due by 31 July of the following calendar year when filed without a tax adviser. The deadline is generally extended to the last day of February of the second following calendar year when the return is prepared and filed by a tax adviser, subject to statutory exceptions and administrative extensions. The exact deadline must be confirmed for the relevant assessment year because temporary legislative extensions may apply.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany generally requires contemporaneous transfer pricing records for cross-border related-party transactions under section 90 of the Fiscal Code. The statutory materiality thresholds for detailed records are generally EUR 6,000,000 of annual consideration for supplies of goods and EUR 600,000 of annual consideration for services and other transactions, including royalties, financing, and similar services. A master file is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group whose consolidated sales revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000 in the preceding financial year. Documentation may be requested by the tax authority, and the taxpayer generally has 30 days to submit it unless a shorter period applies for extraordinary transactions.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore ESR statutes. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the German general anti-abuse rule in section 42 of the Fiscal Code, treaty anti-abuse provisions, the principal-purpose test in applicable treaties, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse rule, beneficial-ownership analysis, controlled foreign company rules, and transfer pricing principles. A German UG licensing music should maintain genuine German business functions, decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or documented operating arrangements, accounting records, contracts, bank-account control, rights to exploit the audio catalogue, and evidence that royalty pricing reflects functions, assets, and risks. A conduit arrangement, circular payment flow, nominal director, absence of control over intellectual property, or artificial treaty-resident recipient may cause denial of treaty or directive relief, recharacterisation of payments, withholding tax assessments, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code may result in a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, and in especially serious cases imprisonment from six months to ten years. The tax remains payable with interest and possible late-payment surcharges. The legal entity may face corporate fines and confiscation or disgorgement of economic benefit.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal tax liability under section 69 of the Fiscal Code for culpable failure to fulfil the company's tax obligations. Criminal exposure, asset confiscation, professional disqualification, and enforcement against personal assets may apply. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be imposed in criminal or enforcement proceedings where legally justified.
Incorrect or Missing Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate the taxable result and impose a transfer pricing documentation penalty generally ranging from EUR 5,000 to EUR 1,000,000 for certain documentation failures, with additional percentage-based penalties for incomplete or unusable documentation. The precise amount depends on the breach and applicable version of section 162 of the Fiscal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for tax losses caused by intentional or grossly negligent failure to maintain records. Criminal tax exposure arises if the documentation failure forms part of deliberate tax evasion.
Improper Treaty or EU Directive Relief

Statutory sanction: The German payer may become liable for unpaid withholding tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, and administrative penalties. The recipient may be denied a refund or exemption certificate. Deliberate false claims may constitute tax evasion or aiding and abetting tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Managing directors responsible for withholding may be personally liable for the tax not withheld or remitted. Criminal liability may apply where the director knowingly submits false certificates or participates in an abusive conduit structure.
VAT Misclassification of Digital Audio Supplies

Statutory sanction: Additional VAT, interest, late-payment surcharges, estimated assessments, correction obligations, and penalties may apply. Intentional VAT understatement may constitute VAT evasion under section 370 of the Fiscal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for VAT not paid where the failure resulted from culpable non-compliance. Criminal prosecution is possible for deliberate VAT fraud.
Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The amount may be added back to the UG's taxable income, treated as a dividend for withholding tax, and subject to corporation tax, trade tax, solidarity surcharge, withholding tax, interest, and penalties. The payment may also be denied as a business deduction.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid withholding tax and, under company-law rules, for payments made in breach of capital-maintenance or insolvency-protection duties. Shareholders may be required to repay unlawful distributions.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny treaty benefits, apply section 42 of the Fiscal Code, recharacterise transactions, impose withholding tax, assess additional corporation and trade tax, and refer the matter for criminal investigation. Asset confiscation and company dissolution may be relevant in criminal or insolvency proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil damages, tax liability, criminal liability for fraud or tax evasion, confiscation, and disqualification. Travel restrictions or pre-trial detention may occur only under applicable criminal-procedure conditions.
Money Laundering and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Breaches of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act may result in administrative fines, reporting obligations, account restrictions, confiscation, and criminal sanctions. Money laundering under section 261 of the Criminal Code may result in fines or imprisonment, with aggravated cases carrying higher penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance-responsible persons may be personally prosecuted for participation, aiding, concealment, or negligent organisational failure where the statutory elements are met. Personal asset confiscation and restrictions on business activity may apply.
Insolvency and Late-Filing Liability

Statutory sanction: The managing director may face civil repayment claims, insolvency-related damages, fines, and criminal sanctions. Insolvency filing violations may result in imprisonment of up to three years or a fine, subject to the applicable Criminal Code provision and facts.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, creditor losses, unpaid social-security contributions, and certain unpaid taxes. A court may impose professional disqualification and, in criminal proceedings, detention or travel restrictions where statutory conditions are satisfied.
Failure to Maintain Statutory Accounts and Records

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, compulsory filing measures, estimated tax assessments, late-filing charges, publication enforcement, and possible criminal sanctions for serious accounting or insolvency-related misconduct may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors are responsible for proper accounting, tax compliance, retention of records, and timely filing. Culpable failure may support personal damages claims, tax liability, and criminal prosecution.
Copyright and Royalty-Chain Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil injunctions, damages, surrender of profits, removal of infringing content, contractual claims, and criminal copyright sanctions may apply. Tax deductions and withholding treatment may also be challenged.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for knowingly facilitating infringement, breach of fiduciary duties, or unlawful payments. Criminal liability depends on knowledge, participation, and the applicable copyright offence.
False Corporate Residence or Place of Effective Management

Statutory sanction: Germany may assert unlimited corporation tax liability, deny treaty benefits, impose additional tax and withholding obligations, charge interest, and apply penalties for inaccurate returns or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for resulting tax losses and may face criminal sanctions for deliberate false declarations, fraudulent residence claims, or participation in treaty abuse.
