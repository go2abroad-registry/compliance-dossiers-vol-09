Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for a UG; the share capital may be EUR 1 or more but must be fully paid up before registration and be at the free disposal of the managing directors. There is no mandatory escrow account; funds may be transferred to the company's business account and documentation of free disposal is required. The UG must annually appropriate 25% of net profit to a statutory reserve until the reserve reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the local Bauordnungsamt confirming escape routes, fire extinguishers, and smoke alarm layout; structural load inspection for server racks and heavy studio equipment by the building authority or a licensed structural engineer; workplace safety clearance by Gewerbeaufsichtsamt and DGUV covering ergonomic workplace arrangement, electrical installation safety, and documented fire drills.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office; local Finanzamt; ELSTER (www.elster.de) electronic filing portal

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German UG must first register its business commencement with the locally competent Finanzamt by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' via ELSTER; the Finanzamt issues the statutory Steuernummer for corporate income tax, VAT, and payroll withholding. For intra-Community supplies and export VAT-ID evidence, the UG separately applies to the BZSt for a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr). A non-resident entity without a German establishment must register through its permanent tax representative or, in the absence of one, with the competent Finanzamt determined by BZSt guidance; a German tax advisor may act as statutory fiscal representative. After VAT registration, the UG may elect the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) through ELSTER/BZSt for distance sales and B2C digital services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Germany. The UG is per se an opaque corporation subject to entity-level corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Statutory elections available include VAT group status (Organschaft) under §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 UStG and corporate income/trade tax group status under KStG §§14-19 and GewStG §2 Abs.2 upon conclusion of a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag). Hybrid-mismatch structuring options are constrained by the German ATAD implementation (§4k EStG); no elective pass-through or unilateral hybrid classification is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Under §19 UStG, the domestic small-business exemption applies when prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover is projected not to exceed EUR 50,000; for intra-Community distance sales and B2C digital services the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies. Otherwise VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction, including for foreign-established suppliers making German-taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported physically from Germany to a third-country destination qualify for tax-free treatment under §6 UStG provided the goods leave the EU customs territory and the exporter retains verified export evidence. Intra-Community supplies are exempt under §6a UStG when the acquirer is a taxable person holding a valid USt-IdNr and the goods are dispatched to another EU Member State. Cross-border B2B services supplied to a non-German entrepreneur are generally outside German VAT under §3a Abs.2 UStG and reverse-charged in the customer's jurisdiction; services physically performed on exported movable goods may be zero-rated. Electronically supplied AI subtitling and voice-cloning services to B2B clients abroad are outside German VAT where the recipient is an entrepreneur; B2C digital supplies to EU final consumers remain taxable at the customer's country German or OSS-reported VAT rate and are not conventionally zero-rated.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / airway bill
  • Export substantiation certificate (Ausfuhrnachweis) under §10 UStDV with customs exit confirmation
  • Bank SWIFT / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export payment where FX repatriation is demonstrated

Germany does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API stamping, or digital tax-stamp regime. E-invoicing is phased in for domestic B2B supplies: from 1 January 2025 every taxable person must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant e-invoices (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027 taxpayers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue such e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B invoices must be issued in EN 16931 format through approved transmission channels (e.g., Peppol or authenticated email). VAT returns, advance returns, and EC sales lists are submitted electronically via ELSTER; no fiscal API clearance is required at invoicing time.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary audit by the Finanzamt and customs authorities through the ATLAS/ECS electronic export-monitoring system. If the exporter fails to provide complete export and payment evidence, zero-rating is denied and VAT is reassessed at the 19% standard rate, with late-filing penalties, late-payment default interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO), and administrative fines up to EUR 25,000. In cases of intentional or reckless false statements, tax evasion penalties apply under §370 AO, including imprisonment up to five years or fines; the German limitation period for VAT assessment extends to ten years in fraud cases, permitting retrospective clawback of all zero-rated VAT benefits plus interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible within Germany. Germany is a EU Member State with complete liberalisation of current-account and capital-account transactions under Articles 63–66 TFEU. No central-bank permit or foreign-exchange declaration is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, royalty transfer or equity injection to/from Germany. Practical hurdles are not FX-related but prudential and tax-related: German anti-money-laundering (GWG) know-your-customer standards, Beneficial Owner Register disclosure, German corporate law formalities for capital maintenance, and, for cross-border royalties or dividends, potential withholding taxes reduced under EU directives or double-tax treaties. A UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) may freely remit profits to foreign shareholders after adoption of an annual financial statement and a shareholder distribution resolution, provided the company remains solvent and net-asset cover is preserved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no compulsory foreign-exchange conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency may be retained in the UG's bank accounts, including multi-currency accounts, and may be used directly for outbound trade payments or FX hedging. The only applicable statutory duty is statistical: cross-border payments and receivables/payables exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported electronically to the Deutsche Bundesbank under AWV, on a transaction-by-transaction basis, with correct economic-payment codes. There is no time limit on the holding of foreign-currency balances and no ratio of foreign currency to EUR that must be converted. For the AI subtitle/dubbing entity under ISIC 5912, exports are predominantly services received via bank transfer, e-wallet payouts or platform settlement; these are reportable as non-material service exports if they cross the threshold.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) acting as central monetary authority and administrator of the external payments reporting regime under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV), together with the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) as the prudential supervisor for licensed banks, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions; the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) issues the underlying foreign-trade licences/controls, but no general exchange-control approval is required for ordinary trade and investment flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for EU-authorised electronic-money institutions (EMIs) operating in Germany under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2) via passporting or a German licence; this includes core fintech rails such as Wise and Stripe when used as ancillary payout/collection accounts. Low for non-EU offshore EMIs: they may not lawfully take deposits or provide payment services to German residents without BaFin authorisation or a valid European passport, and they cannot serve as the main account for a German UG's statutory obligations, tax administration, or annual audit documentary chain. A fully compliant structure is a tier-1 German commercial bank account as the treasury master account, with an EU-licensed EMI used solely for low-value platform payouts and international vendor invoices.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a UG, shareholder cash contribution should be made by SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) if the shareholder is in SEPA, or by SWIFT MT103/MT202 if outside the EEA. The transfer must originate from a bank account in the name of the shareholder, never from a third party, and must be labelled with the company name and a reference such as 'Kapitalerhöhung' or 'Stammeinlage'. In the absence of any German capital-control requirement, the only mandatory step is Bundesbank statistical reporting under AWV for the transaction if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500; the code used should identify an equity investment (e.g., 'EQUI' or the relevant AWV sub-code). Recommended supporting documentation for the bank and, if applicable, the tax advisor: notarised share subscription or capital-increase resolution, current trade-register excerpt, beneficial-owner declaration, proof of shareholder identity, and a statement of account confirming the capital contribution (Schuldanerkenntnis). If the UG is registered with a share capital above the minimum EUR 1, the contribution can be recorded as the statutory registered capital on a designated capital sub-account; no escrow at a notary is legally required unless agreed contractually.The primary clearing rail is SEPA Credit Transfer within the EEA, to be used for payroll, freelancers, domestic VAT payments and EU vendor costs. Cross-border non-EEA disbursements for subtitle outsourcing, cinema/platform delivery, AI compute capacity and voice-talent licensing should use SWIFT MT103, referencing the commercial invoice number and an appropriate SWIFT/ISO purpose code (e.g., 'LICENSE-FEE' for rights payments, 'ROYALTIES' for royalty distributions, 'SERV' for service payments). A commercial documentary letter of credit (L/C) is permissible but generally unnecessary because service exports lack transport documents; if an L/C is used, it should be a clean L/C payable on outstanding service acceptance. In practice, for the target payment rails (Stripe and Wise), inbound platform settlements occur via SEPA payout to the UG's German bank account, while Wise is used only for onward conversion and payout to small foreign suppliers; all conversions must be recorded and separately reconciled in the UG's treasury. Every outbound service disbursement must be accompanied by a fully itemised invoice, proof of service delivery, withholding-tax certificate (where German tax is required to be withheld) and, for related-party transactions, a transfer-pricing benchmark.Outright EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and EUR/CNY FX forward contracts with a German commercial bank under a documented treasury policy (MaRisk-compliant, non-speculative, tied to forecast service export receipts).FX swaps and cross-currency swaps limited to hedging the UG's balance-sheet receivables or trade accounts denominated in foreign currency, with maturity matched to settlement dates.Zero-cost collars and purchased FX options for cash-flow protection on large subtitle/dubbing platform receivables, provided options are bought only to hedge existing exposure and the pricing confirms best-execution under MiFID II.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to currencies with no full convertibility or restricted settlement with the UG's bank; NDFs are not required for EUR/USD but are permissible as an exceptional alternative where bilateral deliverability is restricted by the local counterparty's regulations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (solidarity surcharge) on the CIT amount and municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at typical combined rates of 14%–17%; aggregate effective corporate tax burden approximately 30% in most German municipalities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the withholding tax) on gross dividends distributed by a German UG; EU/EEA corporate parents holding at least 10% for at least 12 months may claim a full 0% withholding exemption under Section 43b EStG and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No de-minimis statutory threshold for intercompany transactions: Section 90(3) AO requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all material cross-border related-party dealings. In administrative practice, Local File documentation is generally prioritised for transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per associated enterprise; Master File documentation is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. EUR is the local currency threshold.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies a substance-over-form doctrine under Section 42 AO: a UG without real economic substance, such as no office, no employees, no management functions in Germany, or no actual risk-bearing capacity, may be disregarded for tax purposes or its transactions recharacterized. The shell-company risk is particularly severe for treaty relief: a UG that is merely a letterbox in Germany will be denied the benefits of German DTAs and of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. Additionally, German CFC rules (AStG) can attribute passive income, including licensing and AI-service revenue, back to resident shareholders if the UG is controlled by non-residents and the substance test is failed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Under Section 370 AO, imprisonment of up to five years or a monetary fine; in especially serious cases, imprisonment of six months to ten years and confiscation of assets. The tax authority will additionally assess the evaded tax, late payment interest at 6% per annum, and a late filing surcharge; administrative fines for aggravated non-compliance can reach EUR 100,000.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under Section 69 AO for tax debts of the UG arising from intentional or grossly negligent breach of tax obligations. Criminal conviction triggers travel bans and arrest under Section 326 AO, disqualification from serving as a director, and personal seizure of assets. The corporate veil is pierced where the director used the UG as a façade to commit tax fraud, resulting in civil restitution and personal bankruptcy exposure.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Under Section 162(4) AO, the tax authority may estimate taxable income and impose a penalty surcharge of 5% of the resulting adjustment, with a minimum of EUR 5,000. Where documentation is omitted intentionally or in bad faith, a surcharge up to 20% of the adjustment may apply; combined with 6% annual interest and potential criminal sanctions if the failure is part of tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The managing director who withheld documentation or instructed erroneous transfer pricing bears personal liability for the corporate tax shortfall under Section 69 AO, is subject to enforcement through travel restrictions and asset freezes, and may be sued by creditors or shareholders for breach of fiduciary duty. In serious cases, the director is personally prosecuted for abetting tax evasion under Section 369/370 AO.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Section 42 AO the tax authority disregards the UG or recharacterises transactions, attributing the income to the actual beneficial owner, thereby denying treaty relief and imposing German corporate income tax, trade tax, solidarity surcharge, and late interest. Additional penalty surcharges and fines under the Administrative Offences Act (OWiG) may apply; registration of shell entities with German commercial register may be challenged.

Director personal liability: A director who intentionally sets up or operates a sham entity faces personal liability for the UG's unpaid taxes under Section 69 AO, confiscation of assets, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Civil courts treat the UG as an alter-ego, imposing joint and several liability for all contractual and tax obligations. Travel bans under Section 326 AO are routinely imposed during criminal investigation, and the director can be barred from holding any management position in Germany.
Money Laundering and AML Compliance Failures

Statutory sanction: Under Section 261 StGB (money laundering), imprisonment of three months to five years and a fine; if the offending party acts commercially, imprisonment of one to ten years and mandatory confiscation of all proceeds. The AG (Corporation) may be fined up to EUR 10 million under Section 30 OWiG; German supervisory authorities can prohibit the UG from opening or maintaining bank accounts and revoke its commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally subject to arrest, travel bans, and asset seizure at the pre-trial stage. If the director had knowledge or turned a blind eye to suspicious client funds, they are prosecuted as principals or accomplices under Section 261 StGB. The civil liability extends to restitution of laundered amounts, and the personal bank accounts and property of directors are attached; a final conviction triggers disqualification from directorship for up to five years under the Limited Liability Companies Act.
