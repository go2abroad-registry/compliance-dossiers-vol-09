Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — the national federal tax office for VAT identification numbers, OSS schemes and administrative assistance; local Finanzamt — the competent local tax office at the registered seat for TIN, corporate tax, trade tax and VAT assessment; ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung, www.elster.de) — the mandatory electronic filing and clearance portal.

E-government filing portal

1) After notarial incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), the managing director of the UG must file the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the local Finanzamt, usually electronically via ELSTER, within four weeks of incorporation. 2) The Finanzamt issues a personal tax number (Steuernummer) which constitutes the statutory TIN for corporate, trade and VAT purposes. 3) For cross-border supplies to EU Member States or OSS filings, the UG must obtain a separate VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) from the BZSt by filing Form USt 1 TN / via ELSTER. 4) Non-resident ownership is freely permitted; no fiscal representative is required for EU-based shareholders, but the UG must maintain a domestic address for service. 5) The registration questionnaire must specify the intended scope of metaverse services, payment rails and expected turnover; late filing may trigger a penalty of up to EUR 2,500 under Sec. 152a AO.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) with statutory seat or place of management in Germany and is therefore an unlimited German tax resident; it is subject to corporation tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 5.5% of the corporate tax, and trade tax at a municipal effective rate of approximately 7%–17.3%; profits are taxed at entity level, and shareholder dividends are separately taxed at 26.375% withholding (or personal tax) with no full imputation. As the enterprise is engaged in B2C virtual concerts and digital ticket tokenization (ISIC 7110), it is also a VAT entrepreneur with global taxable turnover subject to German place-of-supply rules.

Available election forms

Germany has no US-style check-the-box regime; the UG is mandatorily taxable as a corporation. The relevant statutory elections and mechanisms are: (a) Corporate tax and VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Sec. 14 KStG and Sec. 2 para 2 No. 2 UStG via a profit-pooling and domination agreement, allowing vertical tax consolidation; (b) Small-business VAT exemption under Sec. 19 UStG if prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000; (c) Election to apply the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) non-Union or Union scheme for quarterly filing of EU-wide B2C digital service VAT; (d) Sec. 4k EStG hybrid-mismatch alignment declarations and documentation elections for cross-border token or licensing instruments; (e) Trade tax options for partnership conversion (Sec. 1a KStG) are not available to the UG; transparency is never elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for German-resident entrepreneurs: VAT registration is triggered by the first taxable supply, unless the small-business exemption applies (prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 25,000 and expected current-year turnover ≤ EUR 100,000). Non-resident businesses are subject to mandatory German VAT registration without any threshold if they make taxable supplies in Germany. For cross-border B2C digital services (including virtual event tickets and metaverse events), the annual EU-wide threshold is EUR 10,000: below that threshold the supply is taxed in Germany, above it the UG must either use the OSS scheme or register directly in each customer jurisdiction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

German VAT zero-rating at 0% applies materially only to physical exports of goods (Sec. 6 UStG) and qualifying intra-Community supplies (Sec. 6a UStG) supported by the customer's valid VAT ID and transport evidence. For a virtual concert, digital ticketing and NFT-based metaverse access, the supply is an electronically supplied service; such services are not zero-rated but are excluded from German VAT by place-of-supply rules under Sec. 3a UStG. B2B supplies to non-German entrepreneurs are located where the customer is established, with reverse-charge transfer of liability. B2C supplies to consumers outside Germany are located at the customer's residence or usual abode. To substantiate exclusion from German VAT, the UG must retain two independent, non-conflicting proofs of customer location: billing and IP address, payment gateway country, bank BIN, mobile country code, residence declaration, or the customer's German VAT ID. Physical export zero-rating applies only if the UG also ships tangible goods such as branded merchandise; in that case the export exemption must be evidenced by the customs declaration and transport documents.

  • Ausfuhranmeldung — Customs Export Declaration processed via the German ATLAS system with an MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Transport document evidencing departure from EU customs territory: Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase order in original
  • Proof of foreign-exchange inflow (bank statement or SWIFT MT103) as export consideration substantiation
  • For virtual/offshore services only: digital evidence bundle — gateway settlement data, IP/geolocation logs, customer declarations, and token issuance receipts; customs documents are inapplicable to pure metaverse services

Germany has phased in mandatory B2B e-invoicing for domestic supplies: from 1 January 2025 suppliers must be able to receive EN 16931-conformant electronic invoices; from 1 January 2027 enterprises with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B supplies must be issued electronically. Accepted formats are XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, and PEPPOL-based XML; transmission must be machine-readable, and structured VAT data must be included. Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control system (unlike Italy or Hungary); instead, periodic checks are performed through VAT advance returns (monthly or quarterly), Dauerfristverlängerung (extension with SEPA debit mandate), an annual VAT return via ELSTER, and OSS quarterly declarations. B2C tickets and event access codes may be tokenized on-chain, but the issuer invoice obligation under Sec. 14 UStG remains fully applicable.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or false place-of-supply declarations constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years, fines, and full confiscation of the tax benefit. The Finanzamt may issue a retrospective VAT assessment at 19% (or 7% if applicable) under Sec. 233 AO, plus monthly interest at 0.5% under Sec. 233a AO, late-payment surcharges under Sec. 240 AO, and an administrative penalty of up to EUR 50,000 under Sec. 378 AO for negligent understatement. Refunds already granted are clawed back with statutory interest, and the managing director is personally liable as a responsible representative under Sec. 34, 69 AO. Defective or falsified blockchain/NFT records are treated as aggravated falsification of evidence, extending the re-assessment period to ten years under Sec. 169Abs.2 AO. The BZSt and local Finanzamt coordinate cross-border audit exchanges via DAC 7 and the EU anti-fraud network.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU Treaty articles 63–66 (free movement of capital) and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (AWV). No exchange-licensing, exchange-budget allocation, or prior-approval mechanism applies to ordinary service transactions or inward equity investment. Profit remittance from an Unternehmergesellschaft (UG) to a non-resident shareholder is legally unrestricted via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA. The only material hurdle is tax: dividend distributions are subject to German withholding tax of 26.375% (25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge) unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty; VAT on virtual event ticketing, where EU-distance or e-service rules apply, must be separately structured. Banking due diligence, not FX law, remains the practical bottleneck for repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Germany does not impose mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines for export proceeds. Cross-border payment statistics reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank is required for transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 under the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation, but this is a statistical, not licensing, obligation and does not block liquidity.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, with BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) as the conduct-of-business and banking-license supervisor; Germany has no separate foreign-exchange control administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-licensed EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) and Low for offshore non-EEA digital EMIs. An EMI must hold an EU/EETA authorization and either a German passport/agent notification to BaFin or operate through a licensed German partner bank. Pure offshore EMIs cannot issue German IBANs and frequently refuse entities with cryptocurrency exposure. Given the USDC element, an EEA-licensed EMI may accept the UG only with enhanced KYC, a clear source-of-funds trail, and an explanation that USDC is solely a tokenized ticket settlement vehicle rather than a speculative currency trading activity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal burden of 15.825%; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and depends on the municipality's multiplier, with a statutory minimum effective rate of 7% and a common aggregate burden of approximately 29.8% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital-yield withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective rate of 26.375%. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain exemption under the German implementation of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally subject to minimum participation, minimum holding-period and anti-abuse requirements. Treaty reductions or refunds may apply where the recipient is the beneficial owner and treaty conditions are met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany has no universal monetary threshold below which cross-border related-party transactions are exempt from arm's-length requirements. A Local File is generally required for cross-border controlled transactions and extraordinary business transactions under Sections 90 AO and GAufzV. The commonly applied ordinary-transaction thresholds are EUR 6 million for supplies of goods and EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, royalties and financing; extraordinary transactions may require documentation irrespective of value. A Master File is generally required for German taxpayers belonging to a multinational group where the statutory group-revenue threshold is met, commonly EUR 100 million of consolidated group revenue under the German documentation rules. Current law and transaction classification must be confirmed for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single standalone set of Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations. Substance is nevertheless tested through the arm's-length principle, beneficial-ownership rules, anti-treaty-shopping provisions, general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 AO, place-of-effective-management rules, permanent-establishment principles, business-purpose requirements and anti-hybrid rules. A German UG receiving royalties, management fees or financing income should maintain real decision-making, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or functional infrastructure, bank-account control, risk assumption, contemporaneous records and commercial rationale. A virtual-event business must separately analyze whether servers, employees, dependent agents, directors or decision-makers create a permanent establishment in another jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under Section 370 AO is punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years; particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest, late-filing charges and confiscation or recovery of economic benefit may also apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal liability, tax liability under Sections 34 and 69 AO, civil restitution and insolvency-related claims. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings where legally justified.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The UG may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, interest and enforcement costs. Intentional or reckless conduct can create tax-criminal or administrative-offence exposure, depending on the facts.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for tax that should have been withheld and remitted where culpable breach of duty is established. Civil claims and criminal investigation are possible.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, trade-tax adjustments, withholding-tax exposure, interest, documentation penalties and potentially extended audit scrutiny may apply. Missing or unusable documentation may permit the tax authority to estimate income and impose a surcharge, generally up to 1% of the relevant documented transaction amount, subject to statutory minimums and maximums.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for deliberate or negligent tax damage and may face criminal exposure if false records or intentional tax evasion are involved.
Shell company and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty or statutory relief may be denied, withholding tax assessed or refunded relief reversed, with interest and possible anti-abuse, tax-evasion or general anti-abuse consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal tax liability and criminal exposure where they knowingly certified an abusive structure or submitted false beneficial-ownership information.
VAT fraud and digital-location misclassification

Statutory sanction: VAT assessments, repayment of input VAT, interest, late-filing charges, invoice-liability assessments and tax-criminal sanctions may apply. Severe intentional cases may constitute VAT evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT and face criminal investigation where they approved or tolerated fraudulent reporting.
Crypto-asset accounting and proceeds concealment

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, accounting violations, criminal tax exposure and potential confiscation of proceeds may result. Payment-service, financial-regulatory or licensing issues may arise depending on the token and activities.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deficient books, tax losses and insolvency damage, and may face criminal liability for intentional concealment.
Anti-money-laundering and sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, criminal sanctions, asset freezing, confiscation, business restrictions and reporting obligations may apply under German AML and sanctions legislation. The exact sanction depends on the regulated status and conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal administrative or criminal liability for intentional or negligent failures. Travel bans or detention are case-specific and not automatic.
Payroll, disguised employment and social-security evasion

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, social-security contributions, interest, surcharges, fines and criminal prosecution for withholding or social-security evasion may apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for employee withholding tax and intentionally unpaid social-security contributions, in addition to civil and criminal exposure.
Insolvency and capital-maintenance breach

Statutory sanction: Directors may face insolvency offences, repayment claims, damages, fines or imprisonment. Insolvency filing duties generally arise without undue delay and, subject to statutory conditions, no later than three weeks after inability to pay and six weeks after over-indebtedness.

Director personal liability: Personal restitution, director-disqualification consequences, civil damages and criminal liability are possible. Courts may impose travel restrictions in individual criminal or enforcement cases, but no automatic travel ban applies.
Failure to maintain books and records

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate the tax base, reject deductions, impose documentation penalties and pursue administrative or criminal sanctions where records are intentionally falsified or destroyed.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for tax damage caused by deficient organization and may face obstruction or tax-criminal consequences for deliberate destruction or concealment.
Corporate separation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: The payment may be reclassified as a hidden profit distribution, denied as a deduction, subject to withholding tax and treated as a shareholder repayment obligation. Capital-maintenance and insolvency consequences may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders may face restitution, tax liability, creditor claims and, in serious cases, criminal liability for fraudulent or insolvency-related conduct.
