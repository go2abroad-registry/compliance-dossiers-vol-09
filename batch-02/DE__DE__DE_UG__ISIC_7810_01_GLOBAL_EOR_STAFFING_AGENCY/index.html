Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) for VAT ID issuance, e-invoicing governance, and cross-border tax matters; local Tax Offices (Finanzämter) for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT assessment, and issuance of the national tax number (Steuernummer); General Directorate of Customs (Generalzolldirektion) and Main Customs Offices (Hauptzollämter) for export clearance and customs documentation; official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities deriving taxable German revenue must file the statutory registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung, Form 15) with the competent Finanzamt, either via ELSTER or in hard copy, to obtain a Steuernummer. After issuance of the Steuernummer, the entity applies online to the BZSt for a German VAT ID (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, DE + 9 digits) using the BZSt portal; the VAT ID is activated retroactively from the first taxable supply. Non-resident entrepreneurs have no de minimis threshold and must register from the first taxable supply in Germany; where the German recipient is an entrepreneur, the reverse charge under § 13b UStG may shift VAT liability, but the foreign supplier must still register if it invoices German business customers without a German VAT ID. The UG must append its commercial register extract and a German registered address or fiscal representative mandate to the questionnaire; the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer within approximately four to eight weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; an UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is mandatorily taxable at entity level for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Available statutory elections are limited to: (a) domestic fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§ 14-19 KStG and § 2(2) UStG, consolidating the UG with a German parent for corporate tax and VAT; (b) optional pro-rata input VAT allocation under § 15(4) UStG; (c) hybrid-mismatch alignment under § 4k EStG and § 8b KStG implementing ATAD 2; (d) the de-minimis small-entrepreneur election under § 19 UStG where applicable; and (e) choice between permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte) registration and incorporation of a German subsidiary. No elective pass-through regime exists for a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-resident entrepreneurs: German VAT registration is obligatory from the first taxable supply (EUR 0 de minimis). For resident small businesses, the optional Kleinunternehmerregelung exempts supplies where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 25,000 (from 1 January 2025; EUR 22,000 in 2024). An EOR service provider must maintain a German VAT registration address, file quarterly or monthly VAT returns (depending on turnover), and, where cross-border B2B services are supplied, apply the place-of-supply and reverse-charge rules instead of charging German VAT.

Mandatory structured e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) for domestic Germany-to-Germany B2B supplies from 1 January 2025 under § 14 UStG as amended by the Steuermodernisierungsgesetz. E-invoices must be issued and transmitted in a structured XML format compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X) and delivered via qualified channels, including the PEPPOL network; PDF-only invoices are transitionally tolerated until 2027. Receipt of e-invoices has been mandatory since 2025 for all entrepreneurs; suppliers with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 in 2025, and all suppliers from 1 January 2027, must issue e-invoices. There is no Italian-style real-time fiscal clearance, SdI-like stamping, or API-based transaction clearance in force in Germany as at 2025; the BZSt/ELSTER operates as the official receiving and validation channel, and the future EU ViDA real-time transaction reporting framework is not yet in force.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions as a member of the EU and OECD. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (if applicable) and compliance with anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) requirements. No exchange control approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital repatriation, provided the underlying transactions are properly documented and reported under the AWV for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender or conversion requirement for export proceeds in Germany. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border payments and receipts exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes within a specified timeline (typically within 5 days of the transaction). The reporting obligation applies to both residents and non-residents, and failure to comply may result in administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) in conjunction with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; foreign exchange controls are governed by the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) and administered by the Bundesbank for statistical and monitoring purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Fintech EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) such as Wise, Penta, or Qonto can provide cross-border payment solutions and multi-currency accounts. However, they are not full-service banks and may not offer credit facilities or escrow services. For a UG engaged in global EOR and payroll, a full banking relationship with a tier-1 bank is recommended for statutory compliance and client trust. EMIs can be used as supplementary rails for low-value cross-border payments, but they must be licensed under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) or passport from another EU state. Legal constraints include potential limitations on holding client funds and the need for separate safeguarding accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for capital injection into a German UG: Use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'S101' (equity injection) or 'S301' (loan) as per the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance. The transfer must be accompanied by a copy of the shareholder resolution and the notarized deed of contribution. For inbound payments from non-EU countries, the receiving bank will require a 'Zahlungszweck' (payment purpose) declaration. The Bundesbank must be notified of the capital inflow for statistical purposes if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. Verification includes the bank's compliance check against sanctions lists and the UBO register.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For payroll disbursements to employees and contractors, use SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers or SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EU-based payments. For commercial trade settlements (e.g., paying subcontractors or suppliers), use SWIFT MT103 or a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) via the bank's trade finance department. The UG must maintain a dedicated payroll account to segregate client funds and ensure compliance with the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) if acting as a payment institution. For high-volume payroll, consider using a payment service provider (PSP) with direct access to the SEPA and SWIFT networks, but ensure the PSP is licensed and the funds are safeguarded.Currency Forwards (Devisentermingeschäfte) – OTC contracts with a bank to fix exchange rates for future payroll disbursements.Currency Swaps (Währungsswaps) – For longer-term hedging of multi-currency cash flows, subject to bank credit approval.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – Permissible for currencies with restricted convertibility, though less common for EUR-based entities; may be used for emerging market currencies where the UG has payroll obligations.FX Options (Währungsoptionen) – Over-the-counter options to hedge against adverse exchange rate movements, with premium payments.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Körperschaftsteuer (CIT): 15.00% federal flat rate + 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag levied on the CIT amount (0.825 percentage points) = 15.825% combined federal/national burden; plus municipal Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at the UG's seat of management and permanent establishment, computed as 3.5% Steuermesszahl x municipal Hebesatz (commonly 200%-490%; e.g., Berlin 410% results in 14.35% trade tax; Munich 490% results in 17.15%). Total nominal combined burden on ordinary commercial profit therefore ranges approximately 29.8%-33.0% before the trade tax deduction for income tax purposes.
