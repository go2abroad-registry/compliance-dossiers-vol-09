Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office, Bonn (VAT identification numbers, EU OSS, withholding tax administration, www.bzst.de); locally competent Finanzamt (corporate income tax, trade tax, payroll and VAT assessments, allocation of the Steuernummer); statutory e-filing via ELSTER and BundesOnlinePortal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a UG formed by foreign shareholders, the company first completes notarial incorporation and entry in the Commercial Register, then submits the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) through ELSTER or to the local Finanzamt competent for the registered seat; the Finanzamt issues a Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax, and payroll within 2-6 weeks. A separate statutory VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is applied for electronically from BZSt once business operations commence. Foreign entities without a German seat that directly supply SaaS/e-services to German consumers may register solely for VAT through the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or obtain a German VAT registration through the simplified non-resident procedure; a foreign parent establishing a German branch or permanent establishment must appoint a German authorised representative and complete the same registration questionnaire with the local Finanzamt to procure a TIN and, if applicable, a VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or elective-entity regime exists under German tax law; a UG is per se a taxable corporate entity (Körperschaft) subject to Körperschaftsteuer and trade tax, and is not eligible for pass-through classification. Relevant elections include: formation of an Organschaft (corporate income tax and VAT fiscal unity) with a financially and economically integrated German parent; waiver of the § 19 UStG small-business exemption; election to opt into tax group allocation of trade tax via Organschaft; HGB accounting simplifications for small corporations; and EU OSS/OSS elections for cross-border B2C supplies. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective: § 4k EStG and § 36 GewStG statutorily deny deductions where hybrid instruments, hybrid entity structures, or permanent-establishment mismatches create double non-taxation, overriding treat-through results under double tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general de-minimis registration threshold: any entrepreneur established in Germany is mandatorily VAT-registered, subject to the § 19 UStG small-business exemption. From 1 January 2025, exemption applies where preceding-year turnover (incl. VAT) did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000. The EU-wide distance-sale / OSS registration threshold is EUR 10,000 of intra-EU cross-border supplies of goods or electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For sales of goods, a 0% rate under § 6 UStG applies when the goods are removed from EU territory to a third country within 3 months and an approved proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) is retained; intra-Community supplies to EU entrepreneurs are exempt with credit under § 6a UStG when the customer's valid EU VAT ID is obtained, transport to another Member State is evidenced, and the recapitulative statement is filed. For B2B enterprise SaaS (ISIC 6201), the supply of electronically supplied services is localised at the customer's establishment under § 3a UStG, so supplies to non-EU entrepreneurs are outside the scope of German VAT and supplies to EU entrepreneurs are reverse-charged in the customer's Member State; no German 0% mechanism is required. To sustain offshore treatment the exporter must identify the customer as an entrepreneur, document the customer's foreign establishment, and verify non-German use or exploitation of the SaaS; B2C e-services to German consumers remain taxable at 19% (subject to OSS at the seller's Member State), and the Kleinunternehmer exemption does not apply to reverse-charge or B2C supplies requiring VAT invoicing.

  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note or equivalent transport document demonstrating departure from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/Wise/Stripe settlement statement evidencing receipt of export consideration within the 180-day repatriation window

Germany operates a phased B2B e-invoicing mandate under § 14 UStG, not a real-time fiscal clearance regime: from 1 January 2025 all German-established businesses must be able to receive e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 issuers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B issuers must issue e-invoices. Mandatory formats are structured XML per EN 16931 (XRechnung and ZUGFeRD 2.x), transmitted via PEPPOL or any electronic exchange channel; no tax-authority pre-clearance, digital stamp, or Sicurezza-type authorisation code is required. VAT advance returns, annual VAT returns, recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung) and OSS returns are filed digitally through ELSTER; e-invoices and source documents must be retained for 10 years (§§ 146-147 AO).

Export zero-rating, § 6a exemption and foreign-place-of-supply treatments are systematically audited by the local Finanzamt, BZSt and customs tax supervision. Missing, inconsistent or fraudulent export documentation triggers retrospective VAT assessments at the 19% standard rate on the full consideration, together with late-payment surcharges of 1% per commenced month under § 240 AO, default interest of 0.15% per month (6% per annum) under § 233a AO, and denial of input-VAT deduction. Intentional falsification constitutes tax evasion under § 370 AO (up to 5 years' imprisonment or a criminal fine); grossly negligent understatement under § 378 AO is finable up to EUR 500,000. Managing directors may be held personally and jointly liable under §§ 34, 69 AO, and the BZSt coordinates cross-border information exchange to claw back fraudulent zero-rating in VAT-carousel and hybrid-mismatch arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a euro-area member, operates a fully convertible currency regime for both current-account and capital-account transactions; Article 63 TFEU guarantees free movement of capital, and there are no Bundesbank or BaFin exchange controls, prior authorization procedures for profit remittance, or restrictions on non-resident ownership of a UG. Dividend and loan remittances are freely executable through the banking system; the only statutory burden is statistical reporting, not approval. The UG has no statutory minimum capital, so capital injection is not subject to prudential verification by the Bundesbank; however, formation law requires the managing director to confirm full payment of the subscribed capital before commercial register registration, and any in-kind contribution must be covered by a notarial formation report.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender, force-conversion, or deferred-payment rules on export proceeds; all SaaS export receipts remain freely held and deployable in any currency. A mandatory statistical reporting duty arises under § 68 AWV: any resident-to/from-non-resident payment exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the Z4/Z5 electronic statistical channel by the fifth working day of the month following the month of payment receipt or disbursement. Aggregated SaaS settlements from Stripe Billing and Wise Business must be disaggregated by the UG into the underlying client invoices and reported under the appropriate service code for software/SaaS licensing.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank; operator of external trade and payments statistics under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) and Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz (BMWK; responsible for AWV policy and foreign trade controls). Banking supervision and licensing of credit institutions, payment institutions and e-money institutions is exercised by Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as a complementary treasury rail but not as the primary statutory deposit. Wise Europe SA, an e-money institution passported into Germany from Belgium, provides a German EUR IBAN through partner bank Deutsche Bank AG, plus local currency accounts (USD, GBP) for efficient disbursement; it is suitable for receivables collection and supplier payouts but is not covered by German deposit protection and cannot serve as a locked statutory capital account. Stripe Billing is a payment gateway/merchant of record that does not provide an IBAN and must be paired with a bank settlement account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection from the foreign parent executes via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR) into the UG’s statutory capital collection account with the payment reference ‘Kapitaleinzahlung gem. Errichtungsprotokoll/Urkundenrolle Nr. …’ to avoid misclassification as trade revenue. The receiving bank validates the originator of funds and economic purpose; the UG must include the inflow in the Bundesbank Z4 report within the § 68 AWV statistical deadline if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500 (report by the fifth working day of the subsequent month). For USD or other foreign-currency injections, conversion is executed at the bank’s reference rate with EUR-equivalent booking; the UG should instruct ‘beneficiary receives exact EUR amount’ (Quantity: amount in EUR) and request the bank to quote the EUR leg via CLS-settled F/X to eliminate conversion ambiguity.Intra-EEA vendor payments and dividend/salary disbursements settle through SEPA Credit Transfer (standard or instant), while non-EEA disbursements use SWIFT MT103 with SWIFT GPI (Global Payments Innovation) tracking; for enterprise SaaS licensing fees, direct MT103 with the underlying invoice number is the industry-standard rail. Commercial documentary LCs (UCP 600) are permissible in jurisdictions where the buyer’s import regulations or bank require them, but they are operationally heavy and should be confirmed irrevocable LCs issued by an ECB/EU prime bank for high-value one-off software projects. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% federal corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional: the federal base rate is 3.5% multiplied by the municipality's assessment factor (Hebesatz), so the total rate varies by location and is commonly approximately 30%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a German UG are generally subject to 25% German withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, giving an effective statutory withholding rate of 26.375%, before any church tax where applicable. Domestic law, an applicable double tax treaty, or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may reduce or eliminate the rate if all substantive and procedural conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year taxpayer, annual returns are generally due by 31 July of the following year when prepared without a tax adviser. Where a duly appointed tax adviser prepares the return, the general statutory deadline is generally the last day of February of the second following year, subject to statutory extensions and year-specific rules. For non-calendar fiscal years, the deadline is calculated from the fiscal year-end. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Germany has no general monetary threshold below which cross-border related-party transactions are exempt from the arm's-length principle or documentation obligations. A local records package is generally required for relevant cross-border related-party dealings under section 90 AO. A Master File is generally required where the group's consolidated revenue for the preceding fiscal year is at least EUR 100 million. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Transaction-specific materiality and documentation rules may also apply.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no separate general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore regimes. German transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership concepts, anti-abuse provisions, general anti-avoidance rule (section 42 AO), place-of-effective-management rules, and treaty anti-abuse standards require the stated arrangements to reflect actual functions, assets, risks, decision-making, and commercial purpose. A nominal or shell arrangement may be challenged or denied tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion or reckless tax understatement

Statutory sanction: Intentional tax evasion under section 370 AO may be punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to five years, and in especially serious cases up to ten years. Reckless tax reduction under section 378 AO may result in a fine. Tax, interest, and applicable surcharges remain payable.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face personal criminal or administrative liability for their own conduct and may be liable for company tax debts under statutory representative-liability rules where culpable breach of tax duties causes non-payment. Criminal liability does not require veil piercing; imprisonment is possible. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction.
Failure to withhold or remit dividend, royalty, or other withholding tax

Statutory sanction: The German payer may be liable for the unwithheld tax, interest, and applicable late-payment surcharges. Intentional or reckless conduct may create tax-offence exposure under the AO.

Director personal liability: Managing directors responsible for withholding and remittance may incur personal liability under sections 34 and 69 AO for culpable breaches, in addition to potential criminal or administrative proceedings.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust taxable income, impose additional tax and interest, and apply documentation penalties under section 162(4) AO. The penalty is generally 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, subject to a minimum of EUR 5,000; late or incomplete submissions may attract a daily penalty, subject to statutory limits and exceptions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for culpable tax-duty breaches and may face penalties or criminal liability if intentional concealment or false reporting is established. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Company-law, accounting, and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include repayment, civil damages, fines, and criminal liability under applicable GmbHG, insolvency, and accounting provisions. A managing director must file for insolvency without culpable delay and within the statutory maximum period when the relevant insolvency ground exists.

Director personal liability: UG limited liability does not protect directors from personal statutory liability for their own breaches, including certain prohibited payments and insolvency-related conduct. Imprisonment may apply to specified criminal offences; travel bans are not an automatic consequence.
Money laundering, sanctions, or beneficial-ownership violations

Statutory sanction: Sanctions depend on the specific offence and may include substantial fines, confiscation, and criminal penalties under the German Money Laundering Act, Criminal Code, and applicable EU sanctions measures.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible individuals may be personally prosecuted or fined for their own conduct or culpable supervisory failures. Corporate liability and confiscation may also apply. A travel ban is not automatic and requires a separate legal basis.
