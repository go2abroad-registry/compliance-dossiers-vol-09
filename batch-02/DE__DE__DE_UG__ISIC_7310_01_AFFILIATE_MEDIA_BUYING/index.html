Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Unternehmensregister and Handelsregister portal at www.handelsregister.de; the filing is submitted through a German notary to the competent local registry court. Germany has no single federal online name-reservation portal. Preliminary name review is commonly obtained from the competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (Industrie- und Handelskammer, IHK) and the relevant registry court.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no generally applicable statutory company-name reservation period or automatic 30-day reservation. Obtain a written preliminary assessment from the competent IHK and instruct the German notary to submit the formation filing to the competent registry court. If the court rejects or objects to the name, obtain the written objection, identify the cited HGB, GmbHG, commercial-name, or misleading-name issue, and submit a corrected name or substantiated response through the notary. A formal complaint against a court decision may be available under the German Court and Non-Contentious Matters Act (FamFG), commonly with a one-month period after service where applicable; the exact remedy and deadline must be confirmed from the decision. An IHK opinion is not itself an administrative appealable decision. Refile after resolving distinctiveness, legal-form designation, misleading content, or regulated-activity concerns.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For a UG (haftungsbeschränkt), nominal share capital may be EUR 1.00 or more but must be fully paid in cash before registration. The funds must be freely available to the managing director at the company's bank account, and the notary must receive payment confirmation. Until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, the company must retain 25% of annual net profits under Section 5a(3) GmbHG.

Funding & unlock path: The office-based activity requires no fire safety certification, structural load inspection or special civil-defense clearance under federal or state law; the company must nevertheless comply with the Workplace Ordinance (ArbStättV), fire escape provisions of the state building code, and applicable first-aid and office safety documentation. The local building authority's use permit for the leased office suffices.

Paid-in capital rule: For a UG (haftungsbeschränkt), nominal share capital may be EUR 1.00 or more but must be fully paid in cash before registration. The funds must be freely available to the managing director at the company's bank account, and the notary must receive payment confirmation. Until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, the company must retain 25% of annual net profits under Section 5a(3) GmbHG.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT identification number administration and international tax matters; competent local Finanzamt for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessment; official electronic portal is ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt portal (www.bzst.de)

E-government filing portal

The UG (haftungsbeschränkt), whether German-resident or foreign-owned, must register with the competent local Finanzamt under §§ 134-138, 149 AO via the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung, submitted online through ELSTER. Required attachments include notarised articles of association, commercial register excerpt, managing director identification, beneficial ownership filings under § 138 AO, and place-of-management information. The Finanzamt issues the local tax number (Steuernummer). A separate VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is applied for with the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Form USt 1 U? or BZSt ONLINE portal) once intra-Community or economically relevant activity begins; non-resident UGs register via the competent Finanzamt at their domestic permanent establishment or direct VAT assessment point. Statutory processing time typically 4 to 12 weeks; failure to file carries registration penalties and assessment by estimation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation with unlimited corporate tax liability for resident UGs; the UG is a legal person taxed under the Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG) at 15% plus solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on CIT and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with unlimited corporate tax liability for resident UGs; the UG is a legal person taxed under the Corporate Income Tax Act (KStG) at 15% plus solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on CIT and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer). No pass-through status is available; non-resident UGs are limited to German-source income in territorial scope.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in German law. Statutory elections and group mechanisms that may apply include VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, corporate income tax fiscal unity (steuerliche Organschaft) under §§ 14-17 KStG requiring profit and loss transfer agreement, and trade tax group treatment under § 2 Abs. 2 GewStG. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are not elective; mandatory adjustment rules under §§ 12-13s AStG and § 4k EStG reverse tax arbitrage structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no EUR-denominated de minimis VAT registration threshold for entrepreneurs under § 2 UStG; however, a small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmerregelung) applies under § 19 UStG where prior-year gross turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover is not expected to exceed EUR 100,000. Exceeding either threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration with the local Finanzamt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods exports is available under § 6 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 UStG when the seller proves the goods left the EU customs territory in the course of an external Union transit/goods movement; for intra-EU delivery, tax exemption applies under § 6a UStG subject to valid customer VAT identification number and a proper recapitulative statement (§ 18a UStG). Cross-border advertising/media-buying services to an EU or B2B recipient are excluded from German VAT if the recipient supplies the service in its Member State of establishment according to § 3a Abs. 2 UStG; for third-country recipients no German VAT is charged as the place of supply is outside Germany.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Electronic Export Accompanying Document / Ausfuhranmeldung), pre-departure or assigned customs office
  • Formal certificate of departure (Ausfuhrlieferungsnachweis) or alternative transport-route evidence
  • Commercial invoice and INCOTERMS-dated sales contract
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or certified transit registry demonstrating physical departure from EU customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT/ACH proof of payment received from the foreign buyer
  • Customer VAT identification number where intra-Community export exemption is claimed
  • Proof of service performance for offshore media-buying services under § 3a UStG, including contract, service description, and beneficiary establishment evidence

Germany does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or central VAT clearance by the tax authority. From 1 January 2025, every domestic B2B seller must be able to receive structured e-invoices compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD 2.0.1); issuance obligation phases in as follows: businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices from 1 January 2027, and all businesses from 1 January 2028. Cash-based businesses must maintain a tax authority-certified electronic recording system (TSE) under § 146a AO.

German tax authorities and customs investigate fraudulent export zero-rating through § 370 AO tax evasion, § 370a AO VAT fraud, and § 371 AO self-disclosure rules. Penalties range from up to 10% of the VAT amount as assessment interest under § 233a AO to imprisonment for deliberate evasion. Burdensome documentary proof under §§ 6, 8-10 UStDV must be retained for 10 years; missing or inconsistent evidence results in retroactive VAT assessment at 19%, late payment surcharges of 1% per month, late interest of 6% per annum, and potential liability of managing directors under § 69 AO.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a Eurozone member with full capital and current account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63 to 66. No exchange control or prior approval applies to profit remittances, dividend distributions, affiliate media buying service fees, or intercompany funding. Cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 must be reported to Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV). Dividend remittances are subject to German withholding tax at 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, reducible or exempt under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, but no FX conversion or surrender requirement applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in Germany or the Eurozone. Export and service receipts may be retained in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency without mandatory conversion ratios or timeline. German residents receiving cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 per filing month must file an AWV statistical report with Deutsche Bundesbank, generally by the seventh calendar day of the following month. No Central Bank permission or forced conversion is required for trade or media-buying receipts.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as an auxiliary payment gateway, Low as the primary treasury institution. Under the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, payment services for a German entity must be rendered by a licensed credit institution or a BaFin/EBA passported e-money institution. Offshore EMIs not holding an EU/EEA licence or passport cannot lawfully execute payments or maintain settlement accounts for the German UG. A compliant EEA-licensed EMI with a BaFin passport may be used for payment processing, but the UG should retain a full commercial bank account for capital, professional tax payments, and preservation of creditor protection.

The receiving German bank must receive the SWIFT confirmation or SEPA receipt, and the UG must post the contribution to capital reserves in its books.
