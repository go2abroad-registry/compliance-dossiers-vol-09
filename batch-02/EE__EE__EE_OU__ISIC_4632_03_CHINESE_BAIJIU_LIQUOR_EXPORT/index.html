Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Commercial Register (Äriregister) automatically issues a registry code (registrikood) which functions as the statutory TIN for the Osaühing; no separate TIN application is required. For VAT purposes, registration is made via e-MTA using the standard VAT registration application (form KÄR): resident companies exceeding the threshold apply within 5 business days after the end of the month in which turnover exceeded the limit, with effect from the first day of that month; non-resident foreign entities without a permanent establishment may register for VAT directly via e-MTA, with e-Residency enabling digital authentication, and are required to appoint a domestic tax representative if they are not established in a jurisdiction with an applicable tax treaty; the VAT number is the registry code prefixed by 'EE'.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style hybrid classification regime exists; statutory elections include: (i) VAT group registration under VAT Act § 7 for VAT solidarity groups of closely related resident entities; (ii) the reduced 14/86 corporate income tax rate election for 'regular' dividend distributions under Income Tax Act § 50(1²), requiring a management-board decision and satisfaction of the look-back distribution regularity test; (iii) election to treat a foreign permanent establishment as excluded from the income-tax-neutral exemption scope; and (iv) excise warehouse authorization election for alcohol storage under the Alcohol, Tobacco, Fuel and Electricity Excise Duty Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year (VAT Act § 3(6)); €35,000 annual distance-sales threshold for imported goods supplied from other EU Member States; no turnover threshold for non-resident suppliers of locally supplied goods; excise registration mandatory for alcohol wholesale operators irrespective of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to the supply of goods (including baijiu spirits) dispatched to third countries, provided (i) the goods leave Estonia and the EU customs territory under an accepted customs export declaration, (ii) transport and supporting documentation is retained proving departure within 90 days of supply, (iii) the exporter holds proof of legal acquisition/ownership of the goods and of payment, and (iv) for excise goods such as spirits, the export occurs from a registered excise warehouse or registered-trader regime in excise suspension; closely related transport, freight, handling and insurance services supplied to non-EU counterparties may also qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX form, electronic via e-MTA/ICS) with movement reference number
  • Bill of Lading (maritime), CMR (road), CIM (rail), or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, export sales contract, and packing list describing ISIC 4632 alcoholic beverage supplies
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / authenticated SWIFT wire or Letter of Credit settlement record evidencing foreign exchange receipt
  • Excise administrative document (eAD) or warehouse removal document if goods moved under excise suspension

No real-time fiscal clearance or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate; e-invoicing (E-arve, UBL/Finvoice standards) is mandatory for B2G invoices since 1 July 2019; VAT returns, VIES recapitulative statements and Intrastat filings are submitted electronically via e-MTA; alcohol (excise) movements require electronic administrative documents (eAD) via the Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS) and the national excise warehouse system; third-country exports are processed through the EU customs export system integrated in e-MTA, requiring digital customs declarations (SAD, EX).

If the exporter fails to substantiate departure within the statutory 90-day period, the zero rating is reversed and VAT becomes chargeable at 22%, assessed by the MTA with default interest under MKS § 128 plus late-payment interest calculated on the unpaid amount; retrospective clawback of input VAT previously recovered on the export is enforced with a correction notice; negligent misdeclaration attracts a fine up to €1,200 (MKS § 154), repeated violations up to €2,400; deliberate falsification of export documents or sham export schemes for VAT fraud constitute criminal tax fraud under Penal Code §§ 404–405, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years, confiscation, and joint liability of the management board; the MTA conducts risk-based targeted audits of spirit exporters, prioritizing warehouses with EMCS data mismatches, forming an effective presumption of illegality against non-compliant filings.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under full capital account convertibility and current account convertibility as an EU member state pursuant to the EU Capital Movements Directive and related regulations. No capital controls apply to corporate profit remittances or export-related FX transactions. All legitimate corporate FX flows are unrestricted subject to standard EU AML/CFT verification and corporate tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio requirements exist under EU law. Export receipts may be converted to EUR or other currencies immediately upon receipt and repatriated without restriction. Statistical reporting to Eesti Pank is mandatory for transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 within 30 calendar days using appropriate statistical reporting forms.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the gross amount of distributed profits, economically equivalent to 22/78 of the net distribution, effective from 1 January 2025. Estonia generally does not impose corporate income tax on retained or reinvested profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on ordinary dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ, including to a non-resident shareholder, because the company generally pays corporate income tax on the distributed profit at 22/78. Transitional rules can produce a 7% withholding obligation for certain distributions from profits taxed under the former 14/86 reduced dividend regime. The recipient's residence, beneficial ownership, and the source regime must be checked for each distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The annual report is generally filed within six months after the end of the financial year, subject to the statutory Commercial Register rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory local-file or master-file obligations can apply to specified categories of Estonian taxpayers and cross-border related-party transactions, including qualifying large companies meeting at least one statutory size threshold generally expressed as annual turnover of EUR 50,000,000, balance-sheet total of EUR 43,000,000, or an average of 250 employees, as well as certain regulated financial and listed entities and transactions involving low-tax jurisdictions. The exact obligation depends on taxpayer category and the applicable Estonian regulation. Documentation should be prepared contemporaneously and retained even where the statutory threshold is not reached.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a separate UAE-, Cayman-, or Jersey-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is nevertheless required under general corporate tax, transfer-pricing, anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, CFC, accounting, and EU anti-abuse principles. The OÜ should maintain genuine decision-making in Estonia where claimed, appropriate directors and authority, commercial premises or operational arrangements, personnel or outsourced functions proportionate to the business, local books and records, independent banking controls, inventory and logistics evidence, and demonstrable commercial risk. e-Residency is an identity and access mechanism, not tax residence, management substance, or a permanent-establishment exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and concealed profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment for unpaid corporate income tax, late-payment interest generally at 0.06% per day, administrative fines, recovery of unlawful tax benefits, and possible criminal liability for deliberate large-scale tax fraud or submission of false information.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability for intentional tax offences, civil liability to the company and creditors for breach of duty, joint or subsidiary tax liability in legally prescribed cases, asset-seizure measures, and travel restrictions during criminal proceedings. Imprisonment is possible for aggravated or large-scale tax offences; it is not an automatic consequence of an ordinary filing error.
VAT fraud and false export evidence

Statutory sanction: Assessment of VAT, denial of input VAT or zero rating, late-payment interest, administrative fines, customs consequences, revocation of VAT registration in appropriate cases, and criminal prosecution for intentional VAT fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling persons may incur criminal liability, civil restitution, confiscation or asset-freezing measures, and potential imprisonment for aggravated fraud. Personal liability depends on participation, knowledge, intent, and statutory grounds rather than title alone.
Unlicensed alcohol wholesale, excise, and customs non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, seizure or confiscation of goods, excise-tax and customs-duty assessments, interest, suspension or withdrawal of authorizations, and criminal liability for serious smuggling or repeated unlawful trade.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and operational managers may face fines, civil recovery, confiscation, and imprisonment where they knowingly organize or facilitate smuggling, fraudulent declarations, or unauthorized alcohol trade.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable amounts or deductible expenses to arm's-length conditions, tax assessment and interest, documentation-related penalties, denial of unsupported deductions, and possible general anti-avoidance application.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for knowingly approving abusive or fictitious arrangements, especially where the conduct causes insolvency or creditor loss. Criminal imprisonment generally requires proof of an underlying intentional offence.
Permanent establishment and place-of-effective-management concealment

Statutory sanction: Foreign jurisdiction may assert corporate income tax, VAT, payroll tax, withholding tax, penalties, interest, and permanent-establishment obligations. Estonia may also challenge residence, expense deductions, or treaty positions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and tax liability for false residence claims, misleading filings, or breach of fiduciary duties. Criminal exposure arises where concealment is intentional and satisfies tax-fraud or false-documentation elements.
Beneficial-ownership, AML, and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Enhanced due diligence, transaction refusal, account freezing or closure, suspicious-activity reporting, administrative fines, forfeiture, sanctions penalties, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or sanctions evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and compliance personnel may face personal fines, criminal liability, asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for intentional money laundering, sanctions evasion, or deliberate concealment of beneficial ownership.
False accounting and annual-report violations

Statutory sanction: Fines, compulsory correction, tax reassessment, director-disqualification consequences in appropriate proceedings, deletion or dissolution risk for persistent annual-report failure, and criminal liability for materially false accounting or document fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable to the company, creditors, and shareholders for losses caused by breach of accounting and filing duties. Intentional falsification may result in criminal prosecution, asset measures, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Improper profit repatriation and unlawful distributions

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, corporate income tax and interest on taxable profit extraction, creditor claims, insolvency-related recovery, fines, and possible criminal liability where assets are deliberately diverted.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore losses, repay unlawful distributions, contribute to insolvency losses, and defend claims from creditors or the insolvency estate. Imprisonment is reserved for conduct meeting criminal elements such as deliberate asset diversion or fraud.
Employment, payroll, and disguised remuneration violations

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of payments, assessment of income tax, social tax, unemployment insurance and pension contributions, interest, administrative fines, and potential criminal liability for deliberate payroll fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they knowingly approve payroll evasion or false records, with civil recovery and possible criminal sanctions. Ordinary classification mistakes generally produce tax and administrative consequences rather than imprisonment.
