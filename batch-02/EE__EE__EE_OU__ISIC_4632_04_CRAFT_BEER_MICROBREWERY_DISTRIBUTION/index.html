Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

As of 1 January 2023 the minimum statutory share capital of €2,500 was abolished; an OÜ may be formed with share capital set freely in the articles of association, including an amount below €2,500, while the aggregate paid-up capital must not fall below the issued share capital. No mandatory escrow account is imposed by law; if founders elect to pay the capital before registration, the bank confirms the credit on a dedicated payment account, and profit-distribution restrictions (§256 of the Commercial Code) apply until minimum capital is fully paid in. E-Residency founders may use a collection account opened prior to filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA); digital service portal e-MTA at https://e-mta.emta.ee and Customs Single Window e-toil at https://www.emta.ee.

E-government filing portal

An Osaühing (OÜ) is entered in the Estonian Commercial Register and automatically receives an 8-digit registry code (äriregistrikood) that functions as the base taxpayer identification. VAT registration is mandatory when taxable turnover exceeds €40,000 in any rolling 12-month period; the application is filed through e-MTA (or on paper for non-residents), and a VAT number in the format EE + 9 digits is issued and validated via VIES. A non-resident without an establishment in Estonia may be required to register through a tax representative or file the standard non-resident VAT registration application with EMTA. The craft-beer exporter must also obtain an EORI number through the Customs Single Window for export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An OÜ is a separate taxable person and no pass-through status exists. Estonia applies a territorial, distribution-based corporate income tax: retained and reinvested profits are taxed at 0% until distribution; profit distributions are taxed at 20/80, and at 14/86 for regular dividends paid out of profits that have already been subject to corporate income tax. A dividend paid to a non-resident shareholder is subject to the 20/80 withholding mechanism unless an EU participation-exemption or tax treaty rate applies.

Available election forms

Estonia does not provide check-the-box or equivalent entity classification elections; legal form is fixed by the Commercial Code. The principal elections are: (i) the shareholder resolution determining the timing and amount of profit distribution, which triggers the 20/80 or 14/86 corporate income tax liability; (ii) the election under the Income Tax Act to exempt income derived through a foreign permanent establishment, thereby keeping the income outside the distributed-profit tax base; and (iii) for group financing and hybrid arrangements, the ATAD 1/2 anti-avoidance rules apply mandatorily and are not elective. VAT grouping under Article 11 of the VAT Directive is not available in Estonia.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 of taxable turnover (excluding VAT) in any rolling 12-month period; registration becomes compulsory from the first day of the month following the month in which the ceiling is exceeded. For cross-border B2C distance sales, the €10,000 EU-wide OSS threshold applies, and the business should register in Estonia if the threshold is exceeded and OSS is not used. A craft-beer distributor moving beer under excise duty suspension to another Member State must hold a license as an excise warehousekeeper, registered consignee, or registered consignor, and must use the EMCS system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) exports of goods to destinations outside the EU where the goods physically leave the EU and the exit is evidenced by the export customs declaration and transport documents; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered taxable person in another Member State, provided the customer's VAT number is valid in VIES and the goods are dispatched to that Member State; and (iii) ancillary services directly related to the export movement, such as cold-chain freight and carrier services for the kegs. For craft beer, the zero-rating claim must be reconcilable with the EMCS e-AD export destination record, the excise-warehouse removal documentation, and the export customs declaration. Input VAT is recovered in the ordinary monthly VAT return, with a 30-day target for repayment absent audit risk.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX) with Movement Reference Number issued via the Estonian e-toil Customs Single Window
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from an EU customs office of exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank statement proving export proceeds receipt within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and cold-chain temperature records for the artisanal hazy IPA keg consignment
  • EMCS electronic administrative document (e-AD) with 'Export' destination code for beer moved under excise duty suspension, including proof-of-exit notification

  • EORI certificate and excise license (excise warehousekeeper/registered consignee) evidencing lawful handling of alcoholic beverages

Estonia does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital invoice stamping. Compliance is administered through the e-MTA portal using X-Road secure exchange; the monthly VAT return (KMD with annexes) is due by the 20th day of the month following the tax period. E-invoicing compliant with EN 16931/PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 is compulsory for invoices to Estonian public-sector buyers and is the recommended standard for B2B; the national e-invoice operator Arveldusportaal is interoperable with e-MTA. Export declarations are filed via the e-toil Customs Single Window, and beer movements under excise duty suspension are tracked through EMCS electronic administrative documents; no API-based clearance or stamping is otherwise required.

EMTA applies risk-based desk audits to export refund claims, especially where remittance is delayed, transport routes are atypical, the e-AD exit confirmation conflicts with the export declaration, or counterparties are located in high-risk jurisdictions. If the zero-rating is not substantiated, EMTA reverses the 0% rate, assesses VAT at 22%, charges late-payment interest at 0.06% per day, and imposes a 20% tax surcharge where reassessment results from concealment or failure to submit declarations. Deliberate use of false customs documents or fictitious exports triggers criminal liability for tax fraud under section 397 of the Penal Code, with imprisonment of up to five years and confiscation. The clawback period generally covers three years from the filing deadline and is extended in concealment cases; repeat offenders may lose simplification rights and be required to post security for future refunds. In parallel, failure to prove the lawful exit of beer in excise duty suspension recharges the full excise duty with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital account operations under European Union law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-65). There are no exchange controls, no restricted currencies, and no prior approval requirements for foreign direct investment. Profit remittance, dividends, shareholder loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be freely repatriated in EUR without hurdle, subject only to standard anti-money-laundering documentation and documentation supporting the underlying corporate transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion obligation. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or domestic accounts without a mandatory conversion timeline. All export settlements must be reported for balance-of-payments statistical purposes to Eesti Pank, typically via the reporting bank, with country-specific transaction codes; no tax clearance is required to move proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting within the Eurosystem, alongside the European Central Bank (ECB); conduct-of-bank supervision is performed by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for non-bank payment collection and cross-border disbursement; Low for core treasury and statutory excise payments. PSD2-licensed EU/EEA electronic money institutions (e.g. Holvi, Monese, Wise Business) may passport into Estonia and offer payment accounts; however, alcohol distribution and excise tax remittance typically require a domestic credit institution partner or a full ECB-licensed bank. EMIs cannot lend, cannot offer investment products, and may impose immediate restriction on high-risk alcohol code activity. For the craft beer exporter, a Tier-1 Estonian bank remains mandatory as the settlement rail; an EMI may be used as a low-value collection auxiliary but must not hold more than a few days of treasury cash.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the non-EU shareholder should be executed as an inbound SWIFT MT103 or domestic EUR transfer with purpose code `EQ` / `EQUI` (equity capital) referencing `OÜ EE_OU share capital contribution under Estonian Commercial Code § 145` or `shareholder loan under resolution dated YYYY-MM-DD`. The receiving bank verifies the injection against the Commercial Register extract, board resolution authorizing opening of bank account, confirmation of beneficial ownership, and proof of source of funds. Outbound payments require documentary reference to the underlying trade invoice, uplc-numbered customs declaration for intra-EU acquisition or import, and, where applicable, excise duty payment or deferred excise proof.
