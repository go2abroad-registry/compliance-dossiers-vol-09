Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (Federal Central Tax Office) for VAT ID allocation and EU transaction matters; local Finanzamt (Tax Office) for income, corporate, trade, and VAT registration; ELSTER portal for electronic tax filings

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German Unternehmergesellschaft (UG) must first register the UG with the commercial register (Handelsregister) through a notary. Upon incorporation, apply to the responsible local Finanzamt using Form 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Questionnaire for Tax Registration) to obtain the local German tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT. EU-wide or cross-border VAT purposes require a separate VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) issued by the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, applied for via ELSTER or Form USt 1. Non-resident shareholders are permitted, and the UG may be represented by a director resident outside Germany; however, local service of process and tax compliance obligations remain applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG is a domestic corporation, subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT (Umsatzsteuer). It is not eligible for pass-through treatment under German domestic law. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the UG is a domestic corporation, subject to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT (Umsatzsteuer). It is not eligible for pass-through treatment under German domestic law. Its worldwide income is taxable in Germany unless a tax treaty provides otherwise.

Available election forms

No German 'check-the-box' election exists for corporations. The UG may form an Organschaft (fiscal unity) for corporate income trade tax and VAT purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections and structures are limited by the German Implementation of ATAD provisions (Anti-Hybrid Rules) under the German Income Tax Act. A UG cannot elect to be treated as a partnership or transparent entity for German tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold applies to foreign entrepreneurs supplying taxable goods or services in Germany; any supply in the course of business requires VAT registration. Domestic small business proprietors qualify for the §19 UStG small-business exemption if annual turnover does not exceed €22,000 and is not expected to exceed €50,000 in the following year, such exemption not applying automatically to cross-border suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU are zero-rated under §4 Nr.1 UStG and §6 UStG. Intra-Community supplies to EU customers who hold a valid VAT identification number may also be zero-rated under §4 Nr.1 and §6a UStG. The exporter must provide reliable documentary evidence of transportation or dispatch to another EU/third-country destination. Services connected with multilingual translation, localization, and media dubbing may be zero-rated as business-to-business supplies where the customer is located outside Germany, subject to the reverse-charge rule and proof of the customer's business establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / ATLAS filing)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and gross/net values
  • Proof of export receipt confirming goods left the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/payment provider evidence of cross-border payment receipt

Germany mandates the receipt of structured electronic invoices for all B2B domestic supplies from 2025 and the issuance of such invoices by 2027 (or by 2028 for businesses with turnover below €800,000). The relevant formats are XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or EDI-compliant XML, transmitted through the EORI/ELSTER infrastructure. Germany does not operate a transactional real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system comparable to Italy's SDI; compliance is ensured through periodic VAT returns (Voranmeldung) and tax audits based on invoice databases.

Export zero-rating is examined strictly by the customs and tax authorities. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT assessment, interest under §233a AO, late-payment penalties, and fines under the German Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung). Criminal tax evasion under §370 AO may result in up to ten years' imprisonment, and the responsible director/shareholder may be personally liable for underpaid VAT; corrective invoices and joint liability claims are commonly pursued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full capital and current account convertibility under EU legal frameworks (Articles 63–66 TFEU). There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no prior administrative approvals for conversion of EUR or foreign currency. Profits, dividends, branch proceeds, and liquidation distributions may be repatriated freely after tax. Only statistical reporting obligations to the Deutsche Bundesbank (AWV/Required Notification System) apply for any cross-border payment above EUR 12,500, but such notification is non-restrictive and does not imply a license or permit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds. Exporters are entitled to retain foreign currency balances or convert them at their own discretion through any licensed German bank. Statistical monitoring thresholds apply solely for balance-of-payments purposes: single receipts or payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five working days, but no surrender or conversion is required.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank), acting within the Eurosystem and in cooperation with the European Central Bank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a complementary rail, but not as sole banking infrastructure. Legal constraints: only EMIs with a German BaFin license or a valid EU passport under PSD2 (for example, Wise, Payoneer, Penta, or Qonto) may offer German IBAN accounts. These EMIs cannot grant credit, they are not covered by the statutory deposit guarantee scheme, and they often restrict multi-currency trade settlement documentation. They are appropriate for collections, supplier disbursements, and low-value cross-border payments, but must be underpinned by a relationship account at a prudentially supervised German banking institution for tax audit purposes and for receiving capital injections above EUR 10,000.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be wired via SEPA or SWIFT MT103 to the UG's German commercial account in the exact legal entity name. The mandatory purpose code must be communicated as 'CAPT' (capital contribution to a non-financial enterprise) or the bank-specific code 'S1010' for capital contribution; if no standardized code exists, the narrative must state 'Einlage gemäß Gesellschaftsvertrag — Kapitalrücklage'. The sending institution must include the UG's registered address and tax number, and the underlying source-of-funds documentation must be retained for the annual financial statements and tax audit. Germany imposes no Fx surrender or confirmation requirement, so contributions may be made in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency; the receiving bank will place the funds in a standard escrow-designated sub-account until the registry entry is verified, then release them for operating purposes.Outbound settlements use three clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments exceeding EUR 50,000, with exact beneficiary bank codes, but the transaction must be reported for statistical balance-of-payments purposes when it exceeds EUR 12,500; (2) commercial letters of credit (import/export LCs) issued or confirmed through the UG's substantive German relationship bank, ensuring that all documents comply with UCP 600 and with the underlying localization service contract; (3) SEPA Instant Credit Transfers through TARGET2 for EU business creditors when same-day value is needed. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT, equivalent to 0.825 percentage points, plus trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at the municipal Hebesatz, producing an aggregate statutory burden of approximately 22.8% to 33.0% depending on the municipality.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, resulting in a combined 26.375% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a German UG; a qualifying corporate recipient may rely on the 95% dividend exemption under §8b KStG.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GAufzV/§90(3) AO documentation obligations are triggered for annual cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 for goods and EUR 500,000 for services; Master File documentation is expected for groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 100,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting applies at EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; genuine economic substance is enforced through §10 AO on place of management, §§7-13 AStG CFC rules, §42 AO general anti-abuse rule, and DTA anti-abuse jurisprudence. A UG with no office, no staff, or no management activity in Germany may be disregarded as a shell, re-characterised as transparent, or denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under §370 AO: imprisonment up to 5 years, up to 10 years in aggravated cases, court-ordered fines, disgorgement of the evaded amount, and tax interest at 6% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate and withholding taxes under §§34, 69 AO; civil damages under §823 BGB; travel bans, asset seizure, and custodial imprisonment.
Shell Company / Abuse of Legal Form

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the entity under §42 AO, full re-assessment of profits, denial of DTA benefits, revocation of licences where relevant, and protective tax assessments with surcharges.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for abuse of corporate form, liability for payments made after loss of capital under §64 GmbHG, veil-lifting in cases of asset commingling, travel bans, and disqualification from managing German companies.
