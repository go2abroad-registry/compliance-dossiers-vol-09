Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), with national e-services portal e-MTA (e-maksuamet.ee) and the e-Business Register (Äriregister / ariregister.rik.ee) for entity and TIN issuance.

E-government filing portal

An OÜ obtains its statutory TIN automatically as an 8-digit registry code at incorporation in the e-Business Register; non-resident founders can incorporate remotely through the e-Residency programme. A non-resident entity that is not otherwise registered must apply to the MTA via e-MTA for entry into the Register of Taxable Persons using Form KNR; EU/EEA applicants supply their home VAT ID, while third-country applicants must normally appoint an Estonian tax representative. The MTA processes the application within 3-5 business days and issues a VAT identification number in the format EE1001xxxxxx.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - OÜ is an Estonian tax-resident entity. Corporate income tax (CIT) is 0% on all retained and undistributed profits (deferred or distribution-based taxation). CIT crystallises at entity level on profit distributions: 20% effective rate (20/80 of the net dividend) for irregular or excessive distributions, and a reduced 14% effective rate (14/86) for 'regular' distributions up to the three-year-average distributed profit cap. No withholding tax is levied on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; the entity-level CIT is the final domestic tax. — An OÜ obtains its statutory TIN automatically as an 8-digit registry code at incorporation in the e-Business Register; non-resident founders can incorporate remotely through the e-Residency programme. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - OÜ is an Estonian tax-resident entity. Corporate income tax (CIT) is 0% on all retained and undistributed profits (deferred or distribution-based taxation). CIT crystallises at entity level on profit distributions: 20% effective rate (20/80 of the net dividend) for irregular or excessive distributions, and a reduced 14% effective rate (14/86) for 'regular' distributions up to the three-year-average distributed profit cap. No withholding tax is levied on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; the entity-level CIT is the final domestic tax.

Available election forms

Elections available: (i) declaring distributions as 'regular' periodic dividends to access the 14/86 rate; (ii) voluntary VAT registration before reaching the EUR 40,000 general threshold; (iii) VAT group (fiscal unity) election for closely-linked Estonian-resident taxable persons; (iv) special cross-border OSS/IOSS schemes for distance sales and electronically supplied services. Check-the-box or disregarded-entity elections are not recognised; ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules apply mandatorily without opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year (general domestic threshold). Non-resident suppliers are registrable from their first taxable supply in Estonia unless the supply is reverse-charged to the Estonian customer or covered by OSS/IOSS; intra-EU distance sales of goods remain subject to the EU-wide EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies where the supplier transfers the right to dispose of the goods and the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU, evidenced by a customs export declaration (SAD/EX) with MTA exit confirmation and transport documents. Intra-EU supplies qualify for 0% VAT where the customer is a VAT-registered business with a valid EU VAT ID and the supply is reported in the REC. Services: B2B cross-border services to non-EU businesses are subject to place-of-supply rules and fall outside Estonian VAT (outside scope / 0%); ancillary export services are zero-rated. Input VAT attributable to exported goods is fully deductible, and excess credits are refunded by the MTA, generally within 30 days of filing the KMD return.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX) with MTA exit acceptance record
  • Bill of Lading (sea or air) or CMR / rail consignment note proving dispatch to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract or purchase order, and Incoterms evidence
  • Packing list and, where applicable, certificate of origin (EUR.1 or ATR)
  • SWIFT MT103 / inward remittance certificate and bank cash-conversion record for payments routed in foreign currency
  • Intra-EU supplies: customer's valid EU VAT ID and matching EC Sales List (REC) entry

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory (from 1 July 2019) using the European standard EN 16931 via an e-invoice operator network; no single national central platform exists. B2B and B2C e-invoicing remains optional. Estonia has no real-time fiscal clearance, no central API stamping and no digital-signature mandate. Tax filings are electronic: the monthly VAT return (KMD) is due by the 20th day of the following month, supported by the EC Sales List/recapitulative statement (REC) and Intrastat declarations as applicable; customs export declarations are lodged electronically.

MTA audit practice scrutinises the physical-export chain (SAD exit record, transport timing, Incoterms risk transfer and payment flows). Unsupported zero-ratings are re-characterised as domestic taxable supplies, resulting in a 22% VAT assessment, per-diem statutory default interest, and restitution of any input VAT refunds. The standard assessment window is three years from the end of the relevant tax period, extended where wilful concealment or criminal tax fraud is shown. Administrative fines apply under the Estonian Tax Code (MKS); deliberately false export declarations may constitute tax fraud under Penal Code section 147 (imprisonment up to 5 years in aggravated cases) with forfeiture. Customs debt and exporter/carrier joint liability may also arise under the Union Customs Code. Estonia has no exchange-control legislation, so no statutory FX-repatriation deadline exists; the 180-day benchmark is the documentary standard applied by banks and the MTA in export audit substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a member of the euro area and applies a fully liberalised capital and current account convertibility regime, consistent with EU Treaty provisions and OECD standards. There are no foreign exchange controls on profit remittances, dividends, or capital withdrawals for non-resident shareholders. Payments related to current account transactions are unrestricted; capital transactions are subject only to general anti-money-laundering and sanction screening protocols. For an Estonian OÜ engaged in packaged specialty food manufacturing, no prior central bank approval is required for cross-border transactions. The primary practical hurdles are not convertibility restrictions but rather compliance with tax reporting obligations (e.g., Form TSD for dividend distributions, withholding tax mechanics, and ultimate beneficial owner disclosure under the Estonian Commercial Register).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Estonia. Exporters may retain earnings in euro or any foreign currency without an obligation to surrender or convert. Export receipts must be recorded in the statutory accounting and reported to Eesti Pank under the balance-of-payments statistical reporting framework, specifically through the Financial Statements and Payments Reports system (SALAS), with timelines and thresholds as prescribed by Eesti Pank regulations. In practice, for low-risk trade payments, reporting is event-driven (e.g., payments above EUR 12,500 are subject to statistical reporting under Reg. 2019/4 of Eesti Pank). No exchange control certificate is required for trade settlement.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate to High, subject to legal constraints. Non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions may only provide cross-border services in Estonia under a valid licence issued in an EU/EEA member state and properly passported into Estonia under PSD2. An offshore EMI incorporated outside the EEA without a licence or passport is legally prohibited from holding client funds or executing payment transactions in Estonia. Unregulated offshore digital EMIs pose significant correspondent banking and sanctions compliance risks, and most Estonian tier-1 banks will reject incoming transfers from such entities. If the EMI holds a Maltese, Lithuanian, or UK (post-Brexit with appropriate branch licensing) e-money licence with a valid Estonian passport, it can serve as a secondary clearing rail, but the primary corporate bank account must be maintained with a domestically licensed credit institution for statutory capital and payroll purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT with the euro as settlement currency. The remitting investor should present the corporate tax identification number (registry code) of the Estonian OÜ, the account IBAN at a tier-1 local bank, and a payment purpose code reflecting 'equity capital contribution' under ECB Balance of Payments purposes (e.g., 'XE' for direct investment equity or 'XN' for investment fund shares; for capital contribution to a subsidiary, 'OE' is used for other equity). The remitting bank must transmit the investor's legal entity identifiers, UBO information, and the commercial rationale in the remittance instruction to avoid triggering AML review. Upon receipt, the Estonian OÜ must arrange statutory registration of the share capital increase in the e-Business Register within the notarised time limit; no central bank approval is required. Funds should remain in the capital escrow account until registration is perfected. For loans from the shareholder, use purpose code 'A70' or 'L' for intercompany debt, accompanied by a written loan agreement and arm's length interest rate to avoid thin capitalisation challenges.For settlement of import purchases (packaging, rice, seasoning, retort machinery) and export receivables, the principal clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers and commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 issued by the buyer's bank, MT707 amendments, MT798 for pre-advice, and MT734/MT750/MT752 documentary credit transactions) for larger trade flows. Exporters may also use SWIFT MT101 for wholesale batch transfers through their bank. For a low-risk specialty food manufacturer, banks will typically require complete trade documents—commercial invoice, packing list, inspection certificate, and Bill of Lading/Airway Bill—for any LC operation. Direct TT outbound disbursements must include the proper Estonian International Transactions Code (e.g., code 1.1.1 for import payments for goods) and must align with the customs declaration. Box 1 / Distribution line Gross taxable profit distributions, deemed distributions and fringe benefits paid by the OÜ subject to 20/80 CIT. Enter all distributions made to non-resident shareholders in the aggregate. The OÜ is liable for CIT; no withholding is made from the non-resident recipient. Late filing penalty EUR 60 to EUR 3,200, with daily late-payment interest of 0.06%; incorrect or omitted distributions are re-assessed with a penalty of 30%–100% of the tax evaded; persistent non-filing may lead to a tax enforcement order and criminal prosecution.
Box 2 / Non-resident withholding line Payments to non-resident recipients that are subject to source-country income tax withholding (wages, management board fees, certain royalties/services, where not exempt). Apply the domestic 20% withholding unless a DTA or EU directive provides a lower rate; obtain a valid Estonian Tax Residence Certificate (TRC) from the recipient's jurisdiction before filing. Late filing penalty EUR 60 to EUR 3,200, with daily late-payment interest of 0.06%; incorrect or omitted distributions are re-assessed with a penalty of 30%–100% of the tax evaded; persistent non-filing may lead to a tax enforcement order and criminal prosecution.
Annex 5 / Dividend distribution annex Itemised shareholder-level data for every taxable distribution, including names, country of residence, tax ID and applied exemption basis. Non-resident shareholder must provide its TRC, OECD TIN and a signed representation of beneficial ownership before the OÜ files the monthly TSD; absent documentation, statutory 20% distribution tax applies. Late filing penalty EUR 60 to EUR 3,200, with daily late-payment interest of 0.06%; incorrect or omitted distributions are re-assessed with a penalty of 30%–100% of the tax evaded; persistent non-filing may lead to a tax enforcement order and criminal prosecution.
