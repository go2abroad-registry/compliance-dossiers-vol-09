Name reservation / lock period

Germany has no uniform statutory nationwide name-reservation period. Before execution, request a non-binding name assessment from the competent local Chamber of Industry and Commerce (IHK), where available, and search the relevant commercial register, trademark registers, and domain records. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) division of the Local Court (Amtsgericht – Registergericht) at the registered seat; filing is submitted exclusively through a licensed German notary (Notar).

Governing statute

German Act on Limited Liability Companies (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG): §§ 1, 2 (formation and Articles), § 5a (UG haftungsbeschränkt), §§ 7–8 (application for registration), § 10 (legal effect of registration), §§ 35 and 39 (managing directors), § 40 (shareholder list); supplemented by Handelsregisterverordnung (HRV) for electronic filing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Articles of Association in the simplified model protocol form (Musterprotokoll) or individual Gesellschaftsvertrag, together with the formal registration application (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister) submitted electronically by the notary; entry in the Commercial Register (HRB) creates the legal personality of the UG.

Mandatory retention rule: the UG must reserve 25% of annual net profits until stated capital reaches EUR 25,000 (§ 5a(3) GmbHG).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — Federal Central Tax Office (VAT ID, EU OSS, withholding and central tax administration; portal www.bzst.de) and the locally competent Finanzamt (municipal/local tax office responsible for Steuernummer, corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT assessments; electronic filing portal ELSTER at www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1) Notarize the UG articles of association, file with the local Handelsregister (commercial register) and obtain the registered company number. 2) Submit the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (tax registration questionnaire) to the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER, which issues the statutory Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT. 3) File a Gewerbeanmeldung with the local Gewerbeamt (trade office) to obtain the trade licence (Gewerbeschein). 4) Apply separately for the USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number) from BZSt through ELSTER BZSt online service. 5) For non-resident shareholders no restriction applies; for a foreign-entity shareholder with no German establishment, dividends are subject to 25% withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% aggregate) unless reduced under a double tax treaty; treaty relief requires the BZSt/Finanzamt exemption certificate (Freistellungsbescheinigung nach §50d EStG). 6) A UE/foreign corporation that begins a German permanent establishment or makes taxable supplies in Germany must register through a tax representative (Steuerlicher Vertreter) under §155(3) AO if no German branch is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope: Resident UG is a body corporate taxed on worldwide income (unlimited tax liability under §1(1) Nr.1 KStG); credit and treaty relief apply for non-German income. No pass-through eligibility. Tax burden: Körperschaftsteuer 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% (effective 15.825%) plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax, base 3.5% × municipal Hebesatz, effective approx. 14–17%; total statutory aggregate approx. 30–33%). The UG must retain €0.25 of each annual net profit as a statutory capital reserve until share capital reaches €25,000 (§5a GmbHG). Non-resident ownership does not alter entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Germany. Available statutory elections and groupings: a) Steuerliche Organschaft (corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT fiscal unity) under §§14–19 KStG and §2(2) Nr.2 UStG — the UG as controlled company may pool income/zero-rate intra-group flows and centralize VAT; b) §8b(1)–(5) KStG election for 95% exemption of dividend income and gains from qualifying shareholdings (elective full exemption possible in some settings); c) VAT small-business exemption election under §19(2) UStG is generally not available to capital companies once turnover exceeds the €25,000 threshold; d) transfer-pricing advance pricing agreement (APA) concluded with BZSt under §89a AO for the financial newswire licensing model; e) family/group profit-and-loss pooling agreements (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) enabling Organschaft; f) EU Exit VAT grouping not applicable — no EU-level cross-border fiscal unity beyond German Organschaft.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Kleinunternehmer (§19 UStG): €25,000 annual turnover entry threshold (€22,000 for 2024) — a UG conducting commercial news-feed activities is a Kapitalgesellschaft and, as a legal entity with a commercial purpose, is ordinarily excluded from the small-business exemption; registration is therefore mandatory from the start of business. Foreign B2B suppliers of the news-feed API to German taxable persons are not required to register (reverse charge under §13b UStG grants liability to the German recipient). Distance/electronic services to German private consumers trigger German VAT registration only if EU-wide digital sales exceed €10,000 (One-Stop-Shop threshold) under §18(4c) UStG, unless non-EU supplier rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports to third countries: 0% VAT under §4 Nr.1 lit.a and §6 UStG, conditioned on the exporter being an entrepreneur, the goods leaving the EU being evidenced, and the foreign acquirer being an entrepreneur or legal person (non-entrepreneur acquisitions are also eligible if proof of export exists). Intra-EU B2B supplies: 0% VAT under §4 Nr.1 lit.b and §6a UStG, requiring the acquirer's valid EU VAT ID, intra-community transport, and the written/general confirmation (Belegnachweis) in the register of intra-community supplies. Services of the financial newswire/API (news aggregation and data syndication, not exempt financial-service intermediation): the supply is outside German VAT where the B2B recipient belongs in a third territory (deemed not taxable in Germany under §3a(2) UStG); for EU B2B recipients the supply is taxed at the recipient's place of establishment (reverse charge). For EU B2C subscribed accounts, the digital-services place-of-supply rule §3a(3) Nr.4 UStG applies; OSS return used to remit foreign VAT, no German VAT charge.

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) in Germany: from 1 January 2025 every domestic B2B issuer must be able to receive a structured electronic invoice; from 1 January 2027 issuers with prior-year turnover exceeding €800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B issuers must issue structured e-invoices. Applicable formats: XRechnung (XML) and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X (hybrid PDF/A-3 plus embedded XML), both EN16931-compliant. No real-time fiscal clearance (no continuous transaction control), no tax authority pre-approval, no digital stamping; invoices are transmitted electronically by email, portal or API. For the financial newswire API service, e-invoicing applies to domestic B2B contracts; cross-border B2B invoices and pure B2C receipts are outside the German e-invoicing mandate but remain subject to EU e-invoicing interoperability requirements.

German tax administration applies strict substantiation rules: failure to provide the export evidence or VAT-ID proof within the statutory documentation frames results in retrospective assessment of 19% VAT plus interest under §233a AO (0.5% monthly) and late-payment penalties under §240 AO. Falsified export documentation or abuse of the intra-EU 0% mechanism triggers §6a(4) UStG (knowledge-based test) and full clawback of zero-rating. Intentional or negligent misdeclaration constitutes tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under §370 AO, punishable by fines up to €50,000 and imprisonment from 6 months up to 10 years in aggravated cases; attempted evasion is equally punishable. Recovery of VAT in intra-EU fraud cases is governed by §3a, §6a UStG EU-wide joint and several liability. Documentation retention period for all export/zero-rating evidence is 10 years under §147 AO. Repository must also provide electronic audit access (GoBD compliant) including API access logs and remittance matching to satisfy audit cycle §147a AO.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full currency convertibility on both current and capital accounts under AWG §1 and EU free-movement-of-capital principles. No exchange-control authorization, central-bank approval, or prior declaration is required to credit, convert, or repatriate EUR/USD for the UG's newswire subscription business. Capital inflows into a UG by non-resident shareholders are free and require no license, subject only to AWG §§55–60 national-security screening for critical-infrastructure investments, which is inapplicable to a financial-news aggregation entity (ISIC 6391/NAICS 519110). Profit remittance hurdles are purely tax-driven: dividend distributions by the UG to non-resident shareholders are subject to 25% German dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (26.375% all-inclusive) unless reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty (the Germany–US treaty may reduce the rate to 5%; no Germany–Uganda treaty is in force as of this blueprint, so the statutory 26.375% applies to Uganda-resident shareholders). Service fee receipts of the UG are not subject to any German withholding tax. Inbound B2B subscription fees from EU clients are free of German VAT on supply; the customer reverse-charges under §13b UStG.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Germany imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, no deferred repatriation timeline, and no designation of export proceeds for the UG's cross-border service receipts. The sole statutory obligation is statistical reporting under AWV §67 (Zahlungsverkehrsstatistik): any payment received from or paid to a non-resident in an amount of €12,500 or more per transaction (or aggregated series of related transactions) must be captured in the Bundesbank's AWV register. The custodian bank normally files the report, but the UG must provide (i) the counterparty's name and country, (ii) the NGR purpose code applicable to 'information and data services' (S63/S64 - EDV-/Informationsdienste), and (iii) invoice-level evidence on request. Records must be retained for four years.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central monetary authority and administrator of the German foreign trade statistics and AWV reporting portal), together with BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht) as the micro-prudential supervisor of banks and payment institutions. Overall exchange-control policy is set by the BMWK (Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action) under the Außenwirtschaftsgesetz (AWG). Germany has no separate foreign-exchange administration; the Bundesbank is the competent data-processing authority for cross-border payment statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

EU-passported digital EMIs are viable at a Medium-to-High rating for collection-only purposes. Revolut Business UAB (Lithuanian EMI), Penta (operating with Solarisbank, Germany), and Qonto S.A. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal CIT rate of 15.825%; trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is separately imposed at municipal rates of approximately 7% to 17%, resulting in a combined effective corporate tax burden of roughly 30% to 33% depending on the municipality's Hebesatz.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, giving an aggregate statutory withholding rate of 26.375%. For German resident corporate shareholders qualifying for the domestic 95% dividend exemption, an exemption or refund mechanism may apply. For non-resident corporate shareholders, the statutory 25% plus solidarity surcharge is withheld at distribution by the UG and can be reduced under an applicable DTA or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally 31 July of the year following the calendar tax year; if the UG has a non-calendar fiscal year, the return is due seven months after fiscal year-end. Automatic extension to 28 February of the second year following the tax year is available when the return is prepared and transmitted electronically by a German tax advisor under § 149 AO.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Under § 90 Abs. 3 AO and the GAufzV Transfer Pricing Documentation Regulation, contemporaneous documentation is required when controlled cross-border transactions for tangible goods exceed EUR 500,000 in a tax year or controlled services and other transactions exceed EUR 100,000 in a tax year. A Master File may be required if the worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 100 million, and a German Local File must be prepared when the tax auditor requests transfer pricing records during an audit.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but economic substance is mandatory through the German general anti-abuse rule under § 42 AO, the ATAD-compliant GAAR, and transfer pricing substance requirements. A UG engaged in financial market news aggregation, syndicated finance newswire, and API distribution must have real management, employees, office facilities, contractual counterparties, technical infrastructure, and decision-making functions in Germany. Arrangements that are artificial, circular, or shell-like can be disregarded, income can be reattributed to the entity performing the value-creating functions, and treaty access can be denied under the principal purpose test and the OECD MLI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years or a criminal fine under § 370 AO; in especially severe cases imprisonment from six months to ten years. The tax authority additionally assesses the unpaid tax, default interest, late payment surcharges, and criminal forfeiture of economic benefit.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who knowingly participate face criminal liability. Under §§ 34, 69 AO and § 43 GmbHG, directors can be held personally liable for corporate tax debts if they culpably fail to pay taxes from available company funds, and civil restitution claims can be brought by the tax authority. German courts may impose travel bans and asset freezes pending fraud proceedings.
Shell Company and Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the structure under § 42 AO and the ATAD GAAR; recharacterization of the purported transactions; denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test; retroactive assessment of corporate income tax, trade tax, and solidarity surcharge; regulatory fines and compulsory dissolution proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false statements of no substance or approve sham contracts are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax underpayments; a finding of abusive structuring can justify piercing the corporate veil, personal fines, disqualification from company management, and criminal liability for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Upward income adjustment under § 1 AStG and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; a documentation surcharge of 5% of the tax adjustment, minimum EUR 5,000, under § 162 Abs. 4 AO; reclassification of excessive payments as constructive dividends under § 8 Abs. 3 KStG; imposition of dividend withholding tax on the deemed distribution; and criminal prosecution for tax evasion if the conduct is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for the improper reduction of corporate profits if they knowingly approved non-arm's length contracts. The company's right to recover damages from the directors under § 43 GmbHG can be enforced by the tax authority in insolvency proceedings, and directors can face personal fines and imprisonment for tax evasion through transfer pricing manipulation.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the GwG of up to EUR 100,000 per violation; serious or systematic violations can result in higher fines up to EUR 1 million or 10% of annual turnover, plus mandatory compliance orders and public enforcement notices.

Director personal liability: The managing director is the designated person responsible for filing UBO information; non-compliance can lead to separate personal fines. If the UG accepts undisclosed financing from third-party beneficial owners, the director can be subjected to AML investigations, travel bans, asset freezes, and possible criminal liability for facilitating money laundering.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Dividend Tax

Statutory sanction: Back-assessment of the withheld tax against the UG; late payment surcharges and interest; fines under § 380 AO for negligent failure to withhold; and criminal sanctions under § 370 AO for intentional non-declaration of distributions.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §§ 34, 69 AO and § 44 Abs. 5 EStG for amounts that should have been withheld and paid to the tax authority, even if the company is insolvent; civil recovery, attachment of personal assets, and in serious cases imprisonment can follow.
