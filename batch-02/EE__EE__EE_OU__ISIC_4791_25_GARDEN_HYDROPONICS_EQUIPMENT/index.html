Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (ETCB)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board via the e-MTA portal to obtain a statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT ID. Non-residents submit incorporation documents, business registration details, and proof of economic nexus; the Board processes applications within 30 days, issuing electronic credentials upon approval.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must register with the Estonian Tax and Customs Board via the e-MTA portal to obtain a statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT ID. Non-residents submit incorporation documents, business registration details, and proof of economic nexus; the Board processes applications within 30 days, issuing electronic credentials upon approval.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not offer check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. Taxation follows strict residency rules: Estonian-resident entities (including OÜ) are taxed on worldwide income at entity level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the EU or services supplied to non-EU customers qualify for 0% VAT rate if the supply is evidenced by customs export declaration, transport documents, and proof that the goods/services did not enter Estonia or the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Tollideklaratsioon)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent proof of payment to non-EU customer

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing via X-Road and e-ariregister systems. All VAT invoices must be submitted electronically to the Tax Board for immediate clearance and digital stamping; non-compliance triggers automatic suspension of e-invoicing rights.

Zero-rated exports are subject to post-export audit. Fraudulent claims result in retrospective VAT liability at 22%, penalties of up to 100% of evaded tax, interest charges, and potential criminal prosecution.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a Eurozone member and fully applies the EU acquis on capital and current account liberalisation (TFEU Article 63; Directive 88/361/EEC). The euro is the sole legal tender. There are no restrictions on current account transactions, capital inflows, dividends, interest, royalties, or branch profit repatriation. No approval, import/export license or exchange control permit is required for cross-border transfers. Profit remittance is subject solely to Estonia's unique corporate income tax at distribution (standard 20/80, or 14/86 for regular dividends), which is a tax event not a currency control. All payments are reported by banks to the Bank of Estonia for balance-of-payments statistics, but this is data collection rather than a control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline exists under Estonian law. An OÜ may retain export receipts in any freely convertible currency and settle in EUR or otherwise at its discretion. The Accounting Act requires bookkeeping in EUR; conversion is performed using the official exchange rate published by the Eesti Pank or the European Central Bank for tax and accounting purposes. VAT zero-rating for exports is conditioned on proof of transport and exemptions, but payment receipt timing does not trigger surrender or conversion obligations.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) in conjunction with the Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority) and the European Central Bank's Single Supervisory Mechanism for prudential/regulatory oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High with legal constraints. EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Estonia under PSD2 (e.g., Wise, Monese, Holvi) can serve as ancillary payment accounts for processing gateway settlements (Stripe/PayPal payouts). However, they are not recommended as the primary or escrow account for a registered OÜ because they: (i) may not provide an Estonian local IBAN within TARGET2-Eesti, (ii) do not support Estonian e-invoicing or direct debit mandates as seamlessly as tier-1 banks, (iii) can impose foreign-currency conversion spreads and daily balance caps, and (iv) are subject to their home-country KYC/AML supervision, which may not align with local Estonian tax authority documentation standards. Offshore EMIs outside the EEA are prohibited from holding assets or offering payment services to Estonian residents without an Estonian or EEA licence, making them non-viable for regulatory compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated revenue for Country-by-Country Reporting obligations; Estonia sets no statutory Local File or Master File monetary threshold, but OECD-compliant documentation must be produced on EMTA demand and is commercially advisable for any OÜ transacting with foreign related parties

Economic substance scope: Estonia applies no standalone Economic Substance Regulations of the UAE type, but substantive operation is enforced through ATAD-compliant CFC rules, the general anti-abuse rule, physical management-and-control requirements, Commercial Register dissolution powers, and payment-gateway AML de-risking (Stripe/PayPal). An e-Residency OÜ selling garden hydroponics equipment must prove genuine administrative activity, operational decision-making, and real business function; otherwise retained profits may lose their 0% privilege via recharacterization as deemed distributions to the true beneficial owners

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealed E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Full tax reassessment; late-payment interest of 0.06% per day; administrative tax penalty up to 20% of the concealed tax; criminal prosecution under the Estonian Criminal Code with imprisonment up to 3 years, increased to 5 years for aggravated large-scale evasion

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for restored tax and creditor harm; asset freezes, travel bans, disqualification from management, and direct compensation orders to EMTA and creditors
Shell Company / Artificial E-Residency Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Compulsory dissolution via Commercial Register strike-off; retroactive recharacterization of accumulated retained profits as taxable distributions at 20%; administrative fines; denial of treaty benefits and financial-sector account termination

Director personal liability: Civil restitution to the company and third parties for mismanagement; personal disqualification and travel bans; criminal exposure where the artificial structure facilitates money laundering or tax fraud
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Hidden Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment; excess payment recharacterized as deemed profit distribution taxed at 20% (20/80); daily penalty interest at 0.06%; additional penalty up to 20% of the adjusted tax; criminal referral for fraudulent intent

Director personal liability: Directors personally assessed for the company's unpaid tax where they voted or signed off the distorted pricing; personal restitution for unlawful depletion of corporate assets; disqualification and creditor claw-backs
Anti-Money Laundering / KYC and Sanctions Failure

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Estonian Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act of up to EUR 5 million or 10% of worldwide annual turnover for legal persons; freezing of corporate assets; mandatory termination of payment-acquirer agreements

Director personal liability: Individual administrative fines up to EUR 500,000; criminal money-laundering liability with imprisonment up to 3 years; permanent director disqualification and personal civil liability to aggrieved counterparties
Illegal Dividend Distribution and Distributable-Reserve Breach

Statutory sanction: The distribution is void and must be returned to the company; the OÜ remains liable for unpaid CIT plus 0.06% daily interest; Commercial Register liquidation proceedings for balance-sheet insolvency

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable to reimburse the company for the unlawful payout; creditor claw-back actions; fraudulent-conveyance liability; imprisonment for deliberate insolvent distributions
