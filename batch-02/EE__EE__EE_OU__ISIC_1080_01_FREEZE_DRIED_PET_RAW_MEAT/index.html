Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital; an OÜ may be incorporated with €0 capital. If share capital equals or exceeds €2,500, it must be fully paid before registration and may be held in a bank escrow account until the registry code is issued. For capital below €2,500, no escrow deposit is required, but management board members assume a statutory suretyship for obligations arising from pre-dissolution transactions (limitation period of two years).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) covering fire design, extinguishing systems, and annual self-inspection; structural load inspection and building use permit from the local municipal construction authority for vacuum freeze-dryer installation; occupational safety clearance through the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon), including workplace risk assessment registration and documented safe-work procedures.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital; an OÜ may be incorporated with €0 capital. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); official portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet) at https://emta.eesti.ee

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity first registers in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) through the e-Business Register (e-äriregistri) portal; the registry code constitutes the statutory TIN. VAT identification is then applied for through the MTA's e-MTA platform; the VAT number is issued upon EU VAT identification. Non-resident entities may use the EU VAT non-union scheme or apply directly for Estonian VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (unique Estonian deferred corporate income tax system: undistributed profit is not taxed, while distributed profits are taxable at 20/80 of gross distribution, with a 14/86 reduced rate for qualifying regular dividend distributions) — A foreign entity first registers in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) through the e-Business Register (e-äriregistri) portal; the registry code constitutes the statutory TIN. VAT identification is then applied for through the MTA's e-MTA platform; the VAT number is issued upon EU VAT identification. Non-resident entities may use the EU VAT non-union scheme or apply directly for Estonian VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (unique Estonian deferred corporate income tax system: undistributed profit is not taxed, while distributed profits are taxable at 20/80 of gross distribution, with a 14/86 reduced rate for qualifying regular dividend distributions)

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Estonia; hybrid mismatch rules per ATAD apply as transposed in the Estonian Income Tax Act. The OÜ may elect annually to apply the reduced 14/86 dividend rate by submitting the 'dividend decision' form to MTA, subject to holding at least a 10% participation in the distributing company. There is no entity classification ballot; classification follows the Commercial Register legal form. Cross-border mismatches are neutralised under the Income Tax Act §§61–63.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 in annual taxable supplies within Estonia (mandatory registration for resident taxable persons; a further €40,000 threshold applies to cross-border distance sales of goods and OSS-eligible supplies, while non-established foreign suppliers of goods or certain services may be required to register regardless of threshold under specific circumstances)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating (0%) applies to goods exported to an address outside the EU provided the goods leave EU territory within 90 days from the supply date, evidenced by the customs export declaration. Services ancillary to exports (e.g., commission, transport, loading) and services directly connected to export goods are zero rated. Intra-EU supplies of the freeze-dried pet raw meat product to a VAT-registered counterparty are subject to 0% intra-Community acquisition rules, requiring valid VAT number and EU Sales List (VIES) reporting. Proof of export must be retained; the burden of proof rests on the supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/EX1 accepted by Estonian Customs)
  • Transport document evidencing physical departure from EU (Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or air waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Invoice, purchase order, and signed goods delivery confirmation

Estonia does not implement a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime; there is no CTC mandate. Electronic invoicing is mandatory for Business-to-Government supply via PEPPOL format. VAT returns, amendments, and XML-based supplementary data must be submitted electronically through the e-MTA portal via MTA API and X-Road integration; failure to file electronically invokes prescribed penalties and interest.

MTA audits export files retrospectively; failure to substantiate zero rating within statutory limitation periods triggers reversal to the 22% standard rate, plus penalty interest at 0.06% per day and a surcharge of up to 10% of the under-declared VAT for failure to declare. Deliberate false documentation constitutes tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code, punishable by fines and, for aggravated false export declarations, up to 7 years' imprisonment. Refund claims on legitimate zero-rated exports are settled within 30 days from the due date of the VAT return.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility exists for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The euro is Estonia's currency, so there is no separate exchange-control regime, no surrender/approval mechanism for inbound or outbound capital, and no general permission requirement for dividend or loan repatriation. Profit remittance is conditional only on corporate-law and tax-law distribution requirements (taxable distributions at 20% or 14% under the Estonian corporate income tax system), a shareholders' resolution, and confirmation that the company's net assets remain positive after distribution. Cross-border transactions are subject to Eesti Pank statistical balance-of-payments reporting but not to FX licensing. EU/EEA capital-movement freedoms apply, with limited safeguards only under exceptional EU sanctions or national-security measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no minimum conversion ratio into EUR, and no obligation to hold export earnings in Estonian-regulated accounts. Export receipts may remain in USD, GBP, or another currency at the company's chosen credit institution. The OÜ must nevertheless ensure precise conformity between export declarations (customs Single Administrative Document / EXA), commercial invoices, and SWIFT settlement messages; Eesti Pank's statistical BOP reporting thresholds apply to each settlement, and under-reporting or misclassification is administratively penalized.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting within the Eurosystem and as the Estonian macro-prudential, financial-stability, and balance-of-payments statistics authority; foreign-exchange policy is governed by EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital, EU financial-services regulation, and Estonian statutes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viable but restricted: an EEA-licensed e-money institution (EMI) that has passporting rights in Estonia under PSD2 can be used for day-to-day collections, supplier disbursements within SEPA, platform-pay settlements, and virtual-card management. Such an EMI cannot hold share capital in escrow, cannot issue credit, cannot take customer deposits in the regulated sense, and generally cannot act as the sole treasury bank for an entity with debt-financed capital or substantial trade-contract commitments. Non-EEA EMIs offer low viability due to the absence of an Estonian/EU branch, lack of local IBAN ownership, AML/CFT supervisory gaps, and inability to support Eesti Pank BOP reporting; we recommend using an EMI only as a complement, not a substitute, for a tier-1 credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed profits; 20% on distributed profits (14% reduced rate for regular dividends qualifying under the three-year average rule, 0% for EU parent-subsidiary qualifying distributions)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory dividend income tax (owed by the OÜ; not a classic WHT); reduced 14% for regular dividends; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when the parent holds at least 10% of shares for no less than 2 years

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold codified in Estonian law; the tax authority generally requires contemporaneous local-file documentation when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 50,000; Country-by-Country (CbC) reporting and Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not impose Curaçao/BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the Income Tax Act §84 General Anti-Avoidance Rule and EU ATAD / CJEU case law require genuine board decisions, physical office presence, actual strategic management in Estonia and adequate office, staff and accounting substance. An OÜ operated as an e-Residency digital shell without local activity will be denied treaty residence, be treated as managed and controlled abroad, and may be struck off from the Commercial Register under the Commercial Code §60(2) for absence of legal address or management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment for the avoided amount, daily late-payment interest at 0.06%, a tax avoidance penalty up to 100% of the evaded amount, and criminal prosecution under Penal Code §389-1 with imprisonment up to 7 years for aggravated tax evasion

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally liable for unpaid tax if it arises from a breach of fiduciary duty under Law of Obligations Act §174–178; civil restitution of hidden dividends, travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts and disqualification from directorships for up to 3 years
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, full 20% income tax on all historical outbound payments (assessed as constructive dividends), a false-declaration fine up to €1,300, and compulsory dissolution by the Commercial Registry for absence of legal address or management

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for wrongful dissolution filings; may incur unlimited civil liability for tax debts under Wrongful Trading principles; the director's e-Residency authorisation can be revoked with a 5-year travel ban from the Schengen area if fraud is proved
Transfer Pricing Adjustment

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the arm's length price, recognition of a deemed distribution subject to 20% tax, 0.06% daily interest from the original due date, and a €1,300 penalty for non-production of TP documents

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's length pricing are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax and interest; if the adjustment is linked to a deliberate asset-stripping scheme, the director can be arrested and extradited under the EU Arrest Warrant when criminal tax evasion charges exceed a 4-year threshold
Money Laundering / UBO Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act §81, a fine up to EUR 5 million or 10% of global turnover; continued non-filing of UBO triggers a coercive periodic payment of €1,000 per month until compliance

Director personal liability: Actual beneficial owners can be charged with money laundering under Penal Code §394 and complicity in tax fraud; the court may impose travel bans, asset freezes, removal from directorship and civil liability to the OÜ for all resulting legal costs and reputational damages
Statutory Food Licence & Product Safety Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: An administrative fine up to €4,000 under the Food Act §39; an order to stop production, withdrawal and recall of affected lots; repeated or deliberate violation is a criminal offence under Penal Code §381 (operating without a required activity licence) carrying a fine and up to 3 years' imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable and may be prohibited from managing a food business for up to 5 years; strict liability tort claims for injuries to animals or consumers can be brought directly against the director in personal capacity (unlimited damages)
