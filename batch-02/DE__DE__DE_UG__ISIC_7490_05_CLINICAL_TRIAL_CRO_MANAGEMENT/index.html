Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Local Court (Amtsgericht) of the registered office district, Registry Department (Registergericht); nationwide through the Justizportal with the electronic SECtione / XJustiz filing interface.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG): § 1, §§ 4–5a (UG formation, obligation to retain 25% of annual profit until Stammkapital reaches €25,000), § 8 (application content), §§ 9c, 10 (court review and entry), §§ 36–39, 78 (managing director and representation); Commercial Register Ordinance (Handelsregisterverordnung – HRV) §§ 9–11 (electronic filing); notarial certification per Civil Code (BGB) § 128 and the Notarization Act (Beurkundungsgesetz) §§ 9–11.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) or the statutory Model Protocol for the one-person UG (Musterprotokoll gemäß GmbHG Anlage 1), together with the Application for Registration in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zur Eintragung in das Handelsregister).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 31 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The UG requires a statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 1.00; contributions must be fully paid in cash (in-kind contributions are not permitted with the standard Musterprotokoll) and credited to a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto or Sonderkonto für Kapitalaufbringung) opened in the name of the company in formation under the sole control of the managing director; the registration application must declare that the capital is at the company's free disposal (freie Verfügung). Mandatory retention of 25% of annual net profits applies until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, upon which the UG converts into a regular limited liability company (GmbH).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: mandatory fire inspection (Brandverhütungsschau) by the local fire authority, with approved fire safety concept, certified fire alarms, extinguishers, escape routes, and fire-risk assessment per § 3 of the Operational Safety Ordinance (Betriebssicherheitsverordnung); structural load clearance: static load certificate for archives, freezers, and IMP storage racks confirming floor-load capacities to DIN EN standards and compliance with the building permit (Baugenehmigung); occupational health clearance: workplace conformity per the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung – ArbStättV) for ventilation, lighting, sanitary facilities, and emergency exits; appointment of a safety officer (Fachkraft für Arbeitssicherheit) and a physician for occupational health, registration with the Employer's Liability Insurance Association for Health Services (BG Gesundheitsdienst und Wohlfahrtspflege), and confirmation of GCP-compliant environmental monitoring (temperature and humidity mapping) for clinical trial sample storage.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG requires a statutory minimum share capital (Stammkapital) of EUR 1.00; contributions must be fully paid in cash (in-kind contributions are not permitted with the standard Musterprotokoll) and credited to a blocked escrow account (Sperrkonto or Sonderkonto für Kapitalaufbringung) opened in the name of the company in formation under the sole control of the managing director; the registration application must declare that the capital is at the company's free disposal (freie Verfügung). Mandatory retention of 25% of annual net profits applies until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, upon which the UG converts into a regular limited liability company (GmbH).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) and local tax office (Finanzamt); official e-filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

E-government filing portal

File the federal tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent local Finanzamt before business commencement; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (tax number) for income, trade and VAT purposes. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately at the BZSt, electronically via ELSTER or the BZSt portal using form USt 1 TU. A German UG is registered as a taxpayer through this standard procedure upon incorporation and commencement of operations; a foreign CRO without a German permanent establishment uses the BZSt foreign-entity VAT-ID route, and if a permanent establishment or dependent agent exists, that PE must be registered locally with the competent Finanzamt and obtain a Steuernummer.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — File the federal tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent local Finanzamt before business commencement; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (tax number) for income, trade and VAT purposes. The VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for separately at the BZSt, electronically via ELSTER or the BZSt portal using form USt 1 TU. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election or pass-through treatment is available; a UG is mandatorily an opaque taxable corporation. Available statutory elections/consolidations: Organschaft (fiscal unity for corporate income tax and trade tax) and VAT Organschaft (VAT group), provided the controlling German entrepreneur and the UG satisfy the required financial, economic and organisational integration. Hybrid mismatch elections are overridden by the German ATAD transposition rules, including Sec. 4k EStG; no elective partnership-style transparency exists for a UG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for an entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany unless the small-entrepreneur exemption under Sec. 19 UStG applies. The exemption threshold is EUR 22,000 of turnover in the preceding calendar year and expected turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000 in the current calendar year; above those thresholds, or where input VAT deduction is claimed, registration and VAT invoicing are compulsory. A UG engaged in commercial CRO services will ordinarily meet the registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the UG to a destination outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Sec. 6 UStG where the goods are physically removed from the EU and the exporter retains sufficient export evidence. B2B CRO services supplied to a foreign sponsor are excluded from German VAT where the place of supply is determined to be outside Germany under Sec. 3a(2) UStG, i.e. the customer’s business establishment is abroad and the services are not attributable to a German fixed establishment of that customer. The UG must retain proof of the customer’s business status (valid VAT ID or equivalent foreign registration), the contract, and payment documentation. Input VAT on costs attributable to zero-rated exports remains creditable within the ordinary VAT return and refund mechanism.

  • Customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) processed through ATLAS and accepted by German Customs
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and reference to the underlying export contract
  • Proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) or electronic export accompanying document (EAD)
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent SWIFT MT103/bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system, API-based tax control tape, or government digital stamp. The B2B e-invoicing mandate (E-Rechnung) applies in phases: from 1 January 2025 every business must be able to receive EN 16931-compliant structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD); from 1 January 2027 businesses with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all businesses must issue e-invoices. Invoices must contain all statutory VAT fields and be transmitted electronically via Peppol, EDI or XML-based exchange; no real-time government clearance code is required.

The Finanzamt may disallow zero-rating and retroactively assess German VAT plus interest under Sec. 233a AO if export documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, untimely, or the foreign-payment evidence is missing. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in particularly serious cases, together with fines and confiscation of the financial benefit. The limitation period for substantiated tax evasion is up to ten years, and the UG bears the burden of proof; repeated export refund or zero-rating claims may trigger extended audit scrutiny and enhanced documentary verification by the customs and tax authorities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both capital and current account transactions in Germany. European Union Treaty provisions (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and the AWV/DWA regime guarantee unrestricted capital movements, including profit remittances and dividend distributions by a German UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)) to non-resident shareholders. No exchange control approval is required for clinical trial fee invoicing, loan servicing, milestone payments, or repatriation of retained earnings. Practical remittance hurdles arise exclusively from standard tax compliance: dividend withholding tax of 25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, unless reduced by a double taxation treaty, and from banking AML/KYC source-of-funds verification. The UG is fully at liberty to hold and transact in euro and foreign currencies; no convertibility restrictions apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. Clinical trial CRO services rendered by a German UG to non-resident sponsors are treated as cross-border service exports; the resulting receivables in EUR or foreign currency remain freely disposable and may be retained in any currency. Statutory reporting only applies for statistical purposes: cross-border payments above EUR 12,500 (or aggregated related payments) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five payment banking days in accordance with AWV Section 67. Failure to report is subject to an administrative fine. There is no legal requirement to convert, repatriate within a specified timeline, or sell foreign currency to the central bank.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Federal Bank) within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks; foreign exchange and cross-border trade reporting is administered under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to medium for primary treasury operations and low for regulatory capital clearance. PSD2-licensed e-money institutions and fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Wise for Business, Bits) can supply euro IBANs and cheap cross-border payment routing, but they are not credit institutions and cannot hold protected commercial deposits, grant overdrafts, or issue standby letters of credit. German tax authorities and commercial banks often treat EMI-held funds as insufficient provenance for capital injection verifications. EMIs may be used as supplementary disbursement channels for trial-site payments, but the UG must maintain a core Tier-1 bank account with a fully licensed German credit institution to meet salary payments, corporate tax debits, and regulator evidence of a substantive local bank relationship.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Lack of local economic substance and no demonstrable commercial rationale for a German UG CRO shell.

Root cause: Lack of local economic substance and no demonstrable commercial rationale for a German UG CRO shell.

Defense evidence: Provide the UG's notarized articles of association, proof of registration with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister), current statement from the local tax office confirming substance, a lease or virtual office contract situated in Germany, employment contracts of local clinical trial coordinators, two executed CRO agreements with biotech or pharma sponsors, payroll records showing German tips/salary disbursements, and documented transfer-pricing analysis aligned with the OECD Arm’s Length Principle.
Unclear or opaque beneficial ownership chain, including nominee shareholders or intermediate corporate shareholders in low-tax jurisdictions.

Root cause: Unclear or opaque beneficial ownership chain, including nominee shareholders or intermediate corporate shareholders in low-tax jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Obtain a current extract from the German Transparency Register (Transparenzregister), notarized share register, full organisational chart covering every shareholder up to ultimate real persons, certified passports of UBOs, and a signed statutory declaration disavowing any nominee arrangements or bearer instruments.
Inbound funds originate from clinical trial sponsors located in increased-risk jurisdictions, including offshore financial centres.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital injections into the German UG must be executed as a transparent SWIFT MT103 wire to the Tier-1 German bank account, using the statutory purpose code for capital transfers and identifying the sending shareholder precisely. For initial UG formation, the payment must reference 'Stammeinlage UG (haftungsbeschränkt)' and correspond to the capital amount stated in the notarized articles of association; for subsequent increases, submit the notarized capital increase resolution and Commercial Register filing proof before transfer. Shareholder loans must be framed with an executed loan agreement, the purpose code 'Darlehensvertrag' / 'Finanzierung', and interest at arm's length to avoid recharacterization as hidden equity. The receiving bank will verify the sending institution BIC, FATCA/CRS self-certification, sanctions screening, and the economic purpose documentation. Derivative evidence of capital clearance is produced by a funds receipt confirmation issued by the bank, which then enables the Commercial Register registration.Operating trade disbursements from the UG flow through standard SWIFT MT103 payment instructions for trial-site payments, investigator honoraria, central laboratory fees, insurance premiums, and consultant payments. For larger vendor contracts and any tangible trial equipment import, a Commercial Letter of Credit (irrevocable, confirmed, payable by acceptance) is preferred, especially where the vendor requires documentary assurance. EUR payments settle through TARGET2 with immediate finality; third-currency payments (USD, GBP, CHF, CNY) use correspondent bank clearing rails. Each disbursement must reference an actual underlying invoice and trial protocol number, with approved bank signatories and, when necessary, VAT reverse-charge entries. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax burden (0.825 percentage points), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, typical additive burden 14-17%); aggregate headline effective rate approximately 30-33%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding amount (aggregate 26.375%) for dividends paid by a UG to non-resident shareholders; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying 10%-or-more direct shareholders in an EU/EEA member state holding the shares for at least 12 months; DTA reductions between 5% and 15% apply for other treaty-protected shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year equals calendar year. Base deadline under §149(2) AO is 31 May of the following year; administrative extension to 31 July is common; with a mandated tax adviser (Steuerberater), extension to 28/29 February of the second following year is routinely granted. The UG must also file the trade tax return (GewSt 1) on the same cycle.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same due cycle as KSt 1: base deadline 31 May after year-end; extension to 31 July or, in tax-adviser cases, to 28/29 February of the second following year. A UG operating as a clinical-trial CRO is subject to trade tax on its business income unless a permanent establishment exemption applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions in a financial year triggers mandatory Local File documentation; Master File is required when the German CRO is part of a multinational group with consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 100,000,000; tax authorities may demand documentation within 30-60 days upon audit.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the offshore sense, but EU ATAD substance requirements and OECD norms apply. A UG engaged in clinical-trial CRO management must maintain real staff, physical premises, decision-making capacity, and contractual assumption of risk and function. The German general anti-abuse rule (§42 AO) and the Principal Purpose Test under the MLI may disregard a conduit UG or deny treaty benefits absent genuine economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Revenue Underdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under §370 AO: imprisonment up to five years, in aggravated cases one to ten years; monetary fine up to the amount evaded; forfeiture of proceeds; reassessment plus 6% annual interest; corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT assessed by estimation; severe VAT evasion additionally triggers license revocation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are joint debtors for unpaid corporate tax, trade tax, and VAT under §§34 and 69 AO if the shortfall arises from willful or grossly negligent conduct; criminal imprisonment and civil restitution apply; courts may order surrender of passport and travel restrictions in pending criminal proceedings; a criminal conviction disqualifies the person from acting as Geschäftsführer under §6(2) GmbHG for up to five years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: §162(4) AO authorizes estimation of additional income; a mandatory surcharge of at least 5% of the transfer-pricing adjustment is imposed where documentation duties are materially violated; late delivery can trigger fixed penalties of EUR 2,500 per day up to EUR 100,000 in commercial practice; 6% annual interest on adjustment; deliberate concealment can escalate to criminal tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the corporate tax debt to the extent the underpayment is attributable to their negligent breach of TP documentation obligations; they may be summoned under §71 AO to produce records; intent to conceal income qualifies as tax evasion triggering incarceration; civil liability to co-shareholders for breach of fiduciary duty also attaches.
Shell Company / Lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may disregard the entity as a sham under §42 AO, deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test or limitation-of-benefits clauses, allocate clinical-trial revenues to the actual service provider, impose German corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT with penalty additions, and void the withholding exemptions; local trade license may also be revoked.

Director personal liability: German courts permit piercing the corporate veil (Durchgriffshaftung) when the company is used abusively; directors are treated as de facto taxpayers and may be personally assessed; criminal conviction for tax evasion through sham structure carries imprisonment; travel restrictions and seizure of personal assets can be ordered before final verdict.
AML / Money Laundering via CRO Payment Flows

Statutory sanction: Infringement of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) carries administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities; criminal money laundering under §261 StGB is punishable by up to five years imprisonment; the proceeds are subject to forfeiture; the German FIU and supervisory bodies may initiate enhanced audits and revoke registrations.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or approve suspicious CRO fund movements are prosecuted as principals or accessories; they face criminal restitution orders and confiscation of assets; preliminary travel bans are permissible through court order; personal disqualification from bank signatory powers and board office follows conviction.
Disguised Profit Repatriation / Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: The German tax authorities recharacterize excess payments as constructive dividends: 26.375% withholding tax including solidarity surcharge is due, corporate income tax deductibility is denied, and 6% annual interest plus late-payment penalties are imposed; the UG, as statutory withholding agent, is liable for uncollected withholding tax jointly with the shareholder.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally liable under §69 AO for unwithheld dividend tax, even after the company becomes insolvent; criminal tax evasion under §370 AO may apply; personal assets can be seized during preliminary proceedings; a director aware of disguised profit extraction is simultaneously exposed to breach-of-duty civil claims from co-shareholders and creditors.
