Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Estonian law does not require a mandatory minimum paid-up capital for formation of an OÜ; the articles fix the share capital and share nominal value, and a EUR 1.00 single-share structure is standard. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity procedure: (1) obtain Estonian registry code (registrikood) via e-Business Register (e-äriregister) filing, which serves as the general statutory tax identification number; (2) apply for VAT registration through e-MTA when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 per calendar year or, for a non-resident with no fixed establishment, before commencing any taxable supply in Estonia regardless of turnover; (3) receive Estonian VAT identification number in the format EE + 9 digits issued by MTA via the e-MTA electronic channel; (4) register separately for excise duties, withholding tax, or employment tax only if applicable to the specific activity of collectible trading card printing and export.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections include: (a) regular 20/80 dividend distribution rule versus reduced 14/86 rate when distributing to an Estonian resident individual or qualifying non-resident, subject to a 7% withholding obligation; (b) VAT group registration election under VAT Act § 27(7) for closely connected persons; (c) election by a non-resident with a permanent establishment to keep separate accounts rather than applying deemed profit basis; (d) ATAD-compliant hybrid mismatch rules under the Income Tax Act apply automatically with limited elective relief; (e) fixed place of business registration versus direct VAT registration for non-resident e-commerce sellers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year for a person established in Estonia; a non-resident with no fixed establishment in Estonia must register for VAT before commencing taxable activities (no minimum threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods exported to a third country where title passes to the foreign buyer and goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the European Union within 3 months of supply (extendable to 12 months under Council Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/1912). The supplier must retain reliable transport evidence: contract of carriage, transport document, or confirmation by the carrier. 0% also applies to intra-Community supplies where the customer's valid VAT ID is stated and goods move to another Member State. For offshore services: B2B services are outside Estonian VAT scope when the recipient is established in a third country; B2C digital/offshore services qualify for 0% only when the place of consumption is demonstrably outside the EU.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic export declaration EXS confirmed by Estonian Customs exit record)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or other transport document evidencing departure from the EU
  • Export accompanying document (EAD) or T1 transit document where applicable
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank transfer proof of payment from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract with stated Incoterms
  • Internally generated evidence of dispatch (delivery note, carrier tracking, customs exit confirmation via Customs ECS system)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime is in force in Estonia. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory: suppliers to Estonian public sector entities must issue electronic invoices using the Estonian e-invoice standard or PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 (UBL). B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary. VAT return (form KMD) must be filed by the 20th day of the month following the tax period; VAT export listings and Intrastat declarations apply as applicable. Invoices may be issued electronically under the recipient's consent, and the MTA provides interoperability through the e-MTA platform.

Estonian VAT law imposes no statutory 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure functions as the contractual/banking review benchmark. Fraudulent or insufficiently substantiated zero-rating claims trigger MTA reassessment at the 22% standard rate plus late payment interest of 0.06% per day. Administrative fines up to EUR 3,200 apply for documentary violations; deliberate tax fraud is a criminal offence under Penal Code § 385, punishable for a legal person by a fine up to EUR 5,000,000 (court discretion) and for natural persons by up to 5 years' imprisonment. Records substantiating export exemption must be retained for 7 years under VAT Act § 102. MTA conducts ex-post inspection of export evidence (including bank statements, customs records, and carrier data) and retrospectively claws back input VAT and export VAT relief where the documentary bundle is incomplete or inconsistent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unconditional EUR convertibility. Estonia abolished exchange controls upon EU accession (1 May 2004); the Foreign Exchange Act was repealed, and no capital-account, current-account, licensing, or monetary-authority approval regime exists. Free movement of capital and payments applies under TFEU Art. 63; only statistical BoP/ITRS reporting through resident banks is required. Profit remittance hurdles are fiscal rather than exchange-based: under the Income Tax Act §§ 50, 501, 504, the OÜ itself pays distributed-profit corporate income tax at 20/80 of net distribution (14/86 for recurrent distributions qualifying as regular dividends), and no Estonian withholding tax is imposed on dividends to EU/EEA or treaty-resident shareholders. Dividends may be paid in EUR or any convertible currency without central-bank permission; the only practical gate is settling the CIT via the e-MTA (TSD form) before payment seasoning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation window, and no forced conversion ratios for export, royalty, or e-commerce receipts. USD, GBP, CNH, and other export proceeds may be held indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts with Estonian credit institutions, EEA EMIs, or offshore banks without notification to Eesti Pank; aggregate cross-border position data is captured only through annual BoP/ITRS statistical returns and the e-Business Registry annual report. Nevertheless, the board must retain compliant export documentation (commercial invoices, Incoterms, customs entries under EMCS/ECS) to support the EUR/USD re-invoicing, VAT zero-rating on exports, and transfer-pricing file during bank transaction reviews.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — the central bank of the Republic of Estonia and member of the Eurosystem/ESCB; responsible with the European Central Bank for euro monetary policy and for TARGET2-Eesti settlement; prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions is exercised jointly with the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) under the Credit Institutions Act and the Estonian Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act (MERS).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: High as a secondary collection and conversion rail; Medium as the sole treasury bank. EEA-licensed EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Monese) may passport into Estonia under PSD2/MERS and provide EUR (often foreign) IBANs. Legal constraints: (i) EMI client funds are safeguarded, not deposit-insured — the Deposit Guarantee Scheme does not apply, so long-term capital injections and dividend-warchest balances should stay below EUR 50,000 per EMI; (ii) EMIs can unilaterally terminate accounts, with automated freezes on high-velocity e-commerce or gaming-adjacent card flows; (iii) USD/CNH conversion carries hidden FX margins and slower correspondent rails; (iv) for statutory e-Business Registry filings and tax liability guarantees, the bank of record should be a supervised credit institution. Recommended topology: Stripe/Authorize.Net → USD settlement into Wise Business → EUR SEPA conversion → AS LHV Pank operational account; retain LHV or Swedbank as the official treasury account for VAT (KMKR), corporate tax, and counterparty standing orders.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: High as a secondary collection and conversion rail; Medium as the sole treasury bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on profit distributions (computed as 20/80 of the net dividend, equating to a 25% effective burden on net cash distributed); 0% on undistributed and reinvested profits retained by the OÜ.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on gross profit distributions under Estonian domestic law (effectively 25% on net distribution because the company must pay 20/80 of the net dividend); reduced to 14% on regular qualifying distributions; 0% is achievable for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders that hold at least 10% of the OÜ and satisfy the holding-period conditions. Treaty rates generally range from 0% to 15%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is expected when the aggregate annual value of controlled cross-border transactions of the OÜ with related non-residents exceeds EUR 1,000,000; Master File is typically prepared at group turnover above EUR 50,000,000, and Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Estonia is an EU/EEA substance jurisdiction and does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, Estonian GAAR, substance-over-form principles, the MLI Principal Purpose Test, ATAD rules, and the upcoming EU Unshell Directive require an OÜ to maintain real management, personnel, office premises, board meetings, and operational substance in Estonia. An e-Residency shell with no substance that channels trading-card royalties or dividends to foreign stakeholders will face treaty-benefit denial, recharacterization, and possible reclassification of the OÜ as tax resident or as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion on Undeclared Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment of the tax due plus 0.06% daily arrears interest; administrative fines under the Estonian Taxation Act; criminal tax evasion under Penal Code Section 347 with monetary penalties, confiscation, and imprisonment of up to five years for aggravated offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally charged with the criminal offense, subjected to travel bans and asset freezing, ordered to pay restitution to the tax authority, and disqualified from board service; the corporate veil can be pierced where the director caused a wrongful distribution that rendered the OÜ insolvent.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing Estonian taxable distributions, recalculation of the 20% corporate income tax, imposition of daily interest, and additive tax penalties of up to 100% of the under-declared base in aggravated cases; CbC and documentation penalties apply separately.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute non-arm's-length transactions may be jointly and severally liable for tax arrears and damages; in bankruptcy, the administrator may reclaim the under-priced benefit from the foreign related party through the director; criminal liability may arise for fraudulent tax evasion.
Shell Company and Misuse of e-Residency

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; recharacterization of the arrangement as a sham and deemed distribution; revocation of e-Residency status; compulsory liquidation under the Commercial Code; additional tax and interest with no statute of limitations in fraud cases.

Director personal liability: Courts can pierce the veil and hold the e-Residency founder personally liable for the OÜ's unpaid taxes if the entity is a facade; directors may face disqualification and extradition-eligible criminal charges for document fraud or money laundering.
AML and Card-Payment Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Estonian Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act carry fines up to EUR 5,000,000 for legal persons in egregious cases, freezing of merchant accounts, asset seizure, and termination of payment-processor relationships; aggravated money laundering under Penal Code Section 394 carries imprisonment up to eight years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal fines, confiscation orders, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to eight years; factual-control founders hiding behind nominee directors can be prosecuted as actual beneficial owners, and bank/processor liabilities can trigger clawback claims against the OÜ and its principals.
Failure to File Monthly TSD and Pay Distribution Tax

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty, 0.06% daily default interest, enforced collection and seizure of Estonian assets; repeated offenses escalate to criminal liability for tax evasion with monetary penalties and incarceration.

Director personal liability: The responsible director is the addressee of EMTA enforcement orders and criminal summons; personal liability attaches for taxes arising after a director knowingly failed to file, and in liquidation the director can be ordered personally to compensate the OÜ for resulting tax damages.
