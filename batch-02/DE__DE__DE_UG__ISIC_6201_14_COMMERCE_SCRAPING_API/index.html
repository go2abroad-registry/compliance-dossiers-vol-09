Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — responsible for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and EU/third-country cross-border VAT procedures; located in Saarlouis, online portal at bzst.de. Local tax office (Finanzamt) — administers the Steuernummer (corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT) and receives tax returns via the ELSTER electronic filing portal (elster.de). Customs matters are administered by the Generalzolldirektion (ATE/ATLAS export system).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the UG (haftungsbeschränkt) before a German notary and register it in the Handelsregister (commercial register) — this creates German corporate tax residency (place of registered seat/management). Step 2: File the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER with the locally competent Finanzamt, disclosing intended activities, shareholding, and expected turnover; the Finanzamt issues the Steuernummer. Step 3: Apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) via the BZSt online portal; this is mandatory for cross-border invoicing and for transactions with EU business customers. Step 4: For non-resident founders, provide proof of identity, apostilled corporate documents, and a domestic business address/registered office; non-EU foreign entities that are not otherwise registered in Germany must appoint a German fiscal representative for VAT purposes under §22a UStG, whereas EU/EEA entities may register directly. Step 5: Register for the EU OSS/IOSS schemes if making intra-Community distance sales or cross-border electronic services to consumers; open a German bank account for VAT refund flows and customs deposits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists under German law; a UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is mandatorily classified as a corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) subject to entity-level taxation at 15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate typically 14%–17%), yielding a combined nominal burden of roughly 30%–33%. Available elections: (i) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under §§14–19 KStG, §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG and Gewerbesteuergesetz to consolidate a controlled subsidiary into a single taxpayer for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT — particularly useful for German data-pipeline subsidiaries; (ii) VAT grouping (Organschaft) election; (iii) option for limited tax liability on German-source income for non-resident shareholders (no election, mandatory rule); (iv) hybrid mismatch treatment is mandatory disallowance under §4k EStG (ATAD implementation) — no elective hybrid instrument treatment is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual turnover (small-business exemption, §19 UStG) — a UG with turnover above this ceiling must register for VAT and charge 19% standard rate (7% reduced rate for qualifying goods; 0% for exports/qualifying offshore B2B services); additionally, the EU intra-Community distance-sales and digital-services threshold is EUR 10,000 (OSS registration) for cross-border sales to consumers in other Member States.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports (§4 Nr. 1a, §6 UStG): 0% VAT applies if the goods physically leave the EU within 3 months of dispatch and the exporter holds proper Beleg- und Buchnachweis (evidence and accounting records per §§8–10, 13–14 UStDV); services: for the web-scraping/proxy-API business model, B2B services supplied to a non-EU entrepreneur fall under the recipient-jurisdiction place-of-supply rule (§3a Abs. 2 UStG) and hence are outside German VAT (effectively 0%), provided the recipient is a taxable person in a third country and the service is not connected to German real property; B2C electronically supplied services to non-EU consumers are also place-of-supply at the consumer's residence with no German VAT; intra-Community B2B services are reverse-charged to the EU customer (§13b UStG). Germany imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, although the export shipment must occur within the statutory 3-month window; the 180-day repatriation figure in this dossier reflects standard banking/commercial credit terms, not a German tax-law requirement.

  • ATLAS/AES electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) evidencing clearance from German customs
  • Commercial invoice (Rechnung) issued per §§14, 14a UStG with a complete VAT ID, invoice date, service/goods description, quantity, and tax rate/zero-rating reference
  • Transport documentation: bill of lading (B/L), CMR consignment note, or airway bill (AWB) demonstrating physical movement across the EU customs border
  • Belegnachweis export evidence per §§8–10 UStDV (e.g., customs stamp, export certificate) and Buchnachweis accounting ledger records per §§13–14 UStDV
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment confirmation (not a statutory VAT prerequisite in Germany, but routinely requested during export-audit verification of payment traceability)

B2B e-invoicing is mandatorily required from 1 January 2025 (obligation to receive e-invoices), with phased mandatory issuance: from 1 January 2027 for businesses with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all businesses; the structured format must comply with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x XML) and be transmitted electronically (EDI or PEPPOL network is typical). Germany does not operate a Brazilian-style fiscal clearance/stamping system, but a real-time transaction reporting obligation to the BZSt is phased in from 2027/2028 under the national implementation of the EU ViDA framework, with API-based transmission of invoice metadata; OSS/IOSS returns are filed electronically; no digital stamp or prior authorization is required for individual invoices.

Export zero-rating is strictly audited through the Beleg- und Buchnachweis; missing or defective documentation at the time of the transaction retroactively reclassifies the supply as taxable at 19% VAT for the entire assessment period, with interest at 6% per annum (§233a AO), monthly late-payment surcharges of 1% (§240 AO), and denial of input VAT deduction; criminal penalties for fraudulent zero-rating or documentary falsification under §370 AO can reach 10 years' imprisonment plus fines, while administrative fines under §26a UStG reach EUR 5,000; the statutory limitation for retrospective VAT assessments is 4 years generally (§169 AO), extended to 10 years in cases of tax evasion; managing directors and legal representatives face personal joint and several liability under §§34, 71 AO for unpaid VAT and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a fully convertible Eurozone jurisdiction. Under Art. 63 TFEU and AWV, there are no FX-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. A German UG may hold unlimited EUR/USD/foreign-currency accounts, convert USDC into EUR at regulated platforms, and repatriate dividends without central-bank approval. The only friction to profit remittance is tax-withholding law, not FX law: dividends to non-resident shareholders attract 25% capital-yields tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on such tax (26.375% combined), reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% shareholding and 12-month holding period) or to an applicable treaty rate upon submission of a Freistellungsbescheinigung. Wire transmission itself is never blocked or delayed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. API and scraping-service export receipts denominated in EUR, USD or USDC may be retained indefinitely in any currency. The sole compliance duty is statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank: any cross-border payment received from or paid to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 equivalent must be entered into the account-keeping bank's ZMV electronic reporting chain within five banking days after the end of the calendar month in which the payment occurred, classified under the correct AWV purpose code. This is a reporting obligation, not a conversion or repatriation requirement.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank; administers external-sector payment statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance / Außenwirtschaftsverordnung AWV), acting jointly with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking, payment-services and crypto-asset-service licensing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditional Medium as an auxiliary rail, Low as a sole treasury solution. EU EMIs regulated under PSD2 / German ZAG (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Qonto) may lawfully open payment accounts for a German UG and are usable for SEPA collections and low-value vendor payments. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they cannot hold capital-escrow or stated-purpose reserve sub-accounts; funds are safeguarded (ZAG Section 69), not deposit-guaranteed; standard EMI risk policies often reject data-scraping/API reseller profiles and prohibit USDC settlement. A viable alternative is a BaFin-authorised banking partner with embedded crypto-fiat conversion (e.g., Solaris-type infrastructure or a MiCA/CASP-regulated partner), but only if the UG accepts continuous transaction monitoring and contractual disclosure of its scraping business model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires: (1) the shareholder to remit from a personal or corporate bank account, never cash or third-party funds; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA remittance; (3) payment description 'Kapitaleinlage in DE_UG / Direktinvestition' plus a unique contribution reference; (4) the account-keeping bank to map the transaction into the Deutsche Bundesbank external-statistics purpose-code catalogue and, for amounts above EUR 12,500, to file the AWV-based ZMV report within the statutory timeframe; (5) segregation of the capital injection on the dedicated Operational Account or Capital-Reserve Sub-Account, never commingled with API revenue; (6) after booking, entry into the UG's books by a German tax adviser and, where the contribution increases registered share capital, notification to the commercial register; (7) retention of the notarised articles, shareholder resolution and bank confirmation as the verification bundle for both bank and tax authorities.For a cross-border digital-services UG, the primary clearing rails are: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EU vendor settlements (hosting, proxy bandwidth, legal fees, subcontractors) with low cost and finality; (ii) SWIFT MT103 (and CHIPS/Fedwire correspondent routing for USD) for non-EEA vendors such as US cloud, GPU and carrier-infrastructure providers, always quoting the invoice number and the cleared AWV purpose; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600, irrevocable and confirmed, payable at sight) only for high-value hardware, server or network-equipment procurement where the seller demands payment security; recurring API subscription expenses should never be routed through L/Cs due to document-matching friction; (iv) USDC-denominated payments must be converted to EUR or USD on a BaFin/MiCA-regulated trading venue and settled via IBAN or SWIFT, never paid directly in cryptocurrency to third parties without an authorised custodian; (v) every outgoing cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 must enter the Bundesbank ZMV reporting chain and be reproducible in the UG's AWV and tax records.Deliverable EUR/USD outright forward (plain vanilla, maturity up to 12 months, documented commercial exposure)EUR/USD vanilla FX options (call or put, including zero-premium collars, permitted with bank risk disclosure and approved by the relationship manager)Cross-currency swap (EUR/USD fixed-to-floating, maturity-matched to a long-term infrastructure or license liability)Non-deliverable forward (NDF) on currencies with restricted interbank convertibility (e.g., KRW, INR, BRL) where the UG holds verifiable invoiced non-EUR receivables and executes ISDA/CEMA confirmations with the German counterparty bankUSDC/EUR spot conversion through BaFin/MiCA-regulated crypto custody platforms, used as treasury execution rather than a speculative derivative position

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Conditional Medium as an auxiliary rail, Low as a sole treasury solution. EU EMIs regulated under PSD2 / German ZAG (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Qonto) may lawfully open payment accounts for a German UG and are usable for SEPA collections and low-value vendor payments. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they cannot hold capital-escrow or stated-purpose reserve sub-accounts; funds are safeguarded (ZAG Section 69), not deposit-guaranteed; standard EMI risk policies often reject data-scraping/API reseller profiles and prohibit USDC settlement. A viable alternative is a BaFin-authorised banking partner with embedded crypto-fiat conversion (e.g., Solaris-type infrastructure or a MiCA/CASP-regulated partner), but only if the UG accepts continuous transaction monitoring and contractual disclosure of its scraping business model.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT, plus trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal multipliers (typical combined effective burden 30–33%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital-yield withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, resulting in an aggregate rate of 26.375%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 (goods) or EUR 500,000 (services) per fiscal year. Master File: required when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations comparable to UAE; however, under § 42 AO (abuse of law) and EU ATAD, a UG must have real substance (office, employees, actual management) to maintain treaty benefits and avoid forced reclassification. License payments to low-tax jurisdictions may be subject to section 4j EStG restrictions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 5 years or fine; in especially severe cases up to 10 years; plus payment of evaded tax, default interest, late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for tax debts under § 34 AO, civil restitution claims, travel bans, and criminal prosecution; corporate veil can be pierced if the UG is used as a sham entity.
Shell Company (Abuse of Legal Form)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation of transactions, retroactive tax assessments with 6% annual interest, trade licence revocation, and forced dissolution in extreme cases.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the difference between actual tax and recalculated tax, plus costs; travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed; criminal charges for aiding tax evasion.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines under GwG (German AML Act) up to €1,000,000 per violation, or 10% of global annual turnover for legal entities; cancellation of payment licences; criminal sanctions for embargo violations.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for fines and damages; injunctions and travel bans may be issued; imprisonment up to 5 years for wilful money-laundering facilitation.
