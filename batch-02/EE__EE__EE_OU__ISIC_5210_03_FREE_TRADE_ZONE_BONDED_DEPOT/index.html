Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital at formation (2011 deregulation; share capital may be registered at any positive amount). Where the issued capital is below EUR 2,500, the company is precluded from distributing profits until net assets reach EUR 2,500. Monetary contributions must be fully credited to the company's bank/payment account before filing; no notarized escrow account is mandatory. In-kind contributions require a valuer's valuation and a notarized transfer instrument.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); primary electronic portal: e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee). Customs declarations are generally submitted through the Estonian customs electronic systems integrated with the EU customs environment.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ receives its principal legal identifier as the registry code issued through the Estonian Commercial Register. The company must create access for its management board or authorized representative in e-MTA, register as a taxable person where required, and obtain an Estonian VAT identification number by submitting a VAT registration application to the MTA with the registry extract, articles or constitutional information, business description, evidence of actual or planned taxable activity, premises and warehouse details, management and beneficial-owner information, and banking or commercial evidence where requested. The Estonian VAT number generally has the format EE followed by nine digits. A foreign company without an Estonian permanent establishment may register directly with the MTA when it carries out transactions giving rise to Estonian VAT obligations. A non-EU applicant may be required to appoint an Estonian tax representative, subject to applicable EU mutual-assistance rules and the applicant's country of establishment. Customs registration generally requires an Economic Operators Registration and Identification number (EORI), obtained through the MTA or the competent customs authority of another EU Member State. A bonded warehouse operator must obtain the applicable customs authorization and maintain the records, guarantees, controls, and premises arrangements required by the Union Customs Code and Estonian customs administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ and no elective transparent-tax treatment for a standard Estonian private limited company. An OÜ is generally subject to Estonian corporate income tax when profits are distributed or otherwise treated as distributed, including dividends, certain profit distributions, non-business expenses, gifts, donations, fringe benefits, and payments linked to profit distributions. Undistributed and retained profits are generally not subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the time of earning. A nonresident company may be taxable in Estonia on income attributable to an Estonian permanent establishment and on specified Estonian-source income. Tax-treaty residence, permanent-establishment allocation, group-contribution rules, and legally available exemptions operate by law or treaty and are not entity-classification elections. Hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion outcomes under applicable Estonian and EU anti-hybrid legislation; they do not create a check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply generated in Estonia during a calendar year. Registration may be required earlier where the legal conditions for taxable supply, intra-Community acquisition, imports, or other special transactions are met. The threshold does not replace separate registration obligations that can arise for non-established businesses or specific cross-border transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate may apply to goods exported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union where the supplier, the supplier's agent, or the customer transports the goods out of the EU and the supplier can substantiate the export and exit from the EU with reliable customs and commercial evidence. The goods must be supplied in connection with an actual export, and the transaction must be correctly invoiced and recorded. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% VAT where the customer is VAT-identified in another EU Member State, the goods are transported from Estonia to that Member State, and the supplier holds the required transport and customer-status evidence. Services supplied to nonresident business customers are generally governed by place-of-supply and reverse-charge rules rather than an automatic export zero rate. Services connected with goods placed under customs warehousing, temporary storage, or other customs procedures may receive specific VAT treatment only where the statutory conditions and documentary requirements are satisfied. A bonded warehouse location or free-zone designation alone does not automatically make all supplies zero-rated.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and export MRN or equivalent customs reference
  • Customs confirmation that the goods exited the customs territory of the European Union, including exit results or equivalent electronic exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice and contractual order or supply agreement
  • Packing list and detailed goods description, including quantities, values, commodity codes, and warehouse references
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or other transport document appropriate to the mode of transport
  • Transport and delivery evidence linking the goods to the export declaration and the customer
  • Bonded-warehouse or customs-inventory records showing receipt, storage, release, and removal of the goods
  • Evidence of customer identity and, for intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number
  • Accounting records and VAT return reconciliation supporting the zero-rated transaction
  • Proof of payment or other commercial settlement evidence where reasonably required for audit substantiation; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a statutory Estonian prerequisite for export zero-rating

Estonia does not operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system for all domestic B2B transactions. VAT invoices may generally be issued electronically if statutory invoice-content, authenticity, integrity, readability, and recipient-acceptance requirements are satisfied. Public-sector procurement may require structured electronic invoices. VAT returns and related reports are filed electronically through e-MTA, generally monthly, with the VAT return and payment ordinarily due by the 20th day of the following month. There is no general mandatory digital tax stamp on ordinary VAT invoices and no general real-time clearance API comparable to clearance regimes in certain other jurisdictions. Customs declarations and warehouse-control records must be submitted or maintained through the applicable electronic customs systems, and the operator must preserve auditable links between customs declarations, inventory movements, invoices, transport documents, and VAT reporting.

Estonia has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement for an inward-remittance certificate as a condition of applying export zero-rating. If export or intra-Community evidence is missing, contradictory, fabricated, or does not establish transport or exit, the MTA may deny the 0% treatment, assess the applicable VAT retrospectively, charge statutory tax interest, and impose administrative penalties. Incorrect VAT returns may require correction and payment of the resulting tax liability. Fraudulent customs declarations or false supporting documents may trigger customs debt, seizure or forfeiture measures, administrative liability, and, in serious cases, criminal liability. Deliberate tax fraud, concealment of taxable turnover, or false documentation may also expose responsible persons to criminal sanctions. The tax authority may audit and reassess within the applicable statutory limitation period, which can be extended for intentional violations under Estonian law. Exporters and warehouse operators should retain customs, transport, inventory, invoicing, customer, and payment records for the applicable statutory retention period and maintain a transaction-level audit trail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area member state and applies full convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance by resident entities to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted under EU free-movement-of-capital rules. The only practical hurdles are EU-wide sanctions screening, AML/CFT customer due diligence, and beneficial ownership transparency filings required by the Estonian Commercial Register and Financial Intelligence Unit

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists in Estonia. Section 2 of the current foreign-exchange legal framework, read together with the Eurosystem rules, permits exporters to retain, hold, denominate, and convert export receipts in any freely convertible currency. Receipts must only be reported indirectly through corporate tax returns, customs export declarations, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting where applicable

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem; Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) is the administering licensing and supervisory authority for credit institutions, EMIs, and forex service providers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: CONDITIONAL MEDIUM. Estonia permits PSD2-licensed electronic money institutions and EU-passported fintech EMIs to onboard Estonian legal entities remotely. However, large Estonian commercial banks act as correspondent-keeper banks and routinely de-risk free-zone, high-volume cross-border entities. A bonded-depot OÜ should use an EU-authorized EMI (e.g., LHV-owned fintech rails, or SWIFT-connected platform) only as a secondary disbursement rail, never as the primary escrow or customs duty account. Primary customs duty payments must be routed through an EMTA-compatible domestic credit institution or an EMI with a direct account at Eesti Pank and a valid customs-guarantee arrangement

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% (applied only upon profit distribution; undistributed retained earnings are taxed at 0%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions threshold for mandatory transfer pricing documentation; no separate Master File requirement for groups below EUR 7 million consolidated turnover and fewer than 250 employees, but the MTA may request contemporaneous documentation where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds EUR 50,000; the Local File obligation applies to taxpayers with related-party transactions exceeding this threshold; the Master File is required only when the group's consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 50 million; documentation must be prepared by the statutory deadline of the tax return (31 May of the following year) and submitted to MTA within 60 days of a written request

Economic substance scope: Estonia historically does not maintain a statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) system in the same manner as zero-tax jurisdictions (e.g., UAE, Barbados, Cayman), but the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under section 6 of the Estonian Income Tax Act, the Tax Information Exchange framework, the OECD BEPS Action 5 transparency framework, and the EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules impose a mandatory substantive economic-activity requirement for treaty benefits, participation exemption, EU directive relief, and transfer pricing defense; an OÜ operating as an FTZ bonded warehouse depot must demonstrate real business presence through (i) licensed customs warehouse operations under the Estonian Customs Act, (ii) physical inventory management and controlled movement records, (iii) actual employment of licensed customs brokers and logistics personnel, (iv) local decision-making functions regarding warehouse contracts and bonded inventory, (v) local bank accounts and ERP systems, and (vi) compliance with the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act (RahaPTS) requiring a designated AML compliance officer, risk assessment, and suspicious transaction reporting; failure to maintain economic substance creates rebuttable presumption of a shell company for e-Residency business structures, exposing the corporate veil to piercing and the shareholders to personal liability for unpaid withholding taxes and customs duties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Sham Dividend-Only Structure

Statutory sanction: Imposition of 20% corporate income tax and penalty interest at 0.06% per day, plus a tax evasion fine of up to EUR 1,200 per submission and a criminal fine under the Estonian Penal Code for tax fraud up to EUR 60,000; the MTA may recharacterize the entire accumulated reserve as a deemed dividend distribution in the year of cessation of residence or liquidation

Director personal liability: Members of the management board are jointly and severally liable under the Commercial Code and Tax Code for unpaid corporate tax withholding obligations if the company's assets are insufficient due to intentional concealment or gross negligence; directors may face criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years for significant tax fraud; travel bans and asset freezing orders can be imposed; civil restitution to the tax authority includes the full underpaid tax, penalty interest, and procedural fines
Transfer Pricing Manipulation in Bonded Storage Fees

Statutory sanction: The MTA is authorized to make a transfer pricing adjustment reflecting arm's length consideration, treat the difference as a deemed distribution subject to 20/80 corporate income tax and withholding tax on the deemed dividend, and levy a tax arrears interest of 0.06% per day; additional fine up to EUR 1,200 per incorrect declaration; in repeat cases, a criminal fine up to EUR 60,000 or imprisonment up to 3 years

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved falsified TP documentation may be held personally liable for the tax adjustment under the principle of fault-based liability; the Estonian Supreme Court has upheld director liability for deliberate under-declaration; imprisonment up to 3 years, disqualification from serving as a director for up to 5 years, and confiscation of personal assets
Customs Bonded Warehouse Duty Evasion

Statutory sanction: Customs debt arises immediately for the full amount of import duties and VAT (20% or 22% as applicable) plus excise duties if relevant; a customs penalty of up to EUR 3,000 per infraction; bond cancellation and revocation of the bonded warehouse license; criminal confiscation of goods and a criminal fine up to EUR 600,000 under the Customs Act as a felony offense

Director personal liability: The licensed warehouse operator's management is personally liable under customs law if the bond agreement was signed personally; directors face imprisonment of up to 5 years for large-scale customs fraud, seizure of personal bank accounts, prohibition on leaving Estonia (travel ban), and full civil restitution of evaded duties and VAT
AML/Sanctions Breach in Cross-Border Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Supervisory fine from the Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal persons for AML/CFT violations; prohibition of the natural person from acting in management; suspension of the bonded warehouse license; criminal penalty under Penal Code sections for money laundering up to EUR 4.8 million or imprisonment up to 7 years

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated AML compliance officer bear personal criminal liability for willful failure to report suspicious transactions under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; penalties include imprisonment up to 7 years, confiscation of assets involved in the transaction, a travel ban, disqualification from corporate management, and civil liability to the tax authority and affected third parties for damages caused by the AML breach
E-Residency Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the OÜ as a tax-transparent entity or deemed non-resident under the place-of-effective-management test; retroactive assessment of 20% tax on all undistributed profits for up to 5 years plus interest; denial of EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive withholding relief; cancellation of e-Residency digital ID and exclusion from accessing the e-MTA portal; fine up to EUR 2,000 for false declarations in the Commercial Register

Director personal liability: Shareholders who are the directing minds may be personally assessed for all unpaid corporate taxes under the Dominant Influence Doctrine; imprisonment up to 3 years for fraudulent registration; permanent entry ban to the Schengen area may be applied by the Estonian Police and Border Guard Board if the fraudulent activity involved cross-border smuggling or AML violations; civil tort liability to the tax authority for losses from the tax assessment
