Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) and central services; local Finanzamt (tax office) responsible for national tax number (Steuernummer); official portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt online portal (www.bzst.de)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity must register with the competent local Finanzamt for the jurisdiction where the German permanent establishment or economic activity is situated by filing the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER or in paper form. After assignment of the Steuernummer, the entity applies to BZSt for a VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.). For B2C electronically supplied services, a foreign seller may alternatively register under the EU OSS (One-Stop-Shop) scheme on the BZSt portal; registration number is used for VAT remittance in Germany. Non-resident entities must also obtain a Steuernummer for withholding tax and corporate income tax filings when they have German-source income.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not apply check-the-box classification. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers of digital services or for any entrepreneur without a domestic seat; tax liability arises from the first euro of taxable supplies in Germany. Domestic micro-businesses may apply Sec. 19 UStG small-business exemption for prior-year turnover up to €22,000 and current-year turnover up to €50,000, but this exemption is not available to foreign entrepreneurs; mandatory VAT registration applies immediately for the UG if it makes taxable supplies in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (Steuerfreiheit mit Vorsteuerabzug) applies to exports of goods (Sec. 6 UStG) where the goods physically depart the EU through a customs export declaration, and to certain cross-border services listed in Sec. 0 UStG (e.g., services directly related to exported goods, transfers of intellectual property, consultancy and digital services rendered to a recipient not resident in the EU, provided the recipient is an entrepreneur or the place of supply is outside the EU). For the video-chat app supplying digital services to non-EU consumers, the place of supply is outside the EU (Sec. 3a UStG), so German VAT is not chargeable; to claim zero-rating/foreign-place-of-supply, the supplier must hold credible residence evidence for the recipient, including a non-EU IP address, registered user country, payment method BIN country, bank location, and customer declaration. Supplies of digital services to EU consumers are subjected to respective EU Member State VAT via the OSS procedure, not zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrpapier) approved by German customs (ATLAS export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, Airwaybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing transport of goods out of the EU
  • Commercial invoice with incoterms and export reason code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or payment transfer records proving consideration received from the non-EU counterparty
  • Service contract and evidence of non-EU recipient residence: IP address logs, geolocation data, mobile country code, payment instrument BIN country, and customer self-certification

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased: by 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/FACTUR-X, or EDI). From 1 January 2027, issuance of e-invoices to domestic B2B customers is mandatory for taxpayers with certain turnover thresholds (fully mandatory from 2028). There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no government tax-authority API clearing, and no digital stamping regime for Germany. E-invoices must be transmitted in a structured electronic format with authentic integrity/readability; B2C supplies by the video-chat app are governed by standard invoicing rules only when the customer requires an invoice.

Fraudulent or unjustified export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment under Sec. 14c UStG for incorrect invoice tax, plus interest under Sec. 233a AO. Evasion penalties range from fines up to €50,000 per offense and imprisonment up to five years for tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, with an extended limitation period of ten years. In addition, BZSt can cancel VAT ID numbers and deny import/export authorization; platform operators may be jointly liable under Sec. 25e UStG for trader VAT claims in marketplace contexts. Repayment claims by the tax authority include civil-law clawback of improperly retained export VAT refunds plus penalty surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro on both the current account and the capital account, in accordance with European Union Treaty provisions on free movement of capital, the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG). There is no governmental approval requirement, no currency licensing, and no repatriation bottleneck for legitimate profits, dividends, licence fees, or loan repayments. Profit remittance from a German UG requires only a valid shareholder meeting resolution, a commercially audited annual financial statement, and proof that distributable reserves exist under the German Commercial Code (HGB §§ 266, 268, 272); such distributions are not subject to German FX surrender or conversion rules. Withholding tax at 26.375% inclusive of solidarity surcharge applies to dividend payments to non-resident shareholders, subject to reduction under an applicable double tax treaty. The sole administrative friction is the statistical cross-border payment reporting obligation to Deutsche Bundesbank once cumulative transaction values exceed EUR 12,500.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. Exporters and cross-border service providers may retain all export proceeds indefinitely in foreign currency accounts with any German or non-German bank. The OECD/EU principle is complete retention without forced conversion. The compliance obligation instead is Deutsche Bundesbank balance-of-payments and external trade statistics reporting under the AWG and the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV): any cross-border payment received or made by a German resident in excess of EUR 12,500 per transaction, including export prepayments and settlement proceeds, must be reported electronically to Deutsche Bundesbank by the fifth business day following the month in which the payment occurs. Where a third-party payment intermediary acts, the intermediary may be jointly and severally liable for accurate reporting. No approval is required and there is no minimum export surrender percentage.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Zentralbank) with prudential and AML/CFT oversight jointly exercised with Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) and Deutsche Zoll for foreign trade enforcement; Germany operates no separate Central Bank of Foreign Exchange Administration authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: CONDITIONAL / MEDIUM with significant governance constraints. Non-bank fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Wise Business, and Pliant, and German-licensed EMIs operating under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and the Second E-Money Directive, can provide rapid multi-currency IBAN-holding accounts, virtual cards, and Paymentwall/Apple-IAP payouts. Such institutions are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG); they may not grant credit, hold client funds indefinitely, or provide the full German Einlagensicherung deposit protection. A major substantive blocker is that German banks now apply 'look-through' KYC requirements to underlying commercial activity; a real-time video match application classified by BaFin typologies as a 'higher potential for illicit content' business (possible adult services or human trafficking associated activity) will be subjected to enhanced monitoring, may be denied by automated underwriting engines, and may require an emotional or sexual content policy compliance attestation. Therefore, an EMI licence or a foreign fintech passport is legally permissible only for payment initiation, collection, and remittance flows; it is unsuitable for the statutory treasury core, which must sit in a fully licensed German commercial bank with DGS insurance and European Central Bank supervision.

Root cause: Payment platform aggregation: Apple IAP and Paymentwall use aggregated merchant settlement accounts, high card-not-present transaction velocity, cross-border chargeback risk, and store-of-value concerns under BaFin's guidance on payment token merchants and third-party payouts.

Defense evidence: Defensive bundle: (1) signed direct processing agreement with the respective payment platform and comprehensive merchant service agreement; (2) monthly chargeback ratio report maintained below Visa/Mastercard scheme thresholds (target < 0.40% in transaction counts); (3) chargeback reserve and escrow arrangement equal to 120% of audited average monthly chargeback volume; (4) documented compliance policy that all hosting fees, video credits and premium gifts represent clearly marked non-refundable platform content credits, not stored-value e-money instruments; (5) software escrow and user refund policy to eliminate 'purchase plus claim' typology; (6) evidence of KYC on paying end-users (name, date of birth, IP geolocation, device ID) and payee merchants; and (7) legal confirmation from the payment providers that their licences and obtaining solely from Paymentwall's licensing do not create a German crowdfunding or initial coin offering exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign shareholders or strategic investors must be processed only through a banking channel with full transparency of the final originator and the economic substance of the transacting entity. The valid route is: (a) notarise the equity capital increase or shareholder loan agreement in the German commercial register chain; (b) obtain an executed irrevocable payment order through SWIFT MT103 with the investor's local compliance reference number and the mandate that the beneficiary bank release funds only after receipt of the German corporate register excerpt and a segregated capital account confirmation; (c) use the official ISO 20022 purpose code 'INVC' for capital contribution, 'LOAN' for shareholder loans and 'REM' for ordinary remittances; alternatively use the Deutsche Bundesbank 'Tite' field providing the legal basis 'Kapitalmaßnahme GmbH' or 'Darlehen'; (d) where the aggregate payment or any connected payment exceeds EUR 12,500, the receiving UG must report the transaction to Deutsche Bundesbank as a cross-border payment using the MEMAN portal or through the bank's automated reporting, providing the counterparty country, the legal blue code (Außenwirtschaftsgeschäft), the type of payment and the amount in euro or original currency; (e) the bank will verify against cleared KYC, UBO, sanctions and PEP lists before posting. For SELL funds in US dollars, an additional final exchange-rate confirmation and a funds-received-receipt declaration are required; funds must never be looped via offshore shell accounts and should mirror the executed statutory corporate for-profit business purpose.International trade payouts and service fees from the UG should be executed through: (1) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfers for cross-border supplier payments, app-store payouts, content moderators, AI API usage fees, cloud infrastructure costs and shareholder-remuneration payments outside the EU; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit (SCT Inst) for intra-EU settle balances under 100,000 EUR with immediate finality through TARGET2; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (Masters, Credit or Standby) only where the counterparty insists on documentary security, in which case the UG opens an LC via a confirming German bank, uses SWIFT MT700/MT707 messages, and draws only against compliant documents; and (4) Paymentwall / Apple IAP settlement wire transfers denominated in EUR to the operating account. Each outbound disbursement must contain the invoice number, commercial contract number, and valid purpose reference in the mandatory remittance information fields; the 26-character IBAN, BIC and Legal Entity Identifier for a German bank account; and immediate voluntary Bundesbank reporting for any single transaction exceeding EUR 12,500. For payments to restricted countries, a targeted sanctions screening on the ultimate beneficiary and end use must be completed and archived; non-compliant sanctions matches are an immediate hard blocker under EU Regulation 833/2014 and the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance.EUR/USD deliverable FX outright forwards (Spot Next and Forward Vanilla) executed with a German licensed credit institution under ISDA 2002 MasterEUR/GBP and EUR/SGD deliverable FX forwards for app-store and third-party settlement liquidityOTC cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) to stabilise EUR/USD funding costs when fixed-rate investor loans existEuropean-style FX options (put/call) on EUR/USD and EUR/CNH for hedging contingent royalty and China-based traffic monetisation flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for ring-fenced offshore KRW, INR and CNY-denominated payment curves where local convertibility limits block an onshore deliverable hedge, provided the bank has a legal right of set-off and the hedge is documented under an ISDA Schedule recognising close-out and the UG's non-resident status

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: CONDITIONAL / MEDIUM with significant governance constraints. Non-bank fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Wise Business, and Pliant, and German-licensed EMIs operating under the Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) and the Second E-Money Directive, can provide rapid multi-currency IBAN-holding accounts, virtual cards, and Paymentwall/Apple-IAP payouts. Such institutions are not credit institutions under the German Banking Act (KWG); they may not grant credit, hold client funds indefinitely, or provide the full German Einlagensicherung deposit protection. A major substantive blocker is that German banks now apply 'look-through' KYC requirements to underlying commercial activity; a real-time video match application classified by BaFin typologies as a 'higher potential for illicit content' business (possible adult services or human trafficking associated activity) will be subjected to enhanced monitoring, may be denied by automated underwriting engines, and may require an emotional or sexual content policy compliance attestation. Therefore, an EMI licence or a foreign fintech passport is legally permissible only for payment initiation, collection, and remittance flows; it is unsuitable for the statutory treasury core, which must sit in a fully licensed German commercial bank with DGS insurance and European Central Bank supervision.
