Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-MTA digital portal (https://e-mta.mta.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an OÜ must first register in the Estonian Commercial Registry (Äriregister); the registry code (registrycard) is the statutory TIN for both corporate income tax and VAT. Registration for tax obligations is concluded electronically through e-MTA with simultaneous assignment of the VAT ID (KMKR) if taxable turnover will exceed the threshold or voluntary registration is requested. No separate TIN form is required for TIN purposes beyond the commercial registry entry and e-MTA activation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an OÜ must first register in the Estonian Commercial Registry (Äriregister); the registry code (registrycard) is the statutory TIN for both corporate income tax and VAT. Registration for tax obligations is concluded electronically through e-MTA with simultaneous assignment of the VAT ID (KMKR) if taxable turnover will exceed the threshold or voluntary registration is requested. No separate TIN form is required for TIN purposes beyond the commercial registry entry and e-MTA activation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonian corporate income tax is imposed at the entity level on distributed profits (tax-deferred until distribution); no US-style check-the-box classification is available for an OÜ. Elections include the reduced-rate VAT scheme for small enterprises under VAT Directive Art. 281, voluntary VAT registration, VAT group registration (käibemaksukogum) under the Estonian VAT Act, and the application of hybrid mismatch rule elections under ATAD/EE VAT law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year (mandatory VAT registration threshold); EUR 0 for non-EU-established businesses supplying taxable services to non-taxable persons where deemed located in Estonia

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported directly out of the EU by the supplier, or as qualifying transportation to a destination outside the EU, are zero-rated under Section 14 of the Estonian Value Added Tax Act if the exporter holds documentary proof of removal from the EU within the statutory period (generally 30 days after supply) and is the taxable person submitting the export declaration. Certain services directly linked to exported goods (freight, handling) and certain B2B services to non-EU customers (e.g., intangible services) also qualify for zero rating under Sections 17–18 of the VAT Act provided the customer is a taxable person abroad and the service is effectively used and enjoyed outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / electronic Export Control System)
  • Bill of Lading or AWB / CMR transport document with destination proof outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank wire confirmation evidencing receipt of export payment
  • Signed commercial invoice and delivery note, including international commercial terms
  • Intrastat statistical declaration (when applicable for intra-EU movement prior to export)

No real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance or digital stamping exists in Estonia. E-invoicing is mandatory for B2G transactions (supplier must transmit structured electronic invoices in the national transport system). All VAT returns, Intrastat, and export declarations are filed electronically through e-MTA. Peppol-compliant and ISO 20022 XML formats are accepted for e-invoicing, but there is no mandatory API tag-and-file clearance. The Tax and Customs Board uses risk-based post-clearance automated cross-checking of invoices and customs export data.

Exports claims are subject to ex-post audit using customs export data and bank payment records. Where zero-rating evidence is incomplete, the VAT is re-assessed with interest at the statutory rate (0.06% per day, currently approx. 21.9% annualized) and a penalty surcharge of up to 20% of the assessed amount under the Tax Information Act. Knowingly false statements constitute a tax offence punishable by corrective assessment, loss of future zero-rating entitlement, and criminal liability under Penal Code §§ 345–1, 397–1 including monetary fines and imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. Estonia adopted the euro on 1 January 2011 and is bound by TFEU Article 63 free-movement-of-capital rules, with no foreign-exchange permits, prior authorizations, quotas, or approval requirements for non-resident investment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds. The only profit-remittance hurdle is fiscal: the Estonian corporate income tax system taxes distributions (20% regular rate on grossed-up dividends; reduced 14% for regular, consistently distributed profits) rather than undistributed earnings; qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% benefit from a 0% withholding tax under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, while interest and royalty payments to EU/EEA associates are exempt under Directive 2003/49/EC. No convertibility restriction prevents payment in any hard currency, and the ECB reference rate is the practical benchmark for local conversions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonian law imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation on export proceeds. Exporters may hold foreign-currency balances in any bank, convert them at market rates, or keep them indefinitely. The only compliance layers are statistical (Intrastat/Extrastat declarations and Eesti Pank balance-of-payments reporting submitted by the settling bank), tax (transfer pricing documentation for related-party trade), and EU sanctions screening, which operates as the effective control on proceeds originating from restricted jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), a Eurosystem member, is the central monetary authority for the euro area; prudential supervision over credit institutions, payment institutions and trade-finance providers is exercised by Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority); AML/CFT supervision and financial-intelligence functions rest with Rahapesu Andmebüroo (Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit), in coordination with the Ministry of Finance. Estonia maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration; capital and FX policy are EU and Eurosystem competences.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational clearing (invoicing, distributor collections, payroll, FX conversion) but Low for trade-finance rails. EEA-authorized electronic money institutions and payment institutions, whether Estonian-licensed or passporting into Estonia, can issue segregated client-fund accounts with EUR IBANs, execute SEPA, SEPA Instant, and SWIFT transfers, and provide multi-currency payments with interbank FX margins. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700), bank guarantees, acceptance credits, or trade loans, and cannot conduct deposit-taking; such institutions also apply stricter risk appetites to physical-goods manufacturers in the regulated IVD sector, often imposing enhanced manual verification, transfer caps, and a higher account-freeze probability. The architecturally recommended pattern is an EMI for low-value collections and operational disbursements below EUR 25,000, paired with a Tier-1 credit institution as the sole issuer of documentary credits and the receiver of the equity capital injection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the equity injection must be wired via SWIFT MT103 in EUR through SEPA/TARGET2 (or through correspondent banks in USD/GBP) to the Estonian entity's IBAN, with the remittance-information field 70 explicitly stating 'Capital contribution – share subscription' and an accepted EU balance-of-payments purpose code for foreign direct investment (equity capital). The receiving bank auto-submits the statistical BoP declaration to Eesti Pank. Verification pack: board resolution and shareholder decision authorizing the capital increase, updated articles of association, proof of registration in the e-Business Register, source-of-funds documentation from the remitting shareholder, and a capital-utilization plan; the funds may be held in a temporary share-capital collection account before registration, then reclassified to equity reserves. No exchange-control permit is required, and receipt may be maintained in the original currency; for paid-in-capital accounting, conversion to EUR should be journaled at the ECB mid-rate.Recommended clearing rails for the microfluidic cartridge and immunochemistry analyzer supply chain: (i) SWIFT MT103 (telegraphic transfer) for reagent, polymer, and packaging suppliers below EUR 50,000 per shipment, quoting the invoice and BoP product code for goods; (ii) irrevocable documentary letter of credit (SWIFT MT700, subject to UCP600 and ISBP745) issued by the Estonian Tier-1 bank for high-value capital equipment, with the supplier's bank as advising or confirming bank and reimbursement through SWIFT MT202 upon satisfying document-compliance examination; (iii) documentary collections (cash-against-documents, D/P, D/A; MT400/MT422) for tested repeat suppliers after at least two clean transaction cycles; (iv) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EU suppliers at values up to EUR 100,000.
