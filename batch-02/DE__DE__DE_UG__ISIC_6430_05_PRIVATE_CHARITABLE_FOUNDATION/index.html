Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) and the locally competent Municipal Tax Office (Finanzamt); electronic filing portal: ELSTER (eisteronline.de). VAT identification numbers are issued by the BZSt after the local Finanzamt has processed the VAT registration.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated UG must register its activities with the local Finanzamt using the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (ELSTER or paper). The Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT pre-registration. Foreign entities without a domestic seat that have German-source income or German VAT obligations must file the same questionnaire with the Finanzamt competent for their fiscal representative or place of business and must separately apply to the BZSt for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using Form USt 1 TN. Once the local Finanzamt approves VAT registration, the BZSt issues the VAT ID in writing, typically within one to three months.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A newly incorporated UG must register its activities with the local Finanzamt using the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (ELSTER or paper). The Finanzamt assigns the tax number (Steuernummer) for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT pre-registration. Foreign entities without a domestic seat that have German-source income or German VAT obligations must file the same questionnaire with the Finanzamt competent for their fiscal representative or place of business and must separately apply to the BZSt for a German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) using Form USt 1 TN. Once the local Finanzamt approves VAT registration, the BZSt issues the VAT ID in writing, typically within one to three months.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime. A UG is a corporation and is mandatorily taxable at entity level as a Kapitalgesellschaft. It may, however, elect to be recognized as a charitable entity (gemeinnützige Körperschaft) under §§51-68 AO, provided exclusively philanthropic pursuits are carried on, thereby creating exemption from corporation tax, trade tax and VAT for statutory exempt activities. An intra-group unit may also elect an Organschaft (tax and VAT grouping) with its controlling parent under §§14, 17 KStG and §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG. Hybrid mismatch arrangements must be adjusted and disclosed under §4k EStG; no elective pass-through classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no absolute VAT registration threshold for domestic entrepreneurs; any entrepreneur making taxable supplies in Germany is liable to VAT. The small-business relief (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under §19 UStG applies where prior-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and expected current-year turnover does not exceed €50,000; with effect from 1 January 2025 the prior-year limit is €25,000 and the expected current-year limit is €100,000. Non-resident suppliers making supplies taxable in Germany have no threshold and must register or be subject to reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export supplies of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate as tax-free export deliveries (steuerfreie Ausfuhrlieferung) under §§4 Nr. 1, 6 UStG where the supplier is an entrepreneur, the goods are physically dispatched or carried to a destination outside the EU, and the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration accepted by the customs authorities and the goods actually leave the EU. Services supplied to non-EU entrepreneurs are generally not subject to German VAT where the place of supply is in the customer's jurisdiction under §§3a, 3b, 3e and 3f UStG; certain services to non-EU private consumers may also be zero-rated or exempt. A charitable UG that also conducts fund management must separately verify whether its investment-management services fall under the special investment-fund exemption; unrestricted zero-rating requires complete statutory proof of cross-border movement or delivery.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhrzollanmeldung) with MRN and EU exit confirmation (ATLAS/IAS), or paper customs declaration where lawfully permitted
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and sales contract identifying the non-EU customer and agreed Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR waybill or rail consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence showing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency from the non-EU buyer

From 1 January 2025, all German-established B2B suppliers must issue structured electronic invoices (E-Rechnung) for domestic transactions between German resident persons; accepted formats are XRechnung and ZUGFeRD/Factur-X. The obligation is phased: during 2025 and 2026, suppliers with prior-year turnover not exceeding €800,000 may continue to issue paper or PDF invoices, and EDI-based formats remain permissible; from 1 January 2027 all German B2B suppliers must issue e-invoices compliant with EN 16931. Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control for VAT purposes; VAT returns, advance returns, and intra-Community recapitulative statements are transmitted electronically through ELSTER.

The Finanzamt may require the exporter to substantiate the zero-rating during the applicable assessment period. If the proof is missing, incomplete or irregular, the export zero-rating is reversed and output VAT becomes due at 19%, together with late-payment interest under §233a AO and late-payment surcharges under §240 AO. Fraudulent assertion of export zero-rating, falsified customs or transport documents, or misleading declarations constitute criminal tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a fine, with enhanced penalties of up to ten years in especially serious cases; administrative fines may also be imposed under §379 AO. Joint liability of participants follows from §§71, 25 AO. The regular VAT assessment limitation period is four years, but is extended to five years for gross negligence and ten years for intentional tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the AWG/AWV. There is no general approval requirement for profit remittance, endowment distribution, or grant disbursement, subject only to EU and UN sanctions, anti-money-laundering verification, and public-policy restrictions. For a charitable UG, the principal constraint is not currency convertibility but allocation and disposition of funds in accordance with the charitable use-of-funds requirements under the German Fiscal Code to preserve tax-exempt status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. All export receipts and other cross-border payment receipts involving non-residents must be reported to the Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics if the individual transaction amount equals or exceeds €12,500 or the holding of foreign balances exceeds €5,000; however, the proceeds may be retained in any freely convertible foreign currency and no forced conversion into EUR applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank, exercising balance-of-payments statistics and foreign exchange monitoring functions together with BAFA (Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz – AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); BaFin supervises the financial institutions executing the transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Under Section 1(1)(2) and Section 32 of the German Banking Act (KWG), institutions performing payment services or issuing electronic money in Germany must hold a BaFin licence or a valid EU/EEA passport. Non-EEA digital EMIs have low compliance viability as primary bank accounts because they are not deposit-taking banks in the German sense and may lack clear insolvency segregation for foundation endowment balances. EEA passporting EMIs such as Wise or Revolut can be used as supplementary FX or crossing tools, but grant disbursements and capital escrow should be maintained with a licensed German or EU credit institution to satisfy charitable treasury and transparency expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound endowment and donor funds into a German UG must be routed via SWIFT MT103 for foreign-currency wires or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated contributions. The payment reference must state the grant deed number or capital-contribution resolution; for balance-of-payments statistics, a Bundesbank reporting form is required when the value is ≥ €12,500, with the statistical purpose code for capital contribution selected as 'Vermögenseinlage' in the context of shareholder or equivalent endowment capital; donor grants without a capital claim are reported under the German AWV external-service/donation codes. Correspondent banks will require the beneficiary BIC and IBAN in the legal name of the UG; the German receiving bank will verify the charitable exemption, sanctions screening, and source of funds before crediting the capital escrow account.Grant distributions and operating trade settlements should be cleared through SEPA CT for euro payments to EU beneficiaries, via SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR international grant wires, and through commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) when goods or services are purchased from foreign counterparties on documentary terms. Additional clearing rails include deferred-payment documentary collections (MT400) and bank guarantee demand (MT760) for performance-linked grants. Each outward payment must be flagged under the correct Bundesbank statistical purpose code, and the UG’s bank will require the signed grant instruction, underlying grant contract, invoice or acceptance certificate to satisfy its payout defence and AML monitoring.FX Forward contracts with a German or EU-authorized credit institution strictly limited to hedging known grant commitments, with maturity aligned to the underlying programme disbursement date and no speculative rollover leverage.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) contracts for restricted-currency markets (e.g. CNY, INR, BRL) where the foundation has a firm foreign grant obligation and the hedge is documented as treasury risk management in the internal investment policy.Cross-currency structure swaps or simple FX swaps used solely to manage liquidity between foreign endowment currency and EUR for matching grant-program cash flows, preserving principal and documented as asset-preservation and liquidity-management instruments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Koerperschaftsteuer plus 0.825% Solidaritaetszuschlag (aggregate 15.825%); German trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is separately imposed at municipal rates from 7% to 17%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritaetszuschlag = 26.375% aggregate withholding on dividends. A 0% withholding rate applies under §43b EStG to qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted 12-month period. DTA reduction commonly ranges from 15% to 0% depending on the treaty and shareholder type.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory euro threshold exists for the German Local File; documentation is required under §90(3) AO for all material cross-border controlled transactions. In administrative practice, reduced documentation may be accepted where the annual aggregate related-party transaction volume is below EUR 500,000. The German Master File is generally expected for multinational groups with German turnover above EUR 100 million, and country-by-country reporting is mandatory where the group has consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not have a separately labelled Economic Substance Regulation. Nonetheless, the general anti-abuse rule under §42 AO and the economic-substance principle (wirtschaftliche Betrachtungsweise) require a German UG to maintain genuine local presence, management decisions, bookkeeping, grant-making operations, and purpose-bound endowment management. A letter-box UG or a purely artificial interposition will be disregarded, the charitable exemption revoked, and taxable income re-attributed to the economic owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false tax return (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years or a fine under §370 AO; in especially severe cases (large tax detriment, commercial or band misuse) imprisonment from 6 months to 10 years; all underpaid taxes remain due together with 1.80% per annum interest and late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face personal criminal conviction, personal liability under §§34 and 69 AO for unpaid taxes, and civil liability under §43 GmbHG. Personal assets may be seized; in cross-border cases, travel bans and provisional arrest may be ordered as criminal procedural measures.
Misuse of charitable status (Gemeinnuetzigkeitsmissbrauch)

Statutory sanction: Retroactive revocation of charitable status under §§51–68 AO; current and prior tax exemptions lapse, leading to assessment at 15.825% corporate tax plus trade tax; distributions may attract 26.375% withholding tax; §370 AO penalties apply where false declarations accompanied the abuse.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for corporate taxes and social costs under §§34, 69 AO and §43 GmbHG; personal restitution to the tax authority and to donors may be ordered; fraud investigations may trigger travel bans and asset freezes.
AML / anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing compliance (GwG/StGB)

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under §89c StGB (terrorist financing) with imprisonment up to 10 years; regulatory fines under the GwG up to EUR 5,000,000, or 10% of total annual turnover in aggravated cases; asset freezes, closure of bank accounts, and revocation of the entity's charitable status.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be personally prosecuted and imprisoned; personal asset confiscation and civil restitution orders are available; the FIU/BaFin, the public prosecutor, and financial institutions may impose travel bans and freezing orders under German and EU sanctions law.
Shell company / artificial arrangement lacking economic substance (Missbrauch von Gestaltungsmoeglichkeiten)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the arrangement under §42 AO, re-attribution of income to the economic owner, denial of treaty benefits, retroactive loss of charitable exemption, additional taxes and interest, and criminal penalties under §370 AO if false returns accompany the arrangement.

Director personal liability: German courts will only exceptionally pierce the veil, but §§34 and 69 AO impose personal tax liability on managing directors for deliberate or grossly negligent breach of tax duties; directors may face civil damages, criminal imprisonment, and court-ordered travel bans during investigation.
Transfer pricing documentation failure / cross-border profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax re-assessment at the assumed arm's-length price and mandatory surcharges under §162(3) AO of 5%–10% of the positive tax adjustment, with a minimum surcharge of EUR 5,000; additional interest under §233a AO and, for deliberate conduct, prosecution under §370 AO.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are personally responsible for maintaining contemporaneous documentation and ensuring accurate reporting; personal tax liability under §§34 and 69 AO may attach for resulting underpayments, and intentional documentation failures may expose directors to criminal conviction, fines, and imprisonment. Travel bans can be imposed in cross-border criminal tax investigations.
