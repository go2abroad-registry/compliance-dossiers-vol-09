Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet; 'MTA'), Hallikma 4, 10617 Tallinn; unified digital portal e-MTA (e-maksuamet / e-toillamet) at www.emta.ee; e-Residency onboarding portal at www.e-resident.gov.ee. All VAT returns (KMD), VAT information declarations (KMD-INF), withholding/employment declarations (TSD/VTD), and customs filings are submitted electronically via e-MTA authenticated by mobile-ID, Smart-ID, ID-card, or the e-Residency digital ID.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident founder establishing an Osaühing (OÜ): (1) Obtain an e-Residency digital ID card from an Estonian embassy/consulate abroad, enabling secure e-signing of registry filings; (2) File the incorporation notice in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister / e-Business Register) — minimum share capital is not required (MEMO contribution permitted) and 24-hour online incorporation is standard; (3) Upon registration, the MTA automatically assigns the OÜ a national tax identification number (the 8-digit registry code, with 'EE' prefixed for VAT purposes); no separate TIN application is needed for a resident OÜ; (4) A foreign legal entity without an Estonian seat must apply directly to the MTA via e-MTA (VAT registration application R-KM) to obtain a non-resident VAT ID before making taxable supplies in Estonia; the MTA may require appointment of a local tax representative under § 43 of the Estonian VAT Act; (5) The OÜ should simultaneously activate the e-MTA secure mailbox, elect the VAT filing period (monthly/quarterly by €40,000 threshold rules), and, if relevant, register for the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal in the e-MTA for intra-EU distance sales of resin statues; (6) All MTA decisions are electronically served and deemed received on the seventh day after sending (Taxation Act § 49).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian distributive corporate income tax model): the OÜ is a resident legal entity subject to tax only on profit distributions and non-business disbursements; undistributed annual profits are taxed at 0%; the headline rate on distributed profits is 20/80 (i.e., 20% of gross dividend, equivalent to 25% of the net distribution); a reduced rate of 14/86 applies to recurring 'regular dividends' where cumulative distributions do not exceed the average taxable dividends distributed in the three preceding tax years, with a supplementary 7% final withholding applicable in limited recipient cases. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Estonian distributive corporate income tax model): the OÜ is a resident legal entity subject to tax only on profit distributions and non-business disbursements; undistributed annual profits are taxed at 0%; the headline rate on distributed profits is 20/80 (i.e., 20% of gross dividend, equivalent to 25% of the net distribution); a reduced rate of 14/86 applies to recurring 'regular dividends' where cumulative distributions do not exceed the average taxable dividends distributed in the three preceding tax years, with a supplementary 7% final withholding applicable in limited recipient cases. A non-resident shareholder of an OÜ is not taxed at shareholder level in Estonia except withholding on distributions; the entity is not a pass-through; Territorial_Scope: Estonia taxes a resident OÜ on its worldwide income on a distribution-triggered basis, while foreign business income attributable to a foreign permanent establishment may be exempt from Estonian income tax under § 55 of the Income Tax Act; Tax_Exempt_Zone: none applies to ISIC 3240 production — the entire national territory is subject to standard VAT and income tax rules, with the deferred 0% retained-profit rate constituting the principal statutory tax shield, aligning with the digital-nomad e-Residency business model.

Available election forms

No domestic check-the-box election, no fiscal unity or consolidated group regime exists in Estonia (each OÜ is taxed independently). Available elections: (a) reduced-rate regular dividend election (14/86) under § 50(21)–(22) of the Income Tax Act, subject to the three-year look-back averaging rule; (b) tax treaty/Parent-Subsidiary Directive election to exempt or reduce withholding on qualifying EU/third-country corporate dividend distributions by filing entitlement declarations with the MTA; (c) ATAD hybrid mismatch corrective elections (transposed with effect from 1 January 2020) — where a hybrid entity or instrument (e.g., a US check-the-box LLC treated fiscally transparent abroad but opaque in Estonia) produces a deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcome, the MTA counteracts the mismatch at the payer/recipient level and no elective opt-out is available; (d) VAT small-trader exemption election for domestic supplies below €40,000 annual turnover, unavailable once cross-border exports commence; (e) VAT cash-accounting (cash basis) election for taxpayers with an annual turnover not exceeding €200,000, deferring output VAT liability until payment is received from collectors.

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover in Estonia for resident taxable persons (KMS § 3(4)); a non-resident foreign entity has no threshold — any taxable supply made in Estonia, including B2C distance sales of goods physically located in Estonia, triggers compulsory VAT registration. Supplemented by the EU-wide €10,000 threshold for intra-EU B2C distance sales, above which the OÜ must account for destination-country VAT through the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) or register locally; the €40,000 domestic threshold is evaluated on the preceding calendar year's turnover, and voluntary registration is permitted below it.

No real-time fiscal clearance system, no continuous transaction control (CTC), and no digital stamping mandate analogous to Italy or Hungary is in force. Mandatory e-invoicing applies in the B2G segment: invoices to Estonian public-sector bodies must be transmitted via the PEPPOL network in a structured format (EU e-invoice standard, EN 16931). For B2B/B2C supplies of resin statues, the MTA does not require pre-clearance of invoices; however, the OÜ must retain all VAT-credible invoices electronically for 7 years and file the KMD return and KMD-INF (recapitulative statement) electronically via the e-MTA API, which is integrated with the X-Road data-exchange layer. Cash registers and online payment rails (Stripe/PayPal) must support audit-file extraction, and issuance of sequential numbered invoices is mandatory; INTRASTAT-supplementary declarations apply once intra-EU dispatches of collectible statues exceed the annually adjusted dispatch threshold (approximately €130,000), and import VAT is cleared through the electronic ECS/EMCS customs environment. No excise tax or fiscal mark applies to polyresin figurines (ISIC 3240).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Republic of Estonia maintains full de jure and de facto capital and current account convertibility under the Estonian Foreign Exchange Act (Välisvaluutaseadus), Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and Estonia's OECD obligations. The euro is the sole legal tender and is freely convertible for all cross-border payments, trade settlements, profit remittances, and capital transactions. No prior exchange-control approval, import licence, or central bank consent is required for inbound capital injections, dividend distributions, or outbound trade disbursements. Profit remittance is structurally effected through a Board-approved dividend distribution under the Estonian corporate income tax deferral model (income tax at 0% on undistributed profits; 20/80 corporate income tax on distributed amounts, or 14/86 at reduced rate for regular distributions under Income Tax Act §50); no additional withholding tax applies to non-resident shareholders receiving dividends from an Estonian OÜ provided the recipient is a resident of a treaty jurisdiction, an EU/EEA resident, or otherwise covered by the Interest and Royalties Directive analogies and domestic relief provisions. The only administrative frictions are statistical balance-of-payments (BoP) reporting thresholds administered by Eesti Pank, and the AML/KYC documentary burden imposed by Estonian credit institutions on non-resident controlled entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement in Estonia. Exporters are permitted to retain export receivables in any foreign currency, hold them in non-resident or domestic foreign-currency accounts, and convert to EUR at their discretion through licensed credit institutions or authorised payment service providers. Obligations are limited to (i) compliance with EU restrictive measures and financial sanctions screening under the Estonian International Sanctions Act, (ii) BoP statistical reporting under Regulation (EU) No 184/2014, whereby resident legal persons must report cross-border transactions and positions to Eesti Pank upon request or on a threshold-triggered basis (reporting threshold and instrument codes as published by Eesti Pank), and (iii) factually accurate VAT invoicing and Intrastat declarations, where applicable, for the export of physical resin statues. In practice, an OÜ distributing hand-painted limited-edition statues to retail customers via Stripe/PayPal and to wholesale distributors via commercial invoices may hold USD, GBP, and EUR balances simultaneously without any surrender obligation.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), a member of the Eurosystem since 1 January 2011, operating within the framework of the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; prudential supervision of credit institutions is delegated to Finantsinspektsioon (the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for auxiliary payment collection and payout channels; LOW as the sole treasury banking infrastructure. Estonia is the founding jurisdiction of the e-Residency programme, and licensed Estonian e-money institutions, credit institutions, and EEA-passported EMIs (notably Monese, Wise, PayPal Business, and Ponto) can provide functional multi-currency IBAN accounts, payment cards, and API-driven reconciliation rails. Legal constraints: (i) EMI client funds are safeguarded under Directive (EU) 2009/110/EC and are not covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund deposit insurance scheme, exposing the treasury to principal loss risk on EMI insolvency; (ii) EMI accounts are frequently domiciled in Lithuanian, Dutch, or British entities with limited KYC alignment with the Estonian Commercial Register beneficial-ownership file, creating an audit trail discontinuity; (iii) Finantsinspektsioon does not exercise prudential supervision over passported EMIs, which may complicate an Estonian tax and banking audit; and (iv) certain EMIs apply restrictive acceptable-use policies that prohibit or flag high-volume pre-order / crowdfunding-like deposit flows characteristic of limited-edition statue pre-sales. Recommended architecture: maintain the statutory operating account and liquidity reserve at a Tier 1 Estonian bank, with a single passported EMI (e.g., Wise Business) used solely for foreign-currency receipts and vendor payouts in USD/GBP/CNY, subject to daily balance caps and automatic sweeping to the Tier 1 bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for auxiliary payment collection and payout channels; LOW as the sole treasury banking infrastructure. Estonia is the founding jurisdiction of the e-Residency programme, and licensed Estonian e-money institutions, credit institutions, and EEA-passported EMIs (notably Monese, Wise, PayPal Business, and Ponto) can provide functional multi-currency IBAN accounts, payment cards, and API-driven reconciliation rails. Legal constraints: (i) EMI client funds are safeguarded under Directive (EU) 2009/110/EC and are not covered by the Estonian Guarantee Fund deposit insurance scheme, exposing the treasury to principal loss risk on EMI insolvency; (ii) EMI accounts are frequently domiciled in Lithuanian, Dutch, or British entities with limited KYC alignment with the Estonian Commercial Register beneficial-ownership file, creating an audit trail discontinuity; (iii) Finantsinspektsioon does not exercise prudential supervision over passported EMIs, which may complicate an Estonian tax and banking audit; and (iv) certain EMIs apply restrictive acceptable-use policies that prohibit or flag high-volume pre-order / crowdfunding-like deposit flows characteristic of limited-edition statue pre-sales. Recommended architecture: maintain the statutory operating account and liquidity reserve at a Tier 1 Estonian bank, with a single passported EMI (e.g., Wise Business) used solely for foreign-currency receipts and vendor payouts in USD/GBP/CNY, subject to daily balance caps and automatic sweeping to the Tier 1 bank.
