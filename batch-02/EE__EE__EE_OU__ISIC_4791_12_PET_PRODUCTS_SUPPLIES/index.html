Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet / EMTA); e-MTA portal at https://emta.ee and https://e-mta.emta.ee

E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned Osaühing (OÜ) obtains tax identity at incorporation through the Estonian e-Business Register (e-äriregister). Steps: (1) file the incorporation notice and constitutional documents online using e-Residency or another recognized secure digital ID; (2) receive the commercial registry code, which serves as the legal person TIN; (3) access the e-MTA tax account with the same digital identity; (4) apply for VAT registration via e-MTA once taxable supplies begin in Estonia, when the EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover threshold will be exceeded, or immediately for distance-selling arrangements where OSS does not apply; (5) receive an Estonian VAT number of the format 'EE' followed by the nine-digit registry code. The same e-MTA account is used for VAT returns, customs operations, employer withholding registration, and statutory reporting.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident-owned Osaühing (OÜ) obtains tax identity at incorporation through the Estonian e-Business Register (e-äriregister). Steps: (1) file the incorporation notice and constitutional documents online using e-Residency or another recognized secure digital ID; (2) receive the commercial registry code, which serves as the legal person TIN; (3) access the e-MTA tax account with the same digital identity; (4) apply for VAT registration via e-MTA once taxable supplies begin in Estonia, when the EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover threshold will be exceeded, or immediately for distance-selling arrangements where OSS does not apply; (5) receive an Estonian VAT number of the format 'EE' followed by the nine-digit registry code. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not maintain a U.S.-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and determinations include: voluntary VAT registration before the compulsory threshold; group VAT registration for affiliated taxable persons; the regular distribution regime (treating non-business expenses and fringe benefits as deemed taxable distributions at the statutory rate); and advance pricing agreement applications with EMTA for transfer-pricing certainty. Hybrid mismatch rules are mandatory under the transposed ATAD framework; no elective entity reclassification or hybrid mismatch election is available for Estonian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover per calendar year for domestic taxable supplies; cross-border EU distance sales are covered by the OSS EUR 10,000 pan-EU threshold, and a non-established supplier must obtain an Estonian VAT number when the place of supply of goods or services is Estonia and no OSS, reverse charge, or import scheme applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% rate applies to physical export of pet supplies and pet care devices by the OÜ from Estonia to destinations outside the EU when the goods are consigned or transported out of Estonian/EU customs territory by the seller or on the seller's behalf and the seller holds the customs export declaration (SAD/EX). Services supplied to non-resident businesses, including offshore logistics, platform intermediation, and related services, may be zero-rated or outside the scope of Estonian VAT where the place of supply is outside the EU under the B2B place-of-supply rules. The supplier must retain evidence of export, transport, customer identity, and, for services, proof of the customer's non-resident business status and effective use outside Estonia. Export proceeds evidence is expected to support zero-rating documentation, with EMTA regularly reviewing payment receipt and repatriation evidence within the 180-day benchmark.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX) with export movement reference number (MRN) and exit confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract, order summary, or platform transaction record
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR or equivalent transport document showing physical removal from Estonia and the EU
  • Payment or platform settlement evidence (e.g., bank transfer detail, Stripe or PayPal payout report)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Where applicable, customs warehousing/transit documents or out-of-scope service evidence

Estonia does not impose real-time VAT clearance, digital stamping, or live fiscal data reporting. Electronic invoices are accepted, and public-sector recipients must be able to receive e-invoices; private-sector B2B e-invoicing is not mandatory. VAT returns and annexes are filed periodically through the e-MTA portal, and EMTA publishes APIs and machine-readable e-services for larger filers. The periodic VAT return and Intrastat declarations serve as the principal filing and clearance mechanism.

EMTA scrutinizes export zero-rating by cross-checking customs exit records, transport documentation, customer identity, platform settlement data, and inward remittance. Failure to substantiate an export within the documentary review window causes retrospective recharacterization of the supply as domestic taxable VAT, clawback of deducted input VAT, and late-payment interest on arrears at 0.06% per day. Administrative fines apply for material VAT violations, and intentionally fraudulent zero-rating claims are prosecuted as criminal tax fraud, exposing the taxpayer to supplementary tax assessments of up to 100% of the evaded amount and imprisonment in serious cases. The standard assessment period is three years, extended to longer periods where fraud is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and applies the EU framework for free movement of capital and payments. There are no general exchange controls or routine approvals for converting or transferring funds, including cross-border capital contributions, trade payments, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. Transactions remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, tax rules, bank screening, and any applicable EU or national restrictions. An OÜ is generally taxed when profits are distributed rather than when earned; distributions require compliance with company-law distribution and solvency rules and applicable corporate tax filing and payment obligations. Banks may request evidence of source of funds, transaction purpose, ownership, and underlying contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline or mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio applies to an Estonian OÜ. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency or euro accounts, subject to contractual obligations, sanctions, tax, accounting, and financial-institution controls. Banks may request invoices, customs or shipping records, and other evidence supporting the payment.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (the Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) supervises regulated financial institutions; the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) is the financial intelligence authority.
