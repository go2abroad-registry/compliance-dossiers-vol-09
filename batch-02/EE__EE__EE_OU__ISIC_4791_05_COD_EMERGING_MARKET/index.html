Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An OÜ has no fixed statutory minimum capital amount; the nominal value of one share and the total share capital may be at least EUR 0.01. Contributions must generally be made before filing, although the articles may permit deferred payment where legally available. Deferred contributions create founder liability until fully paid. A separate escrow account is not generally mandatory; a bank payment account or payment-service account may be used for capital payment evidence.

Funding & unlock path: Premises must comply with the Rescue Act, applicable fire-safety requirements, building and use-permit rules, and occupational health and safety legislation. Depending on the facility, approvals may include fire-safety documentation or inspection by the Estonian Rescue Board, building-use or occupancy approval from the local municipality, structural and storage-rack load verification, emergency-exit and fire-equipment inspections, and workplace risk assessment and safety instructions under the Labour Inspectorate framework.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); primary electronic portal: e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee). Company establishment and registry identity are administered through the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister).

E-government filing portal

An OÜ obtains its Estonian Commercial Register code upon incorporation or registration in the Commercial Register. The registry code is generally used as the corporate tax identification reference. VAT registration is separately applied for with the MTA through e-MTA or by authorized representative, supported by constitutional documents, business-activity description, expected taxable turnover, contracts, invoices, banking information, and evidence of genuine economic activity. A foreign company without an Estonian subsidiary may apply for Estonian VAT registration where statutory registration or voluntary-registration conditions are met. The MTA may request additional substance and transaction evidence. The Estonian VAT number is issued in the format EE plus nine digits.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An OÜ obtains its Estonian Commercial Register code upon incorporation or registration in the Commercial Register. The registry code is generally used as the corporate tax identification reference. VAT registration is separately applied for with the MTA through e-MTA or by authorized representative, supported by constitutional documents, business-activity description, expected taxable turnover, contracts, invoices, banking information, and evidence of genuine economic activity. A foreign company without an Estonian subsidiary may apply for Estonian VAT registration where statutory registration or voluntary-registration conditions are met. The MTA may request additional substance and transaction evidence. The Estonian VAT number is issued in the format EE plus nine digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ. An Estonian-resident OÜ is a separate taxable legal person. Corporate income tax is generally imposed when profits are distributed or treated as distributed rather than when profits are earned; retained profits are not generally subject to ordinary corporate income tax. Relevant deemed-distribution rules apply to certain non-business expenses, hidden profit distributions, fringe benefits, gifts, donations, and transfer-pricing adjustments. Estonia implements EU anti-hybrid-mismatch rules, but these are statutory rules rather than elective entity-classification elections. Tax residence may also be affected by place of effective management and applicable tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover arising in Estonia during a calendar year, subject to statutory aggregation, exclusions, and special rules. Voluntary registration is generally available before the threshold is reached where the applicant demonstrates a genuine business activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the goods leave the EU and the supplier retains reliable evidence of export and the transaction satisfies Estonian VAT Act requirements. Intra-Community supplies may qualify for 0% VAT where the customer is VAT-identified in another EU Member State, the goods are dispatched or transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains transport and customer-VAT evidence. Services supplied to non-Estonian business customers are generally subject to customer-location and place-of-supply rules and may be outside Estonian VAT or qualify for 0% treatment depending on the service category; special rules apply to services connected with immovable property, passenger transport, events, short-term vehicle hire, telecommunications, broadcasting, electronically supplied services, and consumer transactions. Cash-on-delivery settlement does not itself establish export eligibility or zero-rating.

  • Customs export declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN), where required
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic evidence that the goods left the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and transport documentation appropriate to the shipment, such as a bill of lading, airway bill, or consignment note
  • Evidence identifying the supplier, customer, goods, destination, and transaction value
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative reporting
  • For offshore or cross-border services: contract, customer-location evidence, service description, invoices, and evidence supporting the applicable place-of-supply rule
  • Bank or payment records where relevant to proving the commercial transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not a general Estonian statutory VAT requirement

Estonia does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice-clearance or mandatory digital-stamping system for all private-sector transactions. VAT invoices must satisfy statutory content, issuance, retention, and audit-trail requirements and may be issued electronically with required authenticity and integrity controls. Structured e-invoicing is used for public-sector procurement and may be required under applicable public-procurement arrangements. VAT returns and related reporting are filed electronically through e-MTA. No universal API pre-clearance of each invoice is generally required.

Estonia has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general 180-day repatriation requirement. The MTA may deny or reverse zero-rating where export, transport, customer status, place-of-supply, or transaction-substance evidence is inadequate. Incorrectly zero-rated supplies may be reassessed at the applicable VAT rate, with statutory late-payment interest and administrative penalties. Fraudulent or deliberately misleading documentation may result in additional tax assessments, penalties, loss of input-VAT entitlement, recovery of refunds, and criminal liability where the conduct meets the elements of a tax offense. Records should be retained for the statutory accounting and tax-retention period and be capable of linking each COD order, logistics record, customs event, customer, payment, and VAT treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia maintains full convertibility of the euro on both capital and current accounts without exchange controls, surrender quotas, or repatriation licensing. As an EU and euro area member (euro adopted 1 January 2011), Estonia applies Article 63 TFEU free movement of capital. Profit remittance by dividend distribution requires no central bank approval; the sole fiscal lever is the Estonian distributed-profit corporate income tax (20/80 gross-up, reduced 14/86 for regular distributions), with no withholding tax at the boundary for non-resident corporate shareholders. Practical constraints: (i) EU/UN sanctions screening and asset-freeze implementation (e.g., Russia/Belarus-related payments), (ii) correspondent-bank enhanced due diligence on emerging-market COD corridors, and (iii) mandatory balance-of-payments statistics reporting to Eesti Pank once aggregate non-resident settlements exceed EUR 12,500 on a monthly basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The legacy surrender-and-conversion regime was abolished with the 1992 Currency Act and no provision was reintroduced under the euro regime. Export proceeds, including cash-on-delivery collections remitted from logistics partners, may be retained, transferred, or converted freely in any convertible currency. Statutory obligations are limited to (i) faithful accounting under the Estonian Commercial Code, (ii) BoP statistical filing with Eesti Pank when the monthly threshold is crossed, and (iii) AML identification of the ultimate beneficiary of the receivable.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — statutory central bank of the Republic of Estonia and a Eurosystem member; monetary policy, foreign-exchange intervention competence and euro-area liquidity oversight rest with the European Central Bank (ECB). Prudential AML/CFT supervision of banks and payment institutions is exercised by the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) under Estonian law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability as a supplementary settlement and FX wallet; Medium viability as the sole treasury repository. EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs), including Airwallex (EEA e-money authorisation with passporting rights) and comparable providers (Wise, Revolut Business, Nexpay), may lawfully onboard an Estonian OÜ remotely under PSD2 with enhanced KYC; their relevance is decisive for the ISIC 4791 model because they supply sub-account multi-currency wallets, local emerging-market payout rails (NGN, KES, GHS, INR) and mid-market FX. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits or extend credit, client funds require safeguarding at tier-1 custodian banks, and EMIs may unilaterally down-tier or close accounts that exhibit sustained elevated-risk emerging-market corridors. Recommended architecture: tier-1 Estonian bank as anchor + Airwallex as transactional rail, never EMI-only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

EU-domiciled shareholder: SEPA Credit Transfer (T+1) with remittance text 'Equity injection / Share capital increase EE__EE__EE_OU__ISIC_4791_05_COD_EMERGING_MARKET' and reference to the registered capital-increase resolution. Non-EU shareholder: SWIFT MT103 via gpi, purpose code in field 23E set to EQUI (Equity), field 70 containing 'CAPITAL INJECTION — EE OU' and full legal entity identifiers; instruct FULLOF in field 72 to force a confirming message. Verification bundle: notarized shareholder resolution, updated Business Registry extract, issued share register (Aktsiaraamat) and executed share-subscription contract; the injection must be tagged in the monthly BoP statistical report to Eesti Pank under 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital'. No exchange-control approval or customs declaration is needed; standard sanctions screening applies.Three-tier disbursement architecture: (1) EU/EEA suppliers and refunds — SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant via bank API with narrative 'Trade payment invoice [number]'; (2) non-EU high-value inventory and logistics settlements — SWIFT MT103 via SWIFT gpi (trackable by UETR) and, for reliable order financing, an irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit (MT700 per UCP600); (3) emerging-market cash-on-delivery payouts — Airwallex EMI rail performing EUR-to-local-fiat conversions and local clearing through NIBSS Instant Payment (NGN), PesaLink (KES), GhIPSS (GHS) and IMPS/NEFT (INR), each payout carrying the logistics settlement ID as remittance reference. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability as a supplementary settlement and FX wallet; Medium viability as the sole treasury repository. EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs), including Airwallex (EEA e-money authorisation with passporting rights) and comparable providers (Wise, Revolut Business, Nexpay), may lawfully onboard an Estonian OÜ remotely under PSD2 with enhanced KYC; their relevance is decisive for the ISIC 4791 model because they supply sub-account multi-currency wallets, local emerging-market payout rails (NGN, KES, GHS, INR) and mid-market FX. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits or extend credit, client funds require safeguarding at tier-1 custodian banks, and EMIs may unilaterally down-tier or close accounts that exhibit sustained elevated-risk emerging-market corridors. Recommended architecture: tier-1 Estonian bank as anchor + Airwallex as transactional rail, never EMI-only.
