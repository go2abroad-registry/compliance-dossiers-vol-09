Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For a UG, the statutory minimum paid-up share capital is EUR 1.00 (one euro) as stated in § 5a GmbHG. Cash contributions must be fully paid and be at the unrestricted disposal of the managing directors; no escrow account is required solely for cash contributions. If a contribution in kind is made, the value must be justified and documented in a notarial report. The UG must retain at least 25% of annual net profits as a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, at which point it may be converted into a regular GmbH.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance (Brandschutzabnahme) is required for occupied office premises; a structural stability inspection (Standsicherheitsnachweis) is mandated for any load-bearing alterations; occupational safety measures must meet the Arbeitsschutzgesetz, ArbStättV, and DGUV regulations. The Gewerbeaufsichtsamt provides occupational safety clearance before full-scale operation of the offices.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

German tax administration is decentralized. The competent direct and indirect tax collecting authority is the local Finanzamt (municipal tax office) responsible for the UG's registered statutory seat, operating under the fiscal jurisdiction of the respective Land (Bundesland); federal supervision is exercised by the Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF). The Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, www.bzst.de) in Saarlouis is the federal authority competent for issuing VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr), registering foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic seat, issuing tax clearance certificates (Freistellungsbescheinigungen), and operating the central risk/EDP audit systems. Electronic filing infrastructure and certificates: ELSTER portal (www.elster.de) used for all corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT prepayment, and annual tax returns.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: After notarial incorporation, the UG is entered in the commercial register (Handelsregister Abteilung B) at the local Amtsgericht; the court extract (Handelsregisterauszug) and articles of association form the legal identity bundle. Step 2: The managing director (Geschäftsführer) must file the official electronic questionnaire on fiscal capture ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' pursuant to § 137–138 AO) with the Finanzamt in whose district the UG has its seat within one month of business start. Step 3: The Finanzamt assigns the statutory Steuernummer (13-digit national tax number) used for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT returns; the BZSt simultaneously assigns the Wirtschafts-ID-Nummer for electronic VAT notification filings. Step 4: For cross-border activities, the UG must apply to the BZSt for a VAT identification number in format DE + 9 digits (USt-IdNr) using the specific online VAT ID application form; issuance typically takes two to four weeks. Step 5: Foreign entrepreneurs without a German seat, permanent establishment, or fixed establishment register centrally with the BZSt rather than a local Finanzamt and receive a USt-IdNr for their German VAT compliance obligations. Step 6: Tax returns and prepayments are submitted electronically via ELSTER; VAT prepayment returns are due on the 10th day after the end of each calendar month or quarter, subject to a quarterly filing threshold and optional permanent extension (Dauerfristverlängerung).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)) is a capital company organized as a GmbH variant with no minimum statutory capital (minimum EUR 1, no minimum capital requirement in practice). It is a fully separate legal taxpayer with unlimited German tax residence and worldwide income taxation (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht), with credit for foreign taxes on foreign-source income. It is always taxed at entity level and cannot elect pass-through treatment except by converting to a commercial partnership (Umwandlung) in a separate restructuring proceeding. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)) is a capital company organized as a GmbH variant with no minimum statutory capital (minimum EUR 1, no minimum capital requirement in practice). It is a fully separate legal taxpayer with unlimited German tax residence and worldwide income taxation (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht), with credit for foreign taxes on foreign-source income. The UG is subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) at 15%, solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of the assessed corporate income tax, and trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (effective trade tax burden approximately 7% to 17% depending on municipality). It is always taxed at entity level and cannot elect pass-through treatment except by converting to a commercial partnership (Umwandlung) in a separate restructuring proceeding. The UG's shareholders are taxed only on distributions subject to 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge, or on hidden profit distributions (verdeckte Gewinnausschüttung).

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections and mechanisms for the UG: (1) Corporate-group fiscal unity (Organschaft): a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Gewinnabführungsvertrag) may be concluded with a parent corporation to consolidate corporate income tax and trade tax, requiring majority voting rights and certain term conditions; VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) may arise without formal agreement where financial, economic, and organizational integration exists. (2) VAT option to waive exemptions under § 9 UStG for letting real property and certain supplies. (3) Small-entrepreneur election (Kleinunternehmerregelung under § 19 UStG) available if prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 25,000 and current-year projected turnover ≤ EUR 100,000, waiving output VAT and input VAT deduction. (4) VAT prepayment permanent extension (Dauerfristverlängerung) with payment of a special advance. (5) Hybrid mismatch election: under German ATAD 1/2 implementation, deduction/non-inclusion mismatches are disallowed (§ 4k EStG); the UG may apply a binding advance tax ruling (verbindliche Auskunft, § 89 AO) to clarify hybrid characterization of an instrument, entity, or permanent establishment, and may elect to align German treatment with the OECD Model Rules where conflicts arise, subject to strict documentation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted capital account and current account convertibility under EU and IMF standards. Resident corporations face no capital controls or profit repatriation restrictions. All cross-border remittances are permitted subject solely to standard AML/KYC screening and German tax authority notifications.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists under German law. Exporters may freely retain and utilize foreign currency receipts. Transactions exceeding €12,500 per transaction or €50,000 annually require statistical reporting to the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern). Market exchange rates apply without forced conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the corporate tax assessed, producing an effective federal rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is imposed on top at a rate of 7%-17.15% depending on the municipal levy factor (Hebesatz, typically 200%-490%), giving a combined effective income tax burden of approximately 30%-33% on taxable income of the UG.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% on gross dividends distributed by the UG to its shareholders, composed of 25% capital-yields tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that 25% (§ 43(1)(1), § 43a(1)(1) EStG). Where the shareholder is a qualifying EU/EEA company holding at least 10% of the UG's share capital for an uninterrupted period of 12 months, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as transposed in § 43b EStG reduces the withholding rate to 0%; under double taxation agreements (DTAs) the statutory 26.375% is reduced to the treaty rate, commonly 5%-15%, with the German Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) granting full or partial exemption or refund.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed by 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end for calendar-year UGs. Where preparation is delegated to a tax advisor, the statutory extension allows filing by the end of February of the second calendar year following the fiscal year-end (14 months after the balance-sheet date); additional discretionary extension requires justification under § 109 AO. Trade tax return (GewSt 1) follows the identical deadline mechanism.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline of 31 July of the following year for calendar-year UGs; extension to 28/29 February of the second following year when filed through a tax advisor; trade tax thereafter must be paid within one month of the assessment notice to avoid § 240 AO default surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation applies where the UG's cross-border transactions with related parties exceed EUR 1,000,000 annually for services and intangible property or EUR 5,000,000 annually for goods; a Master File is required when the consolidated worldwide group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously, retained at filing, and produced within 60 days of a specific tax-audit request (§ 90(3) AO), with benchmark studies submitted within 30 days; failure to produce proper documentation triggers a penalty of EUR 5,000 to EUR 1,000,000 or, if higher, 10% of the transfer-pricing adjustment made by the auditor (§ 162(4) AO). German transfer-pricing law applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as incorporated in the Verwaltungsgrundsätze Verrechnungspreise 2023, including the 7.5% German royalty adjustment rule for low-taxed group intangible licensing.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply the UAE/Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations as a discrete statute; equivalent substance controls are enforced through § 42 AO (abuse of legal arrangements), § 50d(3) EStG (denial of DTA relief to treaty-shopping entities), the German transparency register obligations under the GwG, and the OECD BEPS Action 6 Limitation-on-Benefits provisions. A UG acting as a government-affairs advisory conduit must demonstrate a real economic presence in Germany: business management at the German seat, substantive employment contracts for policy analysts, an operating office, independent decision-making authority, client-facing personnel and actual performance of lobbying services in Germany. Where the UG is reduced to an invoicing shell for foreign stakeholder fees, the tax authority recharacterizes the income as directly earned by the foreign principal, denies the treaty exemption, disallows cost deductions, assesses the full 26.375% dividend withholding tax on the deemed distribution, and applies the § 162(4) AO 10% surcharge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung, § 370 AO)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of the managing director (or any authorized representative) for up to 5 years, or fine; aggravated cases under § 370(3) AO (large-scale evasion, abuse of office, or destruction of records) carry imprisonment of 6 months to 10 years. The UG itself faces confiscation of the evaded amount under § 73 StGB (withdrawal of profits), reassessment of the correct tax plus interest at 0.15% per month under § 233a AO, and extension of the statute of limitations to 10 years. Multiple or organized offences can trigger company-level criminal fines under § 30 OWiG of up to EUR 10 million.

Director personal liability: The director is prosecuted in person: criminal record, travel ban and entry-exit restriction imposed by the German courts, EU arrest warrant in transnational cases, and disqualification from serving as a managing director of any German company after conviction (§ 6(2) GmbHG). Civil liability attaches under § 43 GmbHG for breach of fiduciary duty, with restitution of the full tax shortfall; § 69 AO makes the director jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes where bookkeeping, filing or payment obligations were culpably breached, piercing the corporate veil of the UG.
Negligent False Declaration and Late Filing (Leichtfertige Steuerverkürzung, § 378 AO)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to EUR 50,000 per assessment year per person involved (§ 378(2) AO); automatic late-filing surcharge of 0.25% per month capped at EUR 25,000; interest at 0.15% per month on back taxes; the tax office may issue an estimated assessment at maximum rates (§ 162 AO) with an additional 10% surcharge on the upward adjustment, and refuse crediting of prepayments already made.

Director personal liability: The fine is imposed personally on the director; § 69 AO establishes direct personal liability for the tax deficit caused by the negligent failure, enforceable against the director's personal assets, including bank garnishment and property seizures; repeated violations trigger revocation of the director's void-record certificate (Führungszeugnis) and loss of the statutory licence to operate regulated lobbying advisory businesses.
Shell Company, Letterbox Structure and Treaty Shopping (§ 42 AO, § 50d(3) EStG, Transparency Register / GwG)

Statutory sanction: The tax authority recharacterizes the underlying transactions under § 42 AO and reassigns income to the beneficial owner; DTA relief is denied or clawed-back with 10-year statute of limitations; the UG is struck off the commercial register as an inactive shell (§ 60 GmbHG); GwG administrative fines of up to EUR 1,000,000 for natural persons and up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of worldwide annual turnover for the legal entity (§ 56 GwG); and the foreign parent is subjected to a 26.375% deemed-distribution withholding tax with punitive interest.

Director personal liability: Director faces personal fines under the GwG, criminal liability for money laundering (§ 261 StGB) where the shell services conceal proceeds of foreign corruption, civil restitution of all fees and tax shortfalls, a travel ban pending the criminal investigation, and a prohibition on holding corporate directorships for up to 5 years; the corporate veil is pierced and the foreign parent's assets within Germany become garnishable to satisfy the UG's tax and penalty debt.
Unlicensed Lobbying Activity and Lobby Register Violation (Lobbyregistergesetz / LobbyRG, criminal corruption §§ 334, 108e StGB)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to EUR 50,000 per violation under § 6 LobbyRG; unregistered entities are excluded from parliamentary and ministry consultation hearings and barred from federal public procurement contracts; contingency-fee lobbying contracts are void as contrary to public policy; bribery or influence-peddling constitutes a criminal offence under § 334 StGB (bribery of public officials) and § 108e StGB (deputy bribery), punishable by imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years and mandatory confiscation of the proceeds of the lobbying contract.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally criminally liable for the corruption offence with immediate arrest and travel-ban risk, professional disqualification from managing a licensed advisory firm, and civil liability under § 823(2) BGB for damages to the UG's clients and the deceived government authority; § 43 GmbHG grounds a personal restitution claim by the UG for all unlawful fees received by the director; the UG's registered lobbying licences are revoked and the corporate veil is pierced in restitution proceedings, with the director's personal assets available to satisfy the monetary recoveries.
