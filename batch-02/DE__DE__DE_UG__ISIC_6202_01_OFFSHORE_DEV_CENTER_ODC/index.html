Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Handelsregister Abteilung B (Commercial Register Section B) administered by the local Amtsgericht (Local Court) in whose district the German registered office is located.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG) as amended, Sections 1, 2, 4a, 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt), 7, 8, 10, 41, 78; Commercial Code (HGB) Sections 8, 11 and 106 as applicable.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized articles of association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) and notarized application for commercial register registration (Anmeldung zur Handelsregistereintragung), including the shareholder list and managing director list.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG requires a minimum share capital of EUR 1.00 (one euro). The cash contribution must be paid up before filing and be freely disposable by the managing director; no statutory escrow account is required. The UG is subject to a mandatory statutory reserve of 25% of annual net profit until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace safety clearance under the German Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung); fire-safety certification of the office building provided by the building owner; registration of hazardous-substance uses is not required for a low-risk ICT workplace; occupancy must comply with the structural load and escape-route provisions of the building permit; no civil-defense clearance is required.

Paid-in capital rule: The UG requires a minimum share capital of EUR 1.00 (one euro). The cash contribution must be paid up before filing and be freely disposable by the managing director; no statutory escrow account is required. The UG is subject to a mandatory statutory reserve of 25% of annual net profit until the subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for VAT identification numbers and central tax matters; local municipal Tax Office (Finanzamt) at the registered seat for corporate income tax and trade tax administration. Electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

1) Notarize the UG articles of association and register with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). 2) Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER within one month of incorporation, declaring corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT (Umsatzsteuer). 3) Receive a local income-tax number (Steuernummer) from the competent Finanzamt. 4) Apply separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) via ELSTER, required for intra-Community supplies and certain B2B transactions. 5) Update the questionnaire via ELSTER upon any change of address, shareholder, managing director, or business activity. Foreign shareholders may register directly without a fiscal representative, though a domestic agent may be required where no permanent establishment exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a private limited company legally distinct from its shareholders, taxed as a corporation at entity level: corporate income tax 15%, solidarity surcharge 0.825% of corporate tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate typically 7%-17% depending on the local multiplier). Germany taxes resident corporations on worldwide income with no territorial carve-out; no pass-through or check-the-box alternative exists for the UG. Distributions to shareholders are subject to 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge (partial imputation is not available). — 1) Notarize the UG articles of association and register with the Commercial Register (Handelsregister). 2) Submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) electronically via ELSTER within one month of incorporation, declaring corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT (Umsatzsteuer). 3) Receive a local income-tax number (Steuernummer) from the competent Finanzamt. 4) Apply separately to BZSt for a VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) via ELSTER, required for intra-Community supplies and certain B2B transactions. 5) Update the questionnaire via ELSTER upon any change of address, shareholder, managing director, or business activity. Foreign shareholders may register directly without a fiscal representative, though a domestic agent may be required where no permanent establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a private limited company legally distinct from its shareholders, taxed as a corporation at entity level: corporate income tax 15%, solidarity surcharge 0.825% of corporate tax, and municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer, effective rate typically 7%-17% depending on the local multiplier). Germany taxes resident corporations on worldwide income with no territorial carve-out; no pass-through or check-the-box alternative exists for the UG. Distributions to shareholders are subject to 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge (partial imputation is not available).

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; legal form determines tax status. Available elections and options: (1) VAT small-entrepreneur scheme (§19 UStG) below EUR 25,000 annual turnover; (2) VAT group consolidation (Organschaft) for corporate income tax and VAT purposes; (3) intra-Community supply zero-rating subject to mandatory EC Sales List and VIES validation; (4) trade tax loss carry-forward utilization; (5) documentation-based elections for hybrid mismatch treatment under §4k EStG; (6) option to charge VAT on otherwise exempt real-estate transactions under §9 UStG; (7) treaty-based permanent establishment characterization elections in cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual turnover for the domestic small-entrepreneur threshold (§19 UStG); no registration threshold for non-resident suppliers supplying B2B services, telecommunications, digital, or e-commerce services into Germany — mandatory VAT registration and reverse-charge compliance apply immediately. A UG acting as an offshore development center with German taxable supplies is mandatorily VAT registered from its first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for 0% VAT under §4 No. 1 and §6 UStG if goods physically leave the EU and the exporter holds valid export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis). B2B services supplied to non-resident customers — including offshore software development, maintenance, and support — are outside the scope of German VAT under §3a (2) UStG, with the place of supply in the customer's jurisdiction and reverse charge applying to the customer. B2C digital and remote services to non-EU customers are zero-rated under §3a (5) UStG where two independent non-conflicting proofs of customer residence are held. Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers are zero-rated under §4 No. 1b UStG, conditional on the customer's valid VAT ID and submission of the EC Sales List.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung via ATLAS) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial Invoice (Rechnungsnachweis)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank payment/foreign exchange confirmation)
  • Export Proof Certificate (Ausfuhrnachweis) based on transport, shipping or awaiting documents
  • VIES validation record for intra-Community supplies
  • Service agreement and delivery acceptance records for exported services

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing (E-Rechnung) from 1 January 2025: all German-resident businesses must be capable of receiving structured electronic invoices (XRechnung / ZUGFeRD 2.x formats). From 1 January 2027, businesses with previous-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all businesses must issue e-invoices (EDI-based hybrid formats grandfathered only until end-2027 via bilateral agreement). Germany does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance or electronic stamping regime; however, EU ViDA digital reporting requirements (real-time transaction e-ledgers) are expected to apply by 2030, and ELSTER/ERP integration APIs (ErpConnect) already facilitate fully electronic VAT return submission.

Export zero-rating is audited by the Finanzamt with cross-referenced ATLAS customs data, bank payment flows, and corporate transfer-pricing documentation. If the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is missed, the chain of export proof is incomplete, or the customer is ultimately deemed German-resident, the 0% rate is retrospectively revoked and German VAT at 19% is clawed back, plus default surcharges (Säumniszuschläge) of 1% per month and interest of 0.5% per month (6% p.a.) under §233a AO from the original due date. Negligent or intentional mis-declaration constitutes tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung) under §370 AO, carrying imprisonment up to 10 years, fines, and administrative penalties up to EUR 25,000 under §§378-384 AO; managing directors incur joint and several personal liability under §§34, 69 AO; and fraudulent misuse leads to revocation of the VAT ID and loss of EU-wide good standing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag (0.825 percentage points) = 15.825% federal; municipal Gewerbesteuer at 3.5% base × Hebesatz of typically 340–470% yields a combined effective burden of approximately 29–32%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge = 26.375% on gross dividends; this is a final withholding tax for non-resident shareholders unless a DTA provides otherwise.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

31 July of the year following the assessment year; extended to 31 July of the second following year when the return is prepared by a Steuerberater (tax adviser). Example: 2024 return = ordinary deadline 31 July 2025, adviser-assisted = 31 July 2026.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same as KSt 1: 31 July of the following year; for adviser-assisted returns 31 July of the second following year.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box KSt 1 – General information (Mantelbogen: legal form, tax number, business year, seat) Legal identity, registration court/HRB number, tax office (Finanzamt) and tax number, fiscal year opening and closing dates. Non-resident parent: no entry unless the UG has a German permanent establishment (Betriebsstätte); the UG itself is a resident taxpayer subject to unlimited tax liability. For a UG with a PE in Germany, allocate income attributable to the German PE; with no German-source business, no CIT return is required (income is taxed at the shareholder level via dividends). Line 5: Taxable income (Einkommen) and CIT computation Apply 15% CIT rate, then 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag; state the assessed tax payable after credits and advance payments (Vorauszahlungen). Non-resident shareholders do not file this line; the UG computes and pays CIT on its worldwide income as a resident entity. Late-filing surcharge (Verspätungszuschlag): €25 per commenced month minimum, 0.25% of the assessed tax per month, capped at 25% of the tax (§ 152 AO); estimated assessment with risk-increasing surcharge (§ 162 AO); late-payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag): 1% of the unpaid tax per commenced month (§ 240 AO); tax interest of 0.15% per month from 15 months after the assessment year (§ 233a AO); coercive fines (Zwangsgeld) up to €25,000.
Gewerbeertrag (trade earnings base) Start with the taxable income per the KSt return; add-back 25% of interest expenses exceeding €100,000 (financing costs) and other add-backs; deduct the statutory exemptions (e.g. EU dividends). A PE of a foreign company is subject to trade tax on the profit allocated to the German PE; pure dividend recipients without a German PE are outside the trade tax net. Same late-filing and payment surcharge regime as the CIT return (§§ 152, 240 AO); trade tax is also subject to estimated assessment and coercive collection.
Steuermessbetrag and municipal Hebesatz Multiply the rounded Gewerbeertrag by 3.5% to obtain the tax base measure, then by the municipal collection rate (Germ. Hebesatz; commonly 340–470%). The PE must pay trade tax to the municipality where the PE is located; the parent can claim a foreign tax credit for trade tax under the applicable DTA. Same late-filing and payment surcharge regime as the CIT return (§§ 152, 240 AO); trade tax is also subject to estimated assessment and coercive collection.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory duty under § 90(3) AO and the GAufzV: all taxpayers with cross-border related-party transactions must prepare contemporaneous documentation. Master File threshold: multinational group consolidated revenue exceeding €100 million. Local File: required for all German taxpayers with covered transactions; simplified record-keeping is permitted where the local annual turnover is below €6 million (no full comparability analysis required). Documentation must be submitted within 60 days (Local File) / 30 days (Master File) upon notice; non-submission triggers a surcharge of 10% of the adjusted income increase, minimum €80,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no 'Economic Substance Regulations' (ESR) in the Gulf sense, but applies equivalent anti-abuse principles: § 42 AO (abuse of legal form), § 50d Abs. 3 EStG (treaty-shoppping/beneficial ownership), the permanent establishment concept under § 12 AO, CFC rules (§§ 7–13 AStG), and EU ATAD implementation. A German UG/ODC must evidence real substance: a physical office, local employees and payroll, local management decisions, board minutes, service agreements, and technological/operational capacity. Without substance, the tax authorities may reattribute income to the parent, disallow treaty benefits, and apply penalties.
