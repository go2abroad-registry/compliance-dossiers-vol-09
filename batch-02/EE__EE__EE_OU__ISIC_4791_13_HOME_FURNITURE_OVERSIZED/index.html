Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a euro-area member state with full capital and current account liberalisation under EU law (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, arts. 63-65; Currency Act; Eesti Pank Act). No exchange controls restrict current account transactions, capital movements, or profit repatriation. Dividends from an OÜ to non-resident shareholders are freely transferable, subject to withholding tax under the Estonian Income Tax Act (20% regular rate, 14% for regularly distributed dividends in certain cases, 7% for specific cases, or 0% at the corporate level on undistributed profits). The euro is fully convertible; banks freely execute EUR and non-EUR wire transfers. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to compliance with AML/CFT due diligence by the correspondent bank, not to FX or Central Bank restrictions. For a home-furniture e-commerce exporter, proceeds from EU distance sales are treated as intra-Community supplies and are not subject to any surrender or conversion requirement. Non-EU online sales in USD/GBP may be settled in those currencies or converted to EUR without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for use of EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, or Verifo as treasury complements, but with strict legal constraints: (i) the OÜ's Estonian banking and payment accounts must be held with a credit institution or an authorised EMI subject to PSD2/EBA regulatory equivalence; (ii) non-EU/EEA EMIs cannot serve as the primary treasury rail; they may only be used if they operate under an Estonian or EU passported electron-money licence and maintain segregated safeguarding accounts; (iii) outsourcing of core payment processing remains permissible, but the OÜ must exercise effective KYC and AML oversight under the Eesti Pank / Estonian FIU supervision; (iv) digital-only EMIs do not replace the requirement of a stable commercial banking relationship for escrowed consumer funds in the Estonian e-commerce market.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

UBO or director resident in a FATF-high-risk jurisdiction

Root cause: UBO or director resident in a FATF-high-risk jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Complete org chart; notarised passport and address proof; declaration of funds origin; CV and business references; evidence that the director/UBO holds a valid Estonian e-Residency digital ID; audited financial statements of the OÜ or parent; detailed purpose of the Estonian accounts; closing AML questionnaire confirming no PEP relationship.
High customer payment refund rates typical in oversized-furniture e-commerce

Root cause: High customer payment refund rates typical in oversized-furniture e-commerce

Defense evidence: Detailed returns, refund and chargeback policy; shipment and insurance contracts; customer feedback resolution protocol; reserved chargeback funds; payment processor agreements (Stripe and Affirm) showing confirmation of refund handling capability; treasury policy limiting refund liability by holding a designated refund reserve in the operational account.
Large volume of cross-border e-commerce settlement credits from BNPL and card acquirers

Root cause: Large volume of cross-border e-commerce settlement credits from BNPL and card acquirers

Defense evidence: Acquiring/PSP contract showing settlement cadence and finality; export and order documentation for the underlying furniture sales; VAT/EU distance selling registration; a treasury flow matrix reconciling customer payment by Stripe/Affirm to the bank IBAN; audited monthly sales reports and Freedom of Payment Explanation letter.
Unusual financing of the OÜ via shareholder loan or intercompany booking to avoid dividend tax

Root cause: Unusual financing of the OÜ via shareholder loan or intercompany booking to avoid dividend tax

Defense evidence: Loan agreement with market-rate interest; board resolution approving the loan; repayment schedule; funds-path demonstration from lender to OÜ; CBC/tax transparency statements confirming arm's length and no hidden dividend distribution under the Estonian Income Tax Act §50-53.
No physical warehouse or commercial address in Estonia

Root cause: No physical warehouse or commercial address in Estonia

Defense evidence: If the company is an e-commerce intermediary without storage, present: registered business address in Estonia; contract with a licensed Estonian logistics partner or fulfilment centre in a EU country; comprehensive liability insurance; local director with actual management authority; and evidence that the Estonian address is not a mere virtual shell (meeting room, phone line, mailbox used regularly).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocols: inbound equity or shareholder loan must be received via SWIFT MT103 (SEPA SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated flows) to the OÜ's designated IBAN account. The injection is documented in the e-Business Register within 30 days of board approval. Purpose codes should use the ISO 20022 universal purpose 'COLC' (capital contribution to a resident company by non-resident shareholder) or 'SLOA' (intercompany shareholder loan) as per Eesti Pank Codes; for simpler bank forms, 'Equity Injection' or 'Shareholder Loan' must be clearly indicated. Verification: banks require the OÜ's board resolution, the shareholder's UBO KYC documentation, the source-of-wealth statement, and, where the capital injection exceeds EUR 30,000, an Estonian accounting certificate or tax declaration of contributed capital.
