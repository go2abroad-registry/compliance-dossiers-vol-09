Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) — central allocation of the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) and management of the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registry; local Finanzamt (tax office having jurisdiction over the UG's registered seat) — assessment of corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT; ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) portal — mandatory electronic filing channel with certificate-based credentials for all tax returns.

E-government filing portal

Within four weeks after commercial registration in the Handelsregister (commercial register), the UG, including with non-resident shareholders, must file the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung', statutory basis §138 AO) with the competent Finanzamt. The Finanzamt assigns the corporate Steuernummer and issues advance tax assessments (Vorauszahlungsbescheide) for corporation tax and trade tax. Separately, the UG applies for the VAT identification number from BZSt using the online form (basis §27a UStG); issuance typically takes two to four weeks. For cross-border B2C digital supplies to EU consumers, the UG concurrently registers on the BZSt OSS portal (Union scheme). There is no minimum capital requirement (UG statutory minimum EUR 1), no nationality or residency restriction on shareholders, and no requirement to appoint a local tax agent for a German-resident UG.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG is a Kapitalgesellschaft (limited-liability corporation) and is always taxed as a separate taxpayer; no pass-through eligibility exists in any circumstance. Tax burden: Körperschaftsteuer 15%; Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% of the corporation tax (effective 0.825%); Gewerbesteuer, which applies to all corporations by legal form regardless of activity, at a base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200% to 490%), producing an effective range of approximately 7% to 17.15%. Territorial scope: worldwide income for resident UGs, with foreign-source income exempt or credit-relieved under double tax treaties. Distributions to shareholders are subject to additional final withholding/income tax of 26.375% (25% plus solidarity surcharge, plus church tax where applicable). — Within four weeks after commercial registration in the Handelsregister (commercial register), the UG, including with non-resident shareholders, must file the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung', statutory basis §138 AO) with the competent Finanzamt. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG is a Kapitalgesellschaft (limited-liability corporation) and is always taxed as a separate taxpayer; no pass-through eligibility exists in any circumstance. Tax burden: Körperschaftsteuer 15%; Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% of the corporation tax (effective 0.825%); Gewerbesteuer, which applies to all corporations by legal form regardless of activity, at a base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200% to 490%), producing an effective range of approximately 7% to 17.15%. Territorial scope: worldwide income for resident UGs, with foreign-source income exempt or credit-relieved under double tax treaties. Distributions to shareholders are subject to additional final withholding/income tax of 26.375% (25% plus solidarity surcharge, plus church tax where applicable).

Available election forms

Germany has no check-the-box or comparable entity-classification election. Available elections: (1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under KStG §§14-17 and UStG §2 Abs.2 Nr.2 — the UG may be integrated as a subsidiary of a German parent for profit and VAT consolidation if financial, economic, and organizational integration exists and a profit-and-loss transfer agreement has been concluded; (2) Kleinunternehmerregelung (§19 UStG) — exemption for turnover margins not exceeding EUR 25,000 in the prior year and EUR 100,000 in the current year (thresholds effective 1 January 2025), which waives VAT output but bars input VAT deduction; rarely advantageous for platform sales; (3) Ist-Versteuerung (§20 UStG) — cash-basis VAT accounting election available where prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000; (4) hybrid mismatch rules (EStG §4k, KStG §4k, ATAD implementation) apply mandatorily, with no elective mitigation; no elective hybrid-instrument treatment is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established or foreign suppliers engaged in taxable German supplies. Domestic small-business (Kleinunternehmer) concession: EUR 25,000 prior-year and EUR 100,000 current-year turnover. German VAT nexus for the stock-photo and AI visual-assets business follows place-of-supply rules under UStG §3a: (i) B2C digital services to German consumers are taxable in Germany at 19% with no de minimis; (ii) B2C digital services to other EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's Member State and declared via the EU OSS, with no per-country registration or threshold; (iii) B2B digital services are reverse-charged to the customer under §13b UStG (no German VAT); (iv) physical merchandising sales would trigger the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-selling threshold under OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are zero-rated under UStG §4 Nr.1a i.V.m. §6 upon proof of dispatch to non-EU territory, evidenced by the ATLAS customs declaration and transport documents; the export-evidence deadline is 60 days under §10 UStDV. For the digital stock-photo business, zero-rating is available only for (a) B2B supplies of digital/licensing services to customers established outside Germany under the reverse-charge mechanism, and (b) B2C digital supplies to consumers resident outside the EU, which fall outside German VAT scope. B2C digital supplies to EU consumers are not zero-rated and must be declared through OSS at the destination-state rate. Input VAT on domestic cost inputs (cloud infrastructure, software, licensing, imagery) is recoverable through the regular German VAT return; non-established suppliers would use the EU 13th Directive or §18 Abs.9 UStG refund route. Germany imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline under free-capital-movement rules; the 60-day figure below is the statutory VAT export-evidence window.

  • ATLAS electronic customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with export accompanying document (Ausfuhrbegleitdokument) or automated release confirmation
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (Konnossement), CMR international consignment note, or airway bill with arrival endorsement (UStAE §13.6)
  • Commercial invoice accurately describing the licensed digital assets and evidencing the cross-border nature of the supply
  • Stripe and PayPal settlement reports (API or PDF) evidencing receipt of consideration from foreign customers, supplemented by bank credit statements for currency substantiation
  • Standard terms of service and license agreements establishing the place of customer establishment for B2B reverse-charge purposes

No real-time fiscal clearance, no government digital stamping, and no fiscal API mandate exists. E-invoicing mandate: from 1 January 2025, every German B2B supplier must be able to receive structured e-invoices conforming to EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x); mandatory issuance applies from 1 January 2027 for suppliers whose prior-year turnover exceeded EUR 800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for all other suppliers. Exchange may occur outside the Peppol network; no prior tax authority clearance is required. Complementary reporting obligations: CESOP payment-data reporting by PSPs (Stripe/PayPal) to BZSt for cross-border B2C card/payment transactions; DAC7 platform-reporting rules for marketplace operators; and monthly/quarterly VAT returns plus annual recapitulative statements (Zusammenfassende Meldung) filed via ELSTER.

Audit scrutiny (Betriebsprüfung) concentrates on zero-rated digital supplies lacking demonstrable foreign customer establishment or export substantiation. Consequences: retrospective VAT assessment at 19% plus statutory interest of 0.5% per month (§233a AO, annualized 6%); late-payment surcharge of 1% per month (§240 AO); input-VAT clawback under the §15a UStG adjustment regime (5-year adjustment period for movable and digital assets). Administrative and criminal penalties: §370 AO tax evasion — fine of up to 360 daily rates (each daily rate up to EUR 30,000) and imprisonment up to 10 years; §378 AO negligent tax shortfall — fine up to EUR 50,000; §379 AO endangering taxation — fine up to EUR 5,000; OSS misuse leads to exclusion from OSS with resulting multi-jurisdiction VAT registrations. Assessment limitation period: 6 years standard, 10 years in evasion cases (§169 AO); documentary retention obligation: 10 years (AO §147, UStAE §13.9).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital framework (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66). The Euro (EUR) is a fully convertible currency with no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation for non-resident shareholders. However, all cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV §67). Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax (15% plus solidarity surcharge and trade tax) and, if applicable, withholding tax under the German-Swiss or other double tax treaties, but no exchange control hurdles exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances in domestic or foreign accounts. However, all cross-border receipts and payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank within seven days of the transaction (AWV §67). For the stock photography business, revenue from foreign platforms (e.g., Stripe, PayPal) is treated as export of services; no conversion ratio is mandated, but the UG must ensure proper tax declaration and transfer pricing documentation if intercompany transactions occur.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; foreign exchange controls are administered under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV) by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action and the Bundesbank for statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for low-risk digital businesses, but subject to legal constraints. German and EU Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, Airwallex, and MangoPay can provide multi-currency IBANs and cross-border payment rails. However, EMIs are not licensed as full banks and do not offer deposit insurance (Einlagensicherung) beyond the EMI's own safeguarding requirements. For a UG with a stock photography platform, EMIs are viable for receiving payments from global marketplaces (Stripe, PayPal) and for FX conversion, but the primary corporate bank account should be held with a tier-1 bank to ensure full regulatory compliance and access to credit facilities. EMIs must be authorized by BaFin or another EU regulator under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injection from non-resident shareholders, the UG must receive funds via SWIFT wire transfer using the correct purpose code. Under the AWV, the purpose code for equity injection is 'K' (Kapitalverkehr) with sub-code 'K1' for direct investment. The transfer must be reported to the Bundesbank if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500. The bank will require a copy of the shareholder resolution and the capital contribution agreement. For a UG, the minimum capital is EUR 1, but for credibility, a higher injection (e.g., EUR 10,000) is recommended. The funds must be credited to the UG's operational account, and the bank will verify the source of funds to comply with AML regulations.For trade settlements (e.g., paying content contributors, licensing fees, or software subscriptions), the UG can use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers or commercial letters of credit (LC) for larger transactions. Since the business is digital, MT103 is the primary rail. The bank will require the purpose code for services (e.g., 'S' for services) and may request supporting invoices. For recurring payments to EU entities, SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) transfers are more cost-effective. For non-EU payments, SWIFT is mandatory. The UG should maintain a clear audit trail of all outbound payments to avoid AML scrutiny.FX Forwards: Over-the-counter (OTC) forward contracts with a German bank or EU-licensed broker to lock in exchange rates for future USD/EUR conversions from Stripe/PayPal revenue.FX Swaps: Currency swaps for managing longer-term exposure if the UG holds significant USD balances.NDFs (Non-Deliverable Forwards): Not applicable for EUR/USD as the EUR is freely convertible; NDFs are only used for restricted currencies. However, if the UG transacts with emerging market currencies (e.g., INR, CNY), NDFs may be permissible but are not standard for the primary EUR/USD pair.Options: Over-the-counter FX options (call/put) to hedge against adverse rate movements, though these are less common for small entities and require a credit line with the bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High for low-risk digital businesses, but subject to legal constraints. German and EU Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as PPRO, Airwallex, and MangoPay can provide multi-currency IBANs and cross-border payment rails. However, EMIs are not licensed as full banks and do not offer deposit insurance (Einlagensicherung) beyond the EMI's own safeguarding requirements. For a UG with a stock photography platform, EMIs are viable for receiving payments from global marketplaces (Stripe, PayPal) and for FX conversion, but the primary corporate bank account should be held with a tier-1 bank to ensure full regulatory compliance and access to credit facilities. EMIs must be authorized by BaFin or another EU regulator under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2).
