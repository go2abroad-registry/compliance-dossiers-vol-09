Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a Eurozone member (since 1 January 2011) and maintains full, unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The Estonian Foreign Exchange Act contains no exchange-control restrictions on payment transfers, repatriation, or profit remittance. Capital injections, dividend distributions, and intra-group treasury flows are freely permitted. The only hurdle for profit remittance is the Estonian corporate income tax on distributed profits (20% on gross distribution, 14% on regular and lower distributions to individuals), which is a tax rule and not a currency restriction. For a non-resident corporate shareholder, withholding tax may be eliminated under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, an applicable double-tax treaty, or the Estonian Participation Exemption rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Estonia. Exporters are not obliged to convert foreign currency proceeds within any timeline and may lawfully retain their clearing currency. All export and import payments must nevertheless be recorded in the balance-of-payments survey. Where the Estonian OÜ maintains the euro account with a licensed Estonian bank, the bank submits BOP statistical reporting to Eesti Pank. Companies using non-Estonian payment service providers are subject to direct statistical reporting obligations upon request, and must retain export documentation for at least 7 years under general accounting rules.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the central bank and member of the Eurosystem; overall financial supervisory authority is the Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority). There is no separate foreign-exchange administration jurisdiction. Foreign-exchange statistics and balance-of-payments reporting are collected under the Eesti Pank Statistical Norms, and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions is conducted by the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) and Finantsinspektsioon.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-to-Low for primary treasury rails, viable only as a supplemental operational float. EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe, Revolut Business, and Payoneer offer convenient SEPA and multicurrency acquisition, but they are governed by PSD2 and cannot legally provide the full suite of corporate banking services required for a Class III medical device manufacturer, particularly (i) documentary letters of credit, (ii) trade finance with supplier guarantees, (iii) cross-currency settlement with counter-bank SWIFT ISPA clearing, and (iv) permissible FX hedging execution. An unlicensed offshore EMI should not be used for any capital injection or export proceeds collection, as the risk of frozen funds and regulatory non-attribution is high. An Estonian OÜ may lawfully use an EEA-passported EMI only after the EMI has undergone due diligence on the underlying production site and the commercial invoices supporting high-value wire flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for the Estonian OÜ requires a SWIFT MT103 or an intra-EEA SEPA CT from a bank account held in the shareholder’s legal name, payable to the OÜ’s dedicated IBAN at a licensed Estonian credit institution. SWIFT field /purpose/ must clearly indicate `SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION 8648` or, for shareholder loans, `SUBORDINATED LOAN – NON-RESIDENT SHAREHOLDER`. The crediting bank will internally use the Balance-of-Payments code for 'Other Investment: Equity Shares and Fund Shares' under Eesti Pank BOP guidelines; no government licence is required. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained/undistributed profits; 20% (gross) / 20-on-80 formula (25% on net distribution) when profits are distributed; 14% (gross) / 14-on-86 (16.28% on net distribution) for regular distributions made at least once in each of the preceding three tax years.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

No shareholder-level withholding: the tax regime is payable by the distributing OÜ at 20% gross (20/80 = 25% of the net dividend); 14% reduced rate for regular distributions (14/86 = 16.28% of net). The net dividend is remitted abroad without any Estonian tax credit certificate — the tax is discharged by the Estonian entity.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD: due by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the distribution/payment/withholding event occurred. Nil declarations must also be submitted. Annual accounts: due within 6 months of the financial year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Part I, Row A — Dividends / profit distribution (20% rate) Gross amount of dividends and other profit distributions subject to 20% CIT. Declare the full gross distribution to the non-resident shareholder; the OÜ self-assesses and remits 20/80 of the net dividend. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 — Local File is mandatory when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with a single related party in a tax year exceeds EUR 50,000 (Taxation Act § 77). Master File: required when the EEA/worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50 million; CbCR reporting at the OECD/EU threshold of EUR 750 million group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply a classic ESR law (e.g., UAE ICAA), but substance is legally determinative through: (i) the central-management-and-control test for tax residence (Taxation Act § 6); (ii) the general anti-avoidance clause (Taxation Act § 83); and (iii) the deemed-distribution rules under Income Tax Act § 50. An OÜ will be disregarded for treaty benefit if its board functions, decision-making, Class III device supervision and medical evidence obligations under EU MDR 2017/745 are not executed from Estonia. The e-Residency instrument does not create corporate substance; it only facilitates digital formalities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Unlawful Non-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penal Code § 397: pecuniary fine or imprisonment up to 3 years; aggravated cases (large-scale tax evasion) imprisonment of 1–5 years; the court also orders the unpaid tax, 0.06% daily late-payment interest and any administrative fine (up to €3,000) to be discharged.

Director personal liability: Directors of the OÜ face imprisonment as the penal addressee of § 397; after formal insolvency, the management board is jointly and severally liable for the tax arrears if it fails to file the bankruptcy petition within 20 days of solvency being questioned (Taxation Act § 14(5), Commercial Code § 302).
Shell Company / Abuse of e-Residency / Artificial Conduct

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of accumulated profits as distributed — immediate 20% CIT plus daily late-payment interest; transaction relief (Taxation Act § 83) may annul the corporate arrangement and subject the income to the actual beneficiary-trust or foreign-entity treatment; the OÜ loses treaty and EU directive protection.

Director personal liability: Commercial Code § 37(4) — a shareholder or director who uses the company to evade law is personally liable for the company's obligations; travel restrictions/border-bans may be applied by the tax authority during pending arrears; commercial name disqualification in the e-Äriregister.
Transfer Pricing Malaise

Statutory sanction: The deductible expense is disallowed and reclassified as a deemed dividend → 20% CIT on the grossed-up amount (20/80 formula) plus 0.06% daily interest and penalties under Taxation Act § 77; the tax authority may rely on the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for 2022 to apply an arm's-length margin of 8–15% for a routine CMO.

Director personal liability: If the TP result causes insolvency, directors incur joint liability for the corporate tax debt; intentional TP manipulation is penalised under Penal Code § 397 with up to 3 years' imprisonment; professional disqualification bars the director from board positions in Estonia.
Unlicensed Class III Medical Device Activity / Patient-Safety Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of €1,200–€1,000,000 and product withdrawal under the Medicinal Products Act; criminal liability under Penal Code § 117 (grave endangerment of health) and § 362 for unauthorized supply of medicinal devices — imprisonment up to 5 years; prohibition from management up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Public-liability claims via the extraordinary tort law route; board members can be personally sued by injured patients under general delict rules (Law of Obligations Act § 1043) if the device-caused injury resulted from the board's personal causal misconduct; immediate suspension of the OÜ's market authorisation and revocation of the e-service account.
