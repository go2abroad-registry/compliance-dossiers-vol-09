Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus – RIK) operating the e-Business Register (Äriregistri portaal); registration is performed by the Registry Department of Tartu County Court.

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), Chapter 4, §§ 135–165 (formation of private limited company), § 136 (share capital), § 140 (payment of contributions), §§ 175–179 (management board); Legal Registers Act (Äriregistri seadus) §§ 19–36; Obligations Act §§ 26–30 (legal person seat). Digital route is governed by the Digital Signatures Act and the e-Business Register rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation notice of a private limited company (Osaühingu asutamisteatis) filed electronically in the e-Business Register and digitally signed by all founders; the notice incorporates the Articles of Association (põhikiri) and management board consents. Notarial certification is required only when the paper/notary channel is used.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 10 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital since 1 March 2011; an OÜ may be incorporated with zero share capital (Commercial Code § 136). If founders declare a paid-up share capital, monetary contributions must be fully credited to the company's bank account opened with a credit institution or Estonian payment service provider before filing, and the bank's confirmation or a capital-account statement must be lodged with the registry. No escrow deposit is required for a zero-capital company. Non-monetary contributions are permitted; where their value is uncertain an auditor's appraisal must accompany the filing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: premises must satisfy the Fire Safety Act minimum protection requirements; premises exceeding 600 m² or storing hazardous chemical inventories require a fire safety audit by a licensed expert filed with the Rescue Board, while smaller offices and warehouses require a completed fire safety self-inspection and maintained extinguishers. Occupational health: before the first hire, the employer must commission an occupational health risk assessment and workplace measurements (Workplace Health and Safety Act § 13), adopt a preventive action plan, and appoint an employee trustee where the workforce exceeds ten persons. Structural clearance: the leased or owned unit must hold a valid building usage permit (kasutusluba) issued by the local municipality authorising office/warehouse use, and the electrical installation must be covered by the contractor's conformity declaration kept on site.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital since 1 March 2011; an OÜ may be incorporated with zero share capital (Commercial Code § 136). If founders declare a paid-up share capital, monetary contributions must be fully credited to the company's bank account opened with a credit institution or Estonian payment service provider before filing, and the bank's confirmation or a capital-account statement must be lodged with the registry. No escrow deposit is required for a zero-capital company. Non-monetary contributions are permitted; where their value is uncertain an auditor's appraisal must accompany the filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet), via the e-MTA electronic portal (e-maksuamet)

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor forms an Osaühing (OÜ) through the Estonian e-Business / Commercial Register. Upon incorporation, the registrar automatically issues the company's registry code, which serves as the legal entity tax identification number (TIN) and VAT identification number basis. The OÜ must apply for VAT registration directly through the e-MTA portal if taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold or if the company elects to register voluntarily. For non-resident entities without a local permanent establishment, similar registration may be required under the Value Added Tax Act for applicable distance sales of goods to Estonian consumers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime is available. Estonia applies a unique distributed-profits taxation system: retained and undistributed profits of an OÜ are free of corporate income tax, while distributions attract corporate income tax at either 20/80 of the net amount (i.e. effectively 25% gross-up) or 14/86 for regular dividend payments meeting statutory criteria. Taxpayers may elect to pay income tax on the rental and other categories separately under the simplified fixed quarterly rule where applicable. No hybrid mismatch elections exist under domestic law; EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive alignment applies, and VAT grouping is available only under the conditions set out in the Estonian VAT Act.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year of taxable supply value; special distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 applies to cross-border electronic and remote sales to Estonian consumers unless an election for destination-state taxation has been made

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate may be applied on the sale of 3D printers and resin filament where the goods are physically dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union and the vendor retains documented export proof within the statutory period. Intra-Community supply zero-rating is also available when the purchaser supplies a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are transported to another EU Member State. The export transaction is treated as zero-rated only for the direct exportation of goods; ancillary domestic pickup or warehousing services before export remain taxable unless separately relieved.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/EAD accepted by Estonian Tax and Customs Board)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing cross-border transport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment proof showing consideration received
  • Sales invoice referencing the 0% VAT rate and the reason for export

Estonia has not introduced a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance mechanism for private-sector B2B/B2C e-invoicing. VAT is accounted for periodically via the KMD VAT return and the VAT declaration information supplements (KMD INF). Structured electronic invoices are recognized and recommended via the Estonian X-Road network, and official arrangements exist for interoperability with PEPPOL-type invoicing, but there is no statutory API-based real-time clearance, fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control requirement.

Estonian Tax and Customs Board may retroactively disallow export zero-rating if the export documentation dossier is incomplete, false, or does not clearly establish that goods left the EU within the required time frame. Reclassified supplies become taxable at 22% VAT, with default interest at the statutory rate and late-payment surcharges. Intentional or materially fraudulent zero-rating claims can attract administrative fines and criminal penalties under the Penal Code, including imprisonment in cases of substantial tax fraud, together with a solidarity obligation for the unpaid tax, penalties, and procedural costs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions is guaranteed by EU treaty obligations (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and Estonia's European Monetary Union membership effective 1 January 2011. The euro is the sole legal tender; there are no exchange control approval requirements for dividend remittances, profit repatriation, or share capital reductions. Estonia imposes no withholding tax on dividend distributions to resident or non-resident corporate shareholders, provided the distribution meets the regulatory solvency and annual report filings under the Commercial Code (Äriseadustik) and Taxation Act (Tulumaksuseadus).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement. Export receipts may be held in any bank account operated in Estonia (euro or foreign currency) and freely converted. Settlement for e-commerce exports via payment operators (e.g., Stripe) is not blocked, and no mandatory conversion to EUR is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) operates within the Eurosystem; prudential banking and AML supervision is jointly exercised by the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) and the Estonian FIU under the Ministry of Finance. Foreign exchange policy is fully delegated to the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility under the Estonian Payment Institutions and E-money Institutions Act (Makseasutuste ja e-raha asutuste seadus) as implemented from EU Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2). Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., solutions passporting from the UK or Singapore) are accepted only if they hold an EEA passport under PSD2 or are licensed by Finantsinspektsioon. Legal constraints include mandatory segregated client funds, robust AML/CFT KYC standards, and compliance with sanctions screening. For the low-risk online retail of 3D printers and consumables, EMIs provide efficient SWIFT/SEPA routing but should not serve as a substitute for a compliant domestic Tier-1 corporate bank account for statutory capital receipts.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed retained profits; 20% corporate income tax on distributed profits, computed as 20/80 of the net distribution and equal to 20% of the gross distribution amount.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% shareholder-level withholding tax; the relevant levy is the distributing company's corporate income tax at 20% on the gross distribution (20/80 on the net cash dividend).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Cross-border controlled transactions in aggregate exceeding EUR 50,000 in value for goods and EUR 25,000 in value for services, royalties, and other intra-group charges trigger mandatory Local File preparation. Master File obligations attach when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds EUR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, economic substance remains decisive through place-of-effective-management analysis, controlled foreign company rules under ATAD, the Estonian General Anti-Abuse Rule, and beneficial ownership tests for treaty access. An OÜ that exists only on paper, lacks Estonian board decision-making, has no local bank account or operational staff, and is managed from abroad will be vulnerable to recharacterization as a foreign-resident company or a CFC, thereby destroying the 0% retained-earnings tax benefit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Hidden Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under the Estonian Penal Code; legal persons may be fined up to EUR 7,200,000, with confiscation of the proceeds of crime. Natural persons involved in large-scale evasion are exposed to imprisonment of up to 5 years, with up to 7 years for aggravated tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate income tax, interest, and penalties where the failure results from willful or grossly negligent conduct. Civil restitution orders, asset seizures, travel bans, and custodial sentences may be imposed on directors who approve or conceal the distribution.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Estonian tax authorities may disregard the entity under the General Anti-Abuse Rule and reallocate income to the place of effective management; the entity may be treated as tax-resident abroad. Fines under tax enforcement law and revocation of e-Residency digital ID privileges may follow where the arrangement is part of a tax evasion scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for taxes and penalties imposed on the deemed foreign-resident entity; they may also face deprivation of liberty if the sham entity is used to commit criminal tax or fraud offenses. Civil recovery and travel restrictions are commonly applied where foreign tax claims are enforced through mutual assistance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board, additional taxable distributions recomputed on a grossed-up basis, penalty interest of 0.06% per day, late filing penalties up to EUR 1,300, and coercive fines up to EUR 10,000 for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length terms face civil liability for the tax deficiency; where artificial pricing is accompanied by false invoices, criminal tax evasion sanctions, including imprisonment up to 5 years, may be imposed.
AML / Payment Processing Violations

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act; supervisory fines for legal persons can reach EUR 5,000,000 or, where applicable, 10% of total annual turnover. Repeat or intentional violations may lead to revocation of payment service access and compulsory winding-up.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers are personally liable for non-compliance; courts may impose fines and imprisonment of up to 4 years for money laundering facilitation, alongside personal asset forfeiture and travel bans.
Failure to File TSD / Persistent Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty up to EUR 1,300; daily late payment interest at 0.06%; coercive penalties up to EUR 10,000; seizure of bank accounts or movable property of the OÜ for enforced tax collection.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to file for three months after due dates while the OÜ is insolvent can be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax obligations under the Estonian Commercial Code and Tax Information Act, including forfeiture of personal assets and, in egregious cases, prison sentences for criminal tax fraud.
