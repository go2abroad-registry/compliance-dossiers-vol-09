Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A UG has no statutory requirement to maintain share capital of €25,000; the minimum stated capital is €1.00 and must be fully paid in cash into a German business bank account in the name of the company before registration, with the funds remaining at the unrestricted disposal of the managing director. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for German VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.) and the ELSTER electronic tax platform operated with the German federal states; the locally competent tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT registration, tax numbers, filings, and assessments.

E-government filing portal

A German UG must register with the competent Finanzamt by submitting the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER. The Finanzamt issues the domestic tax number (Steuernummer). A VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is separately requested from the BZSt, normally through the relevant tax registration process or the BZSt application service. The application should include the commercial-register extract, articles of association, registered office, directors, beneficial ownership information, business activities, expected turnover, bank details, VAT transaction profile, and evidence of cross-border transactions. A foreign entity without a German establishment applies to the designated Finanzamt for non-established traders and requests a BZSt VAT identification number where required for intra-Community transactions. Non-EU businesses may require a German fiscal representative or tax agent in specific VAT procedures, but Germany does not impose a general fiscal-representation requirement for every non-EU taxable person. Registration is generally required before making taxable supplies for which the entity is the liable person, unless a reverse-charge or other statutory exception applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German capital company and is subject to entity-level corporation tax, solidarity surcharge, and municipal trade tax; it has no US-style check-the-box election. German partnership classification elections under Section 1a of the Corporate Income Tax Act generally do not apply to a UG. Relevant statutory choices may include opting out of the VAT small-business exemption under Section 19 of the VAT Act, joining a VAT group (Organschaft) where all statutory financial, organizational, and economic integration conditions are met, and electing applicable VAT reporting periods where permitted. German anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 4k and related provisions of the Income Tax Act can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for hybrid arrangements; they are not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany has no general turnover threshold below which a business making taxable supplies is automatically exempt from VAT registration. The Section 19 small-business exemption may apply where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000, subject to statutory conditions; the business may waive the exemption and remain bound for the election period. A German-established business generally registers when it conducts taxable supplies for which it is liable to collect VAT. For EU cross-border B2C telecommunications, broadcasting, electronic, and other electronically supplied services and intra-Community distance sales, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold may apply, with taxation in the customer Member State or use of the One Stop Shop (OSS). Reverse-charge transactions may not require German VAT collection, but they can still create registration, reporting, invoicing, or recapitulative-statement obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax credit under Sections 4 no. 1(a) and 6 UStG if the goods are transported or dispatched to the third country, the exporter or its customer satisfies the statutory export conditions, and the exporter maintains prescribed documentary and accounting evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for VAT exemption under Section 4 no. 1(b) and Section 6a UStG where the goods are transported to another EU Member State, the customer is an eligible taxable person or specified legal person with a valid VAT identification number, the goods arrive in the other Member State, and the supplier satisfies invoice, accounting, and recapitulative-statement requirements. B2B services supplied to a business customer established outside Germany are generally outside the scope of German VAT under the customer-location rule in Section 3a(2) UStG, rather than technically zero-rated exports; reverse-charge or local registration obligations may arise in the customer jurisdiction. Electronically supplied B2C services are subject to EU customer-location rules, the EUR 10,000 threshold where applicable, and OSS or non-Union OSS procedures.

Germany does not currently operate universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory invoice-by-invoice digital stamping. Under Section 14 UStG, domestic B2B businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices from 1 January 2025. Transitional issuing rules generally permit other invoice formats through 2026; from 2027, businesses with prior-calendar-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must generally issue structured e-invoices for qualifying domestic B2B transactions, and from 2028 structured e-invoicing becomes the general rule for qualifying domestic B2B transactions. Compliant formats include XRechnung and other EN 16931-compliant structured formats, including qualifying hybrid ZUGFeRD invoices. The transition does not generally cover B2C supplies, many cross-border supplies, or exempt transactions outside the statutory scope. VAT registrations, returns, recapitulative statements, and most tax communications are filed electronically through ELSTER; Germany does not generally require pre-clearance API transmission of every invoice.

Germany has no general foreign-exchange control or statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days. Export exemption and related input-tax refunds can be denied or clawed back if export, transport, customer-status, place-of-supply, or accounting evidence is missing, inconsistent, fabricated, or obtained after the fact. The Finanzamt may assess the unpaid VAT, recover improperly refunded input tax, impose tax interest under the German Fiscal Code, require corrections to VAT returns and recapitulative statements, and apply late-filing or inaccurate-filing penalties. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under Section 370 of the Fiscal Code, punishable by fines or imprisonment of up to five years and, in particularly serious cases, imprisonment of up to ten years. Participation in fraudulent export or VAT chains may also trigger criminal liability, joint liability, customs sanctions under applicable EU customs law, denial of deduction, and extended audit periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account regime under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and EU rules on free movement of capital (TFEU Articles 63-66). The euro is fully convertible and freely transferable. No exchange control approvals are required for foreign direct investment, dividend repatriation, or softwar licensing royalties. There are no restrictions on the holding of foreign currency accounts by German resident companies, and no permission is needed for offshore ownership of a UG. The only material compliance duty is statistical reporting to the Bundesbank: cross-border payments or transfers by a resident (including the UG) to or from a non-resident must be reported if the individual payment exceeds EUR 50,000 (threshold effective 1 June 2024, raised from EUR 12,500) through the Bundesbank's Integrated Reporting Procedure (Integriertes Sammelmeldeverfahren, ISS). Profit repatriation by a UG to a non-resident shareholder is legally unrestricted but may attract dividend withholding tax at 25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, subject to reduction under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable double taxation treaty; the UG also must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until its share capital, together with retained reserves, reaches EUR 25,000 (Section 5a GmbHG).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements on export proceeds. Export receivables (including receipts from AI video synthesis SaaS subscriptions, licensing, or royalty payments from foreign customers) may be held in foreign currency, converted at market rate at the UG's discretion, or used directly to settle foreign-currency liabilities. The only statutory timeline concerns AWV reporting: a Z3 (balance-of-payments) statistical report applicable to service exports and cross-border payment flows must be filed electronically via the Bundesbank ISS system no later than the 10th working day of the month following the reporting month; for certain capital transactions, a Z4 single-transaction report is due within five working days. The report must classify the transaction and state the contractual purpose, counterparty, amount, and currency. Failure to file can result in administrative fines under Section 33 AWG.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Frankfurt am Main) acting within the Eurosystem, with the European Central Bank holding ultimate monetary authority for the euro area. The Bundesbank is the competent German authority for balance-of-payments statistics, minimum reserve administration, and reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) supervises banks, payment institutions, and securities firms; the Bundesbank operates as the operational arm of banking supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: HIGH for SaaS payment collection, low-cost currency conversion, and rapid onboarding; MEDIUM-LOW as the sole treasury banking rail. Legal constraints: offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer) operate under deposits of the PSD2 Electronic Money Institution licence of their home EU member state through passporting into Germany, not under a German banking licence. Funds held with an EMI are safeguarded rather than accepted as bank deposits, are not covered by the German statutory deposit guarantee scheme, and may be invested only in permitted low-risk assets. The EMI may impose enhanced due diligence or terminate the relationship if the AI avatar video generation business is perceived as a deepfake or fraud-adjacent sector. The UG's primary corporate account should therefore be held with a BaFin-supervised German credit institution; EMIs and PSPs such as Stripe and Paddle should be deployed only as collection and payout rails, with daily or weekly sweeps of balances to the German account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) is 15% on taxable profits. A 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) is added to the corporate tax liability, resulting in a combined federal rate of 15.825%. Municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is separately levied at a statutory tax base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (commonly 200%–490%), yielding an effective municipal trade tax of approximately 7%–17%. After the deductibility of trade tax as a business expense for corporate income tax purposes, the aggregate effective tax burden for a typical UG is approximately 26%–30%, depending on the municipality.
