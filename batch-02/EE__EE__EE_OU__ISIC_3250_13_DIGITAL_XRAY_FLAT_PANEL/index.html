Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; nominal share capital may be fixed at as little as €1 (Commercial Code section 135(1)–(2)). Cash contributions paid before registration are credited to the e-Business Register's designated collection sub-account and released to the company's own bank account upon entry; no notary escrow is mandated for fully electronic filings. If share capital is set below €2,500, profit distribution is deferred until capital is increased to at least €2,500, and the contribution schedule is fixed in the articles of association.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety inspection and certificate from the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) for the industrial premises; building usage permit (kasutusluba) from the local municipal building-control authority confirming structural load capacity for cleanroom and lead-shielded X-ray test bays; work-environment registration and risk assessment filed with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon); mandatory personnel radiation dosimetry through an accredited dosimetry service for X-ray test operators; emergency response plans and first-aid provision per the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Töötervishoiu ja tööohutuse seadus).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (EMTA) – e-MTA online portal (https://emta.ee / https://eestico.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Estonian OÜ must (1) submit the incorporation application to the Commercial Registry through e-Business Register; (2) obtain an Estonian Registry Code (the corporate TIN) automatically upon registration; (3) separately register for Estonian VAT via e-MTA using the registry code, if the €40,000 threshold is met or exceeded or if required for cross-border acquisitions/receipts of services; (4) obtain a VAT number (VAT ID) in a standard EU format (EE1000xxxxx) upon VAT registration. If the company has no physical presence in Estonia, it may use the e-Residency digital ID for all filings and signatures.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an Estonian OÜ must (1) submit the incorporation application to the Commercial Registry through e-Business Register; (2) obtain an Estonian Registry Code (the corporate TIN) automatically upon registration; (3) separately register for Estonian VAT via e-MTA using the registry code, if the €40,000 threshold is met or exceeded or if required for cross-border acquisitions/receipts of services; (4) obtain a VAT number (VAT ID) in a standard EU format (EE1000xxxxx) upon VAT registration. If the company has no physical presence in Estonia, it may use the e-Residency digital ID for all filings and signatures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box system exists in Estonia. An OÜ is automatically an entity-level taxpayer. Certain statutory elections available under the Income Tax Act: (i) profit distribution taxation election to apply the 14% reduced rate on regular profit distributions if distributed amount does not exceed the two-year average of taxed dividends; (ii) VAT group registration election for closely linked companies; (iii) hybrid mismatch rules are implemented under ATAD and no beneficial election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 in taxable supplies per calendar year (for foreign entities supplying goods or services in Estonia, mandatory registration is required upon exceeding the threshold or when acquiring goods from other Member States subject to excise duty; non-established suppliers may be required to register regardless of threshold when performing certain supplies in Estonia, especially B2C distance sales or installed goods)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to (i) exports of goods physically transported from Estonia to a third country, provided the seller keeps transport documents and customs evidence; (ii) intra-Community supplies to a customer with a valid EU VAT ID and dispatch to another Member State; (iii) certain services directly linked to exported goods (e.g., freight, loading) and services rendered for goods to be exported. The zero rate requires full documentary proof; input VAT on costs incurred to make zero-rated export sales is fully creditable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD lodged via RAPEX and confirmed by the Estonian Customs)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from Estonia to a non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Port/warehouse cargo movement evidence or cargo manifest (alternative transport proof)
  • Bank payment confirmation or inward remittance certificate (if available and requested by verifying the export payment)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Estonia. E-invoicing is voluntary but encouraged; mandatory e-invoicing applies for B2G contracts under EU Directive 2014/55/EU (PEPPOL network). VAT returns and Intrastat are filed through the e-MTA portal. Standard e-invoice formats (XML, EDIFACT) and PDF invoices are accepted. Fiscal receipts are not electronically stamped by the tax authority.

Estonian Tax and Customs Board performs documentary and physical checks on zero-rated exports. If the export documentation is deficient or false, the entire VAT at 20% is retroactively assessed on the original invoice amount, plus interest of 0.06% per day. A separate extraterritorial violation penalty of up to €3,200 may be imposed for failure to keep transport documents, and deliberate tax evasion is criminally liable under the Estonian Penal Code (fines, corrective punishment, or imprisonment up to 5 years). Reclaimed input VAT is concurrently clawed back and an increased tax surcharge of 30% may apply in cases of incorrect declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general exchange-control regime. Current-account payments and capital transfers are generally unrestricted, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, bank screening, and applicable reporting obligations. There is no general requirement for prior central-bank approval to convert currency, receive foreign investment, remit dividends, or repay legitimate cross-border debt. Banks may require evidence of source of funds, transaction purpose, underlying contracts, tax compliance, and beneficial ownership. Dividend distributions by an Estonian OÜ are subject to Estonian corporate income tax when distributed; retained profits are generally not taxed at entity level, but the tax treatment and rate in force at distribution must be confirmed. Shareholder-level taxation and withholding obligations depend on recipient status, treaty entitlement, and applicable law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed conversion deadline. Export proceeds may generally be retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual terms, sanctions, accounting, tax, and bank AML requirements. Exporters should retain customs and transport records, customer contracts, invoices, proof of delivery, and bank receipt evidence. EU statistical reporting, including Intrastat where applicable, is separate from foreign-exchange surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (the Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. The Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU), under the Ministry of Finance, is the principal financial intelligence authority for anti-money-laundering matters; the Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority supervises relevant regulated financial-sector institutions.

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High
