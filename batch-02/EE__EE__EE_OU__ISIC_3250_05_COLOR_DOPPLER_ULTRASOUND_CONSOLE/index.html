Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); e-MTA digital portal (https://emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

Incorporate the OÜ in the Commercial Register (Äriregister, e-business portal) to obtain the national registry/TIN code (Äriregister code) automatically upon entry. Subsequently, register as a taxable person for VAT by filing Form KÄM-1 via e-MTA; the MTA issues an EE VAT identification number. Non-resident foreign legal entities without a resident seat must submit KÄM-1 with constitutional/licence documents and may be required to appoint a tax representative; the MTA assigns a non-resident tax identification code for interaction in the tax register.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (resident OÜ is a separate legal taxpayer subject to Estonian corporate income tax exclusively on profit distributions, deemed fringe-benefit transactions, gifts, and transfer-pricing recharacterisations under the Territorial Scope model: undistributed active income is untaxed at the entity level; no US-style pass-through classification exists for an OÜ). — Incorporate the OÜ in the Commercial Register (Äriregister, e-business portal) to obtain the national registry/TIN code (Äriregister code) automatically upon entry. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident OÜ is a separate legal taxpayer subject to Estonian corporate income tax exclusively on profit distributions, deemed fringe-benefit transactions, gifts, and transfer-pricing recharacterisations under the Territorial Scope model: undistributed active income is untaxed at the entity level; no US-style pass-through classification exists for an OÜ).

Available election forms

No OECD-style check-the-box; classification follows the Commercial Code and the Taxation Act (TSA). Elective mechanisms include: (i) dividend classification election between regular distributions (14% rate on gross, applied as 14/86 of the net amount) and irregular/liquidation distributions (20%/25% gross rate) under TSA § 50; (ii) hybrid-mismatch transparency alignment for foreign hybrid entities under ATAD (TSA § 50(6)-(9)); (iii) foreign permanent establishment election to apply the exemption method under TSA § 54; (iv) CFC substantive-activity/attribution elections under TSA § 55.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year (excl. VAT) under VAT Act § 3(1) for resident/established taxable persons; non-resident suppliers with no fixed establishment in Estonia are registrable from the first taxable supply (no threshold) unless the reverse-charge mechanism or EU distance-selling rules assign liability to the recipient.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exported goods (including the color-Doppler ultrasound console) when the goods are dispatched by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf to an address outside the EU, title has passed to the non-resident customer, and the supplier possesses a customs export declaration released by an Estonian or EU customs office evidencing physical exit from the EU; the export may be evidenced within the statutory 90-day confirmation window. The zero-rating also covers directly related transport and ancillary services; a distinct 0% regime applies to intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers (VRN validation required).

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX1/SAD with MRN and customs release date)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing EU exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit advice or LC settlement document evidencing receipt of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and medical-device export compliance documentation
  • Certificate of Origin (EUR.1 / REX statement) where preferential tariff treatment is claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; VAT is accounted for through periodic electronic filing of the KMD VAT return and KMDINF annexes via e-MTA by the 20th day following the reporting month, using e-MTA web/API (XML) channels. Public-sector (B2G) e-invoicing is mandatory under the e-invoice framework (EN 16931/UBL formats); private B2B e-invoicing is optional. Intra-Community recapitulative statements (VAT Information Exchange System) must be filed with export invoices.

The MTA may re-assess the supply at 22% VAT plus default interest (under TSA § 92, Eesti Pank refinancing rate plus 6 percentage points; approximately 0.06% per day under the applicable rate schedule) if export evidence is missing. Administrative fines up to €1,200 for inaccurate VAT declarations; criminal tax fraud penalties under Penal Code § 154 include fines of up to €2,000,000 for legal persons and imprisonment up to 5 years for responsible individuals; input-VAT refunds obtained on false export claims are clawed back with interest, and the taxable person may lose the right to zero-rating for a prospective control period. The general audit limitation period is 3 years, extended to 7 years for fraudulent conduct. Note: Estonian law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is a pipeline-schema benchmark and not a tax-law clawback trigger.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and has no general exchange controls, capital-account controls, profit-repatriation restrictions, or mandatory central-bank approval for ordinary cross-border corporate payments. Current-account and capital-account transactions are generally freely transferable within the European Union and internationally, subject to bank AML, sanctions, tax, accounting, payment-services, and documentary controls. An Estonian OÜ may remit dividends, interest, royalties, trade payments, and shareholder-loan repayments if legally distributable, properly documented, and compliant with Estonian corporate law, transfer-pricing requirements, withholding-tax rules, beneficial-ownership verification, and applicable anti-abuse provisions. Dividend distributions generally require distributable equity and shareholder approval; Estonia generally imposes corporate income tax when profits are distributed rather than when earned. Banks may request contracts, invoices, board or shareholder resolutions, tax evidence, source-of-funds records, and commercial rationale before executing higher-risk or unusual transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed deadline requiring an exporter to convert foreign-currency receipts into euros or transfer them to an Estonian bank. Export receipts may generally remain in the invoiced currency or be held in foreign-currency accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, AML, customs, and contractual requirements. Banks may impose operational cut-off, documentary, currency-conversion, or transaction-monitoring requirements under their customer agreements.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia's central bank and participates in Eurosystem monetary operations. Banking supervision is conducted by the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon), while customer due diligence and suspicious-transaction controls are also administered under the supervision of the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR collections and payments, subject to provider risk appetite. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide services in Estonia through passporting or a local establishment, but the company must verify the provider's authorization, permitted services, safeguarding arrangements, geographic coverage, transaction limits, and sanctions and AML controls. An offshore non-EEA digital EMI cannot provide regulated payment or e-money services in Estonia merely because it operates online; it generally requires an applicable EU authorization, local authorization, agent arrangement, or use of a properly authorized intermediary. Fintech accounts should not be assumed to replace a domestic bank for capital contributions, bank guarantees, commercial letters of credit, regulated medical-device settlement, cash management, or high-value international trade. E-residency is an identification and administrative-access program, not a banking license or a guarantee of account approval.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution from 1 January 2025; retained and reinvested profits are not subject to corporate income tax until distribution.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian resident company to a non-resident corporate shareholder. The Estonian company instead pays corporate income tax of 22/78 on the grossed-up distribution. Dividends paid to resident individuals are subject to the rules applicable from 2025, including the general 22% corporate distribution tax system and the abolition of the former regular 14% distribution regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory detailed transfer-pricing documentation generally applies to qualifying large enterprises and groups, including an Estonian company with at least 250 employees, annual turnover of at least EUR 50 million or balance-sheet total of at least EUR 43 million, and to specified transactions or relationships covered by Estonian transfer-pricing rules. Master-file obligations may apply to entities belonging to qualifying large multinational groups, including groups within the Country-by-Country Reporting scope, generally using the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold. Smaller entities may still be required to demonstrate arm's-length pricing when challenged by the tax authority.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board may challenge arrangements under general anti-avoidance, beneficial-ownership, substance-over-form and transfer-pricing principles. The OÜ should maintain genuine commercial activity, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, decision-making in Estonia where represented as local management, suitable premises or operational infrastructure, independent bank-account controls, contemporaneous contracts, real risk assumption and evidence that transactions benefit the Estonian business. EU ATAD measures, including controlled-foreign-company and interest-limitation rules, may apply to relevant structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment, statutory late-payment interest, administrative fines, recovery of unpaid tax, seizure or enforcement against assets and possible criminal prosecution. Serious intentional tax evasion may result in a fine or imprisonment, with enhanced consequences for aggravated or large-scale conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability for intentional evasion, personal tax liability in legally defined cases involving deliberate breach of tax obligations, civil restitution, disqualification or removal consequences, asset seizure and travel restrictions where ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings. The OÜ's limited liability does not protect directors from their own unlawful acts.
Undeclared profit distribution and prohibited shareholder extraction

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax on the deemed distribution, repayment or recovery of unlawfully extracted assets, accounting and management-board liability, interest and possible administrative or criminal sanctions where concealment or fraud is involved.

Director personal liability: Management-board members may be jointly or personally liable for unlawful distributions, creditor damage and restitution. A foreign shareholder may also be exposed if it knowingly receives an unlawful distribution or participates in abuse.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and related-party abuse

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length profit, additional corporate income tax when the adjustment represents a distribution or non-business expense, withholding-tax assessment, interest, documentation penalties and possible double-tax exposure if the counterparty jurisdiction does not grant corresponding relief.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for negligent or intentional damage to the company or creditors. Personal criminal exposure arises where transfer-pricing manipulation is part of deliberate tax fraud, false accounting or asset concealment.
VAT fraud and customs evasion

Statutory sanction: Denial of input VAT, repayment of incorrectly refunded VAT, tax assessment, interest, penalties, customs debt, seizure and criminal prosecution for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face criminal liability, asset confiscation, civil restitution and restrictions on business activity. Limited liability does not shield a person who knowingly participates in VAT or customs fraud.
Shell company, beneficial ownership and substance failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of payments, tax reassessment, withholding-tax liability, beneficial-owner and registry penalties, bank-account restrictions, enhanced due diligence and possible account termination.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil liability, administrative sanctions and criminal liability for false declarations, money laundering or tax fraud. Travel bans, asset freezing and imprisonment may be ordered in criminal proceedings where statutory conditions are met.
Anti-money-laundering and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting obligations, transaction blocking, account freezing, administrative fines, confiscation and criminal prosecution. Corporate penalties may reach substantial statutory amounts depending on the offense and applicable AML or sanctions legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners may face personal administrative or criminal liability, civil confiscation, restitution, asset freezing, travel bans and imprisonment for intentional or grossly negligent participation.
Medical-device regulatory and export-control non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Product withdrawal or recall, prohibition on placing products on the market, administrative fines, customs detention, loss of market access and civil or criminal liability for unsafe or unlawfully marketed devices.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, regulatory disqualification and criminal liability where deliberate or negligent conduct causes injury, unsafe products, customs fraud or sanctions breaches. Corporate status does not exclude personal liability for direct misconduct.
Accounting, annual-report and corporate-record failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, compulsory filing orders, estimated assessment, loss of good standing, enforcement proceedings and potential criminal sanctions for intentional false accounting or obstruction.

Director personal liability: Management-board members may be personally liable for damage caused by breach of statutory duties, creditor prejudice and false reporting. Courts may impose disqualification, asset-related measures, travel restrictions or imprisonment where criminal conduct is established.
