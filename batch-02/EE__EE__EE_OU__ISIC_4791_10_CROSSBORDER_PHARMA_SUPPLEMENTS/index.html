Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Commercial Code sets a statutory minimum share capital of EUR 2,500 for an OÜ. No escrow or prior bank deposit is mandatory; capital may be monetary or non-cash and may be contributed after registration. If the paid-up capital is below the statutory minimum, the company must increase it within a statutory period. Distribution of profits is prohibited unless net assets exceed share capital plus statutory reserves per § 176(1).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate or a written explanatory assessment by the local rescue board (Päästeamet); structural load and electrical safety report by a certified expert; occupational health risk assessment and workers' registration with the Labour Inspectorate (Tööinspektsioon); premises use notification to the local municipality under planning and construction law requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); electronic tax services are provided through the e-MTA portal.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ is incorporated in the Estonian Commercial Register and receives a registry code, which is used as its general tax identification number. To obtain an Estonian VAT identification number, the company applies to the MTA through e-MTA or in writing, stating its commencement of taxable activity and supplying requested supporting information. The MTA generally decides a complete application within five working days. A non-resident taxable person making supplies for which Estonia is the place of supply must register before making those supplies; the domestic small-business threshold generally does not protect a non-established taxable person. An Estonian VAT number has the format EE followed by the company’s registry code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An OÜ is not eligible for a US-style check-the-box election or a general pass-through election. Estonia generally imposes corporate income tax when profits are distributed or treated as distributed; retained and reinvested profits are not subject to ordinary corporate income tax. Distributed profits are generally taxed at 22/78 of the net distribution from 2025. Estonian law does not provide a general hybrid-mismatch election that changes an OÜ’s entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of relevant taxable supply in Estonia in a calendar year for an Estonia-established taxable person. Special rules apply to non-established persons, intra-Community acquisitions, and cross-border EU small-enterprise relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Estonia or another EU Member State to a destination outside the EU may generally be zero-rated where the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier retains reliable evidence that the goods left the EU. Intra-Community supplies may qualify for zero-rating subject to the customer’s valid VAT identification number, reporting, and transport evidence. Cross-border services are governed principally by place-of-supply rules: qualifying B2B services supplied to a customer outside Estonia are generally outside Estonian VAT rather than automatically treated as zero-rated exports. Domestic sales of dietary supplements are generally taxable at the applicable rate; product classification and any applicable regulatory requirements must be assessed separately.

  • Export customs declaration and evidence of release for export and exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, supplier, customer, and transaction
  • Bill of lading, carrier confirmation, or other independent transport evidence linking the goods to their destination outside the EU
  • Customer order, contract, and delivery records supporting the transaction
  • For intra-Community zero-rated supplies: customer VAT identification number validated through VIES, transport evidence, and required recapitulative reporting
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a general Estonian statutory requirement; retain payment and accounting records where relevant

Estonia does not operate general real-time VAT invoice clearance, fiscal stamping, or a mandatory API pre-clearance system. VAT returns are generally filed electronically through e-MTA, ordinarily by the 20th day of the following month. Under Estonian accounting rules, business recipients may have a right to require structured e-invoices in specified circumstances; this is not a tax-authority clearance regime.

Estonia has no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline or requirement to repatriate export proceeds. Unsupported or incorrectly reported zero-rating may result in reassessment of VAT, statutory interest, and applicable administrative or criminal penalties. The taxpayer must substantiate the statutory conditions and retain records for the applicable retention period; deliberate falsification or tax fraud may attract more serious sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility within the EU/EEA. Estonia abandoned the kroon currency board on euro adoption (1 January 2011); there is no exchange-control statute, no capital-account permit regime and no CFIUS-type FDI screening applicable to nutraceutical e-commerce. Current-account payments (trade, dividends, royalties) are unrestricted under TFEU Article 63; capital movements are equally liberalised. Practical hurdles: (i) bank proprietary AML de-risking, not a legal restriction; (ii) statutory pre-market notification of food supplements to the Veterinary and Food Board (VTA) and registration of the e-commerce information-society service with the Consumer Protection and Technical Regulatory Authority (TTJA); (iii) profit repatriation is taxed under Estonia's distribution-based CIT — 20% of net distributions generally, a 20/25% gross-up rate for fringe and deemed benefits, and a 14% concessionary rate for regular dividends satisfying the 25% distribution test; no withholding tax applies to non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonian law imposes no surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion into euro, no repatriation deadline and no requirement to hold foreign currency with an Estonian bank. The exporter must retain export evidence (customs declarations, invoices, CMR/BOL) for the seven-year tax-limitation period. Distance sales to EU consumers require EU OSS registration; imports of goods below EUR 150 from non-EU countries require IOSS registration and import-VAT payment. FX translation gains and losses are recognised in IFRS/EE GAAP accounts but remain CIT-neutral until a distribution event.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) — member of the Eurosystem. Ultimate monetary and FX authority is delegated to the European Central Bank; prudential and AML/CFT supervision of Estonian credit institutions rests with Finantsinspektionen (Estonian Financial Supervisory Authority), while the Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo) operates within the Police and Border Guard Board. Eesti Pank's Payment Systems Oversight Division oversees SEPA, TARGET2 and card-scheme clearing infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as a primary treasury, MEDIUM as a secondary rail. EU-regulated EMIs (Paysera LT, Mistertango LT, Verifo LT, Unlimint LT/CY, Wise Business BE/IE) classify dietary supplements as a restricted category: most prohibit medicinal-claim products and any multilevel-marketing or distributor structure, and often impose rolling reserves or surcharges for high-risk MCC codes. Legal constraints: EMI e-money is not covered by the Estonian Deposit Guarantee Fund (Hoiuste Tagamise Seadus) or the Deposit Guarantee Scheme Act; EMIs may not lend or provide credit; card-scheme rules may block high-risk supplement merchants entirely. Recommended architecture: keep the primary treasury account with a Tier-1 bank (LHV or Swedbank) and use an EMI only for low-value B2C collection from non-EU customers or contractor expense cards.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions, triggering mandatory Annex 8 disclosure to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board; a full OECD-aligned Local File and Master File must be contemporaneously prepared if aggregate related-party transactions exceed EUR 250,000, and must be available within 30 days upon request

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a standalone Economic Substances Regulations regime analogous to offshore financial centers; however, the OECD/G20 BEPS minimum standards, EU ATAD CFC rules, and the Estonian general anti-abuse principle (substance over form) require that the OÜ demonstrate real economic activity, including local management, operational office, payroll, and decision-making in Estonia. A pure 'shell' or e-Residency-only company without substantive presence risks the denial of tax residency benefits, reattribution of profits to a foreign parent via transfer pricing, and disqualification from treaty relief, leading to immediate tax reclassification and penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud conviction punishable by a fine and imprisonment of up to 5 years; compulsory repayment of undeclared taxes plus penalty interest at 0.06% per day, and a penalty of 50% of the unpaid tax; revocation of the OÜ's operating licenses, including the special license for dietary supplement retail sales

Director personal liability: The management board may be personally jointly liable for all tax arrears if taxes are not paid due to gross negligence or intentional breach of fiduciary duties; directors can face asset seizure, a travel ban during criminal proceedings, and imprisonment for up to 8 years in aggravated cases
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment increasing the taxable base to the arm's length price, coupled with penalty interest and a 50% transfer-pricing penalty; in serious cases, a fine and imprisonment for tax evasion if intent is proven; the TP documentation may be disregarded for audit credibility

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or sign manipulative intercompany contracts may be held civilly liable for the resulting tax shortfall; criminal conviction triggers disqualification as a director and a ban from entering any EEA/EU jurisdiction under a European Arrest Warrant for serious fraud cases
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of Estonian tax residency if the place of effective management is found to be abroad, subjecting the entity to reclassification as a foreign company and immediate deemed dividend tax on retained profits; fines up to EUR 20,000; the tax authorities may also pierce the corporate veil to levy tax on the actual beneficial owner at a 25% rate

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for the unpaid tax of the shell entity under the Commercial Code § 187; travel bans may be imposed to secure criminal tax prosecution; custodial sentences of 1 to 7 years may be ordered for misrepresenting the substance and location of management
AML / High-Risk Payment Processing Violations

Statutory sanction: Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit fines up to EUR 500,000 per violation; forced closure of the merchant account; revocation of the OÜ's registration as a taxable person; criminal anti-money laundering penalties include imprisonment of up to 7 years and forfeiture of illicit proceeds; the e-Residency digital authorization may be revoked permanently

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act if they fail to appoint a compliance officer and implement internal controls; they may face criminal conviction with imprisonment, a travel ban pending trial, and civil restitution to third-party victims of the laundering scheme
