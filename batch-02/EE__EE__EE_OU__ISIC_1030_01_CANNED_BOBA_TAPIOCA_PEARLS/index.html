Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, 'MTA'); online taxpayer portal: e-MTA at emta.ee; company registration via the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) at ariregister.rik.ee; food-business activity licensing by the Veterinary and Food Board (Veterinaar- ja Toiduamet) is a statutory precondition for canned-boba production.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Non-resident founders (including e-Residents holding an e-Residency digital ID) file an incorporation notice in the e-Business Register; the Commercial Register immediately assigns the OÜ's 8-digit registry code, which is the statutory legal-entity tax identification number (TIN). Step 2: Where taxable turnover will exceed EUR 40,000 in a 12-month period (resident OÜ) or where the non-resident supplier makes taxable supplies with place of supply in Estonia (no threshold), file Form K ('VAT registration') in e-MTA; the MTA issues the national VAT number (KMKR) within 5 business days. Step 3: If the OÜ will pay salaries or management fees, register as an employer with the MTA via e-MTA (Form A); intercompany payment flows (Swift LC/TT rails) must be supported by commercial contracts and are reported through MTA statistical declarations. Step 4: Obtain the Veterinary and Food Board activity licence; all licences and permits are linked to the registry code.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based corporate income tax model): an Estonian-resident OÜ is taxed only on distributions and deemed distributions. Annual retained profits bear 0% corporate income tax; ordinary dividend distributions are taxed at 20/80 of the net amount (effective 20% of gross); the 14/86 rate (effective 14%) applies to 'regular' dividends paid up to the average amount of dividends distributed in the three immediately preceding years; dividends received from qualifying EU/EEA/Swiss participations (at least 10% holding held for at least 12 months) are exempt under the participation exemption. Territorial scope: the OÜ is resident in Estonia; income is taxed on a worldwide basis but only upon distribution, producing a cash-flow deferral advantage. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based corporate income tax model): an Estonian-resident OÜ is taxed only on distributions and deemed distributions. Annual retained profits bear 0% corporate income tax; ordinary dividend distributions are taxed at 20/80 of the net amount (effective 20% of gross); the 14/86 rate (effective 14%) applies to 'regular' dividends paid up to the average amount of dividends distributed in the three immediately preceding years; dividends received from qualifying EU/EEA/Swiss participations (at least 10% holding held for at least 12 months) are exempt under the participation exemption. Territorial scope: the OÜ is resident in Estonia; income is taxed on a worldwide basis but only upon distribution, producing a cash-flow deferral advantage. No sub-state tax distinctions exist within unitary Estonia.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or pass-through election is available; every OÜ is automatically a corporate taxpayer under the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus, TuMS). Available elections and management mechanisms include: (i) dividend timing and regularity planning to access the 14/86 reduced rate; (ii) voluntary VAT registration before the EUR 40,000 threshold to recover input VAT; (iii) designation of an Estonian permanent establishment (PE) by a foreign parent, with PE profits taxed upon remittance at 20/80 and foreign-tax credit relief; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD/ATAD2 (TuMS section 50 cube) governing hybrid financial instruments, deductible payments to hybrid entities, reverse hybrids, and imported mismatches; (v) thin-capitalisation and transfer-pricing documentation obligations for related-party financing and cross-border service flows.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 aggregate taxable turnover of supplies in Estonia by a resident taxable person during any 12-month period. Non-resident suppliers are subject to mandatory registration without any turnover threshold whenever the place of supply of their supplies is in Estonia. Intra-EU distance sales of goods by a resident supplier up to the EUR 10,000 annual cross-border threshold continue to be taxed in Estonia under the OSS regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) export supplies of canned boba tapioca pearls physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU, provided the OÜ (or its logistics agent) holds customs export evidence (IE-599/SAD) proving actual removal from the EU; (ii) intra-Community supplies to a buyer registered for VAT in another member state, evidenced by a valid EU VAT number (VIES), movement of goods to the other member state within 3 months of supply, and inclusion in the KMD INF recapitulative statement; (iii) goods placed under customs warehousing, transit, or free-zone procedures; and (iv) ancillary supply-chain services (freight, handling, inspection) supplied to non-residents where place-of-supply rules so provide. The complete export evidence bundle must be in the OÜ's possession no later than the filing date of the VAT return for the relevant period; otherwise the standard 22% rate is assessable.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX) and MTA customs receipt (IE-599 / export confirmation) proving the goods physically left the EU from Estonia
  • Original or on-board Bill of Lading (sea freight), CMR waybill (road), or air waybill showing the Estonian loading point and the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and the VAT sales invoice, cross-referenced to the customs export declaration number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment confirmation evidencing receipt of sale proceeds from the foreign buyer in the OÜ's euro-denominated bank account (LC or TT payment rails)
  • Copy of the buyer's foreign VAT registration certificate or VIES validation output, and, where a third-party logistics operator or buyer-collects arrangement is used, a power of attorney or dispatcher contract

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping mandate exists in Estonia. B2G invoices must be transmitted as structured e-invoices (EN 16931) to public-sector clients via the MTA e-invoice channel; B2B e-invoicing is optional but XML/EDI formats are accepted. A VAT invoice must be issued no later than 7 calendar days after the supply; the supplier must retain the original XML/EDI audit trail and all customs and transport documentation for at least 7 years. Periodic compliance: monthly or quarterly VAT return (Form KMD) plus recapitulative statements (KMD INF, CSL, Intrastat, ECS) are filed electronically via e-MTA; no transaction-level pre-clearance is required before issuing an export invoice.

The MTA may retrospectively reassess a zero-rated export at 22% VAT (with statutory late-payment interest of 0.06% per calendar day from the original due date) if the export evidence is incomplete, the goods did not in fact leave the EU, or sale proceeds are not received from the foreign buyer within a reasonable period; 180 days is the administrative benchmark before enhanced audit scrutiny. The standard limitation period for reassessment is 3 years from the end of the relevant tax period, extended to 6 years in cases of fraud or gross negligence under the Tax and Tax Procedure Act. Administrative fines for false or missing export declarations are applied per the statutory fine-unit scale (one fine unit = EUR 4); aggravated criminal tax fraud under Penal Code sections 344-1 and 344-2 carries imprisonment of up to 7 years (natural persons) and monetary penalties against the legal entity under section 44 of the Penal Code, together with confiscation of the proceeds of fraudulent export schemes, suspension or revocation of the OÜ's VAT registration, and loss of export facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro and applies EU rules on free movement of capital and payments. There are no general exchange controls or restrictions on converting or transferring euro or foreign-currency funds for current or capital transactions. Banks may apply sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering checks, transaction monitoring, and documentation requirements. Dividends and other lawful distributions may be paid to non-resident owners without a foreign-exchange approval, subject to corporate-law requirements, tax settlement, accurate accounting, and any applicable withholding or reporting obligations. Estonia generally taxes corporate profits when distributed rather than when earned; the applicable distribution-tax rate and treatment should be confirmed for the distribution date and transaction structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no general statutory deadline requiring exporters to repatriate export proceeds and no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio. Export receipts may be retained in euros or another currency, subject to contractual terms, bank compliance checks, sanctions restrictions, accounting requirements, and applicable tax and customs rules.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s national central bank and a member of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank conducts euro-area monetary policy. Estonia has no separate foreign-exchange administration or general foreign-exchange licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for routine euro payments when the provider is an appropriately authorised EU or EEA payment institution or electronic money institution and provides services in Estonia under applicable passporting or local authorisation rules. An EMI account is not necessarily a bank deposit, may not provide credit or escrow services, and may not support every capital contribution, letter of credit, or trade-finance need. Offshore non-EU providers require verification of authorisation, service permissions, safeguarding arrangements, sanctions controls, and acceptance by counterparties. Maintain a regulated bank account for statutory, capital, and trade-finance functions; confirm each provider’s permitted account use and deposit-protection status.

Maintain a regulated bank account for statutory, capital, and trade-finance functions; confirm each provider’s permitted account use and deposit-protection status.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1,000,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions with related parties

Economic substance scope: Estonia imposes no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, a company must demonstrate real economic substance, including actual management and decision-making in Estonia, to substantiate tax residency and entitlement to 0% taxation on retained earnings and treaty benefits. Arrangements lacking substance may be challenged under general anti-avoidance rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Estonian Penal Code, intentional tax evasion is punishable by a fine of up to EUR 1.6 million for legal persons and imprisonment of up to three years for responsible natural persons; additional criminal forfeiture of evaded amounts and interest

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax if they knowingly participated in the evasion; travel bans and prohibition on business may be imposed, and imprisonment up to five years for aggravated cases
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the entity as a tax-transparent company or foreign-controlled permanent establishment, resulting in imposition of 20% income tax on undistributed profits and back taxes; administrative fines up to EUR 32,000

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for misrepresentation, as well as disqualification from directorship; in cross-border schemes, criminal charges for aiding tax fraud and potential extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties
Money Laundering and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, penalties for legal persons reach up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover, plus revocation of the operating licence

Director personal liability: Directors and key personnel face criminal imprisonment up to five years for wilful AML breaches; civil liability for resulting damages and confiscation of assets
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the unwithheld amount; late remittance triggers a penalty of 30% of the tax and a default interest charge of 0.06% per day

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct non-withholding are personally liable for the tax shortfall and may be barred from serving as directors for up to three years; potential criminal sanctions for fraudulent omissions
