Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the competent local district court (Amtsgericht) for the company's registered office.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG), particularly sections 2 (formation and notarization), 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft), 7–8 (registration and application), and 10 (registration particulars).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized application for entry in the Commercial Register (Anmeldung zum Handelsregister), accompanied by the notarized articles of association or the statutory model protocol and required statutory declarations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG may be formed with share capital below EUR 25,000, including EUR 1. The full cash contribution must be paid before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited at formation. No statutory escrow account is required, but the managing director must substantiate payment to the notary or registry as requested. The company must allocate 25% of annual net profit, after loss carryforwards, to the statutory reserve until the reserve and share capital reach EUR 25,000.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial fire-safety or structural-load clearance is ordinarily required for a software office beyond applicable building-use and workplace requirements. The employer must comply with occupational health and safety duties, including workplace risk assessment and required fire-protection arrangements; additional building or fire approvals depend on the premises and local rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF) as policy authority; operational competence vested in the local Finanzamt (Tax Office) responsible for the taxpayer's registered seat/permanent establishment and, for VAT identification numbers and central administration of foreign entities, the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt). Electronic filing portal: ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at www.elster.de; BZSt online portal for USt-IdNr. applications at www.bzst.de.

E-government filing portal

Foreign and domestic entrepreneurs must file the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (Questionnaire for tax registration) with the competent local Finanzamt, either via ELSTER or the prescribed paper form, within one month of commencing business. The Finanzamt assigns a statutory local tax number (Steuernummer) for income tax, corporate tax, VAT and trade tax purposes. For cross-border transactions, a separate VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) is applied for through the BZSt, using the 'USt 1 TB' form or an electronic ELSTER/BZSt application; the VAT ID is issued upon verification of identity and the prospective taxable activity. Foreign entities with only limited German tax nexus apply to the BZSt (or the competent Finanzamt designated for non-residents) under § 27 AO and § 18 UStG.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A German Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) (UG) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) and a body corporate under § 1 GmbHG and § 7 KStG. It is a separate taxpayer liable for Körperschaftsteuer (15%) plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax) plus municipal Gewerbesteuer. It is not transparent and cannot elect pass-through taxation. Territorial scope: resident corporations (seat or place of management in Germany) are subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability (Welteinkommensprinzip), with relief for foreign-source income granted through tax treaties and unilateral measures; non-resident shareholders of the UG are, in principle, not taxed at the German entity level on non-German dividends, but German withholding tax applies on dividends distributed. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A German Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) (UG) is a capital company (Kapitalgesellschaft) and a body corporate under § 1 GmbHG and § 7 KStG. It is a separate taxpayer liable for Körperschaftsteuer (15%) plus solidarity surcharge (5.5% of corporate tax) plus municipal Gewerbesteuer. It is not transparent and cannot elect pass-through taxation. Territorial scope: resident corporations (seat or place of management in Germany) are subject to unlimited worldwide tax liability (Welteinkommensprinzip), with relief for foreign-source income granted through tax treaties and unilateral measures; non-resident shareholders of the UG are, in principle, not taxed at the German entity level on non-German dividends, but German withholding tax applies on dividends distributed. A UG is not a domestic tax-exempt entity; no tax-exempt zone treatment applies at the federal level.

Available election forms

Germany does not operate a 'check-the-box' classification regime. Relevant elections and special regimes include: (i) Bildung eines ertragsteuerlichen Organschafts (fiscal unity) under §§ 14, 17 KStG and §§ 2, 3 GewStG, allowing a German controlling entity to attribute the UG's profit/loss for corporate and trade tax purposes if certain profit-and-loss transfer and financial integration requirements are met; (ii) small business VAT election under § 19 UStG for resident entrepreneurs with turnover below €25,000 (from 2025), waiving VAT; (iii) one-stop-shop (OSS) and non-union (import one-stop-shop IOSS) elections for cross-border supplies of digital services; (iv) anti-hybrid election-adjacent planning under § 4k EStG (ATAD 2) which disallows deduction of payments in hybrid mismatch arrangements, with documentation duties; (v) transfer-pricing documentation elections and Advance Pricing Agreements; and (vi) group VAT treatment does not exist in Germany save for Organschaft for VAT purposes under § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG, which is a mandatory rule, not an elective one, once the criteria are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold applies for non-resident entrepreneurs providing taxable supplies in Germany; any foreign enterprise supplying goods or services in Germany must register for VAT regardless of turnover. For resident small businesses, the § 19 UStG exemption and de-registration threshold apply up to €25,000 (from 1 January 2025; previously €22,000). For EU-wide intra-Community distance sales of goods and cross-border B2C electronically supplied services, the pan-EU threshold is €10,000 total annual value; above this threshold the German supplier must charge German VAT on German customers or register under OSS. For VPN-network and privacy-software SaaS supplied to German consumers, the place of supply is Germany (customer location under § 3a UStG), and the foreign/domestic supplier must register and charge 19% VAT unless covered by OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) export deliveries of goods to third-country territories under § 6 UStG when the goods physically leave the EU and the exporter (VAT-registered supplier) retains documentary proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis); (ii) services performed directly in relation to exported goods (§ 4 Nr. 1 Buchst. b UStG); (iii) cross-border B2B services supplied to a non-EU recipient whose place of business is outside the EU, where the place of supply is located outside Germany pursuant to § 3a UStG; (iv) digital services (VPN/privacy software) supplied to non-EU consumers outside the EU, which are outside the scope of German VAT if the customer's residence/usual place of abode is outside the EU; and (v) supplies subject to reverse-charge in the customer's country. For B2B digital services to EU customers, the German supplier applies the reverse-charge mechanism if the customer is VAT-registered in another Member State, with the invoice stating 'reverse charge'; for B2C digital services to EU consumers, the supplier charges VAT of the customer's country and may use OSS.

  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing dispatch or transport and leaving the EU territory

Germany mandates electronic invoicing for domestic B2B supplies for all German-established enterprises from 1 January 2025: invoices must be transmitted in a structured electronic format compliant with EN 16931 (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD), and taxpayers must be able to receive such invoices. A full mandatory acceptance obligation for all taxpayers is phased in by 2027-2028, with a domestic real-time clearance and digital stamping regime not yet fully operational; the current requirement is structured e-invoicing without per-transaction fiscalization. For cash-register receipts, the KassenNachV (Cash Register Accounting Ordinance) mandates a certified Technical Security Element (TSE) with digital signature/stamping for every taxable point-of-sale transaction, and cloud-based TSE solutions must integrate via approved APIs. Foreign suppliers invoicing German customers must comply with the German invoice-content rules under § 14 UStG, and where they use OSS, the OSS return constitutes the fiscal declaration and clearance mechanism; no separate German e-invoice clearance API is required for OSS registrants.

The Finanzamt and customs authorities conduct regular export-VAT audits with heightened scrutiny on digital-export zero-rating claims. Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessments at 19% plus statutory interest of 0.5% per month (§ 233a AO), late-payment surcharges under § 240 AO, and, where intent or gross negligence is established, penalties for tax evasion under § 370 AO of up to five years' imprisonment or fines; attempted negligent underdeclaration is punishable as tax shortfall under § 378 AO. Repeated violations additionally give rise to denial of zero-rating, revocation of VAT IDs and OSS registrations, EU OLAF cross-border fraud investigations, and joint and several liability of the UG's managing directors under § 69 AO. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day window and unsupported by valid FX documentation will be treated as unsubstantiated and the underlying supply reclassified as taxable within Germany, with VAT clawback and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is a fully convertible currency. Germany maintains no exchange controls on capital or current account transactions. Profit remittance from a German UG is unrestricted by FX regulation, but outbound dividends are subject to German withholding tax at a 25% statutory rate (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, and church tax where applicable), reducible under bilateral double tax treaties or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (inbound holding structures) upon presentation of appropriate tax clearance certificates. Capital accounts are freely disposable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany does not impose any statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters may retain all proceeds in EUR or any other currency, subject to balance-of-payments reporting requirements to the Deutsche Bundesbank when amounts exceed EUR 12,500 per transaction or as otherwise required by the reporting regulation.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Deutsche Bundesbank) acting as Germany's central bank and competent foreign exchange administration; operative framework governed by German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and EC Treaty provisions on free movement of capital

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A foreign EMIs operating cross-border into Germany must be either BaFin-licensed (ZAG) or EEA-passported and properly registered with BaFin. EMI accounts do not provide full SCOPE of corporate banking products such as commercial letters of credit or surplus collateral management; they remain viable for receivables collection and low-risk disbursements but are not a substitute for a Tier-1 bank relationship given the VPN/network security sector's enhanced due diligence profile.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
KSt 1, Lines 1-3 (Identification) Enter tax number (Steuernummer), legal form (UG haftungsbeschränkt), registered address, date of incorporation and fiscal year-end. Non-resident directors must disclose the UG's German tax number, the foreign residence of the director and whether the UG is managed within Germany; Form KSt 1 S is required for non-resident corporate shareholders with German-sourced income. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag): 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum EUR 25,000 or 10% of the assessed tax; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag): 1% per commenced month; assessment interest: 6% per annum from 15 months after the end of the assessment year; forced fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000; automatic estimation of profits.
KSt 1, E-Bilanz taxonomy (electronic balance sheet) Transmit the statutory balance sheet and profit-and-loss statement using the German XBRL taxonomy (Taxonomie 6.x). VPN app development costs and acquired customer contracts must be capitalised as intangible assets; deferred revenue from Stripe/Paymentwall subscriptions must be recognised separately. Foreign accounting standards must be reconciled to German HGB tax bases; the E-Bilanz remains mandatory even if the UG prepares its accounts abroad. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag): 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum EUR 25,000 or 10% of the assessed tax; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag): 1% per commenced month; assessment interest: 6% per annum from 15 months after the end of the assessment year; forced fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000; automatic estimation of profits.
KSt 1, Lines 68-70 (Taxable income and rate) Report taxable income after non-deductible expenses (e.g. 25% of entertainment costs, fines, VAT penalties) and apply the 15.0% corporate income tax rate; solidarity surcharge is calculated at 5.5% of the corporate income tax. Where a foreign permanent establishment exists (e.g. offshore engineering team), treaty-exempt or treaty-imputed income must be excluded or credited using the corresponding schedules. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag): 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum EUR 25,000 or 10% of the assessed tax; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag): 1% per commenced month; assessment interest: 6% per annum from 15 months after the end of the assessment year; forced fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000; automatic estimation of profits.
KSt 1, Lines 72-73 (Loss utilisation) Apply loss carryback and loss carryforward (Verlustrücktrag and Verlustvortrag); annual utilisation is capped at EUR 1 million plus 60% of the excess under the minimum taxation rule. Losses of the UG remain trapped at entity level and cannot be attributed to non-resident shareholders; shareholders must declare the UG's positive results within CFC structures if the UG is used as a conduit. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag): 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum EUR 25,000 or 10% of the assessed tax; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag): 1% per commenced month; assessment interest: 6% per annum from 15 months after the end of the assessment year; forced fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000; automatic estimation of profits.
KSt 1, Lines 85-89 (DTA relief and foreign income) Identify foreign-source income (e.g. licence fees from server-side encryption, SaaS revenue arising in foreign jurisdictions) and calculate exemption or credit under the applicable double tax agreement. Attach the certificate of residence (Ansässigkeitsbescheinigung) of the beneficial owner when treaty relief or withholding tax reduction is claimed. Late filing penalty (Verspätungszuschlag): 0.25% of the assessed tax per commenced month, minimum EUR 25 per month, maximum EUR 25,000 or 10% of the assessed tax; late payment surcharge (Säumniszuschlag): 1% per commenced month; assessment interest: 6% per annum from 15 months after the end of the assessment year; forced fines (Zwangsgeld) up to EUR 25,000; automatic estimation of profits.
GewSt 1A, Line 1 (Trade income) Compute Gewerbeertrag starting from corporate taxable income, adding back 25% of financing costs, 25% of rent/lease payments and certain royalties, and applying deductions under §9 GewStG. Trade tax is imposed on the UG's German place of management only; app store revenues are allocated to Germany where the VPN development and decisions occur in Germany. Same Verspätungszuschlag regime as for corporate income tax (0.25% per month, min EUR 25, cap EUR 25,000); municipal enforcement and forced fines up to EUR 25,000; 6% per annum assessment interest.
GewSt 1A, Line 3 (Loss carryforward) Utilise the trade tax loss carryforward (Fehlbetrag), subject to the same EUR 1 million/60% minimum taxation limits. A qualifying shareholder change exceeding 25% may forfeit the trade tax loss carryforward; non-resident acquirers must verify the ownership change history. Same Verspätungszuschlag regime as for corporate income tax (0.25% per month, min EUR 25, cap EUR 25,000); municipal enforcement and forced fines up to EUR 25,000; 6% per annum assessment interest.
GewSt 1A, Line 5 (Municipal multiplier) Apply the local Hebesatz; e.g. Berlin 410%, Munich 490%, Hamburg 470%, Cologne 475%; the effective Gewerbesteuer rate is 3.5% multiplied by the Hebesatz. The relevant municipality is where the UG's place of management is located; a Dutch or Swiss mailbox address will not displace German trade tax liability. Same Verspätungszuschlag regime as for corporate income tax (0.25% per month, min EUR 25, cap EUR 25,000); municipal enforcement and forced fines up to EUR 25,000; 6% per annum assessment interest.
GewSt 1A, certification of prepayments Confirm that quarterly prepayments have been made; underpayment above 10% triggers reassessment interest. Managing directors are personally liable for trade tax prepayments of the UG under §69 AO. Same Verspätungszuschlag regime as for corporate income tax (0.25% per month, min EUR 25, cap EUR 25,000); municipal enforcement and forced fines up to EUR 25,000; 6% per annum assessment interest.
