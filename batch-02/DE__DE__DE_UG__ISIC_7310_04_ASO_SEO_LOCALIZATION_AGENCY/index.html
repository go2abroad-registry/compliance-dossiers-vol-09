Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address is generally permissible for UG (haftungsbeschränkt) formation provided that the registered business address is physically accessible and that the UG actually manages its business from a German location, or has the capacity to receive legal service of process at that address. The German Federal Prevention of Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism Act (GwG) and GmbHG § 36(3) require that the registered office is not used solely for mail forwarding and that there is a real degree of commercial substance. For an App Growth Agency engaging in ASO/SEO localization services, the virtual office must be accompanied by a service provider that can receive official documents and evidence operational management at that address. The use of a pure virtual office without any physical presence or service agreements creates substantial risk of forced relocation or dissolution by the local registry (Registergericht).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

UG 'mini-GmbH' minimum share capital is EUR 1.00 (GmbHG § 5a(1)); in practice a fully paid cash capital of at least EUR 1,000 is deposited into a German bank account in the company's formation name before Commercial Register filing. The paid-in capital must be at the free disposal of the managing director; no statutory escrow account is mandatory for cash contributions, but a blocked account may be required in exceptional capital-in-kind contributions. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000 (GmbHG § 5a(3)).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: landlord's fire safety documentation and the fire protection certificate from the local fire authority (Feuerwehr) for the leased office per the State Building Code (Landesbauordnung) of the registration state. Structural load inspections: not required for standard office furniture and IT loads; a static review is triggered only if heavy server racks or specialized localization infrastructure are installed, needing the building landlord's written approval. Occupational clearance: workplace compliance under the German Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung – ArbStättV), an employer's written risk assessment (Gefährdungsbeurteilung), registration with the statutory accident insurance institution (Berufsgenossenschaft), and ergonomic display-screen workstation documentation before employees occupy the premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for federal tax administration and ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) portal for electronic tax filing and registration; local Finanzamt responsible for substantive tax assessment and VAT registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or domestic entrepreneur must register its business with the relevant local Finanzamt by submitting the official 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' (tax registration questionnaire), either in paper form or via the ELSTER portal. The Finanzamt assigns a Steuernummer (tax number) for income tax and, for VAT purposes, a separate Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (VAT ID) issued by the BZSt upon application. The procedure requires disclosure of expected turnover, intended business activities, place of management, and bank account details; foreign entities must appoint a German fiscal representative or authorized agent for service of process where no domestic permanent establishment exists. The UG must complete registration online through ELSTER, receive a preliminary tax number, and may separately apply for a VAT ID using the 'Umsatzsteuer-Nachholung' or a direct BZSt application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box election exists under German tax law. The Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt) is a private limited liability company and is per se a taxable corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer and Gewerbesteuer at entity level. No pass-through election is available. Hybrid mismatch elections may be relevant under the German implementation of ATAD (Anti-Steuervermeidungsrichtlinie) for hybrid instruments or hybrid entities, but such elections are limited to statutory carve-outs and must be agreed with the tax authorities in advance. The UG may elect to apply the small-enterprise VAT scheme (Kleinunternehmerregelung) under Section 19 UStG only if its previous-year turnover does not exceed €22,000 and current-year turnover is expected not to exceed €50,000; however, a cross-border services agency exporting to non-EU and EU clients will typically forgo this election to claim input VAT refunds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic established entities, VAT registration is mandatory irrespective of turnover because any entrepreneurial activity triggers VAT registration. For non-established foreign businesses, a VAT registration obligation arises if supplies of goods or services are performed in Germany or if distance sales exceed the EU-wide intra-Community distance selling threshold of €10,000 annually. For small enterprises, the VAT exemption threshold is €22,000 previous-year turnover and €50,000 current-year expected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Section 6 UStG applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier (or a third party on the supplier's behalf) to a destination outside the European Union, or to an intra-Community supply of goods to an EU customer holding a valid VAT ID. For a digital services agency providing SEO, ASO, and localization services, Section 3a UStG determines the place of supply. Services to business customers (B2B) are zero-rated where the recipient is established outside Germany and uses the services for business purposes; services to private consumers (B2C) are generally taxable at the place of customer residence, requiring reverse-charge mechanics where applicable. To claim zero-rating, the agency must retain documentary evidence that the recipient is a business, that the service is not effectively used in Germany, and that cross-border delivery actually occurred.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung / EX-A document) or Intrastat declaration for intra-EU removals
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing transportation out of the EU for goods exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence showing payment received from a non-German customer
  • VAT ID validity confirmation of the EU customer (from VIES database) or a certified declaration of business status for non-EU customers
  • Service agreement, purchase order, and performance documentation proving the supply of SEO, ASO, or localization services
  • IP address or server-based evidence supporting the place of service performance where digital services are supplied

Germany does not currently mandate a nationwide real-time electronic invoicing clearance system comparable to Italy's SDI. E-invoicing is permitted and increasingly required for B2G invoices; from January 2025, domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive electronic invoices based on the EN 16931 standard, and as of 2028 e-invoicing will become mandatory for all domestic B2B transactions. The obligation is supplemented by the German Tax Administration's requirements for digital archiving of originals, integrity, and readability. No API-based fiscal stamping is currently required, but XML-based e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) must guarantee authentication, immutability, and auditability for tax audits.

The German tax authorities treat export zero-rating as a substantive benefit and require strict documentary evidence. If the supplier cannot produce the required export or B2B proof within the statutory retention period, the Finanzamt may impose an assessment of VAT at the 19% rate with late-payment interest of 0.15% per month (6% per annum). Claiming zero-rating without a genuine cross-border supply or with fraudulent documentation constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO (Abgabenordnung), leading to imprisonment, fines, and a statutory assessment period extension to 10 years. The tax authority may additionally apply a freight-forwarder or intra-community triangular adjustment and issue a retrospective VAT assessment for up to 10 years, plus penalty surcharges under Sections 383 and 384 AO. The participant in a VAT carousel fraud scheme risks denial of input VAT deduction and joint and several liability for taxes owed by prior links in the transaction chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility as an EU member state under Article 63 TFEU. The euro is fully convertible; there are no exchange-control approvals for profit remittance, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation. A UG may freely receive non-resident shareholder capital contributions and make dividend distributions through a German bank account. The only mandatory compliance intervention is statistical reporting to Deutsche Bundesbank for cross-border payments above EUR 12,500. Withholding tax on profit distributions is separate from FX control and may be reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio in Germany. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or in permitted foreign-currency accounts without forced conversion. The operative requirement is statistical: a resident UG must report each cross-border receipt or payment exceeding EUR 12,500 to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV balance-of-payments statistics regime, including export proceeds and service fees, on a monthly reporting basis. No specific timeline exists for converting proceeds, but the bank will require the AWV statistical identification to be provided at the time of credit.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank and balance-of-payments statistics authority) acting together with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action for foreign trade reporting under the Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Cross-border payment and e-money institutions such as Wise and Payoneer are viable as supplementary payment rails for a German UG, but they are not licensed banks and do not provide deposit protection or full corporate banking services. Their EU e-money licenses may be passported into Germany if registered with BaFin or operating under a European Economic Area passport. A UG may use such providers for B2B collections and vendor payouts, but the primary treasury and statutory corporate banking relationship should remain with a German regulated bank to support AWV reporting, commercial lending, and documentary trade instruments. Legal constraints include absence of German deposit insurance for EMI balances, possible segregation-of-client-funds requirements, and bank/EMI-specific KYC thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A non-resident shareholder may inject capital into the German UG by SEPA credit transfer for EUR or by SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR currencies. The remitting party must state the transaction purpose in the payment message as 'shareholder capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan' and avoid intermingling with commercial trade settlement. The German resident UG is the reporting debtor for AWV balance-of-payments statistics if the inbound amount exceeds EUR 12,500; the bank will capture the statistical message via its standard Z1/Z2 reporting process and the UG must ensure the corporate purpose code matches the entity's account activity. For capital contributions, the UG must record the inflow in its annual financial statements and issue no dual-purpose credit for operating revenue. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT = 15.825% composite CIT; plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) of approximately 11.2%–17.2% depending on the local Hebesatz, producing a combined corporate tax burden of approximately 29%–33%.
