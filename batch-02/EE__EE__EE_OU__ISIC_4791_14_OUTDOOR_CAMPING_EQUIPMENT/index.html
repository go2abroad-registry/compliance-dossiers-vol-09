Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); official portal: https://www.emta.ee (e-MTA electronic service portal)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ), the first mandatory registration is in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister), through which the company receives its Registry Code (registrikood). This Registry Code is the primary tax-payer identification number for legal entities. If the company exceeds or expects to exceed the VAT threshold, or wants to register for VAT voluntarily, a VAT registration application must be submitted electronically through the e-MTA (Maksu- ja Tolliameti e-teenused). The VAT administration then issues a separate Estonian VAT number with the prefix 'EE' and nine digits. A foreign entity without a registered OÜ does not normally require an Estonian corporate tax ID unless conducting an Estonian permanent establishment activity. The online retailer using the target payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) does not require any special tax licensing to register, but must submit standard corporate and VAT registration documentation, including an extract from the foreign commercial register and certification of the recipient of the VAT refund under Council Implementing Regulation (EU) No 282/2011 if applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no check-the-box entity classification regime. An OÜ is statutorily treated as an entity-level taxed opaque corporation. The corporate income tax system is a deferred distribution tax: retained profits are not subject to immediate tax, but distributions (including dividends, fringe benefits, gifts and transfer pricing adjustments) are taxable at the corporate level. There is no election to be treated as a pass-through entity. Estonia applies the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules, but no domestic check-the-box or hybrid election is available for a standard OÜ scenario.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 estimated taxable turnover per calendar year; registration becomes mandatory from the commencement of the taxable activity if that threshold is exceeded, and is also required for non-established taxable persons when making supplies in Estonia where distance selling thresholds apply. Voluntary registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods exported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate, provided that (i) the seller is a taxable person acting in their economic activity, (ii) the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by or on behalf of the seller, (iii) the goods leave the EU within the statutory time limits (customarily within three months from the supply date), and (iv) the seller holds adequate transport and customs evidence that the goods have reached a non-EU destination. Online retail sales of outdoor camping equipment to non-EU consumers therefore fall within export zero-rating when the shipment is contracted and evidenced as an export. Certain services related to exported goods and certain offshore services may also be zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX1)
  • Bill of Lading (or alternative transport document such as CMR or AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank payment proof)

Estonia does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous inventory software mandates, or digital transactional stamping for VAT invoices. Instead, taxable persons must issue invoices that satisfy the Estonian VAT Act and submit VAT returns through the e-MTA portal. The mandatory e-invoicing framework (e-Arve) is available and supported for B2B transactions, but there is no State pre-clearance or national invoice stamping requirement under standard circumstances. For distance sales, electronic invoicing records must be retained and made available to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board on request.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may deny zero-rating, reassess output VAT at 22%, and impose statutory default interest where export documentation is missing, incomplete, or contradictory. Fraudulent claims can lead to a retrospective clawback covering the full VAT amount, fines under the Administrative and Criminal Procedure provisions, and criminal liability for tax fraud with penalties up to EUR 6,400 and possible surrender of proceeds. The ordinary limitation period for tax assessment in Estonia is generally six years from the date the tax obligation arose, but intentional evasion may extend the window. The MTA routinely audits export transactions via data analytics, and requires exporters to retain export acceptance proofs, transportation documents, and foreign currency remittance evidence within the statutory retention period.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of the net amount distributed, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution, for distributions made from 1 January 2025. Retained and reinvested profits are not subject to Estonian corporate income tax until distributed or otherwise taxed under a specific rule.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder under the post-2024 distribution regime; the Estonian OÜ instead pays corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net distribution. Recipient-country taxation may still apply. Special rules can apply to distributions linked to prior-period taxation or other statutory circumstances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No single transaction-value threshold applies universally. Under Estonian transfer-pricing documentation rules, Local File requirements can apply to specified categories of taxpayers, including taxpayers meeting the relevant size tests (generally at least 250 employees, annual turnover exceeding EUR 50 million, or balance-sheet total exceeding EUR 43 million), and to other specially defined cases. Master File obligations generally apply to qualifying multinational groups, including groups meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold of EUR 750 million. Confirm the current statutory scope and any transaction-specific requirements before relying on an exemption.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. General anti-avoidance, transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, and business-purpose rules apply. An OÜ must maintain reliable records supporting its functions, assets, risks, management, related-party pricing, and commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Undeclared distributions and tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, daily tax interest, administrative penalties, and, where statutory criminal-offense elements are met, criminal prosecution and fines or imprisonment under Estonian law.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability for their own intentional participation and may be liable under applicable law for damages caused by unlawful management. Corporate limited liability does not shield individual offenders from criminal liability.
Non-business expenses, undocumented payments, and shareholder benefits

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax and, where relevant, employment taxes, interest, and administrative penalties; unsupported or misclassified amounts may be reassessed.

Director personal liability: A director may be personally liable for intentional misconduct or breach-of-duty losses and may face criminal consequences if the conduct constitutes an offense.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance
