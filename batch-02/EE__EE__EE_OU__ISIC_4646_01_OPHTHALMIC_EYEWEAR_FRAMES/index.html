Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), e-MTA portal at https://emta.ee

E-government filing portal

The entity must first be registered in the Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister) by filing an incorporation notice. Upon registration, the registry code (registrikood) automatically serves as the national tax identification number (TIN). For VAT identification, the OÜ must submit a VAT registration application via the e-MTA electronic portal using its registry code. A non-resident entity without a permanent establishment may apply directly to the MTA for a non-resident VAT number for distance sales or digital services, providing proof of foreign incorporation, a power of attorney, and a description of the taxable supplies. Individual foreign shareholders of a non-resident OÜ are not required to obtain an Estonian personal tax ID, but Estonian person codes are issued to shareholders who become resident or hold Estonian real estate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Estonian domestic law. Resident OÜs are automatically treated as corporate taxpayers subject to entity-level income tax on profit distributions and deemed profit distributions. VAT grouping may be elected by associated Estonian entities under VAT Act §14(2). For hybrid mismatch purposes, Estonia applies EU ATAD-aligned rules without unilateral check-the-box options; the 14% reduced distribution income tax rate may be elected upon regular profit distributions under Income Tax Act §50.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover for resident taxable persons; non-resident suppliers providing goods or services in Estonia generally must register irrespective of the threshold (subject to One Stop Shop mechanisms for distance sales, with an EU-wide cross-border threshold of €10,000)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to goods physically dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union, with the exporter retaining a valid customs export declaration and transport evidence. For intra-EU dispatch, 0% applies if the customer's VAT ID is validated and an EC Sales List is filed. Wholesale distribution of acetate eyeglass frames qualifies as export of goods; the supplier must be the exporter of record and must hold evidence of departure from the EU within the statutory documentary retention period.

  • Estonian Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – SAD)
  • Customs export approval or electronic export notification from EST AXIS
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR / Air Waybill) showing actual removal from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving payment received
  • Proof of delivery / customer signed receipt or vendor declaration

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping requirement. Electronic invoices are legally equivalent to paper for VAT. Structured e-invoicing is mandatory for all invoices issued to Estonian public-sector entities. VAT returns, EC Sales Lists, and export customs declarations must be submitted electronically via e-MTA; no government API stamping exists.

The MTA may reassess output VAT at the standard 20% rate if zero-rating is not supported by compliant documentation. Late-payment interest accrues at 0.06% per day on the under-declared tax. A penalty of up to 30% of the additionally assessed VAT can be imposed for severe error or intentional concealment. Criminal liability for VAT fraud, including false export declarations, may carry fines or imprisonment under the Estonian Penal Code. VAT assessment limitation periods are three years generally, extended to seven years for deliberate misdeclaration or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a full euro-area member; the euro is freely convertible across both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63–65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). No capital controls, exchange surrender obligations, or central bank licensing apply to cross-border remittances. Profit repatriation from an OÜ is unrestricted from an FX perspective; the only fiscal friction is the statutory dividend withholding regime (20% regular rate; 14% regular rate for habitual profit distributions), collected by the paying entity via the Estonian Tax and Customs Board, with no separate approval by Eesti Pank or the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon). Intra-EU payments settle in TARGET2 as domestic-value transfers. Practical conversion cost and bank correspondent friction apply only to non-euro currencies (USD, CNH, GBP).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender ratio, no mandatory repatriation deadline, and no requirement to convert export receipts into EUR. Exporters may retain foreign currency offshore indefinitely. Residual obligations are limited to statistical reporting: banks apply Eesti Pank balance-of-payments (BoP) purpose codes when processing receipts, and the entity must maintain invoice-level export documentation (e-invoices, packing lists, CMR/AWB/Bills of Lading) to evidence the commercial transaction. Import/export declarations are handled through the Customs Single Administrative Document (Estonian e-Customs / EU Customs Union) and are unrelated to FX surrender.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; macroeconomic FX policy is delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium — legally viable, operationally partial. Estonian e-Residents can open accounts with EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise (Est. e-money licence), Revolut Business, Holvi, Monese, and Payoneer using remote onboarding. These platforms provide EUR IBANs (often hosted via partner banks), fast SEPA/SWIFT-adjacent transfers, and convenience for low-value platform-pay receipts. However, they are not credit institutions and therefore cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit, provide trade standby facilities, offer deposit protection under the Estonian Guarantee Fund (Tagatisfond), or provide the correspondent-banking depth required for large-frame wholesale distribution settlements. Recommended use: secondary collection/disbursement rail for Platform_Pay flows; primary trade clearing must remain through tier-1 commercial banks with SWIFT membership and LC capabilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injections or shareholder loans arrive via SWIFT MT103 to the OÜ's euro-denominated IBAN at a tier-1 credit institution, accompanied by a BoP purpose code — Statistic Estonia code 'S002' (direct investment – equity) or 'S201' (direct investment – shareholder loan). No Eesti Pank authorisation or exchange-control approval is required; the bank performs standard AML/CFT screening and may request the corporate authorisation to debit/source documentation. Within 30 days of payment, the OÜ must file the capital update in the e-Business Registry and refresh the UBO register, which simultaneously satisfies Finantsinspektsioon transparency expectations. For injections above €10,000, a source-of-funds checklist applies under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act.For supplier settlements and trade disbursements, the OÜ should route through SWIFT MT103 payment orders (field 70 REMI carrying the contractual invoice reference and trade descriptor), with euro-denominated intra-EU supplier payments settling instantly through SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT/SCT Inst) via TARGET2. For documentary trade, commercial Letters of Credit are issued and advised through SWIFT MT700/MT710 message chains under UCP 600, typically with a confirming bank in the supplier's corridor (e.g., China/USA) to mitigate country risk. Non-euro disbursements (USD, CNY) require correspondent nostro routing: USD via Fedwire/CHIPS, offshore CNY via CNH clearing banks (CMU), with SWIFT MT202 COV cover payments for creditor-bank credit protection.
