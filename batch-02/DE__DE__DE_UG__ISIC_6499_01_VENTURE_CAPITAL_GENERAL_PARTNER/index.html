UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: The principal framework is the German Money Laundering Act (Geldwäschegesetz, GwG), particularly Sections 1, 2, 3, 10, 11, 12, 15, 17, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23 and 24c GwG. Beneficial ownership is determined under Section 3 GwG by ownership of more than 25 percent of capital or voting rights, or by control exercised by other means; where no such person is identifiable, the legal representative, managing partner or senior managing official is recorded as the fictitious beneficial owner. German legal entities must obtain, maintain, update and electronically report beneficial-owner information to the Transparency Register under Sections 19 and 20 GwG and the Transparency Register Ordinance (Transparenzregisterverordnung, TrGebV). Register access is restricted under Section 23 GwG following Court of Justice of the European Union judgment C-37/20 and C-601/20, with access available to competent authorities, obliged entities for customer due diligence, and persons demonstrating a legitimate interest under applicable rules. Corporate registration and management are governed principally by the German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung, GmbHG), including Sections 2, 3, 5a, 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 18 and 19. Fund and management activity may be subject to the German Capital Investment Code (Kapitalanlagegesetzbuch, KAGB), including Sections 1, 2, 20, 44 and 48, and supervision by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin).
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A UG must have a German domestic business address and registered seat under Sections 4a, 8 and 10 GmbHG. A serviced or virtual office may be acceptable if it is a genuine address at which official documents can be served and the company can be reached, but a mail-forwarding-only address is inadequate and may create register, tax, banking and AML concerns. For a venture-capital general-partner or fund-management business, a virtual office is high risk where the activity falls within the KAGB, financial-services supervision or BaFin licensing perimeter. The entity should maintain demonstrable German management, records, decision-making capacity, accounting, compliance controls, bank access and personnel or outsourced functions proportionate to its activities. A UG cannot lawfully conduct regulated collective-asset management merely by using a virtual office.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

German Joint States Commercial Register Portal (Gemeinsames Registerportal der Länder), https://www.handelsregister.de; filings are submitted electronically through a German notary to the competent local register court (Amtsgericht). The Unternehmensregister is an information and filing-access portal, not a separate national name-reservation authority.

Name reservation / lock period

Germany provides no general statutory company-name reservation period or nationwide reservation certificate. Before notarization, submit a proposed name and corporate-purpose wording to the competent local register court through the notary for informal pre-clearance, and conduct searches in the Commercial Register, Unternehmensregister, German Patent and Trade Mark Office database, EUIPO and relevant domain registries. If the register court rejects the name, obtain the written or electronically recorded objection and legal basis, revise the name or purpose, and resubmit through the notary. A formal adverse court decision may be challenged using the applicable register-court complaint procedure under the German Court and Notarial Costs Act and FamFG, generally within the statutory appeal period stated in the decision; obtain specialist German procedural advice because the remedy and deadline depend on the decision type. Name rejection does not ordinarily require a ministerial appeal.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) and the locally competent Finanzamt; electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For a UG, the managing director files the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') with the local Finanzamt within one month after commencing business; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer. The separate VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) is applied for via ELSTER to BZSt under §27a UStG. A foreign entity with a German permanent establishment or economic activity must also register through the same questionnaire and, if no local residence, may be required to appoint a VAT agent for procedural notices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG is a German corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax), Solidaritätszuschlag and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax); it is not pass-through eligible and has no comparable check-the-box classification. — For a UG, the managing director files the tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') with the local Finanzamt within one month after commencing business; the Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG is a German corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax), Solidaritätszuschlag and Gewerbesteuer (trade tax); it is not pass-through eligible and has no comparable check-the-box classification.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable because Germany implemented ATAD anti-hybrid rules (§4k EStG). Relevant statutory elections include the conclusion of an income/trade-tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under §§14-17 KStG and VAT grouping (Unternehmenszusammenfassung) under §2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general minimum turnover threshold for foreign or non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Germany. Domestic small-business exemption (§19 UStG) applies to entrepreneurs established in Germany with prior-year turnover ≤ EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover ≤ EUR 50,000; the intra-EU distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000. Once any taxable supply is made in Germany, VAT registration is mandatory unless the small-business exemption applies and no option for taxation is exercised.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export supplies of goods under §4 Nr. 1 and §6 UStG where the goods leave the EU customs territory and are substantiated by an export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) in conformity with §§8-13 UStDV. Offshore services are zero-rated or not subject to German VAT where the place of supply is outside Germany; for B2B services the customer's location determines the place under §3a UStG and the recipient's Entrepreneur status must be evidenced. No VAT is charged if the German exporter/service provider retains the prescribed evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung in ATLAS)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR or Air Waybill showing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of sales proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and export sales contract
  • Export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) maintained under §§8-13 UStDV

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital-stamping regime. From 1 January 2025, the e-invoicing mandate under §14a UStG requires German-to-German B2B suppliers to issue and receive e-invoices in structured formats (XInvoice, ZUGFeRD) and transmit them through interoperable channels (e.g. Peppol network); transitional rules apply through 2027 for larger taxpayers. There is no API-based pre-clearance or prior-approval fiscal stamp.

Incorrect or missing export evidence triggers assessment of German VAT at 19% plus late-payment interest; fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to five years or fines and retrospective clawbacks. German tax authorities may enforce joint and several liability for participants in missing-trader or carousel fraud and apply a limitation period of up to ten years for intentional tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Germany has no general capital controls, mandatory foreign-exchange licensing for ordinary corporate payments, or general restrictions on repatriation of dividends, interest, liquidation proceeds, or investment capital. Transfers remain subject to German and European Union anti-money-laundering, sanctions, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, export-control, and beneficial-ownership requirements. A UG may receive foreign capital and remit distributable profits to non-resident shareholders, subject to corporate-law distributable-profit rules, the UG statutory reserve requirement, withholding-tax treatment, treaty relief or refund procedures, and sufficient evidence of the underlying transaction. A venture-capital general-partner entity may require authorization or registration under the German Capital Investment Code (Kapitalanlagegesetzbuch, KAGB), including an AIFM authorization or registration, depending on the fund structure, assets under management, investor base, marketing activity, delegation model, and regulatory exemptions. A bank may require BaFin authorization evidence, fund constitutional documents, investor and manager information, and source-of-funds evidence before permitting capital calls or investment-related payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general requirement to repatriate or convert export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or received through correspondent banks, subject to contractual, tax, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering controls. Cross-border payments and receipts may trigger AWV statistical reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank when the applicable reporting threshold and reporting category are met; the threshold and exemptions must be checked against the current AWV and Bundesbank reporting instructions because they may change. AWV reporting is generally statistical rather than an approval or currency-conversion requirement. Export controls, dual-use restrictions, embargoes, customs requirements, VAT evidence, and sanctions screening apply independently of proceeds-conversion rules. The entity should maintain invoices, contracts, customs records, shipping evidence, bank advices, and payment-purpose documentation for audit and reporting support.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is the competent central-bank authority for foreign-exchange administration, balance-of-payments reporting, and specified cross-border payment reporting under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) supervises German credit institutions, payment institutions, electronic-money institutions, investment firms, and regulated alternative investment fund managers. The Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle, BAFA) administers export-control licensing and sanctions controls. The European Central Bank also exercises prudential supervisory authority for significant institutions within the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine operating payments and treasury administration; low to medium for incorporation capital, regulated fund cash flows, investor capital calls, and fiduciary or client-money functions. An EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro payment accounts and cross-border payment services under passporting or local authorization, but it is not a credit institution unless separately licensed. Customer funds are generally safeguarded rather than protected by the German statutory deposit-guarantee regime. The provider may reject regulated financial-sector activity, pooled investor money, third-party receipts, complex fund structures, high-volume international flows, cash-equivalent activities, or payments involving sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions. A fintech account should not be represented as a bank deposit account or used as a substitute for a required German formation or capital account without written provider confirmation. The entity must verify the provider's authorization in the European Banking Authority or competent national register, permitted services, safeguarding model, settlement cut-offs, account ownership, chargeback rules, transaction limits, and termination rights.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital, shareholder loans, and investor capital calls through an account held in the UG's exact legal name at a German credit institution or an appropriately authorized EU payment institution, using the contractual investor or shareholder as remitter. For a newly incorporated UG, use the formation or capital-injection account specified by the bank and release funds only in accordance with the notarial and commercial-register process. Use EUR where commercially efficient; foreign-currency receipts may be converted by the bank at the agreed rate or retained if permitted by the account terms. Include a precise payment reference identifying the legal entity, instrument, subscription or loan agreement, capital-call number, and fund or portfolio purpose. Germany does not use a universal statutory corporate FX purpose-code system comparable to certain controlled-currency jurisdictions; banks may nevertheless require internal purpose classifications, LEI, remitter information, UBO data, and AWV reporting information. Confirm whether the transfer is reportable under the current AWV and Deutsche Bundesbank reporting rules, including applicable thresholds and exemptions. Maintain executed agreements, resolutions, investor registers, capital-call notices, bank confirmations, source-of-funds files, sanctions screening, and reconciliation records. Do not route unexplained third-party funds, investor money through a personal account, or restricted-country payments. For USD and other non-euro transfers, use a bank with appropriate correspondent coverage and validate intermediary-bank sanctions and payment-message requirements before release.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area, and SWIFT cross-border payments for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. A SWIFT MT103 is the traditional customer-credit-transfer message; banks are migrating to ISO 20022 formats such as pacs.008, so the exact message format depends on the sending and receiving institutions. Provide beneficiary legal name, address, account number or IBAN, BIC, invoice or contract reference, payment purpose, currency, fees instruction, and complete originator and beneficiary information. Use a commercial documentary letter of credit where the counterparty, country, goods, or performance risk justifies it; the bank will require an application, credit approval or cash collateral, compliant invoice and transport documents, insurance evidence where applicable, and sanctions and export-control screening. For venture-capital operations, distinguish ordinary supplier payments and portfolio-company investments from regulated fund distributions, investor redemptions, and client-money transfers. Apply four-eyes approval, segregation of duties, beneficiary verification, sanctions screening, fraud controls, payment-limit controls, and post-payment reconciliation. Retain customs, VAT, invoice, contract, shipping, investment, and bank records. Check AWV reporting, BAFA export-control licensing, EU sanctions, and tax withholding obligations independently of the clearing rail.Deliverable EUR foreign-exchange forwards with an authorized bank or eligible regulated investment firm for documented commercial exposures, subject to suitability or appropriateness, master-agreement, collateral, valuation, accounting, and transaction-reporting requirements.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for documented funding, investment, or operating exposures, subject to counterparty credit limits, ISDA or equivalent documentation, collateral terms, valuation controls, and applicable EMIR reporting and risk-mitigation obligations.Foreign-exchange options, including plain-vanilla calls, puts, and collars, where the entity can demonstrate a genuine underlying exposure and has completed product-governance, appropriateness, risk-disclosure, premium, valuation, and authorization checks.Non-deliverable forwards may be used for currencies subject to convertibility or delivery constraints when offered by an authorized counterparty and legally available for the relevant currency and jurisdiction. NDFs are generally unnecessary for EUR exposure and must not be used to evade sanctions, capital controls, licensing requirements, or reporting obligations.Natural hedging through currency matching of receipts, capital calls, investments, and expenses, together with multi-currency bank accounts, may be used without entering a derivative, but the entity must document liquidity, counterparty, settlement, concentration, and foreign-exchange risks.No speculative treasury dealing, retail-style leveraged FX activity, or derivative activity for third parties should be conducted unless separately authorized. Adopt a board-approved treasury policy defining permitted instruments, approved counterparties, exposure limits, hedge ratios, tenor limits, collateral and margin rules, confirmations, independent valuation, accounting treatment, termination rights, and periodic compliance review.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for routine operating payments and treasury administration; low to medium for incorporation capital, regulated fund cash flows, investor capital calls, and fiduciary or client-money functions. An EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro payment accounts and cross-border payment services under passporting or local authorization, but it is not a credit institution unless separately licensed. Customer funds are generally safeguarded rather than protected by the German statutory deposit-guarantee regime. The provider may reject regulated financial-sector activity, pooled investor money, third-party receipts, complex fund structures, high-volume international flows, cash-equivalent activities, or payments involving sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions. A fintech account should not be represented as a bank deposit account or used as a substitute for a required German formation or capital account without written provider confirmation. The entity must verify the provider's authorization in the European Banking Authority or competent national register, permitted services, safeguarding model, settlement cut-offs, account ownership, chargeback rules, transaction limits, and termination rights.
