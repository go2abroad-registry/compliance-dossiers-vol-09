Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern - BZSt) and the competent local tax office (Finanzamt). Primary registration portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de). For non-resident entrepreneurs without a domestic establishment or registered office, the responsible Finanzamt is Finanzamt Neubrandenburg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned German UG must register with the Finanzamt responsible for its registered office using the official questionnaire 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung', thereby receiving a local tax number (Steuernummer). A separate EU-wide VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.) must then be applied for from BZSt using the corresponding ELSTER or paper application. If the foreign entity supplies services in Germany without a German permanent establishment or registered office, the competent tax office is Finanzamt Neubrandenburg. Registration is required before the start of taxable activity; no de minimis threshold applies to non-resident entrepreneurs, and security for VAT may be required where collection is at risk.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box or elective hybrid entity regime exists in Germany. The UG is compulsorily treated as a corporation for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT is assessed separately. Available statutory elections are limited to: (i) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) for financially, economically, and organizationally integrated German entities; (ii) the small-business VAT exemption under Section 19 UStG if the statutory turnover thresholds are met; and (iii) choice of VAT filing periods (monthly or quarterly). Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD apply automatically and are not elective.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible. Germany maintains no exchange-control restrictions under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act and Ordinance (Aussenwirtschaftsgesetz / Aussenwirtschaftsverordnung - AWV). Both current-account and capital-account operations (equity subscription, shareholder loans, profit distribution, intercompany settlement) are free of approval and free of conversion requirements. Profit-remittance hurdles are fiscal only: dividends to non-resident shareholders attract the 25% Abgeltungsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (combined 26.375%) unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable Double Taxation Treaty. In addition, the UG is legally obliged by Sec. 5a(3) GmbHG to retain 25% of its annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the registered share capital reaches EUR 25,000; this is a corporate law retention rule and not a currency restriction. Inward direct investment in a data-center colocation lessor is not within the sector-specific FDI screening rules of Sec. 55-59 AWV because the UG does not operate licensed critical infrastructure (KRITIS), so no permit or clearance is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany imposes no surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio (0%): export or service-lease proceeds may be freely kept in EUR or converted into any currency. Statutory reporting duty under Sec. 67(1) AWV: cross-border payments received or made by the German UG, including SEPA payments with non-German bank accounts, must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistics when the individual payment exceeds EUR 12,500 or when the aggregate payments with the same non-resident counterparty in a calendar month exceed EUR 12,500. The statutory deadline is the 7th day after the end of the calendar month in which the payment was executed; commercial banks ordinarily submit the electronic report on behalf of the client, provided the payment instruction contains a clear purpose code and the counterparty name. The UG should retain the SWIFT/SEPA confirmation and purpose text for each transaction in its treasury audit file.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Zentralbank der Bundesrepublik Deutschland / German Federal Bank) as the central bank and balance-of-payments statistics authority, in supervisory cooperation with the Bundesanstalt fuer Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin) for licensing and conduct-of-business supervision of the banking and EMI counterparties of the UG.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for treasury sub-account use (Penta, Qonto Germany, N26 Business, Revolut Business, Wise Business), each operating under a BaFin licence or an EEA-passported EMI licence. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit overdrafts, issue documentary credits, or execute FX derivatives, so the full banking relationship, including all hedging and trade-finance activity, must remain with a Tier-1 credit institution; (ii) EMIs apply strict transaction-monitoring to hub-and-spoke flows, and a colocation business generating large monthly B2B inflows followed by hardware and power supplier payouts may be flagged; the account should be capped to a defined monthly volume; (iii) the EMI must accept and transmit AWV balance-of-payments statistics to the Deutsche Bundesbank; if the EMI refuses delegated reporting, the UG itself must file the electronic balance-of-payments report by the 7th day of the following month; (iv) EMIs commonly decline onboarding when the UBO chain contains non-EEA intermediate holding companies, so the UG should complete its German transparency-register filing first and present a clean natural-person ownership chain.

Compliant protocol: (1) the non-resident shareholder wires the capital in EUR to the UG's Tier-1 account via SWIFT MT103, with field 70 containing the beneficiary corporate name, the DE_UG IBAN, and the reference Kapitaleinlage DE_UG;
