Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Germany: Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, Bonn) for VAT ID allocation and central administration; competent local Finanzamt for statutory tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax and trade tax; ELSTER (elster.de) is the authorized electronic portal for registration and returns.

E-government filing portal

For the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt): (1) Notarize articles and register with Amtsgericht (registered office); (2) File Gewerbeanmeldung with the local Gewerbeamt; (3) Submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' to the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER to obtain the 10-11 digit Steuernummer and the corporate income tax / trade tax / VAT account setup; (4) Apply separately to BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.) through BZSt-Online or ELSTER form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr'. Foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic establishment obtain a direct VAT registration and USt-IdNr through BZSt on submission of the same electronic questionnaire under Sections 27a, 18(1) UStG; statutory processing time typically 4-8 weeks; registrations are mandatory before any taxable turnover is invoiced.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG is a German corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) resident in Germany if its registered seat (Sitz) or place of management is in Germany (Sec. 1 KStG). It is subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporate tax (0.825% effective), and Gewerbesteuer at municipal rates (standard levies 3.5% - 4.9% times local multiplier, typically resulting in ~14-17% trade tax; total effective rate commonly ~30-33%). No pass-through is available; distributions to shareholders are subject to 26.375% withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer + solidarity surcharge). — For the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt): (1) Notarize articles and register with Amtsgericht (registered office); (2) File Gewerbeanmeldung with the local Gewerbeamt; (3) Submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' to the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER to obtain the 10-11 digit Steuernummer and the corporate income tax / trade tax / VAT account setup; (4) Apply separately to BZSt for the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.) through BZSt-Online or ELSTER form 'Antrag auf Erteilung einer USt-IdNr'. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG is a German corporation (Kapitalgesellschaft) resident in Germany if its registered seat (Sitz) or place of management is in Germany (Sec. 1 KStG). It is subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the corporate tax (0.825% effective), and Gewerbesteuer at municipal rates (standard levies 3.5% - 4.9% times local multiplier, typically resulting in ~14-17% trade tax; total effective rate commonly ~30-33%). No pass-through is available; distributions to shareholders are subject to 26.375% withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer + solidarity surcharge).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in German tax law; entity classification is fixed by civil law. Available statutory elections include: (a) Organschaft (corporate income tax / trade tax fiscal unity under Secs. 14-19 KStG) and VAT fiscal unity under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG; (b) Kleinunternehmer waiver: election to apply standard VAT despite Sec. 19 UStG small-business exemption (Sec. 19(2) UStG); (c) partial exemption deduction for mixed-use input VAT (Sec. 15(4) UStG); (d) hybrid mismatch adjustment elections and documentation filings under Sec. 4k EStG and Secs. 14-21 AStG, including the Sec. 14 AStG negative-confirmation mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 22,000 (prior-year taxable turnover) with EUR 50,000 current-year cap to qualify as Kleinunternehmer exemption (Sec. 19 UStG); there is no entry threshold for the UG itself — VAT registration is compulsory upon establishment and from the first taxable transaction; a separate EUR 12,500 threshold applies to intra-Community acquisitions (Sec. 1a UStG). Financial and revenue-based financing services are generally exempt from VAT under Sec. 4 No. 8 UStG and do not count toward taxable turnover except value-added ancillary services; cross-border B2B services shift the place of supply to the recipient (Sec. 3a(4) UStG) with reverse charge under Sec. 13b UStG.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under Secs. 4(1)(a) and 6 UStG applies if the UG as supplier sends goods to a third-country recipient, the goods physically leave Germany/Union territory, transport is commissioned and commencement of export is documented, and proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis under Sec. 9 UStDV) is retained (including customs export declaration ATLAS / Ausfuhrbegleitdokument). Services: German VAT does not attach where the place of supply is outside Germany under Sec. 3a UStG (B2B: recipient jurisdiction; B2C: recipient jurisdiction for specified services); B2B services to non-EU or EU recipients are reverse-charged to the recipient (Sec. 13b UStG). For the revenue-based financing / royalty fund: pure financing and loan transactions are VAT-exempt in Germany under Sec. 4 No. 8 UStG rather than zero-rated; royalties and financing yields from debtors are within or outside German VAT solely by reference to the place of supply of the underlying service, requiring contractual residency evidence of the counterparty and, for exempt domestic supplies, substantiation of the exempt character under Sec. 4 No. 8 UStG.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping exists in Germany. Since 1 January 2025, domestic B2B suppliers must be able to receive structured e-invoices; issuance obligations phase in from 1 January 2027 (businesses with >EUR 800,000 turnover) and 1 January 2028 (all businesses) under the E-Rechnung mandate (EU standard EN 16931; formats: XRechnung, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X, EDI). B2G invoicing to federal authorities is mandatory via XRechnung since 27 November 2020. Monthly or quarterly VAT prepayment returns and annual VAT returns must be filed electronically through ELSTER; standard audit file (GoBD / digital control file) must be retained in machine-readable form; BZSt and Finanzamt conduct data-driven cross-checks against bank and customs records, but not via transactional API clearance.

German law does not impose a fixed statutory FX repatriation period; the 180-day parameter is the pipeline's internal compliance ceiling and aligns with documentary-evidence freshness required in export audits. Rectification of zero-rated exports (or exemption for cross-border services) without complete proof triggers additional output VAT, late-payment interest at 6% p.a. (0.5% per month, Sec. 233a AO / Sec. 1a UStG), Säumniszuschläge of 1% per commenced month, and, in fraud cases, tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO (imprisonment up to 5 years, up to 10 years in severe cases, plus full monetary clawback of input VAT recovered). Negligent endangerment attracts fines up to EUR 50,000 (Secs. 378-380 AO); VAT assessment periods extend to 7 years, 10 years where evasion occurred (Sec. 169 AO); a GoBD data audit and Umsatzsteuer-Sonderprüfung may be opened without prior notice; protective filing of a corrected Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung through ELSTER is mandatory upon identification of documentary deficiencies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany operates under a regime of high capital account convertibility aligned with EU free movement of capital rules. Capital transactions face no prior approval requirements for most corporate entities. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted without restriction for established UG structures, subject only to post-transaction tax reporting and beneficial ownership verification. Minor hurdles apply for transactions exceeding €100,000, requiring source-of-funds documentation and tax authority notification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds required under German or EU law. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted at prevailing market rates. Statutory timeline: None. However, all foreign currency inflows must be reported to German tax authorities (Finanzamt) within ten days for certain categories, with voluntary surrender encouraged for liquidity management.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Bundesbank) and European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore digital EMIs are not directly licensed in Germany. Entities must partner with a BaFin-licensed German payment institution or obtain a full payment institution license under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz). Strict capital, governance, and AML requirements apply.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Composite German headline: 15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (CIT) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on CIT = 15.825%; plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at the statutory 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%–490%), giving a combined effective corporate tax load of approximately 29%–33% (e.g., Berlin: 15.825% + 14.35% = 30.175%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% on gross dividends paid by the German UG to all shareholders (25% Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge). No German exemption or reduction is available for non-residents absent treaty or § 43b EStG relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline: 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end (fiscal year normally the calendar year). For returns prepared and signed by a German tax adviser, the deadline is automatically extended to 31 October of the following year; further individual extensions of up to 6 months are available on reasoned application under § 109 AO if complete data are available.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous records under § 90 Abs. 3 AO and GAufzV § 6): mandatory if annual cross-border controlled transactions exceed €5,000,000 for deliveries of goods or €500,000 for rendering of services. Master File: mandatory for group consolidated revenue ≥ €750,000,000 per EU/German country-by-country reporting threshold (§ 138a AO). Documentation must be produced within 30 days of demand (60 days for exceptional harm); retention period 6 years.

Economic substance scope: Germany applies no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through: § 42 AO general anti-abuse rule (abusive structuring disregarded), § 50d Abs. 3 EStG anti-treaty-shopping three-prong test for foreign shareholder companies (own corporate taxation, own economic activity, business premises and staff appropriate), OECD BEPS action items, ATAD-1/2 implementation (hybrid mismatch rules, § 8a KStG interest limitation, CFC rules under § 7 ff. AStG), and the German 'substance-over-form' doctrine in § 5 Abs. 1 EStG and §§ 238 ff. HGB. The UG must demonstrate real management substance (board decisions, bank account, revenue contracts, expense settlement, registered seat, employer duties) to secure treaty benefits and avoid reclassification as a shell financing vehicle.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years, or fine, under § 370 AO; especially serious cases (e.g., evasion volume > €50,000, abuse of office) carry 6 months to 10 years' imprisonment; additionally, the full tax undercharge plus 6% p.a. arrears interest (§ 233a AO) and late payment surcharges are assessed; the criminal court will order forfeiture of the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: The Geschäftsführer is prosecuted in person; conviction causes loss of standing, possible enforcement imprisonment (Erzwingungshaft) and seizure of personal assets; civil restitution is owed jointly; serious tax crime has preclusive effect on holding future managing-director offices in tax-relevant entities.
Late or Negligent Filing / Non-Remittance of Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: Late filing surcharge of 0.25% per month (min €25/month, max €25,000); late-payment surcharge 1% per month; negligence fine up to €50,000 (§ 378 AO); the Finanzamt may impose Zwangsgeld (enforcement fine) and seize bank accounts or receivables.

Director personal liability: Personal, immediate, and unlimited liability under §§ 34, 69 AO for taxes due but not paid from company assets, including withheld employee and dividend taxes; the director's private banking and pension assets are attachable.
Shell / Letterbox Entity and Abusive Structuring

Statutory sanction: Under § 42 AO, the structure is disregarded and income is taxed at the actual economic owner; DTA treaty relief is denied by § 50d Abs. 3 EStG, causing reversion to 26.375% dividend/royalty withholding plus interest and penalties; repeated offenders may be assessed through the escaped-substance rules of ATAD and subject to business disqualification.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors bear joint civil liability for taxes re-assessed on disregarded entities; personal fines up to €50,000 for negligent false declarations; criminal prosecution for aiding evasion when the shell is used to disguise the beneficial owner.
Unlicensed Banking / AML Infringement (BaFin & GwG)

Statutory sanction: Conducting banking business without license is a criminal offense under § 54 KWG (imprisonment up to 5 years or fine); BaFin order for immediate cessation and liquidation; fines for violations of the Anti-Money-Laundering Act (GwG) reach up to €5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover; profit from unlawful business is forfeited.

Director personal liability: Managing directors face individual criminal liability; designated AML officer receives personal fines and criminal sanctions; creditors may sue for restitution of losses; relevant individuals may be barred from management in regulated financial institutions.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The Finanzamt estimates taxable income and disallows the excess expenditure under § 8 Abs. 3 KStG as a hidden dividend; additional TP documentation surcharge of at least 5% of the assessment difference (minimum €5,000, in grave cases up to €1,000,000) under § 162 Abs. 4 AO; dividend trigger of the disallowed amount causes 26.375% withholding liability; arrears interest and late surcharges apply.

Director personal liability: The managing director who approved the offside payment is personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax under § 69 AO and for restitution of the corporate asset to the company; criminal liability arises for deliberately underpricing or concealed profit transfer under § 370 AO, with imprisonment possible.
