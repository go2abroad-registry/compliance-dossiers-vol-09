Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia maintains full capital account and current account convertibility under EU acquis. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriations to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted with no FX surrender or prior approval requirements. Capital controls are prohibited, enabling seamless treasury management for resident OÜs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under Estonian or EU law. Export receipts must be reported to the Tax and Customs Board within 30 days of receipt. Voluntary conversion to EUR is standard; no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratios are imposed.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), as the national central bank and member of the European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for cross-border electronic money institutions via licensed providers such as Wise and Revolut Business. Legal constraints include full compliance with EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and Anti-Money Laundering Directive (AMLD5) for digital payment services and wallets.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% corporate income tax on distributed profits, generally calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution from 1 January 2025. Estonia generally does not impose corporate income tax on retained or reinvested accounting profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% additional dividend withholding tax for distributions made by an Estonian OÜ under the post-2024 regime. The OÜ instead pays corporate income tax of 22/78 of the net distribution. Special treatment may apply to historical distributions, individual recipients, anti-abuse cases or payments recharacterised as interest, salary, management remuneration or hidden profit distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment interest, administrative fines and potential criminal liability. Serious intentional tax fraud can result in a monetary penalty or imprisonment under Estonian criminal law, with aggravated consequences for substantial damage or organised conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and management board members may incur personal liability for deliberate or grossly negligent tax damage, unlawful distributions, accounting breaches and criminal conduct. Courts may order restitution, confiscation or compensation. Travel restrictions may arise in criminal proceedings or enforcement circumstances but are not an automatic consequence of an ordinary late filing.
Undeclared Profit Distribution and Shareholder Loans

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a taxable distribution, additional corporate income tax, interest, penalties and possible denial of treaty or withholding-tax relief.

Director personal liability: Management may be liable for unlawful distributions, creditor damage, repayment to the company, insolvency-related claims and compensation for tax losses. A shareholder or director may face criminal exposure where the arrangement constitutes fraud, embezzlement or deliberate creditor prejudice.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length income or expense, additional tax and interest, documentation penalties and potential denial of deductions or treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional or grossly negligent tax damage and for approving transactions without commercial support. Related-party pricing should be supported by functional analysis, comparables, contracts, invoices and contemporaneous documentation.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, recharacterisation of payments, withholding-tax assessment, corporate income tax, interest, penalties and possible reporting to foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability, tax-related personal liability and criminal exposure for fraud or false declarations. Courts or authorities may impose enforcement restrictions, including travel restrictions, in appropriate criminal or debt-enforcement circumstances.
AML, Sanctions and Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Account suspension, transaction rejection, confiscation, administrative fines, reporting to the Financial Intelligence Unit, regulatory enforcement and criminal prosecution for money laundering or sanctions violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal fines, criminal liability, confiscation, management disqualification and civil restitution. Compliance controls should cover beneficial ownership, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, chargeback patterns and source-of-funds evidence.
Customs, VAT and Import Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Additional VAT, import duties, interest, customs penalties, seizure of goods and possible criminal liability for deliberate customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for intentional or grossly negligent customs and VAT damage, particularly where goods are deliberately misdeclared or records are falsified.
Illegal Dividend or Insolvent Distribution

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unlawful distribution, director liability for creditor damage, insolvency proceedings, additional tax and possible criminal sanctions in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Management board members may be required to restore company assets, compensate creditors and bear insolvency-related liability. Criminal liability may arise for deliberate abuse of the company's insolvency or creditor prejudice.
Employment and Management Remuneration Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Reclassification as employment or management remuneration, payroll tax and social-tax assessments, interest, penalties and possible employment-law consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid payroll taxes and unlawful bookkeeping where the arrangement was deliberate or grossly negligent.
Corporate Records and Beneficial-Owner Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines, coercive measures, tax assessments, loss of filing compliance status, restrictions on registry actions and potential criminal liability for falsified or destroyed records.

Director personal liability: Management board members may face fines, personal compensation claims, disqualification from management and criminal liability for intentional falsification, destruction or obstruction of records.
