Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax, and domestic VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). Tax registration is conducted principally through the ELSTER electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

After commercial registration and commencement of business, the UG submits the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the Finanzamt responsible for its registered office. The Finanzamt assigns a domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The UG may apply to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr.; the number is also generally requested through the tax-registration questionnaire. A foreign entity without a German seat or management that makes transactions requiring German registration must register with the Finanzamt designated for foreign businesses, provide formation, ownership, address, activity, and transaction evidence, and obtain a Steuernummer and, where applicable, a USt-IdNr. A German UG is not a foreign entity for this purpose. Registration obligations depend on the activities and transaction facts; a VAT registration is not itself a substitute for tax registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After commercial registration and commencement of business, the UG submits the electronic questionnaire for tax registration (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the Finanzamt responsible for its registered office. The Finanzamt assigns a domestic tax number (Steuernummer). The UG may apply to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr.; the number is also generally requested through the tax-registration questionnaire. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG is a German corporation and is subject to corporate income tax; it cannot elect pass-through treatment under a German check-the-box regime. German law contains no general entity-classification election equivalent to the US check-the-box rules. Hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD 2 may deny deductions or require income inclusion in specified cross-border arrangements; they are anti-avoidance rules, not elective entity-classification regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a German-established UG from VAT registration where it makes taxable supplies. The small-business scheme (Kleinunternehmerregelung) may apply if statutory turnover conditions are met: generally prior-calendar-year turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 and current-year turnover not exceeding EUR 100,000. It is an exemption from charging VAT on qualifying domestic supplies, not a general registration threshold, and does not remove other VAT reporting or transaction-specific obligations. Special rules apply to non-established suppliers and cross-border transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may be zero-rated (VAT-exempt with input tax credit) where the statutory export-delivery conditions are met, including transport or dispatch of goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU and adequate proof of export. For services, there is no blanket export zero rate: place-of-supply rules determine whether the supply is outside German VAT. Many B2B consultancy services supplied to a business customer established outside Germany are taxed at the customer's location under the general rule; specified services and B2C supplies may follow different rules. The supplier must retain evidence supporting the applicable treatment and report transactions as required.

  • Commercial invoice and order or contract identifying the goods, parties, and transaction
  • Electronic customs export declaration and export accompanying document (EAD), where required
  • Customs exit confirmation or other legally accepted export proof, including the electronic exit result where applicable
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier documentation
  • For services, contract, invoice, customer business-status and establishment evidence, and records supporting the place-of-supply analysis

Germany has no general real-time invoice clearance, fiscal-stamping, or transaction-by-transaction API clearance requirement. Since 1 January 2025, domestic businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Issuance is subject to transitional rules: through 2026, paper invoices and certain other formats may generally continue with recipient consent; qualifying businesses may generally continue until the end of 2027, with further limitations for larger issuers. From 2028, structured e-invoicing is generally required for in-scope domestic B2B transactions, subject to statutory exceptions. Structured invoices must comply with the applicable European standard and accepted German formats; ordinary PDF invoices are not structured e-invoices.

German law imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Unsupported zero-rating may be denied, with output VAT assessed retrospectively, interest, and applicable late-payment or tax-return penalties. Deliberate or reckless tax evasion may result in criminal prosecution under the Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung), including fines or imprisonment; negligent tax reduction may constitute an administrative offence. Input tax deductions and related refunds may also be adjusted, and assessments may be amended within the applicable statutory limitation periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro under Articles 63 to 66 TFEU and under the German AWG/AWV framework. No exchange-control permit is required for inbound market-entry consulting fees, shareholder loans, capital contributions, or outbound profit remittances. Foreign currency may be freely held in multi-currency accounts and converted at market rates. The only practical remittance hurdles are company-law and tax-law constraints rather than FX controls: the UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschraenkt) must retain 25 percent of annual net profit as a statutory reserve until its share capital plus reserve reaches EUR 25,000 under section 5a GmbHG, and distributions to non-resident shareholders attract German dividend withholding tax of 25 percent Kapitalertragsteuer plus 5.5 percent solidarity surcharge, which may be reduced under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender rule exists in Germany; there is no statutory conversion ratio, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no obligation to sell export receipts into euro. A resident UG must, however, comply with the Bundesbank balance-of-payments reporting obligation under section 67 AWV for every cross-border payment received from or made to a non-resident that exceeds EUR 12,500 or the equivalent in another currency. The report must be filed within the first five working days of the month following the transaction; in practice the paying or receiving bank prepares the Meldung electronically for the company unless the company self-declares via the Bundesbank portal. This is a statistical disclosure obligation only and does not restrict convertibility or impose a surrender ratio.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, Frankfurt am Main); foreign exchange and external trade administration is shared with the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (Bundesministerium für Wirtschaft und Klimaschutz, BMWK) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV); prudential supervision of banks, payment institutions and electronic money institutions is exercised by BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht). As the national central bank of the euro area, the Bundesbank executes ECB monetary policy and operates the German settlement component of TARGET2.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for the UG as a supplementary cross-border rail, but not as the primary treasury account. BaFin-licensed German fintechs such as Penta (powered by Solaris SE), Qonto Europe, Kontist and N26 Business can onboard the UG remotely via video identification within one to five days; however, many automatically decline a newly formed UG with nominal capital and a non-resident ownership chain. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business and Revolut operate in Germany through EEA passporting or a German licence and are legally permissible for B2B fee collection and supplier payouts in USD, GBP, and EUR, matching the target rails of Wise_B2B and Bank_Wire. The hard constraints are: the EMI does not issue the German IBAN-based direct-debit relationship needed for Finanzamt tax payments, cannot issue the notarial Einlagebescheinigung for capital-escrow purposes, and does not serve as the statutory account for Bundesbank AWV reporting; therefore the UG must hold at least one full German-resident commercial bank account as the treasury anchor and may attach one EMI as a liquidity overlay only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the formation stage, the non-resident shareholder remits the cash contribution for the UG (minimum EUR 1, although EUR 1,000 to EUR 25,000 is commercially necessary to pass bank gateways) to a bank account held in the name 'UG (haftungsbeschraenkt) i.G.' or to the notary's trust account; the remittance instruction must identify the shareholder, the company, the words 'Einlage Kapitalanteil', and the subscribed share amount, and the bank certifies the receipt with an Einlagebescheinigung for the commercial-register filing. Subsequent equity, shareholder loans, or working-capital injections are transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA SCT for EU/EEA euro transfers) with field 70 remittance information containing the UG legal name, the word 'Einlage' or 'Gesellschafterdarlehen', and the correct AWV balance-of-payments purpose category for inbound direct investment into a German resident; the UG or its bank files the cross-border payment report with the Bundesbank where the transaction value exceeds EUR 12,500. USD or GBP injections are credited either to a multi-currency account or auto-converted to euro at the bank's spot rate; the multi-currency option is recommended to control timing and to reduce conversion spread. To avoid rejection, the sender's bank must have a valid SWIFT RMA, the beneficiary name in field 59 must match the UG's commercial-register name exactly, and the compliance file for the shareholder (source of funds and UBO) must be pre-cleared with the receiving bank.The UG's primary outbound rail for service-related disbursements is SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer within the euro area, with the remittance field carrying the invoice number and the UN/CEFACT purpose code, and SWIFT MT103 for non-EU/EEA counterparties with corresponding-value handling and full remittance details in field 70 including the AWV purpose code; intra-EUR high-value liquidity moves are cleared in real time through TARGET2. For trade settlements of goods that the consultancy may intermediate, a documentary commercial Letter of Credit can be issued by the German bank against pro forma invoices, Incoterms 2020 documents, and a confirmed credit line, but a pure advisory income stream rarely justifies LC infrastructure; a standby LC or bank guarantee is the more typical instrument where a client demands payment security. USD-cleared wires are routed through the German bank's US correspondent, and every outbound wire is subject to OFAC/EU/UN sanctions screening with an RMA-authenticated SWIFT connection. Cross-border outbound payments above EUR 12,500 must likewise be included in the monthly Bundesbank AWV report; documentary evidence of the underlying service contract, invoice, proof of delivery, and receipt must be retained for the statutory limitation period of at least 10 years under German tax and commercial law.Spot FX (Devisenkassageschaft) at T+2 settlement for immediate conversion of consultancy fees and supplier payments.Deliverable FX forwards (Devisentermingeschaefte) of up to 12 months tenor for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF, executed under the Deutsche Rahmenvertrag fuer Finanztermingeschaefte (DRV 2018) with an ISDA credit-support annex where required.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps (CCS) including cross-currency interest rate swaps, used to align non-EUR asset and liability cash flows without speculative positions.OTC FX options (call and put) purchased by the UG to hedge committed overseas tender or acquisition exposure, subject to MiFID II appropriateness/suitability assessment and the bank's derivative-eligibility policy for a low-capital UG.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to currencies with genuine convertibility limitations such as NGN, INR, or CNH, executed only through an authorised German or EU investment firm and only against proven underlying commercial exposure, because speculative NDF positioning by a non-bank entity may breach KWG licensing boundaries.Contractual currency-sharing and renegotiation clauses in overseas consulting agreements, which act as a self-help non-derivative hedge and require no bank authorisation beyond standard treasury policy sign-off.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for the UG as a supplementary cross-border rail, but not as the primary treasury account. BaFin-licensed German fintechs such as Penta (powered by Solaris SE), Qonto Europe, Kontist and N26 Business can onboard the UG remotely via video identification within one to five days; however, many automatically decline a newly formed UG with nominal capital and a non-resident ownership chain. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business and Revolut operate in Germany through EEA passporting or a German licence and are legally permissible for B2B fee collection and supplier payouts in USD, GBP, and EUR, matching the target rails of Wise_B2B and Bank_Wire. The hard constraints are: the EMI does not issue the German IBAN-based direct-debit relationship needed for Finanzamt tax payments, cannot issue the notarial Einlagebescheinigung for capital-escrow purposes, and does not serve as the statutory account for Bundesbank AWV reporting; therefore the UG must hold at least one full German-resident commercial bank account as the treasury anchor and may attach one EMI as a liquidity overlay only.
