Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum stated share capital is EUR 1. Before registration, all cash contributions must be paid in full; contributions in kind are prohibited for a UG under GmbHG section 5a(2). Funds must be placed at the company's disposal, generally in a company bank account; there is no statutory escrow requirement. The EUR 25,000 GmbH capital threshold does not apply to a UG.

Funding & unlock path: No sector-specific clearance is ordinarily required for a digital platform. For premises, comply with applicable building-use and fire-safety requirements, workplace risk-assessment and occupational-safety duties, and any landlord or local-authority requirements. Structural-load inspections are relevant only where required for the premises or proposed use.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent local tax office (Finanzamt) administers corporate income tax, trade tax filings, wage tax and German VAT registration. The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) issues German VAT identification numbers (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.). ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) is the principal electronic tax filing and registration platform. VAT One Stop Shop registration for eligible EU cross-border B2C supplies is administered through the BZSt OSS portal.

E-government filing portal

1. Register the UG with the competent local trade office where its German business establishment is located, if a trade registration is required. 2. File the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER, generally after formation and within the applicable registration period requested by the Finanzamt. 3. Provide the notarized articles or formation documents, commercial-register details, registered office, business activities, directors, shareholders, expected turnover, bank details, accounting period and VAT liability information. 4. The Finanzamt assigns the German tax number (Steuernummer), which is used for domestic tax filings and invoices where required. 5. Apply separately to the BZSt for the USt-IdNr., normally through the BZSt online application process or through the tax registration process where supported. 6. A foreign entity without a German establishment applies to the competent German tax office for non-resident taxpayers, based on the nature and location of its German VAT obligations, and separately obtains a USt-IdNr. where required. 7. Non-EU businesses supplying qualifying B2C electronic services may use the non-Union OSS scheme through the BZSt or another designated EU Member State; this does not replace German domestic tax registration where German taxable supplies remain outside the OSS scope. 8. Maintain evidence of the German establishment, management, payment-processor arrangements, platform contracts, customer-location controls and transaction records. Tax registration does not itself create a permanent establishment; permanent-establishment analysis is separate from VAT establishment analysis.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a German corporation and is generally subject to German corporate income tax at entity level, solidarity surcharge and municipal trade tax. Germany does not provide a US-style check-the-box election for a UG. The UG cannot elect transparent partnership treatment merely by filing a tax classification form. A corporation may, subject to statutory conditions, elect a tax-group regime (Organschaft) with a qualifying parent or subsidiary, including a profit-and-loss transfer agreement and financial, economic and organizational integration. German partnerships are generally fiscally transparent, but that classification is not available to the UG. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the German Foreign Tax Act may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences for arrangements involving hybrid entities, instruments or payments; they do not constitute an elective entity-classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold below which a business with German taxable supplies is automatically outside VAT registration. The small-business exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act (UStG) may apply where total turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 in the preceding calendar year and does not exceed EUR 100,000 in the current calendar year, subject to the statutory rules and effective-period requirements. The exemption is not available for all transaction types and generally prevents deduction of input VAT. A business may waive the exemption, subject to the binding-period rules. For EU cross-border B2C electronic services, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold may determine whether the supplier can continue applying the Member State of establishment rules instead of destination-country taxation; the threshold does not eliminate general German VAT registration obligations where German taxable supplies exist. Intra-Community acquisition thresholds and import VAT rules are separate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax credit under Sections 4 and 6 UStG if the goods are transported or dispatched to a third country and the supplier satisfies the substantive export conditions and documentary evidence requirements. The supplier must establish that the goods left the EU customs territory and must connect the transport evidence to the specific supply. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax credit under the applicable UStG rules, provided the customer has a valid VAT number, the goods are transported to another Member State, reporting and evidence requirements are met, and the transaction is correctly included in the recapitulative statement where required. Services are generally not technically zero-rated merely because the customer is abroad. A B2B service is commonly outside German VAT under the place-of-supply rule where the business customer is established outside Germany, subject to special rules. Electronically supplied B2C services are generally taxable where the consumer resides; the supplier may use the EU OSS for eligible EU consumer supplies. Non-EU B2C digital-service taxation depends on the customer-location rules of the relevant jurisdiction. A webnovel subscription or reading application must separately analyze platform-operator status, commission arrangements, app-store deemed-supplier rules, customer status, customer location, bundled content and any electronically supplied service classification.

  • Export customs declaration, normally submitted electronically through the applicable German customs system, including the Movement Reference Number where issued
  • Electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence demonstrating that the goods exited the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, order or contract identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, destination and transaction reference
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the mode of transport, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, postal evidence or carrier delivery confirmation
  • Proof linking the export declaration and transport evidence to the specific invoice or supply
  • Evidence of the non-EU destination and, where relevant, proof of delivery or receipt by the foreign customer
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number, transport evidence and required recapitulative-statement records
  • Foreign exchange or inward-remittance evidence is not generally a mandatory German VAT condition for export exemption; payment evidence may nevertheless support the audit trail and commercial substance
  • For digital services, customer-location evidence such as billing address, payment details, IP-geolocation evidence, SIM-country information or other permitted evidence required under the EU VAT rules; customs export documents generally do not exist for electronically supplied services

Germany has no general real-time invoice clearance, continuous transaction controls or mandatory pre-clearance API for ordinary domestic VAT invoices. From 1 January 2025, German businesses must generally be able to receive structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. A compliant electronic invoice must be in an EU-standard structured format, commonly XRechnung or a compliant hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD. Transitional issuance rules apply: paper and non-structured electronic invoices may continue during the statutory transition periods, generally through 31 December 2026, and for issuers meeting the applicable prior-year turnover condition through 31 December 2027; from 2028 the structured e-invoice requirement is generally effective for in-scope domestic B2B transactions. The rules primarily concern domestic B2B transactions between German-established businesses and contain exclusions, including certain exempt transactions, small-value invoices and other statutory cases. B2C invoices and many cross-border invoices are not generally subject to the domestic structured e-invoice issuance requirement. Federal public-sector contracting may require XRechnung or another prescribed format. Electronic invoices must preserve authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility, and must be retained in accordance with German tax-record retention rules. Germany does not currently impose a universal digital tax stamp for VAT invoices.

Germany does not impose a general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline as a condition for export VAT exemption or input VAT recovery. The decisive requirements are the substantive export or place-of-supply conditions and reliable documentary evidence. If export conditions or evidence are missing, the Finanzamt may deny the exemption, assess German VAT retroactively, deny or reverse related input VAT recovery, impose VAT interest under the German Fiscal Code, and require amended VAT returns. Incorrect refund claims may be recovered with interest and may trigger tax audits, expanded record requests and liability exposure for responsible officers. Intentional tax evasion may constitute a criminal offense under Section 370 of the German Fiscal Code; negligent tax understatement may constitute an administrative offense under Section 378. Additional administrative fines or criminal consequences may apply for false customs declarations, forged documents, obstruction of customs controls or serious accounting and record-retention violations. Customs duties, import VAT, post-clearance recovery, customs penalties and other border measures may apply independently of the VAT assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany is a Eurozone member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU and the EU Capital Movement Directive. No declaration or approval is required for dividend distributions, profit repatriation, or capital inflows. The Euro serves as the sole functional currency; non-resident shareholders may remit profits without conversion restrictions. The only compliance obligation is the AWV statistical reporting of any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500; this is a data-reporting duty, not an exchange-control restriction. Profit remittance from a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is legally unrestricted, subject to standard German corporate law restrictions requiring free-capital availability under the balance sheet test, corporate tax documentation, trade tax considerations, and applicable withholding tax declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio in Germany. Export and subscription revenues denominated in foreign currency may be retained, converted, or swapped freely at a German bank or through a licensed EU counterparty. German residents must report to the Deutsche Bundesbank, within its electronic payment statistics system, all payments received from abroad and payments made abroad in excess of EUR 12,500; the report is required for statistical purposes only and does not impose any holding, repatriation, or conversion deadline. For platform content exports, the receivables period is governed by the invoice terms and applicable settlement schedules from Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and any licensing distributors.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) with regulatory oversight by the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action; exchange-control statistical reporting is administered under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High, subject to the provider holding a valid EEA e-money licence (PSD2) or a BaFin banking licence, and subject to the availability of a real German IBAN for business operations. An offshore digital EMI without an EEA passport or without a physical or sponsored correspondent banking arrangement is not viable as the principal treasury rail because German commercial counterparties and the UG treasury function require a stable German IBAN, irrevocable SEPA status, and a full-service corporate account. A licensed EMI may be used for segregated cross-border liquidity, FX execution, and multi-currency operations, but it must be complemented by a Tier-1 German bank account to hold registered share capital, to satisfy the UG capital escrow rules, and to operate the primary settlement account. Legal constraints include AML/KYC data-localisation expectations under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG), BaFin supervision of money remittance, investor-protection limitations for corporate treasury accounts, and the requirement that any safeguarded e-money funds be held in segregated accounts with an EU credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections arise either as (a) a cash capital increase in the UG under the Act on the Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung – GmbHG), or (b) a shareholder loan from a non-resident shareholder. For (a), the share capital contribution must be made by a bank transfer to a German bank account held in the name of the UG as an escrow or special subscription account; the bank must confirm that the funds are at the free disposal of the managing director. The German civil-law notary verifies the subscription and the commercial register accepts the proof of payment. The transfer is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or investor, using the UG's IBAN and BIC, with the payment reference containing the notarial matter or purpose such as 'Kapitalerhöhung UG – Einlagezahlung'. Beneficiary operational instructions must include the full legal name of the UG, the German registered address, and the intended tax ID. For payments crossing the German statistical threshold, the receiving German resident must report the payment under AWV; non-resident senders do not need prior Bundesbank approval. For (b), a shareholder loan should be documented by a formal loan agreement with clear maturity, interest, repayment schedule, and subordination provisions, and should be routed as an MT103 with purpose code 'loan disbursement' while preserving the commercial substance and complying with thin-capitalisation and transfer-pricing documentation requirements.For subscription and content-licensing trade flows, outbound disbursements are made primarily by SWIFT MT103 for foreign licensors, translators, and distribution partners; SEPA SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant for European counterparties; and card-acquiring settlement rails through Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, Stripe, PayPal, and regional PSPs for the underlying consumer receipts. Cross-border service fees owed by the UG are executed via MT103 with economically accurate payment references, and each outbound payment remains subject to the AWV reporting threshold. Commercial letters of credit (MT700) are generally not applicable to digital serialised fiction because there is no physical shipment or transportation document; where a large licensing contract requires payment security, an autonomous bank guarantee or standby letter of credit may be issued in favour of a licensor, with reimbursement to be settled by MT103/MT202. The clearing of all outbound flows should concentrate through the Tier-1 German bank operating account, allowing the treasury function to maintain visibility, to reconcile royalty payout reports, and to meet statutory accounting segregation between operational funds, VAT liabilities, and distributable profits.Plain vanilla foreign-exchange forwards on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY, EUR/CHF, and EUR/JPY with a licensed German or EEA bank counterparty, covering forecast subscription, licensing, and content-acquisition cash flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or emerging-market currencies such as EUR/CNY, EUR/INR, or EUR/KRW for managing exchange risk in jurisdictions with onshore convertibility limitations, provided the counterparty executes under a legally enforceable ISDA Master AgreementCross-currency swaps and rolling EUR-foreign-currency swaps to convert investor treasury, licensing, or royalty cash flows into EUR and to maintain multi-currency liquidity buffersForeign-exchange options (EUR call/foreign currency put and EUR put/foreign currency call) used selectively for non-speculative hedging of a platform subscription pipeline, subject to MiFID II suitability and EMIR clearing thresholdsFX swaps for temporary liquidity conversion between EUR and USD used by the platform treasury for app-store receivable concentration, subject to the same internal hedging policy and counterparty credit limits

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High, subject to the provider holding a valid EEA e-money licence (PSD2) or a BaFin banking licence, and subject to the availability of a real German IBAN for business operations. An offshore digital EMI without an EEA passport or without a physical or sponsored correspondent banking arrangement is not viable as the principal treasury rail because German commercial counterparties and the UG treasury function require a stable German IBAN, irrevocable SEPA status, and a full-service corporate account. A licensed EMI may be used for segregated cross-border liquidity, FX execution, and multi-currency operations, but it must be complemented by a Tier-1 German bank account to hold registered share capital, to satisfy the UG capital escrow rules, and to operate the primary settlement account. Legal constraints include AML/KYC data-localisation expectations under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG), BaFin supervision of money remittance, investor-protection limitations for corporate treasury accounts, and the requirement that any safeguarded e-money funds be held in segregated accounts with an EU credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (0.825%), plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer); effective combined statutory rate generally ranges from approximately 29.8% to 32.9% depending on the municipality's trade tax multiplier (Hebesatz).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% dividend withholding tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax (0.825%), resulting in a combined effective withholding rate of 26.375% on gross dividends paid by a German UG to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Statutory deadline is 31 July of the year following the fiscal year-end. Where the return is prepared by a tax advisor, the deadline is automatically extended to 31 May of the second year after the fiscal year-end, pursuant to Section 149 of the German Fiscal Code (AO).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 300,000 for cross-border related-party services and EUR 1.2 million for cross-border related-party goods transactions per year trigger the mandatory Local File documentation. The Master File is required when the global group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100 million. Even below these thresholds, adequate and contemporaneous arm's length documentation is required if challenged.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to offshore jurisdictions, but requires real economic substance as a fundamental principle under general anti-abuse rules (Section 42 AO), beneficial ownership principles, CFC rules (Sections 7-13 AStG), and substance-over-form case law. A German UG engaged in web novel serial platforms must have actual employees, business premises, and operational decision-making in Germany; otherwise, profits may be recharacterized as dividends or other constructive distributions subject to withholding tax and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine under Section 370 AO; in especially serious cases imprisonment of six months to ten years. Monetary sanctions include confiscation of illegally obtained tax advantages and additional penalty payments up to the amount of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for the evaded corporate tax and ancillary amounts under Sections 69 and 71 AO. Directors involved in evasion may face travel bans, seizure of passports, extradition proceedings, and imprisonment, as well as civil liability for damages suffered by the company and creditors.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the legal structure under Section 42 AO, treat the UG as a sham entity, deny treaty benefits, add hidden profit distributions to taxable income, and apply a penalty of 5-10% of adjusted taxable income under Section 162 AO for unreasonable lack of documentation. Unpaid corporate income tax and trade tax are assessed with late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes that the UG could have paid but did not, especially if assets were transferred out while liabilities existed. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors and shareholders civilly liable. Directors can also be barred from serving as legal representatives under Section 6 of the Trade Regulation Act (GewO) and subjected to travel restrictions during tax enforcement.
Anti-Money Laundering / Payment Integrity

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Section 261 of the German Criminal Code (StGB) for money laundering, with imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to EUR 1 million for corporate entities under the Administrative Fines Act (OWiG). Regulatory fines may be imposed by the Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for breaches of the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG).

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be prosecuted as individuals; they face imprisonment, forfeiture of assets, professional bans, and travel bans. Failure to appoint a money laundering compliance officer when required may lead to personal criminal sanctions.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income upward under Section 162 AO and apply a presumptive penalty of 5-10% of the adjustment, plus late fee penalties of up to EUR 5,000 per undocumented transaction; interest on the additional tax liability accrues at 6% per year. In cases of gross intentional manipulation, criminal tax evasion sanctions apply.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who sign false tax declarations or instruct subordinates to falsify transfer pricing records are personally liable for the resulting tax deficiencies. They may additionally face criminal prosecution for fraud, and courts may order travel bans and seizure of assets if there is a risk of flight.
Withholding Tax Misapplication on Cross-Border Distributions

Statutory sanction: The UG is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax; the tax office may assess the full 26.375% dividend withholding tax plus 15.825% royalties withholding tax, impose late payment surcharges of 1% per month, and apply late-payment interest at 6% per annum. In egregious cases, the payment itself may be recharacterized as a constructive dividend with additional penalties.

Director personal liability: German law imposes personal liability on managing directors under Section 69 AO for withholding taxes the UG failed to deduct and remit. Directors may face enforcement against personal assets, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for breach of fiduciary duty, including imprisonment for deliberate failure to collect and pay withholding taxes.
