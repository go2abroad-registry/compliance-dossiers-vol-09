Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU internal-market law, the German Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Aussenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG) and the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Aussenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). There are no exchange-control restrictions on dividend or profit remittance capacity for a German Unternehmergesellschaft (UG). A UG may distribute profits to non-resident shareholders without central-bank approval, provided the distribution is covered by distributable balance-sheet reserves, confirmed by a notarially attested shareholders' resolution, and recorded in the annual financial statements. Non-resident shareholders face German withholding tax at 26.375% on cash dividend distributions, subject to treaty relief and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the shareholder qualifies. For cross-border capital transactions or payments to non-residents exceeding EUR 12,500, a statistical reporting obligation to Deutsche Bundesbank exists under Section 67 AWV; this is a reporting requirement, not a convertibility authorization hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany requires no surrender, compulsory repatriation, or minimum conversion of export proceeds. A German exporter or licensed electronic-money institution may lawfully maintain export receipts in foreign currency in any commercial bank account or use them for cross-border payments without conversion. The only applicable obligation is the statistical reporting of each cross-border receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 to Deutsche Bundesbank under the AWV balance-of-payments reporting regime. For operational efficiency, the UG should instruct its treasury counterparties to identify the payment reference as 'export proceeds' and include the commercial invoice, country of destination, and ultimate payer details, so that the reporting obligation is safely discharged by the bank or the declarant.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank is the German central bank and the competent authority for payment clearing, TARGET2 participation, and external-sector balance-of-payments statistics. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt fuer Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht, BaFin) is the competent licensing and supervisory authority for electronic money institutions under the Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz, ZAG). Germany maintains no separate foreign-exchange administration or exchange-control licensing body.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for a German or EU/EEA-licensed electronic-money institution with an established BaFin licence or valid passporting notification; Low for unlicensed offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot legally obtain direct bank account and clearing access with a German tier-1 bank as a regulated payment institution unless it operates through a German licensed EMI or an EU/EEA sponsor and fully complies with the safeguarding obligations under Section 31 ZAG, the Anti-Money Laundering Act (Geldwaeschegesetz, GwG), and EBA Guidelines on payment and e-money institutions. German banks treat offshore digital EMIs as high-risk if they combine opaque ownership, foreign licence quality, virtual-asset exposure, and cross-border payment complexity. The most bankable route is to complete the BaFin EMI authorisation process or to engage a fully licensed German EMI as a white-label partner, with the UG acting as principal for customer-facing reserve management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103 into the UG's designated domestic bank account with a precise purpose code in the payment narrative, preferably 'CAIT - Capital Injection / Equity Contribution' or the German wording 'Kapitalerhoehung / Kapitaleinlage', including the shareholder name exactly as stated in the notarial deed. For EU/EEA shareholders, a SEPA Credit Transfer through TARGET2 is acceptable; for non-EEA senders, a SWIFT MT103 from a correspondent bank in Frankfurt or a direct cross-border wire is required. The UG must provide the receiving bank with a notarised capital-increase resolution, a notarised shareholder list, a commercial-register filing receipt or transcript where the capital increase is registrable, and source-of-funds evidence for the sending account. If the incoming funds are shareholder loans rather than equity, the UG must instead mark the transfer as a shareholder loan and provide a signed written loan agreement including an arm's-length interest rate to avoid tax recharacterisation and AML classification issues. All inbound wires should be subjected to the bank's funds-verification process, SWIFT GPI tracking, and reconciliation against the UG's treasury ledger before any e-money float is applied.Outbound trade and service disbursements should be executed using SWIFT MT103 for non-EU beneficiary payments, with an MT202 COV message for correspondent cover where required, and SEPA Credit Transfers via TARGET2 for EU beneficiaries. For high-value or documentary trade settlements, the UG may use Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600, with MT700/MT707 messages and confirming-bank support, or Documentary Collections under URC522; a German tier-1 bank will process these only when the UG provides operative trade documentation, invoices, transport documents, and insurance certificates. For e-money transactions, the underlying payment order must include a customer mandate reference, transaction reference, beneficiary name, and compliance trail identical to the stored customer wallet instruction; no outbound disbursement may be made to an unattributed third party or used to effect benefit transfers that circumvent AML screening. The treasury operation should maintain separate funding accounts for customer redemptions, merchant payouts, and supplier settlements, using SWIFT GPI for enhanced tracking and instant reconciliation with the bank's nostro statements.Outright FX forwards against EUR with maturities tailored to cash-flow cycles, usually one to twelve months, executed onshore under a documented ISDA/Proprietary Master Agreement.Deliverable foreign-currency swaps used for liquidity matching between the e-money float portfolio and anticipated settlement obligations in major currencies.Cross-currency swaps for hedging long-duration foreign-currency shareholder loans or capital exposure when the UG has committed future repatriation or refinancing.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to currencies with non-convertibility or no liquid offshore settlement; not required for EUR/USD or EUR/GBP treasury flows.Currency options, including plain-vanilla European options, permitted where the UG has a licensed derivatives mandate, EMIR reporting capacity, and board-approved treasury policy ensuring compliance with the German Securities Trading Act and MiFID II professional-client requirements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) on the corporate tax, producing a headline federal burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is added separately at the statutory base rate of 3.5% multiplied by the municipal collection factor (Hebesatz, typically 380%–420%), bringing the combined typical statutory burden on German taxable profits to approximately 30%–33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory dividend withholding tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, aggregating to 26.375%; German tax treaties typically reduce the rate to 5%–15%; the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (implemented via §44d EStG) provides a 0% rate for EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted period of at least 24 months, provided the anti-abuse/minimum-substance tests are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December 2024, the return is due by 31 July 2025 (seventh month after year-end). Where preparation is undertaken by a licensed tax adviser (mandatory entry requirement for many UG balance-sheet cases), the statutory deadline under §149(3) AO as amended by the Fourth Bureaucracy Relief Act extends to the fifteenth month, i.e., 31 March 2026 for calendar-year 2024; the same July 31/March 31 pattern applies to subsequent years.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory periods as the KSt-1A return: 31 July of the following year without adviser assistance; 31 March of the second following year for adviser-prepared calendar-year returns.

The trade tax liability follows the German place of effective management; a foreign parent's mere shareholding does not create trade tax nexus in Germany. For an EMI, the reserve-management interest and interchange-fee income are allocable to the German trade; no notional allocation to the foreign parent is permitted. Box 3 (Verlustabzug) Deduction of trade-tax loss carryforwards arising before or in the current assessment period. Trade-tax losses of the German UG are extinguished upon a change of 100% of ownership within 5 years under §10a GewStG, a critical planning point for foreign investors. Non-resident directors travelling frequently to Germany do not create a permanent establishment if the UG has no fixed premises; pure travel activity is not zerlegungsrelevant. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory for cross-border controlled transactions exceeding EUR 5,000,000 per transaction category within a fiscal year; Master File is required where the German-headed group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting notification applies at EUR 750,000,000 group revenue threshold. Non-resident-owned UG subsidiaries with any cross-border fee, royalty, or financing transaction must contemporaneously document the arm's-length principle and file a transfer-pricing declaration within 30 days of an official request (60 days for Master File).

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute as used in certain offshore hubs, but the substance-over-form doctrine under §42 AO, the general anti-abuse rule, and §50d EStG require a UG acting as a treasury, wallet-reserve, or IP-holding entity to demonstrate real German management, qualified personnel, and operational decision-making. For an EMI, the BaFin licence and the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) impose substantive local governance, compliance infrastructure, and risk-management functions; absence of such substance results in denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation of intercompany transactions, and compulsory liquidation of the shell entity by the commercial register.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under §370 AO, aggravated cases up to 10 years, plus criminal fines equal to the evaded amount, default interest at 6% per annum, and a 10-year assessment limitation period; the tax office may also order seizure of the UG's bank accounts and impose coercive penalties of up to EUR 25,000.

Director personal liability: Managing directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, wage tax, and VAT that they caused to be evaded; courts pierce the corporate veil for fraudulent conduct, and directors face personal bankruptcy, travel bans (Ausreisesperre), freezing of private assets, and removal from the commercial register of directors.
Unlicensed Electronic Money Activities (KWG/ZAG Violation)

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed payment and e-money services constitute a criminal offence under §54 KWG: imprisonment up to 5 years and fines; BaFin administrative fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of total annual turnover; immediate closure of the business and forced disgorgement of all interchange and float income.

Director personal liability: Directors who initiate or tolerate unlawful operations are criminally liable alongside the legal entity; they face disqualification from serving as company officers, personal restitution to customer reserve accounts under §64 GmbHG, and, in the event of insolvency, personal liability for payments made after the point of over-indebtedness.
Money Laundering / AML Compliance Failure (GwG Breach)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) of up to EUR 1,000,000 in ordinary cases and up to 10% of annual turnover for serious breaches; BaFin can revoke the e-money licence and issue public warnings; criminal money-laundering charges under §261 StGB carry imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Officers responsible for AML oversight are personally fined up to EUR 100,000 for each violation; they may be imprisoned for handling criminal proceeds, and the corporate veil is pierced where the director used the UG as an instrument to launder funds, exposing personal bank accounts and real property to confiscation.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under §162 AO, a penalty surcharge of 5%–10% of the transfer-price adjustment is added when documentation is not timely delivered, with an additional penalty of up to EUR 1,000,000; the tax office is entitled to estimate taxable income and may impose the full corporation tax and trade tax burden on the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate transfer-pricing declarations are joint and several debtors for the resultant under-withholding; in severe arbitrariness cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion under §370 AO is initiated against the director personally, with potential imprisonment and confiscation of personal remuneration received through the manipulated pricing.
Shell Company and Directive Abuse (Beneficial Ownership / Dividend WHT Evasion)

Statutory sanction: The benefit of §44d EStG is denied retroactively: the 26.375% dividend withholding tax and solidarity surcharge are recaptured, plus 6% per annum default interest and a surcharge of 5% of the under-withheld tax; the transaction is recharacterised as a hidden profit distribution, triggering an additional trade tax burden.

Director personal liability: Directors of the UG that facilitated the conduit arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the recaptured withholding tax; intentional participation in the abuse is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment under §370 AO, and the register court may impose a travel ban against directors attempting to leave Germany to avoid enforcement.
