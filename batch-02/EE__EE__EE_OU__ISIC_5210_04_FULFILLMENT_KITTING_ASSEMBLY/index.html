Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) under the Ministry of Finance; official self-service portal e-MTA at https://www.emta.ee and company registration portal e-Ariregister at https://ariregister.rik.ee

E-government filing portal

For an Estonian Osaühing (OÜ), registration in the commercial register automatically issues the Estonian registry code which constitutes the universal TIN for corporate income tax, VAT, social tax and withholding obligations. A foreign legal entity without Estonian incorporation must separately register with the MTA: (1) determine VAT nexus because a non-resident taxable person supplying goods situated in Estonia at the time of supply (including fulfilment, kitting and assembly inventory) is registrable from the first such supply; (2) file the VAT registration application through the e-MTA portal using an e-Residency digital ID or a locally authorised tax representative; (3) submit certified constitutional documents, foreign registration certificate, foreign VAT/equivalent tax number, evidence of the Estonian warehouse or fulfilment address, and identification of the local responsible person; (4) upon approval the MTA issues an Estonian VAT number and opens an e-MTA tax account; a branch (filiaal) registration in the commercial register is also required when the activity constitutes a fixed place of business in Estonia.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Entity classification follows domestic corporate law and substance: an Estonian-resident OÜ or a foreign legal person analogous to a capital company is taxed at entity level, while foreign partnerships analogous to Estonian TÜ/US are generally tax-transparent. Available statutory elections include the reduced-rate regular-dividend election under the Income Tax Act (Tulumaksuseadus §50(2)) for recurring distributions, the VAT-grouping election for closely connected taxable persons, the instalment election for exit tax on cross-border seat and asset transfers, and the right to request an MTA advance ruling on hybrid entity classification. ATAD hybrid-mismatch and CFC rules automatically override contrary elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover in the preceding 12 months for Estonian-established suppliers; no turnover threshold for non-resident taxable persons supplying goods located in Estonia at the time of supply; EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold for cross-border B2C dispatches; reduced VAT rates of 13% and 5% apply to designated supplies following the 1 July 2024 reform

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the 0% export VAT rate only where the supplier proves the goods have left Estonian territory and the European Union under a customs export procedure within 90 days of the supply, and retains the electronic export declaration (SAD/MRN), transport documents, commercial invoice and any other evidence of physical departure. B2B services performed on goods in Estonia, such as kitting, packing and assembly, follow the location-of-goods rule; they are zero-rated only when they form an integral and indispensable part of an actual export of the processed goods, whereas detached logistics services supplied to an Estonian counterparty remain taxable at 22%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD with MRN from the EU Automated Export System)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill evidencing departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 payment evidence where export-payment traceability is examined
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and warehouse/kitting records
  • Foreign customer's business registration/VAT number evidence where zero-rated B2B services are claimed

As of the 2025 assessment period Estonia does not impose a nationwide real-time fiscal clearance, API-based continuous-transaction control or mandatory digital stamping. VAT returns are filed exclusively electronically via e-MTA; invoices to Estonian public-sector buyers must be transmitted as structured EN 16931 e-invoices through the Peppol network; exports are cleared electronically through the EU Automated Export System and ICS2, and all export evidentiary records must be retained and produced to the MTA in digital form upon request.

Estonia does not maintain capital-control FX-repatriation deadlines; the 180-day benchmark is an evidentiary and transfer-pricing standard for unpaid export receivables. Where export documents are missing or do not evidence physical departure within the statutory 90-day window, the MTA reclassifies the supply as domestic and assesses 22% VAT, claws back input VAT and any export refund previously paid, imposes penalty interest of 0.06% per day, and may apply tax-increase penalties and criminal sanctions for intentional evasion or fraudulent customs declarations, including fines and imprisonment under the Penal Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia applies full current and capital account convertibility. The euro is legal tender, and EU free-movement-of-capital rules prohibit exchange controls, capital flow restrictions, or profit remittance permits. Profit repatriation is free at the payment level; however, an OÜ distributing dividends from retained profits triggers 20/80 Estonian corporate income tax on the distributed amount (scheduled to increase to 22/78 for distributions in 2025), creating a tax cost but no currency or banking restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion periods, and no minimum percentage of export receipts to be converted into euros. Export receivables in any convertible currency may be held in foreign-currency bank accounts in Estonia or abroad and disposed of freely.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting as a Eurosystem central bank; no dedicated FX control administration exists because Estonia is a eurozone member and external financing supervision is shared with Finantsinspektsioon for credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for European Economic Area electronic money institutions and licensed payment institutions that have valid passporting notification to Eesti Pank, allowing virtual IBANs and English-document KYC remote onboarding. Mid-tier fintechs such as Airwallex can provide operational multi-currency accounts and payment clearing services, but the ultimate safeguarding of client funds must remain with an EU/EEA credit institution or e-money institution. Pure offshore or unlicensed EMIs are not legally viable in Estonia because they cannot issue e-money or provide local IBAN clearing services without a relevant license.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution from 1 January 2025; retained and reinvested profits are generally not subject to corporate income tax until distribution

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian company to a non-resident recipient. The Estonian company generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the net distribution. Historical reduced-rate dividend regimes and the 7% withholding mechanism do not generally apply to distributions made under the post-1 January 2025 regime, subject to transitional rules and payment-specific analysis.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No universal transaction-value threshold independently triggers a Local File for every Estonian taxpayer. Mandatory detailed transfer-pricing documentation generally applies to qualifying large enterprises and specified categories of controlled transactions. The principal large-enterprise indicators are at least EUR 50 million annual net sales, at least EUR 43 million balance-sheet total, or an average of at least 250 employees, together with other statutory categories such as listed companies, regulated financial institutions, or transactions involving specified low-tax jurisdictions. A Master File may be required for qualifying multinational groups. Arm's-length support remains required even where formal documentation thresholds are not met.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is assessed through transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial-ownership principles, controlled-foreign-company rules, anti-abuse provisions, tax residency analysis, and the legal characterization of transactions. The OÜ should maintain Estonian commercial rationale, decision-making evidence, functional personnel or outsourced operational capability, premises or logistics capacity appropriate to the fulfillment and kitting activity, accounting records, inventory and contract evidence, and risk-control documentation. An e-Residency card, registered address, nominee director, or bank account alone does not establish tax substance or tax residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax and statutory late-payment interest; administrative fines for reporting violations; and potential criminal liability under Estonian criminal law for aggravated tax evasion, including monetary penalties and imprisonment depending on the conduct, amount, intent, and aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment in serious cases, recovery of tax damage where statutory liability conditions are met, and director-disqualification consequences. Travel restrictions may be imposed only through applicable criminal or enforcement procedures; they are not an automatic consequence of an ordinary filing error.
Hidden profit distribution and non-business expenditure

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax on the taxable payment or distribution, late-payment interest, reassessment, penalties, and possible criminal treatment if the conduct is deliberate and fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability to the company for breach of fiduciary duties, restitution obligations, and, in insolvency, liability for damage caused by unlawful or delayed conduct. Criminal penalties may apply where the facts satisfy fraud, embezzlement, or tax-evasion offences.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment based on arm's-length pricing, late-payment interest, penalties for incorrect tax reporting, denial of unsupported deductions, and possible application of anti-avoidance rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where deliberate misconduct, gross negligence, insolvency prejudice, or criminal falsification is established. A mere difference in defensible transfer-pricing methodology does not automatically create personal criminal liability.
Shell-company, beneficial-ownership, and treaty-abuse risk

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic exemption, withholding-tax assessment, corporate income tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and potential reporting or beneficial-owner compliance action.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, tax-related liability where statutory requirements are satisfied, administrative sanctions, and criminal liability for deliberate false declarations, fraud, money laundering, or document falsification.
Anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, account restrictions, supervisory or administrative fines, confiscation, license or registration consequences where applicable, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or sanctions offences.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers and directors may face personal administrative or criminal liability, confiscation, imprisonment for serious money-laundering or sanctions conduct, and civil restitution. Travel restrictions may be ordered by competent authorities in criminal or enforcement proceedings.
Customs, VAT, and inventory misclassification

Statutory sanction: Customs duty and VAT reassessment, interest, administrative penalties, seizure or forfeiture in serious cases, and potential criminal liability for deliberate customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deliberate or grossly negligent breaches, especially where records are falsified or company assets are dissipated. Civil recovery and criminal sanctions depend on the applicable customs, VAT, and criminal provisions.
Payroll, employment, and fringe-benefit non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Back assessment of income tax, social tax, unemployment insurance, and pension contributions; interest; penalties; and employment-law sanctions where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for deliberate payroll-tax avoidance, unlawful employment practices, or insolvency-related non-payment. Criminal imprisonment is reserved for conduct meeting the relevant statutory offence threshold.
Accounting, annual-report, and record-retention failure

Statutory sanction: Compliance orders, administrative fines, forced filing measures, tax reassessment using indirect methods, loss of credibility in audits or tax proceedings, and possible criminal sanctions for intentional accounting falsification.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for damage caused by failure to organize accounting, for creditor prejudice, and for unlawful conduct during insolvency. Serious falsification or obstruction may result in criminal prosecution and imprisonment.
Insolvency and creditor-prejudice conduct

Statutory sanction: Reversal of transactions, insolvency-estate recovery, civil damages, tax recovery, fines, management restrictions, and criminal sanctions for aggravated creditor prejudice, fraudulent bankruptcy, or asset concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil liability for the company's losses, restitution, contribution to the insolvency estate, disqualification, and imprisonment where the conduct constitutes a criminal offence. Travel bans are fact-specific and require competent-authority action.
