Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial Register (Handelsregister) maintained by the Registry Court (Registergericht) at the Local Court (Amtsgericht) of the registered office (local jurisdiction of the Bundesland); all filings are transmitted electronically by a German civil-law notary (Notar) via the XJustiz portal.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (Gesetz betreffend die Gesellschaften mit beschränkter Haftung - GmbHG), Sections 1-8 (formation), 2(1a) (Model Protocol), 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft), 8-12 (Commercial Register registration), 35-44 (management and representation); Commercial Code (HGB), Section 5 et seq.; Civil Code (BGB), Section 181 (restrictions on self-dealing).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarised deed of incorporation - either the statutory Model Protocol (Musterprotokoll) under Section 2(1a) GmbHG or a customised Articles of Association (Gesellschaftsvertrag) - together with the application for registration (Anmeldung zur Eintragung) submitted to the Registergericht.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 40 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for a UG: a single share with a nominal amount of EUR 1.00 is sufficient (Section 5a(1) GmbHG). The fully subscribed cash contribution must be paid in and be freely at the managing director's disposal before registration, evidenced by a bank confirmation or notarial declaration. While the share capital remains below EUR 25,000, the company must retain 25% of its annual net profit as a statutory reserve until the EUR 25,000 threshold is reached. No escrow account is mandatory.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the leased office premises by the municipal building authority (Bauamt) and the fire service (Feuerwehr), including a fire protection concept (Brandschutzkonzept) where the building is open to the public; workplace clearance under the Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung) and the Industrial Safety Ordinance (Betriebssicherheitsverordnung); mandatory registration of employees with the statutory employer's liability insurance association (Berufsgenossenschaft - e.g., Verwaltungs-Berufsgenossenschaft, VBG), together with risk assessments under Section 5 of the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (Federal Ministry of Finance); central administrative authority: Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt); local assessment, audit and collection offices: Finanzamt; official electronic filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

For the domestic UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt): (1) notarial execution of the articles of association and entry in the Handelsregister; (2) submission of the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (standard tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER within 4-6 weeks of incorporation; (3) the locally competent Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (unified reference number for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT); (4) BZSt issues the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (German VAT ID) for intra-Community transactions and export evidence; (5) the UG is entered in the Transparenzregister and, from 2024/2025, must obtain the Wirtschafts-ID-Nummer (WIDN) from the Federal Statistical Office; under commercial law the UG must annually appropriate 25% of net profit to the statutory reserve until share capital of EUR 25,000 is reached. For a foreign entrepreneur without a German establishment: form USt 1 TG (VAT registration for foreign entrepreneurs) is filed with the competent Finanzamt (in practice Finanzamt Neukölln, Berlin, for non-established taxpayers), with evidence of foreign corporate existence and business activity; a German Steuernummer for VAT purposes is assigned, and a German USt-IdNr. is granted only where intra-Community acquisitions/supplies occur; no mandatory fiscal representative is required for standard VAT registration, although a domestic person authorized to accept service (Zustellungsbevollmächtigter) and electronic filing is advisable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG is a Kapitalgesellschaft per se: it is a standalone corporate taxpayer on worldwide income (subject to treaty relief) with Körperschaftsteuer at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 0.825%, and Gewerbesteuer at an effective municipal rate of approximately 7%-17% (14%-17% in major cities); it is a taxable person for VAT under section 2 UStG; shareholders are taxed separately on dividends and no flow-through or look-through treatment is available. — For the domestic UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt): (1) notarial execution of the articles of association and entry in the Handelsregister; (2) submission of the Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung (standard tax registration questionnaire) electronically via ELSTER within 4-6 weeks of incorporation; (3) the locally competent Finanzamt assigns the Steuernummer (unified reference number for corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT); (4) BZSt issues the Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (German VAT ID) for intra-Community transactions and export evidence; (5) the UG is entered in the Transparenzregister and, from 2024/2025, must obtain the Wirtschafts-ID-Nummer (WIDN) from the Federal Statistical Office; under commercial law the UG must annually appropriate 25% of net profit to the statutory reserve until share capital of EUR 25,000 is reached. For a foreign entrepreneur without a German establishment: form USt 1 TG (VAT registration for foreign entrepreneurs) is filed with the competent Finanzamt (in practice Finanzamt Neukölln, Berlin, for non-established taxpayers), with evidence of foreign corporate existence and business activity; a German Steuernummer for VAT purposes is assigned, and a German USt-IdNr. is granted only where intra-Community acquisitions/supplies occur; no mandatory fiscal representative is required for standard VAT registration, although a domestic person authorized to accept service (Zustellungsbevollmächtigter) and electronic filing is advisable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the UG is a Kapitalgesellschaft per se: it is a standalone corporate taxpayer on worldwide income (subject to treaty relief) with Körperschaftsteuer at 15%, solidarity surcharge at 0.825%, and Gewerbesteuer at an effective municipal rate of approximately 7%-17% (14%-17% in major cities); it is a taxable person for VAT under section 2 UStG; shareholders are taxed separately on dividends and no flow-through or look-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

(1) Organschaft (fiscal unity) under sections 14-19 KStG and section 2(2) No. 2 UStG: the UG may be a dependent entity of a German controlling corporation under a five-year profit-and-loss pooling agreement, consolidating corporate income tax, trade tax and VAT for the organ period. (2) Small-business exemption under section 19 UStG (Kleinunternehmerregelung): election to remain VAT-exempt without input VAT recovery where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 22,000 and current-year turnover does not exceed EUR 50,000 (prior-year threshold rising to EUR 25,000 for 2025). (3) Option to tax real property leases under section 9 UStG. (4) Reverse-charge mechanisms under section 13b UStG apply mandatorily in defined sectors and are not freely electable. (5) No US-style check-the-box classification exists; hybrid mismatch rules under sections 4k EStG, 8b KStG and 10 GewStG statutorily recharacterize hybrid instruments and entities without taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic German small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer, section 19 UStG): EUR 22,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 50,000 current-year ceiling (2025; prior-year threshold rising to EUR 25,000). Foreign non-resident suppliers have no general de minimis VAT registration threshold for German taxable supplies: cross-border B2B services supplied by a foreign entrepreneur to a German customer are reverse-charged to the German customer, so the foreign supplier is typically not required to register for German VAT; intra-Community distance sales of goods to German consumers trigger German VAT registration when the EU-wide OSS turnover threshold of EUR 10,000 is exceeded; B2C digital, advertising and electronically supplied services supplied by a non-EU entrepreneur require registration (or OSS filing via BZSt) from the first euro.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports to third countries are VAT-exempt with full input VAT deduction under sections 4 No. 1a and 6 UStG where (i) the German supplier or a commissioned third party physically dispatches or transports the goods, (ii) to a destination outside the EU, (iii) within the statutory period (normally three months; six months in justified cases), and (iv) conclusive export proof (Ausfuhrnachweis) is obtained and retained. For the AdTech/SSP programmatic advertising scope: B2B services supplied to customers established outside Germany or the EU are not taxable in Germany because the place of supply is the recipient's jurisdiction under section 3a(2) UStG (reverse charge falls on the recipient); B2C digital, advertising, DSP/SSP and other electronically supplied services to non-EU consumers fall outside German VAT under section 3a(5) UStG. Such offshore services are 'not subject to German VAT' rather than zero-rated in the goods/export sense, but they achieve the same economic result (no German output VAT, full input VAT credit) provided the supplier substantiates the non-German establishment of the recipient through coherent commercial evidence (contract, billing address, IP/location data, payment trail).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged electronically via the ATLAS system; the German supplier must appear as the customs exporter (Ausführer); the MRN (Movement Reference Number) and export accompanying document must be retained for inspection.
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill / CMR transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU territory, naming the German supplier as shipper and the non-EU destination, with dates consistent with the export declaration.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation (Zahlungsbeleg) evidencing that the foreign customer validly paid the export consideration into the German supplier's bank account, with clear FX reconciliation to the export invoice.

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API validation or digital stamping regime applies in Germany (unlike Italy, Hungary or Poland). Mandatory e-invoicing is phased in under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025 every German-resident business, including the UG, must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x) in B2B transactions; from 1 January 2027 suppliers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices for domestic B2B supplies; from 1 January 2028 all German suppliers must issue e-invoices. B2G invoicing already requires XRechnung submission. A non-resident entrepreneur acting purely as a reverse-charge supplier is not obliged to issue German-structured e-invoices where the German customer self-accounts the VAT.

Retrospective audit exposure: the Finanzamt may conduct an Außenprüfung under section 193 AO for up to ten years (assessment limitation period, section 169 AO). Where export zero-rating or out-of-scope treatment is unsupported, erroneous or fraudulent, German VAT is recomputed at 19% from the original due date with default interest of 0.15% per month (1.8% per annum, section 233a AO) and a late-payment surcharge (section 240 AO). Intentional VAT evasion (section 370 AO) is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, or up to ten years in particularly serious cases, including export-document fraud and carousel/missing-trader schemes; negligent tax shortfall (section 378 AO) attracts fines of up to EUR 50,000. Liability may attach simultaneously to the UG and its managing director, who may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes (section 69 AO); repeated abuse can lead to revocation of the VAT registration, joint and several liability for participants, referral to the customs investigation service (Zollfahndung), and automatic EU administrative cooperation under Regulation (EU) No 904/2010 and Eurofisc.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU law. No restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or capital repatriation for foreign investors. However, cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Bundesbank for statistical purposes (AWV reporting). No prior approval required for FX transactions, but documentation of underlying transactions must be retained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. German entities may hold foreign currency accounts and are free to retain or convert receipts. Reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments (including export proceeds) above €12,500, but no conversion ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) for banking supervision; Bundesbank administers foreign exchange reporting and statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. German fintech EMIs (e.g., N26 Business, Revolut Business, Penta) offer fast onboarding and multi-currency accounts, but may impose transaction limits and lack full corporate banking services (e.g., credit lines, complex trade finance). Legal constraints: EMIs must hold a license under PSD2 and be supervised by BaFin; they can provide IBANs and payment services but are not full banks. For high-volume cross-border adtech flows, traditional banks are more robust, but EMIs can serve as secondary accounts for operational efficiency.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the CIT amount (0.825%) plus municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate of 7.0%–17.15% depending on the municipal Hebesatz; aggregate statutory burden for a German UG typically approximates 29.0%–33.0%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

26.375% composed of 25.0% Kapitalertragsteuer (dividend withholding tax) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (1.375 percentage points); church tax surcharge may apply in addition. The rate is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the non-resident corporate shareholder holds at least 10% of the UG and the 12-month statutory minimum holding period is satisfied; DTA relief may further reduce or eliminate the rate for non-EU shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File: controlled cross-border transactions with related parties exceeding EUR 5,000,000 per annum (goods) or EUR 500,000 per annum (services) under the German Transfer Pricing Documentation Ordinance (GAufzV). Mandatory Master File: multinational group with consolidated annual turnover exceeding EUR 100,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 750,000,000. Failure to submit contemporaneous documentation triggers a penalty of 5%–10% of the transfer pricing adjustment, subject to a minimum of EUR 5,000.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Instead, substantive compliance is enforced through: (i) the place-of-effective-management test under Sec. 10 AO; (ii) the beneficial-ownership requirement for treaty and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief; (iii) German CFC rules under Secs. 7–13 AStG; (iv) the interest barrier (Sec. 4h EStG) and royalty barrier (Sec. 4j EStG); (v) the general anti-abuse doctrine under Sec. 42 AO, which recharacterizes transactions lacking economic substance. A UG operating a programmatic AdTech exchange must demonstrate real German substance — office, employees, management decisions, IT infrastructure, contractual risk-bearing, and client-facing functions — to preserve limited liability, treaty access, and deductibility of intercompany fees.
