Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Äriregister (Estonian Commercial Register), operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK) under the Ministry of Justice; filing is processed by the registry judge of Tartu County Court.

Governing statute

Commercial Code (Äriseadustik, RT I 1995, 26, 355), Chapter 3, §§ 135–147 governing the private limited company (Osaühing / OÜ); Articles of Association filing per § 137; General Part of the Civil Code Act (RT I 2002, 35, 216), §§ 44–55 (legal persons); e-Business Register electronic filing rules under the Register Maintenance Act.

Formation / constitutional filings

Foundation Resolution (asutamisotsus) and Memorandum of Association (asutamisleping) executed as a notarially attested deed or digitally signed via the e-Business Register (e-äriregister) portal, submitted under the standard commercial register application.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Default statutory minimum share capital is €2,500; however, an OÜ may be formed with zero or lower capital if expressly provided in the Articles of Association (§ 135(1)2). Contributions may be made in cash, non-cash assets, or bank guarantee payable within one year of registration. No escrow or blocked account requirement exists under Estonian law; capital becomes freely available after entry in the Commercial Register.

Funding & unlock path: A fire safety certificate (tuleohutussertifikaat) from the Estonian Rescue Board (Päästeamet) is required for the warehouse; structural load inspection of racking and reinforced storage floors; occupational working-environment clearance under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, including machinery inspection and operator training records.

Paid-in capital rule: Default statutory minimum share capital is €2,500; however, an OÜ may be formed with zero or lower capital if expressly provided in the Articles of Association (§ 135(1)2). Contributions may be made in cash, non-cash assets, or bank guarantee payable within one year of registration. No escrow or blocked account requirement exists under Estonian law; capital becomes freely available after entry in the Commercial Register.

No sectoral industrial license is required for wholesale trading in used construction machinery (ISIC 4659). If an operational warehouse or outdoor storage yard is established, the local municipality (vallavalitsus/linnavalitsus) serves as the building authority, and the Estonian Environmental Board (Keskkonnaamet) has jurisdiction over any pollution or waste permits.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (MTA) - Estonian Tax and Customs Board, Tallinn; digital service portal e-MTA (https://e-mta.ee) with access via ID-card, Mobile-ID, e-residency digital identity and the central state portal eesti.ee; company registration is administered jointly with the Department of State's Äriregister (e-Business Register).

E-government filing portal

For a resident Osaühing, incorporation through the e-Business Register automatically issues the statutory 8-digit Estonian registry code, which serves as the legal person's tax identification number and is the mandatory TIN for all MTA filings. A foreign entity without an Estonian registration must first appoint a local tax agent, submit constitutional documents, a power of attorney and proof of the home-state TIN, and, for VAT, file the standard KPR application (Application for Registration as a Taxable Person) through e-MTA; registration is generally completed within 5 business days. The prescribed non-resident VAT identification format is EE plus the 8-digit registry code; the same TIN is used for customs, excise, Intrastat and e-MTA clearance procedures.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the OÜ is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Estonian corporate income tax on profit distributions only (standard 20% gross in the 20/80 system, reduced 14% preferential rate for qualifying regular distributions), with 0% tax on retained and reinvested earnings; the entity has full unlimited tax liability on its worldwide income, but the deferral mechanism produces effective zero cash tax on undistributed export trading profits. Trading in used construction excavators is an ordinary commercial activity with no statutory license, and profits reinvested for working capital, stock of secondhand machinery or receivables from export sales are entirely untaxed until distribution. — For a resident Osaühing, incorporation through the e-Business Register automatically issues the statutory 8-digit Estonian registry code, which serves as the legal person's tax identification number and is the mandatory TIN for all MTA filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the OÜ is a resident corporate taxpayer subject to Estonian corporate income tax on profit distributions only (standard 20% gross in the 20/80 system, reduced 14% preferential rate for qualifying regular distributions), with 0% tax on retained and reinvested earnings; the entity has full unlimited tax liability on its worldwide income, but the deferral mechanism produces effective zero cash tax on undistributed export trading profits. Trading in used construction excavators is an ordinary commercial activity with no statutory license, and profits reinvested for working capital, stock of secondhand machinery or receivables from export sales are entirely untaxed until distribution.

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style check-the-box regime; the relevant statutory elections are: (i) VAT group registration for closely connected taxable persons; (ii) the Special Margin Scheme election for dealers in second-hand goods, which is directly applicable to the remarketing of used excavators and taxes the gross margin rather than the full selling price; (iii) the regular distribution election, by which an OÜ that has paid taxed distributions in at least two of the previous three years may apply the reduced 14/86 corporate income tax rate to further qualifying distributions; (iv) the election to apply EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive treatment and DTT-based withholding reductions to dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; and (v) mandatory alignment under the EU ATAD and anti-hybrid rules with no elective opt-out for hybrid mismatch arrangements involving Estonian entities exempt on retained earnings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supplies per calendar year; an Estonian-established seller must register from the beginning of the month in which the EUR 40,000 threshold is exceeded. A non-resident seller with no fixed establishment and no Estonian VAT registration has no threshold and must register from its first taxable supply in Estonia unless the transaction is covered by the OSS/IOSS schemes, constitutes an intra-Community B2B supply subject to reverse charge, or is an export supplied to a third country at 0%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of used construction excavators is zero-rated at 0% VAT where the OÜ exports the goods outside the European Union and the goods physically leave the EU territory under a customs export procedure. The seller must hold a written export declaration (EXA/LA) accepted by Estonian customs, transport evidence of the outward movement (CMR, bill of lading, air waybill, rail consignment or combined transport document), and the matching commercial documentation; the supply qualifies even where the buyer organises collection by a third party, provided the exporter proves the transfer of title to the non-EU customer and the actual exit from the EU. Use of the used-goods margin scheme does not prejudice zero-rating of the export transaction, and input VAT attributable to the exported machinery remains deductible; export proceeds are commercially expected to be settled and repatriated to the OÜ account within 180 days from the supply date.

  • Customs export declaration accepted by the Estonian Tax and Customs Board (single administrative document EXA/LA) with the export activity code and release for export
  • Transport documents evidencing physical departure from the EU and arrival in the third-country destination (CMR, bill of lading, air waybill, railway consignment or multimodal combined transport document)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract specifying the ISIC 4659 / HS-CN 8429.52-8429.59 used hydraulic excavator or roller, including machine identification number, weight, engine hours, age and a clear statement of 0% VAT zero-rated export supply
  • Bill of lading or master waybill with discrete consignment details matching the customs export number and the description of the used machinery
  • Inward remittance certificate in the form of a Swift MT103 bank payment confirmation or equivalent bank account statement proving that the non-EU buyer's sales proceeds were credited to the OÜ's Estonian bank account
  • Packing list, pre-export inspection certificate, condition report and the original procurement invoice of the previous purchase to substantiate secondhand trading status and the correct application of the margin scheme or input VAT deduction

VAT returns (KMD/KMDF forms and annexes) must be filed electronically via e-MTA on a monthly or general tax period basis no later than the 20th day of the month following the tax period. Estonia does not currently operate a real-time invoice clearance, digital stamping, SAF-T or continuous transaction control system; B2G e-invoicing to Estonian public authorities is mandatory in PEPPOL-BIS 3.0 UBL format, while B2B e-invoices in E-arve, Finvoice or equivalent XML standards are accepted. The EU ViDA digital reporting requirements are not yet transposed and will apply no earlier than 2030; MTA currently relies on the KMD returns, customs export records and Intrastat declarations for transaction-level analytics.

MTA conducts risk-based scrutiny by cross-matching the KMD VAT return, the customs export declaration, the EXA release, transport waybills and the Swift payment trail. Where zero-rating is not substantiated, the supply is recharacterised as a domestic Estonian sale and reassessed at the standard 22% VAT rate, a surcharge of up to 30% of the reassessed VAT difference is imposed under the Tax Procedure Code, daily penalty interest of 0.06% accrues from the original due date, and any input VAT refund is clawed back with interest. Misdemeanour fines for documentary violations reach EUR 1,300 for a legal person, criminal tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years (up to 7 years for large-scale or organised offences), and the general three-year reassessment limitation period is extended to six years in cases of fraud or concealment; false use of the used-goods margin scheme invalidates the scheme retroactively and disqualifies the OÜ from zero-rating on all ongoing export supplies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a Eurozone member with EUR as legal tender. Both current and capital account transactions are fully liberalised under TFEU Articles 63-66, with no exchange controls, no convertibility restrictions, and no authorisation requirement for profit remittance. Corporate retained earnings are taxed at 0%; distributions trigger tax at 20% (or 14% for regular distributions) with no exchange-control hurdle. Qualifying EEA shareholders generally receive dividends without Estonian withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonian legislation imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate export proceeds. Export receipts in EUR or foreign currency may be retained freely in resident or non-resident bank accounts.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), a Eurosystem central bank; financial and banking supervision is conducted by the integrated Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for EUR-denominated current accounts, global payment routing and virtual IBANs from authorised electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Monese, Holvi, Payhawk), which are passported under PSD2 and supervised by Finantsinspektsioon or a home EEA regulator. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot extend credit, issue commercial letters of credit, or hold client funds on its balance sheet; funds must be safeguarded in segregated accounts at a partner bank. High-value used-machinery settlements in USD and documentary credit needs therefore require a Tier-1 bank for the concluding leg; EMIs may set transaction thresholds and enhanced due diligence on wholesale goods traders.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity or shareholder-loan funds are routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (EUR or USD) to the OÜ's EUR IBAN at a Tier-1 Estonian bank using ISO 20022 purpose code CAPT (capital payments) or AITR (investment in trade). The remittance must be accompanied by a notarised board/shareholder resolution specifying equity contribution versus shareholder loan; equity contributions are filed with the Estonian Business Register. Within the SEPA zone, capital injections may be effected by SEPA Credit Transfer with extended remittance information; large same-day euro values may be settled through TARGET2. Beneficiary banks will apply a source-of-funds questionnaire and may require bank statements evidencing the origin of the shareholder's wealth.Open-account supplier payments are executed via SWIFT MT103. Trade settlements with documentary protection use irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued through the bank's trade desk (SWIFT MT700/MT707), or documentary collection under MT400-series if agreed. Intra-EEA euro disbursements may clear via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant; large-value euro obligations clear through TARGET2; USD obligations are cleared through the bank's US correspondent bank (Fedwire or CHIPS leg). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% on distributed profits from 1 January 2025, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution. Retained and reinvested profits are not subject to Estonian corporate income tax until distribution or another taxable extraction.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian OÜ to a non-resident shareholder from 2025 onward. The OÜ generally pays 22% corporate income tax on the distributed profit, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution. Transitional rules may apply to distributions based on profits taxed under the pre-2025 14/86 regime. Dividends paid to resident or non-resident individuals may require separate analysis for historic transitional dividends and foreign residence-country taxation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 annual turnover, EUR 43,000,000 balance-sheet total, and 250 average employees are the principal size thresholds relevant to mandatory comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation for qualifying taxpayers. Additional categories, including certain regulated entities, listed groups, and transactions involving associated persons in low-tax jurisdictions, may trigger documentation or information obligations regardless of ordinary size thresholds. Estonia does not use a single universal transaction-value threshold that safely replaces the entity-level analysis.

Economic substance scope: Estonia has no standalone economic-substance regime identical to the United Arab Emirates ESR framework. Substance remains critical under transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance rule, treaty beneficial-ownership and principal-purpose standards, permanent-establishment rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, and anti-conduit principles. An Estonian OÜ conducting genuine machinery trading should evidence local decision-making, inventory and title control, warehouse or logistics arrangements, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, customer and supplier functions, banking control, commercial risk assumption, and contemporaneous accounting records. E-residency, registered-office services, nominee management, and a bank account alone do not establish commercial substance or tax residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent VAT recovery

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment, repayment of tax and improperly refunded VAT, statutory interest, administrative fines, and criminal liability for intentional tax fraud. Serious or large-scale conduct may carry a fine or imprisonment, with aggravated cases subject to higher imprisonment ranges under the Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Limited liability is not a defense to personal criminal liability. Directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, confiscation, civil recovery, disqualification consequences, and personal tax liability where they intentionally or negligently cause the company to fail to pay tax or make unlawful distributions. Travel restrictions may be ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings where legally justified; imprisonment is possible for qualifying tax crimes.
Money laundering and illicit funds

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting, account restrictions, transaction rejection, asset freezing or confiscation, administrative AML penalties, and criminal money-laundering liability. Corporate and individual sanctions depend on the offense, value, organization, and aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, compliance officers, and persons who knowingly facilitate laundering may be prosecuted personally. Penalties may include fines, imprisonment, confiscation, management disqualification, and procedural travel restrictions. The corporate veil does not shield knowing participants.
Customs fraud, sanctions, and export-control breach

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, duties and import VAT, interest, seizure, forfeiture, administrative penalties, loss of customs simplifications, and criminal sanctions for intentional sanctions evasion, smuggling, or customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible employees may incur personal criminal liability for knowing participation, authorization, or reckless compliance failures. Asset confiscation, management restrictions, travel limitations, and imprisonment may apply in serious cases.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and concealed profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length income or expense, corporate income tax on taxable distributions, withholding-tax exposure, denial of deductions where relevant, interest, documentation penalties, and possible anti-avoidance reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unlawful distributions, creditor damage, accounting breaches, and tax losses caused by willful or grossly negligent conduct. Personal liability is fact-dependent and is not automatic merely because the company is tax reassessed.
Failure to maintain accounting, inventory, and beneficial-ownership records

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, adverse tax assessment, rejection of evidence, VAT denial, accounting-related sanctions, AML enforcement, audit escalation, and possible criminal liability where records are intentionally falsified or destroyed.

Director personal liability: Management-board members have statutory accounting and filing responsibilities. They may face fines, civil damages, disqualification, and criminal liability for deliberate falsification, destruction of records, or obstruction. A registered-office provider or e-resident service does not assume the OÜ's management duties.
Late filing, non-payment, and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Late-payment interest, tax collection measures, enforcement against company assets, administrative fines, personal-liability proceedings, insolvency clawback, and criminal liability for insolvency-related misconduct where statutory elements are satisfied.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to compensate creditors for breaches of duty, file for insolvency when required, repay unlawful distributions, and cover tax damage caused by deliberate or grossly negligent conduct. Courts may impose management disqualification, procedural travel restrictions, or imprisonment for qualifying offenses.
Permanent-establishment and place-of-effective-management failure

Statutory sanction: Foreign-country corporate tax, VAT, payroll, and registration exposure; Estonian reassessment; treaty disputes; interest and penalties; and possible dual-residence or permanent-establishment litigation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal sanctions for false filings, tax evasion, or breach of management duties if the structure is intentionally misrepresented. Ordinary cross-border tax exposure alone does not automatically create personal imprisonment or veil piercing.
