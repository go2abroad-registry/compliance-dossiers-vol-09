Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital EUR 1 per shareholder; capital must be fully subscribed and paid in cash or bank transfer to a German corporate account. No escrow account is mandatory for cash contributions. The UG must retain 25% of annual net profits as a legal reserve until the share capital reaches EUR 25,000, at which point it may convert to a regular GmbH.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Brandschutznachweis) may be required for the office premises if the building is open to the public or has more than 1,800 m² gross floor area; structural load inspection is not required for standard office occupancy; workplace safety clearance under the German Occupational Safety and Health Act (Arbeitsschutzgesetz) and Workplace Ordinance (Arbeitsstättenverordnung), including fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and first-aid provisions. A written occupancy certificate (Nutzungsgenehmigung) for a pure office use is typically exempt if the building is already approved for administrative use.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Ministry of Finance (Bundesministerium der Finanzen, BMF); EU VAT identification number administration by the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) via the 'Mein BZSt' OnlinePortal (https://www.bzst.de); local tax assessment and Steuernummer issuance by the locally competent Finanzamt via the ELSTER portal (https://www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) must first be notarized and registered in the German Commercial Register (Handelsregister). The UG then files the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the locally competent Finanzamt through ELSTER, disclosing shareholders, managing directors, domestic business address, and intended business activities. The Finanzamt assigns the statutory Steuernummer for corporate income tax, trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), payroll, and VAT. For the separate EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), the UG applies to the BZSt through the BZSt OnlinePortal; for entities with a domestic establishment, the VAT ID is issued after Finanzamt confirmation. A foreign entity without a domestic establishment uses BZSt form 'USt 1 TU' and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative. The UG must maintain GoBD-compliant accounting records from inception.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German tax law provides no check-the-box election; a UG is always an opaque, entity-level taxpayer. Available elections include: (i) VAT fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG with a financially integrated domestic parent; (ii) corporate income tax and trade tax Organschaft via a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) under Section 14 KStG and Section 2(2) GewStG; (iii) the Section 1a KStG option for partnerships to elect corporate taxation, which is not applicable to a UG; and (iv) mandatory hybrid-mismatch counteraction measures under Section 4k EStG, which may deny deductions for hybrid instruments between related entities. A UG with its seat and place of management in Germany is subject to unlimited worldwide corporate income tax at 15%, solidarity surcharge of 5.5% on corporate tax, and municipal trade tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 100,000 current-year forecast for the domestic small-business exemption (Kleinunternehmer, Section 19 UStG, effective 1 January 2025; previously EUR 22,000/EUR 50,000); EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual turnover for cross-border B2C distance sales and electronically supplied services under the One-Stop-Shop (OSS); mandatory VAT registration without threshold applies to foreign entrepreneurs without a domestic establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: an export supply to a destination outside the EU is zero-rated under Section 6 UStG, provided the goods physically leave the EU and the supplier retains documentary proof of export (customs export declaration and transport evidence). Services: B2B services supplied to non-EU customers fall outside German VAT because the place of supply is the customer's seat under Section 3a(2) UStG; intra-EU B2B services are taxable in the customer's member state via reverse charge and are zero-rated in Germany with mandatory VIES EC Sales List reporting; B2C electronically supplied services (including PropTech SaaS) are taxed at the customer's residence, so non-EU customers are outside German VAT. The supplier must document customer status (valid EU VAT ID for B2B reverse charge), non-EU residence/seat evidence, contract terms, IP-location logs, payment records, and actual service-delivery records.

  • ATLAS customs export declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) with the ABC-vorgangsnummer / export reference number
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and underlying sales contract
  • Proof of export / arrival: Gelangensbestätigung or destination-country customs clearance certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or authenticated SWIFT MT103 evidencing FX receipt and repatriation

Section 14 UStG mandates B2B e-invoicing in structured XML formats XRechnung or ZUGFeRD/Factur-X. From 1 January 2025, all domestic B2B recipients must be technically able to receive e-invoices; from 1 January 2027, issuers with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028, all domestic B2B issuers must issue e-invoices. Germany does not operate a real-time tax-authority clearance or pre-invoicing API, and no digital stamping or watermark is required. Fiscal audit compliance is enforced through GoBD/GDPdU record-keeping and access rights; cash register systems are separately subject to certified TSE (Technische Sicherheitseinrichtung) requirements, which do not apply to SaaS invoicing.

Export zero-rating is heavily scrutinized by the BZSt and local Finanzamt anti-fraud units. Missing or falsified export evidence triggers retrospective VAT assessment on the exported supplies, denial of corresponding input VAT deductions, and late-payment interest of 0.5% per month under Section 233a AO. Intentional or reckless evasion is a criminal offense under Section 370 AO, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years (up to ten years in aggravated cases) and fines; voluntary disclosure under Section 371 AO is barred once fraud is detected. Managing directors may be personally liable under Sections 34 and 69 AO and third parties under Section 71 AO. The regular VAT assessment period is five years, but a ten-year period applies where facts have been concealed, allowing retrospective clawback of claimed zero ratings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany, as a euro-area Member State, maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility without exchange controls or central-bank approval gates, reflecting Article 63 TFEU free movement of capital and payments. A German UG (haftungsbeschränkt) may invoice, pay, hold, and remit in any convertible currency. Profit-remittance hurdles are tax-structural rather than FX-structural: dividends to non-resident shareholders carry German withholding tax of 26.375% (25% capital-yield tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge, plus church tax where applicable), which may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% direct holding, 12-month minimum holding period) or lowered under an applicable double tax treaty through a refund application filed with the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern). No foreign-exchange permission is required for dividend flows, intercompany interest, royalty, or equity repatriation. Administrative obligations are limited to Deutsche Bundesbank statistical reporting for every cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 and, where the aggregate foreign receivable/payable balance exceeds EUR 5,000,000, to supplementary external-position (Bestandsmeldung) reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany statutorily imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no forced repatriation window, and no mandatory conversion ratio (effective conversion requirement: 0%). A UG may retain 100% of export proceeds and foreign-currency SaaS subscription receipts indefinitely in any bank account, onshore or abroad. The sole binding rule is statistical: each payment received from a non-resident or paid to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank through the electronic AWV reporting portal (Allgemeines Meldeportal) no later than the fifth working day following the month in which the payment occurred. Goods-related export deliveries must also be captured in the Bundesbank goods schedule, and continuing foreign receivables/payables above EUR 5,000,000 require a consolidated balance-of-payments position report. No central-bank approval, license, or earmarking is required for invoicing in USD, CNY, or other foreign currencies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, Frankfurt am Main) as the competent authority for foreign-exchange statistics and cross-border payment reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); prudential banking and AML/CFT supervision rests with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht – BaFin).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs); Low for third-country (non-EEA) digital banks. Under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG), an EMI authorized in another EEA member state may passport into Germany and issue EUR IBAN accounts, multi-currency wallets, and API-driven treasury rails. A UK, US, or Swiss digital bank without an EEA passport cannot lawfully solicit or operate payment accounts for a German-resident UG without a German ZAG/KWG license or an agent model. Material constraints: (i) EMI balances are safeguarded, not deposit-insured, and German tax offices, landlords, and certain contracting public bodies may reject non-German IBANs; (ii) if the PropTech UG collects tenant rents, landlord distributions, or security deposits on behalf of third parties, routing such funds through an offshore EMI without a payment-institution license constitutes unlawful payment services under Section 1 ZAG and Section 32 KWG, exposing the UG to BaFin enforcement; (iii) AWV reporting thresholds apply identically to funds held with EMIs, and the UG remains the reporting obligor. Recommended architecture: use a tier-1 German bank account as the primary treasury and escrow hub, and employ an EEA-passported EMI only as a foreign-currency payout wallet for cross-border vendor disbursements, never as the principal clearing account for tenant funds.

Equity contributions and shareholder loans from non-resident shareholders must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR within the EEA) into the UG's German beneficiary account under the exact registered legal name 'UG (haftungsbeschränkt)'. Recommended ISO 20022 Purpose Codes: 'EQUI' for equity injections and paid-in capital; 'INTC' for intercompany shareholder loans; 'LOAN' for documented bilateral credit arrangements; 'CASH' for general treasury movements. The wire reference should include the shareholder number and capital-contribution file reference so the bank's transaction-monitoring unit can map the credit to the notarized capital-contribution certificate (Einlagenzahlungsbestätigung), the shareholder resolution, and the loan agreement. All inbound payments exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank AWV portal by the fifth working day following the month of receipt; cumulative foreign claims and liabilities above EUR 5,000,000 trigger the supplementary external-position report. The same concentration architecture applies to USD subscription receipts swept from Stripe/ACH Direct: USD collections accumulate in a foreign-currency concentration account at the German primary bank and are converted only at target rates, with each conversion leg separately reported to the Bundesbank where the EUR equivalent exceeds EUR 12,500.Outbound treasury disbursements settle over multi-rail architecture: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) for EUR payments to EEA beneficiaries; (2) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for urgent EUR payments below EUR 100,000; (3) SWIFT MT103 with gpi UETR tracking for USD, GBP, CHF, and all non-EEA or non-euro clearings; (4) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600) for high-value hardware or IoT-device procurement bundled with the real-estate SaaS, where bank-secured settlement against transport documents is required; (5) SEPA Core B2B Direct Debit for collecting landlord subscription fees as a liquidity-acceleration rail. Dividend remittances to foreign shareholders require withholding-tax computation, the shareholder's tax residency certificate (Ansässigkeitsbescheinigung), and, where applicable, an EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief application so the paying bank applies the correct treaty rate. Every outbound cross-border payment above EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within the monthly five-working-day statutory window, and an arm's-length intercompany pricing file (TP documentation) must substantiate all related-party disbursements, including royalty, SaaS-license, and cloud-infrastructure charges.Outright FX Forward (Devisentermingeschäft) on EUR/USD and EUR/GBP matching forecast ACH/Stripe subscription revenue; documented under the German Master Agreement for Financial Derivative Transactions (DRV) or ISDA Master Agreement.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) on restricted currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, IDR, PHP) where onshore deliverability is legally unavailable; settlement in EUR/USD against emerging-market property-tenant remittance inflows.FX Swap (Devisenswap) combining spot and forward legs for rolling EUR/USD liquidity across value dates.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCS) converting EUR fixed-rate debt into USD floating-rate exposure to hedge long-term international software-licensing contracts.Zero-Cost EUR/USD Collar or participating forward preserving upside participation while capping downside on the SaaS dollar revenue stream.Vanilla European-style FX Options under the ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions, with EMIR trade reporting to a registered trade repository and IFRS 9 cash-flow hedge designation where effectiveness testing is passed.Prudential boundary: the non-licensed UG treasury may transact only in risk-reduction hedging instruments; speculative or market-making FX activity would constitute financial services requiring a BaFin licence under Section 1 and Section 32 KWG and is strictly impermissible.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for EEA-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs); Low for third-country (non-EEA) digital banks. Under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz – ZAG), an EMI authorized in another EEA member state may passport into Germany and issue EUR IBAN accounts, multi-currency wallets, and API-driven treasury rails. A UK, US, or Swiss digital bank without an EEA passport cannot lawfully solicit or operate payment accounts for a German-resident UG without a German ZAG/KWG license or an agent model. Material constraints: (i) EMI balances are safeguarded, not deposit-insured, and German tax offices, landlords, and certain contracting public bodies may reject non-German IBANs; (ii) if the PropTech UG collects tenant rents, landlord distributions, or security deposits on behalf of third parties, routing such funds through an offshore EMI without a payment-institution license constitutes unlawful payment services under Section 1 ZAG and Section 32 KWG, exposing the UG to BaFin enforcement; (iii) AWV reporting thresholds apply identically to funds held with EMIs, and the UG remains the reporting obligor. Recommended architecture: use a tier-1 German bank account as the primary treasury and escrow hub, and employ an EEA-passported EMI only as a foreign-currency payout wallet for cross-border vendor disbursements, never as the principal clearing account for tenant funds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15.0% Körperschaftsteuer plus 5.5% Solidaritätszuschlag on the assessed corporate tax (equivalent to 0.825% of taxable income) plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at a 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200–490%, yielding 14–17%); the aggregate effective corporate income tax load for a German UG is ordinarily 30–33% of taxable profit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% Kapitalertragsteuer on gross dividends, plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withheld amount, producing an aggregate statutory rate of 26.375%; the 95% exemption of § 8b KStG is not available to a non-resident shareholder unless the dividend is allocated to a German permanent establishment of the foreign shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Gewinnabgrenzungsaufzeichnungsverordnung (GAufzV, in force since 2021), contemporaneous documentation is mandatory when the annual volume of ordinary cross-border related-party transactions exceeds EUR 100,000 or when a single extraordinary transaction exceeds EUR 50,000; Master File and Local File content following OECD TP Guidelines must be available upon official request under § 90(3) AO within 30–60 days; groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million additionally file a country-by-country report with the BZSt.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); the functional equivalent is the place-of-effective-management doctrine (Ort der Geschäftsleitung) under § 10 AO, requiring the UG to hold board meetings, take strategic decisions, keep management records and maintain operational substance inside Germany. A purely remote-managed UG risks re-allocation of its taxable income to the jurisdiction of actual management, loss of tax preferences, treaty benefit denial under § 50d(3) EStG and re-characterization as an abuse of legal form under § 42 AO.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to five years and an unlimited financial fine under § 370 AO; aggravated cases under § 370(3) AO attract six months to ten years of imprisonment; the fine amount for company-level misdeclarations is ordinarily calculated at up to the amount of tax evaded and may be doubled in aggravated circumstances.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and statutory representatives are personally liable for evaded corporate taxes under §§ 69 and 71 AO even if the UG becomes insolvent; enforcement includes restitution demands, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, professional disqualification and criminal prosecution of the responsible individuals.
Shell company / lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: The Finanzamt re-characterizes the arrangement as an abuse of law under § 42 AO, allocates profits to the actual management jurisdiction, denies treaty relief, reassesses trade tax and imposes punitive interest at 0.5% per month on the resulting underpayments.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to maintain the statutory reserve under § 5a GmbHG or who allow the UG to operate without genuine economic substance are jointly and severally liable; criminal tax investigations may extend to the personal assets of the management, including bans on leaving Germany.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Surcharge under § 162(4) AO of at least 10% of the transfer pricing adjustment, increased to 15% where records are not furnished at all; the Finanzamt additionally may estimate income at its discretion, disallow the expense deduction, and impose interest; documentation may be demanded within 30–60 days.

Director personal liability: Directors bear personal liability for the resulting surcharges and unpaid taxes under §§ 69 and 71 AO; repeated false documentation declarations trigger criminal liability under § 370 AO for the signing officers.
Anti-money-laundering and sanctions exposure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German GwG of up to EUR 10 million or 10% of global annual turnover, and up to twice the economic benefit derived from the violation; a U.S. ACH-cleared payment path exposes the UG to OFAC designation, asset freezes and secondary sanctions.

Director personal liability: Deliberate money laundering is prosecuted under § 261 StGB with imprisonment up to five years; directors additionally face travel bans, personal asset seizure and disqualification from serving in any financial or real-estate related business.
Non-resident dividend withholding under-withholding

Statutory sanction: The German UG becomes liable as withholding agent for the full uncollected amount under §§ 44a–45d EStG; back taxes accrue interest at 6% per annum, late-payment surcharges apply, and separate fines of up to EUR 50,000 may be imposed for failure to issue withholding certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors are made personally liable for the entire non-withheld dividend tax under §§ 69 and 71 AO, the liability being independent of corporate insolvency; intentional under-withholding converts the conduct into criminal tax evasion with up to five years imprisonment.
