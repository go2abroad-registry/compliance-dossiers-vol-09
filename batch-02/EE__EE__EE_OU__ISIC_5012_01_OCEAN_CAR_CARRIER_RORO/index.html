Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); principal electronic portal: e-MTA (https://www.emta.ee). Entity incorporation and registry-code administration are handled through the Estonian Commercial Register maintained by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (Registrite ja Infosüsteemide Keskus, RIK), including the e-Business Register (https://ariregister.rik.ee).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate or register the OÜ and obtain its nine-digit Commercial Register code, which generally functions as the entity identifier for Estonian tax administration. 2. Establish access to e-MTA through an authorized management-board member, Estonian representative, or power-of-attorney holder; e-Residency is an access credential and does not itself create tax residence. 3. Apply electronically in e-MTA or submit the prescribed application to MTA for VAT registration (KMKR) when Estonia taxable supplies require registration. The Estonian VAT number is normally formatted as EE plus the nine-digit registry code. 4. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An OÜ is treated as a separate Estonian resident corporate taxpayer if incorporated in Estonia or managed from Estonia. Estonia generally does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The principal corporate-tax model taxes distributed profits and certain profit-equivalent payments rather than undistributed accounting profits. From 1 January 2025, regular profit distributions are generally subject to corporate income tax at 22/78 of the net amount distributed; retained profits are not subject to ordinary corporate income tax solely because they are earned. No general entity-level election converts an OÜ into a transparent partnership for Estonian tax purposes. Treaty-based residence, permanent-establishment, transfer-pricing, and place-of-effective-management analyses remain relevant. Estonian anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing EU ATAD II can deny deductions, include income, or otherwise neutralize mismatches; they are statutory rules rather than elective classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply generated in Estonia during a calendar year is the principal domestic VAT-registration threshold. The threshold generally does not protect a non-established supplier where the place of supply is Estonia and the applicable reverse-charge or special-scheme exception does not apply. A foreign supplier may therefore require immediate Estonian VAT registration for relevant Estonian taxable supplies. The threshold is not a customs threshold and does not apply to every cross-border transaction. The standard VAT rate is 24% from 1 July 2025; supplies made before that date were subject to the rate then in force.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Estonia or another EU Member State to a destination outside the EU customs territory may generally qualify for 0% VAT if the supplier or an authorized representative can substantiate that the goods left the EU and the transaction satisfies the Estonian VAT Act and Union Customs Code requirements. For a Ro-Ro carrier, the freight service must be analyzed separately from the vehicle sale: international transport of goods and directly related services may qualify for the applicable 0% treatment or exemption where the statutory conditions are met, while a B2B service whose place of supply is outside Estonia is generally outside the scope of Estonian VAT rather than technically zero-rated. Intra-EU supplies are not exports; a qualifying intra-Community supply may be zero-rated only where the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State, the goods move to that Member State, and transport and reporting conditions are satisfied. Export VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, business-purpose evidence, customs or transport evidence, and accurate VAT returns. A valid export declaration alone is insufficient where the carrier cannot link the transport service, goods, customer, and export movement.

  • Electronic customs export declaration containing the correct commodity, consignor, consignee, customs procedure, value, and transport information
  • Export Movement Reference Number (MRN) and electronic exit confirmation, normally evidenced through the EU customs export-control system
  • Commercial invoice, transport contract or booking confirmation, vehicle or cargo manifest, and packing or vehicle list
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, or equivalent maritime transport document identifying the cargo, vessel, ports, and relevant parties
  • Evidence linking the carrier's service invoice to the exported vehicles or other cargo, including loading records, terminal records, and proof of delivery or destination where available
  • For intra-Community supplies rather than exports: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative-statement reporting
  • Foreign-exchange or inward-remittance certificates are not a general statutory Estonian export-VAT requirement; bank records may nevertheless be retained as commercial and audit evidence

Estonia does not impose a universal real-time, transaction-by-transaction VAT clearance mandate for ordinary private-sector invoices. VAT invoices must satisfy the Estonian VAT Act and may be issued electronically if authenticity, integrity, and legibility are maintained. VAT returns and related declarations are filed electronically through e-MTA, generally by the 20th day of the following month; EU recapitulative statements and customs declarations are also electronically processed where applicable. E-invoices are used extensively in public procurement and public-sector transactions, and a buyer may request an e-invoice in circumstances prescribed by Estonian law, but ordinary B2B invoices are not generally subject to mandatory real-time digital stamping. Customs export declarations are submitted through the EU customs electronic systems and receive an MRN; export completion is evidenced by electronic exit confirmation rather than a general VAT-clearance stamp. Structured e-invoice and accounting-system interoperability should be implemented where required by the customer, public procurement rules, or contractual arrangements.

Estonia has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export receipts may remain offshore, subject to ordinary accounting, anti-money-laundering, customs, transfer-pricing, and banking requirements. MTA may deny or reverse 0% VAT where export departure, transport, customer status, place of supply, or documentary linkage is not substantiated; the unpaid VAT becomes assessable, normally together with statutory late-payment interest. Tax assessments may cover the legally permitted assessment period, generally three years and up to five years where intentional non-compliance or a tax offence is involved. Interest is calculated under the applicable Estonian tax-interest rules and may be reduced or increased only as permitted by law. Fraudulent invoices, fabricated export evidence, concealed sales, or deliberate VAT evasion may trigger administrative penalties, recovery of improperly refunded VAT, enforcement against the taxpayer, and criminal liability for serious tax offences. Directors and responsible persons may face personal or criminal consequences where statutory liability conditions are met. The taxpayer must preserve invoices, customs records, transport evidence, contracts, and accounting data for the applicable retention period and must promptly correct inaccurate VAT returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible and the only legal tender in Estonia. Estonia maintains full capital and current account liberalisation as a euro area Member State; no exchange control permits, prior authorisations or repatriation approvals are required for current or capital account transactions under national law. Dividend and profit remittance to EU/EEA shareholders is unrestricted, subject only to the Estonian corporate income tax applicable at the level of the distributing company (20% standard regular distribution, 14% reduced rate for regular distributions under the Estonian Tax Act, and 0% on undistributed profits). No FX conversion approval, surrender requirement or central bank consultation is required for paying freight, charter hire, vessel operating expenses or shareholder distributions, provided that underlying transactions are properly reported under Eesti Pank statistical declarations when exceeding EUR 20,000 per transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia applies no export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or forced repatriation requirements for international shipping services. Export receipts from Ro-Ro automotive transport, charter hire and ancillary port fees are classified as services in the balance of payments and must be reported to Eesti Pank on an aggregated monthly basis if total turnover exceeds EUR 20,000. Such proceeds may be held in EUR or any foreign currency retained in the operating account; no requirement exists to convert to EUR on receipt or at any subsequent date.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), operating within the Eurosystem under the authority of the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and foreign exchange operations; the Estonian Financial Supervisory Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) is not a separate institution and operates as a division of Eesti Pank. Foreign exchange reporting and balance-of-payments compilation are administered by Eesti Pank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for ancillary collection and payer networks; Low for primary treasury and settlement of ocean shipping. EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe, Paysera (UAB Paysera LT) and Revolut (Revolut Ltd) operate in Estonia under the PSD2 payment services framework, enabling current account-opening with remote onboarding and SEPA/MT103-like transfers. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit or issue commercial letters of credit; (ii) trade-related proceeds involving high-value Ro-Ro transactions may trigger strict source-of-wealth review, often resulting in account holds; (iii) Offshore non-EU EMIs without a branch license cannot be used for Estonia-issued capital contributions or trade payments; (iv) the bank account in the Estonian e-Business Register must be a licensed credit institution account to meet corporate liquidity requirements. Therefore, an EMI is only suitable as a supplementary liquidity channel, not as the principal clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 (non-EUR) or SEPA Transfer (EUR) to a Estonian-licensed euro settlement account. The instruction must state the payment purpose as share capital increase or subscription, and include the beneficial owner as the OÜ, the shareholder's international bank account number (IBAN), and a reference to the notarized share subscription resolution entered into the Estonian e-Business Register. Preferred ISO 20022 purpose codes for intra-EEA transfers are 'CONF' (confirmed investments) or 'EQUI' (price risk hedging) as aligned with the bank's guidelines; for non-EUR transactions, MT103 field 70 should contain 'CAPITAL INJECTION EE_OU' and field 72 cover for 'BENEFICIARY ACCOUNT' with the Estonian registry code to avoid tax misclassification. All incoming transfers must be reconciled within 30 days to avoid suspicious-transaction flags; if the amount is above EUR 50,000, Eesti Pank balance-of-payments reporting is triggered automatically by the receiving bank.Outbound payments for vessel charter hire, bunkers, port dues and crew wages in the Ro-Ro segment are settled through SWIFT MT103, after prior validation with an EU anti-money-laundering screening system. For high-value long-term charter payments, the commercial letter of credit via SWIFT MT700 is the preferred rail, typically issued by the Estonian buyer's bank as a revocable or irrevocable LC in favour of the supplier. Under a standard CIS alternative, EUR-denominated freight settlements are executed using the euro real-time gross settlement system (TARGET2) via the Estonian bank's correspondent account, while USD and GBP payments are routed through the bank's offshore nostro accounts (commonly at JPMorgan, Bank of America, or Deutsche Bank). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for ancillary collection and payer networks; Low for primary treasury and settlement of ocean shipping. EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Europe, Paysera (UAB Paysera LT) and Revolut (Revolut Ltd) operate in Estonia under the PSD2 payment services framework, enabling current account-opening with remote onboarding and SEPA/MT103-like transfers. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit or issue commercial letters of credit; (ii) trade-related proceeds involving high-value Ro-Ro transactions may trigger strict source-of-wealth review, often resulting in account holds; (iii) Offshore non-EU EMIs without a branch license cannot be used for Estonia-issued capital contributions or trade payments; (iv) the bank account in the Estonian e-Business Register must be a licensed credit institution account to meet corporate liquidity requirements. Therefore, an EMI is only suitable as a supplementary liquidity channel, not as the principal clearing rail.
