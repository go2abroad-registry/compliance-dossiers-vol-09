Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Estonian Commercial Register, administered by the Registration Department of Tartu County Court (e-Business Register).

Governing statute

Estonian Commercial Code (Äriseadustik), including §§ 135–140 (OÜ liability, capital, formation and contributions), §§ 144–145 (application and articles), and §§ 180–181 (management board and representation); Commercial Register Act governs registration procedure and filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic application for entry of an osaühing (OÜ) in the Commercial Register, accompanied by the articles of association and required formation declarations.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No general statutory minimum share capital applies to an OÜ. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 0.01. Founders must pay the subscribed contributions in full before applying for registration; no statutory escrow account is generally required. The stated USD filing fee is an approximate conversion of the EUR 265 electronic registration fee and may vary with exchange rates.

Funding & unlock path: Before use, obtain the required building permit and use/occupancy authorization through the local-government building authority and the Building Register, as applicable to the works and building. Meet the applicable fire-safety design and operational requirements; the Rescue Board (Päästeamet) supervises fire-safety compliance and may inspect the premises. Ensure structural and refrigeration-system safety, workplace risk assessment, occupational-health and safety measures, emergency arrangements, and required employee training. Confirm any project-specific inspection or approval requirements with the competent local authority and Rescue Board.

Paid-in capital rule: No general statutory minimum share capital applies to an OÜ. Each share must have a nominal value of at least EUR 0.01. Founders must pay the subscribed contributions in full before applying for registration; no statutory escrow account is generally required. The stated USD filing fee is an approximate conversion of the EUR 265 electronic registration fee and may vary with exchange rates.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

No general industrial operating-licence board applies to ordinary cold-storage warehousing. For storage and handling of medicinal products, the competent authority is the State Agency of Medicines (Ravimiamet); local-government building authorities and the Environmental Board (Keskkonnaamet) have separate permitting roles.

Category: Medicinal-products handling activity licence (ravimite käitlemise tegevusluba) where the company stores or handles medicinal products as a regulated wholesale or other licensed activity; pharmaceutical good-distribution-practice compliance is also required. A general cold-storage warehouse has no standalone national operating licence solely by reason of temperature-controlled storage.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); taxpayer portal: e-MTA (e-maksuamet) via emta.ee, interoperable with the e-Residency X-Road data layer, VIES, OSS/IOSS, and EU customs ECS/NCTS systems.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Estonian OÜ: (1) register the company online in the e-Business Register (Äriregister) using an e-Residency digital ID or EU/EEA eID; no minimum capital is required; (2) the Registrar automatically issues the 8-digit registry code (registrikood), which is the legal-person TIN for CIT and labour taxes; (3) apply separately for VAT registration through e-MTA — mandatory from the first taxable supply in Estonia for a non-established entity and once annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 for an established entity; the VAT number is issued as EE + 9 digits; (4) file VAT returns monthly (payment period) or quarterly where annual taxable turnover is below EUR 40,000; non-established suppliers of B2C e-commerce may instead register under the Union/Non-Union OSS without a domestic VAT number; a foreign entity operating through a branch registers the branch in the Äriregister and obtains a branch registry code.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (deferred distribution-based CIT): the OÜ is the taxable person; retained/undistributed profits are taxed at 0%; profit distributions are taxed at 20% (gross-up basis 20/80), with a reduced 14% rate for qualifying regular dividends to resident individuals; non-resident persons are taxable only on Estonian-source income (Territorial_Scope for foreign taxpayers); no pass-through or transparency election is available for OÜ. — For a non-resident-owned Estonian OÜ: (1) register the company online in the e-Business Register (Äriregister) using an e-Residency digital ID or EU/EEA eID; no minimum capital is required; (2) the Registrar automatically issues the 8-digit registry code (registrikood), which is the legal-person TIN for CIT and labour taxes; (3) apply separately for VAT registration through e-MTA — mandatory from the first taxable supply in Estonia for a non-established entity and once annual taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 for an established entity; the VAT number is issued as EE + 9 digits; (4) file VAT returns monthly (payment period) or quarterly where annual taxable turnover is below EUR 40,000; non-established suppliers of B2C e-commerce may instead register under the Union/Non-Union OSS without a domestic VAT number; a foreign entity operating through a branch registers the branch in the Äriregister and obtains a branch registry code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (deferred distribution-based CIT): the OÜ is the taxable person; retained/undistributed profits are taxed at 0%; profit distributions are taxed at 20% (gross-up basis 20/80), with a reduced 14% rate for qualifying regular dividends to resident individuals; non-resident persons are taxable only on Estonian-source income (Territorial_Scope for foreign taxpayers); no pass-through or transparency election is available for OÜ.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch elective regime (ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply automatically and non-electively); available statutory elections and options are limited to: (a) voluntary VAT registration below the EUR 40,000 threshold; (b) the 14% qualifying-regular-dividend tax rate subject to the statutory distribution-history test; (c) OSS/IOSS registration for cross-border B2C distance sales and imports; (d) appointment of a fiscal/tax representative or provision of security where the MTA requires it for non-established businesses; (e) standard EU options on goods transport and intermediary services under the remaining EU VAT derogations applicable to Estonia.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover for taxable persons established in Estonia; no de minimis threshold for non-established persons (registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply, subject to OSS election for distance sales); EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for B2C distance sales of goods for which the seller may apply the Union OSS and tax at destination.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT is applied where the exporter proves that goods have been dispatched or transported out of the EU customs territory within 90 days of the supply and retains the customs export declaration and transport evidence; ancillary cold-chain logistics and handling services provided directly in connection with those exported goods may also be zero-rated as export-related services. Services: B2B temp-controlled warehousing supplied to a taxable customer established outside Estonia falls under the general place-of-supply rule (customer's jurisdiction), with Estonian VAT charged only if the customer is a non-taxable person or the service is effectively used and enjoyed in Estonia; B2B services to EU VAT-registered customers are reverse-charged and free of Estonian VAT. Intra-Community transfers of goods to an EU VAT-registered customer are zero-rated subject to a valid VAT number, EC sales list reporting, and CMR/order documentation. Estonia imposes no statutory FX repatriation certificate for exports (free capital movement); the 180-day benchmark in this dossier is retained as a documentary and refund-prudence standard.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/EX) or electronic export data processed through the Estonian ECS/NCTS customs environment
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill bearing third-party signature or stamp of the consignee/carrier
  • Commercial invoice and packing list indicating HS code, quantity, declared value, and cold-chain temperature requirements
  • Inward remittance / bank payment evidence (SWIFT MT103 or bank certificate) — required by banking practice, not a statutory MTA certificate
  • Cold-chain temperature logs, warehouse gate-in/gate-out records, and service contracts evidencing the logistics scope and chain of custody

Estonia does not operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time fiscal clearance or fiscal stamping regime; VAT reporting is periodic via e-MTA returns and EC sales lists. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased under the Accounting Act: since 1 July 2024 all Estonian undertakings must be technically able to receive electronic invoices (structured e-arve XML aligned with EN 16931, exchanged through Arveportaal and the X-Road layer); e-invoice transmission obligations for suppliers apply in phases from 1 July 2025 (by enterprise-size thresholds) and are scheduled to cover all taxpayers, including micro-entities, by 1 July 2027. Fiscalized cash registers use MTA-certified fiscal-memory devices with offline collection and periodic data transfers; no API-based real-time clearance or SAF-T continuous reporting is in force.

The MTA applies automated export-refund risk screening (KREDA) and targeted audits of zero-rating evidence; a failed substantiation triggers reversal of the 0% rate, additional VAT assessment at 22%, arrears interest at 0.06% per day, and penalty surcharges per the Taxation Act, within the statutory assessment windows (generally 3 years, extended in cases of concealment or false declarations; no limitation once criminal proceedings are initiated). Where export zero-rating is abused (false declarations, fabricated bills of lading, missing-trader and carousel schemes, fictitious refund input VAT), the Penal Code classifies the conduct as tax fraud, punishable by fines, confiscation of the wrongfully claimed refund, and imprisonment of up to 5 years; repeat and organized-circumstance offending carries enhanced penalties, and the MTA may issue a retrospective VAT recovery notice against the exporter and jointly liable persons in the supply chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia operates under full capital account and current account convertibility as an EU member state. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted and subject solely to standard corporate income tax compliance and AML/CFT obligations. No exchange controls, capital controls, or mandatory surrender regimes apply to any current account transactions including export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists for mandatory surrender of export proceeds in Estonia. Export receipts must be recorded in the company's statutory financial statements at the prevailing market exchange rate on the transaction date. Funds may be retained in foreign currency accounts without conversion timeline; however, all such balances require contemporaneous documentation and reporting for both tax and statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of Estonia (Eesti Pank)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on gross distributions (0% on undistributed annual profits; 20/80 gross-up of net dividends)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on gross distribution (no separate shareholder-level tax; final tax borne by the distributing OÜ). Preferential 14% statutory rate for regular dividends up to the three-year average distribution amount.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Must be submitted and tax paid by the 10th day of the calendar month following the month in which the distribution (e.g., dividend) is made. For a non-resident company with an Estonian permanent establishment, the same monthly rule applies to income attributable to the PE.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Section A, Box 1 Gross amount of distributed profits (dividends) paid during the reporting month, including deemed distributions such as shareholder non-business expenses. Non-resident companies with a registered PE in Estonia must enter distributions made by that PE. Non-resident shareholders receiving dividends from an Estonian OÜ have no direct filing obligation; the OÜ files this return as withholding agent. Late filing penalty: EUR 0.75 per calendar day per TSD form, capped after 60 days; late payment interest: statutory daily interest currently 0.06% of the unpaid amount per day; additional tax fraud penalties up to 100% of the evaded amount and criminal liability for intentional non-filing.
Section A, Box 4 Income tax payable on the gross distributions computed at 20% (or 14/86 of net regular dividends under the three-year average rule). For non-resident filers, apply the relevant double taxation treaty rate if a valid Certificate of Tax Residence has been supplied to the Estonian payer; otherwise the statutory 20% is entered. Late filing penalty: EUR 0.75 per calendar day per TSD form, capped after 60 days; late payment interest: statutory daily interest currently 0.06% of the unpaid amount per day; additional tax fraud penalties up to 100% of the evaded amount and criminal liability for intentional non-filing.
Section B, Box 10 Social tax on salaries paid to employees working in Estonia, calculated at 33% of gross wages. Applicable only if the OÜ or PE has Estonian payroll. Non-resident employers without a PE but with short-term dispatched cold-storage personnel must file a separate registration and declaration under the Estonian social tax act; do not include those amounts in the Box 10 of the resident OÜ's TSD. Late filing penalty: EUR 0.75 per calendar day per TSD form, capped after 60 days; late payment interest: statutory daily interest currently 0.06% of the unpaid amount per day; additional tax fraud penalties up to 100% of the evaded amount and criminal liability for intentional non-filing.
Section C, Box 20 Withholding income tax on payments to natural persons (e.g., management board fees) at the statutory rate of 20%. Non-resident directors or supervisors receiving board fees from an Estonian OÜ are subject to 20% final withholding; the OÜ reports the fee and tax in this box. Late filing penalty: EUR 0.75 per calendar day per TSD form, capped after 60 days; late payment interest: statutory daily interest currently 0.06% of the unpaid amount per day; additional tax fraud penalties up to 100% of the evaded amount and criminal liability for intentional non-filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000: mandatory Local File required for a resident OÜ when annual turnover exceeds EUR 5,000,000 and the aggregate value of transactions with related parties exceeds EUR 5,000,000. Group Master File required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 50,000,000. Thresholds expressed in euros (official currency of Estonia).

Economic substance scope: Estonia applies the EU ATAD Anti-Abuse Rule and domestic GAAR (Income Tax Act § 69) requiring genuine economic substance. An OÜ with no physical premises, no local employees, and no substantive management activity in Estonia may be disregarded as an artificial arrangement, with profits reallocated to the actual beneficial owner. For the refrigerated cold-storage business, the presence of a licensed warehouse, inventory records, temperature-monitoring infrastructure, and operational staff in Estonia constitutes sufficient substance to satisfy the doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Disguised Distributions)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the recharacterised distribution at 20% (or 14% if regular dividend rules apply) plus late-payment interest at 0.06% per day and a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded under the Estonian Tax Code § 113.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TSD declarations are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; tax fraud is criminally sanctioned with imprisonment up to 5 years, asset seizure, and a travel ban pending criminal proceedings.
Artificial Shell Arrangement (Substance Deficiency)

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board may disregard the OÜ as an artificial structure, reattribute the income to the true economic owner, and assess the full 20% distribution tax plus an additional penalty of up to 100% of the tax; the entity may also be excluded from treaty benefits and struck off the business register.

Director personal liability: Directors who facilitate the shell arrangement face personal civil liability for restitution to the state and any harmed creditors; criminal exposure includes money laundering charges under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act, with imprisonment up to 3 years and a 10-year ban on serving as directors.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to the arm's length price, additional 20% corporate income tax on the resulting deemed distribution, late-payment interest, and a procedural penalty of up to EUR 1,000 per unsubmitted documentation; penalty up to 100% if non-compliance is deemed intentional.

Director personal liability: The CEO and finance director may be held personally liable for the additional tax if they failed to exercise prudent business judgement in setting prices; under criminal law, negligent or intentional mispricing can result in disqualification from board membership, fines, and imprisonment up to 2 years.
AML and Licensing Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines imposed by the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) up to EUR 1,600,000 for legal persons; the licence for refrigerated logistics operations can be revoked, and the OÜ may be forced to cease operations immediately.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to EUR 180,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years for serious AML breaches; travel bans, extradition, and disqualification from managing licensed activities are additional consequences under the Estonian criminal procedure.
