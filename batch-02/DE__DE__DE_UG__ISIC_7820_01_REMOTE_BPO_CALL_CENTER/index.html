Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt fuer Steuern, BZSt) is the national authority for VAT identification numbers (USt-IdNr.), VIES administration, advance pricing agreements, and cross-border withholding relief; the local tax office (Finanzamt) competent for the UG's registered seat (e.g. Finanzamt Muenchen) administers corporate income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), VAT (Umsatzsteuer), payroll tax (Lohnsteuer), and tax audits; the statutory e-filing portal is ELSTER (https://www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Because the UG is a domestic German corporation, the entity itself obtains all tax identifiers; non-resident shareholders do not require a German fiscal number. Procedure: (1) notarized incorporation (Musterprotokoll or Satzung) with minimum capital of EUR 1; (2) registration in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) at the Local Court via notary, obtaining the HRB number; (3) within 4 weeks of business commencement, file the statutory tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) with the competent Finanzamt via ELSTER, disclosing ISIC 7820 BPO activity, forecast turnover, shareholders, and place of management; (4) receive the Steuernummer (format 123/456/78901) from the Finanzamt; (5) file Form USt 1 TN with BZSt to obtain the EU VAT ID (DE plus 9 digits) for cross-border supplies; (6) if hiring agents, obtain the employer Betriebsnummer from the Federal Employment Agency and file payroll tax registration; (7) configure ELSTER SOAP/HTTP API channels for VAT prepayment returns and annual declarations. Total lead time 4-8 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque corporation; no pass-through; worldwide residency taxation). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque corporation; no pass-through; worldwide residency taxation). The UG is a German capital company subject to: (i) corporate income tax (Koerperschaftsteuer) at 15% of global taxable income; (ii) solidarity surcharge (Solidaritaetszuschlag) at 0.825% effective; (iii) municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) statutorily at 3.5% of trade assessment base multiplied by local Hebesatz, effective burden 14.0%-17.2% (Munich 490% equals 17.15%); combined effective rate approx. 29.8%-33.0%; no territorial, offshore, or tax-exempt zone regime; the UG is tax resident with unlimited tax liability in Germany; non-resident shareholders may own 100% of the UG without restriction.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under German law. Available elections: (1) VAT and income/trade tax fiscal unity (Organschaft) under Sec. 2(2) No. 2 UStG and Sec. 14 KStG with a German parent, requiring majority voting control plus an executed and implemented profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabfuehrungsvertrag); (2) Sec. 19 UStG small-business VAT exemption only while prior-year turnover is below EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast below EUR 100,000, unsuitable for a reverse-charge B2B exporter; (3) quarterly annual VAT prepayment election under Sec. 18(2) UStG; (4) binding advance ruling or advance pricing agreement with BZSt under Sec. 89 AO fixing the cost-plus profit margin for the offshore BPO; (5) hybrid mismatch disclosures and adjustments under Sec. 4k EStG if a foreign double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion structure is present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Germany imposes no general turnover-based VAT registration threshold; VAT liability arises automatically upon the first taxable transaction (Sec. 2(1) UStG), mandating registration, prepayment filings, and invoicing from day one. The only statutory de minimis is the Sec. 19(1) UStG small-business exemption, available if previous-year turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-year forecast turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000. Because the UG bills offshore B2B call-center services under reverse-charge place-of-supply rules and must recover German input VAT on setup costs, it must obtain its Steuernummer and USt-IdNr. before commencing operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Offshore BPO call-center services (ISIC 7820) supplied to foreign entrepreneurial recipients are not charged German VAT because the place of supply is the recipient's country under Sec. 3a(2) UStG: the supply is non-taxable in Germany and the UG issues its invoice without VAT; EU customers self-assess under reverse charge (Sec. 13b UStG); non-EU customers bear no German VAT. The UG retains full input-VAT recovery on German costs because the place of supply is abroad and such supply would be taxable if performed in Germany. Inbound/outbound tele-services supplied to German private consumers remain taxable in Germany at 19%; B2C offshore sales are outside German VAT. Eligibility is documentary and evidentiary, and is tested in tax audits.

  • No customs export declaration, bill of lading, or ATLAS filing applies to ISIC 7820 BPO services; the German export-of-services substantiation bundle for audit is:
  • 1. Executed master services agreement in English or German, statement of work, and German transfer-pricing documentation under Sec. 90(3) AO establishing an arm's length cost-plus margin (typically 5%-10% net) for the call-center services.
  • 2. Invoice issued under Sec. 14 UStG with sequential number, full parties, German supplier VAT ID, customer VAT ID for EU recipients or foreign tax registration number for non-EU recipients, fee currency, and express reference to Sec. 3a(2) UStG place-of-supply and Sec. 13b UStG reverse charge.
  • 3. Evidence of customer entrepreneurial status: VIES validation printout for EU VAT IDs captured on the supply date; for non-EU customers, annual foreign tax-residency certificate or trade-register extract; for intercompany customers, the group affiliation chart.
  • 4. Month-end performance evidence: agent timesheets, call detail records, IVR and CRM reports, seat and FTE utilization files, and customer acceptance minutes demonstrating actual offshore inbound and outbound service delivery from Germany.
  • 5. Inward remittance evidence: Wise B2B payment records or SWIFT MT103/MT940 credit advices posted to the UG's German bank account and matched to invoice references; payments must flow from the contractual debtor.
  • 6. Bundesbank foreign-trade statistics report (AWV) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, mandatory for each cross-border receipt of EUR 12,500 or more, filed via the Bundesbank portal by the 5th day of the month following the payment; records retained for 10 years.

Germany operates no real-time fiscal clearance, transaction-level authorization, or digital stamping; compliance is audit-based. Since 1 January 2025 all German-established businesses must receive and process CEN EN 16931-compliant structured e-invoices (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1) via interoperable networks (PEPPOL); issuing e-invoices becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 for taxpayers with prior-year turnover above EUR 800,000 and from 1 January 2028 for all others; B2G e-invoicing with public bodies requires XRechnung since November 2020; all VAT returns (Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldung and annual return) are filed via ELSTER XML API; invoices and source data must be archived in accordance with GoBD for 10 years.

German tax audit (Aussenpruefung under Sec. 193 AO) will challenge the export characterization if the bundle above is incomplete. Consequences of failure: (i) recharacterization of fees as domestic taxable supplies with German VAT assessed at 19% plus additional liability under Sec. 14c UStG for invoices issued without VAT; (ii) late-payment surcharges (Saeumniszu schlaege, Sec. 240 AO) of 1% per commenced month capped at 60 months, plus interest of 0.15% per month under Sec. 233a AO (1.8% per annum as amended); (iii) input-VAT clawbacks for insufficient Sec. 15 UStG documentation within the standard 4-year limitation period; (iv) deliberate or reckless mischaracterization constitutes tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO with penalties up to EUR 50,000 per count under Sec. 26a UStG, a 10-year limitation period, and potential imprisonment; (v) BZSt VIES data matching and EU transactional peer review will identify false reverse-charge invoices, triggering cross-border information exchange.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full de jure and de facto convertibility of the euro for both current account and capital account transactions, consistent with TFEU free movement of capital and European Economic Area rules. No residual exchange control, no central bank approval, and no profit remittance licence applies to a German UG distributing dividends or servicing shareholder loans to non-residents, subject only to statutory 25% German withholding tax on dividends (plus solidarity surcharge where applicable), treaty relief under the OECD-based German DTA network, and mandatory AWV statistical reporting of cross-border flows above EUR 12,500. The practical remittance hurdle is not regulatory but bank-side: proving economic substance, beneficial ownership, and the underlying service receipts to satisfy Geldwäschegesetz (GwG) due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds or BPO service income in Germany. The euro being the domestic currency, no FX conversion obligation arises for EUR-denominated invoicing. The sole compliance obligation is statistical: under § 67 AWV, every resident (the UG) must report payments received from non-EEA resident counterparties exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Deutsche Bundesbank within seven days of receipt (or, for period-compensated services, on the fifth working day of the following month), using the official electronic eAWV form with the correct Zahlungsgrund (reason code, e.g., code 992 for 'other services'). Non-reporting is an administrative offence subject to fines.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany); prudential banking supervision by BaFin (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht); cross-border payment statistics and foreign trade payment reporting under Außenwirtschaftsverordnung (AWV § 67) administered by the Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High as a supplementary disbursement and collection rail: Wise B2B, Airwallex, Multitude (Ferratum), and PPRO hold EEA e-money or payment institution licences passported into Germany, enabling low-cost cross-border payouts. Medium/Low as the sole or primary banking relationship: an EMI under the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) may not take deposits, grant credit, or open a full Girokonto; payroll tax (Lohnsteuer), VAT settlement, trade registry references, and counterparty procurement requirements effectively demand a German domestic IBAN at a licensed credit institution. SEPA IBAN discrimination is legally prohibited, but a UG's operational viability is materially stronger with a tier-1 German bank anchor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for UG capital: (1) equity contribution or shareholder loan inbound via SWIFT MT103 / SEPA CREDIT TRANSFER with full Originator and Beneficiary names, addresses and account numbers required by Regulation (EU) 2015/847; (2) correct AWV ZW reason code (direct investment capital: code 110-119; shareholder loan: code 990 service-related) reported to Deutsche Bundesbank when over EUR 12,500; (3) no Bundesbank prior approval needed - Germany has abolished capital controls, but the UG must maintain the notarised capital increase resolution and proof of contribution in the commercial file for the subscribing German notary and the bank; (4) funds must land on the existing German Girokonto and cannot be split intentionally to evade the statistical reporting threshold ('smurfing' constitutes an administrative offence); (5) where funds enter from a non-EEA country with OFAC/EU sanctions exposure, the bank runs enhanced screening; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High as a supplementary disbursement and collection rail: Wise B2B, Airwallex, Multitude (Ferratum), and PPRO hold EEA e-money or payment institution licences passported into Germany, enabling low-cost cross-border payouts. Medium/Low as the sole or primary banking relationship: an EMI under the German Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtsgesetz (ZAG) may not take deposits, grant credit, or open a full Girokonto; payroll tax (Lohnsteuer), VAT settlement, trade registry references, and counterparty procurement requirements effectively demand a German domestic IBAN at a licensed credit institution. SEPA IBAN discrimination is legally prohibited, but a UG's operational viability is materially stronger with a tier-1 German bank anchor.
