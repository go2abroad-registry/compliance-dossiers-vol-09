Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, EMTA); electronic tax services are provided through the e-MTA portal.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ receives an Estonian Commercial Register registration code upon incorporation; that code is its domestic tax identification number. To obtain a VAT identification number, submit an application to EMTA through e-MTA or another accepted filing channel. EMTA generally decides an application within five working days after receiving sufficient information. A non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in Estonia must generally register before commencing those supplies; an EU-established applicant may apply directly, while a non-EU-established applicant generally must appoint an Estonian fiscal representative unless an applicable statutory exception or waiver applies. An EU VAT number is issued in the format EE followed by the registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style check-the-box election to treat an OÜ as a pass-through entity. Undistributed profits are generally not subject to corporate income tax; distributions and specified deemed profit distributions are taxed at company level. Estonia applies EU anti-hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD, but these are statutory rules rather than elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of relevant taxable turnover arising in Estonia during a calendar year for a person established in Estonia. The threshold does not generally protect a non-established person making taxable supplies in Estonia; specific exceptions, including reverse-charge treatment, may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the EU may generally qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier can substantiate the export and the statutory conditions are met. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate 0% rules and require evidence of transport and the customer's valid VAT identification number. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: their VAT treatment generally depends on place-of-supply rules, and a service supplied outside Estonia may be outside the scope of Estonian VAT rather than zero-rated.

  • Export customs declaration and electronic confirmation that the goods exited the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract or purchase order identifying the goods, buyer, and transaction
  • Transport evidence, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier confirmation
  • Proof of payment or other transaction evidence; an inward remittance certificate is not a general Estonian statutory requirement

Estonia does not operate a general real-time VAT invoice clearance or fiscal-stamping system. VAT returns and related filings are submitted electronically through e-MTA. From 1 July 2025, a business customer registered in Estonia's e-invoice recipient register may require a supplier to issue an e-invoice; this is not a universal clearance mandate. No general tax-authority API pre-approval is required for domestic invoices.

Estonia imposes no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. If export conditions or evidence are insufficient, EMTA may deny the 0% rate, assess VAT and statutory interest, and recover any resulting tax deficiency. Tax interest is generally 0.06% per day under the Taxation Act. Intentional tax fraud may also result in administrative or criminal sanctions, depending on the facts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. Estonia adopted the euro on 1 January 2011; EUR is the only legal tender and no exchange controls or approval thresholds apply to current-account or capital-account transactions, consistent with Article 63 TFEU on free movement of capital. Profit remittance is unrestricted in flow terms: no withholding tax is levied at non-resident shareholder level; however, the distributing OÜ must settle Estonia's distributed-profits tax of 20/80 on net dividend distributions (20% effective gross rate for ordinary distributions, 14/86 for regular 'benchmark' distributions satisfying the 7-year averaging rule). The only intermediation frictions are standard bank payment-purpose annotations, UBO declarations, and the Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu andmebüroo) reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonian law imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation obligation for export receipts. EUR-denominated export proceeds may be retained in any bank within or outside the EU at the exporter's discretion. Statutory constraints are limited to Estonian Accounting Act bookkeeping, transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany trade, EU cash-control declaration obligations (EUR 10,000 threshold under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672), and customs export-declaration (EXS) records for medical-device consignments.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), the euro-area National Central Bank within the Eurosystem, with statutory oversight of payment and settlement systems; the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) exercises prudential and AML/CFT supervision over credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions. The European Central Bank holds ultimate monetary-policy and foreign-reserve authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium, as secondary treasury wallet only. Estonia-licensed or passportised EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Ponto, Holvi) provide multi-currency IBANs and SEPA-In / SWIFT connectivity, but are legally barred from lending, paying interest, or issuing documentary credits (MT700); counterparty banks treat EMI settlement as higher-risk. For the laparoscopy-tower cycle—which is LC-heavy and customs-linked—the OÜ must retain a full licensed credit institution as the primary clearing node; an EMI account may be used exclusively for EU distributor collections and minor supplier disbursements.
