Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (MTA) - Estonian Tax and Customs Board; digital filing via e-MTA portal (https://e-mta.emta.ee) and the Commercial Register e-Äriregister (https://ariregister.rik.ee)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Osaühing (OÜ) in e-Äriregister; the resulting 8-digit registry code (registrikood) is the company's statutory TIN for all tax and customs purposes. Step 2: Activate e-MTA access via ID-card, Mobile-ID or Smart-ID (e-Residency digital identity is accepted for non-resident directors) and authenticate the management board in e-MTA. Step 3: Apply for VAT registration via e-MTA before the expected EUR 40,000 turnover threshold is crossed or voluntarily at incorporation; the MTA assigns a VAT-ID in the format EE + 9 digits (8-digit registry code plus a check digit). Step 4: Register as a payer of employment taxes where remuneration is paid (TSD registrant). Step 5: All filings (VAT returns, TSD corporate income tax returns, customs declarations and Intrastat reports) are subsequently made under the same registry code/VAT-ID through e-MTA or X-tee-based software.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (deferred distribution-based CIT: 0% on undistributed annual profits; 20/80 on regular dividend distributions; 25/75 on irregular or hidden distributions)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; an OÜ is invariably a resident corporate taxpayer. Permissible elections for the smart-IoT retailer include: (a) voluntary VAT registration below the EUR 40,000 threshold (VAT Act § 3(2)); (b) structuring a dividend to qualify as a 'regular distribution' at the 20/80 rate rather than the 25/75 irregular distribution rate (Income Tax Act § 50); (c) electing the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for intra-EU distance sales and the IOSS for imported low-value IoT goods; (d) applying the automatic participation exemption for dividends received from qualifying subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 2 are hard-wired into Estonian law and cannot be disclaimed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 annual taxable turnover exclusive of VAT (calendar-year basis); for intra-EU distance sales and digital services the cross-border registration threshold is EUR 10,000 aggregated union-wide under the OSS framework

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) export sales of IoT hardware physically dispatched to a non-EU destination within three months of the time of supply, supported by a valid customs export declaration (MRN) and transport documents; (ii) intra-EU B2B supplies to a customer holding a valid VAT-EUID with goods physically moved to another Member State; and (iii) directly related services such as freight and logistics when performed in connection with an export and billed to the exporter. The OÜ must hold an MRN evidencing exit before filing the VAT period return. Input VAT on export-related purchases is fully creditable and refundable through e-MTA (refund target of 30 days for monthly filers, 60 days otherwise).

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-customs SAD / electronic declaration containing the Movement Reference Number - MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch from EU territory to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or payment service provider settlement statement) proving receipt of export proceeds

Estonia does not operate real-time transaction clearance or digital stamping. VAT returns (Form KMD) must be filed electronically via e-MTA by the 20th day of the month following the tax period, accompanied by invoice-level annexes (KMD INF) recording domestic B2B invoices above EUR 1,000. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory in the European standard EN 16931 through the central e-arve exchange; B2B e-invoicing is voluntary, though the e-MTA accepts API/X-tee push of UBL 21 e-invoices for pre-filling. Under the EU VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package, transaction-based digital VAT reporting is expected in Estonia by 2030.

If the MTA finds missing or unreliable export evidence, the 0% rate is reversed and 20% VAT is assessed, together with late-payment interest of 0.06% per day from day 51 and an additional tax-default fine of up to EUR 16,000 per violation for a legal person; the standard assessment window is 3 years, extended to 7 years for deliberately concealed evasion. Fraudulent zero-rating involving false documents attracts criminal liability under Penal Code § 403 (custodial exposure for the OÜ director). Export proceeds not repatriated within 180 days without demonstrated commercial justification may be recharacterized as a hidden profit distribution, triggering 25/75 CIT, and the corresponding input VAT is clawed back from the period in which the export was declared.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible under Estonian law; there are no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions, no licensing of capital movements, and no restrictions on profit remittance or dividend distributions. Full convertibility is anchored in EU primary law (TFEU Articles 63–66) and Eurosystem rules. Administrative obligations are limited to balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Eesti Pank (SKP forms) and, for non-resident shareholders, withholding tax at 20%, reduced to 15% or 0% under double-tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia does not impose export proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation timelines. Exporters may retain receipts in EUR or any foreign currency, and settlement currency is a commercial matter. The only mandatory requirements are statistical classification of export receipts in the Eesti Pank balance-of-payments reporting (SKP) framework and VAT invoicing/traceability under the Estonian VAT Act; no conversion ratio or deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the central bank and Eurosystem member; conduct and AML supervision is shared with the Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) and the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility as a supplementary treasury rail but not as the primary corporate account. An OÜ may use EEA-licensed payment institutions and e-money institutions (EMIs) that passport into Estonia under PSD2 and the Electronic Money Act, including digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, or other EEA-passporting EMI platforms. Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions; they cannot hold statutory share capital in escrow, cannot issue confirmation for e-Business Register capital registration, and segregated-client-account funds are not deposit-insured under the Guarantee Fund Act. For a Low_Risk retail IoT business, the recommended architecture is a fully licensed domestic bank account (primary) with a fintech EMI as a secondary FX/card settlement rail; all PSP merchant balances (Stripe, Shopify Payments) must settle ultimately to the licensed bank account for SEPA/VAT compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections must be denominated in EUR as the statutory currency of the OÜ. The payment is sent via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer if the shareholder is an EU/EEA resident) to the OÜ's dedicated account, using purpose code 'Equity capital injection — direct investment' (BoP classification under the Eesti Pank SKP framework) and the OÜ's Estonian registration code in the payment reference and creditor ID. For share capital subscription prior to registration, the funds must be placed on a blocked escrow account opened in the name of the future OÜ (or a notary's client account) and a bank confirmation of payment is submitted to the Commercial Register petition; alternatively, for an existing OÜ, capital is increased by a notarized resolution and the payment is credited to the company's operational account with a reference to the registry notice. Shareholder loans are routed similarly with a corresponding BoP purpose code for intercompany loans; the loan agreement must be executed before payment and the funds used either as working capital or for third-party trade settlements; cash pooling arrangements are permitted but subject to transfer pricing substance documentation.Within SEPA/Eurozone, supplier payments use standard SEPA Credit Transfer with IBAN and structured creditor reference; outside SEPA, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 with a mandatory UETR (Universal Transaction Identifier) and full documentary compliance for sanctions screening (EU, UN, UK, US). For Smart Home IoT hardware purchases from non-EEA suppliers (e.g., China), the OÜ may use (i) bill-to-bill MT103 open account settlement with 30–60 day terms, (ii) an irrevocable documentary commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) with the buyer's bank as issuing bank for high-value or first-time transactions, or (iii) a standby L/C secured payment structure; SWIFT MT101 (multi-payment) and batch XML ISO 20022 pain.001 messages are permitted for grouped disbursements.
