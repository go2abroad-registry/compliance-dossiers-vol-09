Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Finanzamt (local tax office) for Steuernummer and regular German tax administration; Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for the USt-IdNr (VAT ID) and cross-border matters; central electronic portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de)

        
E-government filing portal
The UG, once incorporated and entered in the German Commercial Register, must submit the 'Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung' to the locally competent Finanzamt before commencing business operations. The Finanzamt assigns the income/corporate tax Steuernummer. For intra-Community supplies and EU VAT purposes, the UG applies separately to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr using Form USt 1 or via ELSTER. A foreign entrepreneur without a domestic registered establishment applies directly to the BZSt; a UG is deemed to have its seat in Germany and is registered through the local Finanzamt procedure. Filing and correspondence are in German; authorised representatives must hold a German tax power of attorney.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
German law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A UG is treated as a corporation taxed at entity level for corporate income tax and trade tax. Available statutory mechanisms include: corporate and VAT tax group (Organschaft) election where financial, organisational and economic integration is established; the small-business VAT exemption under § 19 UStG where turnover thresholds are met; and mandatory application of hybrid mismatch rules under § 4k EStG / ATAD with no elective entity classification.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
For established local businesses, mandatory VAT registration arises once taxable turnover exceeds the small-business exemption: from 2025, prior-year turnover not exceeding EUR 25,000 and expected current-year turnover not exceeding EUR 100,000. Above these thresholds registration is compulsory. Non-resident suppliers providing digital B2C services in Germany cannot rely on the small-business exemption and must register directly or account for VAT through the Union/One-Stop-Shop (OSS). B2B services are normally subject to reverse charge, removing the need for full local registration.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods to third countries qualify for the 0% VAT rate under § 4 No. 1 UStG where the goods physically leave Germany and proof of export is retained. Intra-Community supplies of goods qualify under § 4 No. 1b UStG where the customer holds a valid VAT ID, the goods are transported to another Member State, and the supplier files an EC Sales List. Cross-border B2B services follow the place-of-supply rules under § 3a UStG; if the recipient is established outside Germany, the supply is not German VAT-liable and is generally reverse-charged or outside German VAT. B2C digital services supplied to EU consumers are not zero-rated; they are taxable in the consumer's country and may be reported via OSS.

Germany mandates structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025 using XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x XML formats. There is no real-time tax clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control / API telemetry regime comparable to Italy or Hungary. If the zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or the prerequisite customs/transport/receipt evidence is incomplete, the tax office will retrospectively assess German VAT at 19%, add monthly interest of 0.5% on the assessed tax, and impose late payment surcharges. Intentional or reckless false claims may constitute tax evasion under § 370 AO, carrying imprisonment of up to 5 years, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases, plus fines. Unlawfully refunded VAT is clawed back with penalty interest; the standard tax assessment limitation is up to 10 years for VAT in evasion cases. Audit scrutiny is heightened for cross-border services and digital helpdesk SaaS exports; documentation must be retained for 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Germany maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions by virtue of EU Article 64 TFEU. There are no exchange controls, no required approvals for profit remittance, and no distinction between current and capital account for conversion purposes. Profit remittance from a UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is governed by German company law: distributions are permissible only if the UG's net assets exceed its subscribed capital after payment, and the UG must comply with the mandatory one-quarter retention rule under Sec. 5a GmbHG until subscribed capital reaches EUR 25,000. Cross-border dividend payments to foreign shareholders are freely convertible, subject only to tax treaty withholding relief documentation, CRS/FATCA self-certification, and the absence of thin-capitalization or anti-treaty-shopping issues.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Germany imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts from SaaS/helpdesk services may be retained indefinitely in any currency. However, German reporting obligations apply under Sec. 67 AWV: any cross-border payment received in a calendar month exceeding EUR 12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank, generally by the seventh day after the end of the month. No corporate income tax or VAT clearance is required for the remittance of export proceeds.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank, foreign trade and payments statistics authority), BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority for prudential banking supervision), and the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Viability: Medium. Cross-border electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Monese are permitted under the German Payment Services Supervision Act (ZAG) as long as they operate with an EEA licence and notify the Bundesbank/BaFin. They can be used for receivables collection and low-cost cross-border disbursements and are technically viable for a helpdesk SaaS company. However, they cannot substitute for a full German banking relationship: they are non-banks, balance sheet safeguarding differs from deposit insurance, statutory share capital verification for the UG is not recognized by the commercial register through EMI accounts, and account termination risk is elevated because EMIs routinely close corporate accounts without a court order. Legal constraints include the services-ordering duty under Sec. 26 ZAG, mandatory safeguarding rules, and the inability to hold regulated customer deposits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant inbound capital injection is executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022) for EEA shareholders or SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA shareholders. The remittance information field must clearly identify the payment as a capital contribution under Sec. 5a GmbHG, including the phrase 'Capital contribution to subscribed capital – UG (haftungsbeschränkt)'. The account receiving the contribution must be held in the UG's legal name at a German or other EEA bank. Any cross-border capital receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 in a calendar month must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank under Sec. 67 AWV with the statistical purpose code for capital transactions. Documentary verification includes the notarised capital contribution resolutions, deposit confirmation, and an updated share register.Outbound disbursements for trade and SaaS operations are cleared primarily through SEPA Order Transfer for euro payments within EEA and through SWIFT MT103 for payments to non-EEA counterparties. For high-value procurement requiring documentary assurance, a commercial Letter of Credit payable at sight under UCP600 is permissible, with the UG opening the LC through its tier-1 commercial bank. Recurring SaaS fees can be disbursed via Stripe/PayPal corporate payout rails, but the main treasury concentration account must hold the residual operating funds. All outbound cross-border transfers equal to or above EUR 12,500 must also be reported to the Bundesbank in accordance with the German balance-of-payments statistics requirements.
