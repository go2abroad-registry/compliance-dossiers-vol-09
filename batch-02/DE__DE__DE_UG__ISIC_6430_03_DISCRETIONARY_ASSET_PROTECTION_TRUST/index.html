Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

UG minimum nominal capital is EUR 1; each shareholder's contribution must be at least EUR 1 and fully paid up before registration. The subscription proceeds must be credited to a German business account (Geschäftskonto) in the company's name and confirmed by a bank letter (Bankeinzahlungsbestätigung) as being at the managing director's free disposal. No state escrow deposit is required, but 25% of annual net profit must be allocated to the statutory reserve until share capital reaches EUR 25,000 (Section 5a(3) GmbHG).

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate per the Bavarian Building Code (BayBO) for office premises with occupancy below 30 persons; structural-load certification confirming no change-of-use for the registered office; occupational health inspection by the Trade Authority or semi-public accident insurer (Berufsgenossenschaft) valid for five years; compliance with the ArbStättV requirements for lighting, rest rooms, emergency routes, and first-aid provision; workplace ergonomics and minimum-air-change verification required before the license is activated.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) – Federal Central Tax Office; local Finanzamt (tax office) for income and trade tax; ELSTER online portal at www.elster.de

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a German UG must first register the company with the local Amtsgericht (commercial register). The UG then applies to the competent local Finanzamt for a Steuernummer (tax number) for corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT pre-registration. For a USt-IdNr. (VAT identification number), the UG must file an online application with the BZSt via ELSTER/BZSt portals. Non-resident entities with a permanent establishment or a statutory seat in Germany follow the same local registration path; foreign corporations without German establishment may obtain only a VAT ID through the BZSt if they make taxable supplies in Germany and are subject to German VAT. The UG is permitted to have non-resident shareholders, but the company itself becomes a German tax resident at its place of management unless central administration is outside Germany.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

German law does not apply a US-style check-the-box classification. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is by statute a capital company and is mandatorily opaque for German tax purposes, being liable to corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), solidarity surcharge, and trade tax. Available elections are limited to tax group treatment under the 'Organschaft' regime (profit and loss pooling agreement plus financial and organizational integration) and certain EU/ATAD hybrid mismatch elections under Sec. 4k EStG for determining the domestic treatment of hybrid entities and hybrid financial instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€25,000 annual turnover threshold for the small business exemption (§19 UStG) for resident entrepreneurs; the reduced rate applies up to €100,000 in the following year. Non-resident suppliers crossing even minimal German supplies have no exemption and must register for VAT without a de minimis threshold. For intra-Community distance sales, the EU €10,000 cross-border threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries (Ausfuhrlieferungen) of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Sec. 4 No. 1 and Sec. 6 UStG where the goods are physically removed from the EU by the supplier or at the instigation of the supplier to a non-EU destination, and the export is fully documented. B2B services supplied by the German UG to customers outside the EU are generally outside German VAT because the place of supply is where the customer is established; certain services physically performed on land or on goods inside Germany remain taxable. Trust administration and fiduciary asset-protection services supplied to non-German clients must be carefully reviewed for the place-of-supply rules and may qualify for German zero-rating or reverse-charge treatment depending on the recipient's status.

  • Customs Export Declaration

Germany does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping model. Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions applies from 1 January 2025: sellers must be able to receive XRechnung or ZUGFeRD-standard electronic invoices. Sending obligations are phased, being mandatory for taxable persons with prior-year turnover above €800,000 from 1 January 2027 and for all taxable persons from 1 January 2028. Legal delivery remains via EDI-compliant electronic data interchange or email; all cash-register systems must be certified with a certified technical security module (TSE) under §146a AO for recording sales data.

German tax and customs authorities match zero-rated supplies against electronic export declarations in the ATLAS customs system and require verifiable transport evidence such as air waybills, bills of lading, outward-processing confirmations, and customs export documents. False or merely nominal export paper trails are treated as tax evasion under Sec. 370 AO, triggering retrospective VAT clawback plus interest under Sec. 233a AO at 0.5% per month, late-payment penalties, and fines. Criminal liability reaches imprisonment of up to five years for intentional evasion and, in aggravated cases, up to ten years. Receipts issued without proper zero-rating substantiation may also generate output VAT under Sec. 14c UStG, and the responsible officers or beneficial owners of the trust may face administrative fines and loss of export documentation privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany provides full currency convertibility pursuant to EU free movement of capital (TFEU Article 63); no distinction is drawn between capital and current account convertibility and no exchange licensing exists for profit remittance. The only material hurdle is reputational/KYC rather than FX control: a UG administering offshore trusts must satisfy the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) and bank-internal de-risking policies before banks will process remittances; any cross-border payment exceeding EUR 12,500 triggers a mandatory AWV statistical declaration to the Bundesbank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation in Germany; exporters and treasury entities may retain foreign currency freely. The only compliance requirement is reporting to the Deutsche Bundesbank of all inward and outward payments exceeding EUR 12,500 (or its foreign-currency equivalent), subject to the calendar-month declaration deadline and tagged with the AWV economic-purpose code. Mandatory conversion ratios therefore do not exist.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the German Central Bank) administers external payments statistics under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV); the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) is the competent licensing and banking conduct regulator for all corporate and fiduciary banking activity within Germany.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low: BaFin-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) and digital challenger banks generally reject high-risk fiduciary trust structures, do not offer escrow or trust-account capabilities, and apply strict sectoral de-risking; EMI onboarding may be acceptable only for minor operational disbursements, never as the primary clearing rail.
