Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); digital portal e-MTA at www.emta.ee/e-mta

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without an Estonian seat may obtain a statutory VAT ID by submitting Form V (non-resident taxable person registration) via the e-MTA digital portal. Required particulars include foreign trade registry code, legal form, business address, director/beneficial owner data, and projected date and nature of first taxable supply. On approval, the MTA assigns an EE-prefixed VAT registration number. No local business registry code is required solely for VAT purposes. For corporate income tax, a non-resident entity is not required to register with the MTA unless it carries on business through a permanent establishment in Estonia, derives Estonian-source dividends, or is as employer subject to withholding duties. Alternative route: an entity established in another EU Member State supplying goods/electronically supplied services to Estonian consumers may use the Union/Non-Union OSS schemes, eliminating the need for separate national VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not provide U.S.-type check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. The Income Tax Act offers specific entity elections: (i) optional consolidated group taxation with loss transfer among resident companies; (ii) election to exempt profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment under the residence principle; (iii) election for non-resident pension funds and non-resident public sector entities. Under VAT law, a taxable person may elect for voluntary registration before the statutory threshold is met; a non-resident performing distance sales may elect to register locally instead of using the OSS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover (calendar year); distance sales of goods from other EU Member States to Estonian consumers exceeding €10,000 per year (unless OSS used) and all taxable supplies by an non-established taxable person without local registration may trigger mandatory registration regardless of the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of physical goods to non-EU destinations qualifies for 0% VAT when the supplier retains evidence that the goods have left the customs territory of the EU within 90 days of dispatch and that the purchaser is a non-EU person. Services directly connected to exported goods (freight, insurance, loading) and international transport services are also zero-rated. Intra-Community supplies to EU business customers may be zero-rated if the buyer holds a valid EU VAT ID and the supplier submits the VIES listing; proof of transport to another Member State is required.

Estonia does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or mandatory digital stamping regime. E-invoicing (e.g., PEPPOL-compliant) is accepted and required for B2G transactions through the state interoperability platform (RIHA). VAT invoices, whether paper or electronic, must contain statutory details; aggregated invoice data is transmitted with the periodic VAT return (Form KMD) and sales/purchase listings (KMD INF). No government API gateway is used for per-invoice live validation.

The MTA routinely audits export zero-rating claims, verifying customs records, transport contracts, and payment repatriation. If documents are deficient, contradicted, or the foreign buyer's payment remains uncollected beyond 180 days without justified commercial reason, the zero rate is reversed, and the supplier is assessed 20% VAT retroactively plus default interest (monthly default rate 0.03% on overdue amount). A negligent or intentional violation triggers a fine of up to €8,000; for repeated or fraudulent schemes, the MTA may order aggravated penalties up to EUR 35,000 and criminal prosecution under the Penal Code, with imprisonment up to 7 years. The applicable statute of limitations for tax assessments is six years from the end of the tax period, extending to ten years for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia has full currency convertibility under EU Treaty capital-movement rules and the euro's status as legal tender. Both current-account and capital-account operations are freely convertible, and profit remittance is not subject to any exchange-control permit, Ministry approval, or central-bank licensing. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to commercial-bank AML/KYC source-of-funds verification, tax-clearance documentation obtainable from Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board), and the corporate income tax filing triggered by a dividend distribution. Because the Estonian OÜ defers taxation until distribution, the taxed event is the dividend payment itself; the resulting tax payment and dividend transfer are processed through ordinary payment rails without FX restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline applies to export proceeds. Estonian resident companies may retain export receipts in EUR or any foreign currency in accounts held with authorized credit institutions or e-money institutions operating under an EEA license. For legal and statistical compliance, the export invoice, proof of delivery, and the actual bank credit (SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer) must be retained; foreign-currency receipts must be booked using the transaction-date exchange rate or Eesti Pank published reference rate where relevant.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and cooperating with the European Central Bank for euro-area monetary policy. No separate foreign-exchange administration exists, and no general exchange control law is in force.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Operational High; Capital Receipt / Share-Capital Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs chartered in the EEA (for example, Revolut Payments UAB and Wise Europe) may lawfully provide an EE/SEPA-compliant EUR IBAN, merchant settlement, and cross-border payment services under the EU E-Money Directive passporting regime. They are legally payment/e-money institutions rather than credit institutions: they cannot accept deposits, originate credit, or hold client funds as loanable deposits; funds must be safeguarded or securely ring-fenced. For an OÜ subscription-box treasury, such EMIs are acceptable for collections and low-value supplier disbursements but are not advisable for shareholder capital injections or large escrow arrangements. Estonian Tax and Customs Board interoperability must be checked, and some EMIs do not present an acceptable formal bank-account relationship for capital-call proceeds or tax refunds. An Estonian-licensed EMI must be registered with Eesti Pank and comply with the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

EU/EEA shareholders and investors transmit EUR through SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant to the OÜ's Estonian IBAN. Non-EEA investors transmit through SWIFT MT103 to the Estonian credit institution, stating the beneficiary legal name, EE IBAN, and BIC. The remittance information must identify the economic purpose as equity capital contribution, capital increase, or share premium; the receiving bank will classify the credit for Eesti Pank balance-of-payments statistics under the Eurosystem direct investment category for equity capital in Estonian enterprises. No central-bank approval is required, but banks will demand the following verification set: shareholder resolution (or notarized deed of increase if capital is being formally raised), subscription declaration, updated e-Business Register extract, passport or digital ID copies, proof of source of funds from the sending institution, and, where a non-EU corporate investor is involved, its constitutional documents and beneficial-ownership register. Funds credited as a formal capital contribution must be recorded in the commercial register if they are allocated to share capital, while excess contributions may be booked as share premium or an equity reserve.EUR-denominated supplier payments within the EEA are split out via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant; non-EEA supplier payments in EUR or foreign currency are executed through SWIFT MT103, with MT202 cover where the ordering bank uses a correspondent. For large bulk-merchandise purchases, an irrevocable confirmed commercial Letter of Credit is viable and provides documentary protection against seller non-performance. Card-scheme clearing for subscription collections is achieved through Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net, with daily/weekly settlement to the OÜ's Estonian settlement account and subsequent sweeps to treasury accounts. All outbound disbursements should be matched to supplier invoices, purchase orders, Incoterms, customs declarations, and, where applicable, transfer-pricing documentation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained and reinvested profits; 20% on profit distributions computed on the gross distributed amount (equivalent to 20/80 of the net dividend paid), producing a 25% effective pre-distribution rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% on the gross profit distribution (14% preferential rate for regular profit distributions). This is a corporate income tax imposed on the distributing OÜ and settled through a withhold-and-pay mechanism; there is no separate shareholder-level dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before the 10th day of the month following each monthly reporting period; the TSD covering the month of December of a fiscal year ending 31 December is due on 10 January of the next calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory euro de-minimis threshold exists; contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is required for all material cross-border related-party transactions and must be produced immediately upon an e-MTA request. Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting become mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue reaches EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a distinct Economic Substance Regulations backup, but the general anti-abuse rule, OECD BEPS beneficial ownership principles, and place-of-effective-management rules govern substance. An OÜ operating a subscription-box service must maintain genuine economic activity in Estonia: strategic board decision-making in Estonia, suitable operational capacity (inventory, logistics or outsourced supply chain under local control), real personnel or verifiable sub-contracting arrangements, and a local bank account. When substance is absent, the tax authority may recharacterize profit streams as direct income of the beneficial owner, impose personal income tax at 20%, restore social tax and unemployment insurance obligations, deny treaty and Directive benefits, and retroactively asses taxes for up to 10 years.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Criminal Fraud

Statutory sanction: Penal Code § 393 sets a maximum imprisonment of 5 years where the evaded tax exceeds EUR 10,000, and up to 7 years where the evaded amount exceeds EUR 100,000; a daily-income-based fine is additionally imposed. The Tax and Customs Board reassesses the under-taxed amount together with late interest at 0.06% per day (maximum 50%) and a 50% penalty on the unpaid additional tax in severe cases.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly directs or participates in the evasion is jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax, penalties and late interest. Criminal conviction results in imprisonment, confiscation of personal assets, disqualification from board service, and travel bans pending enforcement.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian general anti-abuse rule the tax authority may disregard the corporate form and tax the whole income at the level of the ultimate beneficial owner at 20% individual income tax; if the income is recharacterized as salary, a 20% employment income tax plus 33% social tax and unemployment insurance are imposed. The tax reassessment period extends to 10 years. The OÜ is at risk of administrative dissolution by the Commercial Register and revocation of e-Residency benefits.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shadow directors face joint and several civil liability for the full tax shortfall and penalties. They may be disqualified from board service for up to 3 years. Travel bans and asset seizure are enforceable. Criminal liability extends to false declarations and money laundering under Penal Code § 394.
Failure to File TSD / Non-Payment of Distribution Tax

Statutory sanction: Late payment interest of 0.06% per day (maximum 50%) is imposed on the unpaid tax; a late filing fine starts at EUR 400 per TSD and increases to EUR 1,200 for a repeated violation. The Tax and Customs Board may assess the tax on an estimated basis, issue a forced collection order, and seize bank accounts or third-party receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if the company's inability to pay results from their breach of duty, including active distribution of assets before satisfying tax obligations. They face civil restitution up to the unpaid tax, public disqualification from management functions, and criminal prosecution involving up to 3 years' imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board may adjust the taxable base and increase the distribution tax by 20% or 14% on the recharacterized profit, add late interest at 0.06% per day, and impose a fine of up to 50% of the additional tax assessed. If the adjustment recharacterizes interest or royalties, a 20% domestic WHT is additionally collected. Criminal penalties apply for falsified TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign off on non arm's-length agreements are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax under-assessment. Corporate veiling may be lifted when TP policies deliberately strip Estonian profit. Personal liability includes civil damages to the company, disqualification from board service, and criminal sanctions in the case of intentional false documentation.
