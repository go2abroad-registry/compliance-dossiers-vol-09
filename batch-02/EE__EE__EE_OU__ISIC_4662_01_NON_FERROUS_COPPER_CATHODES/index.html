Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA) — central digital portal e-Maksuamet (https://emta.ee); corporate registration counterpart: Estonian Commercial Register (Äriregister, https://ariregister.rik.ee), with remote onboarding through the state e-Residency digital-ID programme.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ): (1) incorporate via the e-Äriregister Commercial Register using a notarized application or e-Residency digital ID; the issued business registry code (registrikood) is automatically the statutory Estonian tax ID, so no separate TIN application exists; (2) MTA automatically opens the taxpayer account and e-MTA user profile upon registration; (3) when taxable turnover is expected, file the VAT registration application (form KVA) in e-MTA; MTA also issues the EORI number required for export declarations automatically on VAT registration, or a separate EORI application can be filed in the e-Toll customs portal; (4) a non-resident person making taxable supplies without an Estonian seat, permanent establishment, or EU tax residence must register through the simplified non-resident VAT procedure and may be required to appoint an Estonian fiscal representative (tax agent); (5) documentary evidence for foreign shareholders and beneficial owners includes certified home-registry extract, corporate resolution, registered address and person-of-contact confirmation, and beneficial-ownership declarations for AML purposes; (6) no subnational tax ID or municipal tax registration exists because Estonia is a unitary jurisdiction (only municipal land tax is separately administered and requires no separate identifier).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ): (1) incorporate via the e-Äriregister Commercial Register using a notarized application or e-Residency digital ID; the issued business registry code (registrikood) is automatically the statutory Estonian tax ID, so no separate TIN application exists; (2) MTA automatically opens the taxpayer account and e-MTA user profile upon registration; (3) when taxable turnover is expected, file the VAT registration application (form KVA) in e-MTA; MTA also issues the EORI number required for export declarations automatically on VAT registration, or a separate EORI application can be filed in the e-Toll customs portal; (4) a non-resident person making taxable supplies without an Estonian seat, permanent establishment, or EU tax residence must register through the simplified non-resident VAT procedure and may be required to appoint an Estonian fiscal representative (tax agent); (5) documentary evidence for foreign shareholders and beneficial owners includes certified home-registry extract, corporate resolution, registered address and person-of-contact confirmation, and beneficial-ownership declarations for AML purposes; (6) no subnational tax ID or municipal tax registration exists because Estonia is a unitary jurisdiction (only municipal land tax is separately administered and requires no separate identifier).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia does not maintain a check-the-box or hybrid-election regime comparable to the US. Statutory options include: (1) flexible timing election on declaring dividends — deferred distribution defers the corporate tax event entirely (0% while undistributed; distributed-profit model); (2) election for the preferential 14/86 rate on regular dividend distributions to natural persons, conditional on an established dividend history and the concurrent remittance by the distributing company of a 7% withholding to MTA; (3) group taxation election — a resident parent controlling at least 80% of a subsidiary may elect a consolidated group, so that intra-group dividend distributions do not trigger the distributed-profit tax; (4) VAT group election (common taxable person) for closely connected resident companies redesignated as a single VAT taxpayer; (5) no hybrid mismatch election is available — ATAD anti-hybrid rules are implemented in the Income Tax Act and are self-executing, with no elective redesignation of instruments as debt versus equity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€20,000 — mandatory VAT registration threshold on annual taxable turnover in Estonia effective 1 January 2026 (previously €40,000 from 1 January 2025 and €50,000 before 2025; the standard rate was raised from 20% to 22% on 1 July 2025). Cross-border B2C distance sales of goods or electronic services to EU consumers require OSS registration once EU-wide distance sales exceed €10,000; non-resident taxable persons that physically supply or dispatch goods in Estonia are registrable regardless of the €20,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under VAT Act Section 27, a 0% rate applies to supplies of goods transported by the supplier, the customer, or a third party directly to a non-EU destination (third-country export), including physical delivery of refined Grade A copper cathodes. To secure the zero rate: (1) the goods must physically leave Estonia by the 90th day following the time of supply, evidenced by an accepted EU customs export declaration (EX A/MRN); (2) the exporter must obtain and retain proof of export, such as a customs transport document, bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill; (3) title and risk must pass so that the goods are consigned to the non-EU customer under documented Incoterms; (4) the export customs declaration must name the Estonian OÜ as the exporter with the correct copper cathodes customs tariff code; and (5) all supporting documents must be retained for at least seven years for MTA desk audit and post-clearance customs review. Offshore B2B services supplied to a non-EU business customer are taxable in the customer's jurisdiction under place-of-supply rules and may qualify for 0% without customs documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration — accepted EX A export declaration with Movement Reference Number (MRN) naming the Estonian OÜ as exporter and declaring refined copper cathodes under the applicable customs tariff code
  • Bill of Lading / CMR consignment note / Air Waybill evidencing actual physical departure of the goods from the EU border to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List (grade, quantity, net/gross weight, LME settlement price, Incoterms, shipping marks)
  • LME Warrant or bonded warehouse / terminal certificate evidencing transfer of title in Grade A refined copper cathodes
  • Inward Payment Evidence — SWIFT MT103 or documentary Letter of Credit settlement advice, evidencing receipt of consideration and providing payment traceability for AML-enhanced review

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API stamping, or continuous transaction control is in force. Mandatory periodic VAT return (form KMD) with annexes KD, WSD, and INF is due on the 20th day of the month following the tax period. B2G suppliers must transmit structured e-invoices in the national e-arve (Finvoice-based) interoperable format; B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary, although the Estonian tax reform agenda and the EU ViDA legislative programme are expected to introduce near-real-time transaction reporting and a European e-invoice standard by 2030. Until then, EU-standard XML or PDF invoicing archive retention is sufficient for MTA audit purposes; wholesale commodity trades should, however, maintain full electronic file integrity for LME warehouse warrants, LC instruments, and customs MRN records.

MTA VAT audits apply heightened scrutiny to high-value export trading such as refined copper cathodes. Where the 90-day export deadline or the proof-of-export file is defective, the 0% rate is retrospectively replaced with 22% VAT on the full supply value, plus 0.06% daily default interest under the Tax Procedure Act (MKS Section 128) and fines under VAT Act Section 55 (up to €3,200 per infringement per tax period). Use of falsified or circular export documentation (carousel or ghost-export patterns) constitutes criminal tax fraud under Penal Code Section 385, carrying custodial penalties of up to three years, or up to five years together with confiscation for large-scale evasion; the statutory reassessment window is generally three years, extended where tax returns were not filed or facts were intentionally concealed, and no limitation runs while criminal proceedings are pending. VAT refunds to traders under enhanced AML or tax-risk monitoring may be postponed for up to 60 days pending document verification. Estonia applies EU free movement of capital, so no statutory FX repartriation deadline exists; the 180-day parameter reflects the LME settlement cycle, documentary LC tenor, and the export-audit expectation of clean payment traceability within six months of shipment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a eurozone member with full current and capital account convertibility. The Foreign Exchange Act does not impose conversion restrictions for EUR or foreign currencies. Profit remittance of dividends to non-residents is free from exchange control approval; only standard withholding tax rules and beneficial ownership declarations apply. No capital account permit is required for foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, or trade payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Estonia does not mandate the surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation of export proceeds within a statutory timeline. Copper cathode exporters may retain export receipts in any freely convertible currency in an Estonian bank account or foreign account, without FIU pre-approval, provided that the economic substance and transfer pricing rules of Estonian Income Tax Act are respected.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), as a member of the Eurosystem and the European Central Bank

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Passported EU e-money institutions and digital EMI neobanks can open remote accounts and provide API-based MT103/SEPA and FX execution. However, pure digital EMIs frequently reject non-ferrous metals wholesalers due to the high-risk commodity trading classification, lack of documentary LC issuance, and limited ability to hold LME warrant transfer collateral. Digital EMIs are legally viable only for marginal operational flows, not as the primary clearing rail for copper cathode physical settlement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on all retained and undistributed annual accounting profits. Corporate income tax is imposed exclusively at the moment profit is distributed to a shareholder: 20% of the gross distribution (computed as 20/80 of the net cash dividend). Deemed distributions, fringe benefits, and non-business expenses are taxed at 24/76. The Estonian Osaühing therefore books no current-year CIT expense while profits remain reinvested in trading operations, inventory, or cash reserves.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by the Estonian OÜ to a non-resident shareholder (computed as 20/80 of net dividend); dividends to Estonian resident shareholders are not subject to withholding because the OÜ itself accounts for the 20% distribution tax. Qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of the OÜ for at least 12 months are exempt at source under the transposed Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form TSD for each calendar month, together with full payment of declared tax, must be submitted no later than the 10th day of the following calendar month (e.g., the declaration for January distributions is due on 10 February).

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual report must be filed within six months of the end of the financial year; the financial year may differ from the calendar year if the OÜ's articles of association so provide. Audit is mandatory when the OÜ exceeds two of three thresholds: sales revenue €4,000,000; total assets €2,000,000; average employees 50.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No express statutory monetary threshold exists in the Income Tax Act for transfer pricing documentation; the Estonian Tax and Customs Board's administrative guidance, aligned with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and EU Joint Transfer Pricing Forum standards, expects a contemporaneous Local File and Master File where the aggregate annual value of cross-border controlled transactions of the OÜ exceeds €3,000,000, or where the global group turnover exceeds €50,000,000 and the Estonian entity enters controlled transactions above €500,000. Practical safe-harbour treatment is applied where total related-party transactions are below €250,000.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a codified Economic Substance Regulations statute of the offshore type; however, substance is enforced indirectly with equal force through: the OECD/MLI Principal Purpose Test in every Estonian DTA; the ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule (Income Tax Act Section 83); ATAD Controlled Foreign Company rules imposing 25% deemed taxation on undistributed CFC profits where the foreign entity pays effective tax below 15%; and the Estonian Supreme Court's form-over-substance case law on letterbox companies. An OÜ that has an e-resident board member but no employees, no Estonian office, no board minutes, and no decision-making record for the physical copper trades will be treated as a shell, the treaty benefits on outbound dividends may be denied, and profit reattribution to the true place of management abroad will follow.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Disguised Dividend Distribution

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Section 388 of the Estonian Penal Code: imprisonment up to five years for the responsible natural person; the OÜ as a legal person faces a monetary fine of up to €1,000,000 plus confiscation of the evaded amount; administratively the MTA will impose the full 20/80 distribution tax, a 20% punitive surcharge, and daily overdue interest at 0.06% per day from the original deemed distribution date.

Director personal liability: Board members personally and jointly liable under Section 8 of the Taxation Act for the portion of unpaid tax attributable to their intentional non-declaration; criminal confiscation of the director's assets, travel ban, and imprisonment up to five years; the OÜ veil is pierced where the director distributed the tax funds to the shareholder before settling the MTA claim.
Fatal Misreading of the 0% Retained Profit Regime

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax at 20/80 assessed on the full grossed-up amount, daily interest of 0.06%, and a misdeclaration fine of up to €1,200 per TSD period; if the transaction was deliberately structured, criminal tax fraud penalties apply; the assessment limitation period extends from five to seven years for concealed distributions.

Director personal liability: Director jointly liable where the loan approval exceeded the director's authority or where the director personally benefited; a court may impose a travel ban and the director may be barred for up to three years from new OÜ board positions; enforcement follows the director into EU member states under the EU Recovery Directive.
Letterbox OÜ / e-Residency Abuse / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; the MTA reattributes the profit to the actual seller or to the controlling shareholder's residence and applies the full 20% withholding plus 20/80 deemed distribution tax; administrative fine up to €3,200 for submission of false substance declarations to the Commercial Register; deregistration of the e-residency digital ID and revocation of the Estonian agent mandate.

Director personal liability: The e-resident director may be held personally liable for the tax differential under Section 8 of the Taxation Act where the shell structure was the director's own design; criminal liability for fraudulent avoidance of corporate income tax arises where the scheme causes the Estonian state a tax loss exceeding €20,000, leading to imprisonment of up to five years and a personal fine of up to €500,000.
AML, Sanctions Evasion, and Metals-Trading Illicit Structuring

Statutory sanction: Under the Estonian RTRS and EU Regulation 269/2014: fines for the OÜ up to €8,000,000 or 10% of annual global turnover; confiscation of the copper inventory and the proceeds; criminal sanctions under the Penal Code for circumventing international sanctions, with imprisonment up to eight years; the MTA treats the warrant transfer as a deemed distribution taxable at 20/80 plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to €400,000, imprisonment up to four years for failure to apply customer due diligence where the OÜ transacts in goods against cash above €10,000, and a five-year prohibition on acting as a board member in any Estonian company; the financial intelligence unit (FIU) may issue a freezing order against the director's personal assets and apply a cross-border travel ban.
Persistent Non-Filing and Compulsory Strike-off

Statutory sanction: Compulsory strike-off from the Commercial Register; the OÜ loses legal personality and its assets escheat to the Estate of Estonia; supervisory fines up to €1,300; the founder is barred for five years from registering a new company in Estonia; the Benificial Ownership register entry is flagged to EU member states and the OÜ is denied banking and e-residency services.

Director personal liability: The board member becomes personally liable for all obligations incurred after the date the duty to file was breached; inclusive of the MTA's punitive tax estimate; the director may be banned from entering Estonia and, under the EU Recovery Directive, member states can enforce the personal liability judgment against the director's foreign bank accounts.
