Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Maksu- ja Tolliamet (Estonian Tax and Customs Board, MTA), Tallinn; unified e-services portal e-MTA (e-maksuamet) at https://e-mta.emta.ee; company registration is administered jointly with the e-Business Register (Äriregister) operated by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishes an Osaühing (OÜ) via the e-Business Register using e-Residency digital authentication or a notarized founding resolution; the resulting registry code (registrikood) automatically serves as the statutory TIN for Estonian tax purposes, including VAT. Mandatory VAT registration is filed electronically with MTA through e-MTA once taxable turnover exceeds EUR 40,000 per calendar year, on voluntary registration below that threshold, or without any threshold for non-resident entities making taxable supplies in Estonia, unless the one-stop-shop rules apply. Wholesale trading in marine fuel oil and distillates further requires an excise trader authorization from MTA under the Excise Duty Act and an economic activity license under the General Part of the Economic Activities Code Act where the entity holds or physically handles excise goods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (distribution-based deferral regime): an OÜ is a resident legal entity taxed under Estonian Income Tax Act section 50 at 0% on retained annual profits, with final income tax of 20% (or 14% for qualifying regular distributions) imposed on distributed profits; no check-the-box pass-through election exists for OÜ, while general partnerships and limited partnerships are fiscally transparent.

Available election forms

Estonia has no US-style entity classification election. Available statutory elections include: (a) voluntary VAT registration below threshold; (b) election to classify distributions as regular distributions eligible for the 14% rate under Income Tax Act section 50(3); (c) election under the VAT Act to zero-rate intra-EU supplies where export movement is completed within the statutory dispatch period; (d) application of ATAD hybrid-mismatch and CFC rules through annual income tax declarations; (e) use of OSS/IOSS schemes for distance sales and import VAT; and (f) conclusion of advance pricing agreements or advance VAT rulings with MTA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover per calendar year for resident suppliers (VAT Act section 19); no threshold for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Estonia other than via OSS; intra-EU acquisition threshold of EUR 10,000 in Estonia also triggers registration; zero-rated export supplies are excluded from the threshold calculation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under VAT Act sections 16-17 where goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or a third party, provided the export is evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration and the goods are dispatched within the statutory period (MTA benchmark: three months from supply, with extensions only in documented exceptional cases). Marine fuel and distillates supplied as stores to vessels engaged in international traffic are likewise zero-rated upon proof of international voyage. Cross-border brokerage services of an OÜ are placed by EU VAT place-of-supply rules at the location of the underlying goods when the services are supplied to a taxable person; where those goods lie outside Estonia, the Estonian zero-rating or refund route may not apply and VAT may arise in the foreign port jurisdiction. Excess input VAT on export purchases is offset through the monthly KMD return; EU counterparties use the electronic e-MTA refund procedure and third-country claimants file paper-based applications through a fiscal representative.

  • Customs Export Declaration - Single Administrative Document (SAD/EAD) with MTA customs release and proof of EU exit (RN number).
  • Commercial transport evidence - Bill of Lading or CMR waybill (for marine fuel: cargo manifest plus outward port clearance records) demonstrating physical departure of goods from Estonian or other EU territory.
  • Treasury and FX evidence - Inward Remittance Certificate and Swift MT103 or Standby Letter of Credit drawdown records evidencing receipt of export proceeds into the OÜ's Estonian bank account for third-country collections.

Estonia mandates e-invoicing for all B2G invoices (European e-Invoicing standard since 30 April 2019) but has no national pre-clearance VAT clearance database or mandatory real-time fiscal stamping for ordinary B2B invoices. Monthly VAT returns (KMD) and the KMD-INF annex for intra-EU, export and third-country transactions must be filed through e-MTA by the 20th of the following month; cash-register fiscalization transmits real-time T6/T7 records to MTA; API and X-Road-based machine-to-machine filing are available for fuel traders and large exporters.

Failure to substantiate an export triggers retrospective reassessment of the supply at the standard VAT rate (20% now, 22% from 1 July 2025), daily late-payment interest of 0.06% (approximately 21.9% annually) from the original return due date, and suspension or disallowance of the corresponding input VAT offset until documentary proof is supplied. Administrative fines for incorrect or late filings reach EUR 1,300; deliberate falsification of export documentation or fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax fraud under Penal Code sections 402-403, punishable by imprisonment of up to five years, or from three to seven years in cases of large-scale or organized VAT refund fraud. Directors and managers may incur joint and several liability for VAT and excise debts of the OÜ; standard tax assessments are subject to a three-year limitation, extended to six years for deliberate misreporting and to criminal prosecution timelines for fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia is a eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under the EU Treaty of the Functioning of the European Union free movement of capital rules. There are no foreign exchange controls, surrender requirements, or exchange-rate restrictions for crude oil and refined product wholesale trade. Profit remittance is permitted; however, dividend distributions by an OÜ are subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the time of distribution at a general rate of 20/80 (20% on the gross distribution), with a reduced rate of 14/86 if regular distributions are paid and the withholding/payment rules are met. No additional currency conversion licence or central bank approval is required for legitimate trade-related repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline is imposed by Eesti Pank. Export receipts denominated in foreign currency may be held in EU or non-EU bank accounts, subject to the AML/CFT and tax transparency standards applicable to the operator, and must be reflected in the company's annual accounts. The relevant money laundering reporting obligation under the Estonian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act applies to credit institutions and payment service providers handling such proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) in coordination with the Estonian Financial Supervision Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) for licensed banking and payment institution conduct

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for legitimate bulk commodity treasury operations. Estonian-regulated fintech EMI or payment institutions are permitted under the Payment Institutions Act and EBA passporting; however, they are generally unwilling to onboard physical wholesale fuel commodity businesses due to high AML risk, high transaction volume, and the need for standby letters of credit. Non-EU/offshore digital EMIs are not legally acceptable as primary clearing rails because they cannot access real-time gross settlement via TARGET2 or provide robust SWIFT correspondent banking for marine fuel trades. An EU/EEA-authorised EMI may serve as an ancillary collection vehicle only if a full local bank account and a licensed bank clearing line remain the principal treasury gateway.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on retained/reinvested profits; 20% of the gross distributed amount (i.e., 20/80 of the net cash, equal to 25% effective on cash) on profit distributions; preferential 14% of gross (14/86 of net, 16.28% effective) for regular distributions within the three-year average ceiling

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20% of the gross distribution (20/80 of net cash, i.e., 25% effective on distributed cash) withheld and remitted by the resident OÜ; preferential 14% of gross (14/86 of net) for regular distributions of previously taxed retained earnings within the three-year average cap; no separate non-resident shareholder WHT is levied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the 10th day of the month following the calendar month in which the profit distribution is decided/paid (e.g., a distribution resolved on 25 June is declared and paid by 10 July); if the 10th falls on a non-working day, the deadline moves to the next working day

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the end of the financial year (a 31 December year-end must be filed by 30 June of the following year); the distribution resolution and the underlying TSD follow the same calendar-month deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 annual (consolidated) revenue and controlled transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 1,500,000 in the financial year trigger the Local File obligation; Master File is required for MNE groups with consolidated turnover of at least EUR 50,000,000. All documentation must be submitted within 60 days of the e-MTA's request.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; nevertheless, ITA § 6 (place of effective management), the general anti-abuse rule (Taxation Act § 11) and EU ATAD demand genuine substance. A paper OÜ used merely as an invoice-and-distribution conduit for marine-fuel brokerage profits will be re-characterised as a sham; the 0% advantage collapses and a 20% deemed-distribution tax is imposed on the entire income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent TSD declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine for the legal entity up to €1.6 million; reassessment of the hidden distribution plus daily late-payment interest at 0.06%; tax misdemeanour fine up to €1,200; seizure of bank accounts, SBLC proceeds and vessels/cargo under enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment up to 5 years under Penal Code § 385, civil restitution in the full amount of the evaded tax, disqualification from board positions, and an exit/travel ban (väljasõidukeeld) until the debt is secured or settled.
Shell company and e-Residency substance abuse

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation under GAAR; deemed distribution tax at 20% on all income; loss of DTA benefits; compulsory deregistration from the Commercial Register; retroactive exit taxes enforced on the foreign assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and creditors' claims (Commercial Code § 37); travel bans and personal asset attachments apply; persistent abuse is prosecuted as tax fraud with imprisonment up to 5 years.
