Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet); e-MTA electronic taxpayer portal at https://emta.ee

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign entity engaging in taxable activities in Estonia must first obtain an Estonian registry code from the Commercial Register (Äriregister), which is automatically used as the taxpayer identification number (TIN). The Osaühing (OÜ) acquired through e-Residency digital authentication or notarial deed receives its statutory registry code/TIN at incorporation. For VAT, the entity must apply for an Estonian VAT registration number (KMKR) through the e-MTA portal; a non-EU/non-EEA entity without a permanent establishment in Estonia and without a fixed place of business in Estonia is required to appoint a local fiscal representative, file the prescribed registration application, and obtain VAT ID before making taxable supplies. The Estonian Tax and Customs Board issues the VAT number electronically within five working days of a complete application.

The Estonian Tax and Customs Board issues the VAT number electronically within five working days of a complete application.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box or pass-through election exists in Estonia. An OÜ is mandatorily taxed at entity level under the distribution-based corporate income tax system (0% on retained profits; 20%/25% on distributed profits). Elective VAT registration is available to taxable persons whose turnover is below the EUR 40,000 statutory threshold. Distance sellers of goods may elect the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme for intra-Community and imported low-value consignments. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) are mandatory and not elective.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
22%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 40,000 taxable annual turnover for persons established in Estonia; nil threshold for non-resident suppliers (any single taxable supply triggers registration duty)

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Supplies of goods physically exported from Estonia to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT provided the supplier has documentary proof of export within the statutory time limit and the buyer is a non-EU person. Supply of services closely linked to export transport or directly related to exported goods may also be zero-rated. The AI pet robot goods must leave Estonian customs territory and the export declaration must be filed in the exporter's own name; failure to produce acceptable evidence within 90 days (or extended period permitted by the Tax and Customs Board) triggers assessment at the standard VAT rate.

All VAT returns must be filed electronically via the e-MTA portal using digital signature or API integration. Government (B2G) purchases require structured e-invoices (standardised European format) submitted through the e-MTA interoperable operator directory. Cash register (kassaaparaat) fiscal data must be transmitted in real time to the Estonian Tax and Customs Board through secure fiscal data transmission operator systems. There is no general continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance/API digital stamping mandatory for B2B invoices, but exporters must maintain electronic customs declarations and transport records that are fully machine-readable and accessible to the tax authority.

      
The Estonian Tax and Customs Board may reassess zero-rated export transactions within the general three-year limitation period (extended to six years in cases of suspected fraudulent intent). Where zero-rating is invalidated, the tax authority issues an assessment for 22% VAT plus statutory interest from the due date. Under the Taxation Act, a tax fraud surcharge of up to 20% of the understated tax may be imposed, and deliberate or grossly negligent evasion constitutes a criminal offence under Penal Code Section 404, punishable by fines and up to seven years' imprisonment. Export documentation must be retained for at least seven years to support the 0% rate and any VAT refund claim; for AI hardware exporters using Stripe and credit-card rails, the bank/processor settlement statements must clearly map to each export invoice and satisfy the 180-day customary collection cycle.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Estonia is a euro area Member State and applies the full freedom of capital and current account movements under Article 63 TFEU. Convertibility is unrestricted for euro and foreign currency settlements; there are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements and no administrative approvals for profit remittances. Dividend distributions to non-residents are subject only to the standard Estonian withholding tax framework (generally 20%, reduced to 7% for regularly distributed dividends under the territorial exemption regime), and any EU/EEA treaty relief or income tax credit may further reduce the effective burden. Cross-border transfers are free, but EU Regulation 2015/847 information requirements, sanctions screening and balance-of-payments statistical reporting must be satisfied.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. The euro is legal tender and there is no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within a specified timeline. Export invoices may be denominated in any freely chosen currency; proceeds may be retained in the exporter's foreign-currency account or converted to euro at a licensed credit institution. General banking practice requires the underlying commercial documentation (contracts, customs declarations, invoices) to be available for AML/KYC verification and to support cross-border payment purpose codes.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) in cooperation with Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision Authority) and Rahapesu Andmebüroo (Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit) for AML licensing, cross-border payment monitoring and balance-of-payments statistics reporting

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
High viability for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into Estonia, such as Monese, Wise Business, or Payoneer, provided the EMI has a valid e-money license recognized under EEA passporting and full client-asset safeguarding. Legal constraints require that virtual IBANs be backed by an actual segregated pool at an EEA bank; non-EEA EMIs (US/UK) are less viable due to lack of EEA passporting, Estonian consumer/investor protection thresholds, and restrictions on receiving third-country capital without local onboarding. Use of fintech rails is permitted for operational disbursements, but high-value treasury and FX hedging should be executed with a systemically relevant local or EU bank to meet counterparty and settlement risk standards.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Capital injections into the OÜ may be routed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer using the following Estonian balance-of-payments purpose codes: D.3 (equity/paid-in capital) for share capital or shareholder contributions and F.2 (loan) for shareholder loans. The bank transfer cannot omit the precise narrative: the company Registry Code (Registrikood), the ISIC/NACE code (3240), the transaction description, and the source-of-funds explanation. For non-EU inbound transfers, an additional CBPR+ SWIFT field 72 or bank-specific source-of-funds questionnaire must be completed; the funds must originate from an account in the UBO's name or a documented corporate shareholder account. Verification evidence includes board minutes, share subscription documents, loan agreement, AML evidence of the remitting bank, and registration in the Estonian Commercial Register where equity is registered. Estonia applies no minimum capital, but the injection must align with the financial plan to avoid thin-capitalization issues.Outbound trade settlements may use SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier wire payments, SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for interbank bulk payments, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EEA EUR sub-€100,000 payments, TARGET2/RIX RTGS for urgent high-value local euro settlement, and commercial documentary letters of credit (UCP 600) for import of components from Asia. For Chinese suppliers, offshore RMB (CNH) via SWIFT or documentary collection with banks in Shanghai/Hong Kong is permissible but requires full invoice-matching. All disbursements must carry accurate purpose codes, align with exporter/import customs records, and be blocked for sanctions-affected jurisdictions. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
0% on retained and undistributed profits; 20/80 (25% gross equivalent) of the net amount of any profit distribution, fringe benefit, gift, non-business expense, or exit distribution; reduced 14/86 (16.3% gross equivalent) for regular dividend distributions to resident or EEA-qualifying natural persons up to the three-year average distribution

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
20/80 (25% of gross distribution) on profit distributions to all shareholders not benefiting from the reduced-rate regime; 14/86 (16.3% of gross) for regular dividends to natural persons resident in Estonia, EEA, or treaty states within the three-year average distribution cap; 0% for dividends paid to qualifying corporate shareholders (EU/EEA parent or treaty state company) holding at least 10% of the share capital (participation exemption principle; see § 50(1) of the Estonian Income Tax Act)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Estonia does not impose a statutory Local File / Master File preparation obligation in the Estonian Income Tax Act; in practice, OÜs engaged in related-party transactions with offshore or related EEA entities must be able to produce, upon a tax audit request, documentation for intercompany flows exceeding EUR 50,000 per annum per related counterparty; CbCR applies to multi-national groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (EU Council Directive 2016/881 as transposed in Estonia)

      
Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a UAE-style Statutory ESR framework, but economic substance is tested through the GAAR in the Tax Procedure Act (§ 3(2) of MKS) and the place-of-effective-management (POEM) doctrine: an OÜ whose board is non-resident and whose management decisions occur entirely abroad can be re-domiciled for tax purposes (enterprise deemed Estonian-centre), and a letterbox OÜ with no personnel, premises, or payroll in Estonia risks being treated as a foreign-managed entity; for the purpose of avoiding the 0% dividend tax abuse, the MTA recharacterizes artificial arrangements as sales of the Estonian business (exit taxation on retained profits at 20/80)

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Penal Code § 397)
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal fine or detention; if the evaded tax exceeds the large-scale threshold of EUR 40,000, imprisonment up to 5 years (aggravated up to 7 years if committed in an organized group); the tax authority levies 0.03% daily surcharge and up to a 10% procedural surcharge

        
Director personal liability: Directors as the statutory taxpayers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest, and surcharges when they knew or should have known of the evasion; criminal conviction triggers travel bans, asset seizure, and disqualification from board positions; cross-border e-Resident directors remain exposed to Estonian criminal proceedings

      

      
      

        Constructive Distribution and TP Mispricing (§ 49 TuMS)
        
Statutory sanction: The excess is recharacterized as a dividend distribution and taxed at 20/80, with 0.03% daily late-payment interest; the MTA may impose an additional 10% tax surcharge if the taxpayer fails to disclose the adjustment in a corrective TSD

        
Director personal liability: The board member who signed the TSD is jointly liable for the shortfall if the transfer pricing documentation is absent or fabricated; criminal tax evasion liability attaches at the EUR 40,000 threshold; sanctions include personal bank account seizures and prohibition from practicing as a director for up to 3 years

      

      
      

        Shell Company / False Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Re-domiciliation: profits of the foreign-managed OÜ are treated as Estonia-source and taxed at 20/80 upon distribution; the company becomes a tax non-resident and loses the 0% rate on retained earnings; the MTA may also void the shareholding structure and assess exit taxation on unrealized reserves

        
Director personal liability: Under the Commercial Code, if the annual report is not filed within 8 months and the OÜ is forced to deregister, the board members bear unlimited joint and several liability for all corporate obligations; the use of offshore nominee directors to disguise the true owner triggers money-laundering suspicion reporting to the FIU and potential AML fines
