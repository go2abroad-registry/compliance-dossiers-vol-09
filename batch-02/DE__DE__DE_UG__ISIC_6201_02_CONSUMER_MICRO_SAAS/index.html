Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern, BZSt) for the German VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.); the competent local Tax Office (Finanzamt) for the domestic tax number (Steuernummer), corporate income tax, trade tax, and VAT registration. The principal electronic filing and tax registration platform is ELSTER (Elektronische Steuererklärung) at elster.de.

        
E-government filing portal
A newly incorporated UG must submit the electronic tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) through ELSTER to the competent Finanzamt, generally within one month after commencement of business or acquisition of the legal entity. The questionnaire requires the commercial-register details, registered office, directors, beneficial ownership information where requested, business activities, expected turnover and profit, accounting method, bank details, corporate income tax and trade tax information, and VAT facts. The Finanzamt issues the domestic Steuernummer after review. The UG or its authorized tax adviser may separately apply to the BZSt for a USt-IdNr.; the application requires the legal name, legal form, registered address, domestic Steuernummer, commercial-register details, and responsible persons. A foreign entity making German taxable supplies must register with the competent Finanzamt unless a statutory reverse-charge, marketplace, One-Stop Shop, or other simplification applies. Non-EU businesses generally require a German tax representative only where a specific procedure or representation arrangement makes one necessary; Germany does not impose a universal fiscal-representative requirement for every non-EU VAT registration. VAT registration is not itself evidence of a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
A UG is a German corporation subject to Körperschaftsteuer and solidarity surcharge on taxable income and generally Gewerbesteuer on trade income. Germany has no general United States-style check-the-box election and does not ordinarily permit a UG to elect transparent partnership treatment. Corporate-law conversions, mergers, or statutory reorganizations may change the tax treatment under the Reorganization Tax Act but are not routine classification elections. For VAT, the entity may claim the Kleinunternehmer exemption under Section 19 of the German VAT Act if statutory turnover limits are satisfied; it may waive that exemption and opt into normal VAT taxation, with the election generally binding for the statutory period. EU cross-border B2C digital supplies may be reported through the Union or non-Union OSS, as applicable. Hybrid mismatch rules under Sections 4k of the Income Tax Act and related anti-hybrid provisions can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they are anti-avoidance rules, not elective entity classifications.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
19%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
There is no general German VAT registration threshold for a business making German taxable supplies; registration can be required from the first taxable supply. The German small-business exemption under Section 19 of the VAT Act is available, subject to the applicable statutory conditions, where prior-calendar-year domestic turnover did not exceed EUR 25,000 and current-calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 100,000. The exemption is not a general registration threshold, does not apply to all supplies, and does not generally permit input VAT recovery. For EU-established businesses using the EU-wide cross-border B2C threshold for intra-EU distance sales and electronically supplied services, the relevant aggregate threshold is EUR 10,000, subject to the OSS framework. Non-EU suppliers of B2C electronically supplied services may generally use the non-Union OSS or register in the customer jurisdictions, subject to the applicable rules.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of goods from Germany to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with input-tax recovery under the German export-supply rules if the goods are transported or dispatched to the third country and the supplier can substantiate exportation and the foreign destination through prescribed records. The supplier must retain evidence linking the transaction, goods, purchaser, transport, and exit from the EU customs territory. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is outside Germany. B2B services generally follow the customer-location rule and may be outside the scope of German VAT, often with reverse-charge treatment in the customer jurisdiction. B2C electronically supplied services are generally taxed where the consumer is located; supplies to consumers outside the EU may be outside the scope of German VAT if customer location is properly established. A German SaaS business must distinguish VAT-exempt exports, out-of-scope services, and zero-rated transactions because the legal and documentary consequences differ.

Germany does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system or mandatory government pre-clearance for ordinary domestic invoices. From 1 January 2025, a German-established business must generally be able to receive an electronic invoice for domestic B2B transactions, with statutory transitional rules for issuing structured e-invoices. Under the current transition, ordinary invoices may generally continue under specified conditions through 31 December 2026; businesses whose prior-year turnover did not exceed EUR 800,000 may generally continue issuing non-structured invoices through 31 December 2027. From 1 January 2028, the general domestic B2B e-invoicing requirements are intended to apply, subject to statutory exclusions and technical requirements. A compliant e-invoice must use an approved structured format, principally XRechnung or a compatible hybrid format such as ZUGFeRD, and must contain the mandatory VAT invoice data. No routine digital tax stamp or real-time API clearance is required. B2C invoices, foreign supplies, and many exempt or low-value transactions remain subject to separate rules. SaaS providers should separately assess German B2B, B2C, EU OSS, non-EU OSS, platform-provider, and payment-processor invoicing responsibilities.

      
Germany has no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no universal requirement for an inward remittance certificate. If an export exemption or other VAT treatment is unsupported, the Finanzamt may assess the underpaid VAT retrospectively, generally together with interest under the German Fiscal Code, and may deny associated input-tax recovery where legally applicable. Inaccurate VAT returns can trigger administrative penalties, late-filing consequences, and, where culpability and statutory elements are met, tax-evasion or reckless tax-reduction proceedings. Tax evasion may result in imprisonment or fines; aggravated cases carry materially higher sanctions. Incorrect input-tax refunds, OSS declarations, or export claims may be clawed back through amended assessments and recovery of overpaid refunds. Deliberate fraud, forged customs evidence, false customer-location data, or knowingly incorrect invoices may create criminal, civil, customs, and VAT-liability exposure for the UG, its directors, and participating advisers. Records supporting VAT treatment must generally be retained for the applicable statutory retention period, including the extended retention periods applicable to accounting and tax records under German law.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Germany maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The euro is freely convertible under EU primary law (TFEU Article 63) and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. There are no exchange control permits, no approval requirements for capital inflows or outflows, and no restrictions on profit remittances or dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders. Practical hurdles are limited to company-law distributable profit tests, dividend withholding tax (generally 26.375% inclusive of solidarity surcharge, subject to treaty reduction), and monthly Bundesbank balance-of-payments reporting for individual cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500. No prior central bank or BaFin licensing is required for the UG to receive or disburse cross-border payments.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
German law imposes a commercial collection duty rather than a surrender requirement. Under Section 4 of the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance, export receivables must be collected within the contractual payment terms; where no term is contractually agreed, the Bundesbank presumes a maximum collection period of six months from the date of export. There is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into euro and no ratio of export receipts that must be sold or surrendered to the central bank. Exporters may freely hold or disburse foreign currency, subject only to monthly AWV statistical reporting for cross-border receipts above EUR 12,500.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany and the competent foreign exchange administration authority under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance – Außenwirtschaftsverordnung)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for EEA-licensed and BaFin-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) with German or EU passporting rights; Low for offshore non-EEA digital banks. Legal constraints: an offshore EMI without a German licence or EU passport may not open a German payment account, issue IBANs, or provide SEPA clearing for the UG; relying on such an entity as the primary treasury account creates illegal deposit-taking risk and German VAT compliance deficiencies. EEA-passported EMIs can provide virtual IBANs and cross-border settlement, but the UG should retain at least one fully regulated German bank account as the backbone for statutory capital escrow, notary confirmations, tax authority deposits, and court or trade register filings. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are merchant-of-record payment platforms, not banking institutions, and must not replace a regulated bank treasury.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-resident managing director or beneficial owner without verifiable EU/EEA identification or German tax registration.
        
Root cause: Non-resident managing director or beneficial owner without verifiable EU/EEA identification or German tax registration.

        
Defense evidence: Certified copy of passport or EU identity card with German translation; notarised power of attorney if appointing a German signatory; proof of residential address dated within three months; bank reference letter from the home-country bank; curriculum vitae and business rationale; draft Handelsregister excerpt; confirmation of tax registration from the Federal Central Tax Office and local tax office (VAT and income tax).

      

      
      

        Shareholder or capital injection originating from a high-risk or offshore jurisdiction, or unexplained source of funds for the UG's capital contribution.
        
Root cause: Shareholder or capital injection originating from a high-risk or offshore jurisdiction, or unexplained source of funds for the UG's capital contribution.

        
Defense evidence: Full corporate registry extract of the offshore entity; certificate of incorporation and charter; register of directors and beneficial owners; audited financial statements or certified bank asset statement; legal opinion on source of funds and the commercial purpose of the equity injection; written statement that no third-party nominees are involved; transaction narrative referencing the share subscription resolution and German HRB number.

      

      
      

        Subscription revenue generated exclusively through a merchant-of-record platform (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) without direct customer invoicing or underlying platform evidence.
        
Root cause: Subscription revenue generated exclusively through a merchant-of-record platform (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) without direct customer invoicing or underlying platform evidence.

        
Defense evidence: Execution copy of the platform merchant agreement; latest 12-month payout reports and net settlement statements; sample customer invoices and receipts; breakdown of revenue by country; VAT registration certificates for Germany and relevant EU jurisdictions; platform KYC/KYB documentation; proof that the platform holds money as an agent and not as a credit institution.

      

      
      

        The UG has no physical German office and uses only a virtual or registered service address, triggering substance concerns.
        
Root cause: The UG has no physical German office and uses only a virtual or registered service address, triggering substance concerns.

        
Defense evidence: Lease contract or mailbox-service agreement with the German provider; photograph and use-rights confirmation for meeting rooms; signed representation letter from the local service offering address, mail forwarding and legal service; engagement letter with German tax advisor and lawyer; payroll or contractor agreement proving local management substance and availability during normal business hours.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
15% corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate tax (aggregate 15.825%) plus trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at municipal rates averaging approximately 14%, producing a combined standard burden of about 29.8%

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
26.375% (25% capital gains tax on dividends plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge); reduced to 15% when the recipient is a Corporation in the meaning of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and holds at least 10% of the German UG, subject to compliance with anti-abuse rules and a valid tax residence certificate

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: For the UG micro-SaaS business, transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is mandatory for cross-border transactions with related parties exceeding EUR 6,000,000 annually for goods and services; for lower volumes, the taxpayer must still provide contemporaneous records on request and document the rationale for deviations from the arm's length principle under Section 90 AO; the Federal Ministry of Finance also applies a de minimis administrative threshold of EUR 5,000,000 for intercompany service charges; when the total intra-group transaction volume is below the thresholds, only a simplified cost/evidence file is required

      
Economic substance scope: Germany applies the economic substance doctrine through the German fiscal unity rules, Section 50d(3) EStG anti-treaty-shopping provisions, and the Act on the Currency of International Business Transactions; a UG whose sole activity is holding a micro-SaaS trademark and receiving royalties without staff or operational presence will generally be denied DTA benefits and may be reclassified as a negligible business in Germany, triggering forced liquidation or the denial of foreign income exemption; substantive board meetings, local software development activity, pricing decisions, and bank accounts in Germany are mandatory to maintain residence status under the OECD Model and to resist profit allocation abroad

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Incorrect Declarations
        
Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from six months to ten years under Section 370 AO for gross tax evasion; additional fine equal to the evaded tax plus a surcharge of up to 50% of the evaded amount; denial of loss carryforwards and binding penalty assessments; the tax office will additionally impose incidental tax surcharges under Section 235 AO for VAT and under Section 240 AO for income tax

        
Director personal liability: Managing directors of a UG are personally liable for withheld corporation tax, trade tax, and solidarity surcharge if they have failed to file accurate declarations; under Section 69 AO, up to 100% of the unpaid tax may be recovered from the directors; each director is also exposed to arrest for tax flight, prohibition from managing corporations in Germany, and criminal conviction leading to the forfeiture of corporate assets

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: German tax authorities disregard the UG and allocate the total business income to the foreign substantive entity, disallow all charges and credits, and impose withholding tax on deemed payouts; the UG may be struck off the commercial register under Section 144 FMStBG for inactivity; the Federal Central Tax Office will refuse a German residence certificate and the entity loses access to more than 90% of treaty benefits; administrative fines of up to EUR 100,000 may be combined with criminal sanctions for false declarations regarding real management

        
Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable as apparent commercial owners for all unpaid tax, including foreign corporate taxes, and must compensate creditors for damages caused by operating without prudent substance; the practice of stripping a UG of all assets triggers criminal breach of duty under Section 266 StGB for the manager; directors can be ordered by the tax court to deliver personal passport notices and may face a travel ban under Section 132 AO

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering Violations within Payment Processing
        
Statutory sanction: Fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG) up to EUR 5 million or 10% of total annual turnover for serious breaches; suspension of the UG's permission to hold and manage seized funds; immediate closure of payment accounts and involvement of the FIU for suspicious activity reports; repeated violations can result in exclusion from trade licenses and the appointment of a special inspector under Section 45 GwG

        
Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability under Section 54 GwG for failing to establish an adequate AML compliance system, even for micro-SaaS structures; personal fines up to EUR 100,000 are standard; where the AML breach is linked to tax fraud or terrorist financing, imprisonment up to twelve years becomes possible; the corporate veil is pierced under Sections 6 and 15 GwG so that directors' private assets and foreign bank accounts can be seized
