Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No escrow account is statutorily mandated. Non-cash contributions require an auditor's confirmation of realizable value per § 139. For the heparin API manufacturing license file, paid-up capital of at least EUR 10,000 is commercially recommended to demonstrate financial substance.

Equipment: technical inspection of cleanrooms, autoclaves, and pressure vessels by the Technical Regulatory Authority (TTJA) where statutory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), an agency of the Ministry of Finance; primary electronic portal is e-MTA/e-Toll (https://e-mta.eemta.ee) and the e-Business Register (https://ettevotjaportaal.rik.ee); official web presence is www.emta.ee

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity must first obtain an Estonian registry code from the e-Business Register if it establishes a branch, permanent establishment, or local presence; this code functions as the corporate Estonian TIN. In the absence of a PE, the foreign entity applies to the MTA directly through e-MTA for a non-resident tax number, which also serves as its tax account identifier. For VAT purposes, the non-resident files the KMR registration application before the first taxable supply, appointing a tax representative where the MTA so requires, and the MTA issues an Estonian VAT identification number (format EE + 9 digits) and activates the electronic tax account. All steps may be digitally signed using an e-Residency digital ID; processing is usually completed within 3 to 5 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estionian law has no US-style check-the-box election; the distributed-profit corporation tax regime applies mandatorily to Osaühing entities. Available elections include voluntary VAT registration below the EUR 40,000 annual turnover threshold, election of the reduced 14% dividend withholding rate for regular profit distributions, and the participation exemption for qualifying foreign subsidiary dividends. Hybrid mismatch treatment is non-elective, applying mandatory ATAD anti-hybrid and CFC rules. No tax-transparency election exists for limited companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 taxable turnover per calendar year for domestically established traders; non-established foreign suppliers must register where taxable supplies are made in Estonia, except that intra-EU distance sales into Estonia benefit from the EUR 10,000 EU-wide OSS threshold and imports require import VAT accounting. Registration becomes mandatory upon reaching or anticipating the threshold, with voluntary registration permitted below it.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of pharmaceutical goods, including heparin sodium crude fractionation output, is zero-rated where the goods are physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union under a customs export procedure within three months of supply, or where deemed exported under an approved fiscal warehousing arrangement. Zero-rating also applies to processing services directly performed on export-bound goods and to certain consignment stock transfers; the exporter must maintain documentary evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. Intra-EU sales qualify for 0% only where the customer provides a valid VAT identification number and reliable transit evidence exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX document or NCTS export transit with MRN) evidencing release for export from an EU customs office
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR/combined transport document proving physical exit from the EU within three months of supply
  • Commercial invoice issued in the exporter's name stating the 0% export VAT basis, HS code, and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 / Letter of Credit documentation evidencing settlement of export proceeds to the Estonian bank account of the exporting entity
  • Customs warehouse release or exit summary record from the EU office of exit, where applicable
  • Certificate of origin or Eur.1/invoice declaration if preferential tariff treatment is claimed
  • Proof of the purchaser's non-EU tax status, such as a third-country registration certificate or sworn exporter declaration, for service and toll-processing elements

Structured e-invoicing is mandatory for domestic B2B supplies under the Estonian Accounting Act from 1 January 2022, using the EN 16931 standard or equivalent XML formats; receipt obligations were phased in from 1 July 2019. There is no centralized pre-clearance or digital stamping authority, but taxable persons must transmit VAT returns and related data electronically through e-MTA. Retail cash sales are subject to near-real-time online fiscalization data transmission to the MTA through certified cash-register software. B2B export invoices require no fiscal pre-approval and must be retained in their original structured electronic format for audit.

MTA scrutinizes export zero-rating claims strictly; failure to produce valid customs MRN and transport evidence triggers reassessment of 22% VAT on the invoice value, plus tax arrears interest of 0.06% per day and an additional penalty of up to 30% of the assessed VAT. Intentional use of forged export documents or fictitious chain transactions is prosecuted as VAT fraud under the Estonian Penal Code, carrying fines and imprisonment of up to five years and director disqualification, with potential OLAF and EU anti-fraud proceedings. Export VAT refunds already released are retrospectively clawed back where proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, where documentation is found unreliable, or where the transaction forms part of a chain-fraud scheme, together with interest and statutory penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro as its legal tender and participates in the euro area and the European Union single market. Current-account payments, trade settlements, dividend payments, interest payments, service fees, and ordinary profit repatriation are generally freely convertible and transferable, subject to bank compliance, sanctions screening, tax settlement, beneficial-owner verification, and documentary substantiation. There is no general capital-account approval requirement for foreign ownership, inbound equity, intercompany loans, or outbound distributions. An Estonian OÜ may distribute dividends only from distributable equity in accordance with the Estonian Commercial Code and after applicable corporate income tax is accounted for. Banks may delay or reject transfers involving incomplete corporate records, unexplained source of funds, high-risk jurisdictions, sanctioned parties, unusual pharmaceutical supply chains, cash-intensive activity, or transactions inconsistent with the stated business profile.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Estonia has no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general requirement to convert foreign-currency export receipts into euros within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's commercial discretion. The company must preserve commercial invoices, customs or transport evidence where applicable, contracts, payment records, and counterparty information sufficient for bank AML review, accounting, VAT, customs, and tax purposes. Any currency-conversion, payment-service, or reporting requirement imposed by the servicing bank or payment institution remains contractually and operationally applicable.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia); Estonia has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls. Banking and payment-service supervision is principally conducted by the Finantsinspektsioon (Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority), with the Financial Intelligence Unit responsible for relevant anti-money-laundering supervision and enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR payments and collection; low to medium for a principal treasury relationship, regulated pharmaceutical trade, or complex multi-currency flows. A payment institution or electronic-money institution licensed in Estonia or another EEA state may provide services in Estonia through applicable authorization or passporting arrangements, subject to its license scope and local notification requirements. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot rely solely on offshore incorporation to provide regulated payment or e-money services to Estonian customers; it may require Estonian or EEA authorization, an appropriately regulated partner, or another legally valid market-access basis. Fintech accounts do not replace a bank account for capital contribution evidence, lender requirements, commercial LC issuance, controlled pharmaceutical settlements, or transactions requiring correspondent-bank support. The company should confirm safeguarding, deposit-protection status, virtual-IBAN limitations, settlement cut-offs, sanctions policy, account termination rights, and access to SWIFT or equivalent correspondent rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a bank account held in the investor's name to an Estonian OÜ account or a bank-designated capital-contribution account. Use a payment reference identifying the OÜ, contribution type, shareholder or lender, and relevant corporate resolution. Estonia does not generally use a universal central-bank foreign-exchange purpose-code regime for ordinary corporate wires; the servicing bank may nevertheless require an internal payment purpose, remittance information, LEI, invoice, loan agreement, or capital-contribution documentation. Before remittance, provide the bank with the ownership chart, beneficial-owner evidence, shareholder or board resolution, capitalization table, source-of-funds evidence, investor bank details, loan or subscription agreement, and expected post-funding use. For debt funding, document arm's-length terms, maturity, interest, repayment mechanics, related-party status, and transfer-pricing treatment. Reconcile the inbound payment to the accounting ledger and retain SWIFT confirmation, bank statements, corporate approvals, and evidence of ultimate investor ownership. Do not route capital through an unrelated third party, personal account, cash channel, or unexplained fintech wallet.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the applicable SEPA scheme; use correspondent-bank SWIFT payments for non-SEPA currencies or destinations, with ISO 20022 pacs.008 increasingly replacing legacy MT103 workflows. For documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit issued or confirmed by a bank acceptable to the beneficiary, preferably subject to UCP 600, with document presentation, discrepancy, sanctions, insurance, inspection, transport, and partial-shipment terms defined before issuance. Documentary collections may use URC 522 where commercially suitable. Each payment should match the underlying contract, purchase order, invoice, shipment evidence, customs documentation, beneficiary identity, and end-use review. For pharmaceutical goods, require batch, quality, origin, regulatory, and transport-temperature documentation where relevant. Apply dual approval, sanctions screening, segregation of duties, three-way matching, payment-limit controls, beneficiary-change callback verification, and daily bank reconciliation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for routine EUR payments and collection; low to medium for a principal treasury relationship, regulated pharmaceutical trade, or complex multi-currency flows. A payment institution or electronic-money institution licensed in Estonia or another EEA state may provide services in Estonia through applicable authorization or passporting arrangements, subject to its license scope and local notification requirements. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot rely solely on offshore incorporation to provide regulated payment or e-money services to Estonian customers; it may require Estonian or EEA authorization, an appropriately regulated partner, or another legally valid market-access basis. Fintech accounts do not replace a bank account for capital contribution evidence, lender requirements, commercial LC issuance, controlled pharmaceutical settlements, or transactions requiring correspondent-bank support. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on undistributed retained profits; 20/80 (25% effective on grossed-up distribution) on distributed profits; reduced 14/86 (16.28% effective) for qualifying regular dividend distributions within the permitted three-year average ceiling.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

20/80 (25% effective tax on the grossed-up amount) on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders; reduced statutory rate of 14/86 (16.28% effective) applies to qualifying regular distributions to individuals; 0% applies to qualifying corporate dividend payments under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive participation exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeding EUR 250,000 triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File preparation. A Master File is required when the Estonian group member belongs to a multinational group whose ultimate parent is Estonian tax resident and the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 750 million. Country-by-Country Reporting obligations arise at the EUR 750 million group-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Estonia, as an OECD and EU member, does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, domestic anti-avoidance law and the EU ATAD GAAR require real commercial substance; Paragraph 83(5) of the Income Tax Act empowers the MTA to disregard artificial arrangements or shell structures that lack operational reality, to recharacterize transactions, and to tax distributable profits at the shareholder level. A e-resident OÜ maintaining no Estonian office, staff, or effective management may be challenged for lack of substance and denial of treaty benefits under beneficial-ownership rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Disguised Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: The MTA recalculates the distribution at 20/80 corporate income tax, adds daily late-payment interest at 0.06%, and imposes a fine of up to 20% of the under-declared tax. Criminal prosecution under Penal Code Section 330 carries imprisonment of up to 5 years for intentional tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who approve or sign false Annex 7 declarations are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt under the Tax Administration Act Section 63; restrictions on travel, disqualification from board positions, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment may follow.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The MTA may disregard the legal form under Income Tax Act Section 83(5), treat the OÜ as a transparent vehicle, and assess corporate income tax at 20/80 on all deemed distributions and recharacterized profits. Registry fines up to EUR 5,000 apply for false address or management data; the company can be struck off under the Commercial Code.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and de facto beneficial owners are personally liable for the full tax deficiency, and such liability is enforceable cross-border under EU mutual assistance; travel bans and bans on acting as a director are available under the Estonian Code of Enforcement.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act exposes a legal person to fines up to EUR 1.6 million or 5% of annual turnover; Penal Code Sections 394 and 395 impose imprisonment from 1 to 8 years, comprehensive confiscation of criminal proceeds, and freezing of bank accounts and LC instruments.

Director personal liability: AML officers and board members face individual criminal prosecution for failing to file Suspicious Transaction Reports or knowingly processing sanctions-violating payments; personal asset seizure, travel prohibitions, and management disqualification orders are routinely issued.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The MTA adjusts the taxable income upward to the arm's-length price, treats the adjusted amount as a deemed distribution subject to 20/80 tax, adds 0.06% daily interest, and imposes a gross-negligence penalty of up to 50% of the additional tax. Repeat offenders are referred for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or approve the non-arm's-length transfer pricing are personally liable for the adjusted corporate tax deficiency; cross-border tax debt collection and director debarment under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive and NJA proceedings apply.
