Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); digital filing and registration via the e-MTA portal (e-maksuamet, https://emta.ee)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ) first obtains its Estonian registration code (registrikood, 8-digit legal entity TIN) through the e-Business Register (Äriregistri infosüsteem), either by notarised incorporation or e-Residency digital signature. This registry code is the statutory corporate tax identification number. If the OÜ makes taxable supplies in Estonia or opts to be VAT-registered, the entity files a VAT application form (KPR) through e-MTA, after which the MTA issues an EE-prefixed VAT identification number (VAT number) linked to the registry code. No physical presence or local bank account is required solely for tax registration; non-resident owners may appoint a local contact person or tax representative where the MTA requires service of process.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Osaühing (OÜ) first obtains its Estonian registration code (registrikood, 8-digit legal entity TIN) through the e-Business Register (Äriregistri infosüsteem), either by notarised incorporation or e-Residency digital signature. This registry code is the statutory corporate tax identification number. If the OÜ makes taxable supplies in Estonia or opts to be VAT-registered, the entity files a VAT application form (KPR) through e-MTA, after which the MTA issues an EE-prefixed VAT identification number (VAT number) linked to the registry code. No physical presence or local bank account is required solely for tax registration; non-resident owners may appoint a local contact person or tax representative where the MTA requires service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no check-the-box entity classification regime. Statutory elections available to an OÜ include: (i) election to apply the reduced 14/86 profit-distribution tax rate for periodic or regular distributions meeting statutory conditions (rather than the default 20/80 rate); (ii) election to form a corporate income tax group with an 80%-or-more-owned qualifying subsidiary, allowing profit distributions between group members to be tax-neutral; (iii) election for VAT group registration with qualifying related entities; and (iv) Optional One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registration for EU-wide cross-border distance sales of goods, including non-EU Union OSS schemes for imports. Additionally, a newly formed OÜ may align its tax accounting period with its financial year (up to 12 months) via the e-MTA; no hybrid-mismatch elective regime exists under domestic law beyond the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) aligned limitations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€40,000 annual taxable turnover in Estonia (calendar year). For cross-border B2C distance sales of goods by a non-EU or EU supplier, the EU-wide €10,000 threshold applies unless the supplier elects the One-Stop-Shop; the €40,000 domestic threshold does not apply to non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Estonia, who can become liable to register immediately upon any taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) applies to (a) the supply of goods dispatched or transported by the supplier or on its behalf to a destination outside the European Union (exports) if the exporter retains proof of export; (b) certain services directly related to exported goods (e.g., freight, handling) where the recipient is a non-EU person; (c) supplies of goods to another EU member state (intra-community supplies) where the acquirer's valid EU VAT ID is obtained and the goods arrive in the other member state; and (d) supplies to diplomatic missions, EU institutions, and certain international organisations. For hard enamel lapel pins, the standard commercial scenario is zero-rated export of finished goods sold through Etsy or Stripe to non-EU consumers, with the goods physically leaving Estonia within the statutory time limit. The right to zero-rate is conditioned on the goods being dispatched/transported out of the EU no later than the time of supply plus an acceptable documentation window (usually within three months); for EU intra-community supplies a valid buyer VAT registration number must be shown on the invoice and recapitulative statement (SALES list).

  • Customs Export Declaration (EXS) or electronic export declaration via the Estonian customs system, with MRN (Movement Reference Number) printed on the commercial invoice or packing list
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), or CMR / courier consignment note evidencing physical departure from Estonia and arrival in the non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and proforma invoice showing buyer, seller, Incoterms, HS code (e.g., 7117 for imitation jewellery / 8306 for badges), quantity, and unit value
  • Proof of payment/remittance certificate (e.g., Stripe payout report, PayPal payment confirmation, SWIFT MT103, or bank inward remittance advice) evidencing the foreign buyer's payment and settlement of the export receivable
  • Freight forwarder or logistics warehouse export confirmation and delivery receipt from the destination country (where available) to substantiate external crossing

Estonia imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital signature mandate for B2B/B2C VAT invoices. However, all VAT returns (KMD) and periodic sales/list declarations (KMDINF) must be filed electronically via e-MTA or machine-to-machine APIs. E-invoicing is the statutory default in B2G (public sector) supply, using the European standard EN 16931 (Peppol BIS 3.0) or the Estonian e-arve format. All invoices must contain VAT identification numbers, issued date, supply date, description, quantity, and rate; there is no requirement for a fiscal printer, SAF-T filing, or centralised clearance prior to invoice issuance.

Estonia does not impose exchange-control repatriation rules; the 180-day deadline reflects the payment-receipt benchmark embedded in the compliance dossier. Where zero-rating is claimed without valid export proof, the MTA retrospectively assesses VAT at 22% on the supply, with default interest (currently 0.06% per day on the outstanding amount) and a penalty surcharge of up to 10% of the assessed tax for non-declared or falsely declared amounts. Deliberate fraudulent zero-rating, including issuing invoices for fictitious exports of enamel pins, triggers criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code (Karistusseadustik) for tax fraud, with fines and, for significant amounts, imprisonment; input VAT credit claimed on such supplies is clawed back with interest. The general statute of limitations for tax assessment is three years, extended to seven years where the taxpayer has failed to declare tax or acted fraudulently. The MTA has dedicated audit algorithms cross-matching customs export declarations, e-MTA KMD declarations, payment traits from e-commerce platforms (Etsy/Stripe), and intra-EU VIES data; missing or internally inconsistent export documentation is a primary audit trigger.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia uses the euro. There are no general restrictions on converting euros into foreign currencies, holding foreign-currency accounts, making or receiving cross-border payments, or transferring capital, subject to sanctions, AML/CFT controls, tax rules, bank due diligence, and applicable EU law. Current-account receipts, dividends, and other lawful distributions are not subject to general exchange approval. Estonia taxes corporate profits principally when distributed or otherwise treated as taxable distributions; retained earnings are generally not taxed at entity level merely because they are earned. A distribution remains subject to the applicable Estonian corporate income-tax rules, proper corporate authorization, distributable-profit and solvency requirements, accounting records, and any withholding or reporting rules applicable to the recipient and payment. Banks may require evidence of the source and purpose of funds, beneficial ownership, underlying transactions, and tax treatment. EU sanctions and restrictive measures may prohibit or restrict particular counterparties, jurisdictions, goods, or payment routes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies to an Estonian OÜ’s ordinary export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be retained in euros or other currencies and held domestically or abroad, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, AML/CFT, contractual, and bank requirements. The company should retain invoices, customs/export evidence where applicable, transport records, and bank payment references to substantiate the transaction.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) is Estonia’s national central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. Eesti Pank does not operate a general foreign-exchange licensing or capital-control regime for ordinary corporate transactions. Estonia’s Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu Andmebüroo, RAB) supervises relevant anti-money-laundering obligations, while the Estonian Financial Supervision and Resolution Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) supervises regulated financial institutions within its remit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for routine marketplace and payment processing, subject to provider underwriting, supported-country rules, transaction limits, and sanctions screening. Stripe may provide payment-processing services where the company and business activity meet its current eligibility requirements; Etsy Payments availability and payout terms depend on the seller’s location and platform rules. An electronic-money institution or payment institution account is not a bank deposit and generally does not have the same deposit-guarantee protection as an eligible bank deposit. Use a regulated provider authorized in the relevant jurisdiction, confirm safeguarding and insolvency treatment, and do not rely on an offshore EMI as the sole operating or reserve account. Maintain an Estonian or other EEA bank account for tax, payroll, supplier, and contingency payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Receive shareholder capital contributions or documented shareholder loans into the OÜ’s own account from an account held in the contributor’s name, preferably through SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within the SEPA area or SWIFT for other currencies and corridors. Identify the payment as a share-capital contribution, additional equity contribution, or shareholder loan consistently with the governing resolutions and supporting documents; Estonia does not impose a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code requirement for ordinary corporate transfers. Provide the bank with the subscription or contribution decision, loan agreement if applicable, contributor identity and beneficial-ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, and commercial-register or accounting evidence requested by the bank. Avoid unexplained third-party remitters, cash funding, split transfers intended to evade controls, and inconsistent payment references. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

20% on gross distributed profits (20/80 of net dividend) for ordinary distributions; 14% (14/86) for regular distributions at the reduced rate; 0% on undistributed retained earnings

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Estonia does not impose a separate statutory withholding tax on dividends at the shareholder level. The distribution tax is levied at the company level at 20/80 for ordinary dividends and 14/86 for regular dividends to residents and non-residents alike.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Estonian legal acts do not prescribe a specific financial threshold for preparing the local file. However, in line with OECD guidance, a local file must be prepared if the total value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds EUR 50,000 for goods and EUR 25,000 for services within a fiscal year. A master file is required if the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Entities with lower transaction values must still maintain contemporaneous TP evidence in a standard reasonable format if the tax authority requests it.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not maintain a standalone list of economic substance regulations applicable to all resident companies. However, the general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and the ATAD-based controlled-foreign-company (CFC) rules require that a company have economic activities such as premises, employees, and business functions to justify its profit allocations and corporate residence. For the enamel lapel pin manufacturing and e-commerce trade, the entity must demonstrate that strategic decision-making, procurement, production management, and contract execution occur in Estonia or that the entity has substance where business activity occurs; otherwise, the Estonian Tax and Customs Board may reattribute profits to the management location or deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Estonian Penal Code § 397; the company is subject to a fine of up to EUR 800,000; the amount of evaded tax and interest are collected in addition to a penalty up to 30% of the underdeclared tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly participate in evasion are personally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax and interest. In aggravated cases, imprisonment up to 5 years and a travel ban may be imposed; the corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a mere instrument and the director’s personal assets can be subject to attachment.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Estonian Tax and Customs Board disregards the arrangement under the GAAR, recharacterizes the company’s income as personally attributable to the ultimate owners, and levies distribution tax at 20/80 on the deemed distribution. A fine of up to 5% of the annual turnover may be imposed for failure to submit substance documentation, and the company may be struck off the commercial register.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders are jointly and severally liable for the recharacterized tax, interest, and penalties. If the sham structure is used for money laundering or tax fraud, directors face criminal liability with imprisonment up to 7 years, a travel ban, and confiscation of personal assets.
AML / e-Residency Abuse

Statutory sanction: Estonian AML regime (Rahapesu and terrorismi rahastamise tõkestamise seadus) imposes fines up to EUR 1.2 million on legal persons; the payment institution may terminate the merchant account and report to the Financial Intelligence Unit. The Trade Register may initiate compulsory deletion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate the abuse are criminally liable and face imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering, in addition to civil liability for the proceeds, a travel ban, and a prohibition on serving as director in any Estonian company for up to 3 years.
Profit Repatriation Through Loans

Statutory sanction: The Tax and Customs Board recharacterizes the loan and the forgone interest as a distribution dividend, levying 20/80 tax on the outstanding balance and deemed interest. A penalty of 5% of the recharacterized amount and daily interest of 0.06% are imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the sham loan are personally liable for the distribution tax and penalty. In severe cases, they may be subject to criminal proceedings for evasion of corporate income tax and to a travel ban.
