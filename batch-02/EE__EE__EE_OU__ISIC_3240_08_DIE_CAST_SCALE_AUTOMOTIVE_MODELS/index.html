Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA); principal electronic portal: e-MTA (e-mta.emta.ee). Corporate legal-entity registration is administered through the Estonian Business Register maintained by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems.

E-government filing portal

An Estonian OÜ receives a registry code upon incorporation through the Estonian Business Register. The registry code is the principal corporate identifier and is used for tax administration. The company obtains access to e-MTA through an authorized management-board member, e-Residency digital identity, Estonian bank authentication, or an authorized representative. VAT registration is requested electronically in e-MTA or through the MTA service channel by submitting the legal name, registry code, business address, contact details, activity description, expected taxable supplies, commencement date, and supporting evidence of actual or intended business activity. The MTA may request contracts, invoices, premises information, accounting arrangements, and management or supply-chain details. The VAT number is generally formatted as EE followed by nine digits. A foreign entity without an Estonian registered office or registry code may obtain an Estonian non-resident tax identification record through the MTA where Estonian tax obligations arise; VAT registration is required where statutory Estonian VAT registration conditions are met. VAT registration is generally effective from the date specified in the MTA decision and does not replace customs, payroll, or statistical registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Estonia has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an OÜ. An Estonian resident company is generally subject to corporate income tax when profits are distributed or treated as distributed, rather than when profits are earned and retained. Relevant taxable distributions include dividends, profit distributions, hidden profit distributions, certain non-business expenses, fringe benefits, gifts, donations, and certain payments to low-tax jurisdictions. A company cannot elect ordinary flow-through treatment for retained or distributed profits. Tax residency follows incorporation and statutory residence rules, subject to treaty allocation rules for dual-residence situations. Estonian anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement applicable ATAD requirements and may deny deductions or impose income inclusion in specified hybrid mismatch circumstances; they are statutory rules, not elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

24%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 of taxable supply generated in Estonia during a calendar year. The threshold applies to the statutory Estonian taxable turnover calculation and does not automatically cover all cross-border transactions. Voluntary VAT registration may be available before the threshold is reached, subject to MTA review.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched or transported from Estonia to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for 0% VAT where the supplier can substantiate export from the EU and satisfy the Estonian Value Added Tax Act requirements. The supplier must retain customs export evidence linking the goods, exporter, buyer, and transport. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for 0% VAT where the customer has a valid VAT identification number in another EU Member State, the goods are transported from Estonia to that Member State, and the supplier retains transport and reporting evidence. Services are governed by place-of-supply rules: qualifying B2B services supplied to a business established outside Estonia may be outside the scope of Estonian VAT or subject to reverse charge, while specific services connected with goods, immovable property, transport, events, or consumers may follow special rules. Offshore status alone does not establish zero-rating. For die-cast scale models, export treatment depends on the physical movement of the goods and the customer and destination evidence.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the Estonian supplier, customer, goods description, quantities, values, and delivery terms
  • Customs export declaration and electronic export confirmation, including the customs movement reference and exit results
  • Packing list and transport document, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier export record
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and proof of payment or receivable settlement
  • Evidence linking the exported goods to the VAT invoice and customs declaration
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, VIES validation evidence, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List reporting
  • For services: contract, customer establishment evidence, service deliverables, place-of-supply analysis, and reverse-charge wording where applicable

Estonia does not generally impose universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory continuous transaction controls for ordinary domestic B2B or B2C transactions. VAT invoices may be issued electronically if authenticity, integrity, and legibility are maintained. Public-sector e-invoicing requirements apply to relevant government and public-sector transactions, and structured e-invoices may be required under applicable procurement and accounting rules. VAT returns and related reports are submitted electronically through e-MTA, generally by the twentieth day of the month following the taxable period. There is no general requirement for digital tax stamping or prior MTA approval of each invoice.

Estonia has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must retain reliable evidence that the goods left the EU or that the service satisfied the applicable place-of-supply rule. If 0% VAT is unsupported or incorrectly claimed, the MTA may reclassify the supply as taxable at the applicable Estonian VAT rate, assess the underpaid VAT, charge statutory tax interest, and impose a tax offence or administrative penalty where legally available. Deliberate false documentation, concealment, or fraudulent VAT recovery may trigger criminal liability under Estonian law. Input VAT refunds and export-related VAT positions may be denied, reduced, or clawed back, and the taxpayer may be subject to audit, corrective VAT returns, reporting sanctions, and recovery proceedings. Penalty and interest exposure depends on the conduct, amount, duration, and applicable procedural provisions in force at the assessment date.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Estonia maintains full capital account convertibility under EU acquis. Current account transactions, including profit remittances, are unrestricted with no convertibility hurdles for resident OÜ entities. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders faces no barriers beyond standard Estonian corporate dividend withholding tax (20% on gross dividends).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to Eesti Pank. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts without conversion. No statutory timelines, conversion ratios, or surrender obligations apply under Estonian or EU law.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22% of distributed profit, mechanically calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution from 1 January 2025. Retained and reinvested profits are not subject to Estonian corporate income tax at the time of earning.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian company, including to a non-resident shareholder. The Estonian company generally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the distributed profit. Legacy reduced-rate dividend arrangements and related 7% withholding rules may remain relevant only for historical distributions governed by transitional rules; they should not be assumed for distributions from profits taxed under the current regime.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

TSD and applicable annexes are due by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the taxable payment, distribution, benefit, or other taxable event occurred. Tax must generally be paid by the same deadline.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual report is generally due within six months after the end of the financial year and in all cases within nine months after the financial year-end, subject to the statutory filing rules applicable to the entity. TSD remains due monthly by the 10th day of the following month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No universal transaction-value threshold applies to every Estonian company. All controlled transactions must satisfy the arm's-length principle. Detailed local-file and master-file obligations principally apply to specified large entities, regulated entities, listed companies, and qualifying cross-border groups under the Estonian Income Tax Act and related regulations. Large-entity tests include statutory size criteria such as turnover, balance-sheet total, and average employee count; the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold is relevant to country-by-country reporting rather than a universal local-file threshold. The OÜ should maintain contemporaneous documentation for material related-party purchases, sales, financing, royalties, management services, and toll-manufacturing arrangements even where no formal threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: Estonia does not operate a single standalone domestic Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the regimes of certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under the general anti-avoidance rule, beneficial-ownership analysis, transfer-pricing rules, EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive measures, anti-hybrid rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest-limitation rules, treaty principal-purpose testing, and the business-purpose requirement for deductible or non-distributive expenditure. The OÜ should maintain real decision-making, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure where commercially required, reliable books, contractual control, inventory records, and evidence of actual manufacturing, printing, sales, and distribution activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment interest, administrative penalties, and criminal prosecution where intentional evasion causes substantial or large-scale damage. Criminal sanctions may include a monetary punishment for the legal person and imprisonment or a monetary punishment for responsible individuals, subject to the Penal Code thresholds and court determination.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for tax damage caused by unlawful or negligent conduct, criminal liability for intentional evasion, disqualification from management, enforcement against personal assets in applicable statutory circumstances, and travel restrictions or detention only where ordered under criminal-procedure rules. Automatic veil piercing does not apply merely because the company is insolvent.
Unlawful Profit Distribution and Shareholder Extraction

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as a taxable distribution or non-business expenditure, corporate income tax at the applicable rate, interest, penalties, repayment or restitution claims, and possible insolvency-related clawback.

Director personal liability: Management-board members may be jointly and severally liable for unlawful distributions and damage to the company, creditors, or shareholders. Personal criminal liability may arise where the conduct constitutes breach of management duties, insolvency abuse, fraud, or tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length income or expense, additional corporate income tax where the adjustment constitutes a distribution or non-business expenditure, interest, penalties, and expanded audit scope.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for inadequate governance, unsupported related-party payments, or failure to preserve records. Criminal liability requires additional elements such as intent and statutory criminal damage thresholds.
VAT and Customs Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT and customs reassessment, import duty, interest, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture in applicable cases, and criminal prosecution for intentional or aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Responsible managers may be liable for deliberate false declarations, serious accounting failures, or participation in fraud. Customs and tax authorities may pursue individual liability where statutory conditions are satisfied.
AML, Sanctions, and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, reporting obligations, regulatory fines, account restrictions, confiscation, and criminal sanctions under Estonian AML and sanctions legislation. Penalties depend on the breach, intent, value, and applicable sanctions regime.

Director personal liability: Directors and persons responsible for compliance may face administrative or criminal liability, personal fines, management restrictions, asset seizure, and imprisonment for intentional money laundering, sanctions evasion, or related offences.
Accounting and Annual-Report Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Orders to correct or file, fines, tax assessments based on indirect evidence, enforcement measures, and potential dissolution or deletion proceedings for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Management-board members may be personally liable for damage caused by failure to keep accounts, prepare financial statements, preserve source documents, or comply with filing duties.
Shell Company and Substance Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or directive benefits, recharacterization of payments, transfer-pricing adjustments, corporate income tax and withholding assessments, beneficial-ownership challenges, and possible anti-avoidance proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for false corporate records and criminal liability if the structure is used for fraud, tax evasion, money laundering, or creditor prejudice. E-Residency does not provide tax residence, immunity, or exemption from director duties.
Employment, Payroll, and Director-Fee Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Reclassification, income tax and social tax assessments, unemployment insurance and funded-pension liabilities where applicable, interest, penalties, and potential employment-law sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for unpaid payroll obligations and deliberate misclassification, particularly where the arrangement defeats employee protections or conceals remuneration.
