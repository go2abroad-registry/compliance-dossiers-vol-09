Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Amtsgericht - Registergericht (Commercial Register Division, Handelsregister) of the local court district in which the UG has its registered office (Sitz); electronic filing via the notary's secure mailbox through the federal-state receiving system (xJustiz) and the Common Register Portal of the German states.

Governing statute

German Limited Liability Companies Act (GmbHG) - § 1 (legal form), § 2(1a) (notarized model protocol), § 5a (Unternehmergesellschaft (haftungsbeschränkt)), § 7-§ 9 (application and capital payment), § 10 (content and effect of registration), § 11 (legal existence upon registration), § 35 and § 35a (managing directors; business letter requirements), § 40 (shareholder list). Commerce: HGB §§ 8-10, 106-108 (Handelsregister procedure and filings); GewO § 14 (trade registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarially certified Anmeldung zum Handelsregister (application for entry in Commercial Register Section A - HRB) together with the integrated statutory model protocol (Musterprotokoll) pursuant to § 2(1a) GmbHG; accompanied by the capital payment confirmation and director declarations under § 8 GmbHG.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern – BZSt) responsible for EU VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.), wage tax accounts and international withholding; competent municipal Tax Office (Finanzamt) responsible for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), solidarity surcharge and VAT administration; official filing portal: ELSTER electronic tax declaration system (www.elster.de); foreign trade statistics reporting portal: Deutsche Bundesbank (www.bundesbank.de).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Notarize deed and registration of the UG in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) – the UG 'entsteht' only upon registration. Step 2: Within one month of commencing business, managing director files the electronic tax registration questionnaire ('Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung') with the Finanzamt via ELSTER, declaring anticipated turnover, corporate details and shareholder information (§ 138 AO). Step 3: The Finanzamt assigns the local tax number (Steuernummer) within 2–10 weeks; a UG typically receives provisional monthly VAT advance return obligations. Step 4: Apply to BZSt for the USt-IdNr. (EU VAT number) using Form USt 1 TN via ELSTER, mandatory where goods or services are supplied intra-EU or received from EU suppliers. Step 5: Register the business activity (Gewerbeanmeldung) with the municipal trade office, which triggers Gewerbesteuer assessment. Step 6: Register for Bundesbank AWV statutory reporting (service export statistics) upon first cross-border fee receipt exceeding the EUR 12,500 monthly threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Kapitalgesellschaft). The UG is a taxable corporation under KStG § 1(1) No. 2 with unlimited tax liability on worldwide income; subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15% plus solidarity surcharge of 5.5% of the assessed corporate tax (effective 0.825%) plus Gewerbesteuer (municipal trade tax, effective rate typically 14–17% depending on HebEsatz); combined effective income tax burden generally 30–33% in major cities; distributions to shareholders are subject to a separate 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge (or partial-income method for substantial shareholders); the UG has no fiscal transparency despite its reduced minimum capital of EUR 1.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box or S-corporation-style election; the UG is per se opaque and corporation-taxed. Available statutory elections: (i) corporate tax and VAT group (Organschaft) under § 14 KStG and § 2(2) No. 2 UStG, requiring a profit-and-loss transfer agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and a 50%-plus-one-vote holding; (ii) small-business exemption (§ 19 UStG) for turnover up to EUR 25,000 in 2025 (previously EUR 22,000) with a EUR 100,000 forward-looking cap, and the option under § 19(2) UStG to waive the exemption and be fully VAT-registered; (iii) EBITDA interest deduction cap election and interest carry-forward under § 4h EStG; (iv) mandatory ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules (§ 4k EStG, § 6a EStG) which deny deduction benefits for hybrid mismatch structures and permit no elective avoidance; (v) application for VAT advance-return extension (Dauerfristverlängerung, § 46 UStDV) with monthly prepayment of 1/11 of prior-year VAT.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany enforces full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions under EU fundamental freedoms and German AWV. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are legally unrestricted; however, such distributions are subject to German corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and a 26.375% withholding tax on dividends absent an applicable treaty (e.g., treaty with ultimate shareholder jurisdiction) and a valid Certificate of Residence. There is no capital controls regime, no exchange rate margin, and no central bank pre-approval for trade payments, loan servicing, or dividend distributions. The only operative compliance burden is statistical payment reporting and, in the specific case of payments exceeding EUR 12,500, the obligatory 'Zahlungsmeldung' to the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Germany to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency receipts into euro. Export receivables may be held, utilized, or repatriated freely. Mandatory reporting applies under AWV Section 59: resident enterprises must report inbound cross-border payments and payments received through foreign accounts to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days from the value date or receipt, provided the reported amount, cumulatively per transaction, equals or exceeds EUR 12,500. For trade goods, additional reporting of receivables and outstanding balances is required on a monthly basis under AWV Section 60, unless exempted by Bundesbank reporting order. No fixed conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) and Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action; cross-border payment reporting is governed by the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV) and supervised by the Deutsche Bundesbank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, with significant legal constraints. An offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licensed in the EEA may legally provide a German UG with multi-currency virtual IBANs and SWIFT/revolver services; however, it cannot replace a primary German bank account for statutory share capital escrow, corporate tax direct debit, or in-person KYC-intensive banking relationships. German anti-money-laundering law (GwG) requires a 'business relationship' with a properly identified beneficial owner; many EMIs rely on delegated KYC and may fail German banks' correspondent or bank-account-only standards. For safe treasury operations, an EEA-licensed EMI is viable only as a secondary operational rail for fast international disbursements, never as the principal clearing account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital contributions must be wired as a normal cross-border SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer, clearly identifying the contributing shareholder and using the purpose code 'BEZE' (Business expenses) or, for capital contribution, 'STAL' (Statutory payments) depending on the beneficiary bank's standalone requirement; the UG's articles of association and notarial deed should specify whether the contribution is made in cash. The German statutory minimum capital for a UG is EUR 1, but the bank will require a non-interest-bearing capital element if the UG elects to hold paid-in capital; the funds are frozen for distribution until registered in the commercial register (Handelsregister) and can only be used for operating expenses after initial registration. All inbound cross-border payments equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within five business days. To avoid false matching of funds, the remitter must include the UG's full legal name and, ideally, a reference to 'Stammeinlage' and the notary file number.Preferred clearing rails for trade settlements are SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA / overseas counterparties, and SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Direct Debit (SDD Core Business-to-Business / B2B direct debit) for EU-based retainer clients and suppliers. Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits) may be used only for physical goods or verifiable services requiring delivery security; for a PR and communications agency, SWIFT MT103 wires dominate. For high-frequency media subscription disbursements, an API-connected bank (DB/Commerzbank) can perform automated SEPA payments and FX conversion. Payment purpose codes for cross-border outflow should use ISO 20022 remittance information with the proper CF code (e.g., 'PRGS' for purchase of services) to support bank compliance and avoid holds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Corporate Income Tax (Körperschaftsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag), resulting in an effective national CIT rate of 15.825%; municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at typical collection rates of approximately 14%–17% brings the combined effective tax burden to roughly 29%–33% in major German cities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital gains tax (Kapitalertragsteuer) plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that withholding, producing an effective rate of 26.375% on distributions to non-resident shareholders, before treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Due on 31 May of the following calendar year; extended automatically to 31 July when submitted through a tax advisor. Discretionary extensions up to 31 December may be granted. For fiscal years ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is due within four months after the close of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 5,000,000 in cross-border related-party transactions triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File; the Master File must be prepared when global group consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 100,000,000. Non-documentation penalties under § 162(3) AO amount to 5% of the income adjustment, at least EUR 5,000, and additional surcharges may apply.

Economic substance scope: No standalone economic substance regulation statute akin to Cayman/BVI exists, but German anti-abuse doctrine under § 42 AO, CFC rules in §§ 7-13 AStG, treaty-shopping safeguards under the MLI, and the shell-company orientation in EU Anti-Tax Avoidance jurisprudence require real personnel, active management, and preventive business activity in the German UG. For an overseas PR communications agency, substantive physical and functional capacity must be present; a letterbox conduit operation will be disregarded for treaty relief and tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Steuerhinterziehung)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years under § 370 AO, or up to 10 years in aggravated cases; fines up to EUR 96,000 in addition to back taxes, interest at 1.8% per annum, and confiscation of illicit benefits.

Director personal liability: The managing director of the UG is personally liable for the full amount of evaded corporate taxes and penalties under §§ 34, 69 and 71 AO. A travel ban, arrest warrant, and asset seizure may apply; criminal conviction can lead to loss of legal capacity and prohibition from serving as a director.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: § 42 AO permits recasting and denial of the intended tax treatment, leading to substantive re-assessment, disallowance of treaty benefits, loss carryforward restrictions, and penalty surcharges of up to 20% on the adjusted profit where substance is declared missing.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sued for fraudulent misrepresentation of substance and face personal restitution for all unpaid taxes and interest; travel or exit bans may be ordered by tax courts; if the shell is used in cross-border abuse, constituent-director liability can trigger criminal sanctions.
AML / Money Laundering Facilitation

Statutory sanction: Under § 261 StGB and the German Money Laundering Act (GwG), fines can reach EUR 5,000,000 for the entity or up to 10% of total annual global turnover on a consolidated basis; criminal imprisonment up to 5 years for senior persons involved.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal conviction, asset freezing, confiscation of proceeds, and mandatory disqualification from financial and corporate appointment; the corporate veil is pierced for all underlying funds through regulatory recovery orders.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under § 162(3) and (4) AO, the tax authority may estimate income adversely, impose a minimum surcharge of EUR 5,000 and up to 5% of any adjusted profit, and add default interest at 1.8% per annum from the original due date.

Director personal liability: The managing director who fails to maintain TP documentation is liable as trustee for the resulting tax assessment under §§ 34, 69 AO; indemnity bonds may be demanded, and in audit cases the tax authority can seek a personal enforcement order against the assets of the director.
