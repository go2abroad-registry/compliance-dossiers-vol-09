Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Estonian Tax and Customs Board (Maksu- ja Tolliamet, MTA), operating the e-MTA e-services portal at https://emta.ee; commercial registration and automatic registry code issuance are handled by the e-Business Register (e-Äriregister) at https://ariregister.rik.ee.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the OÜ through the e-Business Register using an e-Resident digital ID; the registry extracts a 7-digit registry code that serves as the general taxpayer file number (Estonian TIN equivalent) for the OÜ. Step 2: Activate e-MTA access in the portal (ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID authentication). Step 3: For VAT, submit a Taxable Persons Register application electronically via e-MTA when the €40,000 calendar-year turnover threshold is exceeded or is expected to be exceeded, or immediately upon the first taxable supply for non-resident suppliers; a separate VAT identification number (KMKR) is then granted. Step 4: Foreign entities without a legal presence must, before filing, apply to the MTA central service for a non-resident TIN and, where relevant, VAT registration per Value Added Tax Act § 3(2), § 6 and § 17; no notarised certified copies are required for e-resident applicants as the procedure is fully electronic.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the OÜ is a resident legal person under Income Tax Act (TMS) § 9; the entity is materially taxed only on distributions (20/80 gross-up, reduced to 14/86 for regularly paid dividends), deemed fringe benefits, gifts and non-business expenses; undistributed and retained profits bear a 0% entity-level charge, and the OÜ is not subject to pass-through taxation; statutory residence is based on place of incorporation and registration.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-specific election exists in Estonian law; available statutory instruments are: (i) regular dividend election - an OÜ distributing dividends at least as large as the average of the three preceding years' distributions may apply the reduced 14/86 withholding rate on the qualifying portion under TMS § 50(1'); (ii) group taxation election for an Estonian parent and its 100% resident subsidiaries (consolidation of deductions and tax bases) under TMS § 50(6); (iii) cash accounting election under Value Added Tax Act § 37 for taxable persons whose turnover does not exceed €200,000 per year, allowing VAT declaration on a payments-received basis; (iv) advance pricing agreements and binding rulings issued by the MTA; (v) no elective hybrid-mismatch rule - automatic application of ATAD interest-barrier and anti-mismatch provisions. All elections are made in the periodic returns filed via e-MTA and cannot be backdated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

22%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 40,000 per calendar year for resident taxable persons (compulsory registration from the moment actual or expected turnover exceeds the threshold; voluntary registration also permitted); EUR 35,000 national distance-selling threshold for EU remote sellers of goods to Estonian private consumers, and EUR 10,000 EU-wide OSS mini-one-stop-shop threshold for cross-border B2C services and distance sales; non-resident suppliers making any taxable supply in Estonia have no general threshold and must register immediately, unless the OSS or IOSS scheme covers the transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to (i) goods physically dispatched or transported out of Estonia into another EU member state to a person identified as taxable for VAT (valid EU VAT number, supported by the VAT Information Exchange System and EC Sales List); (ii) goods exported by the seller or by a third party on its behalf outside the EU customs territory, evidenced by a customs export declaration (EX) accepted by Customs; (iii) services directly linked to the export or intra-Community transport of goods (freight, loading, ancillary transport), and services performed on movable physical property that is subsequently removed from Estonia or the EU within statutory time limits; (iv) intermediary services in connection with exports contracted in the name and for the account of the principal. Passive electronic-component inventory distribution (ISIC 4652) qualifies for zero-rating only where the title to the multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors is transferred to a foreign buyer and the prescribed civil-law and customs evidence is held by the supplier; a mere invoice to a foreign address without physical cross-border movement of goods does not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX/SAD, MRN number) with electronic acceptance record from the Estonian Customs Office
  • Bill of Lading, CMR or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch from Estonia and arrival at the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List and Purchase Order confirming the international transaction and buyer identity
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank Swift/SEPA statement evidencing transfer of export proceeds to the Estonian OÜ account
  • For intra-EU supplies: EC Sales List declaration, customer's valid VAT registration certificate, and delivery note with timed evidence
  • Customs proof-of-export database extract retained in the VAT accounting records for 7 years

Fully digital tax administration: VAT returns, annual turnover declarations, Intrastat and VIES listings and customs records are filed exclusively through e-MTA, with XML and API integration available for machine-to-machine submission; e-invoicing in the European standard (EN 16931) is mandatory for all B2G invoices to state and municipal institutions, administered through the national e-invoice receipt infrastructure operated by the State Information System (RIA), while B2B e-invoicing remains voluntary; Estonia has no real-time clearance engine, no mandatory digital stamping, and no transaction-level cryptographic signature regime (unlike the Italian fiscal-clearance model); the MTA instead relies on e-MTA filings, third-party bank and payment data, customs MRN records, and risk-based audits to verify indirect-tax liabilities.

The MTA conducts risk-based audits comparing zero-rated output, customs MRN records, transport evidence and incoming FX receipts; when export status is not substantiated, the MTA recharges 22% VAT on the transaction and imposes late-payment interest at 0.06% per day (approximately 23.4% per annum) per Tax Information Act (MKS) § 128; intentional misdeclaration triggers a tax-fraud surcharge of up to 100% of the additional tax, monetary fining up to EUR 1,300 in misdemeanour proceedings, and Penal Code § 389 criminal liability with imprisonment of up to three years for significant tax evasion; statutory re-assessment runs for three years (extended to seven years in fraudulent or intentionally concealing cases), allowing a full retrospective clawback of unjustified zero-rating and of input VAT previously refunded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the euro and other currencies on both capital and current accounts is established by Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Estonian Credit Institutions Act. There are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittances to non-residents; however, dividend distributions conventionally trigger a 20% withholding tax (reduced to 0% if paid to a qualifying EU parent under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to substance and reporting requirements). The only procedural hurdles are documentary: financial statements, tax residency certificates, beneficial-ownership declarations, and Sanctions/FATCA/CRS compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or redemption obligation applies to export proceeds in Estonia. Exporters must freely receive EUR/USD into a licensed credit institution without mandatory conversion; only statistical balance-of-payments reporting (NACE/ECB BOP forms) must be filed with Eesti Pank within prescribed deadlines (typically monthly/quarterly for material transactions). Failure to report may trigger administrative fines, not forfeiture.

Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia) as the central bank, with concurrent enforcement authority vested in the Estonian Financial Supervisory Authority (Finantsinspektsioon) and the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (Rahapesu andmete büroo) for cross-border AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate feasibility for EU/EEA-licensed digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) that hold a valid passport; low feasibility for offshore non-EEA EMIs. Estonian law (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Prevention Act) requires EMIs to obtain a financial services license or join an EU regulatory sandbox; fund safeguarding, positive pay screening, and local AML policies are mandatory. Use of an offshore EMI for settlement is legally constrained by beneficiary bank rejection and PSD2 SCA requirements.
