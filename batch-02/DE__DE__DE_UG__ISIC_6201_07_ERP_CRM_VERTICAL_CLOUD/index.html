Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The UG may be formed with stated share capital below EUR 25,000, including EUR 1. The entire cash contribution must be paid before registration; contributions in kind are prohibited. No statutory escrow account is required. The founders ordinarily deposit the capital into a company bank account after notarization and provide the managing director with evidence of payment for the registration filing. The UG must allocate one quarter of annual net profit, after loss carryforwards, to the statutory reserve under GmbHG section 5a(3).

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial fire or structural clearance ordinarily applies to a software office beyond generally applicable building-use, fire-safety, workplace, and occupational-health requirements. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) - Federal Central Tax Office, Bonn/Berlin (allocation of VAT identification numbers and central tax records; www.bzst.de); the locally competent Finanzamt (municipal tax office) for corporate income tax, trade tax, VAT assessment, and wage tax; electronic filing exclusively via the ELSTER portal (www.elster.de).

E-government filing portal

Domestic UG: (1) execute notarial deed and register the UG in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) via certified electronic filing; (2) submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) on ELSTER within four weeks of business commencement - the competent Finanzamt then issues the Steuernummer (tax number); (3) apply for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) from the BZSt using form USt 1 TU (electronically); (4) register the business with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt). Foreign entity with German nexus: registration is mandatory before the first German taxable supply; the application is filed with the BZSt (form USt 1 TU, available in English) and the central German tax office designated for non-resident VAT taxpayers is Finanzamt Neubrandenburg; no mandatory fiscal representative exists for ordinary VAT registration; a non-resident entity without a permanent establishment is assessed centrally for German VAT, must file monthly or quarterly VAT returns via ELSTER, and must appoint a domestic post-receipt authority (Empfangsbevollmächtigter) only where required by the tax office.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a corporation and is tax-opaque, with no pass-through eligibility. It is subject to corporation income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15%, solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of corporate income tax (effective 0.825%), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate of approximately 14%-17% depending on the municipal Hebesatz, and value added tax at 19% (reduced rate 7%). Territorial scope: unlimited tax liability (worldwide income) for the German-resident UG, with relief under double tax treaties (foreign permanent-establishment income generally exempt; foreign passive income typically credit-method). No tax-exempt zone applies to a mainland German UG; only the islands of Helgoland and Büsingen are excluded from the German VAT and excise territory. — Domestic UG: (1) execute notarial deed and register the UG in the Commercial Register (Handelsregister) via certified electronic filing; (2) submit the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) on ELSTER within four weeks of business commencement - the competent Finanzamt then issues the Steuernummer (tax number); (3) apply for the VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer) from the BZSt using form USt 1 TU (electronically); (4) register the business with the local trade office (Gewerbeamt). Foreign entity with German nexus: registration is mandatory before the first German taxable supply; the application is filed with the BZSt (form USt 1 TU, available in English) and the central German tax office designated for non-resident VAT taxpayers is Finanzamt Neubrandenburg; no mandatory fiscal representative exists for ordinary VAT registration; a non-resident entity without a permanent establishment is assessed centrally for German VAT, must file monthly or quarterly VAT returns via ELSTER, and must appoint a domestic post-receipt authority (Empfangsbevollmächtigter) only where required by the tax office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A UG (haftungsbeschränkt) is a corporation and is tax-opaque, with no pass-through eligibility. It is subject to corporation income tax (Körperschaftsteuer) at 15%, solidarity surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) at 5.5% of corporate income tax (effective 0.825%), municipal trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) at an effective rate of approximately 14%-17% depending on the municipal Hebesatz, and value added tax at 19% (reduced rate 7%). Territorial scope: unlimited tax liability (worldwide income) for the German-resident UG, with relief under double tax treaties (foreign permanent-establishment income generally exempt; foreign passive income typically credit-method). No tax-exempt zone applies to a mainland German UG; only the islands of Helgoland and Büsingen are excluded from the German VAT and excise territory.

Available election forms

Germany does not apply a check-the-box regime; legal form is conclusive for tax classification. Relevant statutory elections: (i) corporate income tax Organschaft (fiscal unity) under Sections 14 and 17 KStG and VAT Organschaft under Section 2(2) No. 2 UStG - requires a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) and 75% shareholding, consolidating group companies into one taxpayer and one VAT taxable person; (ii) trade tax group integration; (iii) interest-barrier (Zinsschranke) EBITDA carryforward election under Section 4h EStG; (iv) small-business exemption election under Section 19 UStG (opting out of input VAT deduction); (v) flat-rate input VAT option for trade tax purposes; (vi) hybrid mismatch alignment under ATAD is mandatory, not elective (Sections 4k EStG, 14 KStG, Section 12a GewStG); (vii) advance pricing agreements and cost-contribution arrangements for cross-border software royalty and cloud-service remuneration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Small-business exemption (Section 19 UStG) from 1 January 2025: prior-year turnover up to EUR 25,000 and forecast current-year turnover up to EUR 100,000. Intra-Community distance sales and B2C digital services: union-wide threshold of EUR 10,000 (OSS). Non-resident foreign suppliers: zero turnover threshold - any taxable supply in Germany requires VAT registration, otherwise the domestic recipient accounts for reverse-charge VAT under Section 13b UStG.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(i) Export supplies (Section 6 UStG): 0% VAT on tangible goods dispatched or transported from Germany to a destination outside the EU, provided the exporter transfers control over the goods and possesses conclusive proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis). (ii) Intra-Community supplies (Section 7 UStG): 0% VAT when goods are transported to another EU member state to an acquirer holding a valid VAT ID, reported in the recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung). (iii) B2B cloud/ERP/CRM software services: outside the scope of German VAT - the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction under Section 3a(2) UStG, so the UG issues the invoice without German VAT and the EU customer applies reverse charge; for non-EU customers no German VAT arises. (iv) B2C digital services to EU consumers: taxed in the consumer's EU country via the One-Stop-Shop at the destination rate. (v) Hardware, edge servers, and IoT devices exported as part of the vertical cloud solution are eligible for zero-rating as goods under Section 6 UStG; associated input VAT is refundable through the ordinary VAT return.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (maritime) / Air Waybill (air) / CMR consignment note (road)
  • Proof of export (Ausfuhrnachweis) per Sections 8-11 UStDV: customs-stamped Ausfuhrbescheinigung or alternative evidence (certified export declaration copies, customs seals, carrier documents, customs confirmation in the export document ledger)
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank payment advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds (German practice: receipt and provenance of payment must be fully traceable in the exporter's bank records; no mandatory statutory certificate format)
  • Intra-Community supply evidence: valid VAT ID of the acquirer, delivery notes, transport documents to the destination member state, and the quarterly recapitulative statement (Zusammenfassende Meldung)

Phased domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate under the Growth Opportunities Act (Wachstumschancengesetz): from 1 January 2025 all German entrepreneurs must be able to receive structured e-invoices; from 1 January 2027 issuers with prior-year turnover exceeding EUR 800,000 must issue e-invoices; from 1 January 2028 all domestic B2B issuers must issue e-invoices. Required formats: EN 16931-compliant XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x; EDI and other electronic formats are transitional until 31 December 2027 where contractually agreed. B2G supplies: XRechnung mandatory for all federal public-procurement invoices since 27 November 2020. Germany does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, taxation-at-source, or digital stamping; transmission occurs via the Peppol network, secure API, or EDI, with GoBD-compliant archiving for ten years. The e-invoice must show the VAT IDs of both parties, place of supply, reverse-charge marker, and, for exports, the applicable zero-rating basis (Section 6 or Section 7 UStG).

German law does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline; nevertheless, proof of export must be obtained without undue delay under Section 10 UStDV and, for alternative evidence, generally within six months, and the export proceeds must be traceable to the exporter's bank account. Failure to substantiate zero-rating leads to a retrospective assessment of 19% German VAT on the supply as a domestic transaction, plus statutory interest at 0.5% per month under Section 233a AO, late-payment surcharges of 1% per month under Section 240 AO, and penalty charges under Section 384 AO. Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes tax evasion under Section 370 AO (imprisonment up to five years, or up to ten years in aggravated cases, plus fines); customs violations are prosecuted under the Zollverwaltungsgesetz. Assessment periods: four years for ordinary cases, ten years for intentional evasion; managing directors of the UG are jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT and clawback assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Germany maintains a comprehensive liberal framework with no exchange controls on either current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, and royalties may be transferred abroad without prior governmental approval. The only statutory obligations are (i) uninterrupted reporting of cross-border payments and holdings to Deutsche Bundesbank for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes once certain thresholds are exceeded, and (ii) compliance with EU sanctions and anti-money laundering requirements. There are no exchange-rate restrictions, surrender requirements, or repatriation taxes; corporate treasury operations may freely retain foreign currencies in domestic and international bank accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds exists in Germany. The AWV imposes only a statistical reporting duty: German resident exporters must report each export transaction and receipt on behalf of Deutsche Bundesbank if the payment value equals or exceeds EUR 12,500 (or the equivalent in foreign currency) per calendar month in aggregate for open-market transactions. The report must be filed no later than the seventh day of the month following the month in which the receivable or payment was created or received. There is no requirement to convert a minimum proportion of export proceeds into euro, nor any compulsory deposit or holding period with domestic banks.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (Central Bank of the Federal Republic of Germany) acting as the national foreign exchange and balance-of-payments statistics authority in coordination with the German Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung – AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH with strict legal constraints. Non-German fintech or electronic money institutions (EMIs) holding a valid EU/EEA passport under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and regulated by their home-state supervisor (e.g., FCA, ACPR, BaFin) may lawfully open multi-currency virtual IBAN accounts for German-domiciled entities. Nevertheless, many German domestic corporate banks remain hesitant to accept foreign EMI virtual accounts as the sole treasury rail for high-value cross-border transactions because of (i) unavailability of German account-holder status, (ii) lack of participation in the German SEPA Clearing and Bundesbank settlement infrastructure, and (iii) heightened AML due-diligence expectations for software exporters. A tier-1 German bank account remains advisable as the primary operational account, with an EMI used for limited ancillary FX or disbursement corridors. BaFin does not grant a separate German license to non-EU EMIs; third-country EMIs must establish a German or EU subsidiary to provide account services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan capital injections to the UG's German bank account shall be initiated via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bulk transfers) from the foreign parent bank to the German beneficiary's IBAN (DE + BLZ). The wire message must clearly encode the German foreign-trade statistics code (Zahlungsgrund) for capital contribution, typically 'N90' (general capital transfer) or the specific 'S2050' / 'S2070' types, and must reference the 'Verwendungszweck' with the corporate purpose, e.g., 'Contribution to capital reserve - ERP/CRM entity'. Beneficiary confirmation must be supported by the corporate register excerpt and tax ID. The remitting bank must include the UG's name and address exactly as registered. For shareholder loans, the purpose code 'N71/K1109' (loan granting) should be used. All inbound wires should be matched electronically to the UG's treasury ledger via the bank's host-to-host channel (EBICS) and the central bank report filed with Deutsche Bundesbank if the monthly aggregate exceeds EUR 12,500. If the contribution is in foreign currency, the UG may hold the currency in a separate multi-currency balance or convert at request, but no approval is required.Outbound cross-border trade settlements for software development subcontracting, employee compensation, cloud hosting fees, and distributor commissions shall be executed over the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for single high-value wire transfers and (ii) commercial letters of credit (documentary credits, UCP-600) or SBLC (ICC ISP98) for transactions with high-risk or repo-sensitive counterparts. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% German corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%. Trade tax (Gewerbesteuer) is additional and depends on the municipal multiplier; the typical combined corporate burden is approximately 29% to 33%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% German capital yields withholding tax plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an aggregate domestic burden of 26.375%. A German UG must generally retain the statutory reserve until the legally required capital threshold is reached. Dividends may be distributed only from distributable profits and subject to preservation-of-capital and insolvency rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The annual return is generally due seven months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the ordinary deadline is 31 July of the following year. Tax balances are payable upon assessment; advance payments are due on the statutory quarterly dates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For annual cross-border related-party transactions, a Local File is generally mandatory where the value of supplies of goods exceeds EUR 6 million or the value of other transactions, including services, financing and royalties, exceeds EUR 600,000. A Master File is generally required for a German entity belonging to a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million, subject to German documentation rules. Extraordinary transactions require contemporaneous documentation irrespective of ordinary threshold analysis. Amounts are assessed by transaction category and fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Germany does not operate a single standalone economic substance regulation regime equivalent to certain low-tax jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the German anti-abuse rule in section 42 AO, beneficial-ownership principles, treaty anti-abuse provisions, the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive implementation rules, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules and withholding-tax relief provisions. A German UG should maintain real decision-making, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or infrastructure, commercial risk assumption, books and records, bank activity and evidence of services actually performed. Conduit arrangements, circular payments, artificial directors and profit allocations unsupported by functions, assets and risks are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion under section 370 AO may result in imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine; particularly serious cases may result in imprisonment from six months to ten years. Tax, interest and assessment surcharges remain payable. Voluntary disclosure relief is highly restricted and unavailable after certain discovery or investigative events.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability under section 69 AO for culpable failure to discharge tax obligations, civil damages and insolvency claims. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal or enforcement proceedings. Imprisonment is possible for personally committed or participated-in offenses.
Late filing, non-payment and insolvency-related tax liability

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties, payment surcharges, interest, enforcement costs and estimated assessments may apply. Insolvency proceedings may be opened. Unlawful delay in filing for insolvency can create criminal and civil exposure under the Insolvency Code, including imprisonment or fines in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Managing directors must monitor solvency and over-indebtedness and file for insolvency without undue delay when statutory conditions arise. They may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, unpaid taxes caused by culpable conduct and creditor damage.
Withholding tax failure

Statutory sanction: The German payer may be assessed for the unwithheld tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties. Intentional conduct may constitute tax evasion or aiding tax evasion. Treaty or EU relief may be denied retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible tax officers may incur personal liability under section 69 AO for culpable failure to withhold or remit tax and may face criminal exposure where non-compliance is intentional.
Transfer pricing manipulation and inadequate documentation

Statutory sanction: The Finanzamt may adjust taxable income, deny deductions, impose additional tax and interest, and apply documentation surcharges. Under section 162 AO, a documentation surcharge can generally range from 5% to 10% of the income adjustment, with statutory minimums; delayed submission may also generate a daily surcharge subject to statutory limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the UG for avoidable tax losses, personal liability for culpable tax failures and criminal exposure if documentation is deliberately falsified or used to support tax evasion.
Shell company, conduit and beneficial-ownership abuse

Statutory sanction: Benefits may be denied under section 42 AO, treaty anti-abuse provisions or EU anti-abuse rules. Tax may be reassessed with interest and penalties. Deliberate concealment may constitute tax evasion, money laundering or commercial fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and damages and may face criminal prosecution. Corporate separation is not a defense against personal participation in fraud, tax evasion, money laundering or unlawful distributions.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial ownership violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German Anti-Money Laundering Act may reach statutory maximums of up to EUR 1 million or multiples of the economic benefit, depending on the violation and offender. Serious laundering may result in criminal imprisonment and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance-responsible persons may be fined or prosecuted personally. Confiscation, account restrictions and investigative measures may affect personal and corporate assets. Travel bans are case-specific and not an automatic consequence.
VAT carousel, invoice and digital-services misclassification

Statutory sanction: Input VAT can be denied and output VAT assessed, with interest, penalties and criminal tax proceedings. Serious VAT fraud may attract imprisonment and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for culpable VAT losses and may face criminal liability for participation, instruction or knowing submission of false VAT information.
Unlawful distributions and capital maintenance violations

Statutory sanction: The distribution may be unlawful and recoverable by the company. Tax recharacterization as a hidden profit distribution may deny deductions and trigger dividend withholding tax, solidarity surcharge, interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restitute payments, compensate the UG and face liability for insolvency aggravation or creditor damage. Piercing the corporate veil is exceptional, but personal liability arises directly under statutory director-liability rules where applicable.
Failure to maintain accounting, records and electronic evidence

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may estimate the tax base, deny deductions, impose procedural penalties and treat missing documentation adversely in transfer-pricing proceedings. Deliberate destruction or falsification may be criminal.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal civil and tax liability for negligent record failures and criminal exposure for intentional concealment or destruction of evidence.
