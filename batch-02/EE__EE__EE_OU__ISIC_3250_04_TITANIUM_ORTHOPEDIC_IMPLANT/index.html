Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box election is available; the OÜ is classified by Estonian corporate law as a resident limited company subject to entity-level profit-distribution taxation. Estonia applies a deferred profit tax system: undistributed profits are untaxed, distributions are taxed at 20/80 gross-up for ordinary dividends and at 14/86 for regular dividend payments that satisfy the statutory conditions. Dividends received by a resident company may be exempt under participation exemption rules to the extent they carry statutory credits. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are implemented and limit deduction/non-deduction outcomes. The only substantive 'election' is the decision on whether to distribute profits in a given tax year and the formal declaration submitted in the annual TSD/INF1 filing.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
22%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover in a calendar year for resident and EU-established suppliers; non-established foreign suppliers do not benefit from the threshold and may incur registration/declaration duties from the first taxable transaction in Estonia or rely on reverse-charge/import procedures.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under KMS §16, a supply of goods to a non-EU destination is eligible for 0% VAT if the goods leave the EU by the required customs deadline and the supplier retains proof of export before the VAT return is filed. The exporter must hold a valid customs export declaration and transport evidence showing the goods reached a third country. For goods sold to a non-EU buyer on delivered basis, the OÜ should also secure the buyer's export status and retain the commercial contract. Intra-EU dispatch to a registered customer is zero-rated under KMS subject to the customer's valid VAT number and the EC Sales List (VAT declaration) obligations. Offshore services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the territorial scope of Estonian VAT to the extent the place-of-supply rules for B2B services point to the customer's jurisdiction.

No general real-time VAT clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in Estonia. B2G e-invoicing is mandatory for suppliers to public-sector entities and must use Peppol/EN 16931-compliant formats. VAT returns (KMD/KMDINF) and all tax declarations must be submitted electronically via e-MTA in structured XML. There is no mandatory continuous transaction-control system for B2B or export invoicing beyond regular tax audit access to e-invoices.

      
The ETCB may require production of export and payment documents during audit; where the export cannot be substantiated with customs declarations and transport/remittance records, zero-rating is reversed and VAT at 22% is assessed with late payment interest at the statutory daily rate and applicable tax penalties. Missing or inconsistent documentation can lead to clawback of VAT refunds claimed for input VAT connected with the exported goods. Deliberate false allegations of export or sham export schemes are treated as tax fraud and may trigger criminal liability under the Estonian Penal Code, in addition to administrative fines and retrospective reassessment within the applicable limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Complete de jure and de facto convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and Estonia's membership of the EU guarantee unrestricted movement of capital, payments and transfers. Current-account convertibility covers trade settlements, royalties, freight, commission, and technical-assistance fees without limit or prior approval. Capital-account convertibility permits 100% non-resident ownership of an Osaühing (OÜ), free subscription of share capital in EUR, free intercompany lending, and unimpeded repatriation of dividends and liquidation proceeds. The only practical frictions are fiscal rather than exchange-based: (i) Estonia's distribution tax, normally 20% of gross dividend (with a 14% regular-dividend rate where the 20% rate has been paid on underlying retained profits), (ii) the EUR 10,000 cash-carrier declaration duty under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 for cash crossing EU external borders, and (iii) balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Eesti Pank for international transactions exceeding the EUR 10,000 monthly reporting threshold. A surgical-implant exporter may therefore invoice abroad, hold foreign-currency receivable accounts, and remit dividends to a non-resident shareholder without any central-bank permit or FX authorization. Profit remittance requires only a shareholders' resolution, a distributable-profit computation under the Estonian Commercial Code and tax balance, and settlement via an Estonian credit institution.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Estonia imposes no statutory surrender period, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for export proceeds derived from titanium orthopedic implant sales. The OÜ may freely retain USD, GBP, CNY or other export receipts in its own multi-currency accounts or in accounts opened with licensed banks abroad. Treasury policy for the ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339113 export pipeline should nonetheless establish a disciplined conversion strategy: USD-denominated distributor receivables are converted into EUR within a rolling 30- to 60-day window to fund EUR-denominated titanium powder purchases, payroll and Estonian operational expenditure. No transaction-level authorization for conversion is required by Eesti Pank, but the payments must be captured in the monthly international-transactions statistical return where the EUR-equivalent aggregate exceeds EUR 10,000. Sanctions screening under Council Regulation (EU) 833/2014 (including prohibitions affecting Russian-sourced titanium billet and additive-manufacturing inputs) is a trade-compliance obligation, not a currency-control rule.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Eesti Pank (Bank of Estonia), acting within the Eurosystem as the euro-area national central bank for the Republic of Estonia; since 1 January 2022 Eesti Pank also incorporates the former Financial Supervision Authority, exercising integrated prudential, market-conduct and AML/CFT supervision over Estonian credit institutions and electronic-money institutions. There is no separate Estonian foreign-exchange administration; the Ministry of Finance, the Estonian Financial Intelligence Unit (within the Police and Border Guard Board), and Eesti Pank jointly administer any residual capital-movement and statistical-reporting obligations. As a full euro-area member (euro adopted on 1 January 2011), Estonia has no sovereign currency of its own, and monetary policy, foreign-reserve management and currency-issuance authority rest with the European Central Bank.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium, and strictly bounded to operational treasury sub-flows. EU-passported electronic-money institutions and Estonian-licensed EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, and comparable digital payment providers) are legally permitted to hold client funds, provide multi-currency IBANs, and execute SEPA and SWIFT-adjacent transfers for the OÜ. However, they are not credit institutions: they cannot issue a compliant SWIFT MT700 documentary Letter of Credit, cannot provide advance-payment or performance bank guarantees, and are generally not accepted by the Estonian Commercial Register or a notary as evidence of subscribed share capital being held in a bank account for statutory registration purposes. Finantsinspektsioon/Eesti Pank supervision expects tier-1 concentration for a medium-risk enhanced manufacturing entity; therefore EMIs should be used only for (i) low-value distributor and e-commerce collections, (ii) employee expense-card issuing, and (iii) selected supplier payables under EUR 10,000. All EMI balances must be swept daily or weekly into the tier-1 bank operating account; the statutory capital injection and all LC/standby-LC transactions must be executed exclusively through a licensed Eurosystem credit institution.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, and strictly bounded to operational treasury sub-flows. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
22% corporate income tax on distributed profits from 1 January 2025, calculated as 22/78 of the net distribution. Undistributed and reinvested profits are generally taxed at 0% until distribution or another taxable payment.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
Generally 0% Estonian withholding tax on dividends paid by an Estonian company to a non-resident shareholder under the current ordinary dividend regime. The Estonian company normally pays 22/78 corporate income tax on the net distribution. Special historical reduced-rate dividend regimes and recipient-specific rules should not be applied without confirming the payment date and transitional provisions.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
The TSD and related payment must generally be submitted and paid by the 10th day of the month following the month in which the taxable payment, distribution, fringe benefit, or other reportable event occurred. Estonia generally does not require a separate annual corporate income tax return for ordinary resident-company profit taxation because tax is imposed when profits are distributed or otherwise made taxable.

      

      
      

        
        
Deadline:  · Frequency: 

        
Generally due by the 20th day of the month following the VAT period, with payment due on the same date. Intrastat, customs, and EU sales-list reporting may have separate deadlines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Estonia has no universal transaction-value threshold that independently triggers a Local File or Master File. Detailed documentation obligations generally apply to specified taxpayers, including resident companies meeting all or relevant large-taxpayer criteria of at least 250 employees, annual turnover of at least EUR 50 million, and balance-sheet assets of at least EUR 43 million, as well as certain financial institutions, listed companies, non-resident permanent establishments, and entities in prescribed related-party situations. Other taxpayers must still apply the arm's-length principle and retain proportionate evidence. The relevant local currency is EUR.

      
Economic substance scope: Estonia has no standalone regime formally titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, substance is material under the Estonian general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, treaty beneficial-ownership analysis, EU anti-abuse rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest-limitation rules, and permanent-establishment principles. The OÜ should maintain real decision-making in Estonia where claimed, appropriate directors and personnel, manufacturing or technical capabilities consistent with the business model, premises or operational access, banking and accounting controls, inventory and quality records, commercial risk assumption, and contemporaneous records. E-Residency, a registered address, or a local nominee alone does not establish tax residence, economic substance, or treaty entitlement.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Undeclared distribution and constructive dividend
        
Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax reassessment at the applicable distribution rate, statutory interest, administrative penalties, and possible denial of deductions. Related payroll or withholding taxes may also be assessed.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the company and creditors for unlawful distributions or breach of fiduciary duties. Intentional tax fraud may result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a lawful basis, such as criminal proceedings or enforcement measures; corporate limited liability does not protect fraudulent or personally wrongful conduct.

      

      
      

        Transfer-pricing manipulation
        
Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to an arm's-length result, corporate income tax on a recharacterized distribution or non-business expense, interest, documentation penalties, and potential application of anti-avoidance rules.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for negligent or intentional failure to maintain records and for damage caused to the company or creditors. Criminal liability can arise where records or invoices are deliberately falsified or tax is intentionally evaded.

      

      
      

        False invoices and tax evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Tax assessment, interest, administrative fines, loss of VAT deduction, confiscation of criminal proceeds, and criminal sanctions for intentional large-scale tax fraud or document offences.

        
Director personal liability: Responsible directors, beneficial owners, accountants, or other participants may face personal criminal liability, fines, imprisonment, civil restitution, and seizure of assets. A travel ban may be imposed only under applicable procedural or enforcement rules.

      

      
      

        VAT and customs abuse
        
Statutory sanction: VAT and customs reassessment, import-duty liability, interest, penalties, seizure of goods, loss of customs simplifications, and possible criminal investigation for deliberate evasion.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability and, where deliberate conduct is proven, criminal liability. Customs authorities may pursue responsible persons in addition to the company where statutory conditions are met.

      

      
      

        Unlicensed medical-device manufacturing
        
Statutory sanction: Product withdrawal or recall, prohibition of placing products on the market, administrative fines, suspension of operations, regulatory enforcement, and potential criminal liability for serious or intentional breaches. Tax deductions and VAT treatment may also be challenged where transactions are fictitious or unlawful.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil claims, product-liability exposure, regulatory disqualification, and criminal prosecution where unsafe products, fraud, or deliberate non-compliance causes harm. Imprisonment is fact-dependent and not automatic.

      

      
      

        AML, beneficial-ownership, and sanctions breach
        
Statutory sanction: Supervisory penalties, transaction blocking, account restrictions, confiscation or freezing of assets, licence consequences, and criminal sanctions for money laundering or sanctions evasion.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may incur personal administrative or criminal liability where they knowingly participate in, facilitate, or negligently permit unlawful activity. Asset freezing and travel restrictions may be ordered under applicable law; neither is an automatic consequence of ordinary compliance failure.

      

      
      

        Payroll and director-remuneration non-compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Reassessment of income tax, social tax, unemployment insurance, pension contributions, interest, and penalties, together with possible employment-law enforcement.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate concealment, unlawful non-payment, or damage caused to the company. Criminal exposure is possible for intentional large-scale evasion or falsification.

      

      
      

        Insolvency and creditor prejudice
        
Statutory sanction: Avoidance of transactions, repayment claims, insolvency proceedings, director disqualification, creditor damages, and possible criminal sanctions for insolvency offences.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to compensate creditors or the company and may face management bans, asset enforcement, civil restitution, and imprisonment where statutory insolvency offences are established.

      

      
      

        E-Residency and tax-residence misrepresentation
        
Statutory sanction: Tax-residence reassessment, foreign permanent-establishment exposure, denial of treaty benefits, transfer-pricing adjustments, penalties, and possible duplicate taxation.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for misleading records and personal exposure for intentional false statements or tax fraud. E-Residency does not itself create limited liability protection against personal misconduct, criminal acts, or creditor prejudice.
