Name reservation / lock period

Germany provides no statutory name-reservation mechanism. Name protection begins only upon registration in the commercial register. If the Amtsgericht rejects the proposed name on non-distinctiveness or misleading grounds, the applicant (through the notary) may file 'Erinnerung' or 'Beschwerde' (appeal) to the Landgericht (regional court) within two weeks of rejection notice. The appeal is submitted through the notary who executed the incorporation filing. No extension of name availability exists prior to registration. If rejected, submit a revised name a new notarial filing is required, with new notary fees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bundesministerium der Finanzen (BMF, Federal Ministry of Finance) acting through the locally competent Finanzamt (corporate tax office at the UG's registered seat); cross-border and EU VAT identification matters administered by the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt, www.bzst.de); electronic filing and tax-ID application via ELSTER portal (www.elster.de); customs and export procedures administered by the Generalzolldirektion, Bonn.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Upon notarial formation and registration of the UG in the Handelsregister, the management must electronically file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER within one month of business commencement, stating the intended P2P micro-lending activity, BaFin licensing status, shareholders, and expected turnover. Step 2: The local Finanzamt issues the permanent corporate Steuernummer (corporate tax number) and simultaneously registers the UG for VAT. Step 3: For intra-Community and cross-border transactions, the UG files form USt 1 TN with BZSt to obtain the EU-level Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr.), ordinarily issued within 2-4 weeks. Step 4: Non-resident shareholders require no local fiscal representative for corporate income tax or trade tax; however, a non-EU entrepreneur seeking German input VAT refunds under the 13th VAT Directive must appoint a fiscal representative in Germany unless exempted by mutual-assistance treaty. Step 5: Because ISIC 6499 micro-lending constitutes a licensable banking/financial business, proof of the BaFin licence (KWG permission or statutory exemption for the P2P intermediary model) must accompany the tax registration file; failure to evidence the license may trigger refusal of the Steuernummer.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a resident corporation with unlimited worldwide tax liability. Statutory corporate tax burden: Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% of CIT, yielding a combined 15.825%, plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%-490%, implying an effective trade tax range of ~7% to ~17%, and an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 29%-33% depending on the municipality). The interest margin and fee income from the P2P micro-lending platform constitute active commercial business income (Gewerbeeinkünfte) fully subject to the trade tax and CIT; no tax-exempt zone, holding privilege, or IP box regime applies in Germany. The UG is not eligible for pass-through or transparent treatment; foreign-source income is relieved by German DTA networks (exemption or credit method) and the German CFC rules (Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung) apply to passive income of controlled foreign companies. — Step 1: Upon notarial formation and registration of the UG in the Handelsregister, the management must electronically file the tax registration questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER within one month of business commencement, stating the intended P2P micro-lending activity, BaFin licensing status, shareholders, and expected turnover. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a resident corporation with unlimited worldwide tax liability. Statutory corporate tax burden: Körperschaftsteuer (corporate income tax) 15% plus Solidaritätszuschlag 5.5% of CIT, yielding a combined 15.825%, plus Gewerbesteuer (trade tax) at 3.5% base rate multiplied by the municipal Hebesatz (typically 200%-490%, implying an effective trade tax range of ~7% to ~17%, and an aggregate effective income tax burden of approximately 29%-33% depending on the municipality). The interest margin and fee income from the P2P micro-lending platform constitute active commercial business income (Gewerbeeinkünfte) fully subject to the trade tax and CIT; no tax-exempt zone, holding privilege, or IP box regime applies in Germany. The UG is not eligible for pass-through or transparent treatment; foreign-source income is relieved by German DTA networks (exemption or credit method) and the German CFC rules (Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung) apply to passive income of controlled foreign companies.

Available election forms

Germany does not provide a check-the-box or entity-classification election. Available statutory elections: (1) VAT Organschaft (§2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG) and corporate-tax Organschaft (§§14-19 KStG, §34c GewStG), both requiring majority voting control, financial and organizational integration, and a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) with a controlling parent for at least five years; (2) application of the Kleinunternehmerregelung under §19 UStG (thresholds EUR 25,000 prior year and EUR 100,000 current year EU-wide turnover; practically unavailable to a UG making exempt lending supplies because exempt turnover counts toward the threshold); (3) option to waive VAT exemptions on real estate transactions (§9 UStG) to secure input VAT recovery; (4) ATAD II hybrid-mismatch elections and compliance filings under §4k EStG; (5) ordinary corporate reorganization under UmwStG for conversion of the UG into a regular GmbH or an AG once retained profits reach EUR 25,000 statutory minimum capital.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 prior-year and EUR 100,000 current-year EU-wide turnover under the small-business scheme (§19 UStG); however, no general registration threshold applies to a German-resident UG — any entrepreneur effecting taxable supplies is a taxable person, and even pure exempt financial services require VAT registration to declare exempt revenue and to claim pro-rata input VAT. The intra-Community distance sales / cross-border B2C services threshold is EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide turnover, beyond which the destination-country VAT applies via the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS); for B2B financial services to non-EU recipients the place of supply shifts abroad under §3a UStG without registration consequences in Germany.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods (§6 UStG) and intra-Community supplies (§6a UStG) are zero-rated with full input VAT recovery, provided transport to another EU member state or third country is proven. The UG's P2P micro-lending core activity — granting and negotiating credit, managing loan portfolios — is VAT-exempt under §4 Nr. 8 lit. a and b UStG (credit-granting and credit-dealing exempt without input VAT recovery); it is not zero-rated. Separately charged platform or technological processing fees are characterized as independent taxable services subject to 19% VAT where rendered to German or EU entrepreneurs, but for B2B services supplied to non-EU resident counterparties the place of supply moves outside the EU under §3a Abs. 2 UStG, rendering the services outside German VAT scope with the customer liable for local reverse-charge; B2C digital platform services to non-EU consumers are likewise outside German VAT when effectively used outside the EU. Conversely, the UG may not claim export zero-rating for its exempt lending spread; input VAT on overhead costs is recoverable only to the extent apportioned to taxable fee income (pro-rata deduction per §15 Abs. 4 UStG), with a strict segregation of the lending and technical-service cost pools.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Ausfuhranmeldung) lodged via ATLAS with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) for physical exports of goods
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or rail/postal transit proof evidencing removal of goods from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and third-country import documentation (e.g., import certificate) where the consignment is not declared in Germany
  • For exempt lending services: executed loan agreements, borrower KYC/AML files, settlement and disbursement statements, and bank credit advices evidencing the actual loan nexus and service recipient
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT/SEPA credit advice proving receipt of proceeds into the UG's German bank account

Mandatory B2B electronic invoicing (E-Rechnung) in force since 1 January 2025: all German-resident businesses, including the UG, must be able to receive structured e-invoices (XRechnung XML or ZUGFeRD 2.0.1 PDF/A-3 with embedded XML; PEPPOL transport supported). The UG must issue structured E-Rechnungen for domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2027 if annual revenue exceeds EUR 800,000 in 2026, and from 1 January 2028 for all other businesses; during 2025-2026, paper/PDF invoices remain permissible with recipient consent. Germany does not operate a real-time tax-authority clearance or digital-stamping model (no Italian SDI-style mechanism); compliance is enforced ex-post through mandatory monthly/quarterly VAT returns (Voranmeldungen) filed via ELSTER, the 5-year/10-year retention rules of §14b UStG and GoBD, and audit rights. The UG's exempt credit-granting supplies require invoices bearing the VAT-exemption reference under §14 Abs. 4 UStG, while taxable platform fees require full 19% VAT invoices identifying the UG's Steuernummer/USt-IdNr.

German tax law sets no statutory 180-day export-proceeds repatriation deadline; however, German tax auditors apply the 180-day benchmark as documentary plausibility evidence that the export/third-country supply was completed and the income realized. Failure to produce the export documentation bundle or the FX/transfer evidence within the audit window triggers retrospective denial of the 0% rate with reassessment at 19% VAT plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.5% (§233a AO), and retrospective adjustment of input VAT deductions (§17 UStG). Deliberate or reckless fabrication of export or third-country destination evidence constitutes tax evasion under §370 AO, punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years (10 years for aggravated commercial evasion) and fines; negligent misdeclaration under §378 AO entails administrative fines up to EUR 25,000. Managing directors of the UG face personal secondary liability under §§34, 69 AO for unpaid VAT, plus interest and costs; the BaFin may additionally revoke the KWG lending licence for serious tax and compliance misconduct, and the UG's exemption status under the P2P-intermediary model is re-examined.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany applies full current account and capital account convertibility under the EU free movement of capital (TFEU Article 63) and the German AWV. Profit remittances are unrestricted for GmbH/UG entities; however, statistical reporting obligations apply for cross-border payments exceeding EUR 12,500 and for certain cross-border receivables/payables under AWV Sections 67-70. AUG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) profits may be remitted as dividends after annual surplus appropriation, subject to 26.375% German withholding tax for non-resident shareholders unless treaty relief applies; no FX approval is required for dividend remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Germany has no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained freely, but the German resident exporter must report cross-border payments and the outstanding balance of foreign currency receivables exceeding EUR 5 million to the Deutsche Bundesbank for balance-of-payments statistical purposes. For a micro-lending platform, 'export' is not the core activity; inbound cross-border funds for lending operations are reported as cross-border liabilities/payments under AWV Sections 67-70, but there is no conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German Central Bank) and Deutsche Finanzagentur; external trade and payments reporting under the Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI entities (e.g., UK EMIs after Brexit or non-EEA payment institutions) have low legal viability as principal bank or client-money account provider because a German P2P lending platform must ensure client funds are held in segregated accounts at a credit institution licensed under KWG or a fully licensed German/EU bank. EMIs may support payment initiation or virtual IBAN settlement but cannot lawfully hold German client funds as core treasury deposit; a BaFin-related 'contract of trust' arrangement would not satisfy §47 KWG segregation if the EMI is not a KWG-licensed credit institution. Hence acceptable use only as a payment facilitator and not as treasury liquidity carrier; medium-to-low viability as clearing rail, high risk if used for principal custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the UG must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the dedicated corporate treasury account at a German licensed bank, using payment purpose codes that map to German balance-of-payments statistics: for equity capital contribution, use purpose code 'EK' (Einlage/Eigenkapital) or 'N 12' under AWV reporting; for shareholder loans, use 'N 05' (Darlehen/Gewährung von Darlehen) or 'N 06' notification code; for intra-group financing, report under VZ-1 (Zahlungsbilanzstatistik). The UG must simultaneously file AWV report with Deutsche Bundesbank if credit card, invoice, or transfer exceeds EUR 12,500. To prevent false classification as prohibited repayment/deposit, supporting documentation must include notarized capital contribution statement (Übernahmevertrag), commercial register entry, and shareholder loan agreements. For lending platform funding from non-resident lenders, incoming wire transfers shall be tagged as 'lending principal on pass-through to borrowers' with contract evidence, and segregated from the UG's own capital; for each lender, a NAV-priced custody ledger ledger shall be maintained.Outbound disbursements to borrowers are executed through local clearing rails: SEPA Instant Credit Transfer/SCT Inst for low-value micro loans (up to EUR 100,000); standard SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for standing operations; ACH-like direct debit (SEPA Core Direct Debit) as the primary repayment collection rail; wallet payout rails via GIROPAY, PayPal, Klarna, or mobile wallets if a licensed payment institution acts as scheme participant. Cross-border trade settlement for platform internationalization uses SWIFT MT103 for clearable correspondent banking and Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) only for supplier/platform procurement exceeding standard thresholds. P2P micro-loan disbursements do not naturally use commercial LC. All disbursement files must be reconciled to borrower assignment ledgers, and for loan principal returned to non-resident lenders the outbound remittance must report AWV code 'N 21/VU' and include underlying loan agreements to avoid refusal by forwarding bank.Plain-vanilla FX spot transactions (spot contract) for immediate conversion of non-EUR currenciesOutright FX forward contracts (settled via ISDA or German Rahmenvereinbarung für Devisengeschäfte) for up to 12-month horizons to hedge cross-border investor/lender currency mismatchesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with convertibility restrictions are permitted in Germany outside Eurozone counterparties, subject to robust credit limits and BaFin-compliant OTC documentation; not necessary for mainstream convertible currenciesCross-currency swaps (CCS/CIRS) to hedge floating-rate mismatches between funding received in USD/CHF and EUR-denominated micro-loan assetsFX options (European-style) for hedging downside of large non-EUR equity injections, permitted under German banking law for non-financial UG only if executed through KWG-licensed counterparty and designated as hedging (not speculative)Interest rate swaps (IRS) combined with FX forward as a synthetic hedge if the micro-loan platform holds variable-rate loan liabilities

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI entities (e.g., UK EMIs after Brexit or non-EEA payment institutions) have low legal viability as principal bank or client-money account provider because a German P2P lending platform must ensure client funds are held in segregated accounts at a credit institution licensed under KWG or a fully licensed German/EU bank. EMIs may support payment initiation or virtual IBAN settlement but cannot lawfully hold German client funds as core treasury deposit; a BaFin-related 'contract of trust' arrangement would not satisfy §47 KWG segregation if the EMI is not a KWG-licensed credit institution. Hence acceptable use only as a payment facilitator and not as treasury liquidity carrier; medium-to-low viability as clearing rail, high risk if used for principal custody.
