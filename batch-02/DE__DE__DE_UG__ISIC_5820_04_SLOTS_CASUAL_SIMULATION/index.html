Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Finanzamt (local tax office) for corporate income tax (Körperschaftsteuer), trade tax (Gewerbesteuer), and VAT (Umsatzsteuer); Bundeszentralamt für Steuern (BZSt) for the EU VAT number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer, USt-IdNr.) and intra-Community trading; digital filing portal: ELSTER (www.elster.de) and BZSt Online Portal (www.bzst.de).

E-government filing portal

Upon commercial register (Handelsregister) filing, the managing director must submit the electronic incorporation tax questionnaire (Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung) via ELSTER within 4 weeks to the locally competent Finanzamt, which then assigns a Steuernummer and opens tax accounts for CIT, trade tax, and VAT. The UG (foreign-owned or not) then applies to BZSt for a USt-IdNr. via its online portal, submitting certified company documents and the Steuernummer; USt-IdNr. issuance typically takes 2-6 weeks after Steuernummer assignment. Non-resident directors can submit via ELSTER with basic German language authorization; no statutory tax representative is required for EU/EEA-resident managers, but a fiscal representative is mandatory for German VAT registration of non-EU entities only — a German UG itself requires no such representative. Total end-to-end timeline: 3-12 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company governed by GmbH law with full statutory legal personality; it is subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT, effective 15.825%), Gewerbesteuer at a municipal multiplier-dependent rate between approximately 7% and 17% (national average: ~14.4%), and VAT/Umsatzsteuer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The UG (Unternehmergesellschaft haftungsbeschränkt) is a capital company governed by GmbH law with full statutory legal personality; it is subject to Körperschaftsteuer at 15% (plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge on CIT, effective 15.825%), Gewerbesteuer at a municipal multiplier-dependent rate between approximately 7% and 17% (national average: ~14.4%), and VAT/Umsatzsteuer. Territorial scope: worldwide income taxation for German-resident companies, with treaty-based exemption for foreign permanent establishments and, subject to § 8b KStG and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, participation exemption for foreign dividend income; CFC rules (Hinzurechnungsbesteuerung) apply under §§ 7-13 AStG. No tax-exempt zone status applies; the UG is not eligible for the small-business VAT exemption once turnover exceeds thresholds or if it is a corporation with commercial activity.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in German tax law; the UG is mandatorily a corporation. Available statutory elections: (i) Organschaft (tax group) under §§ 14-19 KStG and § 2 Abs. 2 Nr. 2 UStG — requires a profit-and-loss pooling agreement (Ergebnisabführungsvertrag) for CIT/trade-tax consolidation; (ii) VAT fiscal unity (umsatzsteuerliche Organschaft) integrating subsidiary into the controlling parent's VAT registration; (iii) EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election under §§ 18i and 18j UStG for cross-border EU B2C digital services and intra-Community distance sales, replacing multiple local VAT registrations; (iv) election under § 9 UStG to waive VAT exemption for real property leases, if applicable; (v) application of § 8b KStG 95% dividend exemption (with 5% non-deductible business expense); (vi) hybrid mismatch adjustments per § 4k EStG require documentation of foreign tax treatment mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

19%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business (Kleinunternehmer) threshold under § 19 UStG: €25,000 previous-year turnover and €100,000 projected current-year turnover (2025 onwards); however, a UG operating a commercial virtual-slots business will normally exceed these thresholds, and a mandatory VAT registration applies from the first euro of supply for non-eligible entities. For non-established digital service suppliers (e.g., foreign app publishers) there is no de minimis nexus threshold — the place of supply rule under § 3a Abs. 3 Nr. 5 UStG (B2C electronic services) creates a direct German VAT registration obligation; for cross-border EU B2C digital services, the €10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold allows applying home-country VAT via OSS but once exceeded, harmonized OSS registration (Germany: § 18i UStG) becomes the standard filing route.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital services (including social-casino/slot apps) supplied by the German UG to customers located outside the European Union are outside the scope of German VAT (§ 3a Abs. 2 UStG), effectively zero-rated at 0%. Eligibility requires proof that the customer's habitual residence/business establishment is in a third country; two non-contradictory pieces of evidence per EU Reg. 282/2011 Art. 24f must be retained: (i) customer billing address & bank/payment country, (ii) IP-device geolocation, mobile country code, or landline data. For B2B supplies to third-country buyers, a valid VAT ID or written certification from the buyer suffices. Physical exports of promotional merchandise from Germany also qualify for 0% VAT under § 6 UStG if the goods are directly dispatched outside the EU and accompanied by the customs export declaration. The UG must retain all evidence for 10 years (§ 147 AO); failure to substantiate results in deemed taxable supply at 19%.

  • Customs electronic export declaration (ATLAS MRN number; or GRP Germany Export Procedure) for physical goods exports; alternative evidence: customs export certificate (Ausfuhrbescheinigung) of a foreign customs office
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or airway bill (AWB) or CMR truck consignment note proving physical departure from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase/order contract referencing the export shipment
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation showing foreign-currency payment received from the third-country buyer (repatriation proof)
  • For pure digital services: customer-IP/logfile evidence, billing/contract documentation, App Store sales reports (Apple/Google) and payment gateway records evidencing third-country location
  • OSS records and VAT identification numbers of EU business customers (for cross-border B2B reverse-charge proof)

Germany has no real-time fiscal clearance/authorization (no SAF-T continuous tax-control or digital stamping) for standard B2B transactions. Mandatory E-Rechnung (electronic structured invoice in XInvoice/XRechnung or ZUGFeRD 2.x/EN 16931 XML format) applies to domestic B2B supplies from 1 January 2025 for receiving and from 1 January 2027 for issuing by businesses with turnover above €800,000 p.a.; full mandatory issuance for all taxpayers from 1 January 2028. B2C consumer payments via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are exempt from E-Rechnung obligations; the UG issued simplified invoices under § 33 UStDV. As platform marketplaces (Apple, Google) act as deemed suppliers under § 3 Abs. 3 UStG for non-EEA sellers only — the German UG remains the actual supplier for its own German B2C sales and must charge 19% VAT on domestic app-store purchases; EU B2C sales may be reported via OSS.

German tax law does not contain a codified FX-repatriation deadline; however, administrative practice under the export-substantiation rules (Außenprüfungsrichtlinien and R 6.5 EStR) accepts a 6-month (180-day) payment-receipt window as the standard commercial expectation for retaining zero-rated export treatment; if payment is not received within this period, the Finanzamt may reclassify the supply as domestic taxable at 19% unless the UG provides a written commercial justification (e.g., open-account terms, credit risk, or regulated-payment-rail delays). Penalties: retrospective clawback of input VAT deductions, reassessment plus statutory late-payment interest at 0.5% per month (§ 233a AO; yearly effective up to 6%), negligence fine under § 378 AO up to €50,000, and criminal tax evasion under § 370 AO for intentional fraud with imprisonment up to 5 years and unlimited fines; an intentional misdeclaration of digital-service export turnover is treated as aggravated evasion due to cross-border platform sales, triggering 10-year VAT audit limitation (§ 169 Abs. 2 AO) and joint personal liability of the managing director under § 34 AO and § 69 AO for the full under-declared VAT plus penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Germany has full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current and capital account transactions, grounded in Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital) and the cross-border payment liberalization of the AWG. No exchange-control approval, no investment clearance, no profit-repatriation permit, and no prior authorization for trade payments, royalties, dividends, or intercompany settlement exists. The only binding duty is statistical: any payment from a non-resident or to a non-resident exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month per counterparty must be reported to the Deutsche Bundesbank within 7 days after the end of the month in which the payment was made (AWV §§ 67–69). Dividend distributions from the UG to non-resident shareholders are subject to statutory withholding tax of 26.375% (25% capital gains tax plus 5.5% solidarity surcharge); the rate may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% shareholding held for one year) or to 5–15% under an applicable double-tax treaty, provided the beneficial ownership certificate and treaty-relief application are submitted through the German tax administration or an ATAD-compliant KStB refund route.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio exist under German law. Export receipts — including app-store net settlements from Apple and Google — may be retained in any currency, held in foreign-currency accounts with a German bank, or converted to EUR at the UG's free commercial discretion. The sole statutory duty is AWV reporting of inbound payments from abroad exceeding EUR 12,500 within 7 days after month-end; the reporting classification for trade/service receipts falls under the Bundesbank's 'Zahlungsbilanz' category for services (software publishing, entertainment media), and no Certificate of Inward Remittance or customs documentation is required. Cash payments to non-residents exceeding EUR 15,000 are prohibited unless authorized by the Bundesbank, but bank-mediated wire transfers are entirely unrestricted.

Central bank supervision: Deutsche Bundesbank (German central bank) is the designated foreign-exchange statistics authority under the German Foreign Trade and Payments Regulation (Außenwirtschaftsverordnung, AWV). Financial-market supervision of banks and payment institutions is vested in the Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht (BaFin); the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (BMWK) administers the Foreign Trade and Payments Act (Außenwirtschaftsgesetz, AWG). The Bundesbank operates the 'Allgemeines Meldeportal' electronic reporting system for all cross-border payment statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. German-licensed and EU-passported electronic-money institutions (EMIs) — including Solaris SE, Pliant GmbH, Wise Europe SA (passported into Germany), and enabling-model payment institutions — can legally provide EUR IBANs, virtual IBANs, and multi-currency wallets to a German UG. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not authorized to take deposits and provide no deposit-protection coverage (no German Einlagensicherung), so they are unsuitable as the sole primary settlement account for Apple/Google payout; (ii) most EMI risk frameworks classify social-casino and virtual-slots publishing under an excluded 'gambling-adjacent' vertical, requiring turnover caps below EUR 100,000/month or outright refusal; (iii) Apple and Google developer-payout rails demand a beneficiary bank capable of receiving inbound SWIFT/SEPA corporate credits — a virtual IBAN from an EMI sponsor bank is technically accepted but triggers the sponsor's enhanced due diligence on the gaming horizontal. Strategic recommendation: use a Tier-1 German bank as the primary collection account, and retain only one EMI (e.g., Wise Business) for low-value ad-network payouts and FX conversion where same-day settlement is needed.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% corporate income tax, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the corporate income tax, producing an effective federal burden of 15.825%; municipal trade tax is additional and commonly produces an aggregate burden of approximately 29% to 33%, depending on the municipal multiplier.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% capital income withholding tax, plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on the withholding tax, producing an effective domestic withholding burden of 26.375%. Relief may be available under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in Germany. Under the German participation exemption regime, qualifying corporate shareholders may generally obtain exemption from German dividend withholding tax, often requiring an advance exemption certificate or refund procedure. EU parent relief generally requires, among other conditions, a qualifying direct participation of at least 10% held for the statutory minimum period and satisfaction of anti-abuse requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For cross-border related-party transactions, a German Local File is generally required where annual consideration exceeds EUR 6 million for supplies of goods or EUR 600,000 for other transactions, including services, financing, royalties and similar dealings. A Master File is generally required for German entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 100 million. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and be furnished promptly on request, generally within 30 days; extraordinary business transactions may require documentation within 30 days after the transaction.

Economic substance scope: Germany has no standalone economic substance regulation regime equivalent to the substance regimes of certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant through the general anti-abuse rule in Section 42 of the Fiscal Code, transfer-pricing requirements, beneficial-ownership principles, anti-treaty-shopping rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, permanent-establishment standards and EU anti-avoidance measures. A German UG should maintain real decision-making, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, German accounting and records, commercial rationale, arm's-length agreements, functional control over risks and assets, and evidence that foreign recipients of treaty benefits are not conduit entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Tax Understatement

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion is generally punishable by imprisonment of up to five years or a criminal fine. Particularly serious cases may carry imprisonment from six months to ten years. Back taxes, interest and tax penalties remain payable. Voluntary disclosure relief is restricted and unavailable for certain completed or discovered offenses.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may incur personal tax liability for tax claims caused by intentional or grossly negligent breach of their duties, particularly failure to file or remit taxes. Criminal liability, asset seizure and enforcement may follow. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from criminal enforcement, arrest warrants, bail conditions or immigration measures.
Late Filing, Late Payment and Incorrect Accounting

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, late-payment surcharges, tax estimates, enforcement fees, interest and administrative penalties may apply. Persistent failures may support criminal or misdemeanor proceedings where intent or gross negligence is established.

Director personal liability: Directors must organize compliant bookkeeping, filing and payment controls. Personal liability may arise under the Fiscal Code and insolvency law for taxes that could have been paid but were not, especially after the company becomes insolvent.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Related-Party Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may make transfer-pricing adjustments, impose additional corporate income tax and trade tax, deny deductions, impose documentation-related surcharges and charge interest. A documentation surcharge may be imposed for missing, unusable or late documentation, subject to statutory rules and amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil and tax liability for deliberate or grossly negligent profit shifting, false records or failure to preserve documentation. Criminal tax-evasion exposure exists where the adjustment was intentionally concealed.
Treaty Abuse and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax may be reassessed at the full domestic rate, together with interest, penalties and potential criminal tax proceedings. Exemption certificates may be denied, revoked or rendered ineffective.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for withholding tax not deducted or remitted and may face criminal exposure for knowingly submitting false treaty documentation. Civil recovery may be pursued against the company and responsible persons.
VAT Carousel, Platform Revenue Suppression and Payment-Fraud Conduct

Statutory sanction: VAT assessments, interest, late-payment charges, tax-evasion prosecution, seizure of assets and payment-account restrictions may result. Digital platform records can be obtained or used by tax authorities and financial investigators.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unpaid VAT and may face criminal prosecution for intentional concealment. Insolvency-related payment restrictions can create additional personal exposure.
Anti-Money Laundering, Beneficial-Ownership and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the German AML framework can be substantial and, for serious or repeated breaches, may be linked to statutory maximum amounts or a percentage of annual turnover. Criminal money-laundering and sanctions offenses may carry imprisonment and fines. Assets may be frozen or confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal administrative or criminal liability where they participate in, authorize or recklessly facilitate the conduct. Travel restrictions, detention or passport measures may arise in criminal investigations but are not automatic.
Shell Company, Fraudulent Invoicing and Corporate Veil Abuse

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include tax reassessment, denial of deductions, asset confiscation, civil damages, insolvency clawbacks, fraud prosecution and potential imprisonment. The UG's limited liability does not protect fraudulent or unlawful conduct.

Director personal liability: Courts may impose personal liability based on statutory director duties, wrongful trading, delayed insolvency filing, tort, fraud and unlawful distributions. The corporate veil is not routinely pierced, but deliberate abuse of the company form can expose directors and shareholders to direct claims.
Wrongful Trading and Delayed Insolvency Filing

Statutory sanction: German insolvency law generally requires filing without undue delay and no later than three weeks after illiquidity or six weeks after over-indebtedness, subject to statutory conditions. Breach may result in criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, director liability and clawback of transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for prohibited post-insolvency payments, tax and social-security failures, creditor losses and insolvency-estate claims. Criminal proceedings may include asset seizure and restrictions on travel or management activity.
Unlawful Profit Distribution and UG Reserve Breach

Statutory sanction: Distributions may be repayable, tax reclassified as hidden profit distributions, and treated as withholding-tax events. Directors may face shareholder, creditor and insolvency-estate claims.

Director personal liability: Managing directors may be personally liable for unlawful payments, breach of capital-maintenance rules, withholding-tax failures and losses caused to the UG or its creditors.
Unlicensed or Mischaracterized Gambling Activity

Statutory sanction: The requested social-casino scope may not require a gambling license if users cannot wager money or obtain monetary value, but factual functionality controls. Unlicensed gambling may lead to prohibition orders, payment blocking, administrative or criminal penalties, confiscation and tax consequences.
