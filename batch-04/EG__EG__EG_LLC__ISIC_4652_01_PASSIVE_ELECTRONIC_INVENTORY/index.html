Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a unified Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) from the Egyptian Tax Authority by filing the company's incorporation contract, commercial registry extract, tax card application, legal entity identification data, and notarized power of attorney. Submission is made through the ETA Taxpayer Services Centers or the unified electronic tax portal. The TIN serves simultaneously for corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax, and stamp duty obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – Egypt is a unitary jurisdiction with no sub-state income tax distinction. An Egyptian LLC is a separate juridical person subject to corporate income tax at the headline rate of 22.5% on worldwide taxable income under Law No. 91 of 2005. No pass-through or transparent classification is available for LLCs. Foreign shareholders are additionally subject to withholding tax on dividends and certain payments under domestic law and applicable tax treaties.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Egyptian tax law. An Egyptian LLC is peremptorily classified as fully taxable. A group of wholly-owned Egyptian subsidiaries may elect to file consolidated tax returns if it satisfies the ETA's minimum ownership and holding-period conditions. Investment projects licensed under the Investment Law may elect a tax stabilization regime to fix their applicable tax rate and rules for a guaranteed period. No other entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable turnover of goods and services is the mandatory registration threshold; importers are required to register regardless of threshold, and voluntary registration is permitted for persons carrying on taxable economic activity even below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods, including passive electronic components such as multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors, is zero-rated for VAT purposes when the seller is a registered VAT person, the sale is correctly coded as an export in the ETA e-invoicing system, the goods physically depart from Egypt, and the exporter duly receives and repatriates the foreign currency proceeds. Directly related services (international freight, handling, insurable risk abroad) performed to bring exported goods to foreign markets also qualify for zero-rating where separately evidenced.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • ETA zero-rating export invoice (e-invoice with export code)

Egypt has a mandatory electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) regime administered by the ETA. Registered businesses must submit B2B invoices in real time to the ETA e-invoicing API in the prescribed JSON/XML format for fiscal clearance before issuance to the buyer. Only cleared invoices receive the ETA-generated QR code, unique invoice code, and validation stamp. Unclearable or rejected invoices are treated as non-compliant and, in practice, disqualify input VAT deduction. Egypt also operates a phased e-receipts mandate for B2C retail transactions.

The ETA may retrospectively claw back zero-rated export VAT at the standard 14% rate plus late-payment interest of 1.5% per month or a fraction thereof, up to a maximum of 100% of the assessed VAT, if the exporter fails to produce the full export documentation bundle, misses the 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline, or is found to have issued a zero-rated invoice without genuine physical export. Administrative fines may reach EGP 2,000,000 per violation under Law No. 67 of 2016. Fraudulent export zero-rating with an intent to obtain a tax refund is a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment and fines of up to EGP 5,000,000. The VAT audit look-back period is five years, extended to six years in cases of fraudulent intent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the 6 March 2024 exchange-rate unification, the EGP is a market-determined floating currency with current-account convertibility: import/trade payments, service fees, freight, and commissions execute at any licensed bank without CBE pre-approval, subject to electronic import-form (Form A) registration and underlying customs/shipping documents. Capital-account operations remain administered: inward FDI equity is freely registerable and convertible, but profit/dividend remittances require proof of the original CBE-registered inward FDI, audited accounts evidencing distributable profits, and a tax-clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority, creating a practical 1 to 4 week clearing cycle. The 2022-2023 FX liquidity backlog has largely been absorbed after the float, so a trade-active distributor faces low conversion risk; outbound capital repatriation, offshore borrowings, and portfolio transfers still require prior CBE approval, making EGP a hybrid regime: free current account, controlled capital account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods (HS 8532/8533 capacitors and resistors) must be repatriated and posted to an account at an Egyptian CBE-licensed bank within 90 days from the export customs certificate date, and reported on the CBE electronic exchange-control platform. The mandatory sale/conversion ratio was revoked after the 6 March 2024 float: the LLC may retain 100% of export USD/EUR balances in segregated accounts, apply them directly to offshore supplier invoices, freight, insurance, and commissions, and carry balances forward indefinitely without forced conversion into EGP. Any non-repatriated balance must be accompanied by documented justification (carrier delay, LC dispute, arbitration); failure triggers suspension of the exporter's import and LC facilities, blacklisting for export financing, and fines and penalties under Law No. 194 of 2020.

Central bank supervision: The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) is the sole monetary, FX, and exchange-control authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law). CBE-licensed commercial banks execute all FX operations and register every inflow/outflow in the CBE electronic exchange-control platform; the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) separately licenses non-bank fintech and payment institutions under the Payments Law No. 18 of 2019. No foreign currency transaction may bypass the local banking system: the Egyptian LLC must route all cross-border settlements through its CBE-licensed account banks.
