Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) – Companies Registration Department, One-Shop System, Cairo

Governing statute

Egypt Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended by Law No. 4 of 2009 and Law No. 94 of 2015; Articles 1–29 (formation and incorporation), Article 32 (board resolutions), Article 40 (registration with the Commercial Registry).

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association (Aqd al-Ta'sis wa al-Nizam al-Dakhili) submitted through the GAFI e-registration portal

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Law No. 159/1981; the capital must be fully subscribed and adequate to support the company's operations. Paid-up capital must be deposited in a temporary Egyptian bank account and a bank certificate submitted to GAFI; no escrow account is required except for in-kind capital contributions.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises must obtain a fire safety certificate from the General Administration of Civil Defense (Hay'at al-Difa' al-Madani) and a workplace safety clearance from the Ministry of Manpower and Migration. These clearances are required before commencing occupancy and must be renewed annually; inspections cover fire extinguishers, emergency exits, electrical load, and structural safety for the leased office.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Law No. 159/1981; the capital must be fully subscribed and adequate to support the company's operations. Paid-up capital must be deposited in a temporary Egyptian bank account and a bank certificate submitted to GAFI; no escrow account is required except for in-kind capital contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) at the Ministry of Finance, administered through the official ETA e-services portal and the National Single Window for Foreign Trade (NAFEZA); the E-Invoice and E-Receipt platform is operated by ETA for digital fiscal clearance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC (EG_LLC) obtains a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) by completing ETA registration via the e-services portal or at the competent Tax Office. Steps: (1) obtain Commercial Registration certificate from the Egyptian Commercial Registry and any required establishment license; (2) file the ETA registration application with the LLC incorporation deed, board resolution, power of attorney, certificate of residence for foreign shareholders, proof of registered office address or lease, bank account details, and any home-state TIN; (3) apply simultaneously for VAT registration if the estimated taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold; (4) undergo ETA validation and receive the unified TIN certificate and, if applicable, the VAT registration certificate; (5) enrol in the e-invoice and e-receipt system and obtain the API integration credentials before issuing taxable invoices.

It is also a VAT person when taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold; the standard territorial scope is national Egypt-wide with no sub-national income tax distinction. — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC (EG_LLC) obtains a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) by completing ETA registration via the e-services portal or at the competent Tax Office. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed at entity level on its worldwide income, including revenue, capital gains, and interest under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005; the LLC is not treated as a pass-through entity. It is also a VAT person when taxable supplies exceed the registration threshold; the standard territorial scope is national Egypt-wide with no sub-national income tax distinction.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide US-style check-the-box elections or hybrid-mismatch flow-through elections for an LLC; an Egyptian LLC is per se a taxable corporation. Available statutory elections include: (i) consolidated tax filing for resident corporations meeting the ownership and control conditions under the Income Tax Law; (ii) voluntary VAT registration below the mandatory threshold; (iii) simplified SME or small-taxpayer VAT computation where eligible; (iv) investment and Special Economic Zone incentives under Investment Law No. 72/2017 and the Suez Canal Economic Zone framework; (v) arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation and depreciation method choices permitted by the ETA. No hybrid mismatch election is recognised; substance and the arm's-length principle govern all transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and EGP 1,000,000 of annual taxable supplies of services for mandatory VAT registration, with voluntary registration available below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when physically exported from Egypt to a destination outside Egypt under customs supervision and evidenced by customs export documentation. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a non-resident recipient for use and consumption outside Egypt, including advertising and media-buying services performed in Egypt but delivered to foreign advertisers for offshore audiences. The exporter must hold a valid VAT registration and export/import card, issue a compliant E-Invoice stating the 0% rate, maintain a signed export contract or service agreement, obtain transport and customs proof, and settle the sale through an Egyptian bank in foreign currency. If the service is supplied to, or consumed in, Egypt, the standard 14% VAT applies and reverse-charge rules may apply to imported digital advertising services.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued through the Egyptian Customs Authority / NAFEZA single window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or other recognised transport document evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian bank evidencing the receipt and credit of export proceeds in foreign exchange
  • Commercial invoice and export sale contract or purchase order
  • VAT E-Invoice with ETA-issued QR code and 0% rate
  • Certificate of origin or customs clearance certificate where required by the destination customs regime

Egypt mandates E-Invoice and E-Receipt digital fiscal clearance. Every VAT-registered taxpayer must integrate an approved ERP or invoicing system with the ETA via REST API and transmit each tax invoice to the ETA platform immediately upon issuance. The ETA validates the invoice and returns a unique invoice code and QR code that constitute the fiscal digital stamp. No paper-only or non-stamped invoice is accepted for input VAT deduction or export zero-rating. B2C receipts are similarly transmitted under the E-Receipt regime. Taxpayers must obtain API credentials and complete technical integration before issuing any commercial invoice.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt cross-check customs export records, shipping manifests, E-Invoice data, and bank foreign-exchange ledgers. The 0% VAT rate is provisional until the export and the inward remittance are fully substantiated. Failure to obtain or maintain the prescribed documentation, or failure to repatriate export proceeds in foreign currency within the statutory period, results in withdrawal of the zero-rating, retrospective assessment of 14% VAT on the export value, delay interest, and administrative penalties. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, falsified customs or banking documents, or collusive invoicing expose the taxpayer to tax evasion proceedings under Law No. 67/2016, including VAT clawback, penal fines, prohibition from the E-Invoice and export facilitation systems, and imprisonment for responsible officers in wilful cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified free-floating exchange-rate regime as consolidated on 6 March 2024. Current-account convertibility is broadly available for bona fide trade, service, and digital-advertising disbursements once documentary evidence (contracts, tax invoices, statement of purpose) is presented to an authorized bank. Capital-account convertibility remains conditional: inbound foreign capital injections, profit repatriations, dividend remittances, and inter-company loan reparations are permitted but subject to CBE declaration/reporting, proof of foreign-fund registration, tax clearance, and the entity's good-standing in the Commercial Registry and Tax Authority databases. The practical hurdles are documentary completeness, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and the historical reliance on CBE registration of the original inbound capital to guarantee the future right of outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE instructions applicable to service exporters and revenue-generating digital-media/affiliate operations, the full value of export proceeds must be repatriated into Egypt exclusively through an authorized CBE-licensed bank within the contractual payment deadline, and in any event no later than 120 days from the date of the underlying export invoice or service-completion date, unless the bank has approved a documented deferral. The proceeds must be credited to the exporter's Egyptian bank account; historically, CBE circulars imposed mandatory conversion ratios ranging from 15% to 50% during the 2022-2023 FX rationing period. Following the 6 March 2024 float, the blanket mandatory conversion/surrender ratio is no longer codified; instead, exporters retain the proceeds in foreign currency or convert them to Egyptian pounds pursuant to a written agreement with their bank, with 100% of proceeds nonetheless required to flow through the authorized banking channel.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), together with the Foreign Exchange Committee and the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU). The CBE exercises exclusive authority over licensing of banks, foreign-exchange operations, capital-flow monitoring, and clearing system supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate, solely as auxiliary disbursement rails. Egyptian law does not currently license foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) or offshore digital banks (Wise, Mercury, Payoneer) to hold statutory corporate balances or to receive export/inbound capital for an Egyptian LLC. The CBE mandates that all export proceeds and foreign-capital inflows be credited through a CBE-licensed bank. Offshore EMIs may only be used for ancillary outbound payouts to affiliate partners or adventory platforms if the Egyptian bank is informed, the flows appear in the reconciled ledger, and the EMI counterparty satisfies the bank's AML/CFT assessment; they cannot replace the local primary banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign shareholder executes a SWIFT MT103 transfer in USD or EUR to the Egyptian LLC's account at the designated CBE-licensed bank, using the payment purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment under Law No. 72 of 2017' or 'Capital Increase / Share Subscription', accompanied by the shareholders' resolution, updated Articles of Association, tax-clearance certificates, and a CBE registration form completed by the bank. The inbound capital must be logged with the CBE to confer registered status and preserve the legal right of future profit and capital repatriation. Wire verification includes the bank's cross-check against the Commercial Registry name, UBO data, and an incoming-funds confirmation letter issued to the corporate client. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Per Egyptian Ministerial Decree No. 110/2018, contemporaneous Transfer Pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions between the Egyptian LLC and related parties exceeds EGP 6,000,000. The Master File may also be required for large multinational groups or upon ETA request; Country-by-Country Reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EGP 3,500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime comparable to certain Gulf jurisdictions. Nevertheless, Law No. 91/2005 contains general anti-abuse rules, and the ETA and Egyptian courts will disregard artificial or sham arrangements lacking real economic substance. An affiliate media-buying LLC must have genuine local management, operational staff, physical office or identifiable place of management, commercial contracts, books, bank accounts, and decision-making in Egypt. Without substance, treaty benefits, DTA relief, deduction claims, and the separate legal personality of the LLC may be challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 and the Egyptian Penal Code, tax evasion triggers recovery of the evaded tax, the 2% monthly delay surcharge, monetary fines, and imprisonment. Penalties typically range from fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 100,000 plus up to five years' imprisonment for fraudulent statements, with the ETA also empowered to seize bank accounts and assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns or knowingly direct fraudulent transactions are jointly and severally liable. Corporate veil piercing is available where the LLC is used as an instrument of fraud; directors risk personal asset attachment, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: The ETA and commercial registry may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the arrangement, disallow deductions, and impose CIT at the full 22.5% on a gross-revenue or deemed-profit basis. Administrative dissolution of the LLC and revocation of the commercial registration are possible, with fines under Egyptian company and tax law.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors are exposed to personal recovery of unpaid taxes and fines where the corporate form is a façade. Travel bans and asset freezes are routinely used in Egyptian tax enforcement against directors; criminal liability for aiding tax evasion can result in imprisonment.
AML / Money Laundering in Media-Buying Fund Flows

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian Law No. 80/2016 (Anti-Money Laundering), penalties include imprisonment of up to 7 years, monetary fines up to EGP 1,000,000, freezing/seizure of funds, and confiscation of the proceeds and instrumentalities of the unlawful transaction. The Financial Regulatory Authority may also revoke permits and impose administrative debarment.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are personally liable for willful failure to comply with KYC and suspicious-transaction reporting duties. Conviction carries imprisonment, permanent travel bans, debarment from board service, and personal confiscation of assets.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Impermissible Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The ETA may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, disallow intra-group deductions, recharacterize loans/royalties, and impose additional CIT of 22.5% plus the 2% monthly delay surcharge and fixed administrative fines. Withholding tax that should have been withheld is collected from the Egyptian LLC as agent, with statutory penalties for late remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to withhold or remit statutory WHT are personally liable for the unremitted tax. Deliberate TP documentation destruction or false benchmarking exposes directors to criminal tax evasion liability, travel bans, and imprisonment; a director's personal assets may be attached to satisfy the LLC's tax debt where the corporate veil is pierced.
