Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — central portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (e-services platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg and www.invoice.eta.gov.eg for e-invoicing).

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor forming an Egyptian LLC must first complete commercial registration with GAFI/Commercial Registry, lease and safety-lock approval, then submit the certified MoA, AOA, board resolution, power of attorney, passport copies, and ER extract to the ETA's Registration and Tax Card Department. ETA issues the corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT ID simultaneously where annual supplies are expected to exceed EGP 500,000. Non-resident contracts without a fixed establishment may appoint a tax representative and register on the ETA electronic portal; a specific VAT ID for foreign service providers is available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections in Egypt. A 90%-owned Egyptian subsidiary may elect with the parent company to form a consolidated tax group for CIT purposes (Article 61, Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005), subject to ETA approval. Eligible LLCs may also elect simplified small-taxpayer VAT treatment if turnover remains below the statutory threshold. Foreign branches may elect to be taxed as deemed separate entities limited to Egyptian-source income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies of goods and services; mandatory VAT registration accrues as soon as the threshold is met or exceeded, including intra-Group supplies by foreign contractors.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Egyptian VAT zero-rating is available for: (i) tangible goods physically exported from Egypt within 180 days; and (ii) services supplied directly to a non-resident and wholly used outside Egypt, provided the services do not relate to immovable property located inside Egypt. Cryogenic stainless steel piping and insulation installation performed at an LNG receiving terminal on Egyptian territory is a construction/land-attached service and is generally standard-rated at 14%, unless the terminal is within a designated Special Economic Zone or Free Zone with customs and VAT privileges. Zero-rating on exported materials incorporated in the piping works can be claimed if title passes to a foreign customer and the goods physically depart through Egyptian customs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Shadad / Single Customs Document) stamped by customs authority
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading (to order or consigned to buyer) or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign currency credit advice confirmed by an Egyptian bank) evidencing full receipt of export proceeds

Mandatory phased electronic invoicing for ETA-registered taxable persons. B2B e-invoices are transmitted in XML/JSON to the ETA's central e-invoicing clearing system (CAMS) using ETA-approved APIs or secure portal; each invoice is validated in real time and returned with a unique fiscal code, hash, QR code and digital signature of the issuer. Unregistered e-invoices may be treated as concealed supplies and penalised under Law No. 67/2016.

Advanced export zero-rating in excess of actual entitlement is treated as tax evasion. The ETA reserves a five-year reassessment period; a zero-rated claim is clawed back in full where documentation is incomplete or conversion of export proceeds is not made within 180 days. Penalties include default interest at 1.5% per month on unreported VAT, administrative fines from EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 per fraudulent invoice, and criminal sanctions (imprisonment) for intentional evasion; future zero-rating and refunds are suspended until the clawback is settled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt observes IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility and, following the March 2024 comprehensive foreign-exchange liberalisation, operates a unified market-determined EGP exchange rate with no surrender ratio and no administrative FX rationing; genuine current-account flows (trade invoices, EPC progress payments, service fees, dividends based on audited results) are freely convertible through regulated banks against a documentary-compliance package. The capital account remains administratively controlled: inward equity, shareholder loans, and external borrowings must enter through an Egyptian bank, be registered with the CBE (inward-transfer certificate) and GAFI as applicable, and be aligned to the correct balance-of-payments purpose code before utilisation; outward dividends and profit remittances face no quota but require audited financial statements, a final tax clearance certificate, supporting board resolutions, and a reconciliatory audit trail. For the LNG-receiving-terminal piping entity, practical friction points are remitter-invoice alignment, services-export classification of milestone revenues, and verified onshore arrival of foreign currency within the statutory repatriation window.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Repatriation timelines: merchandise export proceeds must be credited to an Egyptian bank account within 24 months of shipment/customs-export date; services-export proceeds (cryogenic piping, installation milestones, engineering services invoices) must be credited within 180 days of the service-rendered date or certified milestone invoice. Mandatory conversion ratio: Egypt applies a 0% surrender ratio under the post-2016 and post-March-2024 regimes — 100% of export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, converted at the exporter's discretion at prevailing market rates, and applied to permitted FX payments. Compliance duties: the LLC must ensure the overseas EPC contractor remits the exact contractual value by SWIFT to the designated USD account, secure the bank's inward-transfer voucher referencing project code EG__EG__EG_LLC__ISIC_4322_01_LNG_RECEIVING_TERMINAL_PIPING, and report any overdue receipts through the bank before the statutory deadline; non-repatriation is an exchange-control violation exposing the LLC to statutory penalties, FX-account restrictions, and adverse KYC re-rating.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary authority, banking supervisor, and foreign-exchange control regulator of the Arab Republic of Egypt, exercising powers under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020, and Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended by Law No. 8 of 2020). All licensed Egyptian commercial banks operate as statutory foreign-exchange dealers and CBE-appointed agents for exchange-control administration; the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Egyptian Tax Authority hold adjacent responsibilities for FDI registration and tax clearance. The CBE Board issues binding circulars covering transfer purpose codes, export-proceeds repatriation, capital-account registration, and the authorisation of treasury derivatives, making the banking system the exclusive conduit for cross-border liquidity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% flat corporate income tax on net taxable profits of resident companies and permanent establishments, as governed by Egypt Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 as amended.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on cash and in-kind profit distributions made by an Egyptian company to non-resident shareholders; resident corporate shareholders may benefit from participation relief or same-country treatment; distributions from exempt Free Zone operations may be free of dividend withholding where the distributed profits were never taxable in Egypt.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 65 must be filed within four months from the close of the fiscal year. For an LLC with a 31 December fiscal year-end, the statutory deadline is 30 April of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties reaches or exceeds EGP 6,000,000 in the fiscal year; Master File obligations attach where the multinational group has consolidated annual revenue of approximately EGP 500,000,000 and the Egyptian entity has controlled transactions above the local file threshold; a TP disclosure form must accompany Form 65 even below the file threshold where any controlled transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations code in the manner of some MENA jurisdictions, but the ETA and judicial doctrine apply equivalent substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and GAAR principles under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 and Egyptian jurisprudence. An EG LLC that holds a cryogenic LNG piping contract but lacks real staff, premises, bank accounts, equipment, project records, and risk-bearing capacity will be treated as a paper shell; treaty claims will be denied, intercompany costs will be disallowed, and under the Companies Law and Tax Procedures Law directors or beneficial owners may be jointly liable for the resulting tax, penalties, and contract debts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to five years, fines up to five times the evaded tax, reassessment of the full active tax at 22.5%, monthly late-payment surcharge at 1.5%, administrative penalty up to 100% of tax difference, and revocation of tax clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors, finance managers, or legal representatives who sign false returns or knowingly direct concealment are jointly and severally liable to the ETA for tax, penalties, and interest; criminal conviction triggers direct arrest, travel bans, asset freezes, and civil restitution through Egyptian courts.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ETA upward adjustment to arm's-length taxable income, additional 22.5% corporate tax, 2% monthly delay surcharge on the adjusted tax (maximum 50%), TP documentation fines, possible denial of related-party deductions, and publication of a taxpayer risk profile for future audits.

Director personal liability: Executives who sign false TP documentation or approve backdated intercompany agreements may be personally assessed for unpaid tax; Egyptian law allows attachment of personal assets and travel bans for tax debts; willful transfer pricing fraud is treated as criminal tax evasion with imprisonment.
