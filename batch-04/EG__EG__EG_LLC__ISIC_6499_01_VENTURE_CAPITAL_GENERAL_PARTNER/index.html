Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for LLCs under Law No. 4 of 2020; subscribed capital must be fully allocated among partners. The paid-up portion must be deposited in a licensed Egyptian bank account held in the company's name and evidenced by a bank capital certificate (escrow/blocking) before registration; FRA may impose a higher minimum contributed capital threshold for licensed venture-capital GP entities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; e-filing, VAT, and e-invoicing portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) incorporate via GAFI/Commercial Registry to obtain the commercial register certificate; (2) submit the ETA Tax Card (TIN) application with the authenticated articles of association, commercial registration, lease/ownership proof of registered office, and board resolution; (3) after TIN issuance, register on the ETA e-portal for e-filing, VAT, payroll, and e-invoicing, obtaining operator credentials and digital signature certificates; (4) complete mandatory export/bank account notifications for hard-currency proceeds. A non-resident entity without an Egyptian permanent establishment is not eligible for a full TIN and is registered only on the non-resident withholding-tax ledger.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; an Egyptian resident LLC is subject to Corporate Income Tax under Law No. 91/2005 on worldwide income at 22.5%; VC GP capital gains and management fees are taxed at the entity level with no pass-through regime; listed-EGX securities gains bear 10% under enacted capital-gains rules where applicable. — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) incorporate via GAFI/Commercial Registry to obtain the commercial register certificate; (2) submit the ETA Tax Card (TIN) application with the authenticated articles of association, commercial registration, lease/ownership proof of registered office, and board resolution; (3) after TIN issuance, register on the ETA e-portal for e-filing, VAT, payroll, and e-invoicing, obtaining operator credentials and digital signature certificates; (4) complete mandatory export/bank account notifications for hard-currency proceeds. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; an Egyptian resident LLC is subject to Corporate Income Tax under Law No. 91/2005 on worldwide income at 22.5%; VC GP capital gains and management fees are taxed at the entity level with no pass-through regime; listed-EGX securities gains bear 10% under enacted capital-gains rules where applicable.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Egyptian law; LLCs are mandatorily taxable as separate juridical persons. Available elections are limited to: (a) asset depreciation method (straight-line vs. declining-balance) per ministerial guidance; (b) VAT input-credit pro-rata method for mixed-use services; (c) Free Zone investment approval under Investment Law No. 72/2017, which may exempt project profits, imports, and supplies from tax/VAT in qualifying zones; (d) unilateral foreign tax credit election under Article 29 of Law No. 91/2005 for treaty/non-treaty relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services (mandatory registration); voluntary registration available from EGP 50,000 turnover; a VC GP deriving overseas management/advisory fees is a service exporter and should register immediately regardless of threshold to claim zero-rating and input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT where the goods are physically exported within 90 days from the date of supply and supported by a certified customs export declaration. Services exports (including VC GP management fees, advisory, and offshore fund administration) qualify for 0% where the beneficiary is outside Egypt, the services are performed and economically consumed outside Egypt, and the supplier holds documentary evidence of non-resident enjoyment; services rendered to Egyptian-resident investors or fund vehicles remain subject to 14% VAT.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Clean Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing physical exportation
  • Signed commercial invoice (English/Arabic) matching the ETA export VAT schedule
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank Form C35 / ARC) proving receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Service agreement and beneficiary/utilization certificate for offshore services
  • ETA e-invoice export schedule and exchange-control undertaking confirming repatriation

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing (B2B) and e-receipts (B2C) via the ETA system, phased to all VAT-registered taxpayers. Taxpayers must transmit structured JSON/XML invoices through the ETA REST API or official e-portal; the ETA performs fiscal-clearance validation and returns a 26-digit UUID, hash, and QR code before the invoice is legally valid. Integration requires an ETA-certified accounting system or fiscal stamping device; non-cleared invoices are treated as non-issued and deny input VAT recovery, with per-invoice penalties.

ETA export-refund audits triangulate customs export data, bank inward remittance records, and e-invoice clearances. On disallowance, VAT is clawed back at 14% plus a monthly late-payment charge of 2% of tax due (capped at 100%) and an evasion penalty of twice the evaded tax under Article 72 of VAT Law 67/2016. Forged or fraudulent export certificates trigger criminal liability (up to 5 years' imprisonment), fines of EGP 5,000–50,000 per counterfeit invoice, taxpayer blacklisting with mandatory physical pre-shipment inspection, and income-tax recomputation adding back unrepatriated export turnover at 22.5% with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the unified currency float of 6 March 2024, the Egyptian Pound is de facto convertible for genuine current-account and documented capital-account operations transacted through CBE-licensed banks; no prior CBE approval is required for inbound capital calls by non-resident investors, nor for profit, dividend, or carried-interest remittance, provided the bank's documentary threshold is met (audited financial statements, Limited Partnership Agreement, board distribution resolutions, and tax clearance where statutory withholding applies). Residual hurdles are administrative rather than exchange-control restrictions: Arabic-notarised constitutional documents, apostilled powers of attorney, SWIFT-verifiable remitter identity, and bank-level economic-substance checks. The Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 expressly guarantees the repatriation of invested capital and returns through licensed banks without a transfer licence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must repatriate 100% of contractually earned export proceeds to an Egyptian licensed bank within 120 days from the bill-of-lading date, or the contractual payment date where authorised by the CBE; services remuneration is remitted by invoice or contract date. Since the 2016 flotation and subsequent 2024 liberalisation, there is no mandatory conversion ratio into EGP, and proceeds may be held indefinitely in the exporter's foreign-currency account. The EG VC GP is categorically not an exporter, yet any foreign-origin fee or return flow it receives is subject to the same repatriation logic: full receipt via an Egyptian bank, 0% statutory surrender, and strict underlying-document retention.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole foreign-exchange and monetary authority exercising delegated powers under the Foreign Exchange Act No. 194 of 2020 and Law No. 162 of 1951.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for any Egypt-domiciled treasury primary rail. CBE AML regulations require all resident-entity commercial receipts and disbursements to settle through a CBE-licensed bank; offshore digital-only EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business) are not supervised banks in Egypt, cannot issue Egyptian IBANs, and cannot legally collect capital calls on behalf of the EG LLC. Their only permissible role is ancillary, such as a non-Egyptian GP-affiliate vehicle funding its own overseas operational overheads; such EMIs must never hold investor money destined for Egypt, nor make disbursements to Egyptian beneficiaries, as that would constitute unlicensed money-transmission activity.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

UBO structure involving an offshore discretionary trust or foundation in the fund's LP stack with unidentified ultimate beneficiaries

Root cause: UBO structure involving an offshore discretionary trust or foundation in the fund's LP stack with unidentified ultimate beneficiaries

Defense evidence: Full trust deed and letter of wishes disclosed to the bank, tax-residency certificates of settlors and beneficiaries, FATCA/CRS self-certification forms, OFAC/EU/UN sanctions-screening clearance reports, PEP screening certificates, and a legal opinion from Egyptian counsel confirming lawful capacity to hold LP interests under Law No. 80 of 2002.
Source of funds for capital calls flagged as private wealth of the general partner without an audited trail

Root cause: Source of funds for capital calls flagged as private wealth of the general partner without an audited trail

Defense evidence: Three years of audited financial statements for each LP, bank reference letters from top-tier correspondent banks, source-of-wealth declarations, sworn capital-call schedules, and the executed Limited Partnership Agreement together with fund-level subscription documents.
Commingling risk — the GP seeks to hold both LP capital-call proceeds and its own operating fees in a single account

Root cause: Commingling risk — the GP seeks to hold both LP capital-call proceeds and its own operating fees in a single account

Defense evidence: Dual-account architecture evidencing a segregated Capital Escrow Account versus the GP Operational Account, a fund administration agreement with annual NAV reports, audited fund-level financial statements, and the LPA cash-flow waterfall section governing management fees and expenses.
Non-resident director or beneficial owner matches a politically exposed person (PEP) indicator or derives from a high-risk jurisdiction

Root cause: Non-resident director or beneficial owner matches a politically exposed person (PEP) indicator or derives from a high-risk jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Independent PEP search report, sworn no-PEP declaration, official source-of-wealth statement, passport copies with travel history, tax-residency certificates (Form T), and a no-criminal-record certificate from the country of origin.
Perceived lack of economic substance — registered office only, no local payroll, and no resident manager profile

Root cause: Perceived lack of economic substance — registered office only, no local payroll, and no resident manager profile

Defense evidence: GAFI investment certificate, three-year local lease agreement, local payroll records, Egypt-domiciled audited financial statements, Commercial Registry extract, and the appointment of a resident compliance officer for the fund management activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

LP capital calls arrive via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 into the EG LLC's segregated USD Foreign Currency Account at the selected Egyptian bank; the wire narrative must cite the fund's legal name, the capital-call notice reference, and the business purpose declaration according to the bank's CBE-compliant transaction classification, such as 'Foreign Direct Investment — Capital Subscription'. Supporting the transfer, the GP submits the capital-call notice, an LP schedule with committed and called amounts, the executed LPA, and sanctions and UBO screening for each remitter; conversion to EGP, when required for local portfolio deployment, is executed at the onshore unified market rate with no CBE approval.Service-based outbound payments (legal, fund administration, cloud, marketing, audit) are cleared through SWIFT MT103 supported by the underlying invoice or contract and, where statutory withholding applies, a cleared WHT certificate; goods-financing is executed under irrevocable documentary letters of credit (UCP600) or documentary collections (URC522). Distributions of management fees or carried interest to non-resident GP-affiliated persons are remitted on presentation of audited annual statutory accounts, board distribution minutes, the LPA waterfall schedule and, where applicable, a tax clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority; the standard withholding rate on management fees is 20%, subject to treaty relief claims.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP FX forward contracts (tenor up to 12 months) transacted under ISDA documentation with a CBE-licensed bank, documentary proof of an underlying current-account or capital-account exposure.Onshore USD/EGP FX swaps employed for tenor-matching between LP capital-call drawings and Egyptian portfolio round disbursement dates.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (USD floating versus EGP fixed) permitted for matched-funding structures with long-term EGP liabilities, subject to bilateral ISDA/CSA credit support and CBE market-making reporting.Offshore non-deliverable forwards on EGP quoted in the London or New York interbank market; these are not regulated by the CBE and cannot be booked as the entity's sole hedge in Egypt, but they may be used at the fund-group level to hedge translated-portfolio currency exposure.
