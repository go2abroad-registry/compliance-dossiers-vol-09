Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - One-Stop Shop (Investor Service Center), acting jointly with the Commercial Registry under the Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade.

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, Articles 83-97 (Limited Liability Companies), as amended by Law No. 94 of 2005 and Law No. 145 of 2019; Executive Regulations issued by Ministerial Decree No. 96 of 1982 (as amended); Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, Articles 22-27.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association (Aqd Al-Taasis / Nizam Al-Shirkah) authenticated by the Notary Public Authority and submitted to the GAFI One-Stop Shop for issuance of the Commercial Registration Certificate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a trading/re-invoicing LLC under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and current GAFI practice; the founders determine the capital in the Articles of Association. Any subscribed cash capital is deposited into a temporary bank account opened in the company's name, and a bank capital-deposit certificate is filed with GAFI; no escrowed or blocked minimum capital is mandated for this non-regulated commercial activity.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety (Civil Defense) Certificate from the Ministry of Interior Civil Defense Administration for the registered office and any ancillary storage area; structural safety inspection under Building Law No. 119 of 2008; occupational health clearance and labor social insurance enrollment under Social Insurance Law No. 148 of 2019.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a trading/re-invoicing LLC under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and current GAFI practice; the founders determine the capital in the Articles of Association. Any subscribed cash capital is deposited into a temporary bank account opened in the company's name, and a bank capital-deposit certificate is filed with GAFI; no escrowed or blocked minimum capital is mandated for this non-regulated commercial activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Cairo, Egypt. Unified national portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipts clearing platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; non-resident registration desk and taxpayer service center at ETA headquarters (Mohandessin).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) Incorporate at GAFI (General Authority for Investment) and complete Commercial Registry (Sijil) formalities with notarized Arabic Articles of Association; (2) obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate and open a corporate bank account with an Egyptian authorized bank; (3) file ETA registration forms through the unified portal to obtain the TIN (File Number) and electronic tax card within 30 days of commercial registration; (4) register for VAT on ETA Form No. 101 within 30 days of any month in which the value of taxable supplies exceeds EGP 500,000, or immediately if export activity is expected to exceed the threshold; (5) complete mandatory e-invoicing onboarding: issue digital signature certificate, register API credentials (OAuth2), integrate ERP with the ETA sandbox, and obtain the Fiscal Code stamp; (6) appoint a resident tax representative for procedural notifications if the LLC has no local office. The TIN is the single identity key for CIT, VAT, stamp tax, and electronic invoice clearance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - An Egyptian LLC is mandatorily a resident corporate taxpayer under Law No. 91/2005 (Income Tax), Law No. 82/2020 (Tax Procedures), and Law No. 67/2016 (VAT). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - An Egyptian LLC is mandatorily a resident corporate taxpayer under Law No. 91/2005 (Income Tax), Law No. 82/2020 (Tax Procedures), and Law No. 67/2016 (VAT). No pass-through or check-the-box regime exists; the LLC is taxed on worldwide income at entity level.

Available election forms

Egyptian law provides no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; the LLC is a separate CIT and VAT taxpayer. Available statutory elections are limited to: (a) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 exemption threshold to secure input VAT refunds on export costs; (b) election of fiscal year for income tax purposes; (c) depreciation method election (straight-line or declining balance) subject to ETA approval; (d) 5-year tax loss carryforward election with ETA notification and documentation; (e) reverse-charge self-accounting for imported services is mandatory under Law 67/2016 Art. 22 for services consumed in Egypt and is not an optional election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services in Egypt; mandatory registration applies once turnover exceeds this threshold, while small taxpayers below the threshold are exempt but may voluntarily register; non-residents carrying on taxable activities in Egypt are subject to the same aggregated threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Law No. 67 of 2016, Art. 12 (goods) and Art. 13 (services), a 0% VAT rate applies to: (1) goods physically exported from Egypt that cross the Egyptian customs border under a valid customs export declaration (Bayan Khabari / A71); and (2) services rendered to non-residents where the services are consumed entirely outside Egypt and are not linked to taxable activities within Egypt. For a re-invoicing hub, the entity must hold the principal customs export declaration, the transport document evidencing departure from Egypt, and bank evidence of receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency. Pure offshore-to-offshore re-invoicing contracts in which goods never enter Egyptian customs territory fall outside the Egyptian VAT scope and require no ETA VAT filing, subject to corporate income tax permanent establishment analysis for the trading activity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Khabari / A71) certified by the Egyptian Customs Authority evidencing physical exit of the goods
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier evidencing Egypt as the shipment origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice from an Egyptian authorized bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Zero-rated commercial invoice, sale purchase agreement, and Incoterms statement
  • ETA e-invoice approved with Fiscal Code, together with cargo manifest and certificate of origin where re-exported goods are involved

Mandatory real-time electronic invoicing and e-receipts in Egypt (ETA Decision No. 550/2020 and subsequent technical rules, phased by taxpayer turnover and fully applicable to all VAT-registered taxpayers by FY 2023/2024). B2B and export invoices must be generated in the taxpayer's ERP, converted to the ETA XML/JSON schema, and submitted in real time via REST/SOAP APIs with OAuth2 authentication and digital signature certificates. The ETA 'Mossar' system validates schema, performs duplicate checks, cross-checks commercial registry and tax classification, and issues an approval with a 2D barcode 'Fiscal Code' that must appear on the final invoice. Any invoice rejected by the ETA is legally invalid for input VAT deduction and cannot support export zero-rating claims. B2C e-receipts are subject to the identical real-time clearance requirement under ETA Decision No. 371/2022. Export zero-rated invoices pass through the same electronic clearance gateway.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to ETA desk audits and field inspections under a 5-year assessment statute of limitations (6 years for fraud). Missing or contradictory customs, transport, or FX documentation triggers automatic recharacterization as a domestic taxable supply and assessment at 14% VAT plus a late-payment surcharge of 2% per month on the tax due under Law 67/2016 Art. 73. Deliberately false export documentation constitutes tax evasion under Law 67/2016 Art. 74 and Law 82/2020 Art. 52, punishable by imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years and/or a fine of EGP 50,000 to EGP 5,000,000 or 100% of the evaded tax, whichever is greater, together with retrospective clawback of all VAT refunded and denial of the zero-rate privilege. The Central Bank of Egypt may additionally impose penalties under Law No. 194 of 2020 Art. 35 for failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a liberalized managed-float exchange rate regime recognized under Law No. 194 of 2020 (the Central Bank and Banking System Law). Current account convertibility is generally free for trade and service transactions backed by documentary evidence. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, cross-border guarantees, and profit repatriation, are convertible subject to CBE registration, an authorized-dealer bank, and the absence of administrative suspension or sanction-flagging. Profit remittance by a re-invoicing LLC requires an outbound attestation from the tax authority (recent tax clearance), audited accounting evidence, and confirmation that the commercial invoice and customs documentation are internally consistent. Non-resident shareholders may repatriate dividends and capital gains, but the CBE may apply liquidity or documentation-based review when the bank's net open position or FX availability is constrained. Re-invoicing hubs must maintain complete trade-file documentation to avoid classification as an artificial arrangement lacking economic substance, which would trigger CBE anti-money-laundering and tax-base-erosion review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations, export proceeds from goods and services must be received in Egypt through an authorized bank within 90 days from the date of shipment for goods (extendable, with justification, to 4 months for certain bulk or deferred payment goods). There is no mandatory percentage conversion into Egyptian pounds under the managed-float regime; proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account at an authorized bank, sold on the interbank market, or used for eligible trade settlements. The re-invoicing LLC must ensure that each export shipment is covered by a single Electronic Import/Export Registration on the CBE/NAFEIZA trade platform, that the proceeds are remitted via SWIFT/telex in accordance with the shipment's export declaration, and that the receiving bank obtains a cumulative confirmation of proceeds for filing. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline exposes the entity to penalties, denial of banking services, and CBE blacklisting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Banking Supervision and Foreign Exchange Operations Department, supported by the Ministry of Finance and the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for registration and capital account purposes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Egypt does not recognize foreign electronic-money institution (EMI) licenses as standing alone; a foreign EMI cannot open a CBE-supervised settlement account or conduct payment services without obtaining a CBE license or partnering with a licensed Egyptian bank / payment service provider under the CBE's FinTech regulatory sandbox (RegLab). An offshore EMI may provide ancillary treasury aggregation or reporting software, but actual clearing, settlement, foreign exchange, and Documentary Credit issuance must pass through a CBE-authorized bank. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI for EGP flows or trade payments carries significant AML, sanctions, and banking-license risk and would be rejected during corporate KYC profiling.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing requires the shareholder or lender to remit foreign currency through a CBE-authorized bank in Egypt to a designated 'Capital Escrow' or 'Frozen Account' registered in the LLC's name. The remitting instruction must carry an accurate purpose code consistent with the underlying Palestinian-Origin, Gulf, or global capital contribution (foreign direct investment equity, shareholder loan, or pre-export finance). The receiving bank will require the LLC's board resolution approving the injection, the share subscription or facility agreement, a certified copy of the commercial registration and GAFI/Investment certification, and a UBO/source-of-funds declaration. The bank then performs a currency conversion at the interbank market rate (or credits the foreign-currency account) and, upon verified receipt, releases the funds into the operational account in accordance with the CBE's capital account regime. For loans, the facility must be registered with the CBE to secure future debt-service outbound transfers and to avoid loan-equity reclassification under thin capitalization rules.Outbound disbursements are executed through authorized banks using SWIFT MT103 (customer payment) with MT202 cover for interbank clearing, conventional Telegraphic Transfer instructions, and, for documentary trade, Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance L/Cs), standby L/Cs, or documentary collections (D/P and D/A). The re-invoicing LLC's payment instruction must be fully backed by the trade file: purchase order, final confirmed invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, customs entries, inspection report, and any CBE import/export registration reference. The bank will verify the underlying transaction, confirm the availability of foreign currency, and, where required, apply the CBE's margin or anti-avoidance checks for related-party payments. The LLC should use bank-integrated trade finance and host-to-host e-banking channels to capture full audit trails and ensure settlements clear on a same-day or next-day basis under Egyptian liquidity conditions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% on net taxable corporate profit (Law No. 91 of 2005, Art. 8 as amended by Law No. 96 of 2015)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final dividend withholding tax under Articles 56-57 of Law 91/2005, charged on cash and in-kind distributions of the LLC; the LLC must withhold at distribution and remit the tax to the ETA within the statutory WHT return deadline

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and full settlement within four months after the end of the fiscal year (a December year-end LLC must file by 30 April of the following year); the ETA may grant a 30-day extension upon written request and payment of estimated remaining tax

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing simultaneously with Form No. 19 (within four months after fiscal year-end); CbCR notification must be filed with the ETA e-CbCR module before the last day of the reporting fiscal year

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8 million of related-party transactions in any single annual transaction category (goods, services, financing, intangibles) triggers mandatory Master File and Local File preparation; multinational groups with consolidated group revenue at or above EGP 50 billion must additionally file Country-by-Country Report with the ETA

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the ETA enforces the Article 36 arm's length principle, general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR), and place-of-effective-management (POEM) analysis under Article 31 of Law 91/2005 to disregard or recharacterize transactions lacking commercial rationality; a re-invoicing LLC must evidence its own employees, premises, board control, trade documentation, and bank accounts in Egypt; otherwise tainted dividends, deemed PE income, denied treaty benefits, and reassessment may follow

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False or Suppressed Records

Statutory sanction: Article 133 of Law 91/2005: imprisonment for three to five years and a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000, plus recovery of the evaded tax and delay tax of 2% per month; confiscation of documents and equipment used in the evasion

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who signed false returns face joint and several liability for the unpaid CIT, uncollected WHT, and penalty surcharges; the ETA may issue a travel ban and refer the directors to criminal court; conviction results in disqualification from board positions
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA recharacterization of the full profit, disallowance of the related-party markup, delay tax of 2% per month on the adjusted tax, administrative penalty up to EGP 20,000, and a 5-year reassessment window extended to 6 years for willful concealment

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the adjusted tax difference where they directed the transfer pricing policy knowingly; an egregious margin may be reclassified as a disguised dividend, producing a 10% WHT assessment against the LLC and personal recovery against the approving manager
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of the GAAR and POEM rules re-attributing profits: the ETA may tax the entire re-invoicing margin at 22.5%, deny treaty benefits, impose 10% deemed-dividend WHT, and request the Commercial Registry to dissolve the company for false status declarations

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing under Articles 49 and 73 of Companies Law No. 159 of 1981: directors, auditors, and signatory managers are jointly liable for company obligations when the company is used as a facade; travel restrictions and asset seizures may be ordered to secure tax claims
Withholding Tax Violations on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The LLC remains liable for the full withholding amount plus delay tax of 2% per month from the due date, an additional recovery fine of 20% of the uncollected amount, and criminal prosecution of the legal representative where the failure was deliberate

Director personal liability: The general manager and board signatories are joint and several debtors for the uncollected WHT; the ETA may issue charge orders on the LLC's bank accounts after 30 days of formal summons; imprisonment applies in cases of systematic distributions to shell shareholders
Anti-Money Laundering Violations (AML)

Statutory sanction: Articles 14 and 17 of AML Law No. 80 of 2002: imprisonment of three to seven years and a fine of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000, forfeiture of the instrumentalities and proceeds, and freezing of bank accounts; the EMLCU may suspend the trade license upon referral to the Public Prosecutor

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face imprisonment and personal fines if they knowingly execute or authorize suspicious trade transactions; the court may impose a travel ban during the criminal investigation and order disqualification from any financial or commercial management functions in Egypt
