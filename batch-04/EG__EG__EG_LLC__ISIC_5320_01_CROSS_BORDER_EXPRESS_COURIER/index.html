Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the EG LLC is a resident corporate taxpayer under Law No. 91/2005, taxed on worldwide income at 22.5% CIT for fiscal periods beginning on or after 1 January 2020; non-residents are taxed only on Egyptian-source income. Territorial_Scope is therefore resident-worldwide, non-resident-Egypt-source. No pass-through or disregarded-entity status exists for LLCs. Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment (15% consolidated CIT and VAT relief) is available only to licensed projects inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) or designated economic zones; absent an SCZone license the standard regime applies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime under Egyptian law. The lawful elections available are: (1) consolidated tax filing for Egyptian corporate groups per Article 58 of Law 91/2005; (2) simplified small-business percentage-of-turnover taxation under Law No. 152/2020 for taxpayers with annual turnover up to EGP 20 million, including the 0.5%, 1%, and 2.25% revenue-based brackets; (3) Investment Law No. 72/2017 incentive elections locking in a fixed tax treatment and depreciation allowances for qualifying sectors; and (4) treaty-based hybrid-mismatch relief via Egypt's Multilateral Instrument ratification in force since 1 June 2021, which applies tie-breaker and branch-mismatch outcomes but does not create any corporate-level passthrough election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable value of goods and services (mandatory VAT registration); voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold from EGP 100,000 annual taxable turnover; mandatory e-invoicing enrollment applies progressively by ETA schedule once the mandated EGP 500,000 revenue threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from Egypt and to services performed for non-residents that are wholly consumed outside Egypt, including cross-border international transport of parcels, under Articles 12 and 24 of VAT Law No. 67/2016. An express courier may apply 0% on its Egyptian segment of international freight where the parcles leave Egyptian customs territory, the transport contract is with a non-resident principal, the courier fee is booked on an Egyptian export invoice, and the foreign-currency consideration is transferred into an Egyptian bank account. Domestic collection and last-mile delivery legs within Egypt remain taxable at 14% and must be separately scoped.

  • Customs Export Declaration as cleared by the Egyptian Customs Authority, proving physical exit of the consignments
  • Air Waybill or Master Bill of Lading and line-haul manifest covering the exported parcel set
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian authorized bank evidencing receipt of the courier fee in foreign currency
  • ETA-approved 0% export invoice and the monthly consolidated courier export statement
  • Commercial courier agreement or service contract with the non-resident client, plus customs exit logs for the express consolidation

Mandatory national e-invoicing since 15 November 2020 under MoF Decree No. 498/2020 for all VAT-registered entities above phase thresholds. Each e-invoice must be transmitted in XML/JSON to the ETA cloud platform in real time via the certified REST API; the ETA validates the invoice cryptographically, clears it, and returns a unique invoice UUID, a secure fiscal QR code, and a digital fingerprint that must be printed on the issued invoice. B2C sales are additionally reported through the e-receipt clearance module. The system is a cloud-based fiscal clearance mandate with no physical stamping; failed or missing submission triggers automatic rejection by the clearing engine, enhancement of the taxpayer risk score, suspension of the tax card, and the e-invoice non-compliance fine.

The ETA refund and export verification units conduct ex-post review of export zero-rating, matching customs exit data, airway bills, insurance, banking remittances, and e-invoice UUIDs. A claim that fails substantiation is clawed back at 14% VAT plus the Article 70 late-payment surcharge of 2% per month (capped at 100% of the tax due). Fraudulently issued zero-rate export invoices trigger criminal tax-evasion proceedings under Law No. 8/2016 with imprisonment and financial penalties of no less than EGP 100,000, suspension or revocation of the Tax Card, denial of the VAT refund file, and separate EGP 50,000 per forged or un-cleared e-invoice exposure under the e-invoicing decrees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt permits current-account convertibility for trade- and service-related payments, including express courier fees and logistics disbursements. Capital-account convertibility, however, remains administratively controlled: profit remittances, dividend distributions, and equity liquidation require documentary evidence of underlying transactions, a valid tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), and CBE-specified forms (inward FDI notification and outward remittance request). Under the 6 March 2024 exchange-rate liberalization and the IMF Extended Fund Facility framework, conversion delays have materially declined, yet banks still enforce a de facto pre-approval watchdog duty for outbound payments exceeding USD 50,000, including verification of invoices, customs declarations, and shipping manifests.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: 100% of export proceeds (including cross-border express courier receivables) must be repatriated into Egypt through a licensed bank within 4 months from the date of shipment (airway bill or customs export declaration date); an extension to 6 months is available only with CBE-approved exceptional justification. Mandatory conversion ratio: 100% — the full amount must either be converted into Egyptian pounds (EGP) under the official interbank rate or credited to a CBE-authorized foreign-currency account of the exporting entity at the receiving bank. Non-compliance attracts penalties under Law No. 102 of 1993 and potential suspension of the entity's importer/exporter banking facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — sole monetary authority and foreign exchange regulator, established under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), supplemented by Foreign Exchange Law No. 102 of 1993. The CBE administers all onshore FX operations, maintains the interbank USD/EGP fixing, and regulates payment, clearing, and settlement systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for domestic EGP clearing. Egypt does not authorize standalone non-bank electronic-money institutions (EMIs) to open local-currency accounts; non-bank payment service providers must register under Law No. 18 of 2019 and still require a sponsoring Tier-1 bank for settlement. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer cannot maintain EGP accounts and are not considered banks by the CBE; they may be used solely as supplementary USD disbursement rails, subject to CBE rules prohibiting EGP-denominated cross-border settlement outside licensed banks. Recommended architecture: use offshore EMIs only for low-value international payables; route all Egyptian receivables and statutory payments through the Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 credit from the foreign shareholder to the EG_LLC's USD or EGP escrow account at the receiving Tier-1 Egyptian bank, referencing CBE balance-of-payments purpose code 'Capital Account — Equity Injection'. The submitting bank requires the signed share-subscription agreement, board minutes of the EG_LLC, the CBE inward-FDI notification form, and the NTRA license to evidence lawful courier operations. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions for mandatory Local File preparation; Master File applies where the consolidated group revenue exceeds EGP 1,000,000,000; the TP return and full documentation must be made available to the ETA within 30 days of the CIT return due date.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies substance-over-form in tax administration and investment law compliance. Entities operating in free zones or SCZone must maintain locally domiciled management, active logistic assets, payroll, and booking capacity; otherwise the ETA, under its general anti-avoidance powers (Article 112 of Law No. 91/2005), may recharacterize intercompany payments and attribute profits to the Egyptian PE. Thin-capitalization rules disallow interest on shareholder loans where the debt-to-equity ratio exceeds 3:1 without commensurate business justification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for 2 to 5 years and a fine equal to three times the evaded tax amount; full collection of the tax with monthly delay penalties and potential liquidation of assets. Fraudulent invoicing may additionally trigger charges under the Egyptian Criminal Procedure Code and the Customs Law.

Director personal liability: Managing directors with signatory authority are jointly and severally liable for restitution, travel bans, passport holds, and seizure of personal assets; imprisonment is available for both the legal entity's authorized signatories and de facto managers.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the legal existence of the entity for tax purposes, shift all profits to the Egyptian activity, impose a 100% administrative penalty on understated tax, and revoke tax card, customs, or free-zone licenses; concurrent fines under the Investment Law may reach EGP 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to joint civil liability for the entire tax and penalty amount, disqualification from board membership, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent bookkeeping.
AML / Economic Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80 of 2002 on Anti-Money Laundering, as amended, fines ranging from EGP 200,000 to EGP 2,000,000 plus imprisonment of 1 to 5 years for natural persons; legal entities may be subject to closure orders, freezing of permits, and forfeiture of funds.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, confiscation of personal assets, placement on the terrorist/sanctions watchlist, international travel restrictions, and exclusion from banking services.
