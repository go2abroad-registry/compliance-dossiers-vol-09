Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian Limited Liability Company is a resident juridical person taxed at the entity level on worldwide income at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5%; small enterprises with annual turnover up to EGP 20 million are subject to preferential brackets of 10% on the first EGP 1 million and 15% on income up to EGP 20 million; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Egypt-source income subject to withholding; a non-resident entity without local incorporation is within the territorial tax net only through a permanent establishment in Egypt.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections: group tax consolidation for Egyptian subsidiaries where the parent holds at least 95% of capital, governed by Article 40 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and ministerial implementing decisions; SME preferential turnover-bracket treatment elected on meeting the EGP 20 million threshold; optional VAT cash-accounting regime for small taxable persons under Law No. 67 of 2016; treaty-based permanent establishment attribution elections are administrative rather than elective; Egypt applies thin-capitalization and CFC rules in line with OECD BEPS Action 2 and Action 3 with no domestic hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration arises when the aggregate annual value of taxable supplies exceeds EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is available from EGP 250,000 of annual taxable supplies; legal persons supplying zero-rated exports must register regardless of turnover once they exceed the mandatory threshold unless the ETA grants specific exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of tangible fertilizer goods (prilled urea and potassium chloride) physically delivered outside Egypt under contractual or direct shipment terms, provided the exporter is the VAT-registered seller, the goods leave Egypt under a valid customs export declaration, and the sales price is paid in freely convertible foreign currency; international transportation and ancillary export services supplied to non-residents in connection with exported fertilizer may also qualify; all conditions must be evidenced in the exporter's file as at the date of the supply; input VAT on goods and services attributable to exported volume is creditable, and any excess credit is refundable under ETA export refund rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with active CBE intervention. Current-account transactions, including genuine trade settlements, are de jure convertible once transacted through an authorized bank with complete supporting documents; capital-account operations, including FDI equity injection, shareholder loans, and profit or dividend repatriation, are convertible only where the funds entered Egypt exclusively through the banking system and were registered with GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Profit and dividend remittance requires, at minimum, a notarized board resolution, audited financial statements, a valid tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority, a GAFI-issued capital registration certificate, and satisfactory compliance screening by the authorized bank. In practice, remittance is feasible but operationally slow; treasury must reserve at least 30 to 45 days for bank compliance review and CBE liquidity cycles, and must avoid relying on offshore EMIs or non-banking channels that can permanently jeopardize convertibility rights.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE Export Proceeds Settlement Instructions, 100% of export receipts for physical commodities must be repatriated to an account at an authorized bank in Egypt within 6 months from the bill-of-lading date, with documentary extensions available on justifiable grounds such as LC tenor or force majeure. Under the baseline framework, there is no fixed statutory surrender quota: the exporter may retain proceeds in a foreign-currency account or convert to EGP at the interbank rate; however, CBE macroprudential circulars may, during hard-currency stress periods, impose temporary conversion requirements of up to 100% of repatriated proceeds within 5 business days. Treasury must therefore maintain a liquidity contingency plan under which all export collections are immediately swept into a designated USD account at an authorized bank and no export value remains offshore.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), exercising consolidated monetary, regulatory, and supervisory authority under the Banking and Central Bank Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 2020; the CBE licenses all authorized foreign-exchange dealers, operates the national payment and settlement infrastructure, and supervises every banking relationship relevant to foreign investment and commodity trade.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a primary or secondary clearing rail; legally not viable for trade settlement. The CBE regime requires all foreign-currency receipts and payments of Egyptian-resident entities to flow exclusively through CBE-licensed authorized banks. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, or similar platforms are neither licensed nor recognized as collection or settlement channels for export proceeds, import disbursements, or equity injections. Use of an offshore EMI would breach the Foreign Exchange Law, trigger automatic AML and sanctions flags at the priced local bank, and invalidate the imported-funds registration needed for future repatriation. An offshore EMI may support negligible pre-establishment expenses only and must be closed immediately after the Egyptian bank accounts are operational.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the remitter, being the named shareholder only, sends a SWIFT MT103 from a recognized commercial bank to the LLC USD foreign-currency account at the Egyptian authorized bank, with the payment narrative stating equity contribution under Law No. 72 of 2017 and referencing the GAFI file number and the Egyptian Commercial Registry number. The receiving bank completes the CBE Foreign Exchange Operations Declaration, known as Form A, and credits the USD account; afterwards the LLC may convert to EGP at the interbank rate. The capital must be registered as imported funds at GAFI before or within 30 days of receipt; no cash, no third-party payments, no interim wallets, and no email or EMI rails may be used; the consolidated evidence set, comprising the resolution, GAFI certificate, remittance advice, and statement annotations, must be archived for the full life of the investment to secure profit repatriation rights.Preferred clearing rail for fertilizer import and on-sale disbursements is the irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit opened by the Egyptian bank in favor of the supplier via SWIFT MT700, with payment against presentation of conforming documents through the advising and confirming banks; settlement is executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the Egyptian bank nostro account. Direct open-account SWIFT MT103 payment is permissible for goods only after Customs ACI registration and GOEIC import registration, with CBE Form A and the trade-pack evidence attached; banks treat the LC route as the compliance-safe default for the fertilizer-commodity profile. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary or secondary clearing rail; legally not viable for trade settlement. The CBE regime requires all foreign-currency receipts and payments of Egyptian-resident entities to flow exclusively through CBE-licensed authorized banks. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, or similar platforms are neither licensed nor recognized as collection or settlement channels for export proceeds, import disbursements, or equity injections. Use of an offshore EMI would breach the Foreign Exchange Law, trigger automatic AML and sanctions flags at the priced local bank, and invalidate the imported-funds registration needed for future repatriation. An offshore EMI may support negligible pre-establishment expenses only and must be closed immediately after the Egyptian bank accounts are operational.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where controlled transactions in a fiscal year exceed EGP 8 million. Master File mandatory where the group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 200 million and an Egyptian group member exceeds the EGP 8 million controlled transaction threshold. CbCR required where consolidated group revenue reaches EGP 500 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no separate ESR law; however, GAAR under Article 31 of Law 91/2005, substance-over-form jurisprudence, and the Multilateral Instrument treaty-abuse rule require genuine staff, premises, risk control, and real economic activity. Shell LLCs structured as conduit fertilizer traders are recharacterized and denied treaty and free-zone benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fake Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for one to five years and a fine of 200% to 300% of the evaded tax; civil interest at 20% per annum; publication of the conviction notice

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or fail to supervise the false invoicing are jointly liable for the evaded tax, penalties, and costs; travel bans and asset freezes apply.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA recharacterizes the entity as fiscally transparent, disallows DTA benefits, reassesses tax with monthly delay penalties up to 50%, imposes additional fines up to 100%, and may initiate company dissolution under the Companies Act

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face civil restitution of all unpaid tax, disqualification from board membership, and pierce-of-liability proceedings against personal assets.
Anti-Money Laundering / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80 of 2002, imprisonment for three to fifteen years and fines from EGP 100,000 to EGP 2,000,000; forfeiture of cargo and vessels; mandatory revocation of import/export licenses

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally criminally prosecuted, face international travel bans, and their personal bank accounts and properties are subject to seizure.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA reconstructs profits using TNMM or CUP, assesses additional tax at 22.5%, applies monthly late-payment penalties of 5% up to 50%, and levies a false-documentation fine of up to 30% of the adjusted taxable amount

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties where contemporaneous TP documentation is absent; willful distortion triggers criminal proceedings.
