Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; e-services portal at https://eservices.eta.gov.eg and e-invoicing clearance portal at https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Egyptian LLC under the Companies Law (Law 179/2020), file tax registration Form 1 with the local ETA tax office within 30 days to obtain a Tax Card (Bitaqa Dharibya). Register for VAT using Form 801/802 if annual taxable turnover reaches EGP 500,000 or voluntarily at EGP 150,000. For a foreign enterprise without a local entity, appoint a tax representative and obtain a VAT registration number for importer of record activities; all registrations are completed via the ETA e-services portal and generate a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Upon incorporation of the Egyptian LLC under the Companies Law (Law 179/2020), file tax registration Form 1 with the local ETA tax office within 30 days to obtain a Tax Card (Bitaqa Dharibya). Register for VAT using Form 801/802 if annual taxable turnover reaches EGP 500,000 or voluntarily at EGP 150,000. For a foreign enterprise without a local entity, appoint a tax representative and obtain a VAT registration number for importer of record activities; all registrations are completed via the ETA e-services portal and generate a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available under Egyptian law. Applicable elections include: (i) fiscal unity (tax group) under Article 61 of Income Tax Law 91/2005 for companies with ≥95% shareholding; (ii) simplified final taxation for small businesses under Law 152/2020; (iii) voluntary VAT registration when below the threshold; and (iv) Free Zone incentives under Law 83/2002 (elected, not automatic).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory); voluntary registration from EGP 150,000. Registration is also required for any foreign supplier with an economic nexus in Egypt regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate VAT applies to cross-border overland trucking services that move goods into, out of, or through Egypt under TIR Carnets, when supplied to a non-resident carrier/principal, and supported by documented evidence that the transport begins or terminates outside Egypt; ancillary services physically rendered within Egypt but directly connected to exported goods are also zero-rated. The taxpayer must hold the TIR Carnet and export declarations issued by Egyptian Customs (Bayan Sahb).

  • TIR Carnet (official international customs transit document with guarantee chain)
  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Sahb / ACP statement)
  • CMR Waybill (Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage of Goods by Road)
  • Bill of Lading or Transit Transport Document (where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank statement evidencing foreign currency receipt from the freight service)

Mandatory e-invoicing for VAT-registered suppliers through the Farid system; invoices must be cleared by the ETA digital stamping engine in JSON/XML format via API or portal (invoicing.eta.gov.eg). Each invoice receives a unique serial number (UUID) and QR code; e-receipts are separately mandated for B2C transactions. No unsigned paper invoices may be issued by an integrated taxpayer.

Export zero-rating is subject to stringent documentary audit by the ETA. If documentation is missing or false, the claimed 0% rate is reversed and the full 14% VAT is assessed, plus late-payment interest, an additional 30% penalty for misstatement, and criminal fines up to EGP 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for tax evasion under Law 67/2016. Retrospective clawbacks cover six years of assessments, and repeat offenders may be removed from the TIR/zero-rating registers.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) operates under a unified, market-determined flexible exchange-rate regime adopted in March 2024 through the IMF Extended Fund Facility framework; current-account items (freight revenues, TIR transit earnings, fuel, tolls, insurance, driver advances) are freely convertible through the banking channel against underlying transport contracts, CMR waybills, and TIR Carnets; capital-account transactions (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans with tenor over 12 months, intercompany debt, acquisition of Egyptian assets) are transferable only after bank-level commercial-purpose review and, where applicable, CBE no-objection or GAFI registration; dividend and profit remittance is treated as a permitted transfer but requires audited financial statements, the LLC's commercial register extract, an ETA tax-clearance certificate, and a board resolution evidencing the declared distribution; conversion of foreign currency to EGP must occur at the onshore interbank rate, and any attempt to use a parallel or divergent rate for onshore settlement is unlawful.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt requires 100% repatriation of export and service proceeds, including cross-border trucking and TIR carriage receipts: the gross invoice amount must be credited to a bank account in Egypt within 180 days from the date of the transport invoice or completion of the transit operation, and any foreign currency received into an offshore account must be transmitted to the Egyptian settlement account within five working days from the date of offshore crediting; there is no mandatory conversion ratio to EGP in the current flexible-rate environment, so the LLC may elect to retain the entire balance as a foreign-currency deposit; the supporting file — TIR Carnet, CMR waybill, collection order, and bank credit advice — must be retained by the processing bank for CBE periodic verification; non-repatriation may give rise to administrative sanctions, the restriction of import-financing and foreign-currency facilities, and the suspension of future dividend-remittance approvals.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE; البنك المركزي المصري), operating under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and the CBE's binding instructions on banks and foreign-exchange operations; exclusive regulator of the unified interbank foreign-exchange market, payment systems, and bank licensing; coordinates with GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) for inward capital registration and with the Ministry of Finance for tax-clearance-linked remittance controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low as a primary onshore settlement rail; permitted only as an ancillary offshore payment vehicle. An offshore digital EMI (for example, a UK-FCA or EU-EMD licensed e-money institution) cannot hold EGP on behalf of the Egyptian LLC, cannot provide a domestic Egyptian IBAN, and is not admitted to the CBE MIZAN/RTGS clearing architecture. Its permissible use is limited to hard-currency card programmes and e-wallet payments for fuel, tolls, border fees, and driver advances on foreign corridors, provided the entire net treasury position is swept on a daily or contractual basis back to a fully compliant Egyptian bank account and all such flows are reflected in the Egyptian commercial books; any agreement with the EMI must be registered in the transfer-pricing documentation file of the LLC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity and shareholder-advance injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for nostro-to-nostro repositioning) to the EG_LLC capital escrow account held with the authorised Egyptian bank; the wire should carry a BPM6-compatible purpose narrative — 'Direct Investment, Equity Inward' for paid-in capital and 'Intercompany Lending' for advances — together with a reference to the GAFI registration number, the LLC's commercial register number, and the underlying board resolution or shareholder-loan agreement. The receiving bank requires: corporate account-opening mandate; capital subscription board resolution; notarised LLC formation since non-resident ownership is permitted; GAFI compliance certificate under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; and a signed balance-of-payments return for CBE statistical reporting. Share-capital funds must remain in the capital escrow account until the amended memorandum of association is attested by the competent notary; shareholder loans with maturity exceeding 12 months are classified as capital-account obligations and their offshore interest remittance requires CBE no-objection.Operational disbursements for cross-border trucking (fuel-card clearing, lubricants, spare parts, tolls, border fees, driver transit advances, and TIR Carnet charges) are executed through the onshore operational account using local EGP direct debits, card-linked fuel-clearing through the Egyptian payment network, and SWIFT MT103 transfers to foreign beneficiaries supported by the underlying proforma invoice, CMR, and TIR Carnet. Fleet and container purchases are settled via irrevocable commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) or documentary collections; the CBE's February 2022 instruction that imports be made exclusively by documentary credit has been rescinded by the March 2023 instruction, so direct outward transfers are permitted subject to presentation of the customs import declaration and tax-compliance evidence. A standing pre-registered payment file for recurring corridor disbursements (Suez Canal Economic Zone, Red Sea ports, Mediterranean and North African land borders) should be maintained with the settlement bank to accelerate clearing without duplicating KYC or documentary review.Deliverable outright foreign-exchange forwards (plain vanilla, tenor up to 12 months) with CBE-licensed Egyptian banks, supported by genuine underlying trade or transport invoices and recorded in the bank's derivative risk ledger.FX swaps executed bilaterally with the settlement bank to convert EGP into USD or EUR for fuel, tolls, driver cash pooling, and back-conversion of surplus hard-currency corridor revenues.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-horizon USD or EUR fleet-finance liabilities against EGP operational revenues, subject to prior CBE no-objection and bank credit approval.Foreign-currency deposit sweeps combined with collateralised deliverable forwards; onshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not offered by authorised Egyptian banks and the LLF should avoid unlicensed offshore NDF dealing which would breach CBE convertibility supervision.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends and profit distributions made by the Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders (Article 56, Law 91/2005); distributions to resident Egyptian corporate shareholders are exempt under the dividend-received exemption, while distributions to resident individuals are subject to the 10% final WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 113 must be submitted and the balance of tax paid within 4 months after the end of the approved tax year; for a standard Egyptian fiscal year (1 July - 30 June), the deadline is 31 October; for ETA-approved calendar-year filers, 30 April.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed EGP 10,000,000; Master File mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed EGP 20,000,000 or the MNE group exceeds the ETA-prescribed group revenue threshold; CbC Report required for Egyptian ultimate parent entities of MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EGP 12,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt imposes no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the ETA applies anti-abuse and substance doctrines: (i) GAAR under Articles 121-131 of Law 91/2005 allowing re-characterization of arrangements lacking commercial substance; (ii) CFC rules attributing undistributed profits of low-tax foreign companies to Egyptian shareholders; (iii) thin-capitalization and interest-deduction limits on related-party debt; (iv) treaty-abuse prevention via the principal-purpose test under the OECD Multilateral Instrument (Egypt is a signatory); (v) practical substance indicators (real premises, operating staff, own/leased fleet, warehouse control, and business bank accounts) must support the cross-border TIR transport activity to defend deductions and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Collection of evaded tax plus 2% monthly delay surcharge; criminal sanctions under Law 91/2005 including imprisonment (up to 5 years) and fines (EGP 20,000-100,000); publication of the judgment at the offender's expense; the ETA also may seize bank accounts and suspend the unified tax card.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable to pay the evaded tax, penalties, and surcharges; conviction triggers imprisonment, automatic travel ban, and asset freezing.
Late or Non-Filing of CIT Return

Statutory sanction: 2% per-month delay surcharge on the outstanding balance; administrative fines per ETA penalty schedule; loss of the right to offset prepayments; the ETA may issue an assessment by estimation based on bank turnover and enforce via garnishment of transportation receivables.

Director personal liability: The signatory director remains personally liable for taxes withheld at source from salaries and subcontractor fees and is exposed to criminal exposure where non-filing was intentional.
