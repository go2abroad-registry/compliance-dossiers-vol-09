Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; electronic services via the ETA Taxpayer Portal and the national e-invoicing platform at einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; customs clearance through the NAFEZA single window at nafeza.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC through the GAFI One-Stop Shop and complete Egyptian Commercial Registry filing. Step 2: Open a corporate bank account and , where required, deposit the paid-up capital (no statutory minimum for LLCs). Step 3: Submit the articles of incorporation, board resolutions, and power of attorney to the competent ETA tax office to open a tax file and obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card for income tax purposes. Step 4: Upon reaching the VAT registration threshold, apply for VAT registration through the ETA electronic portal; foreign entities without an Egyptian permanent establishment may obtain a VAT ID by lodging certified constitutional documents, foreign tax registration certificates, and a notarized power of attorney. Step 5: Register with the NAFEZA customs system and activate the ETA e-invoicing API profile in preparation for any export or cross-border transaction flows.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide US-style check-the-box or elective hybrid classification regimes. An Egyptian-resident holding LLC may elect to file a consolidated income tax return with resident subsidiaries in which it holds, directly or indirectly, at least 95% of the subscribed capital, provided all other statutory conditions of Law No. 91 of 2005 are met. No VAT grouping election is available. Hybrid mismatch elections are not permitted; domestic GAAR, thin capitalization, and controlled-foreign-company rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies (mandatory); EGP 250,000 voluntary registration threshold; importers are registrable to obtain a VAT ID regardless of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered supplier may apply the zero rate to tangible goods exported outside Egypt where physical export is completed and evidenced by customs documentation; services supplied to a non-resident client for use wholly outside Egypt, including offshore equity-holding management and pure intermediary services, may be zero-rated if a direct contract, ETA-compliant invoice, and non-resident status evidence are maintained. Input VAT recovery is conditional on strict substantive and documentary proof of the export transaction, including timely receipt of export consideration in Egypt.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (NAFEZA Bayed export statement)
  • Bill of Lading or House Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Egyptian territory
  • Commercial invoice and foreign-currency sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT receipt evidencing transfer of export proceeds
  • ETA-approved packing list, freight documents, and multimodal transport evidence
  • Certificate of foreign buyer tax status or residence certification for offshore services

Phase-based national e-invoicing and e-receipt mandate: VAT-registered taxpayers must generate B2B/B2C invoices using ETA-approved systems with unique cryptographic signature and QR code, submit JSON invoices through the ETA API for real-time validation and clearance before delivery, and retain batch acknowledgement codes. API integration requires OAuth2 credentials and production digital certificates; failure to integrate or disputed rejected invoices trigger ETA dashboard escalations and administrative penalties.

ETA audit units apply enhanced scrutiny to recurring export zero-rating and refund claims, requiring full reconciliation among customs declarations, container movements, invoicing, and FX inflows. For fraudulent or falsely documented zero-rating claims, Law No. 67 of 2016 imposes tax evasion penalties of imprisonment from six months to five years together with fines of EGP 200,000 to EGP 2,000,000, plus mandatory repayment of the refunded input VAT with a 2% monthly late-payment surcharge. The Customs Authority may also suspend the exporter's NAFEZA clearance authorization until clawback amounts are fully settled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is partially convertible. Current account transactions, including trade settlement and regular dividend remittance, are materially free and clear with no mandatory pre-approval, subject to source-document evidence and tax clearance. Capital account transactions, including equity injection, capital repatriation, and liquidation proceeds, are governed by the CBE's foreign investment registration rules and the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) requirements. Profit repatriation requires audited financial statements, corporate tax certificates, and confirmation of repatriation entitlement under the investment approval. The CBE and Ministry of Finance retain legal authority to impose temporary convertibility restrictions or capital-flow management measures during acute balance-of-payments stress; current conditions are stable, but compliance planning must anticipate potential future surrender regimes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the current CBE liberalization period, there is no mandatory percentage surrender of export proceeds. Export receipts must be received through Egypt's regulated banking channel, either via telegraphic transfer, documentary collection, or letter of credit, and the exporter is required to deposit the foreign currency into a CBE-designated foreign currency account with a licensed domestic bank within 90 days from the date of export aboard or within the commercial credit term agreed (maximum 120 days for the tangible export). The exporter may retain the proceeds in foreign currency or voluntarily convert into EGP. In the event of renewed exchange crisis, CBE retains the statutory power, under Law No. 80 of 2002, to reintroduce a mandatory surrender ratio (historically up to 50-75%) for all export proceeds. The pure equity holding SPV is not a goods exporter and therefore has no surrender obligation, but dividend remittance is classified as current account consideration and is subject to documentary verification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and the Egyptian Exchange (EGX) are the principal sovereign regulators; the CBE exclusively administers foreign exchange licensing, exchange-rate policy, and banking supervision under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, the Foreign Exchange Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended), and the Anti-Money Laundering/Combating Financing of Terrorism Law No. 80 of 2002.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital financial institutions and EMI-licensed banks located outside Egypt are not viable for primary domestic treasury operations. The CBE does not permit offshore fintech/EMI entities to hold EGP clearing accounts, to issue EGP-denominated instruments, or to act as an Egyptian settlement member for investor capital inflows or dividend repatriation. An offshore EMI may be used solely as a low-value FX-prefunding channel if the ultimate clearing and settlement are executed through a CBE-regulated correspondent bank; however, for an equity holding SPV, the compliance risk, funding ambiguity, and CBE source-of-funds scrutiny render this channel low feasibility and legally weak. The mandated backbone is a direct tier-1 relationship with a licensed domestic Egyptian commercial bank or a local branch of a banking group incorporated as an Egyptian joint-stock company.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital investments must be routed via the Egyptian banking sector under CBE registration and matching with the investment licence from GAFI. The designated purpose code must clearly state 'Inward Direct Investment — Equity Participation' and align with the CBE external transactions classification (Category 1118 — Direct Investment Equity Capital). The sponsor's bank must receive (i) the GAFI certificate, (ii) the Egyptian commercial registry extract showing the LLC, (iii) the SPV's board resolution approving issuance of registered shares or increase of equity, (iv) the parent company's constitutional documents, and (v) a wire-transfer instruction referencing the Egyptian entity identification number and the legal entity identifier code; the bank will verify the incoming funds within 10 business days and issue a CBE inward FX registration certificate, necessary for future repatriation. The clearing rail must always be an authenticated SWIFT MT103 for the actual injection and perhaps an MT202 for the cover payment if correspondent banks require a separate cover.The SPV is a passive holding entity, but any outbound trade-related disbursements, such as payments for professional services, shareholder loans, dividends, or intercompany financings, must be executed under the applicable CBE purpose code. Dividend repatriation uses the 'Dividends and Profit Distribution' purpose code and requires an MT103 to the ultimate parent's account, together with an Egyptian tax withholding certificate, audited financial statements, tax returns for the fiscal year, subsidiary distribution resolutions, and a bank-verified confirmation that all mandatory CBE foreign-asset registration procedures have been completed. For intra-group loan servicing, an MT103 plus MT199 with the full loan agreement, interest computation schedule, and CBE external debt registration is used. The clearing rail may be enhanced with a commercial letter of credit, SWIFT MT700, for any operational trade covered by the SPV's subsidiary chain; however, for the pure equity holding structure, the MT103/MT202 interbank wire network and the Egyptian real-time gross settlement system (EG-RTGS) are the sole practical rails.Onshore EGP/foreign exchange delivered forwards between CBE-licensed banks and the SPV, based on actual current and swiftly upcoming FX exposures arising from dividend receipts or repatriation obligations, with a maximum maturity of 12 months and no collateralisation requirement for the SPV beyond standard ISDA-style credit-support annex or bank guarantee.Non-deliverable offshore EGP forwards transacted with a reputable international bank through an Egyptian licensed bank's offshore branch, provided that the notional exposure is limited to eligible EGP-denominated dividend and capital flows and that the transaction never results in physical delivery within Egypt unlicensed by the CBE.Currency swaps executed with a CBE-licensed bank for simultaneous exchange of EGP and USD principal flows in relation to the SPV's shareholder loans or refinancing, subject to the bank's internal treasury approval and an express confirmation of the commercial purpose below the super-receivable threshold.Currency options and plain-vanilla EGP/USD options, both bought and written, when used strictly as a cashflow hedge against adverse EGP depreciation on the SPV's expected dividend income; any premium financing or speculative options strategy is prohibited.FX swaps under the CBE's interbank FX swap rules, to cover temporary EGP liquidity gaps without altering the SPV's trade-instrument risk profile.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 1,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions, being the ETA threshold that triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File preparation; Master File obligations apply to multinational groups exceeding the ETA-published consolidated revenue ceiling or upon formal ETA request.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no formalised Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the UAE. However, the Egyptian Tax Authority applies a rigorous substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance doctrine, including beneficial ownership tests under the DTA articles and domestic GAAR principles. A pure equity holding SPV must maintain an Egyptian registered office, local management decisions, board minutes, separate bank accounts, and contemporary accounting records; a shell arrangement lacking these elements may be disregarded and its dividend income reattributed to the ultimate parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Falsification of the Annual CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Under Law 91/2005, deliberate evasion carries imprisonment of not less than 3 years and a fine of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000, together with collection of the evaded tax plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and signatories are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; the court may freeze personal assets, impose travel bans, seize passports, and prevent the directors from leaving Egypt pending resolution of the criminal case.
Sham Interposition and Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The ETA disregards the intermediary, attributes the dividend income to the ultimate shareholder, denies treaty benefits, reimposes 22.5% CIT on deemed profit and 10% final dividend withholding, and levies late surcharges and penalties of up to EGP 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced against incorporators and nominees; directors face civil restitution for unpaid taxes, administrative disqualification, and criminal aiding-and-abetting sanctions for facilitating treaty abuse.
Transfer Pricing and Financing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The ETA adjusts the transaction to the arm's length result, disallows the excess deduction, imposes the 2% monthly delay surcharge on the adjusted tax, and applies documentation-specific penalties; repeated violations escalate to criminal false-statement liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer pricing documentation are co-debtors for the additional tax; serious concealment triggers personal criminal prosecution and travel restrictions during the ETA investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Money laundering offences are punishable by imprisonment and fines of up to the value of the laundered funds, mandatory asset forfeiture, blacklisting by the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit, suspension of the company's tax card, and freezing of all corporate bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and beneficial owners are personally prosecuted, subjected to national and international travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition exposure; directors cannot rely on the corporate veil to shield themselves from criminal restitution and imprisonment.
