Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Egypt - official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt fiscal clearance portal: https://einvoice.gov.eg; customs verification counterpart: Egyptian Customs Authority (ECA) operating the Advance Cargo Information (ACI) single window.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must (1) incorporate with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain the Commercial Registry certificate; (2) file ETA Registration Form No. 1 for corporate income tax together with the VAT registration form at the competent ETA tax office, appointing an accredited resident tax representative where management is outside Egypt; (3) submit the legalized Arabic translation of the Articles of Association, GAFI certificate, commercial register extract, board resolution, and passports/power-of-attorney documents; (4) receive a single unified Egyptian Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration certificate, issued within approximately 10 business days; (5) enroll in the e-invoicing platform by obtaining a Qualified Electronic Signature Certificate (QESC), registering an API client ID and client secret, and completing the OAuth 2.0 token enrollment against the ETA sandbox before any taxable invoice is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election; an LLC is mandatorily classified as a separate taxable person subject to entity-level corporate income tax at 22.5% under Income Tax Law 91/2005, as unified by Law 206/2023. Available statutory elections are: (i) the small-business unified tax regime under Law 206/2023, permitting cash-basis accounting and simplified turnover-based tax scales for taxpayers with annual turnover up to EGP 20 million; (ii) the non-resident branch/service election under Art. 56 of Law 91/2005, allowing final gross-basis withholding taxation in lieu of a net-basis assessment; (iii) an Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) election to fix transfer-pricing margins with the ETA; and (iv) placement of the taxpayer into the ETA Large or Mid-Taxpayer Center for centralized audit treatment. Pass-through taxation is available only to Egyptian partnerships, not to LLCs; LLC shareholders are taxed only on distributed dividends.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover in goods and services, being the mandatory domestic VAT registration threshold under Law 67/2016 and its Executive Regulations; non-resident remote sellers of digital/electronic services and e-commerce goods to Egyptian consumers must register for VAT without any turnover threshold through the simplified non-resident VAT registration procedure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Art. 31 of VAT Law 67/2016, goods physically exported outside Egypt and services performed for a non-resident and consumed outside Egypt, including e-commerce and digitally delivered services and the online resale of vintage/secondhand luxury goods shipped to foreign end-customers, are zero-rated at 0%. To qualify, the exporter must: (i) hold a valid VAT registration and a complete, documented purchase chain for the exported goods; (ii) physically ship the goods out of Egypt within the contractual/statutory window and obtain actual Egyptian Customs Authority release through the ACI system; (iii) receive payment in convertible foreign currency and repatriate the proceeds according to Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) instructions; and (iv) retain the full statutory documentation set and submit an electronic VAT refund application. Where vintage luxury goods were originally imported, the import VAT input deduction is recoverable only upon presentation of the import customs release and corresponding tax documentation; purchases from non-registered private consignors do not carry deductable input VAT but the subsequent export sale remains zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACI shipment declaration) duly released by the Egyptian Customs Authority and stamped with the export customs code
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from an Egyptian sea, air, or land port to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an authorized Egyptian licensed bank or correspondent bank evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Export sales invoice in Arabic/English together with the underlying purchase or consignment contract for the vintage luxury goods
  • Electronic VAT refund application form supported by purchase invoices and import declarations substantiating deductible input VAT

Mandatory e-invoicing for all B2B sales under Ministry of Finance Decrees 318/2020 and 46/2021, and mandatory e-receipting for B2C sales under Decree 51/2021. Every e-invoice must be digitally signed with a Qualified Electronic Signature Certificate (QESC) and submitted in JSON/XML to the ETA central fiscal clearance engine through the public REST API using client ID, client secret, OAuth 2.0 token, and an HMAC-SHA256 signature envelope. The ETA validates each legal document in real time and returns a 'final' clearance state, a unique invoice hash/UUID, and a machine-readable QR code/barcode; no physical revenue stamping applies, but un-cleared invoices are legally inadmissible for input VAT deduction and trigger automatic downstream audit flags plus a fine of EGP 10,000-20,000 per non-compliant invoice.

The ETA and Egyptian Customs Authority cross-match every zero-rated export invoice and e-receipt against the ACI/digital signature platform using automated risk matrices. If an export zero-rating claim fails, the ETA assesses 14% VAT on the supply, adds monthly late-payment penalties at 1% per month or part-month up to the statutory cap, and imposes a fine equal to 100%-200% of the evaded VAT together with imprisonment up to five years under Art. 72 of Law 67/2016 and the Anti-Tax Evasion Law 143/2018. Forged customs declarations, backdated bills of lading, falsified inward remittance certificates, or simulated export transactions trigger an automatic five-year retrospective clawback of any refunded VAT, immediate refund cancellation, criminal referral, joint and several liability of the signing tax representative and accountant, suspension from practicing before the ETA, and blacklisting under the e-invoicing charter.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a flexible managed-float exchange-rate regime in which the EGP is broadly convertible for genuine current-account transactions through the interbank market, but capital-account movements remain controlled and evidence-intensive. Inward and outward transfers must be completed through CBE-licensed banks and supported by underlying commercial documents; profit and dividend remittances are lawful after corporate tax settlement, audited financial statements, and an ordinary annual general assembly decision, with the bank recording the remittance in the CBE balance-of-payments framework. For an Egyptian LLC engaged in vintage-luxury online resale, the main convertibility obstacles will be the absence of a fully documented import-and-export chain for styled goods, reliance on offshore payment schemes, and the need to prove that each card or escrow settlement corresponds to a real sale and not to unlicensed money transmission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Current CBE operational instructions require all export proceeds from goods and online merchandise sales to be repatriated into Egypt within 180 days after the date of shipment as evidenced by the bill of lading or air waybill, unless an extended collection period has been approved by the receiving bank. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio set in Law No. 194 of 2020; the operating ratio is effectively 100% collection into the Egyptian banking system, with a permitted retention of foreign currency in a CBE-approved account only when the exporter demonstrates matching genuine current obligations. Receipts not covered by a documented retention approval are converted into EGP by the receiving bank at the prevailing interbank buying rate. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window exposes the LLC to CBE penalties, customs enforcement, cancellation of the importer/exporter code, and suspension of access to bank FX.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 as the national monetary, banking, and foreign-exchange regulator; the CBE supervises all licensed banks, issues trade and exchange-control instructions, operates interbank foreign-exchange systems, and enforces repatriation and anti-money-laundering rules jointly with the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit. The Egyptian Customs Authority supports FX control through the NAFEZA single-window for export and import documentation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Low as a principal treasury rail. Egypt does not recognize offshore electronic-money institution licences, and a resident Egyptian LLC may not maintain core operating funds in an offshore EMI or non-bank payment wallet without violating CBE repatriation and safeguarding obligations. Stripe is not licensed as an Egyptian bank and cannot independently settle EGP clearing; its use for e-commerce card acquiring is viable only through a CBE-licensed local acquiring arrangement under which gross proceeds are settled into an Egyptian bank account. Escrow_Pay is acceptable only as a contractual escrow mechanism; all net escrow proceeds must be transmitted to the LLC's Egyptian bank by SWIFT MT103 and must not be held offshore beyond the settlement period of the underlying transaction. A treasury model built on Payoneer, Wise, Revolut, or similar offshore rails as the primary receivable account will be rejected by Egyptian banks, regarded as unlicensed money movement, and may trigger CBE sanctions and tax audit complications.

Root cause: Export and collection non-repatriation: receivables remain in offshore PSP or escrow wallets beyond the CBE 180-day window or are converted outside the Egyptian interbank market.

Defense evidence: Monthly aging report of offshore receivables, evidence of immediate onward MT103 transmission to the Egyptian bank, timeline demonstrating settlement within the statutory window, CFO or auditor confirmation that all export receipts are recorded in Egypt, CBE bank acknowledgment forms, and proof that no foreign-exchange conversion took place outside an authorized Egyptian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injections must be executed by direct SWIFT MT103 transfer from the non-resident shareholder's own bank account to the LLC's account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, using the CBE/IMF balance-of-payments purpose code for equity participation in a resident enterprise. The receiving bank registers the inward remittance as foreign direct investment in the CBE foreign-investment tracking record, and that registration is the legal foundation for future dividend and liquidation repatriation. Third-party payments, cash deposits, and cryptocurrency conversions are not acceptable. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the LLC must deliver a written loan agreement with clear repayment terms; the loan is recorded separately from equity and repayments are subject to bank documentary review and CBE foreign-exchange reporting. No notarial capital escrow is statutorily required for LLC formation, but counterparties may require a proof-of-funds escrow deposit within the Egyptian bank, recorded as a segregated capital escrow sub-account.Supplier and trade payments must be executed by the LLC's Egyptian bank through SWIFT MT103 settlement with a valid trade purpose code, supported by the commercial invoice, logistics documentation, and customs import evidence where goods cross borders. For high-value vintage-luxury purchases where the overseas supplier requires security before shipment, the compliant rail is an irrevocable documentary letter of credit under UCP600, issued by the Egyptian bank and notified/confirmed through the supplier's bank; payment occurs against presentation of the bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and any specified authentication report. EGP payments to domestic consignors, authentication houses, couriers, and customs brokers clear through the CBE RTGS system or domestic instant-payment rails. Buyer refunds on card transactions must be processed through the original acquiring chain or PSP and may not be paid from the treasury account to unrelated third parties. The LLC must maintain an active importer/exporter code; otherwise, the bank will not process USD outward remittances for merchandise purchases.Deliverable USD/EGP forward contracts up to 12 months, permitted only with documentary proof of genuine current trade exposure.FX swap transactions between USD and EGP used to match the timing gap between card-settlement collections and EGP operating-expense payments.Plain-vanilla EUR/USD or GBP/USD cross-currency swaps and forwards when supplier invoices are denominated in those currencies.European-style currency options, including purchased puts/calls, for protection of large, confirmed buy-side transactions in foreign currency.NDFs are not permissible as the primary hedge for a resident Egyptian LLC because the EGP is a deliverable currency and CBE rules require all onshore FX hedging to settle through the interbank market rather than offshore synthetic markets.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low as a principal treasury rail. Egypt does not recognize offshore electronic-money institution licences, and a resident Egyptian LLC may not maintain core operating funds in an offshore EMI or non-bank payment wallet without violating CBE repatriation and safeguarding obligations. Stripe is not licensed as an Egyptian bank and cannot independently settle EGP clearing; its use for e-commerce card acquiring is viable only through a CBE-licensed local acquiring arrangement under which gross proceeds are settled into an Egyptian bank account. Escrow_Pay is acceptable only as a contractual escrow mechanism; all net escrow proceeds must be transmitted to the LLC's Egyptian bank by SWIFT MT103 and must not be held offshore beyond the settlement period of the underlying transaction. A treasury model built on Payoneer, Wise, Revolut, or similar offshore rails as the primary receivable account will be rejected by Egyptian banks, regarded as unlicensed money movement, and may trigger CBE sanctions and tax audit complications.
