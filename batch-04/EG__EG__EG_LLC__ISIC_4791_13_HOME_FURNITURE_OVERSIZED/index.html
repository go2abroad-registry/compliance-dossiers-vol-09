Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; e-services portal at www.eta.gov.eg (also electronic invoice portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg/en/ElectronicInvoicing)

E-government filing portal

Because non-resident ownership is permitted, the LLC first registers as a juridical person with the Commercial Registry and obtains a unified tax card (United Tax Number / TIN) from the competent Tax Directorate. For VAT identification, the LLC must file an ETA VAT registration application via the ETA's online portal within 60 days of exceeding the registration threshold, appoint a tax representative if a non-resident is concerned, and obtain a VAT certificate. The TIN is mandatory for customs clearance, import/export declarations, and e-invoicing enrollment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not permit check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections. An LLC is mandatorily taxed at entity level; no partnership pass-through election is available. A group may apply for consolidated tax filing for wholly-owned subsidiaries only if the Finance Minister authorises it under the Income Tax Act, but this does not alter entity-level classification. Corporate income tax is 22.5%.

14%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under article 23(1) of Egyptian VAT Law, goods physically exported outside Egypt are zero-rated when the supplier retains evidence of customs clearance and release from Egypt, payment of freight/insurance if sold under CIF terms, export shipping documents, and actual receipt of foreign currency proceeds credited to an Egyptian bank account in accordance with Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) regulations. Offshore services sold to a non-resident and effectively performed outside Egypt may be zero-rated if the service is physically connected to abroad and the recipient is outside Egypt. The LLC must record sales as zero-rated only at the time the physical shipment has left Egyptian customs territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (outward manifest / SAD code for export release)
  • Bill of Lading or similar proof of shipment (or air waybill for air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate made out to the exporting LLC and issued by the collecting Egyptian bank
  • Signed Commercial Invoice and Packing List evidencing consignment, container number and HS classification relevant to oversized furniture retail
  • Certificate of Origin (when required by the destination country or letter of credit)

Egypt mandates near-real-time electronic invoicing ('e-invoice') and electronic receipts ('e-receipt') for VAT-registered persons. Businesses must integrate with the ETA's Invoice Clearance Platform using certified JSON/XML schemas and a token-based HTTPS API. Every invoice must be signed with a private key and receive a unique QR code / fiscal stamp from ETA before issue. The ETA performs real-time schema validation, duplicate detection and duty/quantitative checks; non-integrated sales or rejected invoices trigger monetary penalties, freezing of VAT refunds and suspension of registration.

ETA field audits may demand re-verification of customs release data against the customs system and CBE foreign-currency receipts. If a zero-rated export is unsupported, the input VAT apportioned to that export is clawed back and assessed at the 14% VAT headline rate, plus late-payment interest, delay and non-compliance fines of up to EGP 100,000 per violation; intentional fraud or fictitious export documentation additionally triggers criminal prosecution under the VAT Law (imprisonment and fine), disqualification from e-invoicing and denial of future refunds. Retrospective assessment reaches back five years from the date of filing and may extend to ten years where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is convertible on the current account: Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, allowing unrestricted payments for trade in goods and services, dividends, interest and profit repatriation without CBE pre-approval. The capital account is managed: foreign direct investment (share capital and shareholder loans), EGP-denominated foreign investment and portfolio flows must be channelled through licensed banks with mandatory CBE statistical reporting. A flexible, but managed, exchange-rate regime operates post-December 2022, with interbank market pricing and no formal rationing. Profit remittance by an Egyptian LLC to a foreign parent is lawful provided the bank holds the statutory supporting file: tax card and tax clearance, audited financial statements, board distribution resolution and CBE-declared underlying transaction documents. Practical friction arises when USD liquidity is tight, prompting enhanced due-diligence queues on high-value wires and strict verification of logistics/import documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

100% of export proceeds must be received into the Egyptian banking system within five (5) months from the date of shipment, per CBE instructions to banks. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% — exporters are not obliged to convert receipts into EGP; proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts and used for bona fide trade, service payments or other permitted current transactions. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline exposes the exporter to bank-imposed compliance restrictions, possible suspension of trade-finance facilities and adverse CBE enforcement on the bank and the customer.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the exclusive monetary authority and foreign-exchange regulator, exercising powers under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and the CBE's Rules of Dealing in Foreign Currencies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: LOW–MEDIUM. Offshore digital EMIs (EU/UK e-money licences, digital-only banks) are not authorised to provide payment services, hold EG-persian funds or issue EGP IBANs to Egyptian residents without a CBE licence under Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and CBE e-money regulations. Such EMIs may only be used as auxiliary rails for outbound non-EGP disbursements (e.g., settling overseas logistics or supplier invoices from segregated foreign-currency balances), never for inbound export receipts, local merchant settlement or EGP clearing. Onshore licensed payment fintechs are commercially viable for e-commerce acquiring (including Stripe-style flows), but they do not replace the bank as the clearing node for trade payments and FX conversion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital injections and shareholder loans must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank intermediation) through a licensed Egyptian bank. The remitter must specify the purpose as 'FDI equity subscription' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement' with the CBE statistical-reporting code for direct investment; the receiving Egyptian bank issues a foreign-capital transaction credit advice and reports to the CBE. Verifiable documentation includes the LLC board resolution approving the capital increase, GAFI electronic filing and registration evidence, amended articles of association filed with the commercial registry, and the parent entity's audited financials showing the source of funds. Any conversion of USD/EUR into EGP is executed at the interbank rate at the licensed bank; no CBE pre-approval is required for genuine direct investment under the current liberalised regime. The funds must be used strictly for the declared business purpose to avoid reinvestment/remittance restrictions.Trade settlements for oversized furniture imports use SWIFT MT103 for single-invoice payments, referencing the importer tax card, the GAFI import registration number and Egypt's ACID customs identifier. Large-amount or recurring purchase orders are settled by Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) or documentary collection (MT400/MT422) with title-document accompaniment. Logistics and freight for oversized consignments are paid as trade-related service transfers under SWIFT MT103 supported by freight-forwarding contracts, charter-party bills of lading, customs release declarations and evidence of completed import. All outbound payments are verified against underlying trade documentation and the bank's CBE export-repatriation and import-compliance monitoring obligations; payment to third-country suppliers without full import documentation may be treated as capital outflow and rejected.Deliverable FX forward contracts (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP) with substantiated underlying trade invoices and bank-approved tenors.Cross-currency swap agreements (USD/EGP, EUR/EGP) used to hedge operating cash-flow and asset-liability mismatches.FX option structures (USD/EUR-call or EGP-put) supported by documentary evidence of committed trade exposure.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) instruments: generally not permissible for onshore Egyptian-resident hedging; only non-resident counterparties may access offshore NDF markets, and any such arrangement must not be booked through the Egyptian banking system.

Late payment attracts a surcharge of 2% per month or part of a month on the unpaid tax; late filing exposes the taxpayer to discretionary administrative penalties and automatic assessment by the ETA; wilful non-filing is treated as evasion and referred to criminal prosecution. A tax clearance certificate cannot be issued until all filings and payments are settled.
Box 2 – Cost of Goods Sold Oversized-furniture inventory costs, customs duties, inbound freight, warehousing, and direct fulfillment labor. CEGS and deductible costs must be substantiated via invoices and customs declarations; foreign parent imports into Egypt must be valued on an arm's length basis. Late payment attracts a surcharge of 2% per month or part of a month on the unpaid tax; late filing exposes the taxpayer to discretionary administrative penalties and automatic assessment by the ETA; wilful non-filing is treated as evasion and referred to criminal prosecution. A tax clearance certificate cannot be issued until all filings and payments are settled.
Box 3 – Deductible Operating Expenses Marketing, platform fees, payment processing fees, logistics, insurance, depreciation, and allocated head-office administrative charges. Management and technical service fees paid to non-residents are deductible only if documented and subject to 20% withholding; excessive fees are subject to transfer pricing adjustment. Late payment attracts a surcharge of 2% per month or part of a month on the unpaid tax; late filing exposes the taxpayer to discretionary administrative penalties and automatic assessment by the ETA; wilful non-filing is treated as evasion and referred to criminal prosecution. A tax clearance certificate cannot be issued until all filings and payments are settled.
Box 4 – Related Party Transactions Disclosure Annual TP disclosure of controlled transactions with foreign parent, logistics providers, and group service companies. No foreign tax credit is available to a non-resident without PE; the final balance must be settled by the filing deadline to avoid monthly surcharges. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when a single controlled transaction with a related party exceeds EGP 10 million, or when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed EGP 30 million; Master File is required when group-wide consolidated revenues exceed EGP 15 billion; an annual TP disclosure form must be submitted with the Form 8 return where aggregate controlled transactions exceed EGP 5 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the Gulf Cooperation Council 'Economic Substance Regulations', but the ETA applies general anti-avoidance, beneficial ownership, and PE attribution principles under Law No. 91/2005 as amended. A paper-only Egyptian LLC whose online retail operations, decision-making, and oversized-furniture fulfillment are in substance carried on outside Egypt may be disregarded or treated as a conduit, with income re-attributed to the foreign parent and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Unreported Online Sales

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 2% per month on unreported tax, plus criminal fines; wilful evasion under Articles 133–136 of Law No. 91/2005 is punishable by imprisonment of 3 to 5 years and fines of up to EGP 1 million, together with confiscation of goods and mandatory publication of the judgment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly approve off-book sales face joint civil liability for unpaid tax, personal criminal imprisonment, administrative seizure of personal assets, and travel bans pending full settlement.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA may disregard the LLC as a sham, disallow intercompany deductions, recharacterize income as attributable to the foreign parent's Egyptian PE, levy a deemed-profit assessment, and impose monthly delay surcharges plus double penalties for abusive structuring.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the full tax shortfall under veil-piercing rules, subjected to precautionary attachment of bank accounts and moveable assets, and banned from leaving Egypt until tax clearance is issued.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to EGP 500,000 for documentation failures; discretionary ETA transfer pricing adjustments increasing taxable income; additional tax at 22.5% plus the 2% monthly delay surcharge; potential double taxation absent timely MAP/APA action.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve deliberately false TP documentation face criminal charges for providing false information to the ETA, personal fines, and imprisonment for up to 3 years in aggravated cases.
AML and Payment Rails Misuse

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80/2016 as amended include confiscation of laundered proceeds, fines of up to EGP 2 million, and imprisonment of 7 to 30 years for organized laundering practices; the E-Commerce unit and banking system may permanently blacklist the merchant.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly facilitate payment-rail laundering face personal criminal prosecution, unlimited civil restitution to affected parties and the state, travel restrictions, and disqualification from acting as directors.
Withholding Tax and Profit Repatriation Violations

Statutory sanction: Unpaid withholding tax is recovered at 20%–22.5% of the gross remittance with the 2% monthly surcharge; false deduction of such payments triggers a 100% add-back; CBE exchange-control reporting violations lead to delayed, blocked, or revoked repatriation approvals.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unlawful repatriations are jointly and severally liable for the withholding tax and surcharges, may face precautionary seizure of their assets, and can be criminally prosecuted for aiding the unlawful outward transfer of Egyptian funds.
