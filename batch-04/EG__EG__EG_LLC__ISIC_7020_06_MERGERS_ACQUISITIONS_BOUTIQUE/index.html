Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official e-portal: www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) by registering with the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) through the official e-portal or the competent regional tax office. Required documentation includes: certified copy of incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, local commercial registration extract, power of attorney issued to a local tax representative, certificate of the foreign entity's tax residence, proof of local fixed establishment/address, and valid identification of directors/shareholders. After document review, the ETA issues a combined Tax ID (TIN) covering corporate income tax, VAT, payroll, and stamp tax obligations. Non-resident service providers without a permanent establishment may instead be registered for withholding tax purposes and receive a dedicated non-resident TIN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must obtain a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) by registering with the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) through the official e-portal or the competent regional tax office. Required documentation includes: certified copy of incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, local commercial registration extract, power of attorney issued to a local tax representative, certificate of the foreign entity's tax residence, proof of local fixed establishment/address, and valid identification of directors/shareholders. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a 'check-the-box' regime or domestic hybrid mismatch election mechanism. However, Egyptian Income Tax Law permits, upon ETA approval, consolidated tax returns for economically interdependent resident companies under specified conditions, though this does not alter the default entity-level taxation status of LLCs. Special economic zones (e.g., Suez Canal Economic Zone – SCZone) and investment-zone incentives may offer alternative tax treatments, but these are not entity classification elections; entities remain subject to the standard corporate income tax framework unless a specific investment agreement stipulates otherwise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover threshold; mandatory VAT registration applies to any person making taxable supplies exceeding this amount. Importers of goods are required to register regardless of turnover level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods may be zero-rated at 0% VAT once the goods are physically exported from Egypt and title has passed to a non-resident recipient. Supporting conditions: the export is documented through the official customs procedures; goods are shipped within the statutorily prescribed period from sales date; and for related-party transactions the price and economic substance satisfy transfer-pricing rules. Certain services supplied to non-residents for consumption outside Egypt, or services directly connected with export goods (such as handling, packing, shipping), may also qualify for zero-rating where specified by the VAT Law and explanatory guidelines.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form C & V) bearing customs seal
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT transfer confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency

Egypt enforces mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts through the ETA's electronic invoicing portal. Taxpayers must integrate with the ETA-facing APIs and transmit each invoice/receipt in XML or JSON format in real-time; the ETA validates the submitted data and issues a unique transaction identifier (UUID) plus a QR code, after which the invoice is deemed fiscal and can be delivered to the buyer. The mandate covers all B2B and B2C invoices issued by registered taxpayers. Non-compliance triggers automated blocking of invoice issuance and statutory penalties.

The Egyptian Tax Authority may retroactively claw back zero-rated VAT together with late-return interest and additional tax penalties under the VAT Law (penalties range between 50% and 100% of the undeclared tax due). In addition, fines of up to EGP 500,000 per ineligible invoice can be imposed, and fraudulent or fictitious export documentation triggers criminal prosecution under the Egyptian Penal Code and tax-fraud provisions, including imprisonment sanctions. The ETA also conducts post-clearance audits comparing customs export data, bank repatriation records, and VAT returns to detect abusive zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-float foreign-exchange regime. Current-account payments for licit cross-border advisory fees and operating expenses are generally convertible upon submission of a valid commercial invoice and CBE purpose code, while capital-account transfers including dividend/profit remittances, capital repatriation, and foreign investment exit require demonstrated historical source of funds, CBE approval for certain categories, and compliance with the Investment Law 72 of 2017, Law 194 of 2020 AML/CFT regulations and CBE FX circulars. Profit remittance is permitted after payment of taxes, statutory reserves, and audit; however, banks may request underlying transaction documentation, and residual hard-currency liquidity constraints can cause settlement delays. Foreign shareholders may maintain retained earnings in EGP or convert and remit at prevailing interbank rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE FX regulations, export receipts in foreign currency must be repatriated to an Egyptian licensed bank within 90 days from the date of shipment or performance of service (service fees, including advisory success fees, are treated as service export receipts). There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio after the 2016 liberalization; export proceeds may be kept in a foreign-currency account with an authorized bank or sold immediately to the interbank market. In practice, banks verify the underlying export/service contract, tax invoices, and customs documentation before crediting the account, and proceeds are required to be used for bona fide current or capital transactions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) / Egyptian Exchange Control Authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium with legal constraint: Foreign fintech EMIs are not permitted to open subsidiary accounts or pass-through rails in Egypt unless licensed or authorized by the CBE under Law 194/2020 and the CBE's payment systems and fintech regulations. Offshore e-money institutions may only connect through a licensed Egyptian bank or licensed electronic-payment processor; they cannot independently clear EGP transactions. Cross-border fee collection may use SWIFT via a corresponding Egyptian bank, but a digital EMI establishes only a money-transfer front, not a bank-account relationship.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends distributed by an Egyptian LLC to a non-resident corporate shareholder, unless a lower rate is available under an applicable double tax agreement (DTA). The withholding must be deposited with ETA before the dividend is remitted.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return must be filed within four months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year entity, the filing deadline is 30 April. Quarterly prepayments follow the same schedule. Foreign-company LLCs operating through an Egyptian permanent establishment (PE) are subject to the same deadlines.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Legal name of taxpayer, Egyptian Tax Identification Number (TIN), registered address, and ISIC business activity code 7020. A non-resident LLC with an Egyptian PE must enter the Egyptian TIN and the registered Egyptian branch address. If no PE exists, the entity must not file this CIT return; income would instead be subject to source withholding by the Egyptian payer. Late filing of the annual CIT return carries an administrative penalty of up to EGP 50,000 in addition to monthly default interest of 2% on the overdue tax; understatement caused by deliberate concealment adds an inaccurate-return penalty of 10% of the additional tax assessed and can lead to criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Law 91/2005 Articles 116–119.
Box 2 Fiscal period start and end dates covered by the return. The PE must adopt the same fiscal year as the foreign LLC for annual CIT purposes unless ETA has approved a separate Egyptian accounting year. Closing date must be no later than the foreign entity's tax year-end. Box 3 Return type tick-box: original return, supplementary or amended return. Use 'original' for the initial compliant filing. An amended return may be filed within five years from the statutory due date to correct disclosed mistakes before ETA assessment. Box 4 Total gross revenues and business receipts, including M&A retainers, advisory fees, success fees, and any consulting compensation paid on closing. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and maintained when the aggregate value of Egyptian controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EGP 10,000,000 in a fiscal year. Master File is required when the global group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EGP 200,000,000 or the aggregate Egyptian controlled transactions exceed EGP 10,000,000. The disclosure form TP-1 must be attached to the annual CIT return whenever these thresholds are exceeded; ETA may also demand documentation within 30 days for lower-value transactions where a transfer-pricing accounting mismatch is evident.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted a generic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute equivalent to GCC jurisdictions, but Egyptian tax law applies a strong substance-over-form doctrine, particularly under Article 87 of Law 91/2005. The ETA may disregard artificial contracts and corporate structures that lack genuine economic purpose, recharacterise revenue to the Egyptian entity that actually performs the M&A advisory work, and apply the beneficial-ownership limitation in tax treaties. A non-resident LLC maintaining an Egyptian advisory boutique must show institutional license, personnel, board control, physical premises, deal files, and transaction risk management in Egypt; otherwise treaty benefits are denied and the structure is assessed as a sham PE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Unrecorded Success Fees

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 116–119 of Law 91/2005, tax evaders risk imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EGP 1,000,000, plus assessment of the evaded tax with 2% monthly default interest. The ETA will also impose an inaccurate-return penalty of 10% of the additional assessed tax, and the underlying amount remains collectible through compulsory attachment.

Director personal liability: Executive directors who knowingly route success fees to a related foreign entity without recognition are personally and jointly liable with the company for the unpaid tax, monthly interest, and penalties. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose a travel ban, and order civil restitution; conviction leads to disqualification from board membership and possible imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: ETA may upwardly adjust the Egyptian taxable profit, disallow deductions for related-party payments, impose a TP penalty of up to 20% of the adjusted tax difference, and charge 2% monthly default interest. In egregious cases, the adjustment may be reclassified as tax evasion with criminal fines and imprisonment of responsible executives.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve TP documentation knowing that the arm's-length pricing is false face personal civil liability for the entire tax deficiency plus penalties. The veil is pierced if the related remuneration is shown to have left the Egyptian entity without economic substance or commercial rationale; travel bans and director disqualification apply.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 87 of Law 91/2005 and Egyptian Substance-over-Form case law, ETA will disregard the artificial arrangement, redistribute revenue to the actual function performed in Egypt, and deny deductions for payments made to shell unrelated or related providers. Administrative fines from EGP 50,000 up to EGP 200,000 may be imposed on the sham entity, and Commercial Registry authorities may order involuntary strike-off or winding up.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate an Egyptian shell M&A boutique are personally liable for the taxes that the sham entity was created to avoid, as well as for insolvency costs if the entity was depleted by artificial payments. Travel bans, asset seizures, and personal criminal liability are available where the shell was used to defraud ETA or creditors.
AML/CFT Violations — Success Fee Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80/2002, as amended, an entity found guilty of laundering proceeds can be fined up to EGP 5,000,000 and ordered to forfeit the laundered assets; senior managers may be imprisoned for a term of up to seven years, and the relevant regulatory licence or professional registration of the boutique can be revoked.

Director personal liability: A director or compliance officer who personally executes or conceals illicit fee transfers can be held criminally liable alongside the company, with imprisonment up to seven years, personal fines, confiscation of the director's property up to the laundered amount, and a travel ban pending trial.
Withholding Tax Defaults on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC as withholding agent is personally liable for the full uncollected withholding tax, plus monthly default interest at 2% and administrative fines proportionate to the under-withholding. ETA may issue an attachment order on all local bank accounts and enforce against the LLC's assets, and criminal tax concealment penalties can apply if the remittance was intentionally disguised.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for approving the dividend or repayment must ensure funds are reserved for WHT before any SWIFT remittance. Failure to do so exposes each relevant director to joint liability for the uncollected tax; courts may pierce the corporate veil and order civil personal remedies, travel bans against directors, and, in cases of fraudulent false declaration, imprisonment.
