Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt — official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) obtain commercial registration (CR) issued by the General Authority for Investment and the Ministry of Trade; (2) register with the ETA through the unified electronic tax registration service (Injaz / ETA e-services portal) using the CR, constitutional documents, power of attorney (if applicable), proof of physical premises, and designated bank data; (3) receive a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) that simultaneously serves as the corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax, and stamp duty identifier; (4) for VAT purposes, verify whether the annual taxable supply value exceeds the mandatory threshold — if so, VAT registration is completed automatically upon TIN issuance; if below the threshold, a VAT registration application is filed separately; (5) after TIN allocation, enrol for the e-invoicing system by obtaining encrypted XML signing certificates and API integration credentials from the ETA e-invoicing platform; (6) appoint a local tax representative and file Form T-1 confirming the registered address, directors, and responsible auditor. Standard processing time for a non-resident-owned LLC is 5–15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident juridical person under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 (as amended by Law No. 3/2023). It is subject to corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide income, with a territorial scope rule for non-resident shareholders; dividends paid to non-residents are subject to 10% withholding tax. No fiscal transparency or US-style pass-through treatment is available for LLCs; the LLC itself files, pays, and claims VAT. — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) obtain commercial registration (CR) issued by the General Authority for Investment and the Ministry of Trade; (2) register with the ETA through the unified electronic tax registration service (Injaz / ETA e-services portal) using the CR, constitutional documents, power of attorney (if applicable), proof of physical premises, and designated bank data; (3) receive a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) that simultaneously serves as the corporate income tax, VAT, withholding tax, and stamp duty identifier; (4) for VAT purposes, verify whether the annual taxable supply value exceeds the mandatory threshold — if so, VAT registration is completed automatically upon TIN issuance; if below the threshold, a VAT registration application is filed separately; (5) after TIN allocation, enrol for the e-invoicing system by obtaining encrypted XML signing certificates and API integration credentials from the ETA e-invoicing platform; (6) appoint a local tax representative and file Form T-1 confirming the registered address, directors, and responsible auditor. Standard processing time for a non-resident-owned LLC is 5–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident juridical person under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 (as amended by Law No. 3/2023). It is subject to corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide income, with a territorial scope rule for non-resident shareholders; dividends paid to non-residents are subject to 10% withholding tax. No fiscal transparency or US-style pass-through treatment is available for LLCs; the LLC itself files, pays, and claims VAT.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-classification election exists under Egyptian law. Available statutory elections: (a) group consolidation election under Article 51 of Income Tax Law 91/2005 (minimum 90% ownership, binding for five years); (b) tax loss carryforward election (up to five years, subject to ownership continuity conditions); (c) VAT bad-debt relief election for irrecoverable receivables; (d) accelerated depreciation election for machinery and computer hardware; (e) advance pricing agreement (APA) application for transfer pricing certainty; (f) election for services providers to compute foreign-exchange gains/losses on an annual basis. Hybrid mismatch elections under BEPS Action 2 have not been codified as an elective regime in Egypt as of the current law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT registration (EGP 250,000 for voluntary registration); foreign e-commerce and digital service suppliers without a fixed establishment in Egypt are required to register for VAT from the first Egyptian-source supply with no minimum threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 21 of VAT Law 67/2016 for: (i) physical export of goods outside the Egyptian customs territory, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration; (ii) export-related services directly connected to goods (handling, transport, freight, insurance, international inspection/quality certification) provided the proceeds are transferred from abroad in freely convertible currency; (iii) supplies of goods and services to free zones treated as being outside the tax territory. The Egyptian LLC must demonstrate a genuine B2B export contract with a non-resident buyer, actual delivery of the industrial MRO hardware beyond Egyptian borders, and full receipt of the foreign-currency sales price in an Egyptian bank account.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document (SAD) stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority, including the MASKOUT release certificate
  • Commercial invoice and B2B MRO catalogue order documentation correlated to the export declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipment and matching the stated INCOTERMS
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by the receiving Egyptian bank proving that foreign-currency export proceeds were credited in Egypt
  • ETA Export Zero-Rating Claim Form with the ETA-issued invoice/reference numbers from the e-invoicing platform
  • Packing list and insurance certificate (required for CIF/CIP terms)
  • Certificate of Origin (optional for MRO hardware; required only where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

Mandatory nationwide e-invoicing applies to all VAT-registered B2B and B2G transactions. Invoices must be transmitted to the ETA e-invoicing platform as digital documents (UBL 2.1 / XML), signed with the taxpayer's X.509 certificate, validated in near-real-time (within 24 hours of issuance), and returned with an ETA-generated UUID and QR code. There is no physical paper stamping; clearance is electronic only through the ETA fiscal validation firewall. B2C sales are progressively covered by the parallel e-receipt mandate. Integration is performed via REST/OAuth2 APIs over TLS 1.2. Non-compliance or digital-signature bypass triggers automatic VAT re-assessment and monetary penalties ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 5,000,000 per violation under the amended tax procedure rules.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt jointly reconcile export zero-rated sales against the customs SAD database and bank foreign-currency credit records. If export proceeds are not repatriated to and credited in an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the customs release date, the zero-rating is retroactively revoked. The ETA will assess the full 14% VAT on gross export turnover, plus statutory delay penalties, plus an additional violation amount of up to EGP 50,000 per incorrect return under Article 34 of VAT Law 67/2016. In fraud or deliberate misstatement cases, Article 35 imposes a fine equal to the tax evaded and terms of imprisonment, as intensified by Law 154/2022, and e-invoicing misdeclarations attract fines of EGP 100,000 to EGP 5,000,000 per false declaration. For corporate income tax purposes, un-repatriated or undocumented export receivables may be recharacterised as deemed distributions to the foreign parent, triggering 10% dividend withholding tax, immediate CIT catch-up, and transfer-pricing penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has operated a unified, flexible exchange-rate regime since the CBE decision of 6 March 2024. The Egyptian pound (EGP) is fully convertible at interbank rates for current-account flows — trade, services, profit remittances, and dividend repatriation — with no prior CBE approval for bona fide underlying transactions. Capital-account inflows for foreign equity are liberalized for LLCs, but every foreign-currency receipt must enter through a CBE-licensed bank and be registered with GAFI under the Law on Investment No. 72 of 2017. Dividend, branch-remittance, and liquidation proceeds may be repatriated at the interbank rate upon submission of: (i) audited financial statements, (ii) a tax-clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), and (iii) the bank's FX declaration form. Practical friction is limited to bank-side compliance review queues rather than statutory convertibility ceilings; there is no deposit-holding tenor requirement for non-resident proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: 100% of merchandise export receipts must be repatriated through the Egyptian banking system within 30 days from the date of the bill of lading / air waybill; an extension to 180 days is available only for pre-approved capital-goods and government-procurement contracts with CBE authorization. Mandatory conversion quota: the pre-2024 forced-surrender rule was rescinded by the March 2024 CBE liberalization — exporters may retain the full export value in foreign currency in a CBE-licensed FCY account or convert at the interbank rate. Retention is lawful only where the corresponding customs entry (C21 declaration) is presented to the receiving bank, and utilization of retained FCY is restricted to current-account payments of the same trading business (raw-material imports, trade-finance settlement) to curtail FX fronting and speculative warehousing. The receiving bank must record each repatriation in the CBE consolidated FX database; late receipt is treated as a compliance event requiring an explanatory memorandum to the MLTFCU.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — البنك المركزي المصري, the sole monetary authority and banking supervisor under Law No. 194 of 2020. FX operations, payment systems, and bank licensing are administered by the CBE, with concurrent oversight by the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for inward equity registration and the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (MLTFCU) for AML/CFT enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail. Under Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Fintech-Enabling Law No. 5 of 2022, non-resident e-money institutions (EMIs) licensed in the UK/EU or Asia-Pacific are not recognized as banks in Egypt and cannot hold the LLC's EGP or FCY deposits, open SWIFT-branch accounts, or clear Egyptian electronic payments without a CBE license. Legal viability is limited to two permitted configurations: (i) use of a licensed onshore fintech acquirer (e.g., Paymob, Fawry+) as a card-acceptance front-end, with all settlement sweeps landing in a CBE-licensed operational bank account; or (ii) feeder corporate accounts at foreign EMIs for small non-resident receipts which are subsequently swept by SWIFT MT103 into the Egyptian operational account. In both cases, the settlement leg must terminate at a Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the shareholder's corporate bank into a segregated Capital Account held at the chosen CBE-licensed Egyptian bank. The wire must carry the IMF-BPM6-style balance-of-payments reason code for equity subscription in the non-financial corporate sector (capital contribution category), the LLC's GAFI investment registration reference, and the account beneficiary name exactly matching the Egyptian commercial registry extract. Before the funds are credited, the bank requires: (i) a fully cleared KYC/UBO file, (ii) GAFI registration or investment certificate, (iii) a certified board resolution designating Capital Account signatories and the subscribed amount, (iv) a CPA/auditor source-of-funds letter for the specific wire, and (v) the ETA tax-identification confirmation. Upon receipt, the bank either converts the inflow at the interbank reference rate or maintains the FCY balance in the Capital Account at the investor's written election. The account is marked 'restricted — capital injection', reported to GAFI and CBE in the consolidated FX database, and may not be netted against operational trade payments. No inward equity remittance is legally recognized outside a CBE-licensed bank channel.The treasury clears supplier disbursements on three rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for routine MRO replenishment payments up to approximately USD 50,000 equivalent — the bank verifies the import invoice, customs C21 classification code, and the IMF-style trade reason code (imports of goods) before execution at the interbank offer rate; (2) commercial documentary letters of credit issued via MT700 for bulk procurement tranches, settled through MT202/MT910 reimbursement against compliant presentation (commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate, BL/AWB, and certificate of origin), with a CBE-licensed advising/negotiating bank discounting the supplier's drafts; and (3) inbound B2B card-not-present e-commerce settlement through a licensed onshore acquirer (CIB/NBE/Paymob), which clears card transactions via the Misr Interbank ACH/RTGS infrastructure and nets daily into the LLC's operating account, subject to a standard chargeback reserve. All outbound SWIFT flows must be pre-funded with cleared funds and remain within the bank's sanctioned daily FCY limit framework.Onshore outright EGP/USD FX forwards executed through a CBE-authorized dealer for trade-backed maturities of up to 12 months; naked or speculative forward cover is prohibited.Onshore EGP/USD FX swaps used to align the liquidity gap between export-receipt inflows and import-payable outflows in the treasury calendar.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on EGP executed through international banks (London/New York desks) referencing the CBE published reference rate — deployed as a fallback hedge when onshore dealer limits are exhausted.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) on a real-exposure basis under an ISDA Master Agreement with an OECD or Egyptian counterparty, subject to CBE derivative eligibility, margin, and trade-documentation requirements.Plain-vanilla FX options (EGP call / USD put) via CBE-approved derivatives providers for 3- to 6-month distribution or dividend-repatriation cycles, permitted only when supported by documented current-account exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury rail. Under Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Fintech-Enabling Law No. 5 of 2022, non-resident e-money institutions (EMIs) licensed in the UK/EU or Asia-Pacific are not recognized as banks in Egypt and cannot hold the LLC's EGP or FCY deposits, open SWIFT-branch accounts, or clear Egyptian electronic payments without a CBE license. Legal viability is limited to two permitted configurations: (i) use of a licensed onshore fintech acquirer (e.g., Paymob, Fawry+) as a card-acceptance front-end, with all settlement sweeps landing in a CBE-licensed operational bank account; or (ii) feeder corporate accounts at foreign EMIs for small non-resident receipts which are subsequently swept by SWIFT MT103 into the Egyptian operational account. In both cases, the settlement leg must terminate at a Tier-1 commercial bank.
