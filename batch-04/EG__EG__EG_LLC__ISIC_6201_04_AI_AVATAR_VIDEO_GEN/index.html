Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — official portals: incometax.gov.eg (TIN/income tax registration), eta.gov.eg and einvoicing.eta.gov.eg (VAT registration, e-invoicing clearance and tax card services)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains its statutory TIN and VAT ID by: (1) completing incorporation and commercial registration at the GAFI-affiliated Commercial Registry; (2) submitting registration Form 1 under the Executive Regulations of Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 and VAT Law No. 67/2016 to the ETA through the online tax portal (incometax.gov.eg or ETA e-services), attaching certified Arabic articles of association, commercial registration certificate, lease contract, identity documents of managers and shareholders, board resolution appointing the fiscal representative, and a notarized power of attorney; (3) receiving the unified Tax Card (TIN) normally within 10 to 30 business days and the opening of a separate VAT registration file; (4) for non-resident suppliers of digitally supplied services consumed in Egypt, mandatory VAT registration is performed through an appointed Egyptian tax representative. The same TIN is used for income tax, employer payroll, VAT, customs, and stamp tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egypt-resident LLC is a separate taxable entity subject to corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide income with a foreign tax credit mechanism; LLCs are not pass-through vehicles; branches of non-resident companies are taxed only on Egypt-source income; no general Tax_Exempt_Zone classification applies to standard LLCs outside special economic zones.

Available election forms

Egyptian law provides no check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (a) fiscal unity/consolidated tax filing for resident companies with at least 95% common ownership; (b) simplified cash-basis accounting for qualifying small enterprises; (c) investment incentive and Special Economic Zone elections under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; (d) foreign tax credit election for repatriated foreign profits; (e) no domestic hybrid-mismatch election — Egypt applies OECD BEPS-aligned CFC, transfer pricing, and treaty limitation-on-benefits rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies — mandatory VAT registration for resident suppliers; non-resident providers of electronically supplied services (including AI video synthesis and virtual avatar SaaS) are registrable where services are consumed in Egypt even below the threshold, through an appointed fiscal representative

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) goods physically exported from Egypt outside the customs territory and (b) services performed in Egypt but supplied to and consumed by non-residents abroad, including exported software licenses, AI-generated video content, and SaaS/virtual avatar subscription services, provided the supplier holds a valid VAT registration, issues an ETA-cleared e-invoice at 0%, and retains documented proof of non-resident supply, actual offshore consumption, and receipt of payment in convertible foreign currency; services ancillary to exported goods also qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ECS — Export Customs Statement) submitted through the Egyptian Customs NAFEZA portal for any goods or software-on-media movement
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export (for media or file-based delivery; for pure cloud SaaS, the export is evidenced instead by server and consumption logs demonstrating non-resident usage)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank FX credit advice issued by a licensed Egyptian bank confirming receipt from the non-resident customer in convertible currency
  • Executed export sales or subscription contract and commercial correspondence with the non-resident party
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice (Code64) showing the zero-rate indicator and the customer master file (CMF) code of the buyer

ETA mandates real-time e-invoicing clearance: invoices must be transmitted in XML/JSON under the ETA Invoice Data Protocol through the ETA Data Exchange API (P2P integration), validated in real time, and stamped with a 'Code64' QR code before issuance; non-compliant issuers are barred from VAT deduction and subject to fines; B2C e-receipts are being phased in on the same clearance API.

ETA performs retrospective audits of export zero-rating and refund claims; failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day window, submission of fraudulent documents, or diversion of digital services to domestic consumption triggers VAT reassessment at 14% on the full consideration, monthly delay surcharges, fines ranging from EGP 50,000 up to EGP 5 million, and/or imprisonment under Articles 77 to 79 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, together with full clawback of input VAT credits claimed on export-related purchases; zero-rating is withdrawn retroactively for all uncovered periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) is convertible under a liberalised managed float maintained since March 2024; current-account convertibility (software licensing, SaaS exports, remittance of services proceeds) is permitted through CBE-licensed banks against documented underlying transactions, although episodic USD liquidity constraints and bank-level queuing may affect execution speed. Capital-account operations (FDI equity injection, inter-company borrowing, dividend and profit repatriation) require bank intermediation, GAFI/CBE registration of the investment, audited financials, tax clearance, and statutory proofs of after-tax distributable profits; there are no blanket exchange controls prohibiting remittance, but every transfer must be justified by a purpose code and a complete documentary file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Software/SaaS export revenues qualify as services exports and must be repatriated in full value directly to a CBE-licensed account in Egypt; the CBE requires credit within a target collection period of 30 calendar days from the invoice/payment date, with a maximum permissible extension of a further 30 days on documented justification. The mandatory conversion (surrender) ratio is currently 0% in the liberalised regime: proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely in the interbank market, but they must enter the Egyptian banking system and cannot be warehoused indefinitely in offshore PSP or EMI accounts.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020; foreign-exchange and anti-money-laundering supervision is executed jointly with the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU), while investment registration is administered by the General Authority for Investment (GAFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low — offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) hold no CBE licence, cannot maintain EGP balances, and cannot lawfully act as the final receiving and warehousing vehicle for Egyptian services export proceeds; they are acceptable only as ancillary collection/payout conduits with full and timely repatriation to a CBE-licensed bank within the statutory window. Onshore licensed payment providers (Fawry, Paymob, e-finance) are the structurally compliant alternative for merchant acquiring and settle directly into the LLC's Egyptian account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder remits by SWIFT MT103 with Category Purpose Code 111/112 (investment, transfer to company account) and narrative 'FDI equity contribution / paid-in share capital' into the CBE-licensed bank's nostro; the bank books the receipt to the LLC's capital escrow (settlement) account, issues an advice of receipt for GAFI/CBE investment registration, and releases funds to the operating account only after the Commercial Registry reflects the shareholding or capital increase. Documentary verification comprises the GAFI incorporation certificate, board resolution on share issuance, UBO declarations, source-of-funds evidence, and the audited subscription agreement; no CBE prior approval is required for ordinary capital subscriptions, but the bank must attribute the inflow to the correct balance-of-payments direct-investment category.For SaaS operations, outbound liquidity settles via SWIFT MT103 (Category Purpose Code 104 for services, 101 for trade payments, 112 for inter-company advances) through the corporate bank after documentary checks (service agreement, invoice, tax/withholding confirmation); large one-off software or infrastructure purchases may use sight Commercial LCs, but the standard SaaS practice is clean payments with verified invoices. Merchant-collected revenues from Stripe/Paddle (or onshore PSPs Fawry/Paymob) are swept to the LLC's Egyptian USD/EGP account by SWIFT within the 30-day repatriation window, and foreign-currency outflows (cloud, contractors, royalties, dividends) are purchased through the interbank market with hedging support from the same relationship bank.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts (fixed-date and window) with CBE-licensed banks against documented service-export receivables and payablesFX swaps (spot/forward combination) for working-capital currency conversion timingPlain-vanilla foreign exchange options on a hedge-only basis where the bank offers the productOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) routed through licensed intermediary banks strictly against underlying EGP exposure, with speculation prohibitedCross-currency swaps for long-term USD funding matched to hard-currency service revenues

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatorily required when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EGP 8 million per fiscal year. Master File is required when the global group consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 200 million and the group meets the OECD CbCR threshold equivalent, regardless of Egyptian transaction value.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code akin to UAE. However, the ETA applies a general anti-abuse rule and substance-over-form doctrine under Law 91/2005. Controlled transactions and corporate vehicles must demonstrate real management and activities in Egypt; shell or conduit arrangements that artificially shift profits are disregarded and reconstructed under the arm's length principle. Beneficial ownership is required for all treaty relief claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Willful Falsification of Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 133 and related provisions of Law No. 91 of 2005, tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of not less than 3 years and not more than 5 years, plus a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000. The unpaid tax is immediately collectible with prejudgment interest. The ETA may additionally impose an administrative penalty equal to the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or participate in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax. The corporate veil is pierced under fraud or abuse of law principles, exposing personal bank accounts, and travel bans may be issued under criminal proceedings. Imprisonment is imposed directly on the responsible director or manager, not merely on the corporate entity.
Shell Company / Artificial Profit Shifting with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Egyptian tax law and judicial doctrine disallow artificial arrangements. The ETA may recharacterize the transactions, impose 22.5% corporate tax on the reattributed income, assess a 20% annual late-payment penalty, and impose a civil fine of up to EGP 20,000. For treaty-shopping arrangements, the ETA may deny all DTA benefits and apply the statutory 20% royalty and 20% interest WHT, rather than the reduced treaty rate.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell LLC that uses the vehicle to commit tax fraud face piercing of the corporate veil. They are jointly liable for the entire tax shortfall and fines. Civil trademark or fraud suits can be brought by commercial counterparties; the ETA can request travel bans against directors who attempt to leave Egypt while a criminal charge is pending; imprisonment follows criminal conviction for fraudulent tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Terrorist Financing Violations in Revenue Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended), entities must implement robust KYC procedures. Penalties for AML violations include a fine ranging from EGP 5,000 to EGP 10,000,000 depending on extent, and license suspension or withdrawal of the payment intermediary approval (Stripe/Paddle). Knowingly engaging in money laundering is punishable by imprisonment up to 15 years and a fine equal to between 300% and 500% of the laundered amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally or recklessly bypass the internal AML controls bear personal criminal liability. They may be imprisoned for up to 10 years for aiding money laundering. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets are ordered upon initiation of the criminal case. The corporate veil is pierced for fraud and irregularity, and officers are not indemnified by the company's articles of association.
