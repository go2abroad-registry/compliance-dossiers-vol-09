Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), established under Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 108 of 2021 as the single national tax administration under the Ministry of Finance. Primary e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing platform: https://einvoice.gov.eg; mandatory e-receipt platform: https://ereceipt.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

For a locally incorporated Egyptian LLC: (1) incorporate through GAFI One-Stop Shop and obtain Commercial Registry certificate; (2) within 30 days of commencing activity, apply to the competent ETA district office for the statutory Tax Card, which embeds the TIN (national tax identification number); (3) file VAT registration where annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed EGP 500,000 (compulsory) or where turnover exceeds EGP 50,000 (voluntary election); (4) onboard into the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt environment by registering the Tax Card, obtaining ETA digital certificates, and testing the API integration. For a non-resident foreign entity with no Egyptian establishment, obtain a direct ETA TIN/VAT ID, appoint a tax representative in Egypt, and, if carrying on a trade in Egypt through a fixed place, register the branch/permanent establishment with GAFI and the ETA.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

None. Egypt does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections, partnership-transparency elections for LLCs, or hybrid-mismatch elections. The Egyptian LLC is mandatorily classified as a resident juridical person taxed at entity level under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and is separately assessed; group relief or consolidated taxation is unavailable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; voluntary registration available where annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 50,000; inclusion in the mandatory e-invoice/e-receipt regime is phased by turnover and applies to all VAT-registered taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 10 of Law No. 67 of 2016 applies to: (a) tangible goods physically exported from Egypt, provided the goods are exported within ninety (90) days from the date of supply (or such extended period as the ETA approves) and the sale is settled in foreign currency repatriated to Egypt within one hundred and eighty (180) days; (b) services rendered for the account of a non-resident where the benefit of the service is consumed entirely outside Egypt; and (c) qualifying supplies to designated free zones. A specialty tea salon/lounge exporting packaged tea or rendering tea-ceremony services abroad must be VAT-registered, hold contemporaneous export documentation, and maintain a complete audit trail of foreign-currency receipts to sustain the 0% rate and claim input VAT refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (final certified export entry) evidencing legal exportation of the goods
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) with corresponding packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency in an Egyptian bank account within 180 days
  • Commercial export invoice, signed sales contract or purchase order
  • Certificate of Origin plus any phytosanitary/food-safety export certificate required for tea and food-contact ceramic products

Mandatory e-invoicing (B2B/B2G) and e-receipts (B2C) via ETA-authorised platforms: all VAT-registered taxpayers must issue invoices/receipts in ETA-defined XML/JSON schemas and transmit them near-real-time through REST APIs authenticated with ETA digital certificates; each document receives a digital stamp comprising a unique UUID, electronic signature (KID), and QR code; the ETA validates and clears documents electronically, rejects non-compliant documents, and compares declared output VAT with the buyer's input VAT ledger, with penalties for delayed, missing, or altered fiscal documents.

ETA export-audit scrutiny is stringent: failure to prove physical export within 90 days, failure to repatriate proceeds within 180 days, or submission of false, backdated, or fabricated export papers causes retroactive recharacterisation of the zero-rated supply as a domestic taxable supply at 14% VAT, with assessment of the VAT plus monthly late-payment additional tax of 2% per month (statutorily capped), fines of EGP 5,000-50,000 per violation under Law 67 of 2016, clawback of any input VAT refunded or offset through the exporter ledger, fiscal-file suspension or revocation of e-invoice digital certificates, and criminal tax-evasion liability (imprisonment and/or fines) for fraudulent zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a heavily managed floating exchange regime. Current account transactions are largely convertible: imports, services, royalties, and profit remittances may be routed through CBE-licensed commercial banks without prior CBE approval provided documentary evidence (invoice, tax card, contract) is submitted. Capital account transactions require CBE approval: inbound foreign direct investment is facilitated via registration with CBE on the MISR (Investment) system, but outward remittances of divestment proceeds, loan repayments, and dividends are subject to source-of-funds verification and CBE registration of the original inward remittance. Profit remittance hurdles are moderate but require prompt filing of annual audited financials, tax clearance certificate, and CBE Form 12/13. In practice, hard-currency liquidity pressure in Egypt may cause administrative delays and dividend remittance prioritization by the CBE; as of 2025, there is no blanket ban but banks may impose de facto rationing during shortages. No forced surrender of receipts from current account services is currently applied, but all FX inflows must be repatriated and credited to a CBE-authorized account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be fully repatriated to Egypt within six (6) months from the date of shipment per CBE Circular No. 86 of November 2022 (amended by CBE Circular of April 2024). The mandatory conversion ratio is currently 0% - exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign currency in their domiciled foreign currency accounts at a CBE-licensed bank, provided they provide the original customs bill of lading, the export declaration, and an attested invoice/service contract. However, the CBE may, from time to time, impose a temporary 50% surrender requirement (as was done between March and November 2023); current market conditions indicate the surrender mandate is suspended. Failure to repatriate within the 6-month timeline triggers a civil penalty of 0.2% of the unremitted amount per week and loss of export license benefits.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - also exercising delegated foreign exchange administration powers under Law No. 194 of 2020 and the 1982 Foreign Exchange Law, as amended by Decree No. 24 of 2021.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Egyptian law (Law No. 194 of 2020, as amended) reserves deposit-taking and payment facilitation from Egyptian residents to banks and licensed payment service providers (PSPs) authorized by CBE. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, Currencycloud) have no passporting right into Egypt; they can only be used on a complementary basis outside Egypt for receiving non-resident settlement accounts, but they cannot be the primary operating account for the LLC in Egypt. In practice, CBE wire transfers require a Correspondent Bank relationship which outlaw EMIs. Acceptable only if the entity simultaneously holds a full-service Egyptian bank account and uses the EMI as an ancillary FX conversion tool. Recommendation: avoid structuring bank account hierarchy with an EMI as 'lead'. If used, ensure the EMI is candorally disclosed to CBE and the substance requirements of the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Financing of Terrorism Law No. 80 of 2002 (amended) are met.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC, inbound equity capital must be routed via a CBE-licensed Authorized Dealer bank in Egypt using SWIFT MT103 wire transfer. The sender must clearly quote the CBE investment code 'Capital Import for Direct Investment - Employment Service' (purpose code 401) plus the independent inbound reference number assigned by the CBE's 'Capital Registration' system. The Egyptian bank will generate a 'Inward Remittance Confirmation Letter' (Form 39) within 5 business days and register the capital on the CBE's Foreign Investment database, crucial for future profit/dividend repatriation. For loans: another purpose code '901' (Foreign Loan) must be filed, and the lender must submit a CBE loan declaration, after which the loan can be imported through the same channel. All wires must be in USD, EUR, or GBP; no EGP is permitted for foreign capital injection. Verification: the bank will ask for signed audited financial statements, the LLC's articles of association, and proof of the underlying tea-salon business activity.Trade settlements for importing tea leaf, manufacturing raw ceramics, and lounge operational supplies should be cleared using the following rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct-supplier wires under USD 500,000; (b) Sight Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) for larger purchase orders - available at CIB, Banque Misr, NBE - confirmed irrevocable L/C issued by the Egyptian importer's bank, with payment term up to 120 days; (c) Documentary Collection (D/P or D/A) with Bill of Lading; and (d) for recurring domestic supplier payments, use the Egyptian National Payment System (NPBS) via local Chequand e-clearing. In all cases, the Egyptian customs invoice (Export Invoice) must be dated and stamped by the bank. To avoid penalties, use the correct CBE 'Trade Goods' purpose code (e.g., 0001/200) and attach the commercial contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and Egypt Customs import declaration (AZID). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low. Egyptian law (Law No. 194 of 2020, as amended) reserves deposit-taking and payment facilitation from Egyptian residents to banks and licensed payment service providers (PSPs) authorized by CBE. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, Currencycloud) have no passporting right into Egypt; they can only be used on a complementary basis outside Egypt for receiving non-resident settlement accounts, but they cannot be the primary operating account for the LLC in Egypt. In practice, CBE wire transfers require a Correspondent Bank relationship which outlaw EMIs. Acceptable only if the entity simultaneously holds a full-service Egyptian bank account and uses the EMI as an ancillary FX conversion tool. Recommendation: avoid structuring bank account hierarchy with an EMI as 'lead'. If used, ensure the EMI is candorally disclosed to CBE and the substance requirements of the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Financing of Terrorism Law No. 80 of 2002 (amended) are met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when aggregate related-party controlled transactions exceed EGP 5,000,000 during the tax year; Master File obligations arise when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 45,000,000. The Egyptian Tax Authority may nevertheless request TP documentation case-by-case regardless of these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Egyptian tax law embeds a substance-over-form GAAR (Article 9, Law 91 of 2005) and a broad permanent-establishment definition. There is no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to the UAE, but the tea salon must evidence physical premises, engaged employees, actual management decisions, and genuine operational activity in Egypt to uphold treaty residency claims and avoid re-characterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to two to five times the evaded tax, imprisonment for up to five years, judicial re-assessment, interest on unpaid tax, and forced attachment of company assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; a conviction triggers travel bans, property seizure, and imprisonment for the signatory officer; the corporate veil is pierced where bad faith is established.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterisation of the transaction, disallowance of expenses, additional WHT at 20% on deemed royalties/interest, an anti-avoidance penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided, and possible suspension or revocation of the company's commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Courts will disregard the LLC's separate personality; directors and shareholders may be held personally liable for the entire tax debt, ordered to compensate creditors, and barred from leaving Egypt pending settlement.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Law 80 of 2002 as amended, penalties include imprisonment from five to fifteen years, fines up to EGP 2,000,000, confiscation of proceeds, freezing of bank accounts, and possible closure of the establishment.

Director personal liability: Officers, directors, and the AML compliance officer are criminally liable absent proof of due diligence; the corporate veil is pierced for knowing participation, leading to travel bans, extradition, and personal asset forfeiture.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: A primary TP adjustment increasing taxable income, a 40% penalty levied on the adjustment amount, additional withholding tax on the reclassified distributions, and a fine for incomplete documentation under Decree 181 of 2019.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incomplete tax returns or knowingly approve non-arm's-length intercompany contracts face joint liability for the additional tax and penalties; in aggravated cases of false documentation, directors may be prosecuted for tax evasion and subjected to travel restrictions.
