Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; central e-services portal www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipt portal invoicing.eta.gov.eg; physical registration through ETA district tax offices and GAFI one-stop shops.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the EG LLC with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Commercial Registry; obtain the commercial registration extract, Arabic-language memorandum of association, registered-office occupancy/lease certificate and bank account opening certificate. Step 2: File the ETA Registration Form (request for the Tax Card) attaching the commercial registration extract, notarized Articles of Association, board resolution appointing the tax representative, passports/national IDs of managers and shareholders, office lease, and power of attorney for the appointed tax agent (if any). Step 3: ETA site inspection and issuance of the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card within approximately 2-4 weeks. Step 4: Mandatory VAT registration in the same unified tax file once taxable annual turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; the ETA endorses VAT registration on the Tax Card. Step 5: Mandatory onboarding to the ETA e-invoicing system: register the legal entity on invoicing.eta.gov.eg, generate OAuth2 Client ID/Client Secret, upload the PKCS#12 digital signature certificate, and connect the ERP/accounting system to the ETA validation API before issuing invoices. Non-resident shareholders are not required to obtain a separate TIN for the LLC; their Egyptian-source dividends and interest become subject to withholding by the LLC (10% on dividends, 20% on interest under prevailing law) reported via the ETA e-withholding module.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Egypt; the EG LLC is a per se corporate taxpayer taxed at entity level on its worldwide income (22.5% corporate rate under Law No. 91/2005, subject to foreign tax credit relief). Available elections and administrative options: (i) simplified presumptive tax election under Small and Micro Enterprises Law No. 152/2020 for eligible entities with annual turnover up to EGP 10 million (0.5%-2.75% rates, irrevocable during the 5-year regime, subject to GAFI/ETA pre-approval); (ii) election to claim the investment cost deduction (50% of paid-in capital costs) under the Investment Law No. 72/2017 for qualifying new projects; (iii) election to utilize Suez Canal Economic Zone or public free zone incentives where the licensing entity is registered in the zone (not assumed for this onshore LLC); (iv) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold to recover input VAT on pre-opening container acquisition costs; (v) application for an agreed VAT refund offset plan with the ETA and use of tax dispute reconciliation committees; (vi) no consolidated group filing is permitted, each LLC files standalone.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies (goods, equipment leasing and services) for mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration below the threshold is permitted with ETA approval to allow input VAT recovery; exporters may obtain accelerated refund treatment once e-invoice and customs records are linked.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 2 of VAT Law No. 67/2016, the following may be zero-rated: (i) goods physically exported outside Egypt; (ii) services rendered to non-resident persons and wholly consumed outside Egypt; (iii) services directly connected with international transport of goods or passengers, including the leasing of ISO containers to non-resident ocean carriers for exclusive use on international voyages carrying export cargo from Egypt, where the container is released under terminal out-of-gate records and is not deployed in the domestic freight chain; (iv) ancillary export services such as stevedoring, lashing, terminal handling, weighing and international onward carriage when supported by shipping instructions and bills of lading. The zero-rated bucket gives full credit for input VAT on locally acquired container yard services, depot repairs, security and financing costs under the ETA recovery ratio; input VAT allocated to domestic (non-export) container use remains restricted. Service invoices must be issued in foreign currency with a stated 0% VAT rate through the e-invoicing system and matched to customs and banking records.

  • Customs export declaration (al-Bayan al-Jumruki) bearing the customs seal and export manifest number
  • Original or carrier-issued sea waybill / Bill of Lading (B/L) evidencing the container's outbound movement from an Egyptian port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of the charter-hire/lease payment in foreign currency to the EG LLC's account
  • Zero-rated VAT e-invoice and the signed container master lease agreement with the non-resident shipping line
  • Container terminal gate-in/gate-out reports and depot release orders proving actual cross-border deployment of the specific container units
  • ETA VAT refund application form together with allocation schedules of input VAT between export and domestic container use

Mandatory real-time fiscal validation of all B2B invoices through the centralized ETA e-invoicing system. Every invoice must be transmitted to the ETA in JSON/XML (ETA schema) via REST API with OAuth2 authentication and PKCS#12 digital signing at the moment of issuance; the ETA returns a unique UUID and an ETA-originated QR code which must be printed or displayed on the customer-facing invoice. Rejection of an invoice by the ETA deprives the supply of VAT legal effect. Phased e-invoice onboarding covers all VAT-registered taxpayers; the complementary e-receipt mandate applies at B2C point-of-sale level. Taxpayers must retain the complete digital invoice trail with GS1 supplier/item codes and file VAT returns electronically via the ETA portal.

Export zero-rating claims are audited by the ETA within the general six-year VAT assessment window. The ETA cross-checks the e-invoice pool (UUID), customs export declarations, container gate movement data and the bank's Foreign Exchange Reporting System. Failure to prove physical export, submission of forged bills of lading or customs documents, or non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days triggers: (i) reversal of input VAT credits and reassessment of the underlying output VAT at 14% with monthly interest (central bank discount rate plus 2%) from the original due date; (ii) administrative fines of EGP 1,000-50,000 per non-compliant invoice; (iii) civil evasion penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded; and (iv) criminal prosecution, including imprisonment, under the VAT Law and the Egyptian Penal Code for fraud or forgery in customs and banking documentation. The ETA may also freeze the taxpayer's e-invoice registration and Tax Card, preventing future export clearances until taxes, interest and fines are fully settled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-flexible exchange-rate regime with statutory current-account convertibility for bonafide trade, services, loan servicing, and profit remittance, albeit subject to transaction-level documentary review by the Authorized Dealer. There is no formal requirement to obtain CBE pre-approval for equity contributions under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, provided the inbound wire is routed through a licensed bank and accompanied by the corporate subscription documents and investment registration details. Capital-account transactions, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, enjoy legal transfer guarantees but may be deferred or queued at the Authorized Dealer during periods of foreign-currency liquidity stress. Profit remittance hurdles include submission of the annual audited financial statements, corporate tax return and tax-clearance certificate, evidence that the EGP or foreign-currency account is in good standing, the board resolution authorizing the distribution, and the bank's internal compliance sign-off. For an LLC with foreign ownership, profits may be distributed only from realized net profits after statutory reserves are allocated under the Companies Law; non-resident shareholders are subject to dividend tax except where a tax treaty reduces the rate. Current-account payments for trade, lease fees, insurance, maintenance, and ordinary operating expenses are generally convertible if supported by invoices, contracts, customs documents, and the correct CBE purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current CBE liberalized FX framework, export proceeds from goods and services must be repatriated into Egypt through an Authorized Dealer bank within a commercial norm of 90 days from the date of invoice, bill of lading, or services completion, unless a longer documented payment term is justified and accepted by the bank. There is no standing statutory mandatory conversion ratio applicable to export receipts; exporters may retain foreign-currency proceeds in foreign-currency accounts and use them for permitted foreign-currency obligations or voluntarily sell them to the Authorized Dealer at the prevailing interbank-market exchange rate. The CBE retains emergency authority to impose temporary surrender requirements, and in such circumstance it would issue a decree or circular establishing the percentage to be surrendered to the Central Bank or sold to banks. For the ISO container leasing company, all lease income, container damage compensation, relocation fees, and refurbishment contributions must be credited to the Egyptian bank account promptly and declared with the underlying lease agreement and CBE purpose code in order to avoid under-invoicing or trade-based money-laundering classification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), exercising foreign-exchange administration under Law No. 194 of 2020 promulgating the Central Bank and Banking System Law, Law No. 113 of 1994 on Foreign Exchange, and the CBE's monetary and FX-market regulations. All foreign-exchange operations must be executed through a CBE-licensed Authorized Dealer, i.e., a commercial bank holding a valid CBE banking license, or through a licensed exchange house for limited cash transactions. The CBE is the sole authority for licensing Authorized Dealers, setting reserve requirements, prescribing documentary evidence for remittances, monitoring balance-of-payments reporting, and imposing temporary emergency FX restrictions in cases of severe currency pressure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for any offshore electronic-money institution operating as the primary account provider; MEDIUM for an offshore FinTech or EMI being integrated only as a software front-end while the actual bank accounts are held at a CBE-licensed Egyptian commercial bank. Egypt does not permit unlicensed offshore EMIs to accept deposits, maintain EGP clearing accounts, provide payment accounts to Egyptian corporate entities, or access local ACH payments. Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE payment-system regulations restrict deposit-taking and payment initiation services to banks and licensed payment institutions pursuant to the National Payment Council framework. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or MultiPass can assist with euro-route or multi-currency treasury reporting, but settlement must occur into and out of a CBE-licensed bank account in the name of the Egyptian LLC. Use of a third-party EMI as the named receiving bank in the LLC's contracts, invoices, or lease-payment instructions is an AML red flag and may cause the Egyptian Authorized Dealer to freeze the account until full wire provenance is proven.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-subordinated-loan capital must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the LLC's dedicated capital-escrow account at the Egyptian Authorized Dealer. The remitter must use a purpose code substantially corresponding to direct investment in Egyptian equity capital, commonly described as Direct Investment - Equity Capital in Limited Liability Company, and must ensure the beneficiary account name and account number match the Egyptian bank's register. The LLC's bank will request a certified board resolution approving the capital increase or shareholder loan, the GAFI investment registration if the enterprise is registered under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, the amended form in the Commercial Register if capital was increased, passport/USD account details of the subscriber, and a declaration of source of funds. If the capital injection is in-kind, such as physical ISO containers transferred from the parent, customs valuation and GAFI or customs clearance documentation are additionally required because in-kind contributions must be appraised and witnessed before transfer. No prior CBE exchange-control approval is necessary for direct investment equity remittances in the ordinary course under the current liberalized regime, but the Authorized Dealer must file the transaction in the CBE's balance-of-payments reporting system using the prescribed Egyptian external-transaction code. Funds received in foreign currency may either remain in the USD account or be sold to the bank at the prevailing interbank rate if the LLC's subsequent operating disbursements will be in EGP. The bank will also verify that the entity is not a mere conduit: the LLC must maintain actual substance, including Egyptian registered address, tax registration, a board meeting or management function, and economic activity in container fleet leasing.The principal clearing rail for trade payments is the SWIFT MT103 outgoing wire for ordinary lease fees, container purchase settlements, maintenance and repair expenses, insurance premiums, freight, port charges, and payments to overseas container supply vendors. For larger fleet acquisitions, the Egyptian bank can issue an irrevocable confirmed documentary credit through SWIFT MT700/MT701 with MT799 pre-advice, allowing the beneficiary to present invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, insurance certificates, container condition certificates, and other transport documents against payment or acceptance. The LLC must supply the underlying trade contract, pro-forma invoice, and any required compliance declaration to the Authorized Dealer and must submit the post-shipment supporting documents within the CBE-prescribed time limit. Payments for cross-border operating leases of ISO containers may be treated as service payments, not imports, if the containers are not cleared for domestic use and remain under the foreign owner's or lessor's prow; the LLC should nevertheless keep a full customs bill of entry or temporary admission record where containers enter Egyptian territory. If the LLC pays for containers that will be imported into Egypt and then leased inside Egypt, the payment must be supported by a customs import declaration and the correct import-goods purpose code. Outbound EGP payments to local parties should use the domestic ACH or Egypt's real-time gross settlement system for EGP balances, while foreign-currency disbursements should be executed from the USD operational account through SWIFT, ensuring all payments correspond to an actual signed obligation and do not breach the aggregate bank-approved trade-deal limit.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards between USD and EGP concluded with an Egyptian CBE-licensed Authorized Dealer, limited to the notional and tenor of actual receivables or payables such as forecast lease bills, container purchase installments, insurance, or loan repayments.FX swaps with the same Authorized Dealer to cover temporary mismatches in the timing of USD receipts and EGP operating disbursements, including swap contracts for conversion mismatches on equipment purchases and lease collections.Cross-currency swaps between USD and EUR or between USD and EGP for long-term container-fleet financing, permitted where the underlying exposure is a bank-approved loan or purchase/lease obligation and the hedge does not create an uncovered speculative position.Non-deliverable forwards involving an offshore counterparty on the USD/EGP exchange rate, only where the hedge has a verifiable underlying commercial exposure and the settlement is structured offshore without delivery of EGP inside Egypt; onshore Egyptian banks are generally conservative in accepting NDFs and require the client to present underlying receivables or payables documentation.Interest-rate and currency basis swaps incidental to the bank finance documentation for container acquisitions, provided the hedge transactions are entered into with the same lender or its approved affiliate and are set out in the facility agreement.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for any offshore electronic-money institution operating as the primary account provider; MEDIUM for an offshore FinTech or EMI being integrated only as a software front-end while the actual bank accounts are held at a CBE-licensed Egyptian commercial bank. Egypt does not permit unlicensed offshore EMIs to accept deposits, maintain EGP clearing accounts, provide payment accounts to Egyptian corporate entities, or access local ACH payments. Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE payment-system regulations restrict deposit-taking and payment initiation services to banks and licensed payment institutions pursuant to the National Payment Council framework. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or MultiPass can assist with euro-route or multi-currency treasury reporting, but settlement must occur into and out of a CBE-licensed bank account in the name of the Egyptian LLC. Use of a third-party EMI as the named receiving bank in the LLC's contracts, invoices, or lease-payment instructions is an AML red flag and may cause the Egyptian Authorized Dealer to freeze the account until full wire provenance is proven.
