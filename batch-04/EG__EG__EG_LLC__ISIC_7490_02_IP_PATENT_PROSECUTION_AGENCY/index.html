Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — official portal: www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing clearance portal: einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; service centers in all 25 governorates and the GAFI Investment One-Stop Shop (OSS).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Egyptian LLC with GAFI (General Authority for Investment) and complete commercial registry enrollment under the Companies Law. Step 2: Submit a Tax Registration Application (Form 1/2) to the competent ETA district within 30 days of commercial registration, attaching the company contract, commercial registry extract, power of attorney, and manager national ID/passport copies. Step 3: ETA issues the 9-digit Egyptian Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and the Unified Tax Card (comprising income tax, VAT, and stamp duty registrations). Step 4: Where annual taxable turnover reaches or is expected to reach EGP 500,000, file a separate VAT registration application; a foreign-owned LLC with no local activity may alternatively discharge VAT via reverse charge on imports of services. Step 5: Activate the ETA e-invoicing credentials by registering the legal representative, validating the TIN, and completing the API-based ERP onboarding test in the ETA sandbox environment. Non-resident managers must appoint a resident tax/financial representative with notarized power of attorney in the absence of a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Egyptian tax law. LLCs are separate legal persons subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income; fiscal pass-through treatment is reserved exclusively to non-incorporated partnerships and silent partnerships (companies of persons). The Unified Tax Law (Law No. 3 of 2024) does not grant LLCs a transparency election. The only relevant elections are: (i) voluntary VAT registration for businesses below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (ii) the option to treat intercompany dividends under the participation exemption; (iii) election for consolidated tax group filing, available only upon ETA approval and subject to the shareholding and voting-right conditions prescribed by the Unified Tax Law; and (iv) the transfer-pricing advance pricing agreement (APA) mechanism for cross-border IP service fees. No hybrid mismatch or branch-remittance elections are available for an Egyptian LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory registration within 30 days of crossing the threshold; voluntary VAT registration permitted below the threshold to enable input VAT recovery, particularly for exporters; non-residents supplying taxable services into Egypt are registrable if a permanent establishment is established, otherwise the Egyptian recipient self-assesses via reverse charge).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Egypt qualify for the 0% rate upon submission of the electronic export declaration and proof of physical departure. Services qualify for the 0% rate if: (i) the recipient is a non-resident with no permanent establishment in Egypt; (ii) the benefit and economic use of the service occur entirely outside Egypt; (iii) the contract and invoice are denominated in a convertible foreign currency; and (iv) the consideration is received through the Egyptian banking system in accordance with Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) rules. For a PCT patent prosecution and trademark agency, ETA scrutinizes whether the service is 'performed and used' in Egypt (e.g., filing/prosecution before the Egyptian Patent Office, local agent liability, domestic enforcement of the granted right); if the place of supply is Egypt, the 0% rate may be denied and the fee taxed at 14%, with input VAT recoverability preserved. A comprehensive place-of-supply/place-of-benefit nexus analysis supported by client contracts, work papers, and foreign-currency remittance evidence is mandatory to sustain zero-rating.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic E-Co export manifest) evidencing clearance of the shipment for goods exports
  • Negotiable Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical movement outside Egypt (goods) or the service-performance dossier (services)
  • Original export service contract and final commercial invoice denominated in convertible foreign currency
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-currency credit advice from an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing full and timely repatriation of export proceeds

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance for all B2B invoices and export invoices issued by VAT-registered taxpayers. The ETA maintains a national e-invoicing API gateway (XML/JSON Saturn schema) that returns an ETA-generated UUID and QR code before the invoice is legally deemed issued. ERRORS: any non-cleared invoice is treated as unissued, denying input VAT deduction to the buyer and exposing the seller to penalties. The e-receipts (B2C) framework is phased in and applies where the agency renders services to individuals. Newly registered taxpayers must complete ERP integration with the ETA sandbox and obtain production credentials at registration; invoices for exported services must also be cleared through the same platform, with 'Export' or 'Zero-rated' tax codes and attached attestation of foreign-currency receipt.

ETA and CBE jointly inspect export zero-rating refunds, with a forensic focus on fake remittance certificates, shell intermediary invoices, circular foreign-exchange flows, and IP fee mischaracterization. A finding of fraudulent zero-rating triggers: (i) retrospective VAT clawback of the full 14% output tax, together with monthly late-payment interest of approximately 2%; (ii) administrative fines of three to ten times the tax evaded; (iii) criminal imprisonment of up to five years for the legal representative or persons signing the false declarations; and (iv) denial of input VAT recovery on related costs. Separate CBE penalties, including suspension of banking facilities and loss of the exporter's license treatment, apply if the 180-day repatriation deadline is not met or extended by an approved CBE waiver.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the CBE-led exchange rate liberalization of 6 March 2024, the Egyptian pound (EGP) is fully market-determined on the interbank market and foreign currency is freely tradable against documented underlying transactions. Current account service payments, including patent prosecution agent fees, professional disbursements to foreign patent offices, annuity payments, and dividend remittances, are transferable subject to documentary review by an authorized dealing bank. Capital account flows, including equity injections, additional paid-in capital, and FDI-related repatriation, remain subject to CBE registration and bank-level foreign exchange declaration. Profit remittance requires audited financial statements, an Egyptian Tax Authority tax clearance certificate, corporate resolutions, and proper purpose-code coding; dividend withholding tax obligations are enforced at source.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current post-float CBE regulations, exporters of services, including cross-border IP prosecution and patent agency fees received in USD or EUR, must repatriate proceeds through an authorized bank and deposit them into an Egyptian bank account. The statutory collection window is 90 days from invoice date, extendable up to 180 days where the underlying settlement cycle is demonstrated. The pre-2024 mandatory conversion of 100% of export proceeds into EGP was abolished by the March 2024 liberalization; companies may retain foreign currency balances in operational accounts. Each credit must be supported by an executed service contract, itemized invoice, certificate of services rendered, and the CBE-coded service export purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting as the sole monetary authority and foreign exchange regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking Sector Law), which replaced Banking Law No. 88 of 2003 and retains the regulatory architecture of the former Exchange Control Law No. 38 of 1994.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium as a complementary collection rail; Low as a primary or sole treasury banking relationship. Wise (Wise Europe SA/UK) and Payoneer are not licensed by the CBE as Egyptian credit institutions; they cannot hold EGP corporate balances, issue EGP IBANs, process statutory EGP payroll/tax payments, or serve as the destination for CBE-form export proceeds repatriation. Such platforms are practically acceptable for receiving small-dollar PCT client fees and subsequent onward SWIFT settlement; however, the CBE and Egyptian banks will require all underlying services revenue to be declared and receipted in an Egyptian authorized dealing bank, creating duplication risk. Legal constraint: Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE e-payment Regulation No. 18 of 2019 prohibit foreign EMIs from providing payment services to Egyptian resident entities without a CBE-recognized local presence, and any offshore digital balance in excess of USD 10,000 must be declared as an overseas asset.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Qualifying capital contributions from a foreign parent are routed by SWIFT MT103 to the bank designated in the LLC formation file, using CBE external transactions purpose code for direct investment capital contribution plus the narrative reference to the Egyptian entity's Commercial Registration number. The bank requires: (i) apostilled and Arabic-translated Articles of Association; (ii) board resolution of the Egyptian LLC approving issuance of shares/paid-in capital; (iii) certified notarized extract from the Commercial Registry (the certificate of incorporation issued after deposit of the minimum 25% capital in an escrow account); (iv) valid Tax Card; (v) parent company certificate of good standing; and (vi) proof of the foreign party's capacity. The inward remittance is recorded by the bank as an inward foreign direct investment transfer and the beneficiary receives an Expected Inward Transfer notification (TR-1-equivalent), which is the foundational documentation for all future dividend or capital repatriation.All official patent office payments and client-disbursement settlements are executed by SWIFT MT103 serial payment with full beneficiary BIC and IBAN; the CBE service purpose code for international agency and intellectual-property service fees is quoted in field 72. WIPO PCT Geneva fees are routed to WIPO Finance Division in CHF or USD using UBS Switzerland BIC UBSWCHZH80A and the international application number in the payment narrative. USPTO official fee payments to the US Treasury utilize the Federal Reserve wire routing number 051000 in favor of the US Treasury Consolidated Funds account with patent fee designator, referencing the USPTO application number. National phase filings with CNIPA, EPO, and JPO are similarly settled by MT103 with the corresponding domestic transit code. Egyptian banks require the executed foreign service agreement, the tax clearance record or withholding certificate covering the 5% statutory withholding on outward service disbursements where applicable, and the fee invoice. A dedicated official-fee clearing subaccount is recommended to segregate client funds and demonstrate the pass-through nature. Commercial letters of credit are not an appropriate rail for pure patent prosecution agency transactions; bank-to-bank documentary MT103/MT202COV structures remain the fastest and cleanest clearing method.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP forward contracts with a tenor matching the underlying patent fee receivable or trade payable, supported by documented export invoices and executed through an authorized Egyptian bank under CBE permitted derivative guidelines, typically requiring a 10-20% margin deposit.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/EGP contracted with international banks as offshore benchmark hedging instruments for non-resident counterparties; Egyptian resident companies may utilize NDFs through CBE-approved market-making banks solely against verified underlying patent service export exposure, with settlement in hard currency rather than EGP.Cross-currency swaps between USD and EUR to hedge WIPO annuity fee cycles and maintain stable costs where patent official fees are denominated in Swiss francs or Euros.Plain vanilla EUR/EGP and USD/EUR options as insurance instruments on large multi-jurisdiction filing campaigns, provided the bank's market risk committee approves the corporate hedging mandate and the notional exposure is supported by signed client prosecution instructions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of net taxable profits for Egyptian resident companies and Egyptian permanent establishments of non-resident enterprises.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on dividends distributed by Egyptian resident companies, subject to statutory exemptions, listed-company rules and treaty relief. A 5% rate may apply in specified circumstances for dividends from listed securities where the statutory ownership and holding-period conditions are satisfied. Dividends are generally distributed from after-tax profits and require corporate approvals, accounting records and applicable regulatory filings.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is due within four months after the end of the company's tax year. For a calendar-year taxpayer ending on 31 December, the usual deadline is 30 April of the following year. The return must be filed electronically where electronic filing is mandatory. A tax year other than the calendar year requires calculation from the approved fiscal-year end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation generally requires a Local File for material controlled transactions and a Master File for qualifying multinational enterprise groups. The commonly applied materiality threshold is EGP 8,000,000 for a related-party transaction category during the fiscal year, subject to the current executive regulations, ETA guidance and category-specific rules. A CbC Report is generally relevant to multinational groups meeting the prescribed consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly stated as EGP 3,000,000,000 or its prescribed equivalent for the relevant reporting period. Thresholds and filing mechanics must be checked against the current ETA rules before submission.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime. Economic substance nevertheless remains relevant under Egyptian transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance framework, treaty beneficial-ownership principles, permanent-establishment analysis and deductibility rules. The Egyptian company should maintain real decision-making, qualified personnel, premises or operational capability proportionate to its patent-agency functions, locally controlled records, genuine client activity, commercial risk management and evidence that intercompany services or intellectual-property charges were actually received and benefited the business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, additional tax and statutory administrative fines, with criminal tax-evasion exposure under the Egyptian Tax Law and Tax Procedures Law. Aggravated or repeated conduct may result in imprisonment and substantially increased fines.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, finance officer and knowingly participating directors may face personal criminal liability. Civil recovery may include unpaid tax, additional tax and penalties. A travel ban is not automatic but may be imposed by competent authorities or court order in criminal or tax-debt proceedings; imprisonment may apply where the statutory criminal elements are proved.
False tax return or falsified accounting records

Statutory sanction: Administrative assessment, rejection of the tax computation, fines and criminal prosecution under tax, commercial-accounting and general criminal provisions. Document falsification may create separate criminal liability from the tax violation.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize, sign, prepare or knowingly rely on falsified records may be prosecuted personally. Corporate status does not shield a director from criminal liability for personal participation, authorization or grossly negligent statutory breaches.
Unregistered activity or non-filing

Statutory sanction: Registration enforcement, estimated assessment, fines for non-filing or late filing, late-payment additional tax, licence consequences and possible closure or other administrative measures under the relevant tax and sector rules.

Director personal liability: The manager or authorized representative may be personally exposed to statutory fines and, where deliberate evasion or obstruction is established, criminal sanctions. Travel restrictions require a competent administrative or judicial basis and are not an automatic consequence of every late return.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unwithheld amount from the Egyptian payer, additional tax, reporting penalties and potential criminal exposure for deliberate withholding-tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The responsible legal representative, accounting officer or withholding agent may face personal liability where the failure was authorized, deliberate or otherwise criminally attributable. The company may also bear the full tax cost if it cannot recover the tax from the foreign recipient.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to taxable income, secondary tax consequences where applicable, documentation penalties, denial of deductions, withholding-tax exposure, interest and possible anti-avoidance or tax-evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal exposure if they knowingly approve sham transactions, false benchmarking or unsupported deductions. Mere disagreement over an arm's-length range normally creates a tax adjustment rather than automatic criminal liability, absent fraud or concealment.
Permanent-establishment avoidance

Statutory sanction: Retroactive Egyptian tax assessment on attributable profits, withholding obligations, interest, penalties, registration enforcement and possible treaty dispute or audit escalation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate misrepresentation or obstruction. A lawful treaty position supported by contemporaneous facts and documentation does not itself create personal criminal liability.
Value-added tax and invoice non-compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, input-tax denial, late-payment additional tax, administrative fines, invoice penalties and possible criminal sanctions for deliberate tax fraud.

Director personal liability: The authorized signatory and responsible accounting personnel may be prosecuted for intentional false invoicing or VAT evasion. Directors may incur civil restitution obligations where company assets are insufficient and statutory personal-fault conditions are met.
Anti-money-laundering and suspicious-payment failures

Statutory sanction: AML reporting, identification and recordkeeping breaches may lead to regulatory sanctions, fines, licence consequences, asset freezing or confiscation and criminal prosecution. Money-laundering convictions may carry imprisonment and substantial fines under the Egyptian AML framework.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and employees may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation or intentional failure to report where legally required. Travel bans, asset freezes and imprisonment may arise through competent-authority or court measures.
Bribery and improper influence over patent or trademark officials

Statutory sanction: Criminal corruption, bribery, embezzlement or facilitation-payment sanctions may apply, including imprisonment, fines, confiscation, licence consequences and debarment-related effects.

Director personal liability: Individuals who offer, authorize, arrange, record or conceal the payment may be prosecuted personally. Corporate liability, civil recovery and confiscation may arise in addition to the individual's criminal sentence.
Corporate misuse and insolvency-related misconduct

Statutory sanction: Civil restitution, clawback, attachment, insolvency sanctions, creditor claims, tax recovery and criminal prosecution for fraud, breach of trust or related offences.

Director personal liability: Limited liability does not protect directors from personal liability for fraud, bad faith, unlawful distributions, misuse of corporate assets or statutory breaches. Courts may impose restitution, asset attachment, travel restrictions and imprisonment where criminal conduct is established; veil piercing is fact-specific and not automatic.
