Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian Limited Liability Company; the founders freely fix the capital in the Memorandum of Association and allocate it to subscribed shares. A bank escrow or capital deposit certificate is not a statutory precondition for an LLC, unlike joint-stock companies; cash subscriptions must, however, be paid into the company's corporate bank account within the period fixed by the memorandum.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance – official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; taxpayer e-services portal: https://eservices.eta.gov.eg; national e-invoicing portal: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors establish the Egyptian LLC through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or the Commercial Registry under the Companies Law (Law 4/2020). The LLC must then register with the ETA using Form TIN-101 through the e-services portal to obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and electronic tax card. VAT registration is effected on the same portal once annual taxable supplies reach EGP 500,000. The taxpayer must also enroll in the national e-invoicing system and obtain X.509 digital-signature credentials. A non-resident licensor of Egyptian-source royalties must obtain an Egyptian withholding tax (WHT) account; registration is processed via the payer as statutory withholding agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the Egyptian-resident LLC is subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income under Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 30/2023. Branches of non-resident entities are taxed under Territorial_Scope on Egyptian-source income only. Pass-through treatment is not recognised. Companies licensed within the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) or Investment Law 72/2017 free zones may obtain Tax_Exempt_Zone-modelled treatment under a statutory election, paying an annual 1%–3% duty in lieu of standard income and VAT taxes.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through mechanisms. Available elections include: (i) investment-incentive election under Law 72/2017 (free-zone or special economic zone status), (ii) SCZone regulatory election under Law 83/2015, (iii) voluntary VAT registration election for turnover between EGP 50,000 and EGP 500,000, and (iv) capital-allowance and depreciation elections available to taxpayers. Mandatory anti-avoidance rules — thin capitalisation, controlled-foreign-company and transfer-pricing rules — apply non-elective to IP HoldCo structures; no hybrid-mismatch election is afforded by Egyptian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 – mandatory VAT registration applies when annual taxable supply turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is permitted for taxable turnover between EGP 50,000 and EGP 500,000; export-only free-zone activities may register at zero expected turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for (a) exported goods leaving Egypt under a customs export declaration, and (b) export services rendered to a non-resident recipient, benefitting solely outside Egypt, paid in foreign currency from outside Egypt, and supported by an ETA-issued export file. For IP sub-licensing royalties, zero-rating requires the licensed patent/trademark to be exploited wholly outside Egypt, the royalty to be remitted to a bank account in Egypt in convertible foreign currency, and the service to qualify as an exported service under Article 30 of VAT Law 67/2016 and its Executive Regulations; royalties sourced to Egyptian exploitation remain standard-rated and trigger 20% WHT.

  • Egyptian Customs Export Declaration filed through the NAFEZA single window (Customs Law 207/2020)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / international consignment note evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from a licensed Egyptian bank confirming onshore receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice validated with ETA e-invoice QR clearance code
  • Export or sub-licensing contract, end-user royalty statement and ETA export support file (CEDMS/CARGO-X data match)

Mandatory nationwide e-invoicing (and e-receipts for B2C merchants) under ETA Decree 220/2020, as amended by Decree 180/2021. Taxpayers' ERPs must integrate with the ETA platform using REST APIs and X.509 digital certificates, transmitting XML/JSON invoice payloads for real-time fiscal clearance. Each cleared invoice receives a unique ETA UUID, validation hash and QR digital stamp; invoices not cleared are legally non-compliant and rejected for input VAT recovery on the buyer side. E-receipt integration is phased by turnover, and document rejection generates automated ETA tax audit flags.

The ETA Export Audit Division scrutinises zero-rating claims through full document-matching between customs declarations, e-invoices, bank IRCs and royalty agreements. Failure to repatriate export FX proceeds within 180 days or use of forged documentation results in immediate retrospective clawback of VAT refunds and input credits, a penalty equal to double the evaded tax plus EGP 5,000–50,000 per fraudulent invoice, late-payment interest of 2% per month (capped at 30%), blacklisting from the e-invoicing system, and criminal liability with imprisonment up to five years under VAT Law 67/2016 articles 70–74. Central Bank Law 194/2020 and Anti-Money Laundering Law 80/2002 sanctions may apply cumulatively for foreign-exchange and record-keeping violations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations for current-account convertibility; royalties and profit remittances are freely convertible at the interbank EGP/USD market rate through licensed banks, subject to documentary controls (license contract, WHT clearance, and CBE purpose codes). The capital account is convertible for direct investment, but each inward equity injection or cross-border shareholder loan requires a dedicated BOP reporting code and bank-level compliance screening. The March 2024 exchange-rate float eliminated the dual-rate regime, clearing the historical FX rationing backlog and reducing remittance settlement to one-to-three business days. Remaining hurdles are documentary: overdue Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) certificates, mismatched five-digit purpose codes, and bank-level risk appetite for IP-holding structures with thin local substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2024 float, the mandatory surrender ratio is zero percent: 100% of export and invisible royalty proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account with a licensed Egyptian bank or converted at the interbank rate. Repatriation is mandatory through the banking system within 90 days from the date of the sub-licensee invoice, extendable to 180 days with bank approval and up to five years for project-based milestone licensing contracts; non-compliance triggers CBE penalties, suspension of the exporter's ETA tax card, and exclusion from FX facilities. Each inbound royalty credit must be linked to the underlying license and an ETA tax invoice, or the bank will reject the purpose-code declaration.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the sole currency and exchange-control authority under Banking and Central Bank Law No. 194 of 2020, acting through its Foreign Exchange Committee and the Egyptian Banking Institute clearing systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury rails and MEDIUM for ancillary foreign-currency collection only. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) are not licensed by the CBE under the Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and applicable CBE fintech regulations; they cannot issue EGP IBANs, cannot clear through CBE RTGS/InstaPay, and are prohibited as the sole account for an Egyptian resident licensor. Receipts routed through an offshore EMI break the 90-day CBE repatriation clock and must be re-invoiced and re-routed through a licensed Egyptian bank to avoid recharacterization as undisclosed offshore proceeds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inward equity injection transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank, with field 70 containing '/EQUITY/EG_LLC/' plus the company tax/stamp number and the BOP direct-investment (inward equity) purpose code; the beneficiary Egyptian bank requires the shareholder resolution, commercial-registry extract not older than three months, UBO-KYC on the remitter, and the bank mandate to credit the capital/escrow ledger. Shareholder loans to the LLC must use the BOP foreign-loan purpose code and an executed loan agreement to prevent reclassification as constructive royalties or unlicensed banking activity. Missing or mismatched purpose codes trigger rejection by the CBE electronic clearance gateway, adding three-to-five business days; pre-advice to the receiving branch is therefore mandatory.Outbound upstream royalties: SWIFT MT103 cleared via the bank's correspondent network (typically Fedwire/CHIPS for USD) using BOP services code 277 (charges for use of intellectual property); the bank requires the license agreement, tax invoice, statutory withholding tax settlement certificate (Form 41; 20% domestic rate or treaty-reduced rate) and proof of ETA tax filing (CTD). EGP-denominated operating payments clear through CBE RTGS and InstaPay. For physical-goods trade associated with licensed merchandise, commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) at 30 or 60 days' sight and documentary collections (MT400) are acceptable, supported by the same WHT package and customs invoice to avoid sanctions-screening delays.Deliverable USD/EGP outright forward contracts, tenor up to 12 months and rollable to 24 months for project-backed exposure, executed under an ISDA 2002 or bank Master FX Agreement, permissible solely against committed royalty inflows evidenced by signed license agreements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/EGP, offered by CBE-licensed dealing banks to hedge expected balances that cannot be physically settled in EGP, settled in USD against the CBE interbank reference rate.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (USD/EGP) with tenors up to five years, permissible where the underlying is a multi-year sub-license franchise with contracted and committed royalty streams.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within 4 months after the end of the fiscal (accounting) year; if the ETA approves an extension, the additional period is in practice limited to 60 days. For a calendar-year EG LLC, the statutory deadline is 30 April of the year following the close of the tax period; final tax payment is due on the same filing date, and monthly delay surcharges accrue thereafter.
