Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Companies Registration Department / One-Stop-Shop

Governing statute

Law No. 159 of 1981 on Joint Stock Companies, Partnerships with Limited Shares, and Limited Liability Companies, as amended, together with its Executive Regulations; Law No. 72 of 2017 on Investment, where applicable. LLC formation and governance matters are governed primarily by Articles 20-34 (formation) and Articles 39-46 (management) of Law No. 159/1981.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association (Etaa Wal Nizaam) registered with GAFI and the Commercial Registry, resulting in the issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation and Commercial Registration Extract

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on limited liability companies under Law No. 159/1981. The founders freely determine the capital amount. The subscribed capital must be fully paid, deposited in an authorized Egyptian bank in cash or valued in-kind, and certified by the bank before registration. No separate escrow account is required by law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Civil Defense Authority is required for the office premises and must be obtained after physical inspection of fire extinguishers, evacuation routes, and electrical load safety. A workplace occupancy clearance may also be issued by the district administrative authority after confirming structural load safety and compliance with basic occupational health requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on limited liability companies under Law No. 159/1981. The founders freely determine the capital amount. The subscribed capital must be fully paid, deposited in an authorized Egyptian bank in cash or valued in-kind, and certified by the bank before registration. No separate escrow account is required by law.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED, that the founding members unanimously adopt the Articles of Association of the Limited Liability Company and authorize the incorporation filing with GAFI. RESOLVED FURTHER, that each founder subscribes to the shares stated in the shareholding schedule, that the subscribed capital is treated as fully paid into the company's designated Egyptian bank account, and that the registered office at the address specified in the Articles is adopted as the statutory seat of the company. RESOLVED FURTHER, that the founder appointed as General Manager is hereby authorized to execute all registration filings, open banking accounts, engage legal advisors, and take every necessary action to obtain licenses, permits, and tax registration for the company.

No industrial licensing authority applies; General Authority for Industrial Development has no jurisdiction over ISIC 7020 management consultancy activities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; electronic portal: www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned LLC must file a tax registration application with the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) prior to commencing commercial activity. Required steps: (1) obtain Commercial Registration extract and Chamber of Commerce certificate; (2) execute a notarized power of attorney appointing the LLC's managerial representative in Egypt; (3) submit certified articles of association, lease/purchase agreement for the registered office, and valid owners'/managers' national IDs (or passport copies for foreign managers); (4) open a corporate bank account in Egypt and obtain a bank certificate; (5) file ETA Form 1 ('Tax Card') application with supporting dossiers; (6) upon inspection and approval, ETA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and a Tax Card evidencing VAT, payroll, and corporate income tax registration statuses.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is subject to corporate income tax under Law No. 91 of 2005; no pass-through status is available to LLCs) — A foreign-owned LLC must file a tax registration application with the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) prior to commencing commercial activity. Required steps: (1) obtain Commercial Registration extract and Chamber of Commerce certificate; (2) execute a notarized power of attorney appointing the LLC's managerial representative in Egypt; (3) submit certified articles of association, lease/purchase agreement for the registered office, and valid owners'/managers' national IDs (or passport copies for foreign managers); (4) open a corporate bank account in Egypt and obtain a bank certificate; (5) file ETA Form 1 ('Tax Card') application with supporting dossiers; (6) upon inspection and approval, ETA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and a Tax Card evidencing VAT, payroll, and corporate income tax registration statuses.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is subject to corporate income tax under Law No. 91 of 2005; no pass-through status is available to LLCs)

Available election forms

None. Egypt does not operate a check-the-box regime and does not recognize fiscal transparent LLCs for income tax purposes. No hybrid mismatch or consolidated/unitary tax elections are available; the LLC is mandatorily taxed as a separate juridical entity on worldwide income, and foreign shareholders are taxed on distributions solely at the shareholder level at the relevant non-resident dividend rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for resident/established suppliers; non-resident service suppliers have no de minimis threshold and must register for VAT in Egypt from the first taxable supply if the place of supply is Egypt and no reverse-charge mechanism applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods and services are zero-rated under Article 4 of the Egyptian VAT Law if the following cumulative conditions are satisfied for consultancy services: (a) the recipient is a non-resident person; (b) the service is wholly performed, economically utilized, and consumed outside Egypt; (c) the consideration is in convertible foreign currency and actually transferred to, and repatriated through, an Egyptian bank account; (d) contracts, service reports, and invoices demonstrate an overseas engagement; and (e) the supplier is VAT-registered and has filed the relevant export service list with the ETA. For goods, the exporter must produce a valid customs export declaration and prove the goods physically left Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (fully accepted electronic ACD/AED)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX transfer receipt
  • Service export contract and service report (for ISIC 7020 consultancy)

Mandatory e-invoicing applies to VAT-registered businesses under ETA's real-time clearance mandate (introduced progressively from 2020). Registered suppliers must issue invoices in ETA-compliant XML/JSON format via the integrated e-invoicing API, obtain a QR-digital stamp from the ETA clearance server before dispatch, and submit all e-invoice transactions to the ETA for approval. Taxpayers must maintain an API credential (digital signature certificate) and can be blocked from clearance without valid authorization.

ETA may disallow the zero rate and retroactively claw back input VAT claimed as refundable, with late-payment interest (currently applied per the VAT law) and an additional punitive fine of up to 100% of the tax evaded for negligence or false documentation. Fraudulent use of forged customs or FX instruments triggers the general tax evasion penalties under Law 67/2016, including monetary penalties up to EGP 100,000 and imprisonment for individuals, and a denial of the foreign supplier's or exporter's refund rights. ETA also cross-checks export invoices with customs declaration data and Central Bank FX records; any discrepancy can trigger indirect red-flag audits and export eligibility suspension.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account liberalization for goods, services, and unilateral transfers, but retains capital account restrictions and CBE approval/notification requirements for certain cross-border transactions. Profit and dividend remittances by foreign-owned Egyptian LLCs are permitted through licensed banks upon presentation of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and CBE-verified corporate documentation; however, FX liquidity in Egyptian pounds can cause practical conversion and settlement delays, particularly during balance-of-payments stress, so material profit repatriation should be planned after confirmed FX availability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations, export proceeds must be fully repatriated to an Egyptian bank account within 75 days from the date of shipment or completion of the service. The mandatory surrender ratio is effectively 100% for export receipts: all proceeds must be credited to a bank account in Egypt in convertible foreign currency, with the exporter permitted either to retain the foreign currency in an FX account or to sell it to the bank at the prevailing interbank rate for Egyptian pounds. Supporting documents include the export invoice, bill of lading/airway bill or service delivery certificate, copy of the customs declaration, and the bank credit advice.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) acting through its Banking Supervision Sector and Foreign Exchange Control Department, pursuant to the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE foreign exchange circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium. Egypt does not permit unlicensed offshore fintech EMIs or digital banks to operate domestic EGP clearing, maintain local-currency corporate accounts, or act as official channels for CBE reporting and export-proceeds surrender. Wise B2B may be used only as a supplementary outward-payment facilitation layer for non-EGP corridors after the mandatory Egyptian bank rail has been used for capital inflow, FX conversion, and statutory reporting; it cannot replace a licensed Egyptian corporate bank account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Complex or opaque beneficial ownership structure involving foreign shareholders or layered intermediate holding companies

Root cause: Complex or opaque beneficial ownership structure involving foreign shareholders or layered intermediate holding companies

Defense evidence: Certificate of incorporation, consolidated memorandum and articles, full shareholders register, signed UBO declaration for every natural person holding 25% or more, notarized board resolutions, a clear group organizational chart, certified copies of passports of ultimate beneficial owners, and apostilled powers of attorney for each nominee and signatory.
Source-of-funds mismatch between the amount of proposed capital injection and the parent entity's income profile

Root cause: Source-of-funds mismatch between the amount of proposed capital injection and the parent entity's income profile

Defense evidence: Six months of parent bank statements, audited consolidated financial statements, tax clearance certificates from the parent jurisdiction, invoices or underwriting agreements explaining accumulated profits, board resolution approving the injection, and a detailed written explanation of source of wealth and source of funds.
Sanctions-list hit, weak adverse-media screening, or politically exposed person (PEP) finding on any shareholder, director, or counterparty

Root cause: Sanctions-list hit, weak adverse-media screening, or politically exposed person (PEP) finding on any shareholder, director, or counterparty

Defense evidence: Full sanctions screening reports from reputable databases, beneficial owner and management declarations, adverse-media narrative with official court/registry records, PEP status explanations including public-office evidence and source-of-wealth documents, and, if applicable, an independent legal opinion confirming no exposure to UN, EU, OFAC, or CBE sanctions lists.
Commercial-purpose ambiguity for a cross-border market-entry consultancy receiving foreign-currency payments

Root cause: Commercial-purpose ambiguity for a cross-border market-entry consultancy receiving foreign-currency payments

Defense evidence: Signed feasibility-study engagement letter, detailed scope-of-work describing overseas market entry consulting and regulatory license agency services, target market entry plan, evidence of office or agent relationship in Egypt, projected cash-flow schedule of foreign client receipts, and the corresponding CBE transaction purpose code declarations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital from the overseas parent must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to a licensed Egyptian beneficiary bank, using the CBE-prescribed inward remittance purpose code for foreign direct investment or capital subscription.
