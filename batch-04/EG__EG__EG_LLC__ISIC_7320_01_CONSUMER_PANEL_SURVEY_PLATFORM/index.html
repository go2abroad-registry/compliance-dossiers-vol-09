Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; LLCs are compulsorily taxed at entity level at the 22.5% headline CIT rate. Available elections include consolidated VAT registration for wholly-owned group companies, VAT refund or offset election against corporate tax balances, and tax stabilization agreements for GAFI-approved projects under Investment Law 72/2017. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods and offshore services (including consumer-panel survey SaaS and cross-border market-research subscriptions) are zero-rated where the service is supplied to a non-resident, is not directly consumed or used in Egypt, and the consideration is settled in foreign currency. The exporter must hold an export contract, file the export invoice in the ETA e-invoicing system, and be able to demonstrate foreign end-use on audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED / AFC declaration filed through the CargoX EDA platform)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in Egypt
  • 0%-rated export VAT invoice approved in the ETA e-invoicing system
  • Signed offshore service agreement and certificate of foreign end-use

Mandatory under Law 67/2016 as amended: all B2B and B2C invoices and receipts must be transmitted in near real-time to the ETA e-invoicing platform (https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg) using XML/JSON schemas, digitally signed with the taxpayer's ETA-issued certificate, and presented with a machine-readable QR code. Clearance is driven by the ETA Taxpayer Information API; phased rollout applies by taxpayer bracket. Non-compliant invoicing blocks fiscal clearance and attracts fines.

ETA audits zero-rated claims against customs and remittance records. Insufficient documentation triggers reassessment at 14% VAT plus statutory monthly delay interest, monetary penalties up to the full tax evaded, and suspension of the taxpayer's export-invoice clearing rights. Forged export documents attract criminal tax-evasion sanctions and personal director liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations (since 1997), guaranteeing full convertibility for current-account payments and transfers; the EGP operates under a liberalized market-determined exchange rate restored in March 2024 under the IMF Extended Fund Facility, eliminating prior FX allocation queues. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity capital into an Egyptian LLC, are permissible for non-resident shareholders, subject to routing exclusively through a CBE-authorized bank with BPM6-aligned purpose declarations and post-entry CBE/bank verification of the underlying share-subscription resolution and source of funds. Profit remittance is characterized as current-account repatriation of investment income; the practical hurdles are documentary rather than statutory, requiring CBE-authorized bank certification, Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax-clearance certificates, audited financial statements, and a valid shareholders' dividend resolution. There are no quotas or conversion ceilings on repatriation, but all FX movements must be traceable in the bank's CBE reporting systems.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Revenue from service exports (including cross-border consumer polling, market research SaaS subscriptions, and platform data services) must be repatriated to Egypt through a CBE-authorized bank within 180 calendar days from the later of invoice issuance or service performance; goods exporters receive the same 180-day window measured from customs clearance/shipment. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: 0% of export proceeds must be surrendered, and the LLC may lawfully retain 100% of repatriated receipts in foreign-currency accounts (USD/EUR) at its Egyptian bank. The bank monitors overdue export receivables through CBE electronic export tracking and will suspend FX operations and require a written explanation for any unrepatriated balance beyond the statutory timeline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the lawful foreign-exchange and banking regulator operating under Law No. 194 of 2020, the Foreign Exchange Law No. 114 of 1939 (as amended), and the CBE Rulebook; the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU) concurrently supervises AML/CTF obligations for all authorized banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must remit equity in USD/EUR via SWIFT MT103 from their home bank to the LLC's pre-incorporation capital escrow account or newly opened capital account at the Egyptian bank. The inward remittance form must carry the BPM6-based purpose declaration for Direct Investment – Equity Capital (FDI) in an Egyptian entity, together with the general-assembly subscription resolution and source-of-funds evidence. The bank verifies the documents, credits the funds, and upon completion issues the capital-deposit certificate ('Shahadat Al-Eydaa') required by the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and the Commercial Registry before incorporation is finalized. The capital may remain in foreign currency or be converted to EGP at the prevailing market rate; no capital disbursement is permitted before the certificate is issued, and any subsequent shareholding amendment requires a new deposit certificate and CBE re-verification.Operational and trade outflows are cleared exclusively through the Egyptian bank using SWIFT MT103 for service payments (contractors, overseas panelist mass payouts, cloud hosting, software licenses) and commercial letters of credit (sight L/C, usance L/C) or documentary collections for physical imports and high-value procurement. Each outbound wire must carry the correct BPM6 service category code (e.g., Business and Management Consulting, Computer and Information Services), and be supported by the underlying contract, pro-forma or final invoice, and ETA withholding-tax certificates where treaty rates apply. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 for the mandatory Local File and Master File; however, a simplified transfer-pricing disclosure schedule (Part of Form No. 19) is mandatory for any fiscal year in which aggregate related-party transactions exceed EGP 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code; nevertheless, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 142 of Law No. 91 of 2005, together with commercial substance jurisprudence, empowers the ETA to disregard artificial arrangements and shell companies. The ETA expects actual place of effective management in Egypt, documented board meetings, local payroll, physical records, and an operational commercial purpose. Where a market-research platform is booked in Egypt but managed entirely abroad without substance, the ETA can re-characterise the vehicle as transparent and assess taxes on the controlling foreign owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years and a fine between EGP 100,000 and EGP 1,000,000 under Article 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005; additional civil penalty of 100% of the evaded tax and judicial attachment of bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and de facto managers may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; travel bans and asset freezes can be issued at the request of the ETA under the Tax Collection Regulations.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the shell, assess the controlling beneficiary personally at the full 22.5% corporate rate, and apply a 100% add-on penalty; persistent inactivity may lead to striking off from the Commercial Register.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil upon a showing of fraud or sham; directors are personally liable for restitution of evaded taxes and may be prohibited from leaving Egypt until dues are settled.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fines between EGP 500,000 and EGP 2,000,000 and imprisonment from two to seven years under Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended by Law No. 48 of 2022; possible cancellation of the commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for first-instance AML violations; conviction results in disqualification from board positions, travel restrictions, and freezing of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties of EGP 1,000 to EGP 50,000 per infraction, coupled with the ETA's authority to re-determine taxable profits using OECD-compliant transfer-pricing methods and to levy an additional 50% assessment penalty.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly approved incomplete TP documentation can be personally assessed for the resulting deficiency and penalties; the ETA may further issue criminal referral for taxpayer fraud if false documents were filed.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation and Foreign-Exchange Violation

Statutory sanction: The remittance is recharacterised as a dividend subject to 10% WHT, plus a late-payment surcharge of 2% per month and an additional penalty of up to 100%; violation of Central Bank of Egypt foreign-exchange regulations can incur fines up to EGP 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the un-withheld distribution are jointly and severally liable for the withholding tax and surcharges; travel bans and asset seizure may be ordered for the recovery of public funds.
