Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Egyptian law imposes no fixed minimum paid-up capital for an LLC providing professional services, provided that the subscribed capital is fully declared in the Articles of Association and that at least 50% of the declared subscribed capital is deposited in a bank account opened in the company's name at an Egyptian bank before registration. The deposit is treated as escrowed capital and may be withdrawn only after receipt of the final incorporation certificate.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must obtain a Fire Safety and Civil Defense Certificate from the General Directorate of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) confirming acceptable building fire load, emergency exits, smoke detection, and fire-extinguishing systems. In addition, a Occupational Safety and Health Clearance from the Ministry of Manpower and a Structural Compatibility Certificate from the District/City Municipality are required, verifying that the office premises meet seismic load and operational density requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP operates under a CBE-managed flexible exchange-rate regime in which current-account transactions (trade in goods and services) are fully convertible for resident commercial entities through licensed onshore banks, subject to the CBE electronic e-form platform, documentary proof (notarized service contracts, commercial invoices, tax registrations, and, where relevant, ACI/NAFEZA import registrations), and bank-level customer due diligence. Capital-account transactions are likewise permitted: inbound equity contributions, foreign loans, and capital repatriation require GAFI investment registration and CBE purpose-code classification, while outbound profit remittance (dividends) requires audited financial statements, board dividend minutes, an Egyptian Tax Authority clearance certificate, and a CBE e-form declaration. The practical hurdles are documentary and administrative rather than legal: incomplete underlying files halt conversion, and during periods of acute liquidity stress the spread between onshore interbank and offshore quotations may widen, producing an economic rather than statutory convertibility constraint; profit-remittance restrictions are not currently in force for a tax-compliant LLC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The current statutory regime applies a 0% mandatory conversion ratio: the CBE has abolished compulsory surrender of export and service-export proceeds, and the Egyptian LLC may lawfully retain 100% of its foreign-currency service receivables in onshore Foreign Currency Accounts (FCAs) at authorized dealer banks. The residual mandatory obligation is repatriation: all export and service proceeds must be credited through an Egyptian-licensed authorized dealer bank within the CBE-monitored repatriation deadlines (the bank-applied benchmark for services is the contractual payment maturity, with the CBE inspection reference ceiling for goods being five months from shipment/transport documentation and banks applying a parallel discipline to substantial service receivables); collection through unlicensed offshore channels does not discharge the repatriation duty, and non-compliant exporters are recorded in the CBE exporter-compliance registry, risking restriction of future bank operations and AML/CFT review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE; Al-Bank Al-Markazi Al-Masri), the exclusive foreign-exchange and monetary authority of the Arab Republic of Egypt, operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 promulgating the Central Bank and Banking System Law (which repealed the former Law No. 88 of 2003) and the anti-money laundering framework of Law No. 80 of 2002 as subsequently amended; the CBE discharges FX oversight through its authorized dealer bank network, the unified-float decisions of 6 March 2024, and its commitments under the IMF Extended Fund Facility; all cross-border remittances affecting Egyptian residents require CBE e-form registration and bank intermediation.

Unpaid tax: monthly delay surcharge of 2% of the tax due, capped at 20% of the principal tax. Failure to produce statutory books on audit request: EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000. Deliberate misreporting triggers criminal sanctions under Article 133 and potential imprisonment.
Line 8 - Depreciation Depreciation on fixed assets computed per ETA-prescribed useful lives and basis rules. Assets of a foreign branch are depreciated separately; treaty provisions do not alter the ETA depreciation regime. Late filing: fixed fine of EGP 3,000 to EGP 30,000. Unpaid tax: monthly delay surcharge of 2% of the tax due, capped at 20% of the principal tax. Failure to produce statutory books on audit request: EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000. Deliberate misreporting triggers criminal sanctions under Article 133 and potential imprisonment.
Line 9 - Provisions and Reserves Only deductible provisions (e.g., doubtful debts, employee end-of-service) per Articles 25 to 27 of Law 91/2005. Unpaid tax: monthly delay surcharge of 2% of the tax due, capped at 20% of the principal tax. Failure to produce statutory books on audit request: EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000. Deliberate misreporting triggers criminal sanctions under Article 133 and potential imprisonment.

Deliberate misreporting triggers criminal sanctions under Article 133 and potential imprisonment.
Line 11 - Loss Carry-forward Offset of prior-year tax losses, limited to five years from the loss year. A change in 50% or more of ownership or a change of activity may trigger an ETA audit and loss-deduction denial. Late filing: fixed fine of EGP 3,000 to EGP 30,000. Unpaid tax: monthly delay surcharge of 2% of the tax due, capped at 20% of the principal tax. Failure to produce statutory books on audit request: EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000. Deliberate misreporting triggers criminal sanctions under Article 133 and potential imprisonment.
Line 12 - Taxable Income Net income after loss offset and any TP adjustment. For a PE, applies to Egypt-source profits; head-office overhead allocations must be TP-documented. Late filing: fixed fine of EGP 3,000 to EGP 30,000. Unpaid tax: monthly delay surcharge of 2% of the tax due, capped at 20% of the principal tax. Failure to produce statutory books on audit request: EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000. Deliberate misreporting triggers criminal sanctions under Article 133 and potential imprisonment.
Line 13 - Tax Computation Taxable income multiplied by 22.5%; specific industries may face special rates. Branches are taxed at the same 22.5% rate; no reduced branch rate applies. Late filing: fixed fine of EGP 3,000 to EGP 30,000. Unpaid tax: monthly delay surcharge of 2% of the tax due, capped at 20% of the principal tax.
