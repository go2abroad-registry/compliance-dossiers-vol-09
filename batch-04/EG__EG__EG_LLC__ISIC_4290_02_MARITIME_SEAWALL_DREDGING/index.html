Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not generally provide a United States-style check-the-box election for a limited liability company. An Egyptian LLC is ordinarily subject to entity-level corporate income tax and VAT rules. Tax treatment may instead vary by legally distinct form, permanent-establishment status, approved investment or free-zone regime, or qualifying special economic-zone incentive; these are statutory classifications or incentive applications, not elective pass-through classifications. No general hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover is the principal VAT registration threshold for taxable persons, subject to compulsory-registration rules, sector-specific provisions, and rules applicable to nonresident suppliers and imported services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Egypt to a destination outside Egypt may qualify for VAT at 0% where the export is completed through the prescribed customs process and the supplier retains documentary evidence of export, supply value, and payment. Exported services may qualify for zero rating only where they satisfy the statutory definition and conditions for services exported outside Egypt, including the relevant place-of-supply, recipient, use, and payment requirements. Services physically performed or economically consumed in Egypt, including many construction, dredging, engineering, and port-related activities performed in Egypt, should not be treated as exported services merely because the customer is nonresident. Zero-rating and VAT refunds require consistent invoices, customs records, accounting records, and banking evidence; unsupported claims may be reassessed as domestic taxable supplies.

  • Customs export declaration accepted and finalized by Egyptian Customs
  • Commercial export invoice identifying the Egyptian supplier, foreign consignee, goods, value, currency, and delivery terms
  • Packing list and cargo or shipment documentation
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or other transport document evidencing shipment outside Egypt
  • Customs exit, release, or proof-of-export evidence showing physical departure from Egypt
  • Foreign-currency bank receipt, inward-remittance evidence, or other acceptable proof of consideration, where required for the relevant transaction or refund file
  • Contracts, purchase orders, certificates of completion, and delivery or acceptance records supporting the exported supply
  • VAT invoices and electronic-invoice records containing the required ETA data and unique identifiers
  • Import-country or destination evidence where requested by ETA or customs authorities

Egypt has a mandatory electronic invoicing framework for in-scope business-to-business transactions and other covered taxpayers, implemented in phases by the ETA. Taxpayers must register on the ETA electronic invoicing platform, use approved electronic invoicing software or API integration, apply a qualified electronic signature or approved digital-signature method, generate the prescribed invoice data and unique identifier, and submit invoices for ETA validation or clearance in accordance with the applicable phase. Electronic receipts apply to covered business-to-consumer transactions under the ETA e-receipt framework. Invoices must contain prescribed supplier, customer, tax, supply, and payment information; unregistered, rejected, or technically noncompliant documents may jeopardize input VAT deduction and expense support.

ETA may deny zero rating or a VAT refund and assess the applicable domestic VAT, statutory late-payment additions, and administrative penalties where export, payment, customs-exit, invoicing, or service-export conditions are not substantiated. Refunds paid on the basis of inaccurate, incomplete, fictitious, or duplicated documentation may be recovered with applicable additions and may trigger audit reassessment. Failure to register, issue compliant invoices, maintain records, file returns, or use the required electronic systems may attract penalties under the VAT Law and related tax procedures. Deliberate tax evasion, concealment of taxable turnover, fabrication of export documents, false refund claims, or collusion may constitute criminal tax offenses, exposing responsible persons and entities to fines, imprisonment, cancellation or suspension of tax privileges, and referral to prosecution. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation period should be treated as the required transaction-control deadline for this dossier; the precise FX evidence and timing must be verified against the applicable banking, customs, export-control, contract, and ETA requirements for the transaction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is classified as a partially convertible currency. Current account transactions, including import payments and service fees, may be routed through authorized banks without prior CBE approval provided underlying trade documentation is presented. Capital account transactions, notably shareholder equity injections, foreign loans, and repatriation of investment proceeds, are subject to mandatory registration with the CBE and tax clearance certificates issued by the Egyptian Tax Authority. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted only after filing audited financial statements and settling any corporate tax liabilities, with documented proof of the source of funds for the original investment. Offshore foreign currency accounts maintained locally are subject to aggregate balance limits and are not freely transferable without a valid underlying transaction payment order.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds, including servicing contracts for maritime dredging and breakwater works, must be repatriated to Egypt within 30 days from the date of shipment or completion of the monthly progress milestone, as certified by the competent port authority. The full amount of proceeds received in convertible foreign currency must be offered for sale to an authorized banking dealer within five banking days of credit to the exporter's foreign currency account. Mandatory conversion to EGP is applicable to the entire proceeds at the weighted average market rate, unless the entity holds a valid CBE general authorization to retain a percentage for trade-related operational expenditures, which for dredging sector services, does not exceed 20% of invoiced value. The entity must submit a shipping bill, signed contract, invoicing certificate, and an escrow confirmation from the project owner to the authorized bank for each repatriation and sale transaction.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low with a strict legal prohibition. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as licensed banks in Egypt and cannot provide domestic or foreign currency accounts to Egyptian legal entities. Any entity that accepts payments on behalf of the Egyptian company would violate CBE Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and could result in account freeze, statutory penalties, and administrative sanctions. A licensed Egyptian Fintech partner could act solely as a payment gateway for project collections from third parties, but only if the final settlement is routed into a CBE-licensed commercial bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from foreign shareholders must be routed through an authorized dealer bank in Egypt using a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer with the clearly stated purpose code and narrative referencing the investment registration certificate or project approval from GAFI and the Central Bank of Egypt. The remitting bank must receive a certified board resolution of the Egyptian entity and the executed notarized share subscription agreement. The funds must be credited to a special foreign currency Investment Account opened by the Egyptian entity; the ratio of conversion of the injected funds into EGP is optional, subject to the CBE's market rate. The beneficiary bank must register the operation in the CBE's electronic transfer order system within one business day and obtain a transfer order confirmation. For amounts exceeding a threshold imposed by CBE regulations (approximately USD 1 million or equivalent), the entity must submit a source-of-funds declaration to the compliance officer, together with the apostilled certificate of incorporation and board minutes.Outbound payments for trade and operating expenses (fuel, submarine equipment, maintenance imports, port state dues) must be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for amounts below USD 1,000,000 and via a documentary commercial letter of credit for larger amounts. All foreign payments must be supported with a valid proforma invoice, port authority contract, service agreement, customs declaration, tax certificate, and prior import approval when applicable. Authorized banks will perform a purpose-code screening and, where required, confirm settlement with the CBE's electronic payment approval gateway. Same-day clearing is available only for payments to sanctioned-free, high-income jurisdictions with a strong banking relationship; otherwise, the settlement cycle ranges from two to five business days.
