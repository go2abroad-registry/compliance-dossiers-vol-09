A purely virtual or PO-Box-only address is not accepted by the Egyptian Commercial Registry or by banks for KYC purposes; the LLC must maintain a physical registered address in Egypt. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance of the Arab Republic of Egypt; official portal: www.eta.gov.eg and e-invoicing portal: www.invoice.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor or foreign entity establishing an Egyptian branch or LLC must apply to the Egyptian Tax Authority for a Tax Identification Number (TIN). The application is filed via the ETA registration office (or the General Authority for Investment for freezone entities) using the prescribed Form No. 1 for registration in the taxpayer register. Required documentation includes notarized certificate of incorporation, certified constitutional documents, commercial registry extract, power of attorney/board resolution, lease or ownership evidence for business premises, and valid ID/passport copies of directors and legal representatives. After verification, the ETA issues the tax card (bitaqat daribiyya), the TIN, and registration records for both income tax and VAT. For foreign entities not having Egyptian fixed establishment, a tax representative is required in certain circumstances. The registration must be completed before commencing business operations where taxable supplies are anticipated.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box regime analogous to the U.S. Internal Revenue Code. An Egyptian LLC is automatically treated as a taxpaying person under Law No. 91 of 2005 (Income Tax Law, as amended), and no pass-through election is available to corporate entities. Where the LLC is owned by non-residents, the entity remains subject to corporate income tax at entity level, and distributions are subject to dividend withholding tax with possible treaty relief. Hybrid mismatch elections are not granted under Egyptian law; however, taxpayers may be obliged to apply the Substance-Based Income Exclusion under Pillar Two rules if the group qualifies for the OECD Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) rules adopted via subsequent amendments. For tax group consolidation, Egyptian law does not permit group registration; each juridical entity must file separately.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 aggregate annual value of taxable supplies; mandatory VAT registration under the Executive Regulations of Law No. 67 of 2016 once this threshold is exceeded. Persons carrying on export activities may register voluntarily, and certain persons subject to special regimes (e.g., designated government contractors and service providers) are required to register regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible physical goods (including VHP raw cane centrifugal sugar) are zero-rated under the Egyptian VAT Law provided that the goods are physically exported from Egypt outside the local market within 90 days of the date of issue of the tax invoice, the exporter maintains proof of export, and the proceeds are received through the banking system. Offshore services provided to non-residents are also zero-rated if they are consumed entirely outside Egypt and directly connected to exported goods or to non-resident recipients, subject to documentary evidence and ETA substance review. The zero-rating claim is available whether the shipment occurs under an ordinary sale, on a CIF/FOB basis, or under a Revolving Letter of Credit, provided that the commercial invoice is issued to the foreign buyer and the customs export declaration shows the Egyptian seller as exporter.

  • Customs Export Declaration duly accepted and sealed by Egyptian Customs Authority (Form No. 1 Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill / Air Waybill evidencing the physical departure of the cargo from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized Egyptian bank confirming receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Certificate of Origin (for selected markets and preferential tariff regimes)
  • Commercial invoice stated in a freely convertible currency
  • SGS/Cotton or Commodity Inspection Certificate (where relevant for raw sugar quality and polarization)
  • Bank letter of credit or collection advice for the specific shipment

All VAT-registered taxpayers are under the Egyptian e-invoicing mandate (Phase-by-Phase Implementation), which requires issuance of invoices in XML/JSON format through the ETA's e-invoice application or via the approved API integration. Each invoice must be submitted in real time to the ETA for centralized fiscal clearance; the ETA validates the invoice against tax rules and commercial registration data, and upon acceptance assigns a unique UUID and a validation stamp. QR codes must be generated for printed/PDF versions. B2B and B2G transactions have been compulsorily e-invoiced since 2021-2023, and the mandate is extended progressively to all taxpayers by turnover segment. ETA also operates an e-receipt system for retail supplies. Fiscal electronic devices are required in certain retail sectors under the e-receipt regime. Non-compliance with e-invoicing leads to rejection of invoice clearing and imposition of penalties under Law No. 67 of 2016.

The ETA and Egyptian Customs Authority jointly audit export zero-rating through the Exports Refund Unit. Where false documentation, fictitious shipments, over-valuation, or forged export declarations are detected, the zero-rate is revoked, the tax due at 14% is assessed, and additional duties, late payment penalties (up to 3% per month or portion thereof on the unpaid tax), and administrative fines not exceeding EGP 20,000 may be imposed. Fraudulent export refund claims are criminalized under Law No. 67 of 2016 and Law No. 207 of 2020 (Customs Law), subject to imprisonment and fine equal to the value of the tax evaded plus a penalty of between 50% and 100% of the refund obtained or sought. Funds not repatriated within 180 days without Central Bank of Egypt approval can trigger clawback of the zero-rating and may additionally be treated as unrepatriated export proceeds under Egyptian exchange control regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the 6 March 2024 exchange-rate floatation and the associated IMF-backed reform, the Egyptian Pound operates under a managed floating regime. Current-account transactions (import and export of physical goods, freight, marine insurance, and trade-related services) are freely convertible through CBE-licensed banks without prior central bank approval, subject only to documentary verification (invoices, contracts, bills of lading, and customs declarations). Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend distributions, and liquidation proceeds, are permitted but subject to GAFI registration, a tax clearance certificate, and a CBE inward-transfer declaration. In practice, profit remittance for a commodity-trading LLC is fully feasible provided the funds are traceable to registered paid-in capital or audited retained earnings, and provided the ETA confirms no outstanding tax arrears. The principal operational hurdle is periodic USD liquidity tightness in the onshore interbank market; during such windows banks may sequence large repatriation requests, and treasury should maintain a 20-35% USD buffer plus pre-approval for any remittance above USD 500,000. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders attract withholding tax at the treaty rate or the domestic rate of 10%, with proper Form 10-compliant documentation required for the bank file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The pre-2024 regime imposing a fixed statutory surrender and conversion ratio of export proceeds into EGP was largely repealed by CBE Circular No. 3 of 2024. The current legal framework requires (i) full repatriation of export proceeds into a namesake account at a CBE-licensed bank within 180 days from the bill-of-lading date, extendable to 270 days only with documented commercial justifications; (ii) retention discretion for exporters to hold foreign currency in offshore-style onshore USD accounts; and (iii) conversion of any residual balance to EGP at the interbank rate when the exporter needs EGP liquidity or when the 180-day limit lapses. In bank-level practice a de facto soft surrender ratio of 20-50% may be requested from commodity traders during USD scarcity windows, reflecting the CBE's liquidity-management guidance; refusal to provide soft conversion typically triggers a review of the customer's trade classification and potential curtailment of trade-finance facilities. For raw cane sugar arbitrage operations, the bank will also require proof that each receipt corresponds to a physically settled cargo (vessel name, B/L serial number, polarization certificate) to prevent invoice-advance and round-tripping classifications.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, with delegated foreign-exchange administration powers over all inward and outward financial flows. The CBE supervises the interbank USD/EGP market, issues circulars on trade settlement and capital movements, and coordinates with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) on repatriation entitlements under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. The Ministry of Finance and the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) interface with the CBE through the unified tax clearance process for profit remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Overall feasibility: LOW as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM only as a supplementary collection vehicle. The CBE requires all statutory trade settlements, export repatriation, and capital movements to be executed through CBE-licensed banks operating in Egypt; offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorized under the UK FCA, EU PSD2, or Singapore MAS frameworks are not recognized banks for Egyptian foreign-exchange law and cannot issue or receive documentary credits on behalf of the Egyptian LLC. Virtual IBANs obtained from such EMIs may be used for incidental treasury flows such as directors' expenses, advisory fees, or parent-company pooling, but any use in the settlement of sugar purchase or sale contracts will trigger heightened AML review, require reconciliation evidence against every cargo, and may cause the Egyptian bank to classify the account as having 'opaque and unverified payment rails'. Consequently, the blueprint adopts EMIs exclusively for contingency collection of small receivables under USD 50,000, while all material LC and SWIFT flows remain with Tier-1 Egyptian commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection is routed exclusively as a SWIFT MT103 wire from the parent's own outside-Egypt bank account directly to EG_LLC's capital account at the primary Egyptian bank, denominated in USD or EUR. The wire must reference the Egyptian entity name, the GAFI registration number, and the CBE purpose code 13010 (Direct Investment - Equity Participation); shareholder loans use code 13020 and require a formal loan agreement for thin-capitalization assessment under Article 50 of Egypt's Income Tax Law. The receiving bank requires a complete dossier comprising (i) authenticated GAFI investment certificate; (ii) board resolution approving the injection schedule; (iii) CFO cover letter confirming the purpose as unpaid-capital or operational funding; (iv) executed CBE Inward Transfer Declaration; (v) copy of the underlying commodity contract only if the funds are to prepay imports; and (vi) proof of identity and good standing of the remitting parent. Amounts above USD 500,000 may trigger bank-level approval and a possible 24-72 hour interbank liquidity hold. Upon credit, the bank issues a capital-injection receipt which should be retained for the eventual repatriation file. Equity funding is strongly preferred over intercompany debt because Egyptian withholding tax on interest (up to 20% domestic, reduced by treaty) and the CBE's thin-capitalization ratio create a heavier compliance burden and lower treasury efficiency for a trading entity.Outbound trade disbursements follow three primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for direct payments to sugar suppliers, freight forwarders, marine insurers, and port authorities, recommended for individual transactions below USD 250,000 and for all ancillary fees; (2) Documentary commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 rules by the Egyptian bank and confirmed by a top-tier international bank (e.g., HSBC London, Standard Chartered or Commerzbank) for the first three to five cargoes, with sight or 90-day usance structures, cleared against presentation of the B/L, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, marine insurance certificate, and polarization/quality certificates; and (3) standby letters of credit or SBLC issued in favor of Egyptian port authorities and the Suez Canal Economic Zone for customs-bonded operations and terminal handling guarantees. All outbound payments must be backed by a physical trade file, and the beneficiary bank must be on the CBE-accepted counterparty list. Freight and marine-insurance payments to non-resident carriers may be made under MT103 free of withholding tax where the treaty applies; brokerage and agent commissions require an underlying service agreement and an EGP/USD pricing attestation to avoid transfer-mis-invoicing classification. Direct disbursements above USD 250,000 from the operating account are sequentially queued behind LC margins and may require same-day USD liquidity coverage from the treasury buffer.Onshore USD/EGP commercial forwards transacted through CBE-licensed banks, with tenors up to 12 months and mandatory underlying documentary trade exposure (LC or physical contract).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/EGP executed with offshore bank counterparties, settled against the CBE interbank reference rate and used exclusively to hedge the EGP repatriation and working-capital legs.Foreign-exchange swaps (USD/EGP FX swaps) on the interbank market balancing the LC margin period against the sugar sales receivable cycle.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) converting USD-denominated trade-facility drawings into EGP liabilities, subject to CBE notification when the notional exceeds USD 1 million.Commodity hedging using ICE Futures US Sugar No. 11 and No. 16 contracts executed offshore by the parent treasury, with the Egyptian LLC named as the beneficial party under ISDA/ECSA documentation, thereby protecting physical arbitrage margins without triggering onshore CBE FX-rule restrictions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Overall feasibility: LOW as a primary treasury rail and MEDIUM only as a supplementary collection vehicle. The CBE requires all statutory trade settlements, export repatriation, and capital movements to be executed through CBE-licensed banks operating in Egypt; offshore Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorized under the UK FCA, EU PSD2, or Singapore MAS frameworks are not recognized banks for Egyptian foreign-exchange law and cannot issue or receive documentary credits on behalf of the Egyptian LLC. Virtual IBANs obtained from such EMIs may be used for incidental treasury flows such as directors' expenses, advisory fees, or parent-company pooling, but any use in the settlement of sugar purchase or sale contracts will trigger heightened AML review, require reconciliation evidence against every cargo, and may cause the Egyptian bank to classify the account as having 'opaque and unverified payment rails'. Consequently, the blueprint adopts EMIs exclusively for contingency collection of small receivables under USD 50,000, while all material LC and SWIFT flows remain with Tier-1 Egyptian commercial banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File apply when the Egyptian taxpayer's annual gross revenue equals or exceeds EGP 20,000,000 (approximately USD 1.3 million), per Article 113 of Law 91/2005 and Executive Regulations 2020; the Country-by-Country Report applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EGP 18,000,000,000. The LLC must deliver the Local File within 30 days of ETA request and the Master File within 30 days of the parent's finalization; non-compliance reverses the burden of proof on arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: No standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations are in force; however, Egypt enforces an economic substance doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, Article 113's business-purpose condition, and the ETA's substance-over-form and beneficial-ownership analysis. The sugar arbitrage LLC must demonstrate real Egyptian functions (contract execution, vessel nominations, warehousing, customs clearance, invoicing, and LC management), assets (bank accounts and inventory), and personnel; a bare conduit with no commercial substance is disregarded for treaty claims, its margins are recharacterized, and the distribution becomes taxable at the statutory 10% WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 3 months to 5 years and a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000 under Article 133 of Law 91/2005; automatic application of the 2% monthly late-payment surcharge; ETA may seize bank accounts, sugar inventories, and vessel cargo and issue a provisional assessment based on container positions and cargo manifests.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers are jointly and severally liable for the LLC's unpaid CIT, WHT, and surcharges where the evasion results from their instructions; civil restitution is recoverable from personal assets; travel bans are imposed under ETA debt-collection procedures; the Economic Court may imprison the responsible manager.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of the profit as a taxable service fee at 20% WHT; disallowance of deductions; revocation of the Unified Tax Card; cancellation of the e-invoicing license; and freezing of accounts by the Central Bank of Egypt under customer due diligence rules.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the LLC is proven to be a sham; shareholders and directors face joint liability for taxes and customs debts; travel bans and personal asset seizures are available under tax execution and Companies Law penalties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA re-establishes the arm's length profit, imposes additional CIT at 22.5% on the upward adjustment, adds the 2% monthly surcharge, and may apply an additional penalty of up to 30% of the adjusted tax for intentional non-disclosure; failure to produce the Local File triggers a fixed fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000 and criminal referral for false declarations.

Director personal liability: The CEO and CFO are personally accountable for certified TP evidence; false declarations expose them to joint civil liability and criminal sanctions for fraudulent documentation; internal review and external benchmarking must be documented to avoid personal liability.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80/2002: fines of EGP 20,000 to EGP 1,000,000 for the entity, confiscation of vessel cargo and LC funds, and imprisonment up to 7 years for laundering proceeds through layered commodity transactions; the Economic Court may order dissolution and license revocation.

Director personal liability: Managers and signatories knowingly executing the laundering scheme are personally fined, have personal assets seized, and face imprisonment up to 7 years; limited liability protection is lost where corporate funds are commingled with personal accounts of directors.
