Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No fixed statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an LLC under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981; the members must declare a capital amount reasonably sufficient for the licensed customs brokerage activity. The declared share capital must be contributed in EGP or in eligible in-kind assets and, to satisfy GAFI and customs licensing practice, cash contributions must be deposited into a bank account opened in the name of the company-under-formation; the bank shall hold the funds as a notarial escrow and release them only after the Commercial Registry certificate is issued.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) via the unified electronic tax portal at e-tax.gov.eg (https://eta.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned limited liability company (LLC) must first obtain a tax card by completing online registration on the ETA e-services platform, filing the statutory tax entity registration application, and submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, commercial register extract, proof of legal address, and valid identification/power of attorney for the local legal representative. The ETA issues a unique Tax Identification Number (TIN), which also serves as the VAT identification number upon VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned limited liability company (LLC) must first obtain a tax card by completing online registration on the ETA e-services platform, filing the statutory tax entity registration application, and submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, commercial register extract, proof of legal address, and valid identification/power of attorney for the local legal representative. The ETA issues a unique Tax Identification Number (TIN), which also serves as the VAT identification number upon VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists. An LLC is mandatorily taxed at the entity level. Certain civil companies may elect to be taxed as corporations under the Income Tax Law, but no hybrid mismatch or transparent entity election applies to LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies of goods and services; registration is mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate applies to goods physically exported outside Egypt and to services directly related to exported goods or performed on goods destined for export, provided the exporter is VAT-registered, the export is supported by a customs export declaration, proceeds are repatriated to an Egyptian bank account within the statutory period, and all commercial documents name the Egyptian exporter as seller/supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACD statement)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial invoice and export contract
  • Certificate of origin
  • VAT invoice with zero-rate indication

Mandatory e-invoicing applies to all VAT-registered persons. Issuers must connect through the ETA-approved real-time clearance API. Each invoice is validated digitally, receives a unique invoice identification number and QR code, and must be printed with the ETA-issued code. E-receipts are also mandatory at the point of sale for business-to-consumer transactions.

The ETA may audit zero-rated export transactions for up to five years. False declarations or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline triggers reversal of the zero rate, disallowance of input VAT, recovery of VAT plus default interest (currently up to 1.5% monthly) and statutory penalties. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment for fraudulent export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-determined floating exchange-rate regime for the Egyptian Pound (EGP), effective 6 March 2024, supported by the IMF Extended Fund Facility. Current-account convertibility for trade in goods and services, including customs brokerage fees, freight, port disbursements, and import-duty pass-through, is unrestricted through CBE-licensed banks against full Advanced Cargo Information (ACI) documentation. Capital-account convertibility remains conditional: equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend remittances, and liquidation proceeds require GAFI investment registration, audited financial statements, an Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax-clearance certificate confirming no overdue corporate tax or VAT, and submission of the CBE balance-of-payments declaration through the CBE electronic reporting platform. Bona fide remittances are typically released within 5 to 15 business days after complete documentation is filed; no pre-approval is required post-2024 unification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The March 2024 unified-float reforms abolished all mandatory surrender ratios for export and service proceeds. Exporters, including customs brokerage houses earning fees from foreign principals, must repatriate the full value of services or export proceeds into a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account within 120 days of the service invoice or shipment date, with one documented extension to 180 days permitted. Proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in an onshore foreign-currency account or converted to EGP at the prevailing interbank market rate at the account holder's discretion. Non-repatriation or unjustified offshore retention triggers exchange-control penalties, ACI validation blocks on subsequent consignments, and mandatory reporting to the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit under the AML Law.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Exchange Control Law No. 38 of 1994; the CBE's Banking Regulation and Supervision Sector and the AML/CFT Unit jointly administer foreign-exchange licensing, transactional reporting, and enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, MultiPass, and Mercury are not CBE-licensed and cannot lawfully hold EGP or act as settlement agents for Egyptian customs duties, port fees, or tax payments. CBE Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves payment-institution activity to CBE-licensed entities or operators under the National Payments Council framework, with the CBE Regulatory Sandbox as the only non-bank entry point. A customs brokerage license issued by the Egyptian Customs Authority and GAFI registration does not confer payment-institution status. EMIs are usable only as ancillary receivables channels for non-EGP client fees, while all Egyptian treasury, duty, and tax obligations must clear through CBE-licensed bank rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection in USD, EUR, or GBP is routed via SWIFT MT103 to the LLC's dedicated capital escrow account at a CBE-licensed bank, with the remitter referencing the equity participation purpose code and the GAFI registration number. The receiving bank reports the transaction on the CBE electronic reporting platform, applies the prevailing interbank market rate for conversion unless a designated foreign-currency account is held, and requires original supporting documents including board resolution, subscription or shareholder loan agreement, and UBO identity before releasing funds. No prior capital-import license is required post-2024 unification; banks may request a structured source-of-funds questionnaire for amounts above internal thresholds.Outward settlements of customs duties, port handling, freight and insurance, supplier invoices, and intercompany fees are executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border transfers, (ii) SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit governed by UCP 600 for goods requiring payment against transport documents, and (iii) EGP RTGS transfers through the Egyptian ACH operated by the Egyptian Banks Company for Technology for domestic duty and fee payments. All import-related disbursements require a validated ACID and Load Cycle Number registered on the NAFEZA Single Window before the wire is released; customs fees must be settled from a CBE-licensed bank account in EGP or via the customs authority e-payment gateway. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of annual taxable profits for Egyptian resident companies and Egyptian permanent establishments, subject to applicable special regimes and incentives.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 10% on dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company to a non-resident shareholder, subject to treaty reduction and specific statutory rules. A reduced domestic rate of 5% may apply in qualifying listed-share circumstances. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation generally follows a master-file, Local-File, and country-by-country reporting framework. A Local File is commonly required where related-party transactions exceed approximately EGP 8 million during the relevant fiscal year, subject to transaction-category, annual-indexation, and current executive-regulation rules. A Master File and CbC Report may apply to Egyptian members of multinational groups meeting the applicable group-revenue and Egyptian-entity conditions; the CbC threshold is generally linked to the equivalent of EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue. Thresholds and filing mechanics must be verified against the current Egyptian Tax Authority rules.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance remains material under Egyptian transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-avoidance framework, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse principles, deductibility rules, permanent-establishment analysis, and controlled transaction audits. A customs brokerage LLC should demonstrate Egyptian personnel, licensed operational capability, customs-system access, decision-making, premises, risk control, bank activity, customer contracts, and actual performance of clearance and transit functions. Conduit companies, circular invoicing, non-performing related-party service entities, and head-office charges lacking evidence are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, statutory additional tax, administrative fines, and criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion. Criminal consequences may include imprisonment and a fine or monetary sanction linked to the evaded tax under the applicable tax-procedure and income-tax laws.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, authorized signatories, and tax agents who participate in, order, or knowingly facilitate the conduct may face personal criminal liability, civil restitution, tax recovery, asset attachment, and restrictions on dealing with public authorities. Travel bans are not automatic but may be ordered by competent prosecutorial or judicial authorities.
Unlicensed customs brokerage and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: License suspension or cancellation, customs reassessment, seizure or forfeiture of goods, payment of duties and taxes, customs fines, and criminal penalties under Egyptian customs legislation. Sanctions depend on the conduct, goods, value, intent, and repeat-offense status.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, customs brokers, and employees may face personal criminal prosecution, confiscation, civil claims, professional disqualification, and judicial travel restrictions. Corporate limited liability does not protect persons who personally participate in customs offenses.
VAT and invoicing violations

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, denial of input credit, additional tax, administrative fines, invoice-related penalties, possible suspension of tax registration, and criminal liability for deliberate fraud.

Director personal liability: Authorized representatives and responsible finance personnel may be personally prosecuted where they knowingly approve false invoices, fabricated input tax, or concealed supplies. Civil recovery and asset attachment may accompany prosecution.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian payer may be assessed for the unwithheld amount, additional tax, late-payment additions, fines, and, for deliberate conduct, criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers who knowingly authorize non-compliant payments may face personal liability for tax recovery, civil restitution, and criminal prosecution. Travel restrictions require competent authority action and are not automatic.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment of taxable income, denial of deductions, secondary withholding-tax consequences, penalties for documentation or disclosure failures, and interest or additional tax on the resulting assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil and administrative exposure where they knowingly approve sham arrangements or false records. Criminal liability generally requires evidence of intentional fraud, evasion, forgery, or other separately criminal conduct.
Shell company and absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, reallocation of income, permanent-establishment or beneficial-owner findings, treaty relief denial, tax reassessment, penalties, and possible investigation for tax evasion, fraud, or money laundering where concealment is involved.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate a sham structure may face civil restitution, asset attachment, disqualification, and criminal prosecution. A corporate veil is not a defense to personal fraud, forgery, tax evasion, customs offenses, or money laundering.
Anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance

Statutory sanction: Customer due-diligence failures may lead to regulatory fines, license consequences, reporting obligations, and criminal investigation. Money laundering, terrorist-financing, sanctions, or prohibited-trade offenses may carry severe imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and business restrictions under applicable Egyptian law.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and employees may be personally liable for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or reckless approval. Courts or prosecutors may order asset freezes, confiscation, and travel bans; such measures are case-specific and not automatic.
Forgery, bribery, and official-corruption conduct

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, confiscation, repayment of unlawful gains, contract or license consequences, tax disallowance, and potential debarment from public or customs-related activity.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize, pay, conceal, or benefit from bribery or forgery may be personally imprisoned, fined, ordered to repay gains, barred from management, and subject to asset seizure or travel restrictions by competent authorities.
Recordkeeping and audit obstruction

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, adverse assessment based on available information, denial of deductions or VAT credits, additional tax, license action, and criminal sanctions where obstruction, destruction, or falsification is intentional.

Director personal liability: The responsible director, manager, accountant, or records custodian may face personal fines, prosecution, civil recovery, and court-ordered restrictions. Limited liability does not shield persons responsible for destruction or obstruction.
