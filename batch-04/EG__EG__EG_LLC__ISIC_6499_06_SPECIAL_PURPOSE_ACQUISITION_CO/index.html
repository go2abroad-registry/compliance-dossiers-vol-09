Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Cairo; electronic portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (unified tax registration and e-filing platform).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must first obtain legal existence through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) incorporation procedure, then apply to the Egyptian Tax Authority for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the unified tax registration form. For VAT purposes, the same TIN serves as the VAT ID. The ETA registration requires the entity's constitutional documents, power of attorney for the local tax representative, proof of Egyptian registered address, and identification of legal persons/shareholders. Registration must be completed within 30 days of incorporation. A tax card (Bayan Card) is issued, consolidating CIT, VAT, and payroll tax registrations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must first obtain legal existence through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) incorporation procedure, then apply to the Egyptian Tax Authority for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the unified tax registration form. For VAT purposes, the same TIN serves as the VAT ID. The ETA registration requires the entity's constitutional documents, power of attorney for the local tax representative, proof of Egyptian registered address, and identification of legal persons/shareholders. Registration must be completed within 30 days of incorporation. A tax card (Bayan Card) is issued, consolidating CIT, VAT, and payroll tax registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box classification system. Entity level taxation is mandatory for an Egyptian LLC. Elections available include: (i) application of double taxation treaty relief on foreign income; (ii) election to attribute VAT input tax using the standard credit mechanism; (iii) election under investment incentive regimes (e.g., Special Economic Zones of Suez Canal) for certain tax holidays or exemptions; (iv) declaration to apply the simplified presumptive taxation scheme, if eligible. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized under Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover; mandatory VAT registration is triggered when the value of taxable supplies of goods and services exceeds EGP 500,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under the Egyptian VAT Law applies to: (i) goods physically exported outside Egypt, with proof of actual customs exit; (ii) supplies of goods to free zones and special economic zones if qualifying; (iii) international transport of goods or passengers; (iv) services directly related to exported goods or international transport, carried out and delivered to non-residents; and (v) goods or services sold to foreign-flagged vessels and aircraft. The exporter must hold all shipping, customs, payment, and commercial evidence demonstrating that the goods left Egypt and that the consideration was received from a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Declaration (outward clearance approved by Egyptian Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill / Road Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where required)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit document evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Export contract or purchase order
  • ETA zero-rated export declaration/return

Mandatory electronic e-invoicing is phased in across all B2B transactions. Taxpayers must submit invoice data in real time to the ETA e-invoice system via HTTPS RESTful APIs with digital signature (XML/JSON schema), obtaining a Unique Identifier (UUID) from the ETA for each invoice before issuance to the buyer. Clearance is automated with a maximum of 15 seconds per invoice. E-receipts are mandated for B2C sales. Taxpayers must operate an API middleware through an accredited electronic invoicing service provider or direct integration. Digital stamping of invoices is replaced by the real-time clearance validation. Non-compliance blocks invoice issuance and triggers fines.

ETA and Central Bank of Egypt conduct post-shipment audits of export zero-rating claims. If zero-rating is claimed without actual physical export, proper customs documentation, or timely repatriation of export proceeds, the VAT becomes immediately due at 14%, plus late payment interest calculated daily using the Central Bank discount rate plus 2 percentage points, and a penalty not exceeding the amount of tax evaded. In cases of deliberate fraud or false declarations, administrative penalties range from EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000, with criminal liability including imprisonment under Egyptian VAT Law No. 67 of 2016. Retrospective clawback may cover the preceding five tax years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP is formally convertible in the interbank market, with a market-determined exchange rate managed by CBE interventions during exceptional volatility. Current-account transactions for trade, services, and repatriation of after-tax profits are generally permitted without individual CBE permits, provided the full documentary chain is processed through a licensed Egyptian bank. Capital-account transactions are convertible subject to CBE complaint, including the registration of foreign participation under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Profit and dividend remittance by an Egyptian LLC is legally unconditional but operationally requires: audited financial statements, tax registration certificate and tax clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority, the general meeting's distribution resolution, beneficial ownership declarations, and a formal remittance request signed by the authorized bank signatories. In periods of FX reserve pressure, CBE may impose bank-level priority queues and settlement sequencing, but no general ban on profit remittance exists for compliant entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For any export-influenced inflow to the Egyptian LLC, CBE-licensed banks require full export proceeds to be repatriated to Egypt within 90 days from the date of shipment or earlier if the underlying letter of credit requires; banks must receive the foreign currency amount and credit it to the exporter's export proceeds account. After the 2016 liberalisation there is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio; exporters may retain the foreign currency in a bank-held account or convert at the prevailing market rate, but 100% of the proceeds must be booked in Egypt. The documentary compliance package includes the commodity import/export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and bank foreign receipt. Non-utilisation or set-off without bank consent is prohibited. A pure blank-check and acquisition vehicle that does not itself undertake commercial exports has no standing export surrender obligation unless the target operating company is an Egyptian exporter, in which case the CBE circulars apply at the Egyptian operating entity level.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting through the General Foreign Exchange Administration, the Banking Supervision Sector, and the Foreign Exchange Operations Committee. The statutory basis is the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, together with the Egyptian Foreign Exchange Law No. 38 of 1992 as applicable. The CBE exclusively licenses banks, approves capital account transfers, supervises bank SWIFT/FX operations, and issues binding circulars on inward and outward remittance documentation, nostro/vostro account operations, and usage of Egyptian pound and foreign currency accounts by resident and non-resident legal persons.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore EMI or non-bank fintech providers as primary treasury rails. Under Law No. 194 of 2020, deposit-taking and payment account issuance are restricted to licensed banks and regulated fintech payment institutions supervised by CBE. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully hold EGP client balances, open an Egyptian resident LLC operating account, or provide escrow services without partnering with an Egyptian bank or obtaining a CBE/Non-Bank Financial Regulatory Authority license. An offshore SWIFT-connected B2B platform may support ancillary receivables, but the capital injection, trust escrow, and M&A disbursement flows must be cleared through a licensed Egyptian licensed bank and correspond through the standard nostro network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The sole compliant inward route for foreign equity capital is via SWIFT MT103 from the investor’s bank to the Egyptian licensed bank’s nostro account, with beneficiary being the LLC’s capital escrow account. The remittance must substantively identify the underlying acquisition or SPAC capital subscription in the narrative field and be treated as a capital-account injection, not as a trade payment. The Egyptian bank will verify the letter of approval from Egypt’s investment authority, the LLC’s final capitalization table, the share subscription resolution, and the CBE’s beneficial ownership form. In practice the bank may require the funds to be preliminarily credited to a suspense account pending confirmation of the subscription ledger and the issuance of updated commercial registry records. For technical settlement, the LLC should direct the remitting bank to use the purpose code for acquisition of local equities or capital increase, or the CBE bank-specific remittance reason code used for Foreign Direct Investment, and must ensure that no cash proceeds are released until the escrow restrictions and anti-money laundering conditions are satisfied.Outbound disbursements for SPAC acquisition costs, trust contributions, legal advisor fees, notarial costs, and target securities settlement should be executed by SWIFT MT103 for direct third-party payments, SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank transfers between Egyptian banks and global correspondent banks, and documentary commercial letters of credit or standby letters of credit where the Egyptian operating treasury is purchasing goods or securing earn-out obligations. The bank requires the full underlying contract, tax invoice, corporate resolution, target closing statement, and CBE remittance request form. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% on taxable corporate income (Law 91/2005 as amended)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on cash or in-kind dividend distributions and deemed distributions made by an Egyptian resident company to a non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the close of the taxable fiscal year. For a calendar-year SPAC, the return and full payment are due on or before 30 April of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers the mandatory Local File; the mandatory Master File applies when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 5,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime in the manner of UAE; however, treaty benefit entitlements, free-zone incentives, and anti-abuse rules require genuine commercial substance in an Egyptian SPAC: a real registered office, local governance decisions, actual employees or assets for the acquisition activity, and beneficial owners disclosed to the ETA. A shell or conduit SPAC may be disregarded, denied treaty relief, or assessed on an estimated income basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under Law 91/2005: imprisonment of not less than one year and up to five years, fines of not less than 100% and up to 200% of evaded tax, automatic administrative penalty of 2% per month on unpaid tax, and immediate collection through attachment of assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the SPAC is a mere instrumentality; directors and managers can become jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, face travel bans and asset freezes, and may be sentenced to imprisonment for knowingly filing false returns.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization or disregarding of the entity, denial of DTA protection, assessment of the de facto beneficial owner on a look-through basis at 22.5% CIT, plus late-payment surcharges and restitution interest from the first non-compliant period.

Director personal liability: Directors may be added as respondents to enforcement proceedings; seizure of assets and travel bans may be ordered; egregious abuse can result in disgorgement orders against shareholders who moved assets through the sham vehicle.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002 as amended by Law 91/2021: administrative fines up to EGP 5,000,000, confiscation of involved funds, suspension of operating licenses, deregistration of the SPAC, and criminal prosecution of compliance officers for money laundering facilitation.

Director personal liability: Managers and AML officers face personal criminal liability, imprisonment, travel bans, and freeze orders over personal and corporate accounts where they knowingly permitted prohibited trust payments through the SPAC.
Withholding Tax and Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to 20% of unreported WHT plus 2% monthly late surcharges; under-reporting of profits may be corrected by the ETA using arm's length criteria, with adjustment penalties of up to 30% of the revised tax deficiency; failure to maintain documentation can result in an automatic base-default assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior tax officers can be ordered to settle unremitted WHT personally where corporate assets have been dissipated, and travel bans may be issued against responsible individuals pending full payment to the ETA.
