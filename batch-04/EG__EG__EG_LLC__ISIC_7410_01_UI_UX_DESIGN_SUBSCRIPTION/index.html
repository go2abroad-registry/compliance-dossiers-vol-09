Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), official unified tax and e-invoicing portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (eTax platform).

E-government filing portal

After incorporation with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), the EG_LLC must file the unified tax registration application at the competent ETA tax office or via the eTax portal on www.eta.gov.eg. Submission requires: GAFI incorporation certificate, notarized memorandum of association / articles of incorporation, certificate of the legal address (office lease), passport copies of the managers and shareholders, power of attorney appointing an Egyptian tax representative, and proof of opening an Egyptian bank account. The ETA then issues the 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the tax card (bitaka dharibiya), and activates the taxpayer in the VAT, payroll, and e-invoicing registries.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After incorporation with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), the EG_LLC must file the unified tax registration application at the competent ETA tax office or via the eTax portal on www.eta.gov.eg. Submission requires: GAFI incorporation certificate, notarized memorandum of association / articles of incorporation, certificate of the legal address (office lease), passport copies of the managers and shareholders, power of attorney appointing an Egyptian tax representative, and proof of opening an Egyptian bank account. The ETA then issues the 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the tax card (bitaka dharibiya), and activates the taxpayer in the VAT, payroll, and e-invoicing registries.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not maintain a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification regime under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005; an LLC is mandatorily taxed as a resident corporation at entity level. No pass-through election is available. A foreign investor may elect to operate through a branch treated as a permanent establishment instead of an LLC, but the branch remains entity-level taxed. Egypt applies limited special-regime elections for free-zone enterprises (e.g., 5% of turnover or tax exemption subject to GAFI and ETA approval); none qualify as a US-style classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies (including export zero-rated supplies) in the preceding 12 months; mandatory registration for persons exceeding that threshold, with voluntary registration allowed for lower-turnover exporters to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT zero-rating applies to (i) physical exports of goods leaving Egypt and evidenced by a stamped customs export declaration, and (ii) cross-border services supplied to a non-resident beneficiary for direct use and economic enjoyment outside Egypt, which includes UI/UX design subscription services delivered digitally provided the foreign customer is the sole beneficial recipient. The exporter must hold a valid VAT registration and tax card, produce the export contract and customer purchase orders, receive consideration in convertible foreign currency through an Egyptian licensed bank, and maintain full digital records of service delivery. If the service is partly used or enjoyed within Egypt, zero-rating may be proportionally denied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (final settled ACI/ACS export declaration certified by the Egyptian Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / international CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Foreign Transactions Return Certificate - FTRC) issued by an authorized Egyptian bank confirming receipt of export proceeds

Egypt enforces a mandatory e-invoice and e-receipt regime introduced under Ministerial Decree No. 489 of 2020. VAT-registered taxpayers phased into the system must issue XML/JSON e-invoices and e-receipts through ETA-approved billing applications, transmit them to the ETA platform for real-time validation and clearance, integrate via the ETA REST APIs (contracting, signing and acceptance APIs), apply a qualified digital signature, and print or display a QR code on e-receipts. Issuance in electronic form is mandatory for all B2B and B2C documentation; rejected invoices do not produce a fiscal effect.

The ETA may audit zero-rating claims within the 5-year retrospective assessment period. If goods are not actually exported, payments are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory window, or the services are determined to be supplied or enjoyed in Egypt, the ETA will reverse the zero-rating, assess the 14% standard VAT on the gross consideration, charge default interest and late-payment monthly penalties from the original return due date, and impose administrative fines under Law No. 67 of 2016. Deliberate use of forged customs declarations or fictitious FTRCs, or aiding non-residents to simulate exports, constitutes criminal tax evasion referred to the Economic Court and attracts imprisonment, additional fines up to the tax evaded, and loss of all export incentive and refund rights. The clawback is joint and several against the taxpayer and its legal representative.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a liberalized interbank foreign-exchange framework. Following the March 2024 CBE decision to unify the EGP/USD market and remove exchange-rate restrictions, both current-account service receipts (including cross-border design subscription revenue) and capital-account remittances are, in principle, freely convertible through authorized banks. In practice, every outward flow requires a declared purpose, full AML documentation and available interbank liquidity, and profit remittance is conditional on an Egyptian Tax Authority clearance certificate confirming that corporate and withholding-tax obligations under Law No. 91 of 2005 have been settled. Inward equity is protected by the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 guarantees, which permit unrestricted transfer of investment proceeds subject to standard bank documentation. Treasury-prudent selection of banks with strong nostro liquidity (NBE, CIB, HSBC) mitigates any temporary interbank FX tightness.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For invisible exports, including UI/UX design subscription services, CBE regulations require that foreign-currency proceeds be received in an Egyptian authorized bank within 90 days from the date of service provision or contractual due date, extendable to a maximum of 180 days with documented justification. Under the post-March-2024 flexible exchange-rate regime, the historical 100% mandatory surrender at the official rate has been replaced by a full-repatriation framework: the entire export receipt must be credited to an EGP or foreign-currency account at an Egyptian authorized bank within the statutory window, with any conversion executed through CBE-authorized interbank channels. Unlawful offshore retention, delayed repatriation or under-declaration triggers CBE penalties, suspension of the exporter's banking facilities and transaction-level denial of future outward remittances.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Al-Bank al-Markazi al-Misri, acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) as the sole monetary, licensing and foreign-exchange regulatory authority of the Arab Republic of Egypt

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury depository; Medium viability as an ancillary collection rail. Wise (the named target rail) holds e-money or banking licenses in the EU, UK, Singapore and other jurisdictions but does not hold a CBE payment-institution or banking license under Law No. 194/2020. It therefore cannot lawfully maintain an EG-LLC EGP-denominated treasury account, open a capital-escrow account, or act as a settlement agent for onshore Egyptian disbursements. Stripe Billing is similarly only a global payment gateway that aggregates subscription card transactions. The compliant architecture is a collection conduit: Wise and Stripe sweep USD/EUR receivables directly to the EG-LLC's designated foreign-currency account at a licensed Egyptian tier-1 bank via SWIFT MT103, with every settlement traceable to the underlying subscription contract and to the LLC's registered beneficiary name. Use of these rails without a local bank counterparty is not legally viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder capital injection: funds must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the designated capital-escrow or operational account held at the selected authorized Egyptian bank, with the remitter being the sole corporate shareholder and the beneficiary identified as the EG-LLC capital-subscription account. The payment must carry a balance-of-payments purpose code corresponding to inward direct investment equity participation in the services sector, and the bank requires a debriefing file comprising notarized and apostilled shareholders' and board resolutions, the subscribed-capital schedule, the GAFI certificate or commercial registration reflecting the equity percentage and, where mandated, the CBE declaration-of-purpose form. Verification includes source-of-funds documentation and confirmation that the funds flow directly from the shareholder's corporate account rather than from an unlicensed third party, invoicing platform or cash-heavy jurisdiction.Clearing-rail hierarchy for the EG-LLC: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for routine operating expenses, contractor fees and software or infrastructure subscriptions, executed through the Egyptian bank's correspondent network with full FX availability checks and CBE purpose declarations; (2) SWIFT MT202 COV for priority correspondent-driven settlement where an intermediate beneficiary bank is interposed; (3) commercial documentary credits (UCP600) for any physical procurement or supplier arrangement requiring LC protection; and (4) domestic EGP disbursements for payroll, VAT, corporate tax and onshore vendors via the CBE-operated Egypt Interbank RTGS settlement system. All outward foreign-currency transfers require the underlying commercial contract, and dividend-type or profit-remittance flows additionally require the Egyptian Tax Authority clearance certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions during the fiscal year triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File. A Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 500,000,000. In all cases where related-party transactions exist, the Egyptian LLC must submit the TP declaration annex with the corporate income tax return and keep the full Local File, Master File and TP study available in the local jurisdiction for inspection by the ETA within 30 days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted a dedicated UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute. However, Law 91 of 2005 and the associated GAAR provisions require that every transaction have a genuine commercial purpose and that pricing reflect the functions, assets and risks assumed by each party. For the ISIC 7410 UI/UX design subscription company, the ETA may recharacterise the whole entity as a tax-shell if it lacks an Egyptian office, senior design personnel, binding customer contracts, and independent decision-making. The LLC must maintain contemporaneous records of personnel, design project approvals, subscription pricing policies and bank accounts located in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Under-declaration of Digital Subscription Income

Statutory sanction: A fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 and imprisonment of at least two years under Article 137 of Law 91 of 2005; the ETA will additionally assess the full tax due plus a 50% evasion penalty, and such administrative sanction may not be covered by insurance.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or who participates in the concealment may have the corporate veil pierced, becoming jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax, civil restitution of the diverted funds, travel ban, asset freezing and imprisonment if fraud or gross personal fault is proven.
Shell Company / Artificial Profit Shifting through Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: The ETA may adjust the taxable income to an arm's length result under Law 91 of 2005, imposing a 25% penalty on the additional tax assessed, together with a monthly 0.5% delayed-payment surcharge; the entity can be recharacterised as having a permanent establishment in Egypt and may lose the applicability of tax treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable to civil restitution for the TP adjustment and interest; if the arrangement is found to be a sham for laundering funds, directors may also be subject to criminal prosecution for fraudulent bankruptcy or tax evasion, inclusive of imprisonment and a court-imposed travel ban.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance in Subscription Collections

Statutory sanction: Under the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002, fines up to EGP 100,000 and imprisonment of up to five years, compulsory confiscation of the monies involved, freezing of bank accounts and deregistration of the LLC's commercial register.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may personally face imprisonment, a travel ban and forfeiture of personal assets if they knowingly executed, facilitated or failed to report suspicious transactions; the corporate veil is pierced for intentional AML violations.
Withholding Tax Default on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The unpaid WHT is collected from the LLC together with a 2% monthly late-payment penalty and a fixed fine equal to 50% of the withheld amount; the ETA will also refuse to issue the tax clearance certificate, thereby blocking further dividend distributions and liquidation of the LLC.

Director personal liability: The directors of the LLC, as withholding and remitting officers, are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted WHT; the Egyptian financial courts may order direct attachment of personal bank accounts, a supervisory travel ban and, in egregious cases, custodial sentences.
