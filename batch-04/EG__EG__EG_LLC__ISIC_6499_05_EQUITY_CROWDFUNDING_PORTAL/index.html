Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 149 of 2023 (Egypt) and Executive Regulations issued by GAFI Decree No. 16 of 2024; residual application of Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, Articles 77-104 (Limited Liability Company formation and governance); Commercial Registration Law No. 34 of 1976; Commercial Code Law No. 17 of 1999; Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) Board Decree No. 11 of 2022 regulating crowdfunding activities.

Formation / constitutional filings

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Cairo; official portal: www.eta.gov.eg; electronic services and e-invoicing portal: https://einvoice.invoice.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must file the unified tax registration application at the competent ETA tax office or through the ETA e-services portal. The file must include: notarised and legalised certificate of incorporation of the foreign shareholder; memorandum and articles of association; board resolution authorising incorporation; appointment of the Egyptian manager and fiscal representative; notarised power of attorney and specimen signatures; passport copies of directors and shareholders; Egyptian registered office lease; Egyptian bank account confirmation; and the sectoral licence issued by the Financial Regulatory Authority for an equity crowdfunding portal. Upon validation the ETA issues a single 9-digit unified Tax Identification Number (TIN), which also operates as the VAT registration number. The taxpayer then must complete electronic-invoice onboarding before taxable supplies can begin.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Egyptian LLC is a separate juridical person taxed under Law 91 of 2005 on its worldwide income, with participation exemption and permanent-establishment rules; no pass-through eligibility, no check-the-box classification, and no territorial-streaming election is available for a resident LLC.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a check-the-box regime. Statutory election mechanisms are limited to tax depreciation method elections under Article 27 of the Executive Regulations, approval-based revaluation adjustments, and the carry-forward of tax losses for up to five years subject to legal conditions. There are no hybrid mismatch elections and no fiscal unity or group consolidation elections for LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 (Article 22 of Law 67 of 2016); voluntary registration is permitted below that threshold; non-resident suppliers of digital services to Egyptian consumers are registrable without threshold under the electronic commerce and e-invoicing rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports and qualifying offshore services are zero-rated under Law 67 of 2016 when: the supplier holds a valid VAT registration and cleared e-invoice code; the goods are physically despatched outside Egyptian customs territory under an approved export declaration; services are effectively performed, delivered and enjoyed outside Egypt; and the exporter retains the statutory document set including customs, transport, forex and banking evidence. Input VAT attributable to the exported goods is recoverable through the periodic VAT return after ETA document matching with the customs authority and Central Bank of Egypt foreign-currency records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Bayan entry) validated by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or international consignment note showing despatch from an Egyptian exit point
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List and the executed export sales contract
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate confirming receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Certificate of Origin and any sector-specific export licence where the underlying goods or services are regulated
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice carrying the zero-rated export code and QR stamp

Egypt mandates real-time electronic invoicing and clearance. Each invoice or receipt must be prepared as a digitally signed XML document under the ETA schema, transmitted through the ETA e-invoicing API, and cleared by the ETA before issuance. Cleared invoices receive a unique UUID, approval code, cryptographic QR code and digital stamp. Only cleared e-invoices may support input VAT deductions, zero-rated export claims, and VAT refunds. The e-receipt module applies to business-to-consumer retail transactions. Any new crowdfunding portal must complete API conformance testing and e-invoicing onboarding before the first taxable transaction.

ETA and the Egyptian Customs Authority cross-match export declarations, e-invoice clearance status, customs manifests, and Central Bank of Egypt foreign-exchange receipts. Unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers retrospective clawback of input VAT at 14%, monthly delay additional tax at 2%, administrative fines of up to EGP 500,000 per electronic invoice violation, and criminal liability under Law 67 of 2016 including imprisonment and fines up to five times the tax evaded, as well as suspension or permanent deactivation of e-invoicing privileges.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains full current account convertibility for legitimate trade and service transactions, but capital account transactions remain subject to material administrative controls and licensing. Profit remittances for registered foreign invested entities are generally permitted via banks after verification of audited financial statements, tax clearances, and CBE rules, but may face rationing during periods of FX scarcity. Non-resident capital repatriation is allowed under Law No. 72 of 2017 (Investment Law) provided the investment is registered with GAFI and the FX purchase is supported by certified documentation. For an equity crowdfunding portal holding client escrow balances, strict segregation and avoidance of quasi-deposit liabilities are mandatory to qualify as a corporate treasury operation rather than a banking activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Decree No. 89 of 2022 and related circulars, exporters must repatriate export proceeds into Egypt within the statutory period of 5 months from the date of exportation (unless a specific exception is granted). All export receipts in foreign currency must be either sold to a licensed Egyptian bank or credited to a foreign-currency account maintained in Egypt; no mandatory surrender ratio or fixed conversion percentage is currently imposed, though the CBE may require conversion for certain categories at times of balance-of-payments stress. For portfolio-type services, the relevant 'deemed export' regime may require registration with the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) prior to receipt of foreign funds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — concurrently the monetary authority, banking regulator, and primary FX administrator under Law No. 88 of 2003 and its implementing regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct feasibility. Offshore digital EMI/PSP providers are not licensed to open payment settlement accounts in Egypt, and the CBE prohibits non-licensed non-bank entities from holding customer funds in Egyptian pounds or facilitating local peer-to-peer transfers. Such providers may only serve as upstream technology or card-processing vendors. To operate lawfully, the crowdfunding portal must obtain a non-bank financial license from the FRA (under Law No. 169 of 2020 on Crowdfunding) and a CBE payment system license under the electronic payment law; foreign EMIs cannot act as the primary escrow agent without a full banking license or an authorized partnership with a licensed Egyptian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder financing for the crowdfunding portal must be routed via licensed Egyptian banks using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for larger amounts, with mandatory FX purpose codes (e.g., code '900' for foreign direct investment inflow, or code '501' for equity injection). The capital must be registered with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and the CBE's foreign assets database within 30 days. The receiving bank will generate an FD (Foreign Direct Investment) identification number, which is required for any subsequent repatriation of profit or capital. Escrow deposits from investors are to be received only through an escrow account opened at a licensed Egyptian bank or a blockchain-based payment system approved by the FRA; separate comingling with the portal's operating funds is prohibited.Outbound trade and investment disbursements are executed via SWIFT MT103 (direct wire), MT700/MT710 for Commercial Letters of Credit, and MT400 for documentary collection. For cross-border crowdfunding payouts to project owners located outside Egypt, MT103 with explicit purpose code 'trade finance services' or 'service payment' is required, accompanied by the FRA-approved disbursement instruction and tax identification of the beneficiary. Within Egypt, payments to local project owners are settled through the CBE's RTGS system (RTGS-Star) and Instant Payment Network (IPN) under CBE regulation. All outbound FX transfers above the bank's due diligence threshold (typically EGP 200,000 equivalent) require a signed customs or service-provision contract, a tax card, and clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority for proof of prior tax registration.Plain-vanilla spot FX contracts intermediated by licensed Egyptian banks in the interbank market.Outright FX forwards (up to one year) with the physical delivery of the EGP against a hard currency, permitted for hedging actual current or capital account exposures documented by contract.FX swaps (fixed-to-fixed cross-currency basis swaps) where the underlying exposure is a real trade or investment transaction with a maturity matching the asset.Interest-rate swaps hedging the coupon exposure of the escrow or the portal's own foreign-currency liquidity, subject to confirmatory documentation under ISDA.Exchange-traded futures on USD/EGP offered through authorized brokers, subject to CBE position limits (note that part of EGP trading activity is still restricted for offshore settlement).Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) traded by Egyptian banks with overseas counterparties are not freely permitted for onshore hedgers; an approval from the CBE's Market Operations Department is needed on a case-by-case basis, and only for genuine trade finance hedging where a deliverable forward is unavailable.
