Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Investor Services Center / Commercial Registry, operating as the one-stop shop for company formation in coordination with the Egyptian Commercial Registry and Notary Public offices

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 (as amended by Law No. 94 of 2005 and Law No. 2 of 2010), particularly the Limited Liability Company provisions under Articles 102 through 139, together with Executive Regulations issued by Prime Ministerial Decree No. 96 of 1982, and Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 establishing the one-stop incorporation regime

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Contract and Articles of Association (Aqd Al-Taasis / Nass Al-Nizam Al-Dakhily) signed by all founding members and certified by the competent Notary Public

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 34 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Egyptian law imposes no general statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Limited Liability Company; however, the subscribed capital must be fully allocated, and any contribution from non-resident founders must be deposited into a temporary bank escrow account with an Egyptian licensed bank, evidenced by a certificate of deposit that must be presented to GAFI at the time of filing. The Central Bank of Egypt circulars require the escrow account to remain blocked until the incorporation is registered and the commercial registration certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises require a fire safety certificate from the General Directorate of Civil Defense, and the building must have a valid structural occupancy permit issued by the municipal authorities. Occupational clearance requires no special industrial safety licenses; however, the company must provide a safe working environment, adequate lighting, ergonomic workstations, fire exits, and emergency medical equipment in line with the Egyptian Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 and its implementing regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: Egyptian law imposes no general statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Limited Liability Company; however, the subscribed capital must be fully allocated, and any contribution from non-resident founders must be deposited into a temporary bank escrow account with an Egyptian licensed bank, evidenced by a certificate of deposit that must be presented to GAFI at the time of filing. The Central Bank of Egypt circulars require the escrow account to remain blocked until the incorporation is registered and the commercial registration certificate is issued.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), the single national tax administration under the Ministry of Finance. Official portals: https://www.eta.gov.eg (general e-services) and https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg (e-invoicing and e-receipts platform).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the LLC with GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) and obtain the Commercial Register (CR) extract from the Ministry of Trade and Industry. 2. Open the ETA tax file (Tax Identification Number/TIN application) within 30 days of commercial registration per Article 18 of Law 91/2005, submitting: CR extract, GAFI incorporation certificate, notarized Articles of Association, valid IDs/passports of managers and shareholders, registered office lease, and a valid power of attorney for the approved tax agent. 3. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the LLC is a separate juridical person mandatorily taxed as a resident company on worldwide income at the standard CIT rate of 22.5% (Law 91/2005, Art. 7, as amended). Pass-through eligibility: not available in Egypt. Territorial scope: residents taxed on worldwide income with foreign-tax credit; non-residents taxed on Egyptian-source income only. Tax_Exempt_Zone: available only by election for Free-Zone companies under Investment Law 72/2017 or Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE) companies under Law 83/2002, typically granting 0% CIT for up to 5–10 years followed by reduced rates or a 1–5% gross-revenue tax regime.

Available election forms

None — Egyptian law has no check-the-box, no S-corporation, no partnership-style pass-through election, and no BEPS hybrid-mismatch elections. The only substantive regime election is the Free Zone / SCZONE designation: upon GAFI approval the LLC may elect Free-Zone status, converting the standard 22.5% CIT into a 1–5% gross-revenue tax (depending on zone category) with associated customs and VAT advantages; once granted, the regime applies for the licensed period and the company is excluded from standard CIT computation. No other entity-classification elections are available to an ordinary LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (Law 67/2016, Art. 10); registration is mandatory upon crossing the threshold and voluntary for taxpayers below it, including non-residents supplying taxable services into Egypt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Law 67/2016, Art. 16 and Executive Regulation 622/2016 zero-rate exported goods and services. For ISIC 7820 offshore BPO/call-center services, the conditions are: (i) the service is supplied to a non-resident principal/end-client located outside Egypt; (ii) the place of supply is outside Egypt — the service is performed from Egypt but consumed wholly for the foreign client's business abroad; (iii) the consideration is paid in freely convertible foreign currency and received through an Egyptian bank; (iv) the contract and invoicing are concluded directly with the non-resident entity on arm's-length terms; (v) the exporter maintains the documented export evidence bundle. The ETA recognizes offshore BPO/call-center services by Egyptian suppliers to non-resident recipients as taxable exports at 0%; no reverse charge is owed by the Egyptian supplier, while imported digital/IT services remain subject to reverse-charge VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Egyptian NAFIZA customs export manifest) for any embedded physical goods; for pure BPO services, the ETA-validated zero-rated service-export e-invoice serves as the equivalent export declaration
  • Bill of Lading (for embedded goods) or a service delivery acceptance report containing call-center operational logs, call-detail records, and service-level reports evidencing offshore consumption by the non-resident principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) and foreign-currency credit advice issued by the Egyptian bank evidencing receipt and repatriation/retention of the FX export proceeds within the statutory window

Mandatory for all VAT-registered persons (phased rollout since November 2020). B2B invoices and, progressively, B2C e-receipts must be issued through the ETA e-invoicing platform. Each invoice/XML or JSON record is signed with an ETA-issued digital e-signature, transmitted to the ETA within 24 hours of issuance via the ETA open REST API or the taxpayer portal, centrally validated by the ETA, and returned with a unique acceptance UUID and a QR two-dimensional barcode that must be printed or embedded on the invoice. The model is central validation with near-real-time clearance (not per-invoice fiscal stamping); the ETA retains the complete digital audit trail. Non-compliant invoices without a valid UUID/QR, unsigned, or transmitted late are invalid for input-VAT deduction. Taxpayer systems must comply with the current ETA XML/JSON schema, provider ID, device ID, and document-hash chaining requirements.

The ETA audits zero-rated export claims via e-invoice data analytics, bank remittance matching, and on-demand documentation reviews. Failure of substantiation triggers: (1) retrospective clawback of the zero-rating benefit through assessment of the unpaid 14% VAT on the export consideration, plus a delay penalty of 2% per month on the unpaid tax (Law 67/2016, Art. 43) and statutory interest; (2) reversal and recovery of any input-VAT refund already paid, secured through bank guarantees; (3) criminal tax-evasion exposure for issuing false export invoices or obtaining fraudulent refunds under Law 67/2016, Articles 44–46, carrying imprisonment, fines not less than the tax evaded or refund claimed, disqualification of managers, and publication of the conviction; (4) administrative sanctions including blacklisting from the e-invoicing clearance service, suspension of the VAT registration certificate, and loss of export/accreditation privileges; (5) Central Bank of Egypt exchange-control penalties and AML scrutiny where export proceeds are not received, retained, or converted in an Egyptian bank account within the 180-day repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital account: the EGP is officially convertible for eligible inward foreign direct investment and profit or dividend repatriation under the unified exchange-rate framework effective March 2024, with no statutory quantitative ceiling; banks nevertheless apply documentary gates including audited financial statements, valid Corporate Income Tax registration and e-filing status under Law No. 91 of 2005, CBE profit-remittance declaration forms, and up-to-date enhanced due diligence. Current account: BPO service-export revenue is convertible without monetary limit where an underlying service contract and e-invoice exist; hard-currency credits may be retained in an FCY export-proceeds account without forced conversion but must be received through a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account. Operational friction persists only in clearing latency, such as 24 to 48 hours for large USD settlement and weekend NOSTRO availability, not in substantive controls. For EG_LLC with 100% foreign ownership, no minimum capital is imposed under the LLC framework, so the treasury posture is bankability-driven rather than capital-adequacy-driven.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory repatriation applies: all service-export proceeds, including remote BPO call-center fees, must be credited to a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank within 150 days from the date of service provision. There is currently no forced conversion or surrender ratio; the earlier partial-surrender percentages were discontinued under the post-2024 CBE policy, and 100% of eligible export receipts may remain in an FCY Export Proceeds Account for subsequent external disbursement, voluntary conversion at the interbank rate, or dividend funding. Non-compliance with the 150-day credit deadline results in suspension of priority FX purchase entitlements, downgrading of the entity's CBE risk classification, and formal reporting of the exporter as non-compliant. Documentary retention must include the CBE export declaration or service-export form, SWIFT MT103 or MT950 evidence, the invoice-to-bank credit reconciliation ledger, and the underlying client agreement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), established under Law No. 88 of 2003 (Central Bank, Banking Sector and Money Law), as amended by Law No. 194 of 2020; the CBE acts as the central monetary authority and the foreign exchange administration, exercising oversight through licensed commercial banks and enforcing cross-border capital movement rules under Egypt's AML/CFT Law No. 80 of 2002.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary disbursement rail; Low as the primary treasury capture. An Egypt-domiciled LLC is statutorily required to receive export or service-export proceeds through a CBE-licensed bank, so offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer cannot lawfully act as the sole collection account for BPO revenue. The compliant operating model pairs the licensed Egyptian bank SWIFT MT103 rail as the primary settlement pipe with a fintech EMI confined to low-value operating overhead, such as software subscriptions and sub-threshold contractor payments. Wise_B2B and Wire Transfer are both targeted rails for EG_LLC; Wire Transfer must carry the dominant volume, while Wise_B2B is permitted only for incidental operational spend below USD 2,500 per month and must never accumulate non-repatriated client receivables. Any use of an offshore EMI must be disclosed to the Egyptian onboarding bank as a treasury gate, with an automatic weekly sweep back to the onshore FCY account to preserve the export-proceeds record.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing for initial equity and ongoing shareholder loans is a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or UBO through their home-country bank to the EG_LLC account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, using the CBE balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment or capital contribution, which is typically the 300 or 301 class as shown in the bank's inward-transfer electronic form. The receiving bank must issue an Inward Foreign Investment Remittance Certificate to evidence the capital entry under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; this certificate forms the foundation for later profit remittance clearance. Funds are initially credited to a Capital Escrow Account and released to the Operational Account only after the Commercial Registry registration is completed and the board resolution is authenticated. Equity inflows must never be commingled with service-export revenue in the same purpose code; careful labeling preserves the FDI record and prevents a subsequent dispute with CBE over ownership of funds. When conversion of USD capital to EGP is required, it must be executed at the official interbank rate on the value date, with the conversion contract retained for audit.Primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor, contractor, dividend, interest, and software license disbursements, with each transfer supported by an underlying invoice, contract, or board minute and a matching CBE purpose code such as 090 for services or 231 for royalties and license fees. For physical equipment procurement or high-value turnkey imports, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 is the recommended settlement vehicle. Inside Egypt, the RTGS wholesale system and the InstaPay instant payment network carry EGP payroll, taxes, rent, and supplier settlements at negligible cost. The treasury must adopt a dual-control disbursement policy and a 48-hour booking window, ensuring the FX desk at the Egyptian bank has full visibility of upcoming outbound hard-currency flows. The targeted rails for EG_LLC, Wise_B2B and Wire Transfer, are mapped as follows: Wire Transfer via SWIFT MT103 dominates both receivable collection and principal outbound settlement, preserving the export-proceeds repatriation chain; Wise_B2B is reserved for incidental cross-border operational disbursements under USD 2,500 per month. For profit or dividend remittances, the outbound MT103 must be coupled with the annual audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and the mutually signed CBE profit-transfer form; Egyptian withholding tax on dividends to non-residents will be applied under Law No. 91 of 2005 and available double-taxation treaties.Deliverable OTC outright USD/EGP forward contracts with tenors up to 12 months, executed through a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank against evidenced underlying export receivables or hard-currency payablesNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) on USD/EGP through a recognized offshore counterparty, permitted only as a complement to onshore forward capacity where the CBE interbank market lacks depthCross-currency interest rate swaps for aligning USD-denominated BPO receipts to EGP operating expenditure and debt serviceFX swaps for short-term liquidity equalization between EGP and FCY balances at interbank tenorsVanilla OTC currency options on USD/EGP executed through a licensed authorized dealer, conditional upon board approval and sound risk-governance documentationNatural hedging through retention of USD, EUR, GBP export proceeds in the FCY Export Proceeds Account, reducing reliance on derivative instruments

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary disbursement rail; Low as the primary treasury capture. An Egypt-domiciled LLC is statutorily required to receive export or service-export proceeds through a CBE-licensed bank, so offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer cannot lawfully act as the sole collection account for BPO revenue. The compliant operating model pairs the licensed Egyptian bank SWIFT MT103 rail as the primary settlement pipe with a fintech EMI confined to low-value operating overhead, such as software subscriptions and sub-threshold contractor payments. Wise_B2B and Wire Transfer are both targeted rails for EG_LLC; Wire Transfer must carry the dominant volume, while Wise_B2B is permitted only for incidental operational spend below USD 2,500 per month and must never accumulate non-repatriated client receivables. Any use of an offshore EMI must be disclosed to the Egyptian onboarding bank as a treasury gate, with an automatic weekly sweep back to the onshore FCY account to preserve the export-proceeds record.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% corporate income tax on net taxable profit (Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross dividends and profit distributions paid to non-resident shareholders (Law No. 91 of 2005, Article 75).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final return and full tax payment due within four months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., by 30 April for a calendar-year taxpayer).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions exceed EGP 8 million per tax year; Master File applies when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 2 billion; Country-by-Country Report thresholds apply at group turnover above EGP 15 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not impose Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, tax residence, permanent establishment permanence tests, general anti-abuse rules under Article 60 of Law No. 91/2005, and substance-over-form principles apply. A shell Egyptian LLC with no employees, no local management or no active bank flow is recharacterised and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Suppression of Receipts

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of up to three times the amount evaded, a criminal fine of EGP 5,000–25,000, imprisonment commonly imposed from six months to three years, and confiscation of the proceeds under Law No. 91/2005 as applied with Law No. 30/2022.

Director personal liability: Directors and the authorised manager who signed false returns are criminally liable; civil joint liability for the unpaid tax, travel ban, passport seizure and precautionary freezing of personal assets under Law No. 88/2005 follow until settlement.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation / WHT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The LLC is held as principal debtor for the uncollected withholding tax, plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge and administrative fines up to EGP 20,000; the tax debt is enforceable by forced attachment of bank accounts and movable assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the naked distribution are jointly and severally personally liable for the withheld amounts and may face criminal prosecution when funds are diverted; travel bans and asset freezes are routinely issued by the ETA executive collection authorities.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterisation under Article 60 of Law No. 91/2005 imposes a deemed taxable profit assessed at 22.5%, denies deductibility of the related-party recharges, withdraws DTA relief, and adds a 2% monthly delay charge; the taxpayer is blacklisted and deregistered where evasion is shown.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and board are jointly liable for the whole reassessed corporate tax; if fraud or malicious intent is proven, the corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution; travel bans and seizure orders run against the managers personally.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The ETA makes a TP adjustment raising taxable income at 22.5%, applies the 2% monthly delay surcharge, levies administrative fines for non-documentation under Law No. 30/2022, and can reopen past years up to the statutory examination periods.

Director personal liability: Directors approving distorted TP returns are personally pursued as co-authors of tax evasion; the civil joint liability extends to the adjusted tax, delay penalties and legal expenses while travel bans are imposed pending review.
AML / Unlicensed Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Egyptian AML Law No. 80/2002 carry imprisonment of up to 15 years and fines of two to five times the value of the laundered funds, with confiscation of instruments and proceeds ordered by the criminal court.

Director personal liability: The senior officer and the AML compliance officer are individually criminally liable for omission of supervision; travel bans, passport seizure, asset freezing and disqualification from corporate management are applied during investigation and after conviction.
