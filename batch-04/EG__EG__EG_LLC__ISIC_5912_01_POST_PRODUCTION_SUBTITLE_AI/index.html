Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981. The subscribed share capital is divided into equal nominal shares and fully paid within the period specified in the Articles of Association. The paid-up portion must be deposited in a temporary bank account with a licensed Egyptian bank, and the bank's deposit certificate is filed with GAFI before commercial registration is granted. There is no separate escrow account requirement.

Funding & unlock path: The studio must obtain a fire safety certificate from the General Department of Civil Defense after inspection of fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and electrical wiring. A structural load clearance certificate for the leased or owned premises must also be issued by an accredited engineering office and verified by the municipal building administration. Additionally, the workplace registration and occupational safety and health clearance must be obtained from the Ministry of Manpower under Law No. 12 of 2003, including a first-aid cabinet, fire warden training, and ergonomic workstation compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981. The paid-up portion must be deposited in a temporary bank account with a licensed Egyptian bank, and the bank's deposit certificate is filed with GAFI before commercial registration is granted. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC at the Commercial Registry through GAFI's one-stop-shop under Company Law 159/1981 as amended (no minimum capital; 100% foreign ownership permitted), obtaining the commercial register extract and incorporation certificate. Step 2: File tax registration forms with the competent ETA tax office pursuant to Tax Procedures Law No. 8 of 2010 within 30 days of commencing activity, attaching the incorporation deed, board resolution, passports/IDs of managers, authorized signatories, lease/address proof and business description; the ETA then issues the Tax Card (Bitāqa Ḍarībiyya) and the Tax Registration Number (TIN - tax office code + serial number). Step 3: Register for VAT under Law 67/2016 within 60 days after the registered person exceeds the EGP 500,000 annual turnover threshold, or immediately for voluntary registration, obtaining a separate VAT registration certificate. Step 4: Register as an employer for salary tax and withholding tax (Form 2) and obtain the digital signature certificate required for e-Tax portal access and e-invoicing API authentication. Step 5: Obtain the exporter/customs code through the electronic customs platform and open an Egyptian bank account (EGP and FX) with a CBE-authorized bank, which is required for export proceeds repatriation and for evidencing the inward remittance in export zero-rating claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the EG LLC is a resident company taxed on worldwide net income under Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 30/2023, at 15% on annual taxable profits up to EGP 120 million and 22.5% on the excess. Distributions to a foreign corporate parent are generally subject to a 10% Egyptian withholding tax (gross), while royalties, management fees and technical service fees paid offshore are subject to a 20% withholding tax. A non-resident without a permanent establishment is taxed only on Egypt-source income. No pass-through, partnership or territorial participation exemption is available for an LLC; the export service income remains fully within the Egyptian CIT base.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch classification election. Available statutory elections include: (i) adoption of a non-calendar fiscal year subject to ETA approval; (ii) optional small/micro business simplified cash-basis and turnover-rate taxation for eligible SMEs; (iii) voluntary VAT registration for sub-threshold businesses and the election to recover VAT via refund rather than credit; (iv) transfer-pricing method selection under the best-method rule (CUP, resale price, cost plus, TNMM, profit split) and optional unilateral, bilateral or multilateral Advance Pricing Agreements with the ETA under Decree 320/2019; (v) ordinary foreign tax credit with no basket election; (vi) indefinite carry-forward of tax losses with no carry-back, subject to ownership continuity rules; thin-capitalization rules disallow interest on debt exceeding a 4:1 debt-to-equity ratio, and hybrid financing instruments may be recharacterized by the ETA under general anti-abuse principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of supplies; mandatory VAT registration is required within 60 days after the threshold is exceeded; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold; taxpayers below the threshold may use the simplified small-taxpayer procedure and are not immediately subject to the e-invoicing mandate until integrated by ETA decree.

E-invoicing is compulsory for VAT-registered businesses. Invoices must be issued electronically in JSON or XML, digitally signed (RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 with SHA-256 hash), transmitted to the ETA portal (https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg) or through certified ERP/API integrations, and must receive an ETA acceptance code after real-time validation. Invoices must also display a QR code containing the invoice digest data. The standard B2B submission window is within 24 hours of issuance; rejected invoices are deemed non-issued and may be corrected only by credit/debit notes. The e-receipt regime (real-time B2C transmission) has been rolled out in phases since July 2022. Enforcement of e-invoicing now covers all VAT registrants; failure to transmit and clear invoices triggers fines under Law 67/2016 and the e-invoicing decrees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is classified as a partially convertible currency with a unified market-driven exchange rate since the 2022 liberalization. Current account transactions benefiting bona fide trade in services (including post-production and AI-based subtitling services) are generally permitted, subject to documentary verification of the underlying contract, tax registration, and the submission of a valid invoice or license agreement. Profit remittances and dividend transfers to non-resident shareholders are treated as capital account transactions: they require (i) audited annual financial statements, (ii) a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority, (iii) a Board of Directors resolution authorizing the distribution, and (iv) a foreign currency availability declaration from the commercial bank. The main hurdle is not a statutory cap but the operational allocation of foreign currency liquidity within the authorized bank; banks may apply an administrative queue for USD conversion. Effective repatriation typically takes 5-10 business days once the documentary file is complete.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Circular No. 548/2022 and subsequent monetary policy notices, Egyptian exporters must receive export proceeds in a licensed Egyptian bank within 180 days from the bill of lading/shipping date, extendable to 240 days for service industries with documented milestone billing. The exporter may retain 100% of the foreign currency in an Egyptian Foreign Currency Account or debit it against future import obligations; there is no mandatory conversion to EGP for the export of post-production services. If the exporter converts the proceeds to EGP, conversion must be made at the prevailing market exchange rate on the date of crediting. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window subjects the exporter to CBE penalties, exclusion from subsidized finance facilities, and denial of the annual tax certificate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW. Egypt enforces CBE Law No. 18/2019 on Payment Services and E-Commerce, requiring any entity that issues e-money, executes payment account acquisition, or facilitates cross-border settlement to be a CBE-licensed payment institution with a local branch. Offshore EMI digital wallets (e.g., Wise, Stripe operating as EMI, Revolut) cannot hold an Egyptian correspondent account or directly clear EGP; they may only be engaged as a front-end aggregation layer through CBE-licensed local payment gateways such as PaySky, Fawry, or Amazon Payment Services. For the EG_LLC payout flow, the optimal compliant route is: offshore EMI collects global client invoices in USD/EUR -> EMI wires to the LLC's Egyptian USD operational account via SWIFT MT103 using the purpose code 'P-2' or 'D-2' as remittance for services rendered. Direct EGP custody on an offshore EMI is prohibited and would expose the LLC to non-declared currency transaction penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for a non-resident shareholder's capital injection into EG_LLC: (i) the electing bank must receive the SWIFT MT103 from the shareholding entity's corporate account with a clear remitter name matching the shareholder register; (ii) the message must carry the statutory purpose code 'Equity Capital Contribution' (CBE internal code EB-01) or 'Shareholder Loan' (EB-05); (iii) the company must upload to the bank's electronic portal the CBE 'Inward Remittance Application' (Form A), a copy of the Commercial Registry extract, and a board resolution confirming the injection; (iv) the bank will perform a cross-match of the CBE Central Bank registry to verify the remitter is not on an anti-money-laundering sanction list. If the contribution exceeds USD 100,000, the bank may request the original notarized board minutes and a certificate of capital contribution from the company's auditor. Funds are released to the EGP capital account or USD convertible account within 48 hours of documentary approval.Outbound settlement for trade payables related to production, subcontracting, and licensing will primarily execute via SWIFT MT103 for B2B supplier invoices under a purchase order designated with the Egyptian Customs' import code '579' (software and AI-assisted media content). For disbursements exceeding USD 50,000, the CBE-compliant alternative is a Domestic Commercial Letter of Credit at sight, issued in favor of the non-resident supplier, requiring (i) the commercial invoice, (ii) a bill of lading or delivery certificate, and (iii) the form 'Import Requisition' from the Ministry of Trade. All transfer cables must include the ultimate beneficial owner of the beneficiary to ensure downstream correspondent banks do not reject the instruction. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW. Egypt enforces CBE Law No. 18/2019 on Payment Services and E-Commerce, requiring any entity that issues e-money, executes payment account acquisition, or facilitates cross-border settlement to be a CBE-licensed payment institution with a local branch. Offshore EMI digital wallets (e.g., Wise, Stripe operating as EMI, Revolut) cannot hold an Egyptian correspondent account or directly clear EGP; they may only be engaged as a front-end aggregation layer through CBE-licensed local payment gateways such as PaySky, Fawry, or Amazon Payment Services. For the EG_LLC payout flow, the optimal compliant route is: offshore EMI collects global client invoices in USD/EUR -> EMI wires to the LLC's Egyptian USD operational account via SWIFT MT103 using the purpose code 'P-2' or 'D-2' as remittance for services rendered. Direct EGP custody on an offshore EMI is prohibited and would expose the LLC to non-declared currency transaction penalties.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard Egyptian corporate income tax rate for juridical persons (Law No. 91 of 2005, Art. 8, as amended by Law No. 96 of 2015); branches of foreign companies are taxed at the same 22.5% on Egypt-source profits attributable to a permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends and other profit distributions to non-resident juridical persons without a permanent establishment in Egypt (Law No. 91 of 2005, Art. 56); resident Egyptian corporate shareholders are generally exempt from dividend WHT where the distributing company has fully settled its CIT; a non-resident individual shareholder is also subject to the 10% final dividend tax.
