Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Limited acceptance. Egyptian law requires every LLC to maintain a genuine, physical registered office within the jurisdiction where the statutory ledger, company seal, board minutes, and the notarized Memorandum of Association are kept and may be inspected by the Commercial Registry, GAFI, the Egyptian Tax Authority, and the Social Insurance Authority. For the vertical cloud ERP/CRM software business segment, GAFI practice accepts a qualifying virtual-office arrangement provided that: (a) the virtual office promoter holds a physical lease of at least 8 square meters of dedicated commercial space in an office-rated building; (b) a resident office manager maintains custody of the statutory records and receives service of legal process; (c) the company name is displayed at the entrance panel; and (d) no e-commerce-only designation is used that would exclude the physical address from the commercial registry. A pure desk-service with no resident agent and no dedicated address will be refused by the Commercial Registry and will trigger automatic deferral of the incorporation file.

Governing statute

An LLC may not offer its quotas to the public.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian LLC; the founders freely fix the capital which must be fully subscribed at formation, divided into equal quotas with a nominal value of not less than EGP 100 each (Art. 103, Law 159/1981). Cash subscriptions must be deposited in a bank account of the company-under-formation and evidenced by a bank certificate; in-kind contributions require a certified valuation by a registered accountant. No escrow-hold or freeze requirement applies to software service activities (ISIC 6201); capital may be used immediately after registration for operational expenses.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) for the leased office floor, confirming egress routes and extinguishing systems; structural load inspection by a licensed civil engineer for server and equipment rooms; electrical installation inspection certificate; occupational safety and health clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 (Arts. 221-228) covering ventilation, emergency lighting, and first aid; third-party liability and fire insurance endorsed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian LLC; the founders freely fix the capital which must be fully subscribed at formation, divided into equal quotas with a nominal value of not less than EGP 100 each (Art. 103, Law 159/1981). Cash subscriptions must be deposited in a bank account of the company-under-formation and evidenced by a bank certificate; in-kind contributions require a certified valuation by a registered accountant. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official e-portal: www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing integration via the ETA Raman platform (invoicing.eta.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

The foreign parent must (1) incorporate the Egyptian Limited Liability Company through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) one-stop-shop and obtain the Commercial Register certificate; (2) file the tax registration application with the ETA within 30 days of incorporation, appointing a locally resident tax representative; (3) obtain the Unified Tax Card bearing the 14-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax file number; (4) register for VAT in the same tax file when taxable supplies meet or are expected to meet the EGP 500,000 annual threshold or opt for voluntary VAT registration to recover input VAT; and (5) enroll in the ETA e-invoicing system to obtain the digital certificate, API credentials, and billing entity ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the LLC is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at 22.5% under Law No. 91 of 2005; no fiscal transparency or pass-through status exists; dividends to non-resident shareholders attract 10% domestic withholding tax, subject to treaty relief; Suez Canal Economic Zone and Free Zone entities may elect special regimes.

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch flow-through election; the LLC is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections: (a) Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 tax incentive election allowing an annual deduction of 30% to 50% of invested capital for up to 7 years for certified software/IT projects; (b) Special Economic Zone election under the SCZone authority providing a simplified 0% customs and reduced-rate corporate income tax regime; (c) Free Zone election replacing standard CIT and VAT with a flat annual 1% levy on proceeds for qualifying goods activities, generally unsuitable for domestic cloud services; (d) voluntary VAT registration election enabling input VAT recovery and export zero-rating claims.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover in goods and services per calendar year; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, and registration is mandatory for any foreign entity whose Egyptian taxable supplies exceed this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for export of goods and certain international services. For the ISIC 6201 vertical cloud software/CRM service, the exporter must demonstrate: (a) the recipient is a non-resident customer located outside Egypt; (b) the place of supply and beneficial use of the software occurs wholly outside Egypt, applying the 'use and enjoyment' test; (c) consideration is received in foreign currency through a licensed Egyptian bank; and (d) no part of the service is consumed in Egypt or contributes to Egyptian-source income. The export sale invoice must carry the ETA-cleared UUID/QR code, and the related input VAT may be claimed as a refund or offset after documentary verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (automated NAFEZA single-window clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement (where applicable)
  • Commercial invoice issued at 0% VAT referencing the ETA e-invoice UUID and QR code
  • Signed service contract with the non-resident customer defining destination and use
  • Foreign currency inward remittance certificate issued by the Egyptian bank
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice XML/JSON export file from the Raman platform
  • Evidence of corporate tax file registration and valid Unified Tax Card

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts under Prime Ministerial Decree No. 265 of 2020; every registered taxpayer above the phased turnover requirements must issue invoices through certified billing software integrated via API with the ETA Raman platform in XML/JSON format; each invoice is cleared in near-real time and receives a unique UUID and machine-readable QR code before legal validity; the digital stamping requirement applies to export invoices as well as domestic sales; non-clearance renders the invoice inadmissible for input VAT deduction and export refund purposes.

The ETA may audit export zero-rating claims within the 5-year statutory prescription period and retrospectively claw back the unapplied 14% VAT on the export value together with delay interest of 1% per month where the documentary bundle is incomplete, the foreign-currency remittance is not timely repatriated, or the service is found to be consumed in Egypt. Forgery, false declarations, or fictitious export documents expose the taxpayer to criminal prosecution under Articles 34 and 80 of Law No. 67 of 2016 with imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000, in addition to disgorgement of all refunded amounts, administrative penalties, and inclusion on the ETA high-risk compliance register, which suspends e-invoice clearance and access to export incentive facilities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is largely convertible for current account transactions, including trade settlements and profit remittances, subject to submission of supporting documentation to an authorized bank. Capital account transactions remain subject to CBE approval or post-transaction verification, with foreign direct investment repatriation generally permitted through authorized channels upon presentation of audited financial statements, tax clearance, and bank attestations. Profit remittances of Egyptian LLCs to non-resident shareholders are permitted via local banks against certified financials, annual audited accounts, and tax compliance certificates. No separate repatriation or profit remittance license is required for standard distribution, but banks apply strict beneficial ownership and source-of-funds checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to an Egyptian licensed bank within 90 days from the date of shipment under CBE regulations. There is no mandatory percentage conversion into EGP currently; exporters retain the right to hold foreign currency proceeds in retention accounts. However, banks are obligated to ensure receipt of proceeds within the statutory window, and any extension must be approved by the CBE. Failure to comply may attract administrative penalties or suspension of import privileges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI cross-border viability is Low. Foreign or non-bank digital EMI suppliers are not permitted to open EGP current accounts or receive deposits on behalf of Egyptian entities without a local payment-service license from the CBE. Licensed local fintech players (e.g., Paymob, Fawry) may act as payment gateways and collection agents but cannot substitute for a full corporate banking relationship for trade settlement or capital remittance. An Egyptian LLC must maintain a local corporate account with a CBE-qualified bank for statutory capital, trade, and tax obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign capital injections must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's overseas bank to the LLC's Capital Escrow Account at a CBE-licensed bank. The wire must reference the correct purpose code and a description such as 'INVESTMENT CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION.' The receiving bank will verify the source of funds, the underlying investment committee resolution, and the LLC's capital documents. The bank posts the EGP equivalent or retains the foreign currency, whichever was applied for at the time of conversion, and issues a bank currency conversion slip if conversion is required. Post-transfer compliance includes submitting a capital contribution settlement letter, an EGP foreign currency account statement, and registration of foreign investment through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), if applicable.Operating outbound disbursements for trade settlements and SaaS-related vendor payments use standard SWIFT MT103 payment instructions via the LLC's operational bank. For larger procurement of licences, hardware infrastructure or cross-border data services, the bank may require a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) where the beneficiary is a foreign supplier requesting guaranteed payment; in such cases the operative rail is a documentary sight LC issued on the LLC's bank through a corresponding international bank. Direct debit is permissible only for domestic service billing (telecom, utilities and software charges), and for cross-border recurring payments the LLC should establish ACH or SEPA rails from its foreign subsidiary, if any, or utilise a licensed local payment aggregator only for collection from Egyptian clients. All outbound wires must be mapped to the appropriate SIMAD customs code or CBE payment code, and invoices must be retained for tax audit.Egyptian banks may provide spot and forward (up to 12 months) foreign exchange contracts for genuine trade and capital accounts having sufficient underlying exposure; such forwards require a clear commercial transaction or service compulsion.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not generally permitted by Egyptian licensed banks for hedging EGP, as onshore markets disallow offshore NDF settlement; the CBE prefers outright deliverable forwards for EGP exposures.Currency swaps are permissible only when used to offset a matched cross-border trade flow, with bank approval and strict underlying documentation.Reverse repo structures are not permitted for hedge purposes; simple FX swaps between EGP and USD/EUR are only licensed after documenting the economic need and maintaining active trade evidence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% domestic withholding tax on dividends and profit distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident, unless a lower treaty rate applies or the recipient is subject to a participation-exemption or Free Zone licence privilege. The distribution of distributable reserves, capital-reduction distributions, and liquidation surpluses may be characterised as dividends and taxed at 10% to the extent of accumulated profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions for preparing and submitting the Egyptian Local File; EGP 50,000,000 group-related turnover for the consolidated Egyptian Master File; a transfer-pricing disclosure schedule is required for related-party transactions exceeding EGP 1,000,000, and non-resident cost-sharing or intercompany service charges must be supported by economic analysis and CUP benchmarking records.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not administer a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, the Egyptian Tax Authority applies general anti-avoidance rules under Law 91/2005 and Tax Procedures Law 188/2020 that disregard transactions lacking commercial purpose. Free Zone companies must retain real physical presence, employees and activity to preserve their CIT exemption. Controlled-foreign-company rules and the permanent-establishment allocation rules operate as functional economic-substance requirements for software companies entering and exiting Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Law 91/2005 as amended carries imprisonment for not less than one year and up to five years, a fine equal to 100%–200% of the evaded tax, and a further civil tax assessment at double the unpaid amount. Understating revenue by more than 30% is treated as prima facie fraud and disables the taxpayer from utilising a clearance certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and authorised signatories who execute or assist in the evasion scheme are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and the fraud fine. Egyptian courts may pierce the LLC veil when corporate funds are used for personal purposes or the company is operated as a facade; civil asset attachment, travel bans, and confiscation of personal property are available against culpable directors.
Artificial Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The ETA has authority to disregard the interposed entity and reallocate taxable income to the real owner under the anti-avoidance rules; the purported free-zone exemption is revoked retroactively, WHT of 10% is imposed on all repatriated profits, and a penalty of up to 100% of the uncovered tax may apply. Criminal penalties for sham invoicing and false declarations may be imposed under the Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and legal representatives can be held personally liable for unpaid taxes when the company is a pure conduit and corporate assets were intermingled with the principal's assets. Travel bans, freezing orders, and disqualification from serving on Egyptian companies are routinely issued; imprisonment is possible for knowingly executing sham transactions.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Non-reporting and wilful AML breaches under Law No. 80 of 2002, as amended, are punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and a fine ranging from EGP 50,000 to EGP 500,000; repeat activity can result in confiscation of funds equal to the laundered value and revocation of the software vendor's permit to operate an Egyptian payment collection arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knew or should have known of the unlawful origin of funds are personally liable for fines and damages and may face criminal imprisonment. The EMLCU and public prosecutor may impose personal asset freezes and travel bans, and Egyptian AML law applies extraterritorially to Egyptian-managed cloud platforms that process the proceeds.
Misappropriation of Withholding Taxes

Statutory sanction: Withheld taxes are state money and non-remittance is treated as financial misappropriation. Law 91/2005 imposes a penalty of the higher of 100% of the withheld amount and the applicable monthly delay surcharge, plus criminal fines; persistent failure is punishable by imprisonment of not less than six months and not more than three years.

Director personal liability: The responsible director, tax manager, and board members may be made jointly and severally liable for the withheld amounts even after the LLC is liquidated; judges may issue stop-travel orders and asset attachment directly against the individual signatories. The corporate veil is pierced as a matter of statutory policy for collected-but-unremitted taxes.
