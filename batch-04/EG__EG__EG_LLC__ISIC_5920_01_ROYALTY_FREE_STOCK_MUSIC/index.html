Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — Central Department of Taxpayer Registration; official portal: www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing/fiscal clearance portal: inov.eta.gov.eg; Suez Canal Economic Zone tax units operate under ETA supervision.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) complete company incorporation and commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or competent commercial registry; (2) file Tax Card (TIN) application with ETA Taxpayer Registration Department presenting the incorporation certificate, authenticated Memorandum/Articles of Association (foreign consular attestation required if executed abroad), lease deed or address proof, legal entity data form, notarized board resolutions, and passport copies/POA of foreign managers; (3) obtain the unified Tax Card/TIN; (4) register for VAT within 30 days after taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; (5) enroll in the ETA e-invoicing portal and create a machine-readable API credential for authorized signers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An Egyptian LLC is a resident tax entity subject to corporate income tax (CIT) at 22.5% on worldwide income (Law 91/2005 as amended); no pass-through treatment for members; foreign shareholders' distributions are subject to dividend withholding tax at 10% unless reduced by treaty; profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment may be exempt under treaty conditions. — Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) complete company incorporation and commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or competent commercial registry; (2) file Tax Card (TIN) application with ETA Taxpayer Registration Department presenting the incorporation certificate, authenticated Memorandum/Articles of Association (foreign consular attestation required if executed abroad), lease deed or address proof, legal entity data form, notarized board resolutions, and passport copies/POA of foreign managers; (3) obtain the unified Tax Card/TIN; (4) register for VAT within 30 days after taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; (5) enroll in the ETA e-invoicing portal and create a machine-readable API credential for authorized signers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — An Egyptian LLC is a resident tax entity subject to corporate income tax (CIT) at 22.5% on worldwide income (Law 91/2005 as amended); no pass-through treatment for members; foreign shareholders' distributions are subject to dividend withholding tax at 10% unless reduced by treaty; profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment may be exempt under treaty conditions.

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' regime exists. Available elections: (a) voluntary VAT registration before reaching the EGP 500,000 threshold; (b) election for annual VAT filing with monthly prepayments; (c) election to apply reduced treaty withholding rates on royalties/licensing fees remitted to non-residents (domestic WHT on royalties is 20%; treaty rates generally 0–15% depending on jurisdiction); (d) election for simplified transfer-pricing documentation (summary file) where related-party transactions are below EGP 8 million per counterparty per fiscal year; (e) election to consolidate multiple foreign-source service contracts under a single exported-services zero-rating file.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies of goods and services; mandatory registration within 30 days of crossing the threshold; non-resident suppliers supplying digital services to final Egyptian consumers are required to register via a fiscal representative or direct ETA enrollment regardless of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 31 of VAT Law 67/2016 zero-rates exported goods and services. For royalty-free stock music licensing by an Egyptian LLC: (a) the LLC must be VAT-registered; (b) the license must be granted to a non-resident licensee or digital marketplace acting for non-resident end-users; (c) the economic benefit and consumers of the licensed music must be located outside Egypt; (d) the license fee must be arm's length and received in convertible foreign currency; (e) the export must be evidenced by licensing contracts, distribution platform statements, and bank inward remittance records. Electronic services delivered to non-residents are treated as exported services eligible for 0% VAT.

  • Customs export declaration (goods) or ETA electronic service-export notification (intangible/digital services)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / digital delivery log with foreign IP-address usage records for online music delivery
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from an Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Licensing agreement or sublicense agreement with the non-resident licensee/platform
  • VAT invoice bearing the ETA e-invoice QR code and zero-rated export code
  • Non-resident status confirmation, economic-destination declaration, and beneficial owner self-certificate

Mandatory fiscal clearance in real time: all VAT-registered taxpayers must integrate billing/ERP systems with the ETA e-invoicing platform using REST API (JSON), transmit invoice XML/JSON for pre-clearance, obtain a 2048-bit RSA digital signature and QR code containing the Unique Invoice Identifier; e-receipts are mandatory for B2C retail. Invoices without ETA clearance are legally invalid, non-deductible, and subject to penalties.

ETA audits export zero-rating claims against CBE bank records, platform payout data, and licensee due-diligence files. Failure to remit export proceeds to Egypt within 180 days triggers retrospective VAT clawback at 14% on the full invoice value plus late-remittance late-payment interest of 2% per month under VAT Law Articles 66 and 68. Fraudulent zero-rating or false documentation constitutes tax evasion punishable by a fine of 25%–100% of the evaded tax, imprisonment for not less than six months, suspension of the Tax Card and e-invoicing clearance credentials, disgorgement of VAT refunds, and joint liability of directors/managers under Article 72.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a de facto freely floating EGP regime following the CBE full liberalization of 6 March 2024. Current-account transactions — royalties, licensing fees, sound-recording exports, and service invoices — are fully convertible for bona fide business purposes when supported by commercial contracts, VAT invoices, and the accepting bank's Balance of Payments declaration. Capital-account transactions, principally foreign equity injections into the Egyptian LLC, are convertible but administratively controlled: the inward remittance must be cleared through an authorized bank, credited to an investment account, and registered in the GAFI investment file in order to perfect the legal right to future profit and liquidation proceeds repatriation. Practical profit remittance hurdles are documentary rather than prohibitive: the bank requires audited financial statements, ETA final corporate income tax clearance, proof that applicable dividend withholding or treaty relief has been settled, and CBE/GAFI remittance filings; during episodes of FX liquidity stress, the conversion queue can temporarily delay EGP funding and offshore payouts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE exchange control instructions, and as amended after the 6 March 2024 float, Egyptian exporters — including the LLC benefiting from digital audio licensing revenue: (i) must repatriate the full export proceeds into an Egyptian authorized bank within 90 days from the shipment date, service completion date, or the contractual billing milestone; (ii) may request bank-approved extension for deferred-payment commercial agreements; and (iii) are subject to no mandatory conversion ratio under the current regime — proceeds may be retained in USD/EUR accounts and converted voluntarily at interbank rates. The receiving bank must record the service-export declaration and evidence of underlying license receipts in the CBE reporting system; overdue unrepatriated amounts are flagged as exchange-control breaches and can trigger suspension of an exporter's banking facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the exclusive monetary and foreign exchange authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Banking and Banking System Law), acting jointly with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for capital account entry/exit and repatriation registration, and with the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) for remittance tax clearance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the foreign shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 with field 70 identifying 'Foreign Direct Investment — equity subscription in an Egyptian LLC', destined for the CBE-registered capital account at the Egyptian bank; the recipient bank obtains the GAFI project file and the signed constitutional documentation, credits the USD/EUR capital account or converts into EGP at the interbank rate, issues an investment deposit certificate, and records the investment in the CBE/GAFI system. Foreign-currency equity injection is the standard route for creating the registered foreign investment position; an EGP-denominated injection from abroad is not recognized for GAFI repatriation rights. The LLC must notify the bank of any beneficial-ownership change and maintain the investment file with GAFI's investor support centre.Outbound clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 transfers for recurring royalty, distributor, and vendor payments, each supported by ETA tax clearance, VAT invoices, and CBE foreign-payment declarations; (ii) commercial letters of credit and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for physical-media distribution, master-recording acquisitions, and equipment purchases requiring trade finance; (iii) Egyptian interbank EGP settlements through the national RTGS system for payroll and local operating expenses; and (iv) dividend and profit remittances through the bank's remittance desk after GAFI and ETA clearance, with treaty-relief documentation when applicable. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian rules require arm’s-length pricing and transfer-pricing documentation for controlled transactions. The commonly applied local-file materiality threshold is EGP 8 million of aggregate related-party transactions in a tax period; a master file is generally relevant where the multinational group’s consolidated revenue reaches EGP 3 billion. These thresholds and filing obligations should be verified against current ETA rules and guidance for the relevant tax year. Country-by-country reporting may apply separately to qualifying multinational groups, including the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold and reporting-entity rules.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have a general-purpose Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to that of certain offshore jurisdictions. Egyptian tax, company, beneficial-ownership, anti-avoidance, and treaty rules nevertheless require genuine transactions, arm’s-length terms, accurate accounting, and support for the commercial purpose and performance of cross-border services and licensing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion or fraudulent return

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax and additional tax, administrative fines, and criminal prosecution under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 206 of 2020. Criminal fines and imprisonment depend on the offense, intent, amount, and applicable statutory provisions; obtain case-specific advice before quantifying exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal criminal liability where they participate in, authorize, or knowingly facilitate an offense. Corporate status does not shield an individual from liability for personal conduct. Civil recovery is generally against the taxpayer, subject to statutory rules governing responsible persons and enforcement.
Failure to withhold or remit tax

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian payer may be assessed the unwithheld tax, additional tax, and applicable fines; intentional conduct may trigger criminal consequences. Treaty relief does not excuse withholding where eligibility or documentation requirements are unmet.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may face personal exposure for knowing or intentional participation and under applicable enforcement provisions. No automatic director liability or travel ban should be assumed without a specific statutory basis or court or enforcement order.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA may adjust taxable profits and assess additional tax, interest or additional tax, and applicable penalties. Documentation failures can increase audit and penalty exposure; the amount depends on the applicable provision and facts.

Director personal liability: The company is ordinarily the primary taxpayer. Directors or officers may face individual liability where they knowingly participate in fraud, false declarations, or another offense; a transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Unsubstantiated royalty-free or below-market related-party licensing

Statutory sanction: Possible denial or adjustment of deductions, taxable-profit reallocation, withholding-tax reassessment, and penalties for inaccurate returns or transfer-pricing non-compliance. Copyright infringement may also create separate civil or criminal exposure under applicable intellectual-property law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for knowing infringement, fraud, or other personal misconduct. Ordinary tax adjustments or company copyright liability do not, without more, automatically create personal liability.
Shell-company, beneficial-ownership, or AML misconduct

Statutory sanction: Potential account restrictions, tax and regulatory investigations, asset restraint or confiscation, administrative sanctions, and criminal prosecution under applicable anti-money-laundering and company laws. Penalties depend on the conduct and governing statute.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate may face personal criminal and civil consequences. Travel restrictions or imprisonment require an applicable legal basis and competent authority action; they are not automatic consequences of a tax filing failure.
