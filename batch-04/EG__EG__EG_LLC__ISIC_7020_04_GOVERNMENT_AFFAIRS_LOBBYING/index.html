Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an LLC under Law 159 of 1981 as amended by Investment Law 72 of 2017. No escrow account is mandatory for this service activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; electronic filing portal at www.eta.gov.eg and the unified e-services portal (e-tax) for TIN registration and VAT filings

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC obtains a Tax Identification Number (TIN) by registering with the Egyptian Tax Authority after incorporation with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Commercial Registry. Required filings include the form of incorporation, notarised memorandum of association, Commercial Registry extract, power of attorney for the legal representative, proof of registered office, national ID/passport copies, and sector license where applicable. The ETA opens a tax file for corporate income tax, VAT, payroll tax, and withholding; a separate VAT registration certificate is issued automatically or upon application once taxable turnover exceeds the registration threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC obtains a Tax Identification Number (TIN) by registering with the Egyptian Tax Authority after incorporation with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Commercial Registry. Required filings include the form of incorporation, notarised memorandum of association, Commercial Registry extract, power of attorney for the legal representative, proof of registered office, national ID/passport copies, and sector license where applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists under Egyptian law. An LLC is mandatorily taxed at entity level; the taxpayer may elect the accounting period or tax year and may apply for tax grouping of related entities under conditions set out in the Egyptian Income Tax Law, subject to ETA approval. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Egypt follows a fixed entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services; registration is mandatory from first EGP above the threshold, with voluntary registration permitted below threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods and services exported outside Egypt are zero-rated for VAT where legal ownership or economic enjoyment accrues outside Egypt, or services are directly supplied to non-residents for use abroad. Zero-rating must be substantiated by evidence that the goods were physically shipped and exported within the statutory period, or for services, that the recipient is a foreign person and the service was performed or exploited overseas. Relocation of goods to free zones or economic zones may qualify as exports where handled through Egyptian customs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) duly stamped and registered in the exporter's name
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical international carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming foreign currency receipt from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-matched with customs entry
  • Proof of foreign transfer to the exporter's Egyptian bank account within the repatriation deadline

Egypt has a mandatory e-invoicing system operated by the ETA. Registered taxpayers must integrate billing systems with the ETA via licensed APIs using the Arabic/English unified e-invoice schema, transmit each invoice in real time for validation and clearance, and receive a QR-coded digital stamp on approved electronic invoices. Phased implementation applies to all B2B transactions, with e-receipt mandates being progressively applied to B2C retail transactions.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary examination and post-clearance audit. False or unjustified zero-rating results in reassessment at the 14% VAT rate, denial or clawback of associated input tax credits, late payment penalties of up to 1.5% per month, and administrative fines ranging from 20% to 100% of tax due for under-declaration. Criminal sanctions including imprisonment and director liability may be imposed where fraudulent export declarations are proven. ETA may retrospectively revoke the VAT refund and impose additional penalties for any foreign currency not repatriated within 180 days.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-to-flexible floating exchange rate regime consolidated since March 2024 with a unified interbank rate. Current account transactions are broadly liberalized; remittance of dividends, branch profits, and service fees is permitted through authorized dealers subject to CBE-formulated documentation, clearance of tax obligations from the Egyptian Tax Authority, and anti-money-laundering screening. The capital account remains partially restricted; inbound portfolio and direct investments require registration with the CBE (declaration forms for funding/foreign investment) to secure future repatriation rights. Profit expatriation is statutorily permissible but de facto requires audited financial statements, proof of tax settlement, and a bank-formatted remittance request evidencing underlying retained earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export receipts to the CBE is currently mandated; exporters and service suppliers may retain 100% of foreign currency proceeds in onshore foreign currency accounts. Repatriation of legally due export/service consideration must occur through approved banking channels within reasonable settlement periods per the underlying commercial contract; failure to repatriate within the periods conventionally applied by the CBE (generally 120-180 days for trade receivables unless extended) triggers compliance review and potential denial of subsequent FX facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – Al-Bank Al-Markazi Al-Masri

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for direct operations; offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not recognized as authorized dealers under Egyptian Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and cannot host Egyptian domestic current accounts. Cross-border EMIs may be used only as supplementary receive-only conduits for overseas client wires, and remittance obligations to Egyptian beneficiaries must still clear through a CBE-licensed bank. Legal constraint: routing substantive retainer collections or payroll through an unlicensed EMI constitutes unlicensed money transmission and triggers immediate CBE and AML enforcement; the local LLC must maintain collection accounts at a CBE-licensed authorized dealer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation arises when annual related-party transactions exceed EGP 20,000,000; the Master File is expected where the multinational group’s consolidated annual revenue exceeds EGP 200,000,000. The Transfer Pricing Disclosure Form (Form No. 15) is compulsory once related-party transactions exceed EGP 10,000,000. Thresholds are subject to periodic ETA guidance and should be verified at the filing date.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations code in the style of Gulf jurisdictions. Nevertheless, eligibility for free-zone and investment-zone incentives requires genuine business substance in Egypt: a physical office, locally engaged officers, board management in Egypt, and actual performance of lobbying and consultancy services. The Egyptian general anti-abuse rule permits the tax authority to disregard arrangements whose dominant purpose is tax avoidance and to recharacterise income based on economic reality. Shell entities with passive income booked into zero or lower-tax zones are vulnerable to PE taxation, reclassification, and loss of incentive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine equal to two to three times the tax evaded; additionally the ETA may impose a surcharge equal to the evaded tax amount, close the premises for a fixed period, and require full restitution before the criminal case is dismissed.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers and signatories who prepare, sign or approve false returns may be held personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, fines and surcharges. The criminal court may impose a travel ban, freeze personal assets, and order imprisonment where fraudulent intent is established.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the mispriced transaction, apply the arm’s length principle, and adjust taxable income. The adjustment can trigger an additional tax liability of up to 40-50% of the under-declared amount, plus a monthly 2% late payment surcharge. Non-filing of mandatory TP documentation may remove the taxpayer’s safe harbour and reverse the burden of proof.

Director personal liability: Board members who knowingly approved artificial transfer prices may be jointly liable for the additional tax and penalties. Evidence that a director or compliance officer concealed the TP file may support criminal liability and personal financial recovery from the responsible individuals.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may reclassify the entity as non-existent for tax purposes, treat the profits as accruing to the true beneficial owner, and revoke free-zone or investment-zone benefits. Under the Investment Law 72/2017, GAFI may cancel the license and impose administrative fines; under the tax law, the conduit entity and its beneficial owner are jointly and severally assessed.

Director personal liability: The de facto manager and foreign parent directors exposing personal assets to confiscation, joint tax assessment, and a travel ban for failure to demonstrate the Egyptian CEO’s actual business presence and authority over lobbying contracts; criminal filing evasion may also attach to the most senior Egyptian officer.
AML / Hidden Profit Distribution and Capital Stripping

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the payment as a taxable dividend subject to 10% withholding tax plus penalties; anti-money laundering fines under Egyptian AML Law 80/2002 may reach EGP 5,000,000; imprisonment of up to 7 years for intentional money-laundering connected to politically exposed persons or suspicious government-related transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors, bank compliance officers and legal representatives face personal criminal exposure for failing to file suspicious transaction reports, knowingly executing hidden distributions, or participating in capital exit transactions without tax clearance. The court may issue travel bans, attachment orders, and seize personal assets to satisfy monetary sanctions.
