Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; unified e-services portal at www.e-ta.gov.eg (also accessible via www.eta.gov.eg), including income tax, VAT, e-invoicing, and e-submission modules; customs counterpart is the Egyptian Customs Authority (ECA) under the Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign or non-resident entity forming or participating in an Egyptian LLC must procure the Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card (Al-Karta Al-Darebia) by: (1) filing ETA Form 1 (registration request) with the competent Tax Directorate at the place of the LLC's head office or the non-resident's agent's office; (2) submitting attested and Arabic-translated constitutional documents (MOA/AOA), valid commercial registration certificate, board resolution appointing the tax representative, power of attorney, copy of the legal representative's ID, and evidence of Egyptian source activity or permanent establishment; (3) obtaining VAT registration under the same file if the entity makes taxable supplies above or in expectation of exceeding the EGP 500,000 threshold; (4) receiving the tax card and unique TIN within approximately 30 days for income tax and 30 to 60 days for VAT; (5) mandatory enrollment in the ETA e-Tax, e-invoicing, and e-receipt systems. A foreign PE not having a local entity may register directly with the ETA's Non-Resident Registration Unit and appoint a fiscal agent.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (not pass-through). The Egyptian LLC is an independent juridical person and a fully taxable resident taxpayer under Law No. 91 of 2005 (Income Tax Law as amended by Law No. 14 of 2023). The LLC is taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; losses do not flow through to shareholders; shareholder distributions are subject to a 10% dividend withholding tax. Small and micro enterprises may elect turnover-based final taxation under Law No. 152 of 2020, while large taxpayers with taxable income exceeding EGP 100 million are subject to a 25% rate, and other corporate taxable income below that threshold generally to 22.5%; a 24% rate applies to certain small business income brackets. — A foreign or non-resident entity forming or participating in an Egyptian LLC must procure the Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card (Al-Karta Al-Darebia) by: (1) filing ETA Form 1 (registration request) with the competent Tax Directorate at the place of the LLC's head office or the non-resident's agent's office; (2) submitting attested and Arabic-translated constitutional documents (MOA/AOA), valid commercial registration certificate, board resolution appointing the tax representative, power of attorney, copy of the legal representative's ID, and evidence of Egyptian source activity or permanent establishment; (3) obtaining VAT registration under the same file if the entity makes taxable supplies above or in expectation of exceeding the EGP 500,000 threshold; (4) receiving the tax card and unique TIN within approximately 30 days for income tax and 30 to 60 days for VAT; (5) mandatory enrollment in the ETA e-Tax, e-invoicing, and e-receipt systems. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (not pass-through). The Egyptian LLC is an independent juridical person and a fully taxable resident taxpayer under Law No. 91 of 2005 (Income Tax Law as amended by Law No. 14 of 2023). The LLC is taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; losses do not flow through to shareholders; shareholder distributions are subject to a 10% dividend withholding tax. Small and micro enterprises may elect turnover-based final taxation under Law No. 152 of 2020, while large taxpayers with taxable income exceeding EGP 100 million are subject to a 25% rate, and other corporate taxable income below that threshold generally to 22.5%; a 24% rate applies to certain small business income brackets.

Available election forms

Egypt does not enact an IRC Section 7701 check-the-box or equivalent single-entity pass-through election. Available statutory elections and regimes relevant to a PE buyout SPV include: (1) election for final presumptive turnover-based tax under Law No. 152 of 2020 for qualifying small and micro enterprises (rates from 0.5% to 1.5% of turnover instead of net-basis CIT); (2) election for the 5% capital gains withholding rate on disposal of shares by non-residents in certain cases rather than net-basis assessment; (3) election by a non-resident with a permanent establishment to file a net-basis Egyptian return instead of being assessed on a deemed 15% profit margin; (4) free-zone entity regime election under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 for a delisted investment entity, subject to prior ETA approval of the free-zone certificate; (5) election to claim foreign tax credit by completing Form 25 rather than taking a deduction for foreign taxes. Egypt applies no domestic hybrid mismatch election and has not adopted full OECD BEPS Action 2 rules into domestic law; treaty residence and beneficial ownership determinations operate under the standard double taxation treaty framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is triggered when the annual value of taxable supplies (goods and services supplied in Egypt) exceeds EGP 500,000; a separate registration applies to imports as an importer of record irrespective of value; persons making only zero-rated exports are still required to register to claim input VAT recovery; registration application must be submitted within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; the threshold is an economic turnover test applied per entity and per separate place of business, not aggregated for related parties unless the ETA deems the arrangement artificial.

Egypt operates a mandatory e-invoicing regime with centralized fiscal clearance through the ETA. All VAT-registered taxpayers and income-tax-registered legal persons, including LLC buyout SPVs issuing any invoice, debit note, credit note, or receipt, must transmit XML/JSON invoices to the ETA e-invoicing platform in real time via API integration (synchronous or asynchronous messages), cryptographically sign each document with an Egyptian Trust digital certificate, and print a machine-readable QR (Khalid/ECC code) on the visualized invoice. Batch submission via file upload is allowed only for asynchronous flows; the ETA validates the invoice, issues an acceptance receipt with a unique internal ID, and checks correctness against declared VAT codes, tax cards, registration status, and transaction category. E-invoices in foreign currency for an export sale are accepted with the FX amount and the EGP equivalent rate. B2C cash transactions are covered by the e-receipt system enforced since July 2023. Failure to integrate or issuance of a non-cleared document attracts penalties of EGP 20,000 per violating invoice and suspension of the taxpayer's e-services until rectification; the ETA may re-characterize the whole transaction and deny input VAT recovery for undeclared invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-floating exchange rate regime with partial current-account convertibility and capital-account restrictions. Current-account transactions, including profit remittances, are generally permissible but remain subject to CBE approval or registration requirements, documentary support, and the availability of foreign currency liquidity in the banking system. Capital-account transactions, such as equity injections, must be registered and routed through licensed Egyptian banks. Profit remittance hurdles include a five-business-day clearing cycle, anti-money-laundering controls, and the ability of the receiving bank to source hard currency; no blanket restriction applies, but practical delays and periodic FX stress events may arise. For private-equity SPVs, the right to repatriate invested capital and returns is recognised under Law 8/1997 (Investment Law), subject to CBE registration and Article 40 guarantee rules, which require that the original remittance be tracked and documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate export proceeds and credit them to an account at a licensed Egyptian bank within 30 days from the date of shipment (or such longer period as CBE permits for specific industries). With effect from the 2016/2017 FX liberalisation and successive CBE circulars, there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio to EGP; export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or sold into the interbank market. However, under CBE emergency powers and foreign-currency scarcity episodes, temporary surrender ratios and prioritised conversion orders can be reintroduced. The SPV must maintain a fully compliant export contract, customs invoice, and bank trust receipt to evidence repatriation timelines.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech/e-money institutions (EMIs) are not recognised as licensed banks by the CBE and cannot directly hold client settlement accounts or clear EGP. Cross-border and multi-currency treasury services from non-bank EMI providers are restricted; any use of a global EMI for transitional treasury flows would require a locally licensed correspondent or a registered payment-service provider in Egypt, and would face strict CBE prohibition on non-licensed deposit-taking. Direct EMI integration is only viable for auxiliary payments (e.g., expense reimbursements) provided the SPI's principal transactional and escrow accounts remain with a CBE-licensed commercial bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Complex offshore ownership structure: tiers of holding companies in multiple jurisdictions

Root cause: Complex offshore ownership structure: tiers of holding companies in multiple jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Full corporate chart from ultimate beneficial owner to SPV, notarised and apostilled incorporation certificates of each entity, consolidated beneficial-ownership declaration, an organisational chart showing percentages, and legal documentation for each holding entity. Additionally provide audited financial statements or capital call schedules that evidence commercial rationale.
Private-equity SPV with no active operational income

Root cause: Private-equity SPV with no active operational income

Defense evidence: Detailed investment mandate, SPV business plan, executed share-purchase agreement or term sheet, capital-call and subscription documents, and a source-of-wealth letter per beneficial owner. The bank will require evidence that the transaction is genuine, the SPV has substance in Egypt (registered address, board meetings, accounting records), and no round-tripping or layering pattern exists.
High-risk counterparties or jurisdictions in the transaction chain

Root cause: High-risk counterparties or jurisdictions in the transaction chain

Defense evidence: OECD/FAFT-compliant anti-money-laundering correspondence, sanctions screening results for all transacting parties, transaction flowcharts with purpose codes, and legal opinion on the economic substance of all counterparties. The SPV must also supply calendarised identification of ultimate beneficial owners to detect politically exposed persons (PEPs) and confirm none exercises control.
Unusually large or imminent wire transfers relative to declared capital

Root cause: Unusually large or imminent wire transfers relative to declared capital

Defense evidence: An executed capital call notice, signed loan or equity commitment agreement, valuation report of target company, notarised board resolutions approving the wire, and an incumbency certificate. The bank may require the Amwal (Egyptian FATF) and Ministry of Investment registration certificates showing the SPV's licence exemption and foreign-investment tracking codes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from foreign investors must be routed through a CBE-licensed bank in Egypt using the foreign-currency receipt code for direct investment (Code '500' or the CBE's designated purpose code for capital injections under the Unified Registration System). The remitting bank will transmit a SWIFT MT103 with a detailed purpose code and supporting documentation: a copy of the investment registration certificate under the Egyptian General Authority for Investment (GAFI), legal invoices or contribution notices, and a board resolution authorising the wire. The receiving bank will verify the commercial reality of the transfer, assign a special foreign-currency investment registration number, and credit the SPV's multilateral settlement account. Dividends, profit share, and exit proceeds are subsequently repatriable only upon presenting a completed internal revenue repatriation form, audited financial statements, and proof of tax settlement to the CBE-appointed authorised bank.Trade disbursements from the SPV to non-resident suppliers or target companies are executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages through the participant's Egyptian bank. Settlement for goods and services under confirmed purchase agreements requires compliant classification of the outflow under Egyptian trade regulations, including: a valid import-export number, customs invoices, an executed proforma invoice, and the designated Harmonized System/EEPC export codes. For syndicated loan wires or acquisition payments, the SPV may use an MT103 with mirroring TELEX through the instructing bank and the beneficiary bank's test-key validation. Where the underlying transaction is structured as a commercial letter of credit (LC), the issuing Egyptian bank will open an irrevocable at-sight or usance LC (form UCP600), with the SWIFT MT700 message sent to the beneficiary bank and settlement completed via MT103/MT202 upon presentation of compliant documents. All disbursements above USD 10,000 are subject to CBE AML screening and electronic reporting to the Egyptian Unified Anti-Money-Laundering System.Onshore EGP/USD forward contracts with CBE-licensed banks (with two-way pricing, commonly used for balance-sheet hedging of repatriation exposure).FX swaps between EGP and major hard currencies (USD, EUR, GBP) with authorised dealers, subject to CBE risk-mitigation and margin/collateral requirements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed offshore by licensed foreign dealer banks, permissible as economic hedges for SPV exposures to EGP volatility where the settlement is in USD; such NDFs are not offered onshore but do not violate Egyptian law for the SPV when booked through an international group treasury centre.Currency options and simple cross-currency swaps with Egyptian banks or foreign banks duly licensed in Egypt, provided the SPV can evidence an underlying exposure and obtains CBE conformity for the derivative.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 aggregate value of related-party transactions for the taxpayer (Local File); Master File mandatory when the multinational group’s consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 200,000,000 and the local related-party transactions exceed the EGP 8,000,000 threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime comparable to GCC states; however the ETA applies a general anti-avoidance doctrine and requires a performing entity with local effective management, personnel, board decisions, financial books, and commercial reasons. A passive leveraged buyout SPV lacking substance in Egypt remains exposed to judicial recharacterization, denial of interest deductions, and deemed dividend treatment under Article 94 of the Income Tax Law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EGP 200,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years; administrative additional tax up to 30% of the under-assessed tax; mandatory withdrawal of investment incentives and tax clearance denial.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who sign or procure a false declaration can be personally and jointly liable for the unpaid tax; the criminal court may issue a travel ban, freeze director bank accounts, and order asset confiscation; imprisonment is expressly authorized for intentional tax evasion.
Shell Company Conduit / Sham SPV

Statutory sanction: ETA may disallow the SPV’s existence for tax purposes, impute a deemed distribution to the non-resident shareholder, recharacterize the acquired target financing as an equity dividend, and apply a 10% dividend WHT; under the AML/CFT framework, the entity is also reported as a suspicious shell.

Director personal liability: A nominee director can be separately fined up to EGP 300,000 and disqualified from serving as manager in Egypt; civil liability is asserted for induced tax losses, coupled with a travel ban until full restitution is secured.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EGP 50,000 for each unfiled TP Local File/Master File, plus upward adjustment of taxable profits by the ETA with a secondary 8% annual interest on the additional tax found due.

Director personal liability: Liability is typically fiscal (entity level), but current Egyptian enforcement practice sanctions the responsible finance director with personal fines and, for cross-border fraud, the public prosecutor can expand investigation; international DTA exchange of information proceedings can also expose director bank records and assets.
Negligent Dividend Distribution Without Withholding

Statutory sanction: Full withholding tax liability, penalty equal to 30% of the unreported withholding amount, and criminal fine up to EGP 100,000.

Director personal liability: The responsible managing director is jointly liable as withholding agent; the ETA may issue enforcement notice with a travel ban and a personal distress action against private movable assets.
Anti-Money Laundering / Know-Your-Counterparty Breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fine up to EGP 1,000,000 under the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law, suspension of treasury operations, and referral to the Public Prosecutor; the funding channels may be blocked and the SPV placed on the ETA risk blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for transaction approval may be personally fined and, for knowingly structuring payments to evade disclosure, imprisoned for up to 7 years.
