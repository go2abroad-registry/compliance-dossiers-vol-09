Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) of the Ministry of Finance, headquartered at 1 Ramsis Street, Nasr City, Cairo; official digital portals: https://www.eta.gov.eg (ETA corporate portal), https://www.elmoysr.gov.eg (unified taxpayer services portal), and https://invoice.eta.gov.eg (e-invoicing/e-receipt platform).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) One-Stop-Shop or the local Commercial Registry and obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate stating ISIC 6202 activity. Step 2: File the unified corporate registration application (Form 1) with the ETA, attaching the authenticated Articles of Association, commercial registry extract, lease contract or title deed for the registered office, board/manager resolution appointing the tax representative, and passport copies of all managers and ultimate shareholders; non-resident shareholders require no minimum capital and may own 100% of the LLC. Step 3: Obtain the electronic Tax Card and the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN), which concurrently covers income tax, VAT, payroll tax, and stamp duty withholding obligations. Step 4: Register for VAT under the same TIN either mandatorily (annual taxable supplies exceeding EGP 500,000) or voluntarily (turnover above EGP 50,000) so as to lawfully zero-rate export services; export registration is also recorded in the ETA exporter register and the National Single Window for Foreign Trade (NAFEZA) with the ACID customs pre-registration. Step 5: For cross-border service billing, open a designated foreign-currency bank account in Egypt in the name of the LLC and register the account with the Central Bank of Egypt correspondent bank for incoming SWIFT credits. ETA processing generally completes within 10 to 30 working days when acts through a licensed tax agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt is a unitary civil-law jurisdiction with no US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory elections: (a) approval of a non-calendar fiscal year for corporate income tax purposes under Article 34 of Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 30/2023; (b) the simplified presumptive tax regime for small businesses under Law 152/2020 (annual turnover not exceeding EGP 3 million, as amended), electively replacing standard corporate tax, VAT filing, and payroll administration with turnover-based taxation at graduated rates of 0.5% to 5%; (c) start-up tax exemptions and securities/options deferral elections under Law 30/2023 subject to ETA eligibility certification; (d) definitive election to zero-rate export services under Article 8(2) of the VAT Law and the Executive Regulations upon producing foreign-currency repatriation evidence; and (e) no group/consolidated filing election exists, meaning the LLC is taxed as a stand-alone entity; hybrid-mismatch protection relies on domestic general anti-abuse rules under Articles 60-63 of Law 91/2005 and Egyptian tax treaty applicable provisions, not on an elective instrument.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies (VAT Law 67/2016, Article 15); voluntary registration is available from EGP 50,000 of annual taxable supplies (Executive Regulations, Article 26). An ODC exporting software services is effectively obliged to register regardless of turnover in order to claim the 0% export rate and issue compliant e-invoices with ETA clearance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying services are taxed at 0% under Articles 8 and 9 of VAT Law 67/2016. For ODC software engineering, development, and maintenance services supplied by the Egyptian LLC to a non-resident group company or foreign client, the following cumulative conditions apply: (i) the recipient must be resident outside Egypt and possess no taxable establishment in Egypt for this service; (ii) the commercial and economic benefit of the service is realized entirely outside Egypt; (iii) the consideration is invoiced in foreign currency and settled through Egyptian banks; (iv) the foreign-currency proceeds are repatriated to Egypt within the applicable Central Bank of Egypt deadline; and (v) the taxpayer retains contemporaneous contracts, delivery and acceptance evidence (e.g., technical sign-offs, time sheets, repository handover logs) proving that the software services were rendered from the Egyptian ODC to the offshore recipient. The ETA applies the substance-over-form principle; mere outsourcing labels without staffing, execution, and risk infrastructure will be recharacterized as a domestic service taxable at 14%.

Electronic invoicing is fully mandatory for all VAT registrants in Egypt. Every B2B/B2G invoice must be submitted in structured XML through the ETA Data Exchange Protocol (DXP) using a REST API, SFTP, or the ETA web portal, and the invoice is legally issued only upon real-time ETA validation and stamping (synchronization). Non-integrated paper invoices are invalid for VAT deduction and trigger a fine of EGP 50,000 per invoice (Article 74 of Law 67/2016 as amended). The parallel e-receipt mandate covers B2C transactions. ERP systems must be certified or the taxpayer must use the ETA portal; corrective credit/debit notes are also subject to the same clearance workflow.

The ETA conducts strict post-clearance audits of zero-rating claims. Unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating results in: reassessment at the standard 14% VAT rate; monthly late-payment interest at 2% of the tax due (Article 77 of Law 67/2016 as amended); per-invoice fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 for false or non-electronic invoices; retrospective clawback of any VAT refunds or input-tax recoveries; and criminal sanctions under Articles 70-75 of Law 67/2016 for tax evasion and invoice forgery, including imprisonment and fines of up to double the evaded tax. The ETA's period of assessment is generally five years, extended in cases of deliberate concealment, and the Large Taxpayer Center and Export Recovery Unit may suspend refund cycles and impose immediate withholding offsets when documentation is incomplete.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Post-March 2024 exchange-rate unification under the CBE–IMF program, the Egyptian pound (EGP) trades in a single free-float band; current-account transactions (service receipts/payments) are fully convertible upon documentary compliance with CBE anti-money-laundering and repatriation rules. Capital-account transactions (equity injections, inbound loans, dividends) remain subject to a documentary gate administered by CBE-authorized banks: no pre-approval is required for equity capital contributions, but profit and dividend remittances require (i) audited financial statements, (ii) an Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax clearance certificate, (iii) proof of dividend withholding tax (standard 10%), and (iv) a beneficial-ownership declaration. Practical remittance hurdles are substantially reduced post-float; banks may quote wider spreads during periods of excess volatility, but no formal FX rationing or multiple-rate regime is in force.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The mandatory export-proceeds surrender regime (100% under February-2017 CBE directives, subsequently 50% in 2023) was terminated upon the March-2024 unified-float reforms. There is currently no statutory conversion ratio: exporters and service suppliers may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in a CBE-authorized foreign-currency account or convert at the free market rate. The controlling obligation is repatriation: proceeds from software/IT services (ISIC 6202) must be collected through an authorized Egyptian bank within 90 days from the date of service rendition or invoice maturity, whichever is earlier; each export invoice must be matched to a CBE banking receipt to satisfy both exchange-control and ETA transfer-pricing review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — البنك المركزي المصري, operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), with exclusive jurisdiction over exchange control, authorized-bank licensing, and FX market supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as a stand-alone primary clearing rail; Medium strictly as an ancillary settlement layer. Legal constraints: Wise B2B, Payoneer, and similar EMBs/EMIs hold no CBE banking license and cannot receive statutory export proceeds for Egyptian legal entities under CBE Law 194/2020, which mandates that all export/service proceeds be repatriated via an authorized Egyptian bank within 90 days. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT MT103 from the subscribing shareholder's overseas bank to the LLC's designated capital-collection account (Capital Escrow sub-account) at an authorized Egyptian bank, labeled with CBE statistical purpose code 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (code category 320). The receiving bank files the CBE direct-investment registration and issues a Foreign Investment Certificate; funds may remain in USD or be converted into EGP at the free market rate. Required attachments on first draw: certified articles of association, latest commercial-registry extract, tax-card and VAT registration certificates, notarized subscription resolution, and 3-6 months source-of-funds statements from the remitting shareholder.Primary clearing rail for software-engineering service settlements is SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic wire transfers (operational expenses, subcontractor fees, management fees, dividends, and tax payments); SWIFT MT202 is used for interbank cover in currency liquidity chains. Commercial Letters of Credit (usance/sight) are confined to physical-hardware imports (e.g., servers, workstations) on a standby or documentary basis. Outbound disbursements require the underlying agreement, vendor invoice, and proof of WHT collection (10% standard Egyptian WHT on services/dividends to non-resident payees, subject to DTT relief demonstrated via a certificate of tax residence); dividend wires additionally require audited financial statements and a valid ETA tax clearance certificate.Deliverable USD/EGP FX Forwards (tenor 1-12 months) from CBE-authorized banks, requiring an underlying trade invoice, service contract, or procurement commitment.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on the EGP, permitted post-2024 liberalization for corporate and non-resident counterparties hedging EGP exposure without settling principal.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP) with major Egyptian and international banks for longer-tenor exposures covering capex or recurring receivables.FX options (limited availability on request from largest authorized dealers) for premium-based hedging of adverse EGP moves.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% under Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 for commercial, industrial, and IT service entities

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% domestic withholding tax on cash dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; treaty protection may reduce this to 5% under applicable Double Taxation Agreements

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory deadline is no later than 4 months after the end of the fiscal/accounting year; for a calendar-year LLC, the return is due by April 30 of the following year

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million aggregate value of controlled transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Local File preparation; Master File obligation arises when the consolidated global group revenue exceeds EGP 200 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies above EGP 20 billion consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not administer GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) licensing; nevertheless, the ETA enforces a de facto economic substance standard through anti-avoidance, tax residency, beneficial ownership, and treaty abuse rules. A non-resident-controlled LLC operating as an offshore development center must evidence physical office, payroll, decision-making, disposition over contracts, and genuine operational risk to sustain treaty claims and avoid recharacterization as a shell entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine equal to the evaded tax amount, in addition to automatic surcharges. ETA may impose the higher of the evaded tax plus 100% or 2% monthly delinquency, as well as asset seizure and blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve falsified accounts are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and heavy financial penalties; applicable measures include freezing of personal assets, a blanket travel ban issued by the prosecutor, and criminal incarceration of the designated responsible officer.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA authorized TP adjustment with deemed profit recomputation, underpayment penalty of 30% of additional assessed tax, and denial of deductions. Interest accrues monthly from the original filing date; repeated violations raise the penalty to 50%.

Director personal liability: Directors who implemented intercompany pricing with no documented policy are personally liable for resulting tax assessment shortfall in the course of winding up or asset stripping; they may also face travel restrictions during criminal investigation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, retroactive withholding tax on repatriated profits, disallowance of claimed exempt or reduced-rate treatment, and full re-audit liability. The ETA can reclassify the entity as a controlled foreign activity and treat its entire revenue as subject to income tax.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally ordered to return distributed profits and pay the differential tax plus interest if the corporate veil is pierced; criminal referral follows when the sham structure was used to evade tax and the directors failed to maintain substantive records.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines starting at EGP 50,000 and escalating to EGP 1,000,000, with director disqualification and suspension of electronic filing accounts; fines double for repeated noncompliance.

Director personal liability: Legal liability attaches to the amir of the board and money laundering compliance officer; sanctions include disqualification from serving as a director in Egypt, personal fines, and in cases of intentional concealment, imprisonment for up to 3 years.
