Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for LLCs under the amended Companies Law. The share capital may be declared in the Articles of Association. If the capital is in cash, it must be deposited in a bank account opened for the company before GAFI submission; no separate escrow account is mandatory unless the reviewing authority requests a banker’s certificate, which may occur for consumer prepayment business models.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense inspection and approval are required for the registered office and any warehouse: fire extinguishers, emergency exits, alarm system and building structural safety. Occupational clearance under Labour Law No. 12 of 2003 requires a safe workplace, employee welfare facilities, adequate ventilation and lighting, and a general safety plan for handling incoming goods and delivery parcels.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for LLCs under the amended Companies Law. The share capital may be declared in the Articles of Association. If the capital is in cash, it must be deposited in a bank account opened for the company before GAFI submission; no separate escrow account is mandatory unless the reviewing authority requests a banker’s certificate, which may occur for consumer prepayment business models.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA); official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; tax filing portal: https://etax.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC must (1) incorporate and obtain a Commercial Registration certificate from the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Ministry of Trade and Industry; (2) file the ETA 'Tax Card' application (Form No. 200 - Economic Activity) for income tax registration and receive a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN); (3) register for VAT using Form No. (109) if taxable supply turnover exceeds the statutory threshold or for exporters regardless of threshold; (4) enroll in the Egyptian e-invoicing system, obtaining an XML digital signing certificate and integrating the ERP/payment rails with the ETA's API; (5) appoint a domestic tax representative for domicile notices and service. ETA registration is centralized nationwide; no sub-jurisdictional tax registration is required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC must (1) incorporate and obtain a Commercial Registration certificate from the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Ministry of Trade and Industry; (2) file the ETA 'Tax Card' application (Form No. 200 - Economic Activity) for income tax registration and receive a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN); (3) register for VAT using Form No. (109) if taxable supply turnover exceeds the statutory threshold or for exporters regardless of threshold; (4) enroll in the Egyptian e-invoicing system, obtaining an XML digital signing certificate and integrating the ERP/payment rails with the ETA's API; (5) appoint a domestic tax representative for domicile notices and service. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or US-style pass-through election is available for an Egyptian LLC. The LLC is taxed as a separate juridical entity under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 at the entity level (22.5% CIT for 2025). Limited elections exist for tax group treatment under Article 61 (carry-over of losses among the group) and for the International Trade Facilitation Zone regime; Egyptian law does not recognize a hybrid-mismatch election, and the entity cannot elect to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity for local tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services supplied by a resident; exporters may opt for VAT registration below the threshold to claim refund of input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to export of tangible goods, export of goods shipped to free zones for re-export, and international services performed for non-residents and effectively used outside Egypt. Fast-fashion e-commerce exports qualify when title and risk pass to the offshore buyer, goods leave Egypt under customs control, and the e-commerce operator is not a connected party substituting the supply; exports are evidenced by the customs export declaration and freight documents. The zero rate also applies to physical vouchers/cards that are indistinguishable from goods; digital download/e-services to non-resident consumers are generally zero-rated only where the place of supply is treated as offshore.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CED) stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority (ECA)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air freight) showing the foreign consignee and Egypt port of loading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank through which export proceeds were collected in a convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the CED HS codes
  • e-Invoice/e-Export XML clearance record from the ETA e-invoicing system
  • Sale/purchase transaction evidence or offshore payment gateway settlement report for Klarna_BNPL and Shopify_Payments
  • Bank receipt of FX conversion or credit on the exporter's dollar account with a bank licensed by the Central Bank of Egypt

Mandatory e-invoicing (issued under Law 67 of 2016 and Ministerial Decision 248 of 2021) requires all VAT-registered taxpayers to issue invoices via the ETA e-invoice platform using a template-based XML format. Real-time fiscal clearance is implemented through the ETA's API: invoices are transmitted digitally, and a UUID (Unique Invoice Identifier) with a hash code (QR/bar code) is returned by the ETA for each invoice. The same clearance system applies to e-receipts in retail/BNPL scenarios, and all sales through Klarna_BNPL and Shopify_Payments must be recorded with the fiscal QR code and transmitted within the ETA's real-time validation window. The ETA requires each invoice to be digitally signed with the taxpayer's certificate; non-clearance or mismatched XML data is treated as no valid tax invoice and triggers rejection and fines.

Export zero-rating is subject to ETA/Central Bank joint audits; e-invoicing XML must reconcile with customs declaration. For fraudulent zero-rating (fictitious exports, shell purchasers, mischaracterised domestic supplies, or false FX remittance evidence), the ETA will claw back the input VAT refund with interest and impose a fine of up to 300% of the evaded tax (VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, Articles 33 and 37), plus criminal imprisonment for deliberate evasion and suspension/revocation of the e-invoicing clearance certificate. Negligent non-payment attracts a penalty of 50% to 300% of the tax due depending on repeat and intent. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day CBE deadline triggers a monetary penalty equal to the undistributed export collection amount and potential re-characterisation of the transaction as a domestic taxable supply, eliminating the zero rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains current-account convertibility in line with IMF Article VIII: payments for imports, services, dividends, interest, and profit remittances are transferable through CBE-licensed banks subject to verified underlying documentation and tax clearance (or settlement of withholding). The Egyptian pound (EGP) is technically convertible for trade and investment-related current transactions, but the capital account is restricted; non-resident equity injections into an EG LLC are permitted without prior regulatory licensing, yet the right to repatriate capital and accumulated profits is legally preserved only when the inbound contribution is recorded with GAFI under Investment Law No. 72/2017 and admitted through a licensed Egyptian bank. Profit remittance hurdles include bank-level documentary review (audited financials, board/shareholder resolution, tax certificate), bank tolerance to periodic U.S. dollar liquidity tightness, and the practical requirement to demonstrate a genuine economic exposure to avoid CBE anti-speculation assessment. Following the March 2024 exchange-rate unification and the shift to a flexible, market-determined EGP, the former chronic FX shortage and administered rationing have been materially reduced, but banks retain discretion to require additional economic-purpose documentation for large outward transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE Circular No. 343 of 2019, as amended, imposes the baseline repatriation obligation: exporters must credit the full invoice value of export receipts to an account at a CBE-licensed bank within 3 months from the date of shipment, extendable by bank agreement to a maximum of 6 months. Proceeds may be retained in a domiciled foreign-currency account or converted to EGP at the prevailing market rate. During the FX rationing episode from mid-2022 to March 2024, the CBE instructed banks to apply a mandatory surrender ratio of 100% of export receipts (subsequently relaxed to 50% under certain 2023 circulars permitting import offset), requiring exporters to sell eligible proceeds to the banking system in EGP. After the March 2024 unification, the fixed statutory mandatory conversion ratio has effectively been reduced to zero in normal conditions, and proceeds may remain indefinitely in FCY accounts pending use; nevertheless, the repatriation timeline remains in force and the CBE retains legal authority to reinstate compulsory conversion ratios during balance-of-payments stress. The EG LLC treasury must therefore design cash-flow buffers with an EGP liquidity pool and maintain documentary evidence of every export collection to satisfy periodic bank verification.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), established under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), exercising sole authority over banking licensing, foreign exchange dealing, payment systems, and cross-border capital movements; complementing authorities include the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for FDI registration and repatriation protection under Investment Law No. 72/2017, and the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU) for AML/CFT supervisory clearance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with material legal constraints. Offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, Banking Circle, and MultiPass are not licensed by the CBE and cannot lawfully (i) open an EGP-denominated domestic account for an Egyptian corporate entity, (ii) act as the local settlement bank for an Egyptian e-commerce merchant's EGP collections, or (iii) discharge local payroll, VAT, or customs obligations. An offshore EMI may, however, be legitimately used as an ancillary international collection and payout rail for Shopify Payments, Klarna BNPL, and overseas marketplace proceeds denominated in USD, EUR, or GBP, provided that all balances are periodically swept into the CBE-licensed Egyptian anchor bank account and fully reflected in the CBE's cross-border trade payment reporting. Operating any local payment or collection channel without a CBE license is prohibited under Law No. 194/2020 and the Electronic Payment Systems Law No. 18/2019; the blueprint therefore mandates a paired structure of one offshore EMI account for non-resident settlement efficiency and one Egyptian licensed bank account as the legal treasury hub.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol: SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent's bank to the EG LLC's USD or EUR account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, using the beneficiary's Egyptian IBAN (EG format, 23 characters comprising the country code EG, two check digits, and the domestic account number). The wire's purpose code must categorically identify an equity contribution (foreign direct investment in Egypt) and must not be described as a loan, as loan inflows require separate CBE debt registration for future debt-service remittance. The receiving bank requires: shareholder or board resolution approving the capital increase and account opening, updated commercial register extract, GAFI file showing the LLC's investment record, UBO and source-of-funds declarations, and a completed foreign-currency cover form. To preserve the statutory repatriation guarantee, the total paid-in capital must be recorded with GAFI under Investment Law No. 72/2017 and the bank's receipt advice retained in the corporate treasury archive. Funds may be kept in foreign currency or converted to EGP only as operational needs require, and the treasury should maintain a written FX conversion plan to avoid unnecessary transaction costs.Clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier settlements in USD, EUR, or GBP, ideal for recurring procurement from Asian apparel factories; (ii) irrevocable documentary sight letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) for large-volume purchase orders, providing bank-to-bank payment certainty and title document control; and (iii) domestic Egyptian clearing rails, including the Egyptian Automated Clearing House and the Instant Payment Network, for EGP-denominated local costs such as warehousing, logistics, staff payroll, and VAT. Before effecting any outbound trade payment, the EG LLC must maintain a valid importer registration with the Ministry of Trade and Industry and submit the intended shipment data through NAFEZA, the national single-window platform, enabling the bank to validate the trade payment against the CBE's electronic import registration. Payments for services, commissions, and royalties require the CBE service-import form and underlying executed agreements. All import HS codes must align with the apparel classifications (HS Chapters 61 and 62) and with ISIC 4791 business scope to ensure consistency across the customs declaration, commercial invoices, and bank documentation.Onshore USD/EGP foreign-exchange forwards with a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, maturities up to 12 months, permitted only where the EG LLC demonstrates a documented underlying trade, procurement, or investment exposure under CBE derivative rules.Onshore FX swaps through the local banking relationship, combining spot conversion with a forward reversal, used solely for liquidity steering between EGP operational balances and USD procurement needs.Cross-currency swaps available for longer-dated balance-sheet hedging, subject to CBE approval where the notional amount or tenor exceeds standard local dealing thresholds and where the hedge aligns with real commercial cash flows.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on EGP transacted by the group treasury center in London or the DIFC with a non-resident bank counterparty, permissible only for hedging the EG LLC's actual non-convertibility risk, with settlement outside Egypt and fully documented economic exposure, and strictly not for speculative EGP trading, which the CBE prohibits for Egyptian residents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory for related-party controlled transactions aggregating EGP 10 million or more during the fiscal year; Master File is required if the consolidated group turnover exceeds EGP 500 million. Country-by-Country reporting may apply for groups exceeding EGP 2 billion consolidated turnover.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE; however, ETA's general anti-avoidance rules and the Investment Law require demonstrable economic substance for treaty relief, Free Zone tax exemptions, and intercompany cost deductions. Where no real business activity, distinct premises, or qualified personnel exist, ETA may disregard the entity, recharacterise arrangements, and deny treaty or statutory benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Restitution of all unpaid taxes, a minimum 30% fraud penalty on the evaded tax, and criminal prosecution punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of up to EGP 1 million.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for tax debts, and personal assets can be seized through civil restitution proceedings. Conviction may trigger travel bans, disqualification of board membership, and imprisonment for those knowingly signing false returns.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Additional tax assessment based on the ETA's transfer pricing adjustment, a 30% penalty on the tax underpayment, and monthly surcharges of 2% during the assessment period. The documentation failure itself carries an administrative penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unsubstantiated intercompany pricing may be personally summoned to testify, and the corporate veil may be pierced to enforce civil recovery of unpaid taxes if the company was being used as a mere conduit.
Shell Company Activity

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the taxpayer's structure; attribution of all related profits to the Egyptian permanent establishment, denial of treaty relief, and readjustment at the 22.5% rate plus a 30% fraud penalty and daily surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain artificial structures face personal civil liability for the resulting tax assessment and potential clawback of dividends paid from tax-shielded profits; criminal liability attaches if the arrangement amounts to tax fraud.
Dividend Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The distributing company is liable for the un-withheld tax, a penalty of up to 30% of the withheld amount, and interest surcharges at 2% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise distributions without ensuring withholding are jointly and severally liable for the corporate WHT debt; in serious cases, bank accounts of board members may be frozen and their international travel restricted.
AML and Banking Compliance Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Suspension of bank accounts, referral to the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Unit, penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering Law (including fines up to EGP 500,000), and potential criminal indictment for unlawful proceeds of tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally exposed to confiscation of bank balances, travel bans, and a prison term of 5 to 15 years if the tax evasion is proven to form part of a money laundering enterprise.
