Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for LLC under Law No. 159/1981 as amended by Law No. 177/2018; capital is fixed freely by members and may be in cash or in-kind. Cash subscriptions must be deposited into a temporary bank account in Egypt, and the bank deposit certificate is filed with the registration application; capital is released to the company after incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — official portals: www.eta.gov.eg (tax registration, tax card, and filings) and invoi.eta.gov.eg (mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt fiscal clearance).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC at the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) one-stop shop and obtain the Commercial Registry certificate. Step 2: File the income tax registration at the competent ETA tax office within 30 days of incorporation, submitting the incorporation certificate, memorandum and articles of association, commercial register extract, board resolution, specimen signature card, registered-office lease, and shareholder identification; the ETA issues the Tax Card and the Unified Tax Number (UTN), the single identifier for CIT, VAT, PAYE, and withholding obligations. Step 3: Register for VAT with the ETA within 30 days after annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000, or upon the first taxable supply if the threshold is already exceeded; the VAT certificate carries the same UTN. Step 4: Enroll in the mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt scheme via the invoi.eta.gov.eg portal — request the ETA e-signature digital certificate, create the company portal account, and complete API compliance testing for real-time invoice clearance. Non-resident suppliers without a permanent establishment in Egypt are not registered for CIT, but remain subject to withholding tax at source (10% dividends, 20% interest/royalties/services, subject to treaty relief) and to VAT reverse-charge rules on digital services supplied to Egyptian consumers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian law provides no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch classification election; an Egyptian LLC is mandatorily a resident corporation taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the 22.5% CIT rate under Law 91 of 2005 as amended, with no pass-through option. Available statutory elections include: (a) fiscal-year election — a non-calendar year-end requires prior ETA approval; (b) SME simplified tax regime under Law 152 of 2020 — LLCs with annual gross turnover not exceeding EGP 20 million may elect presumptive taxation at rates of 0.5% to 2% of gross revenue instead of accounting-based taxable income; (c) cash-basis VAT accounting for eligible small taxpayers and ETA-approved refund/offset schemes; (d) transfer-pricing documentation election between the full OECD-aligned master/local file and a simplified file where related-party transactions are below EGP 8 million; (e) fixed-asset valuation policy election (historical cost vs. revaluation) with ETA notification. No hybrid-mismatch-specific statute is in force; the ETA applies substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods or services (Article 29 of Law 67 of 2016), computed on a 12-month rolling basis; once exceeded, registration is due within 30 days. Non-resident suppliers of digital and e-commerce services have no de minimis threshold: electronically supplied services to Egyptian consumers are taxable from the first EGP under reverse-charge and e-marketplace collection rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating under Article 20 of Law 67 of 2016 is available to the Egyptian LLC where: (a) goods are physically exported from Egypt against a valid customs export declaration within 90 days of the date of supply, with ETA extension obtainable; (b) services are supplied to a non-resident recipient and the actual and effective place of consumption lies wholly outside Egypt — for the EdTech subscription scope, zero rating applies only where platform users are located and consuming the service offshore, while Egyptian-resident end users remain taxable at 14% regardless of Apple IAP or Google Play Billing flows; (c) the export consideration is received in convertible foreign currency through an Egyptian licensed bank; and (d) contemporaneous documentary proof links the supply, the payer, and the payment, including the contract, license records, and digital usage logs for online services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (karta/bayan) evidencing physical departure of goods from Egypt; for pure services, a notarized service-export statement and the underlying non-resident contract
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) issued in the exporter's name evidencing actual cross-border movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian licensed bank confirming foreign-currency receipt of the export proceeds

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing applies across all Egyptian governorates to every VAT-registered taxpayer (phased rollout fully completed by 2023). B2B invoices must be transmitted via ETA-certified ERP or API integration to the central platform at invoi.eta.gov.eg before the invoice is treated as fiscally valid; each invoice is instantly validated (Accepted/Rejected with error codes) and digitally stamped with a QR code. B2C sales require ETA-cleared e-receipts with embedded QR and cryptographic stamp codes generated at the point of sale. Payloads must comply with the ETA XML Schema, taxpayer digital certificates, and RSA-based code signing requirements. Non-compliance, including issuing, altering, or cancelling invoices outside the portal, triggers fines of EGP 50,000 to EGP 2,000,000 per incident, possible suspension of the fiscal code, and disallowance of input VAT deduction for non-cleared invoices.

The ETA matches zero-rated claims against the customs declaration database and Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) foreign-currency receipts records. Export proceeds not repatriated within 180 days of shipment, absent CBE extension, or IRC documents that are missing or unmatched, cause the zero rating to be reversed: VAT at 14% becomes due by clawback assessment together with default interest and late-payment penalties. Where false documents or inflated export values are detected, the penalty increases to 100% of the evaded VAT, fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 per fraudulent document, and criminal liability including imprisonment under Law 67 of 2016 and the Customs Code. Residual input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports may be refunded through the ETA e-refund module under a 45-day statutory processing window or offset against other tax liabilities; refund claims are subject to desk audits with a three-year lookback period, extended to five years for fraud. Frequent or anomalous refund patterns trigger the ETA red-flag engine and full field audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the unified exchange-rate float of March 2024, the Egyptian Pound (EGP) is freely convertible on current-account transactions, including profit remittance and foreign-currency settlement for software licensing and EdTech services, provided the underlying commercial documentation, audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates are submitted to a CBE-authorized bank. Capital-account transactions (equity injections, shareholder loans, liquidation proceeds and cross-border intercompany funding) remain subject to CBE/GAFI registration and anti-avoidance screening. Non-resident shareholders of an Egyptian LLC may repatriate dividends and profit shares after withholding tax and settlement of all tax liabilities; the bank will verify the CBE inward remittance declaration, board minutes, and audited balance sheet. In practice, convertibility is reliable and transparent under the current managed-float framework, but the treasury should maintain a complete transaction dossier because the CBE retains reserve powers to defer suspicious or inconsistent remittance requests.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from services (including EdTech subscriptions, online language-learning content, licensing fees and software-related payments) must be received through an authorized bank in Egypt within 90 days from the invoice date or service completion date. After the March 2024 FX reform, there is no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain the foreign currency in a CBE-authorized foreign-currency account or convert to EGP at the interbank rate as needed. The exporter remains obliged to file the CBE export-proceeds declaration with supporting shipping/service documentation, and any non-repatriation beyond the 90-day statutory window must be justified through a formal waiver or approved offsetting arrangement with the CBE. Failure to surrender export receipts within the prescribed timeline triggers administrative penalties, restriction of FX facilities and potential reputational escalation under the Exchange Control Law.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) acting as the primary foreign-exchange administrator under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and Exchange Control Law No. 97 of 1976 (as amended), with complementary licensing and capital-flow supervision performed by the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital financial institutions and electronic-money institutions (EMIs) cannot lawfully obtain Egyptian domestic IBANs, access the Egyptian Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system, or provide local clearing services without a CBE license under the Payment Services Law No. 18 of 2019 and the CBE fintech sandbox framework. Neobanks and global EMI platforms such as Wise or Revolut Business are structurally limited to auxiliary foreign-currency collection points; all core treasury operations must be conducted through a CBE-authorized bank or a licensed Egyptian payment service provider (e.g., Paymob, Fawry, e-finance) to avoid unlicensed deposit-taking and foreign-exchange violation risk. An Egyptian LLC should therefore open the primary corporate account at a tier-1 domestic bank and use fintech/EMI rails only for niche cross-border disbursement experiments with fully documented underlying trade contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections from foreign shareholders must be routed through SWIFT MT103 customer credits to the designated capital-escrow or operational account at the chosen CBE-authorized bank, using the CBE inward remittance purpose code for direct investment (specifically 'Capital participation in an Egyptian company') and accompanied by the GAFI investment registration file, the certified and notarized memorandum of association, the board resolution authorizing capital issuance/contribution, and a completed CBE foreign-funds declaration. Upon confirmation of receipt, the bank will register the inflow in the CBE investment reporting portal; the proceeds may remain in a foreign-currency account or be converted to EGP at the prevailing interbank rate without mandatory rationing. For an Egyptian LLC with non-resident shareholders, the capital escrow account should be opened before commercial operations and released against CBE-approved expenditure schedules, thereby preventing inadvertent classification of equity as trade debt or taxable service income.Outbound EdTech disbursements, including royalties to overseas content providers, cloud-infrastructure charges, marketing agency fees and embedded technical-subcontracting payments, should settle via: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer-initiated credit transfers for recurring service and licensing payments, supported by executed contracts, pro-forma invoices, valid tax invoices, and Egyptian withholding-tax certificates issued through the electronic withholding system to the CBE and the Tax Authority; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707) for any physical procurement or high-value hardware-intense technology deliverables, with the issuing bank examining the underlying software/training and services documents; and (iii) CBE-licensed payment gateways (Paymob, Fawry, e-finance) used solely on the collection side for Egyptian card and mobile-payment settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 500,000,000 (approximately USD 10 million)

Economic substance scope: Egyptian Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) require relevant entities to demonstrate adequate economic substance in Egypt for qualifying activities such as holding companies, IP holding, and financing

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: fines up to three times the evaded tax amount and imprisonment up to five years

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, including imprisonment, fines, and possible disqualification from business activities
Late Filing or Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of EGP 5,000 per day of delay, plus 1% monthly interest on tax due

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability for tax debts; potential criminal prosecution if intentional
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments increasing taxable income, fines up to EGP 1 million or 10% of transaction value

Director personal liability: Personal liability for willful non-compliance, including fines and imprisonment
Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Entity dissolution, back taxation, and fines up to EGP 500,000

Director personal liability: Personal liability for fraudulent incorporation or management, including imprisonment and travel restrictions
