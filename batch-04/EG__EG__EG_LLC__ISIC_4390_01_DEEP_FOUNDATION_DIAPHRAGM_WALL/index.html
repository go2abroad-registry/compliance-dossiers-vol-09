Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; Unified portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; ELMIS electronic tax registration module: https://elmis.eta.gov.eg; Mandatory e-invoicing clearance platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity first registers its Egyptian presence (LLC, branch, or project office) with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), then appoints a tax representative under notarized power of attorney. The unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) is obtained through ETA Form No. 266 filed via ELMIS or at the competent tax office, attaching the GAFI certificate, commercial register excerpt, constitutional documents, and the executed engineering contract for the deep foundation scope. Upon issuance of the tax card, the taxpayer must complete VAT registration when annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 or immediately for large contracts, and subsequently onboard to the ETA e-invoicing sandbox to receive digital signing certificates for real-time fiscal clearance of every invoice.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; an Egyptian LLC is statutorily opaque and always taxed at entity level under Law 91 of 2005. Available elections are limited to (i) the presumptive tax regime for micro, small, and medium enterprises under Law 152 of 2020 where annual turnover does not exceed EGP 20 million, (ii) a deemed-profit assessment basis for foreign contractor permanent establishments that keep inadequate books, and (iii) statutory depreciation and foreign tax credit elections. No hybrid-mismatch election is recognized under Egyptian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover in supplies of goods and services (Article 17/1 of VAT Law 67/2016); a foreign geotechnical contractor performing deep slurry trenching works in Egypt is registrable regardless of turnover because a fixed project site creates a taxable establishment nexus

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 19(1) of VAT Law 67/2016, goods physically exported from Egypt and services rendered to a non-resident beneficiary and wholly consumed outside Egypt are taxable at 0%. For ISIC 4390 deep trenching and diaphragm wall contracting, zero-rating is available only when the construction site and the benefit of the works are located outside Egypt, or when specialized equipment is exported and title passes to a non-resident principal abroad; works performed on domestic Egyptian sites must be invoiced at 14% standard VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued through the NAFEZA single window evidencing physical export
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill proving dispatch and destination outside Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Egyptian bank confirming receipt of the foreign currency export proceeds

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance: every VAT invoice must be generated in certified software, transmitted in XML/JSON to the ETA e-invoicing API (OAuth2-authenticated), validated by the ETA, and returned with a unique UUID and embedded QR code required on the invoice document. B2B and B2C e-invoice/e-receipt mandates apply in phased tranches by taxpayer turnover; un-cleared invoices are legally ineffective, expose the seller to a fine per invoice, and block input VAT deduction and export zero-rating.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to ETA post-clearance audit and electronic reconciliation against the e-invoicing clearance record, customs declarations, and bank FX receipts. If any required document is missing, the export is re-characterized as a domestic taxable supply and the ETA will assess 14% VAT, apply late-payment surcharges at 2% per month from the original VAT return due date, and reverse refunds already paid. Wilful misdeclaration constitutes tax evasion punishable by a fine in the range of 3x to 5x of the evaded tax, imprisonment of the responsible officers, and loss of refund eligibility for a minimum period, with enforced collection via garnishment or lien over project proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) operates under a managed float with full current-account convertibility for bona fide trade and services through CBE-authorized banks. Capital-account transactions (direct investment, equity injections, intercompany loans) are permitted but require structured documentary compliance, CBE registration, and downstream repatriation approval. Profit remittances by foreign shareholders are generally permissible if the original capital was brought into Egypt through the banking system and the CBE's Foreign Investment Notification was obtained at the time of contribution; settlement of current account payments requires an audited statement of the corporate's EGP income and a tax clearance certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export receipts (including proceeds from construction services rendered abroad) must be remitted to Egypt through an authorized bank. Under the CBE's March 2024 exchange-rate liberalization, the prior mandatory conversion ratio of 100% of export proceeds to EGP was suspended; the retained foreign currency may be deposited in a domestic foreign-currency account. The exporter must submit an export declaration and provide documentary evidence of the shipment/service within the CBE-prescribed period (generally 5 business days after receipt for services; goods must be credited within the contractual settlement period not exceeding 180 days). Temporary CBE circulars may re-impose a conversion obligation during balance-of-payments stress; such circulars have historically required conversion within 5–15 days of crediting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole regulatory authority for banking, foreign exchange, and monetary policy under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, its Executive Regulations, and CBE Foreign Exchange Circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail; offshore digital EMIs cannot hold EGP or directly access local clearing. They may be used for funding flows outside Egypt, but all Egypt-denominated operations must clear through a CBE-authorized bank partner. Licensed local fintechs (e.g., under CBE Fintech Regulations) can provide cross-border collection and payment API rails under a bank sponsorship; viability is Medium for sub-disbursement to subcontractors and low-value vendor payments after the bank validates the fintech's AML framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity or subordinated-debt capital must be wired via SWIFT MT202/MT103 to the LLC's capital account at an authorized Egyptian bank, with the remittance advice shown as 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Non-resident Direct Investment' under the Unified Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. The receiving bank will register the inflow with the CBE and issue a Capital Inflow Registration Certificate; this certificate is the only basis for future profit repatriation or capital liquidation. Contributions exceeding the registered share capital are treated as shareholder loans or as adjustments requiring prior GAFI/CBE approval. The board must adopt a capital-integration resolution and open a temporary CBE-designated foreign-currency account before remittance.Vendor and sub-contractor disbursements are routed primarily through SWIFT MT103 from the LLC's operational EGP or foreign-currency account. Commercial import payments are supported by the CBE's ACI platform (Advance Cargo Information) and are settled via an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (UCP600) with a 20–30% cash cover (depending on the bank's limit confinement) or, for smaller transactions, by telegraphic transfer after invoice verification. Standby LCs and demand guarantees for performance bonds are issued against cash collateral or reduced exposure limits under the bank's corporate facility; the bank will approve the LC amount only after the tender/project contract is reviewed and the CBE's import/forfeiting rules are satisfied.USD/EGP deliverable currency forwards with CBE-authorized dealing banks, tenors up to 12 months, for covering contracted import obligations.Egyptian-pound non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for cross-border counterparties, only with CBE-approved banks and under a documented underlying trade exposure.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP) for hedging the US-dollar-denominated debt or receivables of the LLC, subject to CBE ring-fencing rules and representative limits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a Gulf-style standalone ESR regime, but Law No. 91 of 2005 (Arts. 27 and 68) and the executive regulations apply substance-over-form, GAAR, beneficial-ownership tests, a 4:1 thin-capitalization limit on interest, and CFC-type attribution for offshore passive income. The ETA may disregard an artificial intermediate entity, impute the contractor's profits, and deny DTA relief where the EG_LLC fails to demonstrate actual site personnel, equipment, contracts, and decision-making in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False or Deficient CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Law No. 91 of 2005 (Arts. 133-135) and the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 206 of 2020: imprisonment of 6 months to 5 years and a fine not exceeding twice the evaded tax, with the unpaid tax and the 2%-per-month surcharge (capped at 100%) collected in full.

Director personal liability: The managing director signing the return is jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax if willful concealment is proven; the ETA may impose travel bans, freeze bank accounts, and secure asset attachments against the director and the LLC. Corporate-veil piercing under the Egyptian Civil Code (Art. 55) applies where the LLC is operated as the shareholders' alter ego.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: ETA re-computation of the taxable margin under best-judgment principles, disallowance of the mispriced deductions, and a 2%-per-month delayed-payment surcharge on the resulting deficiency. Document failure exposes the taxpayer to fixed TP fines and the loss of the right to adjust the return by audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve and sign the TP declaration face criminal fines where false statements are certified; deliberate intercompany fraud supports evasion charges and personal indemnification, while the ETA can enjoin the director's travel pending the TP audit.
Shell Company Structure / Treaty Abuse (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the artificial entity under GAAR/substance-over-form, re-attribution of the whole contract profit to the EG_LLC, denial of the DTA rate (back to the statutory 20% royalty/interest or 10% dividend WHT), and penalties of up to 200% of the under-withheld tax.

Director personal liability: The veil is pierced: founders and directors become jointly liable for the reassessed tax; the ETA may issue an asset freeze and a travel ban; criminal referral follows when the sham arrangement was used to launder project proceeds.
AML, Cash-Drain and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended): fines from EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000 and imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years for non-reported transfers and false beneficial-ownership filings; the ETA also disallows unbanked cash expenses from the CIT computation, raising the 22.5% effective burden.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors who authorize cash schemes are personally prosecuted; funds used in the violation are confiscated; travel bans and passport holds apply pending the money-laundering investigation; the individual penalty accompanies the corporate fine, leaving directors personally exposed notwithstanding the LLC's limited liability.
