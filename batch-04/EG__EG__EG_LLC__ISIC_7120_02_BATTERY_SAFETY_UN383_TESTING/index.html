Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — an autonomous public authority under the Minister of Finance; unified e-services and e-filing portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoice/receipt portal: https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg; customs and export administration: Egyptian Customs Authority (NAFIZA single window, Ministry of Finance); investment and licensing registration: General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI); foreign-currency export-proceeds control: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and licensed Egyptian banks.

E-government filing portal

EG_LLC is incorporated as an Egyptian limited liability company through GAFI's one-stop-shop. Step 1: notarize the Articles of Association in Arabic (certified Arabic translation if the ultimate corporate shareholder is foreign), file them with the Commercial Registry, and obtain the commercial registration certificate. Step 2: apply at the competent ETA tax office for the unified Tax Card and Tax Identification Number by submitting the commercial registration certificate, AOA, premises lease or title deed, directors' national IDs, and the sectoral UN38.3 testing-laboratory accreditation (accreditation by the Egyptian Organization for Standardization and Quality and/or the Egyptian National Accreditation Council — EGAC). Step 3: obtain the TIN and Tax Card, then register for VAT as soon as annual taxable supplies are expected to exceed EGP 500,000 (or voluntarily and immediately where the lab's activity is export-oriented and input VAT recovery on equipment is required). Step 4: create the ETA e-invoicing account, obtain the digital signature, and certify the ERP/e-invoice connector in the ETA sandbox through an ETA-approved service provider. Step 5: open a foreign-currency bank account with a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, formally notify the bank of export-testing services, and maintain the prescribed export-proceeds repatriation tracking. For a non-resident foreign entity with no Egyptian establishment, a Tax Card cannot be obtained for purely offshore head-office supplies; once the entity operates an Egyptian laboratory or maintains a fixed employee base in Egypt, it is treated as a branch/permanent establishment and must register through an ETA-licensed fiscal representative, file CIT and VAT for Egyptian-source income, and pay the 10% remittance levy on repatriated branch profits. The locally incorporated EG_LLC route described above is the standard and most direct TIN/VAT registration path for this testing activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: An Egyptian LLC is a juridical capital company taxed separately from its shareholders under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and has no pass-through eligibility. The LLC is subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income at the currently gazetted standard CIT rate of 22.5% (aside from any temporary statutory contribution that may be in force). Territorial scope: resident Egyptian companies are taxed on worldwide income, while non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Egyptian-source distributions. Dividends distributed by EG_LLC to non-resident corporate shareholders are generally subject to 10% withholding under Article 58 of Law 91/2005 (treaty relief may apply); resident corporate shareholders are generally exempt under Article 52, and resident natural-person shareholders bear final 10% withholding. A foreign branch/P.E. carrying on the same laboratory activity is taxed at 22.5% on Egyptian-source profits plus a separate 10% tax on net profits remitted abroad. Tax-exempt-zone alternative: if the LLC elects a GAFI Free-Zone license, qualifying export-oriented activities may obtain exemption from CIT and VAT in exchange for an annual statutory fee to GAFI, but this regime is generally suboptimal for a dual domestic-and-export testing laboratory.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election and no entity-reclassification mechanism exists under Egyptian tax law; all LLCs are mandatorily taxed as corporations. The statutory elections available to EG_LLC include: (i) partnership/company-of-persons entities only may elect partner-level taxation under Law 91/2005 where all partners are natural persons and ETA approval is obtained; (ii) Egyptian corporate groups may elect consolidated taxation with at least 90% shareholding and ETA pre-approval; (iii) VAT-filing-period elections (annual, six-month, or quarterly periods as determined by taxpayer class) and the option to apply suspension or deferral treatment to input VAT on imported capital equipment used for production; (iv) Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 incentive elections, including special economic zones such as the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) and targeted export-oriented exemptions; (v) double-tax-treaty residence tie-breakers, which affect residency only and not entity classification. Egypt does not provide hybrid-mismatch elections; instead it applies GAAR, controlled-foreign-company rules, and transfer-pricing provisions under Law 91/2005 to neutralize artificial entity mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 — the mandatory VAT registration threshold applies where annual taxable supplies of goods and services exceed this amount in any twelve-month period (Law 67/2016, Article 16). The threshold can be adjusted by ministerial decree and does not apply to non-resident suppliers carrying out taxable activities in Egypt, who must register through a fiscal representative irrespective of turnover. EG_LLC must register when domestic B2B testing supplies exceed the threshold; exporters may register voluntarily from inception to recover input VAT on test chambers, data-acquisition systems, utilities, and calibrations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and qualifying exported services are zero-rated under Law 67/2016 and its Executive Regulation on export supplies. For EG_LLC's UN38.3 drop-testing and CE-RED electromagnetic/RF laboratory services, zero-rating is claimable where: (i) the customer is a non-resident principal established outside Egypt; (ii) the testing is physically performed in the Egyptian laboratory on batteries or samples destined for physical export, or on foreign materials temporarily imported for testing and subsequently re-exported under the temporary-admission regime; (iii) the deliverables — UN38.3 summary of tests, test reports, and CE-RED declarations/reports — are transmitted to the recipient outside Egypt; (iv) the signed contract, purchase order, and invoices characterize the activity as an export of services; and (v) the full consideration is received in convertible foreign currency credited to EG_LLC's Egyptian bank account, whether by SWIFT wire or an alternative payment rail such as Wise, provided the bank record shows an inward foreign-currency remittance traceable to the foreign client. Zero-rating is denied where the testing service is consumed in Egypt, for example where the laboratory certifies batteries solely for Egyptian domestic-market placement at the request of an Egyptian manufacturer; such supplies are standard-rated at 14%.

Mandatory ETA fiscal clearance of e-invoices and e-receipts. EG_LLC, once phased in by turnover class, must issue Arabic-language invoices (with optional English fields), convert them to the ETA XML/JSON schema, and transmit them in real-time or near-real-time via the ETA REST APIs or through ETA-certified Egyptian e-invoicing service providers. The ETA validates each transaction, applies a cryptographically verifiable ETA electronic signature, and returns a unique UUID and QR code to be printed on or embedded in the invoice; this serves as Egypt's functional equivalent of digital stamping for services. An invoice that is not cleared, or that contains mismatched data such as an incorrect TIN, wrong commodity/service classification code, or incorrect unit value, is legally treated as not issued, exposing the supplier to statutory fines and disallowance of input VAT. E-receipts are being rolled out for B2C transactions, including walk-in laboratory sales. Imported laboratory equipment is cleared through the ACI system requiring an Advance Cargo Information declaration and a unique ACID registration number; VAT is collected at customs unless suspension, AEO status, or an export-linked regime applies. Cancellations and amendments of cleared e-invoices require ETA approval through the API.

The ETA data-matches e-invoice export declarations against NAFIZA customs records, bank foreign-currency receipts, and CBE repatriation reporting. Zero-rating claims are open for reassessment for the statutory window of generally five years, extended up to six years where returns were not filed or fraud is established. Where the export substantiation fails — missing customs evidence, services consumed within Egypt, an invoice not cleared by ETA, or absent inward FX remittance — the ETA re-assesses output VAT at 14% retrospectively, reverses the attributable input VAT credits, adds the statutory delayed-tax surcharge for each month or part-month of non-payment subject to the legal cap, and imposes fixed non-compliance fines under Law 67/2016. The presentation of false customs, contract, or bank documents to support a zero-rated invoice constitutes VAT fraud and triggers criminal prosecution with imprisonment and significant fines, personal liability of the LLC's managers, suspension of ETA e-invoicing privileges, and listing on Egyptian Customs suspect registers; the false-export pattern may additionally be referred to the financial-information unit as trade-based money laundering. Separately, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days exposes EG_LLC to CBE exchange-control sanctions, denial of export-financing facilities, administrative fines, and the possible recharacterization of the outstanding receivable as Egyptian-source taxable profit subject to CIT.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP is partially convertible, operating as a unified managed-flexible exchange rate since 6 March 2024. Current-account transactions (export and import of goods and services, dividends, interest, service fees) are fully convertible through CBE-licensed banks at the interbank market rate without CBE pre-approval; banks verify underlying trade documents, CBE export declarations and tax clearances. Capital-account transactions are partially convertible: FDI inflows must be registered with GAFI and routed through licensed banks; outbound capital repatriation (loan repayments, divestment proceeds, liquidation surpluses) requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates and CBE reporting forms. Since the 2024 unified exchange-rate reform, profit/dividend remittances are no longer subject to FX allocation queues; residual hurdles are documentary rather than quantitative. Offshore EGP access remains controlled: non-residents may hold EGP balances through Non-Resident External (NRE) accounts at licensed banks, but informal foreign-currency dealing and unauthorized offshore netting are prohibited. For the EG_LLC treasury, dividend, service-fee and capital-repatriation outflows are operationally executable at the interbank rate, subject strictly to the corporate documentation pack.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation: proceeds from exported testing services must be credited to a foreign-currency account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank within 4 months from the invoice date (goods: 4 months from the bill-of-lading/airway-bill date), extendable by the CBE to up to 12 months for documented contractual exigencies. Exporter obligation: file the CBE service-export declaration with the corporate bank before/upon service delivery; the bank monitors proceeds on a per-form basis. Mandatory conversion: abolished after the March 2024 liberalization — exporters retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in USD/EUR collection accounts with no obligation to sell to the interbank market; the prior mandatory surrender ratios (50% from February 2023, reduced to 25% from March 2023) no longer apply. Proceeds may be used freely for the exporter's own import payments (offset) or sold to the bank at the daily interbank rate. Non-compliance (non-repatriation within the 4-month window) triggers CBE penalty codes, denial of further trade-finance limits and suspension of FX execution services until regularization.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — البنك المركزي المصري — the sole monetary, banking-licensing and foreign-exchange authority, operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 on the Central Bank and Banking System and Law No. 38 of 1994 on Foreign Exchange, as amended; CBE Unified AML/CFT Rules (2023) govern bank onboarding and transaction monitoring.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard Corporate Income Tax for limited liability companies under Egypt Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 as amended.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on cash dividends, bonus shares and deemed profit distributions paid by an Egyptian LLC to resident or non-resident shareholders. Liquidation distributions exceeding paid-up capital may also be treated as dividends under Egyptian law. Treaty rates may reduce the statutory rate to 5%, 10%, or 15% based on shareholder holding and DTA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 9 must be filed within four months of the end of the taxable year. For an Egyptian LLC with a December 31 fiscal year-end, the filing and final balance payment due date is April 30 of the following year. For years ending on other dates, the deadline is the last day of the fourth month following the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Egyptian Local File) is required when the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds EGP 5,000,000 in a tax year. A consolidated or Master File obligation arises when the Egyptian group turnover or ultimate parent's consolidated gross revenue exceeds approximately EGP 2,000,000,000. Additional TP disclosure schedules must be attached to Form 9 for transactions with foreign and domestic related parties.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the form used in the UAE. However, Egyptian tax law and the reorganizations of the Tax Procedures impose a substance-over-form and business purpose doctrine. A pure shell LLC without local premises, employees, equipment, management decision-making, bank accounts, and actual conduct of laboratory work may be recharacterized by ETA as tax-resident in the owner's jurisdiction or denied treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test. The company must maintain physical laboratory facilities, certified testing staff, signed test logs for UN 38.3 procedures, and CRM/ERP records proving the actual delivery of testing services in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Fine of not less than EGP 50,000 and up to three times the tax evaded, imprisonment for fraudulent tax evasion under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005, seizure of assets, revocation of tax card, and suspension of e-invoicing status. The laboratory license may also be cancelled by GAFI.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally and jointly liable for the evaded tax, subject to civil restitution, travel bans, and pre-trial asset freezing. In serious cases directors face imprisonment terms of one to ten years and direct criminal liability for issuing incorrect corporate records.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the LLC as fiscally transparent or as a foreign-held shell; full disallowance of deductions, re-assessment at 22.5% on deemed profits, denial of DTA benefits, imposition of late-payment surcharges of 2% per month, and administrative penalties for non-compliance with GAFI licenses.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced; directors and beneficial owners personally assessed for unpaid CIT and withholding taxes. Egyptian commercial courts may issue travel bans and criminal judgments when the shell was used to commit tax evasion or defraud third-party clients.
Violation of Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Rules

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80/2020: fines up to EGP 50 million for legal persons, confiscation of assets, imprisonment of senior executives from seven to twenty years, withdrawal of laboratory operation license, and delisting from Egyptian banks.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and ultimate beneficial owners face individual imprisonment, confiscation of private property, travel bans, and enterprise debarment. Bank account freezes can make ongoing UN38.3 testing operations impossible.
Failure to Withhold Dividend, Royalty, and Interest WHT

Statutory sanction: ETA assessment of the unpaid withholding tax, a delayed tax surcharge of 2% per month on the amount overdue, classification of the non-withheld tax as a corporate debt, and penalty proceedings against the withholding agent. Directors may be joined as assessed parties.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or knowingly participate in the distribution or payment without WHT are jointly and severally liable for the full tax amount, surcharges, and duplicate payment risk. Under fraud provisions, the personal assets of directors are attachable and travel bans may be issued.
