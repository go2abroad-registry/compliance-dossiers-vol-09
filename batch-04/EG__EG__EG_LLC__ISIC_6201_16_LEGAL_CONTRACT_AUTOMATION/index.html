Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum aggregate paid-up capital applies to LLCs under Law No. 159 of 1981 as amended; however, the entire share capital must be fully subscribed, allocated in equal quota-shares with equal nominal value, and may be contributed in cash or in-kind (in-kind contributions require a founders' valuation report). No blocked escrow or bank deposit certificate is required for LLC formation before the GAFI; capital sufficiency is evaluated against the business plan and the company's corporate object.

Funding & unlock path: Required – Civil Defense fire safety certificate from the General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) confirming fire extinguishers, fire alarm systems, and evacuation plans; structural and electrical inspection clearance of the leased premises by a licensed engineer; and workplace compliance with Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 for safe working conditions, ventilation, lighting, and first-aid equipment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; primary digital portal: www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt portal: invoicing.eta.gov.eg; unified corporate registration gateway administered by the General Authority for Investment (GAFI).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned LLC incorporates through the GAFI one-stop-shop, obtaining Commercial Registration, National ID, and Tax Card (TIN) in a single submission; ETA issues a 9-digit Tax Identification Number and an electronic tax file. In-person or e-registration via the unified registry triggers mandatory income tax registration within 30 days of commercial registration; VAT registration (Form 8) is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 per year and voluntary from EGP 100,000; e-invoicing onboarding with an ETA-issued digital signing certificate must follow. The LLC must also register for PAYE/social insurance (NASSCOM) and, if it has no Egyptian permanent establishment, appoint a local tax representative for withholding tax portability; all registrations require authenticated shareholder/manager ID, articles of association, and the lease/ownership proof of the Egyptian registered office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - an Egyptian LLC is a resident taxpayer taxed on worldwide income under Law No. 91 of 2005 (as amended by Law No. 30 of 2023); the standard corporate income tax rate is 22.5%, with a temporary 25% rate applied to the slice of net taxable income exceeding EGP 20 million; dividends are not deductible at the entity level and distributions to non-residents are subject to 10% withholding tax (subject to treaty relief); no territorial or pass-through election is available.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Egyptian law. Permitted statutory elections include: (i) consolidated group filing for directly or indirectly owned 90%+ affiliates, subject to ETA pre-approval; (ii) declining-balance depreciation at 125% of the straight-line rate upon ETA consent; (iii) VAT cash-basis accounting for taxpayers with annual supplies below EGP 1,000,000; (iv) ordering of 5-year loss carry-forward utilization; and (v) foreign tax credit election for creditable foreign corporate income taxes against Egyptian CIT, capped at the Egyptian effective rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies (mandatory registration, Article 15 of Law 67/2016); EGP 100,000 annual taxable supplies (voluntary registration); a non-resident supplier deriving Egyptian-source digital or SaaS revenue must register without a local threshold under the deemed-supplier rules for electronically supplied services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated at 0% under Article 29 of Law No. 67/2016. For the LegalTech SaaS/e-signature scope (ISIC 6201), zero-rating of cross-border services is available when: (i) the recipient is a non-resident without an Egyptian permanent establishment; (ii) the service is wholly performed and the economic benefit is realized outside Egypt, with no connection to Egyptian real estate, goods, or taxable activities; (iii) the consideration is received in freely convertible foreign currency and credited to an Egyptian bank account; and (iv) the pricing is at arm's length and documented for transfer pricing. Exporters of zero-rated supplies may reclaim attributable input VAT; refunds are processed through the ETA's electronic refund system, with a statutory 45-day review window for complete claims.

  • Customs export declaration (Bayan) evidencing physical exit from Egypt, with ETA customs matching codes
  • Signed contract and itemized commercial invoice describing the exact digital/e-signature service and its non-Egyptian beneficiary
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate (Foreign Currency Remittance Advice) from an Egyptian bank showing settlement of the export consideration
  • Proof of offshore consumption, including IP address/geo-location of the beneficiary's usage for SaaS authentication events, service-level logs, and offshore server/delivery records
  • Zero-rated supply register (export ledger) reconciling e-invoices to banking and customs data

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance under ETA Decision No. 318 of 2020 and subsequent phased rollouts. All VAT-registered taxpayers, including non-resident e-service suppliers, must issue invoices in the ETA-approved XML or JSON schema, digitally sign each invoice with the ETA-issued certificate, transmit it via REST/SOAP API for instantaneous validation, and include the ETA-returned QR code in the customer-facing document; B2C transactions require the separate e-receipt stream. Cleared e-invoices are the exclusive basis for input VAT deduction; non-clearance or manual substitution invalidates the invoice and triggers penalties.

ETA conducts data-driven audits reconciling e-invoice clearance files, customs declarations, banking remittance records, and server logs; zero-rating claims lacking full substantiation are rejected and the VAT becomes payable with monthly delay tax at 1.5% of the unpaid amount. Under Article 72 of Law 67/2016, fraudulent export claims carry administrative fines, retrospective clawback of all refunded input VAT for a 5-year look-back period, and criminal liability: imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of not less than EGP 20,000 and not more than EGP 200,000, or the full amount of evaded tax, whichever is greater; repeat offenders face doubled penalties and debarment from zero-rated export treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current-account transactions are effectively fully convertible: SaaS subscription receipts, software-licensing fees, e-signature service invoices, and other service-export proceeds can be credited directly to CBE-licensed Egyptian bank accounts in USD or other currencies without prior CBE approval, subject only to standard documentary verification (invoice, contract, proof of service delivery). Capital-account transactions are restricted in form rather than substance: (i) foreign direct investment (FDI) equity injections require registration via CBE Form 10, (ii) shareholder or intercompany loans require Form 12, (iii) profit and dividend remittance requires a valid CBE registration of the original injection, Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax-clearance certificates, audited financial statements, and board resolution, and (iv) non-resident settlement through offshore accounts is prohibited for export receipts — all export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt. Since the March 2024 exchange-rate unification, there is no mandatory conversion of foreign currency to EGP and no surrender requirement; banks execute customer conversions at market-determined rates with no CBE price ceiling. Real remittance friction persists in documentary lead times of 3 to 10 business days rather than convertibility refusal.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation window: 100% of each export invoice value must be received in Egypt through a licensed bank within 120 days (four months) from the export documentation date — bill of lading for goods, or tax invoice/service completion certificate for digital services (applicable to LegalTech SaaS and e-signature licensing). Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% — under CBE Board Decree No. 564 of 2022 (effective 1 January 2023), all export proceeds may be retained in the exporter's foreign-currency account and no surrender to the banking system is mandated; prior percentage-based surrender rules were abolished. Extensions beyond 120 days are grantable by the CBE through the relationship bank upon written justification (long-term SaaS contracts, milestone billing). Late or partial repatriation subjects the exporter to CBE penalties, loss of Export Support Fund privileges, and bank-level debarment of the account's foreign-currency clearing privileges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Arabic: البنك المركزي المصري; supreme regulator under Law No. 88 of 2003 (Central Banking, Banking, and Monetary System Law), as amended by Law No. 162 of 2020; sole authority for foreign-exchange licensing, banking licensing, balance-of-payments reporting, and administration of Egypt's unified flexible exchange-rate regime (floating since March 2024 under the IMF Extended Fund Facility).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MODERATE as a supplementary collection/clearing sub-ledger. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) are not CBE-licensed banks and cannot issue local Egyptian IBANs. CBE Law No. 88 of 2003 requires all export proceeds of Egyptian-resident entities to be repatriated to and settled through a licensed Egyptian bank; therefore, aggregate settlement received by an EMI must be swept (MT103 or SWIFT gpi) into the Egyptian bank account to satisfy the 120-day export-proceeds rule. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot open EGP-denominated accounts for the Egyptian entity; (ii) no CBE Forms 10/12 registration can be executed through an EMI; (iii) Egyptian Data Protection Law No. 151/2020 and CBE payment-systems rules impose data localization and transaction-record requirements that offshore EMIs typically cannot meet for full statutory compliance; (iv) Stripe/Card receipts flowing through a foreign EMI must be mapped to underlying export invoices to avoid an 'unexplained foreign credit' AML flag. Recommended architecture: Stripe/Card settlement → offshore EMI/collection account → same-day SWIFT sweep → Egyptian Tier-1 bank (CIB or NBE) → EGP conversion via interbank market.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection (FDI equity or shareholder loan) is routed exclusively via SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) from the foreign investor's bank to the CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, with instruction tags specifying: (a) beneficiary — EG_LLC's dedicated capital-escrow or foreign-currency account; (b) purpose code — E1 (equity with no option feature) or E2 (equity with option) for FDI, or D1-D8 for loan categories per CBE Balance-of-Payments reporting; (c) Remittance Information field 70: 'CAPITAL INJECTION — EG_LLC — CBE FORM 10' (or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN — FORM 12'). The receiving bank performs (i) legal-entity verification against commercial registry and CBE sanction lists, (ii) source-of-funds verification against the remitter name, (iii) negative-news screening, and (iv) issuance of the CBE Exchange Control registration certificate within 5 to 10 business days. Conversion to EGP is optional at market rate; full retention in USD is permissible. Verification package: notarized board resolution, updated Articles of Association showing issued capital, tax clearance certificate, and audited opening balance sheet.For a LegalTech SaaS exporter, outbound trade payments are service-import disbursements (cloud infrastructure, e-signature certificate authorities, legal-content databases, sub-processing vendors). Recommended rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 (customer direct transfer) with CBE service-import purpose code and the underlying invoice/contract annexed to the bank's e-filing portal; (ii) for larger or letter-of-credit-mandated purchases — commercial documentary L/C (UCP600) with MT700 issuance; (iii) SEPA/SWIFT gpi for euro-denominated sub-vendor payments. Each outbound payment must be cross-referenced to a registered CBE debit note or related inbound capital inflow to evidence balance-of-payments matching; withholding tax (15% for technical-service fees to non-residents, 20% for royalties, subject to treaty reduction under Egypt's double-taxation network) must be certified by ETA Form 41 before bank release. EGP/USD conversion is executed automatically from the USD account using the bank's published market rate.Deliverable OTC FX forward contracts (USD/EGP, EUR/EGP) with CBE-licensed commercial banks, tenors up to 12 months, backed by documented underlying commercial or investment exposure (SaaS export invoices, capital-injection FX commitments).Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP or EUR/EGP) executed with licensed banks for medium-term hedging of subscription-contract receivables and intercompany loan revaluation risk.OTC FX options (call/put on EGP) purchased solely from licensed Egyptian banks — option-premium payments are permissible, while writing or selling options is restricted.Natural hedging via staggered multi-currency liquidity pools (EGP/USD/EUR) and matched invoicing — maintaining USD-denominated SaaS contracts to offset EGP depreciation without derivative cost.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MODERATE as a supplementary collection/clearing sub-ledger. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Platform, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, Airwallex) are not CBE-licensed banks and cannot issue local Egyptian IBANs. CBE Law No. 88 of 2003 requires all export proceeds of Egyptian-resident entities to be repatriated to and settled through a licensed Egyptian bank; therefore, aggregate settlement received by an EMI must be swept (MT103 or SWIFT gpi) into the Egyptian bank account to satisfy the 120-day export-proceeds rule. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot open EGP-denominated accounts for the Egyptian entity; (ii) no CBE Forms 10/12 registration can be executed through an EMI; (iii) Egyptian Data Protection Law No. 151/2020 and CBE payment-systems rules impose data localization and transaction-record requirements that offshore EMIs typically cannot meet for full statutory compliance; (iv) Stripe/Card receipts flowing through a foreign EMI must be mapped to underlying export invoices to avoid an 'unexplained foreign credit' AML flag. Recommended architecture: Stripe/Card settlement → offshore EMI/collection account → same-day SWIFT sweep → Egyptian Tier-1 bank (CIB or NBE) → EGP conversion via interbank market.
