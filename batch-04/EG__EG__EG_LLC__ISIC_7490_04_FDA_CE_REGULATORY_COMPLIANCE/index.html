Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Egyptian LLC has no express statutory minimum capital; however, the capital must be divided into equal quotas with a nominal value not less than EGP 100 (or more as set in the Company Contract) and must be sufficient to achieve the corporate objects. The paid-up portion must be deposited into a temporary capital subscription escrow account at a licensed Egyptian bank, and the bank-issued Capital Deposit Certificate must be submitted to GAFI. Non-resident ownership is permitted; in-kind contributions require independent valuation.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory Civil Defense fire-safety certificate issued by the General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) based on a physical inspection of fire extinguishers, emergency exits, electrical load, and evacuation routes; a structural load and occupancy clearance from the local municipal engineering department verifying safe floor loading; and a workplace occupational safety statement covering ventilation, wiring safe, and emergency lighting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to non-residents and services provided to non-resident recipients that are performed and consumed entirely outside Egypt qualify for 0% VAT. Substantiation requires evidenced physical export or offshore service delivery, supported by contracts and FX remittance records.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Egypt mandates e-invoicing with real-time ETA clearance through API integration. Invoices must be digitally stamped and authenticated via the ETA e-invoicing system before issuance; failure to clear invoices triggers non-deductibility and penalties.

ETA may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and claw back unpaid VAT plus late interest and penalties (up to 100% of tax due) for fraudulent or improperly documented export claims. Criminal penalties may apply for deliberate evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains liberalized current-account convertibility under the CBE-managed float adopted in 2024. Payments for services, royalties, dividends, profit remittances, and commercial settlements are convertible through authorized banks when supported by underlying contracts, invoices, tax clearance, and economic-substance evidence. Capital-account transactions such as equity injections, shareholder loans, liquidation proceeds, and onward foreign investment are permitted only through CBE-licensed authorized dealers and are subject to documentary verification, balance-of-payments classification, and, in certain cases, CBE approval. Profit remittance requires audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority, and the LLC's board/shareholder resolution approving the distribution; no fixed annual remittance quota exists under the current regime, but CBE and the bank will independently verify the underlying distributable profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE directives require exporters of goods to repatriate export proceeds through an authorized Egyptian bank within 90 days of shipment date or the documented commercial payment due date. The prevailing mandatory surrender/conversion ratio is 100% of export receipts, converted at the interbank market rate, unless CBE grants a specific retention allowance for defined operational expenses. Service exporters, including regulatory consulting providers, must similarly route international receipts through an authorized bank; the bank records the proceeds under the applicable current-account service code and applies CBE conversion requirements. Supporting evidence includes customs declarations, signed service agreements, invoices, proof of delivery of regulatory deliverables, SWIFT receipts, and bank credit advices.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), its implementing regulations, and CBE foreign-exchange circulars and exchange-control directives.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Egyptian law does not recognize offshore fintech EMIs as banks, clearing institutions, or local payment-service providers. Only CBE-licensed banks and authorized exchange/payment intermediaries may hold EGP accounts, issue Egyptian IBANs, and clear SWIFT or local currency payments. Offshore EMIs cannot serve as the primary liquidity rail for inbound capital, trade settlements, or dividend remittances. They may be used only as an invoicing or payment-orchestration front end that routes funds to a licensed Egyptian bank account; using them as a standalone treasury solution presents material regulatory, sanctions, and AML risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Incoming equity capital must be remitted from the shareholder's own account at a recognized foreign bank to the LLC's CBE-licensed account in Egypt. The wire must clearly state 'Capital Contribution / Equity Injection' and the LLC's Commercial Registration Number. The receiving bank requires a notarized shareholder resolution approving the capital increase, updated articles of association, and a shareholder declaration identifying the ultimate funder. The transaction is classified under the CBE balance-of-payments code for foreign direct investment in a non-bank Egyptian company, and the bank will issue an inward-investment certificate after verification. Current-account service receipts, including FDA/CE consulting fees from non-resident clients, must be routed with the applicable 'Business and Management Consulting Services' purpose code; the signed engagement letter, scoped deliverable description, and evidence of service performance must be maintained at the bank for transaction-level audit.Service disbursements, such as FDA filing fees, CE technical-file review fees, and overseas legal counsel payments, should be paid by SWIFT MT103 through the LLC's authorized dealing bank using the underlying invoice and contract as clearing support. For goods-related transactions connected to medical-device pilot production or prototype acquisition, commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) may be issued through a CBE-licensed bank, while low-value, repetitive purchases may use direct MT103 remittance if the bank's transaction-monitoring rules tolerate open-account terms. All outbound payments require purpose-code declaration, invoice certification, and, where applicable, tax withholding or transfer-pricing documentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File required when related-party transactions exceed EGP 5 million per transaction or EGP 10 million aggregate; Master File threshold: group consolidated revenue exceeding EGP 200 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no offshore-style ESR regime, but tax law and CFC rules require substantial business purpose; the Tax Authority may apply general anti-abuse rules to disregard entities lacking commercial substance, and Art. 119(bis) of Tax Law 91/2005 (as amended) targets controlled foreign corporations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EGP 200,000 plus 100% of evaded tax; imprisonment up to 5 years; forced closure and blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for evaded tax if personally involved; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; potential imprisonment for 3–5 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and tax credits; reclassification of income as taxable distribution; penalty of 30% of the reclassified amount.
