No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Egyptian Companies Law; the declared paid-up capital must be fully evidenced by a bank deposit certificate or an in-kind contribution valuation approved by GAFI, and the total subscribed capital may be paid within five years from incorporation, except for cash contributions which must be paid at the time of formation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; unified tax registration and e-services portal: https://www.tax.gov.eg; mandatory government e-invoicing platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

For an Egyptian LLC, the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN / Tax Card) is issued through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) one-stop-shop at incorporation; the entity must then activate its tax file with the competent ETA office and complete VAT registration (Law 67/2016) within 30 days of commencing taxable activity or immediately once annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000. A foreign entity without local incorporation obtains a non-resident TIN/VAT ID via direct application at the ETA Large Taxpayer Center or through a fiscal representative when it creates a permanent establishment in Egypt; registration must be completed before the first taxable supply or invoice is issued. The unified TIN governs CIT, VAT, withholding tax, stamp duty, and e-invoicing obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or entity classification election exists under Egyptian law; classification is statutorily determined, and an LLC is mandatorily a separate taxable person subject to corporate income tax under Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 30/2023. Available elections include (i) choice of depreciation method for fixed assets under the Income Tax Law; (ii) application for an Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) under Article 56 of Law 91/2005 for transfer pricing certainty; (iii) formal election for special economic zone / free zone incentives (e.g., Suez Canal Economic Zone) at the licensing stage; and (iv) group companies are taxed as separate entities with no consolidated filing election. Hybrid mismatch rules have not been enacted, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 52 of Law 91/2005) and transfer pricing articles empower the ETA to recharacterize artificial structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 (annual taxable turnover of goods and services) for mandatory registration; voluntary registration is available from EGP 50,000; non-resident suppliers with a fixed establishment, branch, or permanent establishment in Egypt must register irrespective of the turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies under Article 21 of Law 67/2016 to (i) goods physically exported from Egypt to a non-resident buyer, including food products and kitchen supplies if actually shipped and removed from the Egyptian customs territory; and (ii) offshore services rendered to a non-resident recipient and economically consumed outside Egypt, subject to the substantiation conditions of Executive Regulation Decree 66/2017. The Egyptian LLC must maintain a complete export file evidencing contract, shipment, and foreign currency proceeds; domestic sales through delivery aggregator platforms such as UberEats/Careem and DoorDash remain standard-rated supplies at 14% VAT and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs export declaration (ACI/Nafeza S-Declaration or C4 certificate) evidencing removal from the Egyptian customs territory
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or multimodal transport document issued to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list in Arabic/English matching the customs declaration
  • Sales contract or purchase order naming the non-resident counterparty
  • Foreign currency inward remittance certificate (bank credit advice, T/T, or D/A-D/P settlement certificate) confirming proceeds repatriation through an Egyptian bank
  • ETA export VAT refund claim file and zero-rating register reconciled with e-invoicing records

Statutory B2B e-invoicing is mandatory for all ETA-registered taxpayers under Law 67/2016 as amended by Law 26/2020 and implementing ETA decisions. Invoices must be transmitted in XML (UBL 2.1) to the government e-invoicing platform (einvoicing.eta.gov.eg), digitally signed with the taxpayer's ETA-issued digital certificate, and processed through the regulated API/PSD integration. The ETA performs real-time validation and clearance and returns a QR-coded stamped invoice that the buyer must accept before posting; paper or non-cleared invoices are fiscally invalid. Phased B2C e-receipt mandates also apply to retail and delivery transactions, including restaurant and cloud kitchen sales via aggregator platforms, with non-compliance triggering penalties under Law 67/2016 and the unified tax procedure rules.

The ETA conducts pre-refund desk audits of export claims, cross-checking customs data (ACI/Nafeza), transport documents, and bank FX records; refunds are normally payable within 45 days of a complete application, with statutory delay interest where exceeded. If export documentation, shipment, or FX repatriation is defective, the zero-rating is retroactively revoked and 14% VAT is clawed back with interest from the original filing date, plus a monthly delay penalty of 2% and fines of up to 100% of evaded tax under Law 67/2016 Articles 38-42; submission of forged or false documents triggers criminal prosecution, imprisonment, blacklisting by the ETA, and loss of export refund privileges. The GAAR (Article 52 of Law 91/2005) further permits recharacterization of circular or sham export arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations; current-account transactions are freely settleable at the interbank market rate. Capital-account transactions are formally liberalized for foreign direct investment in Egyptian LLCs, with 100% non-resident ownership permitted. Profit and dividend remittances are executed through licensed banks using CBE declaration forms, supported by audited financial statements, Egyptian Tax Authority clearance, and proof that the investment was registered with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI). The 2022-2024 FX shortage period caused de facto rationing and a parallel-market premium; following the March 2024 exchange-rate unification and IMF-supported stabilization, convertibility for trade and investment flows has normalized, though banks retain enhanced due-diligence discretion over the source of EGP liquidity. The LLC must maintain a complete documentary trail of inward shareholder funds, EGP tax compliance, and audited financial records to withstand any future FX-rationing inquiry.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Unified Foreign Exchange Rules, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into an Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the shipment date (bill of lading or airway bill) or, for services, within 90 days from the invoice date, unless the CBE approves an extension. There is currently no fixed statutory percentage of mandatory conversion into EGP; exporters may retain proceeds in a domestic foreign-currency account or sell them through the interbank market. Documentary prerequisites include the export customs declaration, commercial invoice, transport document, and, where applicable, the settlement statement of the foreign delivery platform. Hard-currency receipts of the ghost-kitchen LLC from offshore aggregators such as DoorDash or UberEats USD settlement legs are subject to the same 90-day repatriation rule; local EGP collections from Egyptian platforms do not constitute export proceeds for this regime.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the sole monetary and foreign-exchange authority under Law No. 88 of 2003 as amended by Law No. 194 of 2020, acting through its Foreign Exchange Market Regulations, Unified Banking Rules, and AML/CFT framework. CBE-licensed commercial banks are the only permitted counterparties for corporate FX dealing, statutory reporting, and cross-border remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for use as the primary statutory treasury account; Medium only as a documented settlement bridge for foreign aggregator payouts. An Egyptian LLC must maintain its paid-up capital deposit, operational payroll, and FX-repatriation flows with a CBE-licensed bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business are not CBE-licensed banks and cannot issue the statutory capital-deposit certificate or process CBE-regulated remittances. Balances parked offshore beyond the 90-day repatriation window may be characterized as an unapproved export retention, attracting AML red flags. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires an SWIFT MT103, or MT202 COV for nostro-mediated tranches, from the foreign parent to the LLC's pre-incorporation deposit account at a CBE-licensed bank. The remittance narrative must include the GAFI incorporation-file number, the CBE balance-of-payments purpose classification for Direct Investment Equity, and the LLC's future Egyptian tax identification number. The receiving bank issues the statutory capital-deposit certificate, which is a mandatory annex to the Commercial Registry application. After the Commercial Registry certificate is issued, the pre-incorporation account is converted into the permanent EGP operating account, and shareholder funds may be converted into EGP at the interbank rate. Subsequent injections by way of share premium or shareholder loan must be recorded in the bank's transaction-monitoring system and in the CBE's FDI and external-debt reporting gateway; shareholder loans require registration to permit later interest and principal repatriation without adverse FX characterization.Outbound treasury flows for operating expenses such as bulk raw-food procurement, cloud-kitchen software subscriptions, aggregator commissions, royalties, and freight are settled through: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct remittances under open-account trading terms; (2) documentary commercial letters of credit issued per UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 for larger imported inventory and equipment; and (3) SWIFT MT202 COV where clearing is effected through nostro relationships. Each outbound payment must cite the underlying contract and invoice, an ETA-compliant e-invoice or e-receipt, the customs ACID import record for physical goods, and a tax-clearance or withholding certificate where Egyptian WHT applies to royalties, technical-service fees, or interest. Aggregator commission payments to offshore platform groups must be supported by the marketplace agreement and the platform's settlement statement. Purpose codes follow the CBE balance-of-payments reporting manual: goods in the 100-series, services and commissions in the 200-series, income in the 300-series, and transfers in the 400-series.Deliverable EGP/USD forward contract with a maximum tenor of approximately 12 months, supported by an authenticated underlying trade or investment exposure and margin or collateral per bank credit policyOnshore FX swap combining a spot and forward leg between EGP and USD or EUR to match working-capital receivable and payable timing of aggregator settlementsCross-currency swap between EGP and USD or EUR for medium-term balance-sheet hedging, requiring CBE transaction approval and documented foreign-investment or trade-flow coverOTC European-style FX options offered by tier-1 banks for hedging forecasted aggregator revenues, subject to non-speculative underwriting and periodic position reportingOffshore non-deliverable EGP forwards are not directly available to the resident LLC without a CBE license; the permissible onshore equivalent is the deliverable forward, while NDFs operate only among non-resident counterparties

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for use as the primary statutory treasury account; Medium only as a documented settlement bridge for foreign aggregator payouts. An Egyptian LLC must maintain its paid-up capital deposit, operational payroll, and FX-repatriation flows with a CBE-licensed bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business are not CBE-licensed banks and cannot issue the statutory capital-deposit certificate or process CBE-regulated remittances. Balances parked offshore beyond the 90-day repatriation window may be characterized as an unapproved export retention, attracting AML red flags. If an EMI is used, it must be registered under the provider's own KYC process, and every sweep to the Egyptian bank must be accompanied by the relevant platform settlement report and payment purpose code.

Box 1 – Tax Card and Identification Company name, Egyptian tax card number, commercial registration number, head office address and the competent ETA tax office. The Egyptian branch or permanent establishment (PE) completes this box in its own name using the ETA-issued tax card and the registered Egyptian branch address; a non-resident with no Egyptian PE and no Egyptian-sourced attributable profit does not file Form No. 45 and is instead subject to final withholding.
