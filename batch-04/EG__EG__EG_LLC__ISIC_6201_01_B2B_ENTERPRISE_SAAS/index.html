Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) One-Stop Shop, in coordination with the Commercial Registry (Qism Al-Tuguari) under the Ministry of Trade and Industry.

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended by Law No. 4 of 2018 and Law No. 5 of 2022; LLC formation, capital, management, and registration provisions (Articles 1, 4, 7, 10-15, 26-35); read with Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations (Prime Ministerial Decree 2310/2017).

Formation / constitutional filings

Contract of Incorporation and Articles of Association (Aqd Ta'sis wa Al-Nizam Al-Dakhili) registered in the Commercial Registry (Qayd Al-Sijil Al-Tijari).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Law No. 159/1981; subscribed capital must be fully paid in cash deposited to a bank account in the company's name or in-kind appraised by an accredited evaluator. No statutory escrow of capital is required; the GAFI clearance requires submission of the bank certificate of deposit at incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Law No. 43/1979 fire-safety certificate for the registered office; building structural safety clearance endorsed by a registered engineer; workplace occupational health clearance under Labor Law No. 12/2003.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (for VAT/e-invoicing), and the Egyptian Taxpayers Registration System accessible via the ETA's online services; for customs, the Egypt Customs Authority (ECA) under the Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Egyptian LLC must obtain: (1) a Commercial Registration certificate from the Commercial Registry; (2) a tax card (TIN) from the Egyptian Tax Authority; (3) a VAT registration certificate (Value Added Tax ID) if taxable supplies exceed the statutory registration threshold or if the entity is a direct exporter; (4) a file number for withholding tax purposes. For a non-resident entity without a permanent establishment, registration for VAT and withholding tax may be required only where a direct taxable supply or e-commerce nexus exists. The ETA requires submission of the company incorporation documents, lease/ownership proof of premises, passport copies of managers, and bank account opening certificate. Registration can be completed online through the ETA portal or in person at the relevant tax office. The LLC's TIN (Tax Identification Number) is issued without minimum capital requirement; a 5-10 business day processing time is standard.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt applies a unitary national tax regime without check-the-box elections for LLCs. An Egyptian LLC is a resident entity taxed at the entity level on worldwide income. Hybrid mismatch elections are not generally available; however, thin capitalization and transfer pricing rules apply. Tax consolidation is not permitted for corporate income tax purposes. An LLC may elect to treat certain branches as separate accounting units only for local tax filing convenience, but this does not alter entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (for goods and services, excluding exempt supplies and capital assets). Foreign digital service providers supplying B2B SaaS from outside Egypt are subject to VAT registration and collection obligations regardless of the threshold once making taxable supplies to non-registered customers in Egypt; for B2B supplies, the reverse charge mechanism applies when the recipient is VAT-registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated for Egyptian VAT, provided the exporter is a VAT-registered taxpayer and holds documentary evidence of the actual export. For B2B SaaS and other digital services, zero-rating applies if the service is exported to a recipient resident outside Egypt, provided the recipient is registered in a foreign jurisdiction and the place of supply is outside Egypt. The taxpayer must show that the intellectual property or digital service is used entirely outside Egypt, and that the export is supported by a written contract, proof of foreign currency payment from overseas, and evidence that the service is not consumed locally.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via Egypt Customs Authority — Nafeza platform)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for physical goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate proving receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Foreign Currency Bank Account Statement
  • Service Agreement or SaaS Subscription Confirmation for exported digital services
  • ETA-approved export documentation checklist and underlying transfer pricing documentation if applicable

Egypt mandates a real-time e-invoicing and e-receipt system operated by the ETA. All VAT-registered taxpayers must issue invoices through the ETA-approved e-invoice portal or certified integration system, transmitting invoice data to the ETA for fiscal validation and stamping in near-real-time (typically within 24 hours). Foreign e-commerce and SaaS suppliers are required to integrate with the ETA e-invoicing API to issue compliant electronic invoices compliant with the Egyptian schema. Clearance rules require issuance within the statutory timeframe for each supply; failure to transmit or validate invoices triggers fines and suspension of tax clearance certificates.

The ETA may audit export zero-rating claims for up to five years. Failure to remit foreign currency within the 180-day deadline or use of ineligible documentation results in disallowance of the zero rate, assessment of VAT at 14% on the full invoice value, plus late payment penalties of 3% per month or fraction thereof (cumulative, capped at 100% of the tax). Fraudulent zero-rating claims (e.g., fake export invoices, overstated foreign remittances) are subject to tax evasion penalties of up to 200% of the evaded tax plus imprisonment penalties under Law No. 67 of 2016 and Law No. 73 of 2022. The ETA also imposes fines for e-invoice non-compliance, between EGP 20,000 and EGP 100,000 per invoice, in addition to clawback of VAT refunds and interest at the statutory rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime under the Articles of Agreement of the CBE and the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Current-account payments, including software-subscription fees paid to an Egyptian pure-play B2B Enterprise SaaS provider, are generally convertibility-friendly and may be settled through local commercial banks using the standard corporate foreign-currency account and SWIFT MT103 infrastructure. Capital-account transactions, such as the initial ISIS of the LLC's registered share capital, direct shareholder loans, and future equity injections, are not unconditionally convertible and require a purpose-clearing submission to the Corporate Banking division of the receiving bank together with supporting corporate registry and, where applicable, GAFI investment approvals. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permitted upon satisfaction of the annual audit requirement, payment or clearance of applicable withholding tax under Article 56 of the Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, submission of the audited financial statements and a notarised board resolution declaring distributable profits. In practice, the CBE may impose documentary verification on the underlying SaaS agreement to distinguish genuine subscription/turnkey income from IP royalty leakage; a fully executed master SaaS terms sheet with merchant-identification, usage volumes and price schedule, together with local tax forms, will materially de-risk the repatriation application.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds in respect of goods and services, including B2B Enterprise SaaS subscription fees, must be collected in an allowable foreign currency and transferred directly to the exporter's foreign-currency account maintained with an Egyptian bank. For physical goods, the default statutory settlement window is generally the earlier of the contractual credit period or 180 days from the bill of lading/export date unless the CBE grants an explicit extension; for services/software exports, the receivable must be settled within the period specified in the underlying service agreement, which should not exceed one year from the invoice date, and late settlements require bank-level justification. The permanent statutory principle is that 100% of export value must be repatriated to Egypt before a surrender credit is registered. During periodic liquidity-balance interventions the CBE has imposed mandatory conversion ratios (frequently 50% of eligible export proceeds at the prevailing market rate), and the exact percentage in force at the moment of collection must be observed based on the current CBE Circular; the bank will automatically apply the mandatory conversion and credit the corresponding EGP amount to the operational account.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 on the Central Bank and Banking System and the Foreign Exchange Regulations, Circulars and Executive Instructions issued thereunder; the local unit is the Egyptian Pound (EGP). The CBE is the sole competent authority for exchange-control policy, licensing of banks, operation of the banking file for inward foreign investment and administration of the interbank foreign-exchange market.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% default Egyptian dividend withholding tax for non-resident recipients not covered by an exemption. A 0% rate applies to non-resident corporate shareholders that are beneficial owners and qualify for the Article 48 participation exemption by holding at least 20% of the Egyptian LLC's capital or voting rights for a minimum six-month period. Treaty rates for non-qualifying shareholders range from 5% to 15% depending on the DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 aggregate annual related-party controlled transactions triggers the mandatory Egyptian Local File and contemporaneous TP study. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the Egyptian LLC belongs to an MNE group with consolidated annual group revenue of at least EGP 2,000,000,000, applying OECD-aligned Egyptian Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a stand-alone code. However, Egyptian tax law and the ETA apply OECD-aligned substance-over-form principles, judicial anti-abuse doctrine, and the Egyptian GAAR to disregard letterbox companies and deny treaty benefits or intercompany deductions. A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must demonstrate real premises, an active management board, Egyptian payroll, decision-making functions, operational risk control, and sufficient substance to justify its entitlement to Egyptian DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: The ETA will re-assess the full taxable income and impose the outstanding CIT plus the monthly delay surcharge of 2% per month, capped at 50% of the tax; criminal liability under Law 91/2005 carries imprisonment of 3 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000, together with confiscation and asset attachment.

Director personal liability: Courts and the ETA may pierce the corporate veil where the LLC is used as a sham. Directors and de facto managers are jointly and personally liable for evaded tax, penalties, and civil restitution; upon initiation of criminal proceedings, travel bans, freezing of bank accounts, and seizure of personal assets of the board members may be ordered.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA will disregard the Egyptian LLC's personality for CIT purposes, attribute the SaaS revenue to the foreign parent's Egyptian permanent establishment, subject that revenue to 22.5% CIT, disallow intercompany management and royalty deductions, and impose the 10%-20% WHT on deemed profit repatriation; late payment surcharges and penalties are added.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns for the shell entity may be treated as de facto representatives and held civilly liable for the corporate tax debt; the Egyptian tax execution law permits precautionary travel bans and attachment of directors' personal assets when a company is found to lack real existence and liability is mapped to the controllers.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and False Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: The ETA will make a transfer pricing adjustment to the Egyptian LLC's taxable income, disallow overstated deductions, recompute CIT at 22.5%, and impose the 2% per month delay surcharge (max 50%). Willful submission of false TP documentation is treated as tax evasion, attracting imprisonment of 3 to 5 years and fines of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who endorse false or non-contemporaneous TP declarations may be prosecuted for abetting tax evasion; the law permits the ETA to collect the additional tax, surcharges, and fines from directors where the LLC is undercapitalised or used as an instrument of the fraud, and to impose asset freezes and travel limitations.
Withholding Tax / Profit Repatriation Violation

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian remitter (LLC) becomes directly liable to pay the WHT not withheld, plus the monthly 2% late-payment surcharge up to a maximum of 50%; the amount can be assessed as a debt against the company and the expense may be disallowed for CIT purposes, increasing the 22.5% tax burden.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute an unlawful remittance without withholding are jointly and severally liable to the ETA for the tax that should have been withheld; tax execution procedure can be directed at the bank accounts and personal assets of the responsible officers, and travel bans are routinely issued for unresolved withholding debts.
AML / CFT Breaches Involving B2B SaaS Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Violations of Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002, as amended, attract imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines of up to EGP 10,000,000; money laundering or terrorism-financing related conduct may additionally result in confiscation of funds and accounts, suspension of the entity's commercial licence, and publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated AML compliance officer face personal criminal responsibility, imprisonment, and financial penalties for failing to implement adequate AML controls; assets and bank accounts under their control may be seized, and a travel ban can be imposed pending investigation or trial.
