Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) of the Ministry of Finance; unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) issuance via the ETA electronic tax portal (eta.gov.eg) and the e-invoicing system (einvoice.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned LLC must apply through the ETA electronic registration portal within 30 days of commencing business or establishing a permanent establishment, submitting the notarized and Arabic-legalized incorporation deed, power of attorney to the registered tax agent, signed lease or address certificate, passport copies of managers and directors, and a bank account confirmation (minimum capital is not statutorily mandated); the ETA issues a single unified TIN covering income tax, VAT, and stamp duty within approximately 15-30 working days of complete filing; non-resident software and email-infrastructure suppliers without a PE must register for VAT only if they make taxable supplies deemed situated in Egypt (notably B2C electronic and digital services), and the ETA may require designation of an Egyptian resident tax representative

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned LLC must apply through the ETA electronic registration portal within 30 days of commencing business or establishing a permanent establishment, submitting the notarized and Arabic-legalized incorporation deed, power of attorney to the registered tax agent, signed lease or address certificate, passport copies of managers and directors, and a bank account confirmation (minimum capital is not statutorily mandated); the ETA issues a single unified TIN covering income tax, VAT, and stamp duty within approximately 15-30 working days of complete filing; non-resident software and email-infrastructure suppliers without a PE must register for VAT only if they make taxable supplies deemed situated in Egypt (notably B2C electronic and digital services), and the ETA may require designation of an Egyptian resident tax representative

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt provides no check-the-box or pass-through election for LLCs; the available elective mechanisms are: (i) cash-basis accounting for small taxpayers whose annual turnover does not exceed EGP 10 million, (ii) consolidated corporate tax group filing for a resident parent holding at least 95% of subsidiaries, (iii) the VAT credit refund or offset election for exporters, and (iv) group VAT registration; hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized by mandatory disallowance rules enacted under BEPS Action 2 via law amendments effective 2023, without elective relief

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies for mandatory VAT registration, with voluntary registration permitted from EGP 50,000; non-resident suppliers of electronic and digital services are registrable without local turnover threshold when the place of supply is deemed to be Egypt and the recipient is a non-registered private person or an Egyptian taxable person

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods and services under Law No. 67/2016 when the customer is a non-resident, consideration is received in foreign currency, and the service (including email newsletter infrastructure, transactional email APIs, and hosting platforms) is effectively used or consumed outside Egypt; the exporter must hold a written export contract or subscription agreement, service delivery evidence, and a bank documentary credit or remittance record; input VAT on purchases attributable to such exports is creditable and refundable through the ETA VAT refund mechanism subject to a desk audit

  • Customs export declaration in the Single Administrative Document format, processed electronically through the NAFIZA national single window and stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent Certificate of Non-Physical Delivery evidencing transfer of title or offshore provision of the digital and email-infrastructure service
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian bank documenting receipt of the foreign-currency export proceeds

Mandatory national e-Invoice and e-Receipt regime with real-time fiscal clearance through the ETA Central Data Center (CDC); registered taxpayers are onboarded in phases by turnover band and must transmit invoice JSON/XML files via approved APIs (or the ETA portal for micro-taxpayers), receive a Unique Identification Code (UUID), and embed a QR code or digital stamp before the invoice becomes legally valid; B2B invoices submitted for a given day are cleared on a near-real-time basis, rejected files attract automatic suspension of VAT account privileges, and e-receipt obligations apply to B2C supplies in the retail and service sectors

Zero-rated export claims are subject to ETA ex-post documentary audits and desk reviews of the full proof bundle; if export status is not substantiated, the tax authority claws back the VAT credit with a 2% per month late-payment charge, imposes an additional penalty of up to 40% of the tax evaded, and, upon a finding of forged export documentation, false remittance certificates, or sham non-resident contracts, refers the case to criminal prosecution carrying imprisonment of up to five years and joint personal liability of signatories under the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 206/2020 and Law No. 67/2016

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the March 2024 exchange-rate reform, the CBE operates a flexible, market-determined regime and has dismantled the administrative FX rationing of 2022-2023. Current-account items, including cross-border SaaS licence fees, email-infrastructure hosting and software services, are convertible through authorized dealers upon presentation of a signed contract, commercial invoice and valid tax documentation. Capital-account transactions of GAFI-registered foreign investors are protected under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, but profit remittance and capital repatriation require audited financial statements, proof of settlement of all corporate and withholding taxes, a GAFI registration certificate, and a bank remittance application; post-reform the primary practical hurdle is the bank's sequential documentary review rather than scarcity of FX. Unofficial parallel-market conversion and non-bank foreign-exchange intermediation remain criminal offences under Egyptian law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of software and technology services must repatriate 100% of invoice proceeds to an Egyptian bank account through an authorized dealer within 6 months of the invoice or shipment date, extendable to a maximum of 12 months only with CBE-approved justification. Under the post-March-2024 flexible-rate regime, no fixed statutory percentage of export proceeds must be mandatorily converted into EGP; proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account of the exporter. However, the bank issues the official export-proceeds certificate only after verifying the underlying service agreement, commercial invoice, collection evidence and settlement record, and that certificate is a precondition for claiming VAT input credits and for corporate tax treatment of the export revenue.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), acting as the exclusive monetary authority, foreign-exchange regulator and banking supervisor; CBE-licensed banks are the only authorized dealers permitted to execute currency transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-Medium (restricted). Egypt does not offer an EMI licence class equivalent to the EU or UK regime. Payment services, e-money issuance and digital banking activities require a CBE or Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) licence under Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and CBE fintech rules. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) cannot clear EGP, open regulated onshore accounts for the EG LLC, or serve as the primary clearing rail. They remain viable only as ancillary collection or payout interfaces for the offshore parent receiving Stripe and card-not-present proceeds in USD/EUR; all onshore liquidity must flow through a CBE-licensed bank and its correspondent network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must route funds by SWIFT MT103 from a licensed banking institution to the EG LLC's CBE-licensed account, instructing the CBE trade-nature code for direct investment/equity subscription and stating the purpose as 'Investment in share capital of EG LLC'. The receiving bank verifies the certificate of incorporation, GAFI commercial-registry excerpt, current UBO register and a board resolution approving the capital subscription. USD amounts are credited to the designated foreign-currency account, after which the bank issues an inward remittance certificate and files a transaction report for any remittance exceeding the CBE reporting threshold; the funds may then be converted to EGP at the prevailing market rate or retained in USD for onward cross-border disbursement. All wire instructions must state the precise ISIC/NAICS business activity to avoid misclassification.The EG LLC's outbound settlement rail is SWIFT MT103 for service payments (cloud-hosting fees, software licences, contractor wages) using CBE purpose codes for 'fees for services rendered' and 'royalties and software licences'; each transfer requires an underlying invoice or contract retained in the bank's electronic document window. Irrevocable commercial letters of credit are relevant only for physical imports such as server hardware and must be opened through the same CBE-licensed bank. Dividend and profit remittances to shareholders require audited accounts, proof of final withholding tax, a tax clearance certificate and a formal remittance application before execution of the MT103; the bank will verify GAFI foreign-investment registration at the point of remittance.Deliverable FX forward (USD/EGP or EUR/EGP) via a CBE-licensed dealer, supported by documented export invoices or import contracts; tenor limited to the underlying trade maturity, commonly up to 12 months; initial margin or collateral required; uncovered speculative positions prohibited.FX swap (spot-to-forward) bridging EGP operational liquidity to USD disbursements, documented by invoice-level cash-flow schedules and subject to CBE position limits.Cross-currency swap (USD/EGP) hedging a USD-denominated shareholder loan or term deposit, executed under an ISDA master agreement with an authorized dealer and approved by the CBE as a non-speculative derivative.USD/EGP non-deliverable forward (NDF): not bookable by the onshore EG LLC; the CBE regime requires deliverable EGP settlement through an authorized dealer. Only the offshore parent or a dedicated group treasury vehicle may execute an NDF as a portfolio hedge, and it must never be intermediated through the Egyptian operating entity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% (Article 8, Law 91 of 2005, as amended; applied to net taxable income of an Egyptian LLC engaged in ISIC 6201)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on profit distributions from an Egyptian LLC to its shareholders (Article 56, Law 91/2005); the withholding is a final tax for non-resident shareholders and is remitted by the LLC at the time of distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Ministerial Decree 527/2020 and Article 37-bis of Law 91/2005: Master File is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EGP 5 billion. Local File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions reach EGP 10 million for tangible goods or EGP 5 million for services, intangibles, or financing in the tax year. Transfer Pricing Declaration Form No. 59 must be filed whenever related-party transactions exist, even below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations in the Caribbean format. Substance is enforced through the Egyptian GAAR (Article 30, Law 91/2005), beneficial ownership clauses in Egyptian DTAs, the MLI treaty-abuse rules, and ETA administrative practice. An Egyptian LLC lacking employees, premises, bank accounts, or genuine decision-making in Egypt is treated as a non-tax-resident shell or conduit; deductions are disallowed, treaty benefits denied, and profits re-characterized at the Egyptian-source level.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Declaration or Concealed Revenue

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of 100%-300% of the evaded tax (Articles 134-135, Law 91/2005); civil assessment of the hidden tax plus a 50% delict penalty under Article 112; ex parte estimated assessment by the ETA using indirect liquidity methods when records are missing.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorized signatories are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax unless non-culpability is proven; travel bans on personal passports and bank asset freezes are imposed under tax collection enforcement; conviction triggers imprisonment and restitution orders.
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: ETA disallowance of intra-group deductions, re-characterization of office income as Egyptian-source, application of the GAAR under Article 30, and reassessment at 22.5% without treaty shelter; 2% monthly delay surcharges capped at 50% plus procedural fines of up to EGP 50,000; potential VAT reassessment on undeclared service transfers.

Director personal liability: Personal civil restitution for intercompany expenses approved without arm's length evidence; travel bans and asset seizure; criminal exposure for aiding tax evasion of the foreign parent if the directors knowingly executed the conduit arrangement.
AML / Financial Sanctions Infringement

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law 80/2002: imprisonment of up to 10 years and/or a fine of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000; confiscation of assets; listing and freeze orders by the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit; parallel USD-denominated OFAC sanctions exposure because the payment rails include Stripe and credit cards.

Director personal liability: Direct criminal liability for executing or approving such transfers; personal confiscation of funds moved to personal accounts; permanent travel ban; arrest warrant; disqualification from serving as director of any Egyptian company following conviction.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: ETA disregards the transaction and imputes an arm's length profit; additional tax is assessed plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge capped at 50% of the additional tax; procedural fine of not less than EGP 5,000 and up to EGP 50,000 for refusal to submit documentation; criminal penalties apply if false statements are certified.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false Transfer Pricing certifications face joint liability for the additional assessment and criminal prosecution for false declaration; after final conviction they may be barred from management positions in Egyptian entities.
