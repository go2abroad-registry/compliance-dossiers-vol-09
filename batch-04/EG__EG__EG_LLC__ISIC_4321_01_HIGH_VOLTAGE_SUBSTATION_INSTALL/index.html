Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) of the Ministry of Finance; digital services and registration portal for VAT, corporate income tax, e-invoicing and e-receipts at https://www.eta.gov.eg and https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity that carries on an economic activity in Egypt or is required to register for Egyptian VAT must apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the ETA's online taxpayer registration portal or by filing Form No. 1 with the competent ETA administrative office. The entity must submit the certified constitutional documents, a board resolution for the authorized representative, the commercial registration certificate if any, a valid foreign tax residency certificate, and a domestic address for service in Egypt. The ETA reviews the application and issues a unified tax card/TIN certificate; for VAT specifically, the entity must file the VAT registration application and, where the foreign entity has no permanent establishment in Egypt, the ETA may register the foreign principal with a designated Egyptian fiscal representative for VAT administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Egyptian resident LLC is subject to corporate income tax as an independent juridical person on worldwide income, while non-resident foreign entities are subject to Egyptian tax on Egyptian-source income only; no general territorial tax privilege applies outside free-zone or special-zone regimes.

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election exists under Egyptian tax law; an LLC incorporated or effectively managed in Egypt is per se taxable as a corporation and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Certain statutory elections are available for corporate groups, such as loss offset elections within the group tax return rules, and the VAT regime permits ordinary deduction elections for input tax on mixed supplies. Foreign hybrid entities are evaluated under Egypt's tax-recharacterization principles for Egyptian treaty-tie-breaker purposes, and no proactive hybrid mismatch election is available absent a specific double-taxation agreement provision.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services; once exceeded, mandatory VAT registration is required; threshold applies to composite taxable turnover and excludes exempt supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by taxable persons are zero-rated if they are physically delivered outside Egypt and the export is accompanied by valid customs documentation proving actual departure within the statutory period. Services supplied to a non-resident customer who is outside Egypt and that are wholly or effectively consumed outside Egypt may be zero-rated, including offshore installation and commissioning services on infrastructure located outside Egypt; services directly related to immovable property located outside Egypt are generally outside Egyptian VAT or zero-rated. For all zero-rating claims the exporter must obtain and retain documents evidencing the supplier-customer relationship, the foreign destination, and the receipt of the invoice in hard currency through the Egyptian banking system.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Egypt operates a mandatory real-time e-invoicing and e-receipts clearance system administered by the ETA. All VAT-registered persons in the phased rollout must submit invoice and receipt data to the ETA portal in structured JSON/XML format, obtain a unique transaction UUID and encoded QR code, and integrate their accounting/ERP system with the ETA's application programming interface (API). Fiscal clearance is near-real-time: invoices undergo automated validation and digital stamping before issuance to the final customer; failure to clear invoices electronically can expose the supplier to administrative fines up to EGP 50,000 per non-compliant document and suspension of tax benefits.

Export zero-rating is subject to high audit scrutiny by the ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt. The tax authority may require production of the original export contract, invoices, customs declarations, payment evidence, and bank statements to establish that the exported goods or services actually left Egypt and that foreign currency proceeds were repatriated into Egypt. Fraudulent zero-rating or retrospective failure to repatriate proceeds results in reversal of the zero rate, assessment of avoided VAT, a delay penalty, and an additional monetary penalty of up to 100-200% of the tax evaded; criminal conviction for tax fraud may also carry imprisonment and debarment from exporting privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since March 2024 Egypt operates a flexible, market-determined exchange-rate regime in which EGP/USD and EGP/EUR rates are set on the interbank market and the CBE intervenes only to counter disorderly movements. Current-account transactions are freely convertible: payments for imported gas-insulated switchgear components, subcontractor services, royalties and trade settlements flow through licensed banks against invoice, contract and customs documentation without CBE prior licensing. Capital-account transactions, including non-resident equity injections, shareholder loans, disposal proceeds and profit remittances, are open but procedurally controlled: each flow is booked by the handling bank on the CBE electronic balance-of-payments platform with a Law 194/2020 purpose code, the underlying investment must be registered with GAFI, and the LLC must present audited financials, a tax clearance certificate and proof of the 10% dividend withholding tax (treaty-reduced where applicable) before the bank releases the transfer. The FX-arrears queue that existed before the floatation has been substantially cleared; residual hurdles are the market-driven volatility of the EGP, bank margins on FCY liquidity and the documentary strictness of purpose-code evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE rules on export receipts in force after the 2024 float, an Egyptian exporter must repatriate all export proceeds into the Egyptian banking system within statutory deadlines measured from shipment: generally 11 months for documentary letters of credit and 4 months for collection or negotiation instruments, with service-export receipts due within one year and longer periods available for construction and E&M contracts when certified project schedules are submitted. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to EGP: the EG_LLC may retain 100% of foreign-currency milestone receipts in a Foreign-Currency Account at its Egyptian bank and convert at the interbank rate when commercially attractive. Full repatriation and booking of the proceeds with the CBE Trade and FX monitoring system are mandatory; proceeds must not be held offshore, and for the 500 kV substation installation contracts each invoice must be supported by the owner's progress certificate matching the ISIC 4321 scope.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the unified monetary and exchange-control authority under the Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, acting with the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) for currency-derivative market infrastructure and with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for registration of foreign capital into the EG_LLC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

LOW for treasury-critical flows. Egypt does not license offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) as banks; an offshore EMI cannot open a CBE-cleared EGP account, cannot issue local payment instruments and cannot produce the CBE purpose-code evidence needed for later profit repatriation. The EG_LLC may use a global business account only as an auxiliary reception channel for pre-contract deposits, provided the underlying contract is disclosed to the Egyptian bank and the balance is swept into Egypt by MT103; all subcontractor payments, customs duties, VAT, payroll and tax obligations must be executed through a licensed Egyptian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder wires USD or EUR by SWIFT MT103 to the EG_LLC Foreign-Currency Account at the chosen tier-1 Egyptian bank, with the beneficiary name matching exactly the Commercial Register entry and the payment details field citing 'Capital contribution - LLC - Shareholders resolution No. [...] dated [...] - GAFI file No. [...]'. The receiving bank verifies the CBE balance-of-payments purpose code for 'Direct investment - equity capital', credits the investment account and issues an inward remittance certificate. The LLC then updates the GAFI file and Commercial Register extract within the statutory window of approximately 30 days, after which the bank marks the investment as registered, enabling the statutory right of profit and liquidation remittance under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Shareholder loans must instead be routed under a written facility agreement with arm's-length interest, booked under the loan purpose code and respecting the 4:1 debt-to-equity safe harbor of the Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005; the inbound remittance must be clean principal without any withholding tax deduction, free of sanctions-flagged intermediaries, and sourced from a jurisdiction acceptable to the Egyptian bank.Settlement rails in priority order: (a) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for milestone progress payments to overseas subcontractors and equipment suppliers, each transfer carrying the correct CBE purpose code plus the proforma/commercial invoice reference and, for goods, the ACI Form 4 customs registration; (b) commercial letters of credit issued by the Egyptian bank (SWIFT MT700 with MT707 amendments) under UCP 600 for large GIS component imports, supported by the applicant's credit line, margin and conforming transport documents; (c) SWIFT MT760 standby letters of credit and bank guarantees for advance-payment, performance (typically 5-10% of contract value) and retention obligations toward the EETC or the private owner; (d) domestic EGP wires through the CBE RTGS (EG-RTGS) and Meeza Instant Payment System for local subcontractors, payroll and VAT settlements against E-invoice codes; (e) documentary collections (MT400) only for low-value repeat purchases. Since the March 2024 float, outbound FX payments are not CBE-licensed, but the bank executes them only on proof of the real economic transaction and may time the dispatch to its own interbank USD liquidity; the treasury must therefore maintain committed FX facilities and avoid accumulating large EGP positions between milestones.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracted by the EG_LLC with a licensed Egyptian bank, anchored to a genuine underlying USD/EGP receipt or import commitment, settled at the interbank reference rate at maturity and prohibited for speculative positions.EGP/USD foreign-currency futures listed on the Egyptian Exchange (EGX derivatives segment) with central counterparty clearing and margin posting, used to hedge the EGP value of milestone receivables.USD/EGP or EUR/EGP cross-currency swap with an Egyptian licensed dealer bank, matched to the maturity and interest profile of the LLC's FCY debt service or lumpy milestone receipts, supported by the underlying loan or contract documentation.Interest-rate or commodity overlay swaps (ISDA master agreements) ancillary to the currency swap where the LLC finances imported 500 kV GIS components with SOFR-indexed credit.Offshore synthetic non-deliverable forward (NDF) is permissible only at the level of the foreign parent as a hedge of its Egyptian equity exposure; the Egyptian resident EG_LLC may not transact offshore NDFs because that would breach CBE capital-control strictures.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard CIT rate under Law No. 91 of 2005 for all legal persons; oil and gas extraction subject to a separate 40.55% rate not applicable to ISIC 4321 electrical installation contractors

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends and profit distributions made by the LLC to non-resident shareholders; dividends paid to resident Egyptian corporate shareholders are generally exempt; distributions in kind by foreign-controlled LLCs are equally subject to the 10% WHT on fair market value

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when the aggregate value of controlled transactions of the Egyptian LLC equals or exceeds EGP 10 million per fiscal year; Master File: mandatory when the multinational enterprise group's consolidated worldwide turnover equals or exceeds EGP 1 billion; Form TP-1 remains mandatory for every taxpayer with any controlled transaction, even below the EGP 10 million threshold; documentation, if not maintained, triggers a 30% penalty on the tax adjustment and shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) law as in the UAE, but the ETA applies a general anti-avoidance rule and the substance-over-form doctrine under Law No. 91 of 2005; the LLC must maintain verifiable Egyptian substance: leased or owned head office, Egyptian payroll, site staff and engineers, equipment inventory, bank accounts, and a 500kV electrical installation license from the Egyptian Electricity Transmission Company; a letterbox entity with offshore management and no local operational activity will be disregarded, may lose treaty benefits, and can be assessed on the total contract value rather than on a service fee basis; under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 the company must commence actual activity within six months of licensing or face cancellation

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Documents

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 3 to 5 years and a fine between EGP 10,000 and 50,000, plus payment of double the evaded tax; 48-hour bank account freeze and seizure of project equipment; the ETA reopens all affected years and imposes the 150% cap on monthly delay penalties; debarment from tenders for state electricity projects

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the fines, evaded tax and penalties through piercing of the corporate veil; the Economic Court may impose a travel ban and asset seizure order; board members and the signatory tax agent face criminal prosecution and a 5-year ban on holding office in any Egyptian commercial company
Shell Company and Substance Default

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the commercial registration and investment licence; denial of Article 21 recognition; recharacterization of the LLC as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent; assessment of 22.5% CIT on the full contract proceeds plus 2% monthly late penalties (capped at 150%); automatic refusal of the SCZone or treaty benefits

Director personal liability: Directors and the foreign parent are jointly liable for all unassessed taxes, penalties and interest; the travel ban regime applies to all shareholders and signatories; personal bank accounts holding project receivables are subject to seizure; the responsible officers may be prosecuted for operating an unlicensed electrical installation business
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: ETA makes a transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length profit margin under OECD-aligned rules; additional CIT of 22.5% on the adjusted profit; monthly delay penalty of 2% up to 150% of the additional tax; if the TP file is missing, an automatic penalty equal to 30% of the adjustment is imposed; the entire intercompany interest and royalty deduction may be disallowed

Director personal liability: The finance director and tax manager are personally liable for the additional tax up to the amount of the unjust enrichment; the Economic Court may freeze personal accounts and property where funds were transferred to the parent during the audited period; directors may be prohibited from leaving Egypt until the full assessment is paid or secured
Anti-Money Laundering and Failure to Withhold

Statutory sanction: Under Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002, imprisonment from 3 to 7 years and a fine between EGP 100,000 and 1,000,000, with confiscation of the funds laundered; the ETA separately assesses the unwithheld tax as a liability of the LLC, plus the 2% monthly delay penalty; VAT penalties of 20% of the unpaid VAT and 2% monthly interest are automatically added; bank accounts and LC margins are frozen for the duration of the investigation

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted withholding taxes and AML fines; personal travel bans and seizure orders are routinely issued by the Economic Court; the compliance officer faces independent criminal liability for failing to report suspicious transactions to the Egyptian Money Laundering Combat Unit (EMLCU); the parent company's board may be prosecuted under the joint control doctrine where cash flows were visibly diverted
