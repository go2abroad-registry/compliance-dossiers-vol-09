Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) – One-Stop-Shop (OSS), Cairo; Commercial Registry Department, Ministry of Trade and Industry (functionally integrated within the GAFI-OSS formation workflow).

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 4 of 2023 and its Executive Regulations (LLC formation, registration and governance chapters); Commercial Registration Law No. 34 of 1976 (mandatory commercial registration); Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 (facilitation and investor protections); Law No. 17 of 1999 (Commercial Code) regarding traders' records.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association (Aqd Al-Taasis wa Al-Nizam Al-Asasi), filed through the GAFI One-Stop-Shop statutory incorporation application and subsequently recorded in the Commercial Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Limited Liability Companies under Companies Law No. 4 of 2023; founders determine the share capital in the Memorandum of Association. Capital must be fully subscribed at signature, and the paid-up cash portion must be deposited in a temporary blocked bank account opened in the company-under-formation name in Egypt; funds are released only upon issuance of the Commercial Registration Certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the General Administration of Civil Defense for the storage and office premises; structural load inspection certificate for pallet racking and storage areas in line with Egyptian building standards; workplace occupational safety clearance per Labour Law No. 12 of 2003 covering exit routes, fire extinguishers and medical fitness records; food-grade warehousing hygiene clearance issued by NFSA.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Limited Liability Companies under Companies Law No. 4 of 2023; founders determine the share capital in the Memorandum of Association. Capital must be fully subscribed at signature, and the paid-up cash portion must be deposited in a temporary blocked bank account opened in the company-under-formation name in Egypt; funds are released only upon issuance of the Commercial Registration Certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (The ETA administers income tax, VAT, stamp tax, and payroll taxes under the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 90 of 2022; e-invoicing portal: invoice.eta.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian limited liability company must apply to the ETA on the official Tax Card application (ETA Form No. 1) within 30 days of commencing commercial activity. The dossier includes: notarized Arabic incorporation contract (A/S), commercial registration extract, proof of registered office (lease or title deed), board resolution authorizing the tax representative, power of attorney, certified passports/commercial registration of all foreign shareholders, and opening balance sheet. The ETA issues a single unified Tax Card (Bitaqua Dharibiya) bearing a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) that simultaneously operates as VAT registration number, income tax registration number, and payroll tax ID. Registration must be completed before any invoice issuance and before import/export activity; the tax card must be renewed every 5 years.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income at the 22.5% corporate income tax rate under Law No. 91 of 2005; no pass-through or partnership-style tax transparency is available for LLCs; free-zone licensed entities may instead be classified under a Territorial_Scope-equivalent regime with a 5% lump-sum tax on total annual revenue under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017) — A foreign-owned Egyptian limited liability company must apply to the ETA on the official Tax Card application (ETA Form No. 1) within 30 days of commencing commercial activity. The dossier includes: notarized Arabic incorporation contract (A/S), commercial registration extract, proof of registered office (lease or title deed), board resolution authorizing the tax representative, power of attorney, certified passports/commercial registration of all foreign shareholders, and opening balance sheet. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income at the 22.5% corporate income tax rate under Law No. 91 of 2005; no pass-through or partnership-style tax transparency is available for LLCs; free-zone licensed entities may instead be classified under a Territorial_Scope-equivalent regime with a 5% lump-sum tax on total annual revenue under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in Egyptian tax law. Available elections include: (1) free-zone regime election under Law No. 72 of 2017 (Investment Law), converting entity taxation to a 5% lump-sum on gross revenue with VAT/import duty exemptions; (2) small-business simplified income tax regime under Unified Tax Law No. 90 of 2022, available where annual gross receipts do not exceed EGP 20,000,000, taxing turnover at progressive flat rates; (3) participation exemption election on qualifying dividend and capital gains income under Art. 51 of Law No. 91 of 2005; (4) VAT payment on a cash basis for small taxpayers; (5) no VAT grouping or consolidated VAT filing is permitted; foreign shareholders may elect to appoint a resident tax representative for all tax filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies/goods and services (mandatory VAT registration threshold; voluntary registration available below the threshold under Art. 18 of Law No. 67 of 2016)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods (including organic matcha powder) is zero-rated under Art. 25(1) of the VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 where goods physically leave Egypt within 90 days from the date of the export invoice (extendable to 180 days with prior ETA approval). The exporter must hold a valid Egyptian VAT registration certificate, issue a compliant export e-invoice, and prove that legal ownership of the goods was transferred to a non-resident buyer. Offshore services supplied to non-residents can be zero-rated where the beneficiary is located abroad and use occurs outside Egypt, subject to ETA documentary checks.

  • Customs Export Declaration (official export entry raised via Egypt Customs ACI system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer documentation proving receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency to an Egyptian bank
  • Exported e-invoice bearing the ETA QR code and customs declaration number
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order/contract, and packing list
  • Certificate of Origin (where required by the destination market)
  • Vegetable/health and phytosanitary certificate for organic food product, where required by Egyptian agricultural quarantine

Mandatory B2B electronic invoicing in real-time: all VAT-registered taxpayers must issue e-invoices in Arabic and English in XML/JSON format through the ETA E-Invoice Portal via certified API integration. Each e-invoice receives a QR code containing the invoice QR-digital signature, is cleared electronically by the ETA (fiscal clearance stamp) within a near-real-time window, and must include the customs export declaration reference for export supplies. The e-receipt system (B2C) and the integration phases are mandatory for all VAT registrants as of the latest ETA enforcement schedules; failure to integrate blocks VAT refunds and exposes the taxpayer to administrative penalties.

ETA audit reviews export files within the five-year statutory limitation period and may cross-match customs ACI data, shipping manifests, bank FX receipts, and e-invoices. Any false export claim, overstatement of exported quantity, or failure to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day CBE deadline triggers retrospective clawback of the 14% VAT previously zero-rated, plus monthly delay surcharge (up to 2% per month, non-deductible) and a fine of EGP 20,000 to EGP 2,000,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment in the case of deliberate evasion under Law No. 90 of 2022. The offending exporter is also blacklisted from the e-invoicing/refund system and loses export refund priority.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange rate regime supervised by the CBE. Current account transactions, including trade settlements and profit remittances, are generally convertible with standard documentation. Capital account transactions require prior CBE approval. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject to 15% withholding tax and mandatory repatriation formalities; amounts above certain thresholds trigger enhanced CBE reporting and potential delays.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must surrender 100% of foreign exchange proceeds from eligible exports to authorized Egyptian banks within six months of shipment date. Mandatory conversion occurs at the prevailing CBE reference rate. Required documentation includes commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and proof of delivery. Non-compliance results in penalties, account restrictions, and barriers to future export financing.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is low. CBE imposes strict licensing, minimum capital, and AML requirements on electronic money institutions. Offshore digital EMIs are prohibited without local bank partnerships; any cross-border operations require full CBE authorization and ongoing compliance reporting.
