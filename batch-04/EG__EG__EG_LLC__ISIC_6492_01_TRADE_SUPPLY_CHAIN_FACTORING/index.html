Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Egyptian LLC is an opaque resident taxpayer under Income Tax Law 91/2005: worldwide net income is taxed at 22.5% CIT; no pass-through or fiscal-transparency treatment is permitted; profit distributions attract 10% dividend WHT; a non-resident shareholder may claim treaty relief under Egypt’s double taxation treaties. Territorial relief applies only to foreign branch profits under the conditions of Article 39 of Law 91/2005.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Egyptian law; classification is prescribed by legal form. Available mechanisms: (i) free-zone regime election under Investment Law 72/2017 for ETA/Zone-approved entities (practically unavailable for financial services unless integrated with a SCZONE licence); (ii) voluntary VAT registration; (iii) advance pricing agreements and transfer-pricing documentation elections under Executive Regulations 991/2016; (iv) foreign tax credit election for limited foreign-branch profits; (v) thin-capitalization compliance (4:1 debt-to-equity on shareholder loans) to protect interest deductibility; (vi) hybrid-mismatch documentation to safeguard deduction/credit symmetry.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover (mandatory registration) and EGP 50,000 (voluntary registration); the threshold is applied to the entity as a whole, and no invoice may be issued without prior VAT registration once the threshold is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods carried out of Egypt is zero-rated under the VAT law upon proof of customs exit, contract, commercial invoice, B/L/AWB and inward FX remittance. Services qualify for zero-rating only where the place of supply is outside Egypt and the benefit is effectively consumed abroad; for cross-border trade factoring, the financing spread falls under the financial-services exemption, while separately itemized factoring/administration fees are taxable at 15% but may be zero-rated for a non-resident debtor when supported by the factoring agreement, foreign-currency invoice and bank FX receipt. The ETA should be asked for an advance ruling on the fee/discount split before issuing the first zero-rated invoice.

Mandatory end-to-end e-invoicing for all VAT-registered businesses (phased since 2021 with full coverage of registered taxpayers by 2024). B2B/B2G e-invoices in XML are transmitted in real time (within 24 hours of issuance) via the ETA API for validation, digital stamping and issuance of a unique UUID before the invoice is legally issued; the B2C e-receipt regime has been mandatory for retailers since April 2022. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since March 2024, the CBE abolished the administrative exchange-rate peg and implemented a flexible exchange-rate regime under which the EGP/USD rate is determined by interbank supply and demand through authorized banks. Current-account transactions, including trade payments, service fees, factoring collections, and genuine commercial remittances, are broadly convertible without prior CBE approval provided the underlying transaction has complete commercial documentation and is routed through an authorized Egyptian bank. Capital-account transactions, namely foreign share-capital injections, shareholder loans, and portfolio inflows, are subject to mandatory inflow registration with the CBE and the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI). Profit and dividend repatriation by a non-resident shareholder is legally permitted but practically gated by the following hurdles: (i) audited financial statements for the distributable period; (ii) a signed board resolution authorizing the dividend; (iii) a valid tax card and a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA); (iv) an approved foreign-currency transfer request lodged through the corporate account-holding bank; and (v) the bank's independent KYC verification of the receiving beneficial owner, with no available offshore EGP clearing. There is no statutory percentage cap on dividend remittance, but a foreign shareholder must prove the source of the invested funds at onboarding or the remittance is treated as a portfolio exit requiring additional CBE approval. Non-resident factoring counterparties cannot hold EGP balances outside Egypt. In practice, all EGP conversion for the LLC must be executed physically inside Egypt via a Tier-1 authorized bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE export and banking circulars applicable to Egyptian exporters: (a) all export shipments must be documented and financed through an authorized bank, with the bill of lading, commercial invoice, and certificate of origin lodged with the bank; (b) export proceeds in a CBE-acceptable foreign currency must be credited to the exporter's account in Egypt within 180 days from the date of the bill of lading / airway bill or from the date of the underlying export invoice for services; the exporter's bank can grant a one-time extension of up to a further 180 days upon a documented justification; (c) the applicable mandatory conversion ratio into EGP is currently 0% — Egypt has abolished statutory surrender requirements, and the exporter may retain 100% of export receipts in a Special Foreign Currency Account for approved trade and treasury purposes; (d) notwithstanding the 0% conversion ratio, the CBE has a discretionary statutory power to impose a temporary surrender ratio under emergency balance-of-payments conditions, so the treasury blueprint must preserve full traceability of export receipts and avoid commingling with local EGP collections; (e) the exporter must match each export bill with its corresponding foreign-currency receipt, a process called 'export bill matching', and residues or discrepancies trigger automatic enhanced reporting; (f) for the EG_LLC factoring platform, when the company legally purchases export receivables it assumes the exporter's repatriation obligation, therefore a true-sale assignment must transfer the original export documentation bundle to the LLC and the debtor remittances must flow back to the LLC's Egyptian foreign-currency account within the 180-day window; (g) for reverse factoring of Egyptian importers, the outbound disbursement to a non-resident supplier is classified as an import payment and requires the import-purpose code and the related CBE import-monitoring registration.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) is the sole monetary, banking and foreign exchange supervisory authority in Egypt, operating under the Banking Law No. 194 of 2020. The CBE exercises Exchange Control through its Upper Committee for Foreign Exchange Policy, the General Department for Banking Supervision, and the Foreign Exchange Operations Department. The Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) independently licenses and supervises non-bank financial activities, including factoring and trade receivables securitization, while the CBE retains mandatory registration, repatriation, and monitoring powers over all cross-border currency flows affecting Egyptian legal entities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where aggregate annual related-party controlled transactions exceed EGP 10 million; Master File is required where the worldwide group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 30 million. Country-by-Country reporting obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EGP 19 billion, aligned with the OECD BEPS thresholds as adopted by Egyptian law.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not issue ESR-style declarations as in the Gulf free zones, but the ETA applies the Egyptian GAAR, anti-tax-avoidance provisions, and transfer pricing rules to require genuine economic substance. A trade supply-chain factoring LLC must maintain a physical office in Egypt, full-time commercial and risk staff, active EGP-denominated bank accounts, board decisions in Egypt, and real commercial control over receivables, credit underwriting, and collections. A letter-box entity will be challenged, treaty benefits can be denied, and restructuring may be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, intentional tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment, a criminal fine equal to the tax evaded or twice the amount at risk, and full civil recovery of the tax deficiency plus a 2% monthly late surcharge. The ETA may also blacklist the entity, revoke tax registration, and seize bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who sign or authorise false filings may be held personally and jointly liable for the entire tax debt; Egyptian courts may order asset freezes, travel bans, and attachment of personal bank accounts; criminal conviction can result in disqualification from managing Egyptian companies for up to 10 years.
Shell Company and Artificial Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the corporate entity under the General Anti-Avoidance Rules and tax the full remote-controlled income at 22.5%; all intercompany deductions may be denied; commercial registration may be cancelled under Egyptian Companies Law, and fines of up to EGP 2,000,000 may be imposed for false declaration.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced, making shareholders and shadow directors personally liable for the corporate tax liability; travel bans and freezing of personal assets are commonly ordered where tax is at risk; individuals may also be banned from serving as directors of any Egyptian entity.
Unlicensed Factoring and Anti-Money-Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed trade-finance activities attract confiscation of financial proceeds, suspension of bank accounts, and fines ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 2,000,000 under Egyptian AML Law No. 80 of 2002 and applicable banking/FRA regulations; senior management may face criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners engaged in AML-CFT violations face imprisonment from 3 to 7 years, travel bans, asset freezing, and forfeiture of unexplained wealth; they may also be barred from holding any position in regulated financial institutions.
Transfer-Pricing Mispricing and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The ETA will assess an upward transfer-pricing adjustment at 22.5% corporate income tax, plus a 2% monthly delay surcharge and interest; failure to document controlled transactions may trigger additional penalties under the ETA Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the scope of tax assessments is extended through the statutory open years.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length arrangements without documentary support can be held personally liable for the primary tax and interest resulting from the adjustment; courts may order recovery of shifted profits, impose travel bans, and require personal restitution where the transfer was knowingly artificial.
