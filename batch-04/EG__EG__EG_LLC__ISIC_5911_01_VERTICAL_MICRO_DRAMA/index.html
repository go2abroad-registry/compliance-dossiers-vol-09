Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; electronic services are provided through the ETA eTax portal and the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt platforms.

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA and obtain a tax registration number, generally following incorporation and before commencing taxable activity. The application is submitted through the ETA portal or the competent tax office, supported by the commercial register, articles of association, proof of registered address, authorized representative identification and powers, and other requested corporate records. VAT registration is generally required when taxable turnover reaches the statutory threshold; an application is generally due within 30 days after the registration obligation arises. A nonresident without an Egyptian establishment that supplies electronically delivered services to Egyptian consumers must assess the nonresident simplified VAT-registration rules and register through the ETA’s designated electronic process where applicable. A foreign company operating through an Egyptian permanent establishment or other taxable presence must register under the applicable income-tax and VAT rules. Requirements and portal procedures should be confirmed with the ETA for the particular activity and establishment facts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no US-style check-the-box election for an Egyptian LLC and no general election to treat it as a pass-through entity. An LLC is generally taxed as a separate legal person. No general entity-classification election is available to neutralize hybrid mismatches; applicable treaty, domestic anti-avoidance, and related-party rules must be reviewed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of taxable sales/turnover during the relevant 12-month period is the general VAT-registration threshold under the VAT Law. Specific activities, including certain professional or service activities, may require registration irrespective of that threshold. Nonresident electronic-service suppliers may be subject to a separate simplified registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods may qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the statutory export conditions are met and export outside Egypt is evidenced. Exported services may qualify where the recipient is outside Egypt and the service satisfies the statutory conditions for export, including applicable recipient, use, and consumption requirements. Services supplied to or used or consumed in Egypt do not become zero-rated merely because the customer or payment intermediary is foreign. A streaming subscription supplied to viewers in Egypt is generally a domestic taxable supply unless a specific statutory rule applies. Input VAT recovery or refund remains subject to statutory eligibility, documentation, and ETA verification.

  • Customs export declaration and evidence of customs release/export
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or other transport and delivery evidence for exported goods
  • Commercial invoice, export contract, and evidence identifying the foreign recipient
  • Bank records or other evidence of consideration and foreign-currency receipt where relevant; an inward remittance certificate is supporting evidence where available, not a universal statutory document for every export
  • For exported services: contract, service-delivery records, recipient and residence evidence, invoices, and evidence supporting the place of use or consumption and payment

ETA e-invoicing requirements apply to in-scope business-to-business transactions under the applicable implementation phases. Invoices must be issued in the prescribed structured electronic format, transmitted to the ETA platform for validation and assigned the required unique identifiers; API integration or approved technical connectivity is required for applicable taxpayers. Electronic receipt requirements apply to in-scope business-to-consumer transactions under the relevant rollout. Applicability and onboarding dates depend on taxpayer category and ETA decisions. This is an electronic reporting and validation framework; the precise document and transaction rules should be verified against current ETA specifications.

No general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies to every Egyptian export transaction; sector-specific, contractual, banking, or other regulatory requirements may apply. ETA may deny zero-rating or input-tax recovery, assess underpaid VAT, and impose statutory late-payment additions and penalties where export conditions or evidence are insufficient. Fraudulent evasion may trigger the sanctions, including criminal consequences, prescribed by the VAT Law and other applicable laws. Assessment periods, penalties, and any criminal exposure depend on the conduct and current statutory provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, managed-floating exchange-rate regime as of March 2024, anchored by the CBE's FX liberalization under the IMF Extended Fund Facility. The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is internally convertible: all current-account transactions (goods, services, income, royalties) are bankable against underlying documentation and CBE purpose codes, with service remittances cleared within approximately 48 hours and dividend remittances within 5-7 business days post-reform. Capital-account transactions (foreign equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend and branch-profit repatriation) are legal and convertible but documentary-gated: they require audited financial statements, tax-clearance certificates, and bank FX-committee approval, and may be queued during episodic FX-scarcity periods. The EGP is non-convertible offshore — no deliverable EGP market exists outside Egypt — so all inward remittances must transit CBE-licensed authorized dealers. Profit-repatriation hurdles are materially reduced by completing the CBE Foreign-Capital Registration procedure, which legally guarantees transferability of registered capital and accrued profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE's consolidated foreign-exchange regulations (August 2024 circular amending the 2020 unified FX rules): (i) 100% of export proceeds — including digital-content and licensing receipts of the micro-drama business — must be repatriated to an account at an Egyptian authorized bank within the statutory timeline; (ii) for merchandise exports, proceeds must be received within 30 days of the bill-of-lading date for sight shipments, extending to a maximum of 120 days for usance/letter-of-credit terms; (iii) for services and digital exports, remittance must occur within 90 days of the invoice date or the contractual service-completion certificate; (iv) no mandatory conversion ratio to EGP is currently imposed — the 2016 float abolished forced surrender, so exporters may hold 100% of proceeds in foreign currency, converting only as needed to discharge EGP tax and operating liabilities; (v) late repatriation triggers CBE fines, exclusion from preferential FX facilities, and customs-liability consequences; and (vi) funds received by the bank must be posted to the exporter's FX account within 7 business days after nostro crediting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Al-Bank Al-Markazi Al-Masri (البنك المركزي المصري); the Egyptian Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) exercises adjunct oversight of non-bank financial and capital-market activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary collection or treasury rail, MEDIUM as a supplementary payout processor. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, DLocal, Stripe Treasury) are not authorized dealers under CBE Law 194/2020 and may not hold EGP or maintain virtual USD accounts intercepting Egyptian export receipts. Using them for primary revenue collection would breach the 100% repatriation mandate, expose the LLC to FX-control penalties, and risk account termination. A compliant hybrid model: collect subscription revenues through Apple IAP / Google Play / Stripe into the onshore operational account via platform-payout SWIFT MT103, and use an offshore EMI-vaulted debit/spend rail only for sub-USD 10,000 disbursements to international creators — never for converting EGP or maintaining onshore EGP balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders inject equity via SWIFT MT103/MT202 wires to the LLC's capital escrow sub-account at an approved onshore bank (NBE / Banque Misr / CIB). The CBE Balance-of-Payments purpose code is 'Direct Investment in Egypt — Equity Capital'; the bank requires the certified articles of association, current commercial-registry extract, shareholder resolution, passport/UBO documentation, and a capital-registration application before crediting. Following credit confirmation, the bank issues the Foreign Capital Registration Certificate (Qaid Rasm al-Mal), which protects dividend and capital repatriation rights under the 2024 unified FX regime. Shareholders may retain funds in USD, convert to EGP at interbank rates, or hold a dual-currency position. Apple IAP / Google Play / Stripe payouts are commercial revenue and must NOT be wired to the escrow account; they flow into the operational USD account under service-export purpose codes.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border service payments — creator payouts, production fees, software/platform subscriptions, advertising spend — each supported by the underlying contract and a CBE purpose code ('Payments for Services' or 'Royalties and License Fees'); royalty and technical-fee payments must evidence the 20% statutory Egyptian withholding tax (or treaty-reduced rate) remitted to the Egyptian Tax Authority, with a tax-clearance certificate retained; (ii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (at-sight or usance up to 180 days) and documentary collections for physical merchandise tie-ins; (iii) SWIFT MT103 for dividend repatriation, requiring audited financial statements, a valid tax-clearance certificate, and bank FX-committee sign-off. Individual creator disbursements below USD 100,000 are executed through corporate internet-banking batch templates, while larger tickets escalate to the branch FX committee. Local EGP liquidity clears via the CBE RTGS system (STELLA) in near real time, and cross-currency conversion is executed at the interbank rate plus the applicable margin.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP FX forwards (tenors up to 12 months) available at CIB, NBE, Banque Misr, and HSBC Egypt under CBE hedging regulations, requiring underlying trade or investment exposure documentation.USD/EGP FX swaps and rollover swaps for intra-month liquidity and repatriation smoothing, subject to bank dealing-room credit lines.Plain-vanilla purchased FX options (USD calls / EGP puts and USD puts / EGP calls) with domestic licensed banks, limited to hedging — speculative short-option writing is prohibited.Cross-currency USD/EGP interest-rate swaps, permissible only with CBE pre-approval and solely against an underlying real foreign-currency liability; practical availability is limited for SME-scale treasuries.Offshore EGP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are prohibited for Egyptian-resident counterparties; no onshore NDF market exists — hedges must be executed deliverable onshore.Natural revenue hedging via mandatory retention of USD subscription proceeds in an operational USD account and laddered forward conversion to EGP for projected tax and operating expenses.

Annual Commercial Registration Renewal (Company File Renewal) Anniversary-based: renewal must be completed within 30 days after each anniversary of the initial commercial registration; for calendar-year LLCs, typically due before 31 March. $50 USD Late renewal triggers escalating GAFI administrative fines (starting at approximately EGP 600 / USD 12) and temporary suspension of the commercial file; continued non-renewal results in administrative strike-off of the LLC from the Commercial Registry pursuant to the Companies Law No. 4 of 2022.
Annual Corporate Income Tax Declaration (CIT Return - Form No. 32) with Audited Financial Statements Fiscal-year based: filing due within 4 months after the end of the tax year; for calendar-year LLCs the deadline is 30 April. $0 USD Late filing attracts a fixed penalty of EGP 5,000 to 20,000 (approx. USD 100 to 400); unpaid tax accrues a 2% monthly late-payment addition under Article 137 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005. Persistent non-filing enables ETA bank-account garnishment, asset seizure, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

USD 10,000), plus suspension of the GAFI file and prevention of share-transfer registration.
