Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; online portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (e-invoicing/clearance: https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) at the ETA’s taxpayer registration department before engaging in taxable activities. Submission via the ETA portal or local tax office includes the certified certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, commercial registration extract (current trade registry certificate), power of attorney, passport copies of managers/shareholders, registered office proof, and estimated activity description. The ETA issues the TIN and, separately, a VAT (Sales Tax) registration certificate once the EGP 500,000 compulsory VAT threshold is met or on voluntary application; a non-resident vendor with an Egyptian sales nexus may be required to register and appoint a tax representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the LLC is a separate taxable juristic person under Egypt Law No. 91 of 2005; no pass-through to shareholders. Resident LLCs pay corporate income tax on worldwide income; foreign LLCs are taxed on Egypt-source income only. Ordinary LLCs are not within the Suez Canal Economic Zone tax-exemption regime unless specially licensed within that zone.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available under Egyptian domestic law. An Egyptian LLC is per se opaque and must file corporate income tax. ETA allows a tax group/consolidated return election for a parent and its at-least-90-percent-owned Egyptian subsidiaries, subject to ETA approval. For VAT purposes, an LLC may elect voluntary registration below the statutory threshold but cannot elect to be treated as non-taxable once the threshold is exceeded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies (goods and services) for compulsory VAT registration; voluntary registration is available for lower-turnover persons, and non-resident suppliers of goods/services to Egyptian customers may be registered and required to appoint a fiscal representative under the Executive Regulations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT-registered Egyptian LLC may zero-rate the export of goods where sale and title transfer are made to a non-resident buyer and the goods physically leave Egypt under customs control within the legally required export period. Exported services are zero-rated where the recipient is outside Egypt and the service is actually performed/used outside Egypt in accordance with the Executive Regulations. The exporter must retain complete evidence of export and receipt of hard-currency consideration; absent such evidence the supply is taxed at 14%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (outward customs statement) bearing the ETA/customs release stamp
  • Commercial invoice and matching VAT e-invoice code from the ETA e-invoicing system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian bank classifying the transaction as export proceeds
  • Underlying export contract/purchase order, and Letter of Credit or SWIFT payment instruction when applicable

Since October 2020, VAT-registered taxpayers are integrated into ETA’s real-time e-invoice/e-receipt system phased by turnover. Invoices must be generated through an ETA-certified billing system or the ETA portal in XML/JSON format, digitally signed with the taxpayer’s certificate, assigned a unique invoice UUID/QR code, and submitted to the ETA for fiscal validation (clearance) before or immediately upon issuance. ETA API integration is mandatory for high-volume taxpayers; no paper or handwritten invoices are accepted for the scope of this compliance profile once the electronic mandate applies to the taxpayer.

ETA audit teams verify zero-rated export claims against customs exit data, invoices, and bank foreign-currency records. If documents are incomplete, export is not proven, or export proceeds are not repatriated through Egyptian banks within the 180-day deadline, ETA may issue a revised assessment re-characterizing the supply as taxable at 14%, charge late-payment interest/damages, and levy administrative fines. Fraudulent or false zero-rating documentation is treated as tax evasion: the VAT is clawed back with heavy monetary penalties and possible criminal prosecution, and the taxpayer’s e-invoicing clearance privileges may be suspended.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains open current-account convertibility following the 2016 exchange rate float and the March 2024 unification of the exchange rate, under which the EGP is convertible at the banking-system rate for genuine current-account transactions, including trade services, profit remittances, and dividend repatriation. Capital-account flows such as equity injections, shareholder loans, and repatriation of invested capital remain subject to CBE-supervised bank verification, source-of-funds documentation, and balance of payments purpose coding, with no general ex-ante licensing outside regulated sectors; practical hurdles include periodic commercial-bank foreign-currency liquidity pressure, documentary rigidity (repatriations must be matched to prior inward remittance declarations and, where applicable, the investment registration certificate issued under Law No. 72 of 2017), and the requirement that profit remittances flow only through the Egyptian banking system after verification of audited accounts, tax clearance, and corporate approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt and credited to an account with a CBE-licensed bank within the contractual settlement window not exceeding 90 days from the shipment date; each receipt must be declared under the CBE ITRS with the underlying customs and export documentation. Following the March 2024 exchange-rate unification, the CBE has suspended mandatory percentage surrender requirements (the prior managed-rate regime had imposed temporary conversion ratios of up to 15% on export revenues); exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign-currency Export Proceeds Accounts or convert into EGP at the unified banking rate, provided the full amount is routed through the formal banking system. Failure to repatriate within the timeline triggers bank compliance holds, EGP conversion penalties, and tax-authority scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the sole statutory authority for monetary policy, foreign exchange control, and banking supervision under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), operating the official unified exchange rate regime and the International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) for all cross-border fund movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium (restricted). Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not authorized by the CBE to open standalone clearing or collection accounts for an Egyptian-domiciled LLC or to execute EGP settlement outside the licensed banking system, due to the extraterritorial scope of Law No. 194 of 2020 and the AML/CFT Law No. 80 of 2002. Their lawful use is limited to ancillary merchant-payout aggregation (Stripe/PayPal marketplace settlements) that onward-routes via SWIFT into the LLC's CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account; each aggregated inflow must be declared as export proceeds with full payout-ledger evidence. A fintech EMI may serve as a supplementary receivables-matching rail, but the primary treasury, FX conversion, and outgoing trade-payment rails must remain with an Egyptian commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: shareholders execute a capital-subscription resolution and remit via SWIFT MT103 (values above USD 100,000 should use MT202 cover through a correspondent bank). The purpose code must reflect direct-investment equity capital under the CBE ITRS classification; the receiving Egyptian bank is required to obtain the LLC's commercial registration, tax card, and stamped memorandum evidencing the capital increase, and to file the inbound capital declaration with the CBE before crediting the Capital Escrow Account. Funds may be retained in foreign currency or converted into EGP at the unified banking rate; each injection must be mirrored in the commercial-registry capital records and the annual financial statements, as the bank will require these proofs before any later dividend or capital repatriation. Verification stack: MT103 confirmation, shareholder resolution, source-of-funds declarations, and the bank's inward-remittance certificate; the LLC must not split remittances artificially to bypass CBE reporting thresholds.Trade disbursements to overseas jewelry and timepiece suppliers should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for open-account and cash-against-documents (CAD) settlements, and through SWIFT MT700 for irrevocable commercial letters of credit on high-value consignments; documentary collections (D/P and D/A) may be used via MT400 where suppliers accept draft terms. Every outbound remittance requires the CBE import purpose code, the underlying purchase order and commercial invoice, transport documents (air waybill or bill of lading), the Nafeza national-single-window ACI release/customs declaration reference, and proof of any required Egyptian Customs tariff registration; banks also apply their internal FX-availability queue, giving priority to LC-backed and high-frequency CAD flows. For fine-timepiece imports above USD 50,000, a confirmed irrevocable LC is recommended to mitigate counterparty risk, while open-account MT103 remains efficient for repeat suppliers with established trade history.Deliverable FX forward contracts (outright forwards) with the LLC's CBE-licensed relationship bank, permitted only for genuine current-account hedging against USD/EGP or EUR/EGP trade exposure, supported by underlying invoices and the CBE ITRS declaration.Cross-currency swaps between EGP and hard-currency flows executed through a CBE-licensed bank as an asset-liability management tool for matched export-receipt and import-payment tenors, subject to bank ALCO approval and CBE monitoring.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing the EGP, permitted only where structured by an Egyptian licensed bank and booked as a true hedge of forecast export proceeds; speculative offshore NDF exposure by a resident LLC is prohibited under CBE regulations.Plain-vanilla FX options (call/put spreads) available from international-tier Egyptian banks for premium-budgeted hedging of seasonal watch and jewelry inventory purchases, with immediate documentation of the commercial underlying.Commodity-linked forward or fixed-price supplier contracts for gold bullion and precious-metal inputs, not a regulated FX instrument but a permissible physical-price hedge for jewelry manufacturing treasury.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium (restricted). Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not authorized by the CBE to open standalone clearing or collection accounts for an Egyptian-domiciled LLC or to execute EGP settlement outside the licensed banking system, due to the extraterritorial scope of Law No. 194 of 2020 and the AML/CFT Law No. 80 of 2002. Their lawful use is limited to ancillary merchant-payout aggregation (Stripe/PayPal marketplace settlements) that onward-routes via SWIFT into the LLC's CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account; each aggregated inflow must be declared as export proceeds with full payout-ledger evidence. A fintech EMI may serve as a supplementary receivables-matching rail, but the primary treasury, FX conversion, and outgoing trade-payment rails must remain with an Egyptian commercial bank.
