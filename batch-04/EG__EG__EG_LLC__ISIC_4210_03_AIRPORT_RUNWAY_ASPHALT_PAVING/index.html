Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), attached to the Ministry of Finance. Electronic services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipt integration platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; customs single window: NAFIZA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC (EG_LLC) must register with the ETA before commencement of activity or within 30 days of the first taxable supply. Steps: (1) notarize and Arabic-certify the LLC's constitutional documents and commercial register extract; (2) file the Tax Card application at the competent ETA office attaching the LLC contract, notarized power of attorney for the foreign manager, lease or works contract, and passports of signatories; (3) obtain the unified Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT certificate within approximately 2-4 weeks; (4) integrate the taxpayer with the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt platform and obtain the digital signature certificate; (5) complete NAFIZA customs registration for export operations. Non-resident contractors without a permanent establishment may appoint a resident tax representative for registration and compliance; VAT registration is mandatory within 30 days of exceeding the statutory turnover threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC (EG_LLC) must register with the ETA before commencement of activity or within 30 days of the first taxable supply. Steps: (1) notarize and Arabic-certify the LLC's constitutional documents and commercial register extract; (2) file the Tax Card application at the competent ETA office attaching the LLC contract, notarized power of attorney for the foreign manager, lease or works contract, and passports of signatories; (3) obtain the unified Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT certificate within approximately 2-4 weeks; (4) integrate the taxpayer with the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt platform and obtain the digital signature certificate; (5) complete NAFIZA customs registration for export operations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Egyptian law provides no check-the-box or pass-through election; an LLC is automatically a juridical person taxed at entity level under the Unified Tax Law (Law No. 30 of 2023), with a standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5%. No hybrid-mismatch fiscal-transparency election is available; controlled foreign company, transfer pricing, and anti-hybrid rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual turnover of taxable goods and services, triggering mandatory VAT registration. Non-resident suppliers of services are subject to reverse-charge rules; SCZone free-zone projects may apply zone-specific VAT suspension or zero-rating instead of standard registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies where (i) goods are physically exported from Egypt or delivered to free zones, evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration released through NAFIZA; (ii) services, including airport runway paving and polymer-modified asphalt works, are supplied to a non-resident principal and effectively consumed outside Egypt; or (iii) the supply qualifies under the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) free-zone regime. Construction services performed on-site within Egypt for a domestic airport operator are standard-rated at 14%; zero-rating is available only when the work is performed under an export-oriented contract with a non-resident principal, an international civil aviation contractor, or a qualifying SCZone project. The exporter must evidence receipt of payment in foreign currency through an Egyptian licensed bank and must repatriate proceeds within the applicable Central Bank of Egypt deadline; input VAT on exported supplies is refundable through the ETA's risk-based refund track.

  • Electronic customs export declaration released through NAFIZA (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing receipt of hard currency
  • Signed export contract, irrevocable letter of credit or civil aviation works order, and final commercial invoice
  • Certificate of Origin and any TIR/carnet documentation
  • Packing list, weight/tally certificate, and insurance certificate
  • ETA-cleared zero-rated e-invoice bearing the QR code and export VAT code
  • Contractor's completion and acceptance handover certificate for the runway paving works

Mandatory near-real-time fiscal clearance: B2B e-invoices and B2C e-receipts must be generated in the ETA-prescribed XML/JSON schema, digitally signed with the taxpayer's ETA-issued certificate, and submitted via certified APIs to the ETA integration platform. The ETA validates fields and returns a QR-encoded clearance/acceptance code; invoices lacking clearance are non-compliant, and input VAT deduction is denied. Offline submission is tolerated only during declared ETA system outages.

Zero-rating and refund claims are subject to mandatory desk/field audit and ETA risk-scoring; incomplete export documentation within the statutory response period triggers reversal of the refund. Fraudulent zero-rating exposes the taxpayer to a fine of not less than 100% and not exceeding 300% of the evaded VAT plus imprisonment under VAT Law No. 67/2016 Article 37 and the Tax Procedures Law No. 206/2020, retrospective clawback of refunded VAT with monthly default interest, customs penalties, and suspension or cancellation of the taxpayer's e-invoicing clearance certificate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions through licensed banks, including documented imports, services, debt service, dividends, and other permitted commercial payments. Capital-account transactions, foreign direct investment movements, shareholder loans, intercompany funding, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds are subject to bank documentation, source-of-funds verification, investment and corporate records, tax clearance where applicable, and CBE or other regulatory requirements. There is no general prohibition on foreign ownership or dividend remittance by an Egyptian LLC, but practical delays may arise from foreign-currency liquidity, enhanced compliance reviews, incomplete customs or tax files, mismatches between contracts and invoices, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner concerns, or restrictions applicable to specific goods, counterparties, or jurisdictions. Dividends should be remitted from distributable profits supported by approved financial statements, shareholder resolutions, tax evidence, and proof of payment of applicable Egyptian taxes. Shareholder-loan repayments and capital reductions require evidence of the original inflow, the underlying agreement, corporate approvals, and bank acceptance. Payments must normally be executed through Egyptian licensed banks or other duly licensed payment institutions; offshore digital accounts do not substitute for an Egyptian banking channel for regulated local collections or FX conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt does not generally impose a universal mandatory conversion ratio requiring every exporter to surrender a fixed percentage of export proceeds into Egyptian pounds. Export proceeds must nevertheless be handled through authorized banking channels and supported by export declarations, commercial invoices, shipping and customs records, contracts, and evidence of receipt. A single universal statutory repatriation deadline should not be assumed: applicable timing can depend on CBE instructions, customs and trade-finance rules, the exporter's banking arrangement, the commodity, destination, payment terms, and any approved extension or exception. The company should obtain a written transaction-specific position from its servicing bank before shipment and monitor outstanding export receivables against customs records. Foreign-currency retention, conversion, and use of proceeds are subject to bank controls, documentary consistency, AML review, and available CBE rules. Any mandatory conversion or repatriation requirement applicable to a particular export transaction must be confirmed against the current CBE and banking instructions in force on the transaction date.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, together with the relevant Egyptian banking, customs, investment, anti-money-laundering, and foreign-exchange regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplemental treasury tools and low for core Egyptian collections, FX conversion, payroll, or trade settlement. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment service provider generally cannot independently provide regulated Egyptian deposit-taking, local payment collection, Egyptian-pound conversion, domestic clearing, or trade-finance services unless it operates through an appropriately licensed Egyptian bank or payment provider and satisfies applicable CBE, AML, sanctions, tax, data, and foreign-exchange requirements. Offshore accounts may be used for permitted group treasury functions, but they should not receive Egyptian customer proceeds or fund regulated local activity without a documented Egyptian banking route. The LLC should maintain an Egyptian operational account and use the offshore provider only after bank and legal confirmation of the proposed flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a direct SWIFT transfer from the foreign shareholder or clearly documented group treasury entity to the Egyptian LLC's foreign-currency capital or designated investment account at a CBE-licensed bank. The remittance instruction should identify the legal entity, shareholder or lender, purpose as subscribed capital, capital increase, or documented shareholder loan, and the relevant corporate resolution or agreement. Avoid third-party funding, personal accounts, cash deposits, unexplained netting, and multi-hop routing. Before receipt, provide the bank with the commercial registration, constitutional documents, ownership chart, shareholder resolution, subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and any required GAFI, investment, or corporate filing. After receipt, obtain the bank credit advice and SWIFT evidence, reconcile the amount to the corporate records, and complete any capital-registration or amendment filings. Conversion into Egyptian pounds should occur only for identified local expenditure and through the licensed bank at the applicable bank-confirmed rate. Repatriation of unused capital, loan principal, or dividends should use the same documented bank relationship and should not be characterized as a different purpose.For imported asphalt, bitumen, polymers, machinery, aircraft-runway equipment, spare parts, and engineering inputs, use the Egyptian LLC's bank account and provide the underlying purchase contract, pro forma or commercial invoice, customs and import documentation, product and end-use information, shipping documents, insurance evidence, and supplier and beneficial-owner details. A documentary commercial letter of credit issued or confirmed through a CBE-licensed bank is preferred for high-value or unfamiliar suppliers; the bank may transmit authenticated SWIFT messages and release payment against compliant documents. SWIFT MT103 may be used for open-account, advance, milestone, or documentary payments where the bank accepts the risk and the underlying transaction is documented. Documentary collection, bank guarantee, and standby structures may be available subject to bank approval and applicable trade rules. Payment descriptions, invoice values, customs declarations, tax records, and accounting entries must match. Do not split payments to evade review, route through unrelated offshore accounts, or use personal accounts. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard corporate income tax rate for tax periods beginning on or after 1 January 2024 under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended by the 2023 budget amendment (prior rate: 20%). The special 40.55% regime for oil, gas, and Suez Canal activities does not apply to ISIC 4210 airport runway asphalt paving.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Final Form 41 must be filed within 4 months after the close of the tax year (Law No. 91 of 2005). A 31 December year-end filer must file by 30 April; a 30 June government-aligned year-end filer must file by 31 October. Quarterly provisional returns and advance payments are due within one month after each quarter-end. Extensions are not routinely granted and late filings trigger automatic penalty accrual.
