Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, through the ETA Taxpayer Portal and applicable integrated tax-registration services at https://www.eta.gov.eg. The ETA administers income tax, value-added tax, tax registration, electronic invoicing, and electronic receipt compliance.

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian limited liability company must obtain an ETA tax registration certificate and tax identification number after incorporation and commercial registration. The standard process is: (1) complete incorporation and obtain the commercial registration and constitutional documents from the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones or the competent incorporation authority; (2) obtain or document the registered office, lease, utility evidence, and authorized-manager details; (3) submit the tax-registration application electronically through the ETA Taxpayer Portal or through the competent ETA office, as applicable; (4) provide the commercial registration, articles of association, national identification or passport details of the authorized representative, power of attorney, registered-address evidence, and banking or business information requested by the ETA; (5) undergo address or document verification where required; (6) receive the tax registration certificate and tax identification number; and (7) separately register for VAT if the company conducts taxable activities and reaches the statutory threshold, or if compulsory registration applies to a category not subject to the general threshold. A foreign company carrying on taxable business in Egypt generally requires an Egyptian tax registration number and, where applicable, a VAT registration number. A nonresident digital-service supplier making supplies to Egyptian consumers may be required to use the ETA nonresident or simplified VAT registration process and comply with collection, filing, and payment rules applicable to nonresident suppliers. The ETA may require certified Arabic translations, legalization or apostille of foreign corporate documents, an Egyptian tax representative, and evidence of the Egyptian taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no general United States-style check-the-box election for an Egyptian limited liability company. An Egyptian LLC is ordinarily treated as a separate corporate taxpayer and is subject to Egyptian corporate income tax on its taxable income. A nonresident company is generally taxed on Egyptian-source income or income attributable to an Egyptian permanent establishment. Tax treatment may be affected by a valid double taxation agreement, permanent-establishment analysis, free-zone or special economic-zone legislation, investment incentives, or a statutory exemption, but these are not entity-classification elections. Egyptian tax law does not provide a general hybrid-mismatch election that allows an LLC to elect transparent or pass-through treatment for Egyptian tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover is the general VAT registration threshold under the VAT regime. Registration may also be compulsory without reliance on the threshold for specified goods, services, importers, exporters, and other statutory categories. A company supplying taxable mobile software, subscriptions, in-app functionality, or related digital services in Egypt must assess both the threshold and any sector-specific or nonresident digital-service registration rules. Imports are generally subject to import VAT at the applicable rate, unless a statutory exemption or special treatment applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Egyptian VAT may apply at zero percent to qualifying exports of goods and qualifying services supplied to customers outside Egypt, subject to the statutory conditions and implementing rules. For goods, the goods must be exported from Egypt and supported by customs evidence showing actual export. For services, the service must generally be supplied to a nonresident recipient and used or enjoyed outside Egypt; services performed in or relating to Egyptian real estate, goods, events, or activities in Egypt may fail the offshore-service conditions. An app or digital service is not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer pays through Apple or Google or because the platform is located outside Egypt. The company must establish the supplier of record, customer residence, place of use or enjoyment, contractual scope, transaction value, and payment trail. Export VAT refunds or input-tax credits require valid VAT registration, compliant tax invoices, evidence that input VAT relates to taxable or zero-rated activity, and reconciliation to VAT returns. VAT input relating to exempt activities is generally not recoverable.

Egypt operates an ETA electronic invoicing system for business-to-business and other covered transactions, using the ETA platform, approved integration methods, electronic signatures, and structured invoice data. Covered taxpayers must obtain ETA credentials, register or onboard the relevant taxpayer and devices or systems where required, issue compliant electronic invoices, transmit invoice data to the ETA, and retain electronic records. ETA electronic invoice procedures generally involve digital signing, unique invoice identifiers, validation, and QR-code or equivalent verification features. Electronic receipt requirements apply to covered business-to-consumer transactions through the ETA electronic receipt system, including the use of approved point-of-sale, application, or system integration arrangements. The exact implementation date and scope depend on the taxpayer category, transaction type, and ETA implementation notices. An app-store freemium model must distinguish supplies made by the Egyptian LLC from supplies made by Apple or Google as merchant of record. Platform statements, commission invoices, customer location evidence, VAT treatment, and ETA reporting must reconcile with the entity's contractual role. Failure to issue or transmit a required electronic invoice or receipt can result in administrative penalties, invoice disallowance, tax assessments, and other enforcement measures under Egyptian tax law.

The 180-day period should be treated as a compliance-control assumption for collection or repatriation of export proceeds where applicable under current Egyptian foreign-exchange, banking, customs, or sectoral rules; the company must confirm the operative requirement with its Egyptian bank and current Central Bank and customs guidance because the deadline and exceptions can vary by transaction and exporter status. The ETA may deny zero-rating, recover improperly refunded or credited input VAT, assess output VAT at the applicable rate, impose late-payment interest and statutory penalties, and disallow unsupported expenses or invoices where export conditions are not proven. False customs declarations, fabricated invoices, concealed domestic use, circular payments, sham offshore customers, or fraudulent refund claims may expose the company and responsible officers to tax, customs, accounting, and criminal liability. Consequences can include retrospective VAT assessments, clawback of refunds and credits, interest, administrative fines, suspension or cancellation of tax registration or electronic-invoicing access, customs penalties, denial of future refund claims, and prosecution under applicable tax, customs, anti-money-laundering, and penal laws. Documentation should be retained for the statutory tax-record retention period and made available in Arabic or with an acceptable certified translation when requested by the ETA or another competent authority.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains current-account convertibility for legitimate trade and service transactions through the CBE interbank market. Capital-account inflows and outflows are substantially regulated by CBE foreign-exchange instructions under Law No. 88 of 2003 and Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Non-resident profit remittances are permitted after corporate income tax and applicable withholding tax are settled, provided that: the Egyptian LLC has filed audited financial statements; a tax clearance certificate has been obtained; the funds to be remitted are held in the LLC’s licensed Egyptian bank account; and the remittance is supported by a board resolution and dividend distribution minutes. Practical friction may arise during periods of FX shortage because CBE and commercial banks prioritise essential imports, fuel, medicines, and strategic commodities; therefore, app-store revenue held offshore should be routed to Egypt through transparent SWIFT channels and declared for balance-of-payments reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current CBE policy, export proceeds must be repatriated through, and received by, an Egyptian licensed bank rather than paid directly to an unlicensed offshore account. For merchandise exports, the customary statutory expectation is that the full invoice value must be received by the Egyptian bank within 60 to 90 days from the date of shipment, subject to the underlying commercial terms and CBE-approved exceptions. There is no blanket mandatory conversion percentage; after the 2016 FX liberalisation, exporters may retain export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts with licensed Egyptian banks. However, 100% of export-related collection must enter the Egyptian banking system, be declared to the CBE, and be capable of being matched against the underlying customs or service-export documentation. For digital services and mobile application proceeds sourced from Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Billing, the revenue constitutes services exports and must be received in a bank-supervised FX account, supported by monthly platform statements, tax invoices, and payment-provider reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for use as a principal corporate treasury account. Offshore digital EMI firms are not licensed by the CBE and may not lawfully maintain Egyptian-pound settlement accounts, collect consumer payments on behalf of Egyptian businesses, or hold Egyptian banking licences. Such entities cannot provide compliant EGP clearing rails and are not accepted as replacement accounts for mandatory CBE-monitored trade or capital flows. They may be used only for peripheral pre-collection of USD/EUR from offshore app stores before the funds are formally routed into the Egyptian banking system, provided full beneficiary and source-of-funds trail is retained. Reliance on an offshore EMI to avoid CBE controls is an AML/CTF red flag and will generally result in bank rejection or account freezing in Egypt.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders must subscribe to the LLC’s equity through a formal capital-contribution instruction and transfer capital via SWIFT MT103 from their own bank accounts. The receiving Egyptian bank requires the remittance to include a CBE balance-of-payments purpose code corresponding to inward direct investment/equity capital, together with the GAFI registration number and the LLC’s commercial registration number. Supporting documents must include the board/shareholder resolution approving the capital increase or initial subscription, share-allocation table, amended articles of association, source-of-funds evidence, and a signed declaration for CBE foreign-direct-investment reporting. Cash deposits, crypto transfers, personal accounts, or funds routed through a non-related third party will not be accepted. The bank will issue an inward remittance advice and will register the transaction with the CBE’s foreign-direct-investment and balance-of-payments database.Outbound payments for software licences, cloud infrastructure, legal fees, marketing services, and app-store infrastructure support should be cleared using SWIFT MT103 from the LLC’s Egyptian bank account, supported by the underlying commercial contract, invoice, tax invoice, and the CBE outbound transfer application. Physical-goods payments, if any, should be made through an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit advised and settled through the correspondent banking network, with the Egyptian bank checking the import documentation against the CBE’s import prioritisation and importer-registration rules. All outbound transfers must be from the LLC’s licensed Egyptian account and must disclose the precise CBE remittance purpose code; failure to provide a valid tax card, transfer-pricing documentation for related-party royalties or services, and audited financial basis for profit distribution may cause rejection. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Egyptian Transfer Pricing Guidelines (ETA, 2020) mandate a Local File where aggregate controlled transactions with related parties equal or exceed EGP 8 million in a fiscal year; a Master File is mandatory at or above EGP 30 million in aggregate controlled transactions; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups whose consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EGP 22.5 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). Documentation must be submitted in Arabic or English within 30 days of an ETA request.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the ETA applies a statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Law 91/2005 (Articles 36 and 39) authorizing recharacterization of artificial transactions lacking commercial substance. Treaty relief and TP deductibility are denied to shell vehicles without local employees, office premises, genuine decision-making, and risk-bearing capacity; an Egyptian LLC acting solely as a pass-through for mobile-app IP profits is exposed to full CIT reassessment, penalty surcharges, and loss of DTA protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Willful Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of not less than 2 years and not exceeding 5 years and a fine of three to five times the amount of tax evaded under Law 91/2005 as amended; conviction is published at the taxpayer's expense and the company's tax card may be suspended

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and authorized signatories who directed or participated in the concealment are personally subject to criminal conviction, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and civil restitution of the evaded tax on a joint and several basis
Failure to Withhold and Remit WHT

Statutory sanction: The under-collected WHT is assessed against the Egyptian LLC as agent, plus the 2% monthly delay surcharge on the unpaid amount and fixed administrative fines under the Tax Procedures Law

Director personal liability: Board members and financial officers are jointly and severally liable with the company for WHT that was collected but not remitted; aggravated non-remittance is characterized as embezzlement of public funds and triggers imprisonment and asset freezing
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The ETA disregards the entity and reattributes profits to the controlling foreign enterprise, assessing 22.5% CIT on the full reattributed base, adding the 2% monthly delay surcharge, and extending the look-back period to the full 5-year statute of limitations

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face piercing of the corporate veil under the Egyptian Companies Law; courts may order personal reimbursement of reassessed taxes and impose a travel ban and precautionary attachment pending final judgment
Money Laundering / High-Risk Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Money laundering and related financing offenses under Law No. 80/2002 (as amended by Law No. 177/2020) carry imprisonment and fines of up to EGP 5 million, confiscation of the laundered funds and related assets, and cancellation of the commercial registration of the operating entity

Director personal liability: Directors involved in layering or concealing transaction proceeds face separate criminal prosecution, permanent disqualification from corporate board service, placement on the Egyptian AML blacklist, and civil liability for the full value of the laundered assets with no cap
