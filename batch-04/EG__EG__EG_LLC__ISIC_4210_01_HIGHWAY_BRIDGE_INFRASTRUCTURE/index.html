A virtual office (registered-address-only service) is accepted by GAFI and the Commercial Registry for the basic LLC incorporation and tax registration, provided a legitimate physical address exists within Egypt. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains a statutory nine-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT certificate by filing a registration application with the Egyptian Tax Authority after commercial registration. The application must be submitted through the ETA's electronic filing platform or in person to the competent tax department using: the commercial register certificate, incorporation contract and company documents, statutory books and board resolution, proof of registered office/lease, bank certificate, and national/passport copies of the LLC's managers or legal representative. Licensed tax agents commonly facilitate the process. The ETA issues the Tax Card (Bataqa Darbia), which serves as the unified TIN and VAT ID.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains a statutory nine-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT certificate by filing a registration application with the Egyptian Tax Authority after commercial registration. The application must be submitted through the ETA's electronic filing platform or in person to the competent tax department using: the commercial register certificate, incorporation contract and company documents, statutory books and board resolution, proof of registered office/lease, bank certificate, and national/passport copies of the LLC's managers or legal representative. Licensed tax agents commonly facilitate the process. The ETA issues the Tax Card (Bataqa Darbia), which serves as the unified TIN and VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available under Egyptian tax law; an Egyptian LLC is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available elections are limited to: (i) consolidated tax group filing for resident companies under Article 53 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 (parent holding at least 90% of capital and voting rights, subject to ETA approval); (ii) optional VAT registration for businesses below the compulsory threshold; (iii) fiscal-year election allowing a non-calendar financial year with ETA consent; and (iv) treaty-based permanent establishment or beneficial ownership claims, which are not domestic entity classification elections. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory registration; voluntary registration is permitted from EGP 50,000 annual turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on goods physically exported from Egypt under customs control and on services sold to a non-resident recipient located outside Egypt that are used outside Egypt, to the extent recognized under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016. The supplier must retain export contracts, customs documentation, invoices, and evidence that export proceeds in convertible foreign currency have been credited to an Egyptian bank account. For highway/bridge infrastructure EPC contracting, zero-rating may apply to exported engineering, design, procurement and off-site services supplied to a foreign principal for a project located outside Egypt, whereas onshore construction and assembly in Egypt remain subject to standard-rate VAT or, if the buyer is a non-resident, to reverse-charge/direct-payment mechanisms. Zero-rating is not available for wholly onshore works.

  • Customs Export Declaration (NAFEZA / SAD Form)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank confirming receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and export contract/purchase order signed with the non-resident buyer
  • Bank telex/SWIFT confirmation showing credit of proceeds to the exporter's account in Egypt

Egypt enforces mandatory real-time electronic invoicing and clearance through the ETA's Integrated Electronic Invoicing System (El-Nadem). B2B e-invoices must be transmitted to the ETA in XML/JSON format via the official API portal; the ETA validates the fiscal data and returns a digital stamp/QR code for the invoice. E-receipts are being phased in for B2C retail transactions. Certain annual-turnover thresholds apply for mandatory onboarding, but once registered for VAT, e-invoice transmission is obligatory for all regulable sales. Invoices must be submitted within 24 hours of issuance; failure to obtain clearance renders the VAT input tax deduction unavailable and triggers fines.

The ETA may require exporters to repatriate export proceeds into Egyptian banks within 180 days from the date of shipment. If the exporter fails to provide the mandatory customs, transport and foreign-exchange documentation, or if the proceeds are not received in Egypt within the statutory deadline, the ETA will reverse the zero-rate treatment and assesses VAT at the standard rate on the export supply, plus late-payment and late-filing penalties. Knowingly submitting fictitious or forged export documents constitutes tax evasion and is punishable under Law No. 67 of 2016 and Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 with penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded, criminal prosecution, and imprisonment in the case of forgery or fraud. The ETA may audit the exporter's books and records for up to five years from the end of the relevant fiscal year and retrospectively claw back any VAT refunds paid together with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is convertible for current-account transactions through the interbank market following the 2022 exchange-rate liberalization. Bona fide trade, service, and profit-remittance payments may be executed through licensed banks without prior CBE approval if underlying documentation is complete. Capital-account operations remain restricted: inbound equity injections must be registered with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI); outward capital repatriation and foreign-currency borrowing require CBE registration. Profit remittances from an Egyptian LLC are permitted subject to audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance, and withholding tax certificates. Dividends cannot be remitted from an account without a final Ministry of Finance tax clearance, and EGP remittances to non-resident accounts are strictly prohibited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be credited to an Egyptian-licensed bank account within 6 months of the bill of lading date. Under current CBE external-sector circulars, mandatory conversion to EGP applies to 100% of proceeds unless the exporter holds a CBE-approved foreign-currency account and demonstrates documented export/import offset or hedging commitments. Approved offsets are generally limited to verified merchandise import obligations; any unused converted surplus must be liquidated through the interbank market at the official rate within one year.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by the Central Bank of Egypt, cannot issue local IBANs, cannot perform statutory EGP conversion, and cannot be used for capital injections, dividend remittances, or corporate tax payments. They may support only incidental cross-border supplier disbursements and rely on funding from a fully regulated Egyptian bank account. All primary treasury clearing must be conducted through CBE-licensed commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity for the Egyptian LLC must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to a CBE-licensed bank account. The remittance instruction must include the GAFI investment file number, the LLC's Egyptian tax identification number, and a clear narrative clause stating 'Capital Injection / Construction Infrastructure Project — GAFI Ref.' The receiving bank verifies the source of funds, issues a Foreign Currency Inflow Notice, and credits the beneficiary's capital escrow account. For injections exceeding EGP 100 million, pre-registration with GAFI and a CBE foreign investment declaration are required before the final transfer. The bank may request the original share allocation agreement and audited valuation to confirm the subscription price.Outbound trade settlement is executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 for EGP-denominated local subcontractor payments and through commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) for foreign-currency procurement of structural steel, cement, and heavy equipment. The LLC must submit pro-forma invoices, signed supply contracts, import customs declarations or delivery receipts, and the corporate tax card. Payments exceeding EGP 500,000 require a bank-verified tax clearance certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million aggregate controlled transactions (cross-border or domestic) in a tax year triggers Local File; Master File required if group consolidated revenues exceed EGP 2 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have formal ESR legislation, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Law 91/2005 and the substance-over-form doctrine require the LLC to demonstrate real business activity, employees, and decision-taking offices. Tax authorities may deny treaty benefits or disregard artificial entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for 3-5 years; fine equal to the tax evaded; confiscation of project assets; administrative fines up to 150% of tax due; denial of future government procurement licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for evasion; civil restitution of the full tax plus interest; travel bans and freezing of personal bank accounts; imprisonment up to 5 years; professional disqualification.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Revocation of tax concessions; ex officio reassessment at statutory WHT rates; additional tax of 30-100%; blacklisting of company name; criminal sanctions for submitting false declarations.

Director personal liability: Lifting of corporate veil: directors personally liable for the difference in tax and interest; personal travel bans; visa denials; possible extradition under mutual legal assistance if fraud is proven; imprisonment for up to 3 years.
Money Laundering & AML Compliance (Law 80/2002)

Statutory sanction: Fines between EGP 100,000 and EGP 5 million; freezing of bank accounts; confiscation of project proceeds; disqualification from government tenders; referral to public prosecutor for possible imprisonment of executives.

Director personal liability: Non-executive directors and compliance officers may face criminal liability; personal asset restraint; travel restrictions; 5-10 year imprisonment for laundering construction funds; lifetime director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA TP adjustment to deemed arm's length profit; additional corporate tax at 22.5%; late payment interest of 2% per month; penalty of 10% of adjusted tax if documentation is missing; customs denial on imported construction equipment.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for TP governance may be held negligent under Civil Code; monetary judgment against personal assets for reckless mispricing; travel bans and seizure of personal passports; in egregious cases, criminal fraud prosecution.
