Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official e-services portal: https://e-taxta.gov.eg. The ETA administers corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duties, and the mandatory e-invoicing/e-receipts platforms. The Egyptian Customs Authority (ECA) handles customs/export clearance through the NAFEZA National Single Window (CargoX platform).

E-government filing portal

Incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) at the Egyptian Commercial Registry and obtain the commercial register certificate and memorandum of association (foreign ownership is allowed; no minimum capital requirement). Within 30 days of incorporation, register with the ETA to obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN), tax card, and VAT file. Register as an exporter with GOEIC for the Export Card, open an Egyptian bank account for foreign-currency settlement, and onboard the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt API with a digital signature certificate and production credentials. Foreign shareholders must act through a resident tax agent; all filings are in Arabic.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The EG_LLC is a resident juridical person under Egyptian law taxed on worldwide taxable income at the 22.5% corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Law 91/2005, as unified by Law 30/2023). No pass-through election exists; profit distributions are not deductible at the entity level and are subject to shareholder-level taxation. Locations inside a Free Zone or the Suez Canal Economic Zone may alternatively qualify for entity-level exemption or preferential tax under Investment Law 72/2017 and Law 83/2002.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or OECD hybrid mismatch elections exist under Egyptian tax law. Available statutory elections include: (i) Free Zone election under Investment Law 72/2017 substituting income tax with an annual charge of 1% on goods entering/leaving the zone; (ii) Suez Canal Economic Zone (SEZ) election under Law 83/2002 offering tax exemptions and customs/VAT deferral per zone regulation; (iii) simplified VAT regime election for small taxpayers; (iv) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold to secure export refunds; and (v) investment incentive deduction of up to 50% of capital expenditures. Elections are filed with GAFI/ETA at incorporation and carry a 5-year lock-in period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies of goods or services for mandatory VAT registration; a simplified VAT-basis option applies to small taxpayers; exporters may register voluntarily below the threshold to reclaim input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exported tangible goods — including portable solar generators and solar panels — where the LLC is VAT-registered, the buyer is a non-resident, title passes to the foreign customer, the goods physically leave Egypt with customs export proof, and settlement is received in foreign currency through the banking system. Services physically performed outside Egypt and services directly connected to exports (international freight and export handling) are likewise 0% rated. Input VAT on inventory, warehousing, and outbound shipping is fully creditable and refundable; the ETA targets issuance of export refunds within 45 days of a complete electronic claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the NAFEZA National Single Window and the Egyptian Customs Authority, referencing the ETA-cleared e-invoice UUID and the customs release number
  • Commercial Invoice in the ETA-validated electronic format bearing the fiscal QR code and invoice UUID
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of the goods out of Egypt
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin (recommended, supporting tariff preference claims)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank confirming foreign-currency export proceeds were credited to the LLC's account

Real-time fiscal clearance is mandatory for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Structured XML/JSON e-invoices and e-receipts must be digitally signed (PKCS#12/X.509) and transmitted to the ETA central platform via approved REST/SOAP APIs; the ETA returns a unique invoice UUID and an encrypted QR fiscal code (digital stamp) that must be embedded in the invoice/receipt copy. Invoices rejected by ETA clearance give no right to input VAT deduction, zero-rating, or export refunds. B2C e-commerce sales settled through Stripe and Affirm BNPL must produce an e-receipt at the moment of sale. The e-invoice/e-receipt regime is fully mandatory for all ETA-registered taxpayers as of 2024, with blocking of tax files and penalties for non-compliance.

Export refund claims are subject to ETA desk audits and document verification within the 5-year reassessment window. If zero-rating is disallowed or documents are found false or fraudulent, the ETA will claw back refunded input VAT, re-assess 14% output VAT on the export sales, levy the statutory monthly late-payment surcharge (2% per month of unpaid tax), and apply evasion penalties of imprisonment for 1-5 years and/or a fine of EGP 20,000-50,000 or the tax amount if higher (VAT Law 67/2016, arts. 45-46). Additional consequences include export card suspension, blocking of the e-invoice/e-receipt clearance file, and blacklisting on the ETA exporter risk matrix.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified flexible exchange-rate regime effective 6 March 2024, with the EGP rate determined by CBE-authorized dealing banks; current-account transactions (imports of solar panels, portable generators, components, logistics and e-commerce service fees) are freely convertible subject to standard import documentation (ACI/Nafeza customs declaration, VAT e-invoice, insurance and freight contracts). Capital-account transactions are convertible but controlled: inbound equity capital is freely admitted under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, registered with GAFI, and credited in foreign currency or EGP to a designated capital account at a CBE-licensed bank; outbound profit and capital repatriation is guaranteed by law, executed at the prevailing market exchange rate after the LLC files audited financial statements, a board capital/repatriation resolution, ETA tax clearance certificates, and CBE-approval via the bank's Form for Foreign Transfers. Practical hurdles include periodic USD liquidity tightness, payment-priority queues, enhanced UBO verification for non-resident shareholders, and the requirement that dividends be paid only from after-tax distributable profits supported by audited accounts and a final tax-clearance certificate; no generalized freeze or surrender requirement applies at present, but banks may defer large remittances pending CBE liquidity requests.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation deadline: export proceeds from the LLC's non-oil merchandise must be credited to its Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the dated bill of lading, extendable to a maximum of 120 days for certain perishable or project-specific consignments upon prior CBE approval; non-repatriation triggers a cascading penalty regime (0.5% per month on the un-repatriated amount) and automatic suspension of the exporter's bank facilities. Mandatory conversion ratio: since the March 2024 reform the CBE removed the prior surrender obligation (which had peaked at 100% during the 2022–2023 hard-currency crisis); the LLC may now retain 100% of export proceeds in a Foreign Currency Account (EUR, USD, or other permitted currencies) and convert any portion to EGP at the free market dealing rate. All export contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and shipping documents must be reported electronically to the bank under the CBE's periodic export-reporting framework, and an Export Notification (Form 8) must be lodged with the CBE via the handling bank before shipment to validate the inconvertibility-risk cover for the proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), which superseded Law No. 88 of 2003; CBE retains exclusive authority over licensing of banks, FX dealing, capital account transactions, and enforcement of exchange-control and AML/CFT rules for all Egyptian-licensed credit institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for treasury-depository functions. The CBE does not permit offshore digital EMI platforms (for example, Mercury or Airwallex) to act as the LLC's principal holding account for export/trade proceeds because the receipt of foreign currency by an Egyptian resident through a non-CBE-licensed intermediary is treated as an unauthorized FX-channeling transaction under Circular No. 92/2022 and related AML directives, and banks will reject credits originating from such EMIs without a full source-of-funds reconstruction. Offshore EMIs are viable only as an auxiliary payment-collection layer for consumer card/BNPL receivables (Stripe and Affirm route funds through Egypt-licensed PSP partners such as Fawry, Paymob, or eFin, which then settle in EGP), provided daily sweeps to a CBE-licensed settlement account are performed and all merchant-of-record agreements are disclosed to the corporate bank during KYC.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for treasury-depository functions. The CBE does not permit offshore digital EMI platforms (for example, Mercury or Airwallex) to act as the LLC's principal holding account for export/trade proceeds because the receipt of foreign currency by an Egyptian resident through a non-CBE-licensed intermediary is treated as an unauthorized FX-channeling transaction under Circular No. 92/2022 and related AML directives, and banks will reject credits originating from such EMIs without a full source-of-funds reconstruction. Offshore EMIs are viable only as an auxiliary payment-collection layer for consumer card/BNPL receivables (Stripe and Affirm route funds through Egypt-licensed PSP partners such as Fawry, Paymob, or eFin, which then settle in EGP), provided daily sweeps to a CBE-licensed settlement account are performed and all merchant-of-record agreements are disclosed to the corporate bank during KYC.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% flat rate on net taxable income of resident juridical persons (Law No. 91 of 2005, Article 5, as amended by Law No. 1 of 2020).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends actually distributed to non-resident shareholders (Article 67, Law No. 91 of 2005); no withholding applies to distributions to resident juridical persons meeting the Egyptian participation exemption conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return and audited financial statements must be filed within 4 months of the end of the tax year (Article 46 of Law No. 91 of 2005). For a calendar-year LLC the filing deadline is 30 April; the four instalments are due in the first month of each quarter.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Each instalment is due by the end of the first month of each quarter; the annual return reconciles the four instalments with the final assessed tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the aggregate value of related-party transactions in the fiscal year exceeds EGP 8,000,000; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches or exceeds EGP 2,000,000,000 (approximately USD 41,000,000), with the CbCR notification filed via the ETA portal.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no standalone Economic Substances Regulation statute comparable to the UAE ESR, but the ETA applies a functional economic-substance review under the beneficial-ownership clause of DTAs, the management-and-control test for residence, and the general anti-abuse rules of Law No. 91 of 2005. A portable-power e-commerce LLC must demonstrate genuine presence in Egypt: physical warehouse or fulfillment capacity, personnel exercising procurement and marketing decisions, board minutes held or documented in Egypt, inventory risk, and a rational transfer-pricing profile. A mail-box entity whose online-sales profits are attributed to a foreign parent without local substance will be re-denominated as an Egyptian resident or as having an Egyptian permanent establishment, with full CIT and surcharges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Under-Declared E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years plus fine of EGP 100,000 to EGP 10,000,000 under Law No. 19 of 2016 read with Law No. 15 of 2021; the tax due is recovered with a surcharge of up to 5 times the evaded amount; digital payment records are judicially seized from payment platforms.

Director personal liability: The LLC manager and any director who signed the false declaration face criminal prosecution; the corporate veil is pierced where deducted-but-unremitted withholding taxes are involved; courts order civil restitution of the evaded tax, interest, and fines, and approve travel bans and asset freezes.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the offshore parent as an Egyptian permanent establishment under Article 32 of Law No. 91 of 2005; assessment of 22.5% CIT on the attributable sales profit, monthly surcharges, and denial of treaty benefits under the beneficial-ownership clause; the tax card and import-export code may be suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors who legalised the deceptive Egyptian arrangements are jointly and severally liable for the re-assessed PE tax and late-payment surcharges; repeated violations result in criminal fines and a ban on founding or managing companies in Egypt.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer-pricing adjustment restores the arm's-length margin, disallows the excess cost, and computes additional tax plus a monthly arrears surcharge; failure to file documentation carries fines under Law No. 15 of 2021 of between EGP 500 and EGP 1,000,000; the assessment remains open beyond the standard statute of limitations where concealment is shown.

Director personal liability: The transfer-pricing policy documentation is signed by the director; knowingly false documentation constitutes criminal false declaration; civil liability extends to the adjusted tax, penalties, and the parent's indemnity claim in liquidation.
AML / BNPL Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Penalties under Law No. 80 of 2002 (Anti-Money Laundering Law): administrative fines up to EGP 5,000,000 and imprisonment between 3 months and 7 years; the ETA may revoke the tax clearance certificate, and the banking regulator can suspend merchant-payment processing in Egypt.

Director personal liability: The compliance officer and managing director face personal fines and imprisonment for failing to report suspicious online-retail flows; travel bans are available in money-laundering investigations, and courts may pierce the veil where directors commingle retail proceeds with personal accounts.
