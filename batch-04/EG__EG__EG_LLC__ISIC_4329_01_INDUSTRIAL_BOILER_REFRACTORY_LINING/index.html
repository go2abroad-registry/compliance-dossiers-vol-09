Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; official e-service portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoice submission portal: https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing an Egyptian LLC (on sub-EG_LLC footing) must complete the unified tax registration procedure under Law No. 206 of 2020. Required steps: (1) obtain the LLC's commercial registry extract and notarized articles of association; (2) appoint a resident tax representative / authorized signatory; (3) file the ETA registration application via the unified e-services platform or at the competent tax office, attaching the commercial registry certificate, lease/title deed proving Egyptian head office, manager's national ID or passport, board resolution, and the audited opening balance sheet; (4) the ETA issues the taxpayer identification number (TIN) and opens the income tax file; (5) separately apply for VAT registration where annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 (voluntary registration permitted below threshold) and for withholding tax file registration; registration is administered within the statutory time frame of the unified tax law.

Required steps: (1) obtain the LLC's commercial registry extract and notarized articles of association; (2) appoint a resident tax representative / authorized signatory; (3) file the ETA registration application via the unified e-services platform or at the competent tax office, attaching the commercial registry certificate, lease/title deed proving Egyptian head office, manager's national ID or passport, board resolution, and the audited opening balance sheet; (4) the ETA issues the taxpayer identification number (TIN) and opens the income tax file; (5) separately apply for VAT registration where annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 (voluntary registration permitted below threshold) and for withholding tax file registration; registration is administered within the statutory time frame of the unified tax law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable – Egypt operates no check-the-box or look-through election. The LLC is mandatorily classified as a separate juridical person taxable on its worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and VAT Law No. 67 of 2016; no hybrid mismatch or transparent election is available to foreign owners. Tax treaty relief under Egypt's double tax conventions may be invoked for dividends, interest, royalties, and branch profits provided the beneficial ownership and substance documentation requirements are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services triggers mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration is available to persons below the threshold; non-resident suppliers supplying taxable goods or services to Egyptian persons may be required to register or appoint a fiscal representative where the supply is subject to reverse charge accounting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Egypt are taxed at 0% VAT. Services supplied to non-residents – including offshore engineering and refractory masonry consulting or installation services performed for exporters on movable goods destined for export – qualify for zero-rating where the provider retains satisfactory evidence that the recipient is non-resident and that the services are performed in connection with exported goods or are actually consumed abroad. The applicable proof bundle must be held at the time of VAT return filing and retained for at least five years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration with customs release number)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR waybill evidencing physical export
  • Foreign currency Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an exchange control bank
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract, packing list, and customs transit or shipment order
  • VAT e-invoice (zero-rated) and export settlement report filed under CBE regulations

Mandatory e-invoicing is phased in under ETA Resolution 449 of 2020 and subsequent directives; VAT-registered persons must continuously transmit structured invoices in the ETA-prescribed XML/JSON schema before or at issuance via certified ERP integration or the ETA e-invoice portal. Each invoice undergoes real-time validation and fiscal clearance: the ETA assigns a unique transaction UUID, registers a content-based hash, and requires printing of a two-dimensional QR code on each invoice; invoices that fail validation are rejected and deemed invalid for input VAT deduction and for zero-rating substantiation. E-receipts are likewise mandatory for business-to-consumer retail transactions.

The ETA verifies the export chain through cross-checks with customs export declarations, bank FX settlement records, and transport documents. Claiming zero-rating on goods not actually exported, or on services not qualifying as export services, gives rise to reassessment at 14% VAT plus interest, recovery of any refund erroneously paid, and exclusion from refund programs. Deliberate or repeated violation triggers the criminal penalty regime: imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of not less than EGP 5,000 and not exceeding double the tax evaded, disqualification from tenders, closure of the establishment for up to 6 months, and publication of the judgment; the ETA is empowered to claw back refunds with the addition of a penalty equal to 100% of the tax until the facts are established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) operates under a managed, market-determined flexible exchange-rate regime adopted in March 2024, replacing the prior managed float and import-financing restrictions. Current-account transactions, including trade in refractory materials, equipment, engineering services, and profit/dividend remittances, are fully convertible in principle upon presentation of standard underlying documentation (contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax clearance where required). Profit repatriation by an LLC requires the remitting bank to verify audited financials, corporate tax and stamp-tax clearance, and the foreign-investment repatriation statement under Egypt's Foreign Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; no separate prior CBE approval is now required. The capital account is convertible for equity injections, share-capital refunds, and disposal proceeds of the LLC, but the receiving bank must register the transaction with the CBE for balance-of-payments and foreign-direct-investment statistics and must complete robust anti-money-laundering source-of-funds verification, particularly for shareholder loans. Residual friction is therefore documentary and bank-level rather than statutory, and delays are most commonly caused by incomplete KYC files, unresolved tax certificates, or inconsistent economic substance evidence for offshore shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Following the March 2024 exchange-rate unification and CBE circulars, there is no mandatory minimum surrender percentage of export proceeds to the CBE or the dealing bank. Exporters must nevertheless repatriate 100% of export proceeds to an authorised bank in Egypt as freely convertible currency or EGP within the period stipulated in the bank's credit terms, customarily six months from the bill-of-lading/airway-bill date or within 30 days of the agreed receivable due date, whichever is earlier. The receiving bank may contractually require a negotiated conversion of a portion of gross proceeds to EGP to fund local tax, payroll, and operational obligations, and may downgrade the facility if repatriation deadlines are missed; non-repatriation is an administrative and exchange-control offence that triggers CBE penalties, loss of foreign-currency facilities, and adverse compliance reporting.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary authority and foreign exchange control regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), exercising delegated exchange-control powers through licensed dealing banks and exchange bureaus; the structure is complemented, for tax-linked repatriation matters, by the Ministry of Finance and the Egyptian Tax Authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI banking (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, or similar non-Egyptian e-money institutions) is ranked Low for the LLC's statutory and core treasury functions and Medium only for ancillary, non-regulated disbursements such as small software subscriptions or non-Egyptian subcontractor top-ups. The CBE authorises foreign-exchange dealing, customer onboarding, and collection of export proceeds exclusively through licensed Egyptian banks; offshore EMIs cannot lawfully receive Egyptian export proceeds, cannot be used for payroll, social-insurance, VAT, or corporate-tax payments, and cannot issue documentary credits or fulfil the CBE's export-receipt repatriation requirement. An offshore EMI is therefore unsuitable as the primary settlement rail for the ISIC 4329 refractory-masonry contracting revenue cycle.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Refractory-masonry contracting (ISIC 4329) with lump-sum progress billing, high cash density on site payroll, and no continuous invoice trail to the ultimate industrial client.

Root cause: Refractory-masonry contracting (ISIC 4329) with lump-sum progress billing, high cash density on site payroll, and no continuous invoice trail to the ultimate industrial client.

Defense evidence: Signed EPC or masonry subcontracts; project engineers' bills of quantities and works certificates; progress-payment schedules certified by the client's project manager; supplier invoices for refractory bricks, castables, mortars, and kiln furniture; transport and customs or waybill documents; VAT returns and the social-insurance salary register evidencing that cash draws are limited to genuine payroll and per-diem payments.
Non-resident UBO holding the LLC through an offshore holding structure with no Egyptian tax identity, no audited financial statements, or a disconnected commercial footprint.

Root cause: Non-resident UBO holding the LLC through an offshore holding structure with no Egyptian tax identity, no audited financial statements, or a disconnected commercial footprint.

Defense evidence: Apostilled certificate of incorporation and good-standing certificate of the shareholder; the offshore shareholder's audited financial statements for at least two years; a bank reference letter from the shareholder's principal bank; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds declaration with supporting asset and income documentation; certified passports of UBOs; and a clear Egyptian tax-residence and permanent-establishment analysis for the shareholder showing that the Egyptian LLC alone carries the taxable operational substance.
Large inbound shareholder loans or equity subscriptions denominated in USD/EUR received from a low-corporate-tax or secrecy-preference jurisdiction, triggering transfer-pricing and round-tripping suspicion.

Root cause: Large inbound shareholder loans or equity subscriptions denominated in USD/EUR received from a low-corporate-tax or secrecy-preference jurisdiction, triggering transfer-pricing and round-tripping suspicion.

Defense evidence: LLC board resolutions approving the capital increase or loan; a written, ARM'S-LENGTH loan or subscription agreement with commercial rationale tied to working capital and project Mobilisation; the shareholder's consolidated financials and intercompany ledgers evidencing the source of the funds; official proof of remittance via the banking channel; a certificate of the loan's pricing under Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation requirements; and the CBE foreign-direct-investment notification confirmation issued by the receiving bank.
Payment relationships with suppliers or sub-subcontractors domiciled in sanctions-affected or high-risk jurisdictions, in connection with imported refractory ceramic materials or equipment.

Root cause: Payment relationships with suppliers or sub-subcontractors domiciled in sanctions-affected or high-risk jurisdictions, in connection with imported refractory ceramic materials or equipment.

Defense evidence: Comprehensive sanctions, PEP, and adverse-media screening results for every counterparty; End-User/End-Use certificates for imported refractory and thermal-insulation materials; full shipping, customs, and bill-of-lading file; anti-bribery representations; the LLC's internal trade-compliance policy; and a formal compliance attestation signed by the LLC's designated compliance officer stating that no designated person or sanctioned jurisdiction is the ultimate beneficiary.
Inconsistent account activity: high-frequency progress wires into the account followed immediately by large transfers toward overseas traders, without retention of EGP for local salaries, VAT, and corporate-tax instalments.

Root cause: Inconsistent account activity: high-frequency progress wires into the account followed immediately by large transfers toward overseas traders, without retention of EGP for local salaries, VAT, and corporate-tax instalments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and shareholder-loan injections must be remitted in freely convertible currency (USD/EUR/GBP) by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's corporate account to a licensed Egyptian bank designated by CBE for the LLC's accounts. The wire must clearly reference the direct-investment purpose (equity subscription) or intercompany-loan purpose in the beneficiary and remittance-information fields, together with the LLC's commercial registration number, to permit correct CBE balance-of-payments classification. On receipt, the dealing bank will verify the underlying resolution and subscription/loan agreement, complete the CBE foreign-investment notification for inward direct investment under Law No. 72 of 2017, and issue a foreign-exchange and deposit certificate; funds must initially be credited to the Capital Escrow Account before conversion or transfer to the Operational Account for project disbursement. For shareholder loans, the rate, maturity, and currency must be supported by an arm's-length written agreement and evidenced in Egyptian transfer-pricing records, and the bank may request a tax-clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority before onward conversion of loan proceeds.Disbursements for imported refractory materials shall be executed by SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer or by an irrevocable, confirmed (if required) commercial letter of credit at sight issued by the LLC's Egyptian bank in favour of the offshore supplier, upon presentation of the commercial invoice, packing list, marine insurance certificate, and Egyptian Customs pre-arrival declaration; imports and related transfers are subject to CBE reporting and to the bank's verification of the supplier's sanction status. For local settlement of subcontractors and staff, the LLC must use the CBE-operated RTGS (Real-Time Gross Settlement) for transactions above the bank's high-value threshold and the Bulk Electronic Payment System (BEPS) for low-value and repetitive payments including payroll and social insurance; intercompany dividends and profit remittances to the foreign shareholder may be routed by MT103 only upon presentation of audited financials, tax certificates, and the minutes approving the distribution.Onshore deliverable EGP/USD and EGP/EUR forward contracts with the transactional Egyptian bank, provided the LLC evidences underlying import or export exposure through invoices and trade contracts.Onshore option strategies embedded in risk-participation or barrier-forward products offered by the dealing bank where permitted by CBE product approvals.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps and forward-rate agreements with licensed dealing banks, subject to documented underlying trade or financing exposure and regular mark-to-market collateralisation.Natural-balance-sheet hedging through parallel EGP and USD/EUR operational accounts, matching collections on foreign-currency projects with subsequent imports of refractories and equipment.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) to be used only by the parent treasury as a portfolio overlay outside the Egyptian LLC, and never as a direct onshore hedge for EGP accounting or tax positions, because the Egyptian entity and its regulators require hedges to be economically linked to actual commercial exposure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% flat corporate income tax on taxable profits of an Egyptian Limited Liability Company under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended (banks, petroleum and Suez Canal special regimes excluded).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross dividend distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to its shareholders (Law 91/2005 Article 56); the Egyptian LLC is the statutory withholding agent and must remit the 10% to the ETA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return and final tax balance are due within 4 months after the fiscal year-end (Article 32, Law 91/2005); for a 31-December fiscal year-end, the statutory filing date is 30 April of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate controlled (related-party) transactions exceed EGP 10 million in a tax year; Master File is required when aggregate controlled transactions exceed EGP 60 million or the multinational group meets the Egyptian CbC reporting threshold (consolidated group revenue above approximately EGP 7.2 billion / EUR 750 million equivalent).

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a Gulf-style annual ESR notification regime, but Law 91/2005 and the ETA TP Guidelines require controlled transactions to be supported by genuine economic substance, including actual employees, physical equipment, project sites, and risk-bearing. Arrangements lacking commercial reality or existing primarily to shift taxable profit may be disregarded, recharacterized, or have their intercompany pricing adjusted by the ETA under general anti-avoidance principles. A refractory procurement contract routed through a non-resident intermediary with no personnel or inventory is per se vulnerable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Under-Declared Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution before the Tax Offenses Court: imprisonment for up to 5 years and/or a criminal fine of double the evaded tax (not less than EGP 50,000); the ETA additionally imposes delay tax at 1.5% per month on the evaded amount and may estimate the tax base on a deemed-profit basis.

Director personal liability: The general manager, finance director, and any director certifying the false return face joint criminal liability, personal civil restitution of the evaded tax, placement on the Egyptian travel-ban list, and seizure of personal bank accounts; the corporate veil can be pierced where funds were diverted to the sole shareholder.
Artificial Transfer Pricing and Invoice Pass-Through Shell

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer pricing adjustment under OECD-aligned methodologies; disallowance of the inflated cost portion; recharacterization of the overpayment as a constructive dividend (10% WHT) or deemed technical service fee (20% WHT); plus delay tax at 1.5% per month and general anti-avoidance penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements and TP documentation are jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; deliberate use of a conduit entity to evade Egyptian CIT can trigger criminal tax-evasion charges and confiscation of the intermediary's commissions.
E-Invoicing and ETA B2G Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines per missing e-invoice under Ministerial Decree 352/2020 and Law 17/2018; disallowance of the related CIT deduction; suspension of the tax clearance certificate necessary for bidding on Ministry of Electricity, petroleum, and Suez Canal Economic Zone contracts.

Director personal liability: The responsible director and accounting manager are held jointly liable for the fixed procedural penalties; repeated non-compliance leads to criminal referral and disqualification from public-sector tenders, including travel bans against the legal representative.
AML Violation and Undocumented Cash Labor

Statutory sanction: AML criminal exposure: imprisonment for up to 5 years and fines up to EGP 5,000,000, plus forfeiture of the cash proceeds; on the CIT side, the undocumented wage expense is disallowed, the tax base is re-estimated, and delay tax of 1.5% per month accrues.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated AML compliance officer are personally prosecutable for reporting failures; the cash-rich shell pattern exposes the 100% non-resident shareholder to asset-forfeiture proceedings and civil recovery actions, and the director's personal assets may be reached under veil-piercing doctrines.
