Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on LLCs under the current Egyptian framework; the authorized capital is freely determined in the Articles of Association. The entire issued capital must be fully subscribed at formation and the corresponding funds deposited in a temporary bank account opened in the name of the company under formation, remaining blocked until the Commercial Registry certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: General Civil Defense Directorate fire safety clearance is required for the leased office, including delivery of a fire safety certificate under Civil Defense Law No. 12 of 1983 and administrative regulations. The building must provide structural fitness certificate, fire extinguishers per area, clear evacuation paths, and a workplace health and safety certificate for employees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance, official taxpayer portal https://www.eta.gov.eg; the Egyptian Customs Authority (ECA) is the competent customs authority, with the NAFEZA single-window foreign trade and customs clearance portal at https://www.nafeza.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipts portals at https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg and https://ereceipt.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

The foreign equity participants of an Egyptian limited liability company (EG_LLC) first complete incorporation through the General Authority for Investment and Foreign Trade (GAFI) one-stop shop, obtain the Commercial Registry extract, then file the unified tax registration application at the competent ETA district tax office. Required evidence pack: GAFI incorporation certificate, notarized articles of association translated into Arabic, Commercial Registry extract, board resolution authorizing registration, notarized power of attorney, original lease or purchase contract for the registered office, bank certificate of share capital deposit if applicable, passport copies and tax cards of managers and authorized signatories, and the digital e-signature certificates procured from the ETA accreditation authority. On approval, the ETA issues one unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) covering income tax, VAT, payroll tax and stamp duty, followed by the statutory Tax Card and a separate VAT registration certificate; new LLCs are simultaneously enrolled into the mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts systems with a sandbox integration testing obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The limited liability company is a juridical person under Egyptian Commercial Companies Law 159/1981 and is taxed separately under Income Tax Law 91/2005 at the 22.5% corporate rate on its worldwide profits with no pass-through to shareholders; no check-the-box or fiscal transparency election is available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Egyptian domestic law. Available elections under Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 15/2023 and executive regulations include: (i) depreciation method election (straight-line versus declining balance) subject to ETA approval; (ii) small-business cash-basis election for turnover up to EGP 3,000,000; (iii) VAT input-credit pro-rata apportionment election for mixed taxable and exempt activities; (iv) Investment Law 72/2017 incentive elections, including accelerated depreciation, tax credit certificates and special zone regimes in the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE); and (v) foreign tax credit election instead of a deduction for foreign taxes paid under the applicable bilateral tax treaty, applied on an Egyptian-law-source basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable supplies of goods and services triggers mandatory VAT registration under Article 13 of Law 67/2016; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold. Law 167 of 2022 additionally authorises the Minister of Finance to align a special small taxpayer presumptive regime within an EGP 500,000 to EGP 5,000,000 annual turnover band, under which businesses may be relieved from standard VAT compliance in exchange for a simplified lump-sum charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% for exported goods and qualifying export services arises under Articles 8 and 12 of VAT Law 67/2016. For the multilingual localization agency (ISIC 7490), the following cumulative criteria must be satisfied: (i) the translation, dubbing, subtitling, transcreation or project-management service is contractually supplied to a non-resident client, and the legal, economic and practical benefit of the service is entirely outside Egypt; (ii) the service is truly performed from Egypt but not exploited, used or enjoyed by any Egyptian resident or within Egyptian territory; (iii) the consideration is paid in freely convertible foreign currency received through the Egyptian banking system, with Wise or Payoneer corporate balances subsequently settled into an Egyptian-licensed bank account and evidenced by a banking credit advice; (iv) a written international supply contract identifies the foreign counterparty and deliverables, supported by delivery records, client acceptance correspondence, source and localized file archives and foreign-currency conversion confirmations; and (v) the export sale is correctly declared in the monthly VAT return with full source documentation retained. For physical deliverables included in the commercial scope, legal exportation through Egyptian customs within the regulatory window is additionally required.

  • NAFEZA e-Customs Export Declaration (Aqaba digitization platform) with ETA validation stamp and Egypt customs export code
  • Original Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure of goods from Egyptian customs territory; for cross-border digital services, retained digital delivery and server-use logs demonstrating overseas consumption
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or banking credit advice issued by an Egyptian-licensed bank confirming receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency within the Central Bank of Egypt repatriation window
  • Commercial invoice, perfected export services or goods contract, and non-resident certificate of the foreign buyer or client
  • ETA export evidence register and the monthly VAT return attachments substantiating the 0% declared export supply

Egypt mandates real-time electronic invoicing under ETA decisions 52/2020, 56/2020 and 139/2021 with phased implementation through 2023. Every VAT-registered entity, including a newly incorporated EG_LLC, must technically integrate its billing or ERP system with the ETA platform via the approved REST API (JSON and XML schemas), obtain an ETA integration certificate and SA/RA digital signing certificates, and transmit each B2B e-invoice for fiscal clearance before issuance. The ETA validates every document in real time and returns a Unique Identifier (UUID), an ETA validation code and a QR code in TLV protocol, which acts as the digital stamp replacing any physical rubber stamp or ink mark. B2C e-receipts are separately mandatory from 1 April 2022. Any invoice or receipt not cleared through the ETA platform is legally invalid, non-deductible for input VAT purposes and subject to a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000 per violation under Article 62 of Law 67/2016, with prosecution where repeated.

The ETA applies enhanced scrutiny to exporters claiming zero-rating and input-VAT refunds, cross-matching the NAFEZA customs declarations, the e-invoicing system, the Central Bank of Egypt foreign-currency database, banking remittance records and the beneficiary ownership register. If export zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or with false or incomplete documentation, the ETA will retroactively assess standard-rate VAT of 14% on the full consideration, disallow all related input VAT credits, impose a monthly delay surcharge of 2% per month on the assessed VAT (capped at double the tax), apply fixed administrative fines of EGP 20,000 to EGP 50,000 per violating invoice, and claw back any VAT refunds already paid together with accrued interest under the five-year statutory limitation period. Deliberate evasion is referred to the Public Funds Prosecutor under Article 59 of Law 67/2016 and Article 133 of Income Tax Law 91/2005, exposing responsible legal representatives to imprisonment and criminal fines at least equal to the evaded tax, plus civil penalties of up to 300% of the assessed difference; export support rebate programmes and refund certificate privileges are simultaneously suspended or revoked.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) is convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, in accordance with Article 113 of Law 194/2020 and the CBE's registration requirements; capital account convertibility is restricted and requires prior CBE approval, particularly for derivative transactions and external borrowing. Profit remittance for foreign direct investment is permitted on the basis of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and documentary evidence of the original injection of capital, but may face episodic administrative delays due to foreign currency availability and bank-level correspondent limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE directives and the Unified Banking Law, all export proceeds for goods shipped from Egypt must be credited into an Egyptian licensed bank account within a maximum of six months from the date of issuance of the customs declaration or shipping bill; the mandatory surrender ratio is generally 0% for service exports, but goods exporters are required to repatriate 100% of proceeds into the Egyptian banking system, with the option to retain the foreign currency in an export proceeds account, subject to CBE approval for any conversion to EGP and for onward transfer to third parties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary authority and foreign exchange administrator, operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 and its implementing directives.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Payoneer Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — (Wise_B2B, Payoneer). Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed banks in Egypt and cannot provide local EGP clearing, but they can be used as ancillary cross-border receivables and disbursement channels for low-value localization services, provided all Egyptian source transactions are routed through a licensed bank and all proceeds in foreign currency are repatriated into the Egyptian banking system when the service is export-related; the CBE prohibits the use of EMIs for settlement of domestic Egyptian invoices or for circumvention of the banking channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholder capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from an external account in the name of the foreign shareholder to a temporary Egyptian pound or foreign currency account at a licensed Egyptian bank, using purpose code CBE-Import/Service Payment or 'Capital Investment' under CBE Form 8 (Deposit of Capital), followed by submission of the CBE capital registration certificate to the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and the Egyptian Tax Authority; verification is executed through the commercial invoice, share subscription undertaking, and the bank's Form 10 notification.Trade settlements are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for high-value transferable debt obligations, with direct credit to the Egyptian correspondent account of the receiving bank; commercial letters of credit (L/C) are used for medium-duration localization service procurements involving subcontracted dubbing studios, narration studios, and voice artists in non-EGY countries, and disbursements are governed by UCP 600, requiring presentation of invoices, shipping/video delivery certificates, and customs or intangible service delivery acknowledgment.FX Forwards – deliverable forward contracts with a licensed Egyptian bank for a period not exceeding 360 days and only with an underlying trade or service transactionFX Swap (Cross-Currency Swap) – permitted between EGP and a foreign currency provided that both legs have a confirmed underlying current-account or capital-account economic transaction and the tenors match the underlying settlement datesNon-deliverable forward (NDF) – permitted for offshore risk management through a non-resident bank only if executed as an internal hedge or with the Egyptian bank acting as the pricing agent and the transaction being backed by an actual localization service receivable

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% (Income Tax Law No. 91/2005, Article 8, as in force for tax years beginning 2023).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on profit distributions paid by an Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005, Article 42; no additional branch-remittance tax applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual Form No. 44 is due within four calendar months after the fiscal year-end (e.g., 31 December fiscal year-end -> due 30 April). Quarterly advance installments are due by the end of Q1, Q2 and Q3; any final balance is payable with the annual return.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the last day of each of the first three quarters of the fiscal year; for a 31 December year-end, by 31 March, 30 June and 30 September respectively.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Headers / Registration Legal name, Tax Card number, commercial register number, ETA file number, address, fiscal year-end, reporting currency, and the ISIC 7490 localization business scope. The Egyptian LLC is the taxpayer; non-resident shareholders are not named on the return. A resident legal representative with notarized power of attorney must be identified. Late filing: fixed fine of EGP 100 to EGP 10,000 plus a monthly delay surcharge of 2% of the assessed tax for the first six months, rising to 2.5% per month thereafter, capped at 100% of the tax (Tax Procedures Law No. 206/2020, Articles 74-75). Late quarterly installments attract the same monthly surcharge; non-registration for a Tax Card is punishable with a fine of EGP 20,000 to EGP 50,000.
