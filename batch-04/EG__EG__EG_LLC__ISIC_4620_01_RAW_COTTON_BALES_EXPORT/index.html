Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - One-Stop-Shop (OSS), Cairo Main Office, in coordination with the Commercial Registry Office at the Ministry of Trade and Industry.

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 on Joint Stock Companies, Partnerships Limited by Shares and Limited Liability Companies, as amended by Law No. 4 of 2015, Articles 1, 2, 3, 26, 27 and 70-76; Executive Regulations issued under Ministerial Decree No. 96 of 1982; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations (Ministerial Decree No. 2310 of 2017); Civil Defense Law and the Unified Commercial Registry Law No. 34 of 1976.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Incorporation Contract and Articles of Association (Système de Droit égyptien - 'Uqd Al-Ta'sis wa Al-Nizam Al-Assassi) together with the GAFI Incorporation Application and the Capital Deposit Certificate; final instrument is the GAFI Certificate of Incorporation and the Unified Commercial Registry extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to Egyptian Limited Liability Companies under Law 159 of 1981 as amended by Law 4 of 2015. The founding quotaholders define the nominal capital in the Articles of Association; the subscribed capital must be fully covered by cash deposit or in-kind contribution prior to incorporation. A bank certificate evidencing deposit into a temporary corporate settlement account must be submitted; no escrow account is mandated but the bank shall hold the funds until GAFI issues the incorporation certificate.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for a non-industrial trading and brokerage entity. The general commercial licensing authority is the Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade - Business Licensing Directorate, exercised through the GAFI One-Stop-Shop. No industrial license from the Egyptian Industrial Development Authority (IDA) is triggered for ISIC 4620 wholesale brokerage of ginned raw cotton and synthetic yarns.

Category: Al-Ijtari Business Operation License (commercial registration and shop license) issued under the Egyptian Commercial Law and Ministerial decrees of the Ministry of Trade and Industry, authorizing the wholesale brokerage, export trading and documentary operations in raw cotton bales and synthetic yarns.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — statutory corporate and indirect tax administration; registration portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg/; e-invoicing portal: https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg/

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Ministry of Trade and Industry Commercial Registry. Step 2: Within 30 days of commercial registration, file tax registration Form 8 at the competent ETA District Tax Office, presenting the commercial register extract, certificated constitutional documents, board resolution appointing the local tax representative, ID/passport copies of managers, and proof of the registered head-office premises. Step 3: Obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) certificate. Step 4: Register for VAT (mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; otherwise voluntary registration) in order to claim input VAT refunds on cotton procurement and export overheads. Step 5: Open an Egyptian foreign-currency bank account and obtain the exporter code through the CBE-linked bank to evidence inbound FX for zero-rated export proceeds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: An Egyptian LLC is an opaque resident taxpayer taxed on worldwide income at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5% (Law No. 91 of 2005, Art. 52); non-resident branches are taxed on Egypt-source income only (Territorial_Scope); no pass-through eligibility applies to LLCs; a foreign investor may instead structure through the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) or a 100% foreign-owned LLC without a mandatory local partner.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; Egyptian LLCs are per se corporations. Available elections: (a) election to depreciate fixed assets on the diminishing-balance method with ETA consent (default is straight-line); (b) election of the cash-basis VAT accounting method for sales with a credit period exceeding one year under Art. 48 of the VAT Executive Regulations; (c) election to appoint a fiscal representative for non-resident registrants; (d) tax losses carried forward up to 5 years (mandatory). Hybrid-mismatch rules: Law 91/2005 Art. 55 disallows deductibility of interest, royalties, and management fees paid to related parties located in preferential-tax jurisdictions (effective rate below 50% of the Egyptian rate) and imposes a 20% withholding on such outbound payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration threshold: EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover of goods and services (Art. 15, VAT Law 67/2016); voluntary registration is permitted for traders in zero-rated export activities to obtain refunds of input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (1) goods physically exported from Egypt under the customs export regime within 90 days of supply (ginned cotton bales qualify when title passes to a non-resident buyer and goods leave Egypt); (2) ancillary transport, freight, handling, and export services for such goods; (3) services supplied to non-residents that are actually used or suffered entirely abroad and paid in foreign currency. Conditions: the supplier must be a registered VAT person, the customs export declaration must be matched to the zero-rated invoice, and the full export consideration must be received in foreign currency and credited to an Egyptian-licensed bank account; discounting or assignment of the receivable does not eliminate the FX-proof requirement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Computer-generated 'Bayan' export entry accepted by the Egyptian Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of the cotton bales from Egypt
  • Commercial Invoice (VAT zero-rated, bearing ETA-cleared e-invoice QR code and export invoice number)
  • Certificate of Origin (export-form B, attested by the competent Chamber of Commerce and relevant Ministry of Trade authorities)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit voucher evidencing foreign-currency receipt through an Egyptian bank)
  • Export Contract, SWIFT Letter of Credit, or Documentary Collection instrument matched to the customs value
  • Packing List and pre-shipment weight or grading certificate consistent with the customs declaration

Mandatory e-invoicing: All VAT-registered persons must integrate with the ETA e-Invoicing and B2C e-Receipts platform (einvoice.eta.gov.eg). Fiscal clearance: each invoice must be structured in the ETA JSON schema, digitally signed with the ETA-issued digital certificate, transmitted in real-time to the ETA validation API, cleared by the ETA fiscal engine, and only then stamped with a QR code (UUID-based) for legal issuance; invoices not cleared are treated as unissued. Non-compliance attracts a penalty of EGP 20,000 per invoice/transaction under VAT Law 67/2016 as amended.

The ETA may reassess zero-rated export claims within the general 5-year statutory audit window. If the export is not substantiated or the goods remain in Egypt, the ETA recovers the standard-rate VAT (14%), applies late-delivery additions of 2% per month on the unpaid tax (capped at 100% of the tax) under Law No. 206 of 2020, and for non-compliant e-invoices a penalty of EGP 20,000-50,000 per invoice. Deliberate evasion carries imprisonment (minimum 2 years), a fine of up to double the evaded tax, and personal liability of managers under the tax evasion provisions of Law No. 206 of 2020 Art. 21 and VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 Art. 71. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days additionally triggers Central Bank of Egypt sanctions: suspension of the exporter code, seizure of local-currency equivalent, and denial of future export financing or letter-of-credit facilities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains an IMF Article VIII current-account convertible regime. The Egyptian pound (EGP) is fully convertible for genuine current international transactions, including trade-related payments, service fees, accrued interest, and profit remittances, provided the underlying documentation is verified by an authorized bank. Capital-account operations are more restricted: equity capital injections must be registered with the CBE/Ministry of Investment, repatriation of equity sale proceeds requires proof of original inward transfer and tax clearance, and all foreign exchange operations must be conducted through a CBE-authorized bank. For an Egyptian LLC engaged in raw cotton bale export, profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is legally permissible after statutory annual financial statements are audited, tax clearance is obtained from the Egyptian Tax Authority, and the bank is provided with CBE-relevant remittance forms and board resolution authorizing the dividend/remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the prevailing CBE foreign exchange framework, an Egyptian exporter must repatriate 100% of export proceeds to Egypt through a CBE-authorized bank within 90 days from the date of customs export. No mandatory percentage conversion into EGP is currently required; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) or sell it to the banking system at the freely determined market rate. Raw cotton bale exports must be supported by a cotton export permit / phytosanitary inspection certificate and the presenting bank must match the SWIFT payment reference with the Customs Export Manifest, GOEIC documents, invoice, and packing list. Failure to repatriate within the 90-day statutory timeline may result in CBE penalty proceedings, a downgrade in bank financial standing, and denial of future trade finance facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and the General Authority for Export and Import Control (GOEIC); foreign exchange policy is administered by the CBE under the Exchange Control Act and the Banking Law No. 194/2020

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary banking rail. Offshore digital EMI institutions are not licensed by the Central Bank of Egypt and cannot hold Egyptian clearing accounts, issue local IBANs, or operate as an EGP settlement member. They may only act as auxiliary payment platforms that route funds through a licensed Egyptian commercial bank. Any inbound trade collections or outbound supplier payments in Egypt must ultimately clear through a CBE-authorized bank; use of an unlicensed EMI as the settlement layer creates CBE compliance risk, exchange-control rejection, and cash-flow latency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment capital into the Egyptian LLC must be wired through the SWIFT network to an Egyptian bank account using a clear outward remittance reference under the CBE's foreign-exchange classification for equity capital. The transferor should use an international purpose code consistent with foreign direct investment (typically referenced on the SWIFT MT103 in field 70 as 'CAPITAL INJECTION – FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT EQUITY' or the equivalent correspondent-bank instruction for share subscription). The receiving Egyptian bank will verify the Commercial Registry extract, tax card, board resolution approving share issuance, signed company constitutional documents, and confirmation of the entity's compliance with the CBE's transaction-code catalogue. Once received, the funds are entered into the company's capital escrow account and subsequently reclassified to the operational cumulative balance after the CBE registration process is completed. The company must obtain an internal CBE reference / bank registration letter for the capital inflow since this reference will be required for any future dividend remittance, capital reduction, or liquidation transfer.Outbound payments related to raw cotton trade settlement must be executed through a CBE-authorized bank using SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments, wire advances, branches-telegraphic-transfer settlements, and merchanting transactions; larger and more structured Nile-crossing trade parcels use commercial documentary letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and routed through the Egyptian bank's confirming/negotiating correspondent network. For export documentary collection, the presenting bank forwards shipping documents through DP/DA collection rails, and the final remittance is received by SWIFT MT103/202 from the collecting bank and posted to the exporter's FCA within the 90-day statutory period. Each outbound trade disbursement must be supported by board authorization, proforma or final invoice, purchase order, bill of lading, insurance certificate, and CBE/GOEIC declarations where applicable; banks will match the trade-payment purpose code with the economic activity code associated with ISIC 4620 and the cotton export license.Deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts (onshore, executed with a CBE-authorized bank under documented underlying trade exposure)Cross-currency swaps (plain-vanilla, bank-provided, hedging both principal and accrued cotton-export receivables)Foreign-exchange options (long/certain strategies permitted when the underlying is a genuine export receipt and no speculative net open position is created)Currency swap agreements through Egyptian banks where the CBE has issued a Master Agreement and the operationally embedded documentation satisfies the AML/KYC and trade verification requirementsCombination or structured FX hedging strategies under an ISDA/CSA master arrangement, with pricing collateral terms acceptable to the licensed Egyptian authorized bank

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million of aggregate annual related-party transaction value triggers mandatory Local File preparation and TP Form No. 29 filing; Master File is additionally required where the consolidated group annual turnover exceeds EGP 200 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate English-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the Egyptian Tax Authority applies anti-avoidance principles under Law 91/2005 (GAAR) and TP guidance requiring real business purpose, actual personnel, office, risk-bearing functions, and economic activity in Egypt. A cotton-trading LLC with no local substance, no employees, and no management activity in Egypt can be challenged as a dormant shell; income and costs may be re-attributed to the beneficial owner and free-zone tax exemptions revoked for non-substantive operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for a term not less than six months and not exceeding five years, and/or a fine between EGP 10,000 and EGP 100,000, plus mandatory collection of the evaded tax and an additional fine equal to the aggregate tax evaded, together with monthly late-payment interest under Law 91/2005.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct the under-reporting of cotton export revenue are jointly and severally liable to pay the corporate tax, penalties, and surcharges. Egyptian criminal courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and personal imprisonment.
Sham Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: ETA may disregard the legal personality of the arrangement under GAAR, deny treaty benefits, reattribute the full profit to the true economic beneficiary in Egypt, impose up to 100% additional tax on the under-declared amount, and levy fraud penalties together with cancellation of commercial registration under Companies Law No. 159/1981.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing is available in cases of fraud, sham transactions, or abuse of corporate separate liability; directors can face civil restitution of corporate tax debts, cross-border asset tracing, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment for false statements in corporate filings.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Compulsory tax adjustment by ETA using the TNMM/CUP/RPM/Cost-Plus/Profit-Split methods; once the adjusted taxable profit is re-calculated, the additional tax is assessed with 2% monthly default interest and an administrative penalty of up to 50% of the undercharged tax for non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for establishing and approving the intercompany pricing policy can be held personally liable for the tax adjustment if they knowingly participated in distortion; Egypt-issued travel bans and asset attachment can be imposed pending payment of the adjusted liability.
Money Laundering / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002 on Combating Money Laundering, the statutory penalties include imprisonment for a term between three and seven years and a fine not less than three times the value of the laundered funds, together with confiscation of cash proceeds, frozen bank accounts, and mandatory revocation of the commercial license.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors and senior banking signatories are separately indictable for AML violations; individuals may be held criminally liable for the entity’s acts where they had granting approval or failed to prevent the transaction, leading to arrest, travel bans, and personal asset confiscation independent of the LLC’s liability.
