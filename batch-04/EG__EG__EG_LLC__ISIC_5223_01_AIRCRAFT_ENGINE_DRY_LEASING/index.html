Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for an LLC; the founders set the amount, which must be adequate for the stated purpose. Each quota share must be fully paid at incorporation and the funds deposited into the company's bank account in Egypt, with a bank payment certificate submitted to the Commercial Registry. No escrow account is statutorily mandated.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic services portal for registration and e-invoicing: https://www.invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking to register for Egyptian tax purposes must apply through the ETA's electronic tax registration system using the Company Tax ID (CIF – Code d'Identification Fiscal) obtained from the Commercial Registry. The LLC must first be incorporated and registered with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or the Ministry of Trade and Industry's Commercial Registry, obtain a commercial registration certificate and a tax card (“Kart Daribah”), then apply for a VAT certificate and, if applicable, an e-invoicing verification ID. The application process for foreign-owned LLCs requires a locally appointed legal representative, constitutional documents certified under Egyptian law, proof of registered office, and board resolution approving the representative's signatory authority. TIN issuance is completed electronically within 10 business days, followed by physical issuance of the tax card.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity seeking to register for Egyptian tax purposes must apply through the ETA's electronic tax registration system using the Company Tax ID (CIF – Code d'Identification Fiscal) obtained from the Commercial Registry. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian tax law does not provide a check-the-box regime. An LLC is per se a taxable corporation subject to corporate income tax at entity level. No pass-through election is available for LLCs. Hybrid mismatch elections are governed by the Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended; taxpayers may apply for advance pricing arrangements and for double taxation relief treaty benefits, but no entity classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services; mandatory VAT registration within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; voluntary registration permitted below threshold for exporters and businesses with regular input tax recovery needs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods and services, including the export of turbofan aircraft engines under dry-lease arrangements, provided that the goods are physically exported from Egypt or the supply of services is performed and effectively used outside Egypt. The dry-lease income from a non-resident lessee in respect of an aircraft engine registered in a foreign jurisdiction qualifies as an export service when the lessee is a foreign airline or operator and the engine remains outside Egypt. The taxpayer must hold a valid export registration and must declare zero-rated supplies in the VAT return.

  • Export Customs Declaration (ACDF / ACI electronic filing confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT confirmation showing foreign currency conversion
  • Dry-lease agreement certified under Egyptian law
  • Certificate of Non-Egyptian Registration for the aircraft engine (where applicable)

The ETA mandates real-time electronic invoicing (Tawreed) and electronic receipts (Ghasl) for all registered taxpayers. Invoices must be generated using ETA-certified platforms, submitted digitally to the ETA's clearance system within 2 hours of issuance, and must contain a QR code and unique electronic invoice UUID. All taxable persons carrying on commercial or professional activity are required to integrate with the ETA API and use e-signature certificates. Non-compliance results in invoice rejection and monetary penalties.

The ETA conducts ex-post audits of export zero-rating claims, particularly for cross-border services. If the exporter fails to substantiate export status or does not remit foreign currency proceeds within 180 days (or the extended period granted by the Central Bank of Egypt), the zero-rating is retroactively reversed, VAT at 14% becomes payable, a late-payment addition of double the statutory monthly delay interest is imposed, and penalties up to EGP 50,000 may be assessed. Fraudulent claims trigger criminal liability under the Fraud and Evasion Law and may result in imprisonment, fines of up to EGP 100,000, and blacklisting from e-invoicing systems.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current-account transactions are freely convertible after tax clearance and beneficiary documentation, including aircraft engine rental remittances, service fees, and dividends out of audited profits. Capital-account transactions are subject to CBE-approved bank handling, mandatory purpose-code declaration, and, for foreign loans, CBE registration and substantiation of the underlying commercial transaction. De facto hurdles consist of periodic banking-system USD liquidity strain (as witnessed in 2022-2023), the queueing of dividend repatriation during balance-of-payments stress, the now-abolished mandatory 50% conversion rule, and the banks' practice of gating hard-currency payouts pending complete tax certificates and CBE compliance forms. Since the exchange-rate liberalisation of 6 March 2024, the EGP is market-determined and current-account convertibility is realistic, but capital-account prudence and strict source-of-funds diligence remain firmly enforced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from aircraft engine leasing and related commercial transactions must be repatriated through an Egyptian licensed bank within 180 days from the date of shipment (the on-carriage or delivery certificate date being the operative reference). During the 2022-2023 FX crisis, CBE directed a mandatory conversion of 50% of export proceeds into EGP; under the unified free-float regime effective 6 March 2024, exporters may retain 100% of their foreign-currency proceeds in onshore foreign-currency accounts. The receiving bank must be furnished with the customs export document, the executed dry-leasing agreement, and the cross-border invoice; proceeds cannot be released until the export declaration is matched in the CBE's electronic system.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting as the Foreign Exchange Authority under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and its Executive Regulations, together with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for capital movement registration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Currencycloud) cannot legally maintain EGP accounts or effect direct EGP-denominated clearing without obtaining a CBE payment-service-provider licence or partnering with an onshore licensed PSP under Law No. 18 of 2019 and CBE E-Money Regulations. For inbound lease rental collection, an offshore EMI may act only as a front-end collection agent that subsequent wires USD via SWIFT to the Egyptian bank account, which creates additional KYC friction and slower finality. Onshore PSPs such as Fawry, Paymob, or MNT-Halan can be used for low-value operational utility payments but are not a lawful or prudent substitute for the primary SWIFT/escrow clearing architecture required for high-value engine leasing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the foreign shareholder must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 with the payment purpose stated as 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Capital Contribution' and the beneficiary account designated as the EG_LLC capital-subscription account at the chosen tier-1 Egyptian bank. The sending bank must include the Egyptian registration certificate number and the GAFI project reference in field 72/79. On receipt, the Egyptian bank performs the CBE foreign-exchange declaration using the statutory purpose-code classification for inward FDI, verifies GAFI registration of the LLC, and credits a temporary capital account. The funds are released for operational use only after the capital-account activation formalities are completed with the Companies Registry and the bank has received the final certificate of paid-in capital. Escrow is not a distinct statutory account class in Egypt; the bank designates a ring-fenced capital deposit sub-account that functions as a de facto escrow until regulatory verification is complete. USD wires clear via the CBE's USD correspondent account (Nostro at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York) and EUR wires via TARGET2, with all covering instructions routed exclusively through approved Egyptian banks.Outbound trade and lease-related disbursements are executed through SWIFT MT103 with a clearly defined underlying trade reference: engine rental payments are tagged as 'Aircraft Engine Lease Rental - Operating Lease' and supplier disbursements for parts and MRO are tagged as 'Trade Payment for Aircraft Engine Components or Services'. For large OEM purchases or power-by-the-hour commitments, the Egyptian bank will issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit by SWIFT MT700/MT707 (usance 90-180 days) in favour of the foreign supplier, with presentation against airway bill, inspection certificate, and invoice set. Same-day domestic or emergency AOG disbursements use the target 'direct with cover' (DWC) method to the beneficiary's bank under the SWIFT GPI convention. All outbound USD disbursements are subject to the CBE's electronic matching against the original import/job documents and the maintenance of the required 180-day repatriation evidence, so the treasury must maintain a matching file for every outward debit.Onshore delivery-based FX forward (EGP/USD) - permitted by CBE-regulated Egyptian banks exclusively against an underlying commercial exposure (e.g., collected USD lease rentals converted to EGP for local running costs), tenor matched to the cash-flow profile, with no speculative or unbacked documentation accepted.Cross-currency swap (EGP/USD) - available from tier-1 Egyptian banks for corporates with confirmed USD receivable streams; requires the bank's internal treasury approval and evidenced lease contracts.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) in EGP - not permitted for Egyptian-resident corporates as a speculative overlay; only non-resident counterparties hedging EGP receivables may access the offshore NDF market.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) - permissible where the Egyptian entity has a USD-denominated lease-finance liability and wishes to align with EGP income, executed with a CBE-approved non-resident bank and collateralised in accordance with local margin rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Must be filed within 4 months after the end of the tax period. For a 31 December fiscal year-end, the due date is 30 April of the following calendar year. No statutory extension is generally granted; advance filings may be required for provisional tax settlements.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is identical to that of the corporate income tax return: within 4 months after the fiscal year-end. If the deadline falls on a weekend or public holiday, the due date moves to the next business day in accordance with ETA practice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory Local File is required when the aggregate raw value of all related-party controlled transactions in the tax year equals or exceeds EGP 6,000,000. A mandatory Master File is required when the consolidated annual turnover of the multinational group equals or exceeds EGP 500,000,000. For cross-border aircraft engine leases, all lease payments paid to or received from related parties are counted toward the EGP 6,000,000 threshold, and an annual TP Disclosure Form (Schedule TP-1) must be filed even if that threshold is not met, on the annual corporate tax return.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to the UAE ESR, but the Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 (Article 52) and judicial principles treat the commercial reality of the LLC as decisive. For an Egyptian aircraft engine dry-leasing entity to be recognised for tax and treaty purposes, it must demonstrate: real management in Egypt, local bank account and invoicing records, actual employees engaged in leasing negotiations, a board that exercises active operational decisions, sufficient risk capital at risk, and a profit potential consistent with its functions and risks. If the entity is a shell or its activities are economically hollow, ETA may recharacterise the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, or apply CFC rules to attribute the leasing profits to the parent company. A lease between affiliated companies must preserve arm's-length substance tests; a court or the ETA may pierce the corporate veil when the LLC is used as a pure conduit to hide ownership of aircraft engines.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration of Engine Lease Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 (Arts. 90, 111): imprisonment and a fine of up to 20% of the evaded tax, monthly late-payment penalty of 2% on the unpaid tax (capped at 30%), loss of eligibility for tax incentives, and compulsory collection of the tax due from the company's movable and immovable assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and external accountants who knowingly participate in the false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, interest, and penalties. Criminal conviction can include imprisonment of up to 5 years; the court may order travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts, and seizure of personal property; the directors are disqualified from managing any Egyptian company for up to 5 years.
Shell Company and Artificial Substantive Structure

Statutory sanction: ETA may apply substance-over-form and disregard the entity; the lease income will be reallocated to the true beneficial owner and taxed at 22.5% without treaty relief. Additional tax of at least 30% of the understated tax can be imposed, together with cancellation of tax registration, invalidation of tax clearances, and referral to the Public Prosecutor for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: When the LLC is used to contravene the income tax law, the corporate veil is pierced. Directors are jointly liable for the total tax deficiency, civil damages for trade creditors, and criminal penalties if they signed false declarations; travel bans and asset freezes may be ordered under the tax collection law, and the directors cannot hold board positions in other Egyptian companies during the term of the ban.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Engine Lease Rentals

Statutory sanction: ETA will make a transfer pricing adjustment to an arm's-length rental, disallow the excessive deduction, and levy additional corporate tax at 22.5%. The understatement penalty can reach 40% of the adjusted additional tax; monthly late-payment interest of 2% is also applied. Failure to submit the required disclosure may trigger automatic penalty assessments without further proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign inaccurate TP documentation are solidarily liable for the resulting additional tax and penalties. Criminal prosecution for making a false statement to the ETA can be initiated; the responsible officers may be subjected to travel restrictions and seizure of personal assets, and professional disqualification can follow.
Non-Withholding and Failure to Remit Dividend/Interest/Royalty WHT

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian company is personally liable for the unremitted withholding amount; ETA can demand payment together with a late remittance penalty of 2% per month and a fine up to EGP 50,000. In aggravated cases, criminal proceedings under the Income Tax Law and the AML Law may be commenced if the failure was part of an evasion scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers who direct the payment without withholding are personally liable for the withheld tax plus penalties. The ETA may obtain a civil judgment and enforce travel bans and asset freezes against those individuals. If non-withholding is coupled with offshore bank accounts hiding the payment, directors face imprisonment exposure under the Criminal Code and AML legislation.
Money Laundering and Sanctions Violation in Aircraft Engine Leasing

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002 and its amendments (Law No. 36/2021), money laundering offences attract imprisonment of 3 to 7 years and fines of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000, must be disgorged for the entity involved, and the ETA will report the matter to the Egyptian Money Laundering Combat Unit (started as the AML Unit). The company may be subject to asset forfeiture and registration cancellation; the sanction also includes ruin of operating licences and loss of government contracts.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who intentionally ignore suspicious lease payments or fail to implement AML internal controls are personally liable under the AML law and can be imprisoned, fined, and banned from travelling abroad. The court may impose a proportionate fine equal to the value of the laundered funds, order forfeiture of the directors' own interests in the company, and bar the directors from managing any Egyptian financial or corporate entity for up to 10 years.
