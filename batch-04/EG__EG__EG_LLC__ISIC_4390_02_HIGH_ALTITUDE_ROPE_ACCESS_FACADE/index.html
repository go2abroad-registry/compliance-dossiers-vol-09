Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; central e-portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC must file its contract of association, commercial registry certificate, board resolution or power of attorney, and applicable activity licenses with the ETA to obtain a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card. Registration is effected through the ETA head office or the electronic tax services portal and must precede any taxable supply, import, or invoicing activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC must file its contract of association, commercial registry certificate, board resolution or power of attorney, and applicable activity licenses with the ETA to obtain a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card. Registration is effected through the ETA head office or the electronic tax services portal and must precede any taxable supply, import, or invoicing activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide Check-the-Box or hybrid-mismatch classification elections. LLCs are mandatorily taxed at entity level under Law No. 91 of 2005. Relevant practical elections are limited to voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold, depreciation-method selection under Article 34 of the income tax law, and revaluation elections for assets; no pass-through classification is permitted for LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover; mandatory registration applies once supplies exceed this threshold within a 12-month period

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated under the VAT Law where the export is evidenced by a customs export declaration. Services are zero-rated when rendered and consumed wholly outside Egypt, or directly connected to exported goods, including construction and installation services performed on foreign-located structures. The high-altitude facade services qualify only where the physical worksite is outside Egypt and all economic activity occurs abroad; zero-rating does not apply to services consumed in Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) bearing the ETA customs office stamp and unique export number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice proving FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Signed contract and commercial invoice referencing the zero-rated export supply
  • Proof of service performance abroad (site presence, completion reports) for offshore services

Mandatory electronic invoicing and e-receipting via the ETA API. Each invoice must be transmitted in JSON/XML format and validated in real time; the ETA returns a unique UUID and QR/stamp before the document is recognized for tax purposes. Taxpayers exceeding the phase thresholds must integrate their ERPs or billing systems with the ETA network, apply an electronic QR code, and keep digital records for at least 10 years.

Fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating triggers reassessment of VAT at 14%, withdrawal of the refund, and a penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax plus additional daily late-interest; administrative fines of EGP 20,000 to EGP 1,000,000 apply for electronic invoicing violations, and deliberate misdeclaration carries criminal indictment and imprisonment under Law No. 67 of 2016. The ETA may also claw back refunds for up to five years and assess transfer-pricing adjustments where export pricing is distorted.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the 6 March 2024 exchange-rate unification, the EGP operates as a bank-intermediated managed float with daily weighted-average interbank fixing. Current-account transactions (construction services exports, goods imports) are freely convertible upon documentary compliance (signed subcontracts, pro-forma invoices, import declaration forms). Capital-account transactions — foreign equity injections, shareholder loans, profit remittance — remain administratively restricted: foreign capital must be registered with GAFI or the CBE to secure future repatriation rights, and profit remittance requires audited financial statements, ETA tax clearance, and the original inflow registration. Practical remittance clearing takes 5–15 business days. EGP is not yet fully Article VIII (IMF) convertible; residual surrender and registration obligations persist on capital transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Services export proceeds must be credited to the EG_LLC's designated foreign-currency account at an Egyptian licensed bank within a maximum of 90 days from the invoice date, extendable to 180 days by the dealing bank upon contract-backed justification. Under the CBE directive effective with the 2023–2024 unified-rate framework, 50% of gross services export proceeds must be converted into EGP at the bank's daily published market rate; strategic industrial goods exports are subject to a higher 70% conversion ratio unless expressly exempted. The un-surrendered 50% may be retained in USD in the same export-proceeds account for hedging collateral, import settlements, or permitted distributions. All receipts must flow through the Egyptian designated bank; settlement via any third-country intermediary is treated as non-repatriation and subjects the entity to CBE supervisory sanctions and restriction of new FX facilities. The treasury mandate of the EG_LLC must auto-adapt to the CBE ratio table in force on the date of each receipt.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — statutory mandate under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, the Foreign Exchange Law No. 38 of 1994, and delegated prudential directives issued by the CBE Board of Directors, coordinated with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary treasury operations. Egyptian law does not recognize an electronic money institution (EMI) banking category; only CBE-licensed banks may accept corporate deposits, issue corporate IBANs, or clear through EGCH / InstaPay. The Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) licenses non-bank payment service providers that may facilitate wallets and payment initiation but cannot hold commercial balances or originate SWIFT MT103. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise-type, Qonto, Bankera) cannot onboard the EG_LLC for EGP receipts, cannot apply the CBE export-proceeds surrender standing order, and their use would render proceeds non-repatriated. Permissible ancillary use: a non-resident disbursement-only virtual IBAN for paying GCC-based subcontractor payroll, provided no EGP clearing and no surrender obligation attaches. The primary treasury must remain at NBE, Banque Misr, or CIB.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where related-party transactions in the fiscal year exceed EGP 5 million. Master File is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenues exceeding EGP 50 million. Spontaneous filing deadlines align with the tax return due date.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) regime, but economic substance is comprehensively tested through the general anti-avoidance rule (Article 47, Law 91/2005), the substance-over-form doctrine, and TP rules. A company must demonstrate real local staff, physical assets, and operational management to sustain deductible expenses and treaty benefits.
