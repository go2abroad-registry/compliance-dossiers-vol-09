Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official e-portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg. The ETA administers corporate income tax (Law No. 91 of 2005), Value Added Tax (Law No. 67 of 2016), stamp duty, and payroll-related taxes, while the Egyptian Customs Authority (ECA) separately operates the ASYCUDA World customs platform and export controls.

E-government filing portal

For an Egyptian LLC, the statutory sequence is: (1) obtain commercial registration with the Ministry of Trade and Industry under the Companies Law and file the investment with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI); (2) submit the unified tax registration application to the competent ETA directorate (in person or through the ETA portal), attaching the certificate of incorporation, notarized memorandum of association, board resolution and power of attorney appointing an Egyptian resident tax representative, and proof of the registered office; (3) receive from the ETA the unified Tax Card (Bitaqa Daibia) carrying a single numeric Tax Identification Number (TIN) usable identically for income tax, VAT, customs, and payroll files; and (4) open a taxpayer file with the assigned ETA inspection unit (Mahalla). A non-resident supplier without a permanent establishment may satisfy Egyptian tax obligations through withholding by the Egyptian counterparty and does not strictly require a TIN, but any Egyptian LLC that exports goods must possess a TIN and complete VAT registration before lodging its first export customs declaration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: an Egyptian LLC is a separate juridical person and a resident taxpayer subject to income tax at entity level under Law No. 91 of 2005 at the headline corporate rate of 22.5% (no pass-through or fiscal transparency treatment is available to LLCs). Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, with an exemption for qualifying intercorporate dividends; territorial scope applies only to non-resident companies, which are taxed solely on Egyptian-source income. No presumptive or tax-exempt-zone classification applies unless the venture is separately established in an investment free zone or inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone, in which case special treatment is elective rather than automatic. — For an Egyptian LLC, the statutory sequence is: (1) obtain commercial registration with the Ministry of Trade and Industry under the Companies Law and file the investment with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI); (2) submit the unified tax registration application to the competent ETA directorate (in person or through the ETA portal), attaching the certificate of incorporation, notarized memorandum of association, board resolution and power of attorney appointing an Egyptian resident tax representative, and proof of the registered office; (3) receive from the ETA the unified Tax Card (Bitaqa Daibia) carrying a single numeric Tax Identification Number (TIN) usable identically for income tax, VAT, customs, and payroll files; and (4) open a taxpayer file with the assigned ETA inspection unit (Mahalla). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Egyptian LLC is a separate juridical person and a resident taxpayer subject to income tax at entity level under Law No. 91 of 2005 at the headline corporate rate of 22.5% (no pass-through or fiscal transparency treatment is available to LLCs). Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income, with an exemption for qualifying intercorporate dividends; territorial scope applies only to non-resident companies, which are taxed solely on Egyptian-source income. No presumptive or tax-exempt-zone classification applies unless the venture is separately established in an investment free zone or inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone, in which case special treatment is elective rather than automatic.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a US-style check-the-box or elective transparency regime. Available statutory elections and specialized treatments include: (a) election to establish the trading project in a public or private free-zone scheme under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, which substitutes a presumptive annual levy for standard income tax and VAT; (b) election for investment-law tax incentives in ordinary zones (temporary income-tax relief and customs duty exemptions); (c) group consolidation election subject to Law 91/2005 affiliation, ownership, and loss-limitation conditions; (d) depreciation and capital-allowance elections; (e) tax-loss carry-forward (maximum five tax years); and (f) mandatory application of Egyptian GAAR and substance-over-form rules which deny tax liability reduction through hybrid mismatch outcomes rather than providing an elective hybrid-mismatch mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of taxable annual supplies of goods and services within a rolling 12-month period (Article 23, Law 67/2016); exporters of goods are required to register as a matter of practice even below this threshold in order to issue zero-rated invoices and obtain input VAT refunds. Non-resident suppliers of digital and electronic services are subject to a mandatory registration and collection regime with no threshold, and the threshold does not affect voluntary registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered Egyptian LLC may apply the 0% VAT rate under Article 29 of Law 67/2016 to export sales of bulk soybeans and yellow corn where (i) the goods are physically removed from Egypt under a valid export customs declaration; (ii) title and risk in the maritime cargo pass to a non-resident buyer under an FOB, CFR, CIF, or equivalent export contract; (iii) the export invoice is issued in a convertible foreign currency; (iv) the proceeds are received by a licensed Egyptian bank within the statutory 180-day repatriation window; and (v) the exporter retains the full customs and remittance evidence file supporting the designated export schedule of the periodic VAT return. Export-support brokerage, international transport, and handling services separately invoiced to a foreign counterparty in connection with such exports may likewise be zero-rated. Domestic sales of basic food items may be exempt rather than zero-rated; exemption does not permit input VAT recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Shurki) validated by the Egyptian Customs Authority on ASYCUDA World, evidencing the HS commodity codes (1201 for soybeans, 1005 for yellow corn), quantity, FOB value, vessel name, and date of exit from Egyptian customs territory
  • Bill of Lading issued by the ocean carrier and, where applicable, the charter-party summary confirming delivery and discharge at the non-resident buyer's destination port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian commercial bank (or equivalent SWIFT/electronic FX receipt confirmation) evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in convertible hard currency

Mandatory e-invoicing is phased in across all registered taxpayers (commencing with large taxpayers on 15 November 2020). Registered traders must (i) procure ETA-certified billing software or connect directly to the ETA integration APIs, (ii) issue invoices in the ETA-sanctioned XML/JSON schema, (iii) sign each invoice with the ETA-approved digital signature, (iv) transmit the invoice to the ETA portal for immediate fiscal validation, and (v) deliver the invoice bearing the ETA-issued QR code. Invoices rejected by the ETA are legally treated as not issued and deprive the exporter of the right to zero-rate the underlying supply. The complementary e-receipt mandate applies to business-to-consumer transactions, and export declarations must be lodged electronically on ASYCUDA World and linked to the exporter's VAT file.

The ETA, the Egyptian Customs Authority, and the Central Bank of Egypt operate a linked data-exchange program for export audits. An export sale proven not to have been shipped, not paid, or not remitted within the 180-day window is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply, subject to 14% VAT plus clawback of input VAT and denial of the zero rate, with assessment files reviewable for up to five years. Fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger VAT Law penalties (criminal fines of up to double the tax evaded and imprisonment of up to three years for deliberate evasion), monthly late-payment additions of 2% capped at 130% of the tax due, customs-law sanctions, and possible deregistration or blacklisting with the ETA, which also suspends any pending input VAT refund until full documentary verification of export proceeds is completed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt is an IMF Article VIII member and maintains current account convertibility subject to Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and CBE circulars. Capital account transactions, including inward equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, acquisition of municipal debt instruments, and profit remittances, require commercial-bank registration and documentary verification; certain transactions require prior CBE authorisation or notification. Profit remittance for a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC is legally permissible after submission of audited financial statements, filing of corporate tax returns, settlement of applicable dividend withholding tax, and issuance of the bank's inward remittance certificate. In practice, the speed and availability of foreign currency for outbound dividend payments is tied to prevailing FX liquidity and the bank's internal net open-position controls, but no general convertibility ban has been enacted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in respect of bulk grain (soybeans and yellow corn) must be repatriated and credited to an Egyptian licensed bank account within 180 calendar days from the bill-of-lading date. The CBE's unified float regime currently imposes no mandatory conversion ratio: exporters may retain export proceeds in the settlement currency. However, the CBE retains full regulatory authority, by circular, to impose temporary surrender ratios or minimum conversion thresholds during balance-of-payments adjustment episodes. Export documentation must include the customs export declaration, original commercial invoice, bill of lading, quality certificate, and any LC or contract evidencing the trade, in order to satisfy the bank's reporting obligations under the CBE's BOP return and to preserve the proceeds from being deemed unrepatriated.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Banking Operations Department and Foreign Exchange Control Department

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Egypt's Banking Law and the CBE's Payment Systems Law (Law No. 18 of 2019) require any financial institution effecting payment orders, issuing e-money, or clearing EGP flows to hold a valid CBE license. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK- or EU-licensed e-money institutions) cannot lawfully provide Egyptian IBANs, participate in the national EGP clearing infrastructure, or act as a beneficiary bank for EGP account to account transfers. They can provide ancillary cross-border settlement in hard currencies for non-EGP rails, but the Egyptian LLC will still be required to maintain a domestic bank account with a licensed commercial bank for statutory balance-of-payments reporting, wage payments, and tax remittances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection for the Egyptian LLC (share capital increase or founding subscription) must be routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 through a CBE-authorised commercial bank. The inward wire should reference the specific corporate transaction and include the full legal name and registration number of the buyer-company, the bank's SWIFT/BIC and beneficiary IBAN, the beneficial-owner name, and the designated CBE inward-purpose code (e.g., 'Direct Investment - Capital Increase'). The receiving bank will issue a certificate of inward transfer and register the transaction on the CBE's BOP and foreign-investment reporting systems within the statutory filing window. Supporting verification comprises: the notarised and legally translated board/shareholders' resolution approving the capital increase, the commercial registration (or upcoming amendment), the tax card and tax ID, proof of shareholder source of funds, and when relevant, a CBE equity subscription form. The LLC's bank account must be kept segregated in the books from operational cash flows during the capital escrow period to satisfy the bank's effective independent-monitoring requirement.For bulk soybean and corn trade imports, the principal clearing rails are irrevocable transferable letters of credit (UCP 600) and SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire transfers. An Egyptian issuing bank will open an LC in favour of the seller, often with confirmation from a high-quality correspondent bank when the seller requires country-risk coverage. Settlement against presentation of compliant documents (commercial invoice, bill of lading, phyto-sanitary and quality certificates, insurance, and any required trade/free-sale certificates) occurs via SWIFT. For smaller disbursements (brokerage commissions, demurrage charges, inspection fees), a direct SWIFT MT103 with BOP purpose codes is used. EGP-denominated local payments (customs, inland freight, port charges) are cleared over the CBE-operated RTGS system using the beneficiary's Egyptian IBAN.
