E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete LLC incorporation with GAFI (General Authority for Investment) and the Commercial Registry to obtain the commercial registration number. Step 2: Open a corporate tax file at the competent ETA district office by submitting the incorporation certificate, commercial registration, lease agreement, board resolution, and director ID; ETA issues the Tax Card containing the statutory TIN. No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Egypt's Income Tax Law 91/2005; LLCs are opaque corporate taxpayers. Available statutory elections include: (1) investment incentives and free zone status under the Investment Law 72/2017; (2) consolidated tax filing election for 100%-owned Egyptian resident subsidiaries upon ETA approval; (3) simplified cash-basis VAT accounting election for small taxpayers below the registration threshold; (4) foreign tax credit election to relieve double taxation on foreign-source income under Article 58 of Law 91/2005.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods and services; mandatory registration within 60 days of exceedance; voluntary registration permitted below threshold; simplified small-taxpayer registration available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to direct export of goods and services under Articles 29 and 30 of VAT Law 67/2016: goods physically exported from Egypt and services supplied to non-residents for use entirely outside Egypt, including digital payment infrastructure services, cross-border processing fees, and offshore software hosting; the taxpayer must retain full positive evidence of export and foreign residence of the recipient; services enjoyed or consumed inside Egypt do not qualify.

  • ACID-registered Customs Export Declaration with final outbound release certificate
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing Egyptian port of loading and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and signed export sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming hard-currency receipt and conversion to EGP within the CBE-prescribed period
  • Customs outward manifest validation printout evidencing physical exit
  • For digital services: geo-location logs, IP/UX evidence, and recipient residence certification demonstrating performance and enjoyment outside Egypt

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts regime for all registered taxpayers; real-time clearance of XML/JSON invoices, debit and credit notes through the ETA invoice platform using PKI digital certificates and fiscal QR stamping; phased rollout by taxpayer segment; non-cleared invoices are inadmissible as input VAT deduction evidence; EMI service fees and wallet management charges must carry mandatory fiscal codes; penalties for non-compliance under Law 67/2016 and Unified Tax Procedures Law 108/2021.

ETA conducts desk and field audits of export zero-rating claims, cross-matching customs declarations, bank inflows, and e-invoicing records; false or unsupported zero-rating triggers reversal of input VAT, an additional tax amount of up to 100% of the avoided duty plus late-payment interest, criminal fines and imprisonment for VAT fraud under Law 67/2016 Article 71 and Unified Tax Procedures Law 108/2021; refunds are suspended during criminal investigation; statute of limitations is 5 years for assessment, extended to 10 years where court proceedings are initiated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a flexible, market-determined exchange-rate regime for the Egyptian Pound (EGP), restored by CBE in March 2024 under the IMF Extended Fund Facility; the CBE has withdrawn FX rationing and no longer applies mandatory pre-approval queues for current-account transactions. Egypt maintains Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement: current-account transfers (trade, services, dividends, interest, and profit remittances) are convertible through CBE-authorized banks upon presentation of genuine underlying commercial documentation. Capital-account flows remain restricted: equity injections, quasi-equity shareholder loans, and portfolio transfers require bank intermediation, CBE BOP coding, and in specified cases CBE no-objection; repatriation of sale proceeds of Egyptian investments to non-residents is permitted only with tax clearance and audited financial evidence of the invested amount. Practical residual hurdles for the EG_LLC EMI: (i) banks may defer FX execution where the counterpart invoice, customs declaration, or TIBA e-invoicing record is missing; (ii) outbound payments for foreign technology, software, or royalty services require a CBE-coded services/royalty application with Egyptian tax withholding clearance; (iii) dividend remittances are routinely cleared on the strength of audited financial statements, a valid Commercial Registry extract, and a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority; and (iv) EGP-denominated balances may not be converted and transferred offshore without a real economic transaction, which constrains any speculative treasury round-tripping.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign-exchange instructions applicable to Egyptian residents, proceeds from exported goods must be repatriated into the Egyptian banking system through an authorized bank within 90 days from the bill-of-lading shipment date; proceeds from exported services must be repatriated within 60 days from the invoice or service-completion date, subject to the prevailing CBE circular. Since the March 2024 float, there is no mandatory statutory surrender ratio: exporters may retain proceeds in a foreign-currency account or convert them into EGP at the interbank market rate at their option, unless the CBE temporarily imposes a conversion percentage for balance-sheet stabilization. Applicability to the EG_LLC: its cross-border fee income (virtual IBAN issuance fees, wallet interchange, card scheme commissions, and FX spread income on treasury operations) constitutes services-export proceeds and must therefore be received into the entity's Egyptian bank account within the 60-day services window. No automatic extension applies; any extension request must be submitted by the servicing bank to the CBE with documentary justification, for example where a euro settlement is pending through an EU/EEA SEPA partner.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE). The CBE is the exclusive sovereign authority over monetary policy, foreign-exchange management, payment systems, and banking supervision under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), read together with Law No. 38 of 1994 on Foreign Exchange (as amended) and CBE Board decrees, circulars, and instructions governing foreign-exchange dealing, balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting, and authorized-bank licensing. The CBE also licenses payment service providers and e-money issuers under Law No. 18 of 2019 Regulating Payment Systems and Services and supervises all authorized dealing banks through its Banking Supervision and Payment Systems sectors.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High, subject to structural conditions. The Egyptian LLC, once licensed by the CBE as a payment service provider and e-money issuer under Law No. 18 of 2019, is a domestic entity and cannot be passportable as an EU bank; therefore the cross-border clearing layer (SEPA Credit in EUR) must be executed through one of three legal routes: (i) an EU/EEA-licensed EMI or bank acting as a Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS) partner that provides segregated virtual IBANs and originates SEPA Credit Transfers on behalf of the Egyptian EMI; (ii) the EU branch or euro correspondent of an Egyptian CBE-authorized bank that embeds the Egyptian EMI's euro positions into the euro clearing system; or (iii) a conventional SWIFT MT103 correspondent network without true SEPA membership, which is slower and more costly. Legal constraints: (a) CBE payment-system and FX rules require the e-money float to be ring-fenced in segregated accounts in Egypt unless the CBE grants an express offshore safeguarding exemption applied for through the servicing bank; (b) Egyptian data-protection law (Law No. 151 of 2020) and CBE data-localization requirements require client records and the core data-processing agreement to keep data materially resident in Egypt; (c) the EU BaaS partner will require the Egyptian license, three years of audited financial statements, an independent AML audit, and a contractual indemnity package, mirroring global correspondent-bank de-risking practices; (d) an EU partner's own e-money safeguarding rules may require the European float to be held at an EU bank, which conflicts with CBE reserve rules unless structured as a back-to-back segregated technical account with the partner's guarantee, subject to prior CBE consultation; and (e) the Egyptian EMI may not advertise itself as a licensed bank in any EU jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 for Local File preparation if controlled transactions exceed this threshold in a tax period; Master File required when total annual revenue exceeds EGP 50,000,000. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be filed with the annual CIT return through ETA Form TP-1 and maintained at the taxpayer's disposal.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, yet the Egyptian Tax Authority applies substance-over-form, anti-avoidance and Controlled Foreign Company rules to reject shell licensing or conduit arrangements. An electronic money institution must maintain real management, employees, decision-making, digital wallet reserves, and risk oversight in Egypt to defend profit attribution and to enjoy treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 133 of the Income Tax Law 91/2005, tax evasion carries a fine of 200% to 300% of the evaded tax, a potential prison sentence of up to five years, and the entire assessed tax remains immediately payable.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face criminal liability for signing false returns; civil recovery extends to company assets and personal assets if fraud or malicious intent is established. Egyptian courts may also issue travel bans and attachment orders against non-compliant directors.
Digitised Shell Company / Artificial Structuring

Statutory sanction: The Central Bank of Egypt may revoke the e-money institution license, impose regulatory fines, and force liquidation. The ETA may disallow surplus attribution, reallocate profits to the foreign principal, and impose understatement penalties.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for unpaid corporate taxes can be imposed on directors when the shell structure impairs collection. Egyptian Economic Courts may freeze personal assets, revoke passports, and impose director disqualification where the corporate veil is lifted.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The ETA can reconstruct the arm's-length price, assess additional tax on the upward adjustment, apply a 2% monthly late payment charge, and levy TP fines equalling 1% to 10% of the undisclosed controlled transaction value depending on severity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length contracts can be jointly assessed for the extra tax as agents through whom the company evaded collection. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets are available to secure fiscal claims.
AML / CFT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions include mandatory imprisonment up to 15 years and fines from EGP 100,000 to EGP 5 million for money-laundering predicates; administrative penalties include license cancellation, asset forfeiture, and permanent disbarment from financial services.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failing to establish AML/CFT controls. Court may order restitution, impose imprisonment on senior managers involved in laundering, and implement travel bans; corporate assets and director property are subject to seizure for enforcement.
Improper Profit Repatriation / Withholding Evasion

Statutory sanction: The ETA may recover the withheld tax plus penalties up to 300% of the unwithheld amount and disallow the related expense. Repeated violations may trigger an audit-triggered criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise gross repatriation without withholding are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax. In e-money and fintech high-risk registration contexts, the FRA may also suspend the legal representative's fitness-and-proper status.
