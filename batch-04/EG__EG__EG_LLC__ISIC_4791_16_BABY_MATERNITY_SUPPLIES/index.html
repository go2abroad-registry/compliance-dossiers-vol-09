Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is fixed for LLCs under Law No. 159/1981; the founders determine subscribed capital in the Articles, each share must be fully subscribed, and in-cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank escrow account in the Company's name before notarization; the funds may be released only after the incorporation certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense fire safety certificate under Law 82/1974 for the office and warehouse, including adequate fire extinguishers, emergency exits, smoke detection, fire alarm systems, and signage; structural load and electrical safety inspection certificates from a licensed engineer under the Building Code (Law 119/2008); and workplace occupational health clearance under the Labor Code Law 12/2003 covering lighting, ventilation, sanitation, first-aid readiness, and employee accident prevention procedures.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt. Official taxpayer portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; e-receipt platform: https://ereceipt.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned LLC registration is completed through the GAFI one-stop-shop (Investor Services Center / Nafeza window): (1) file incorporation documents for the LLC with the Commercial Registry (no statutory minimum capital; foreign ownership is permitted); (2) obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate and publication evidence; (3) register simultaneously with the ETA for corporate income tax (Law 91/2005), payroll and stamp taxes (effective collection via payroll returns), and VAT (Law 67/2016); (4) receive the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card; (5) complete mandatory e-invoicing onboarding (SANAD service codes, ETA API credentials or ETA-approved certified service provider) and submit the actual activity commencement declaration. The Tax Card must be issued within 10 business days of complete filing; foreign identity and constitutional documents require apostille and certified Arabic translation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not apply a check-the-box election; an LLC is mandatorily taxable at entity level as a resident juridical person, subject to corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide income. Available statutory mechanisms include: (a) fiscal-unity / tax-group treatment for Egyptian subsidiaries held at least 90% (ETA pre-approval required, minimum 3-year commitment); (b) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (c) accelerated export VAT refund election under Article 31 bis of the VAT Law for compliant exporters; (d) treaty-based foreign tax credit / unilateral double tax relief election under Article 54 of Law 91/2005; hybrid mismatch consequences follow domestic and MLI rules without an elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover (mandatory VAT registration threshold for goods and services; voluntary registration permitted below threshold; sectoral service thresholds may be applied by the ETA local branch and should be verified at registration)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Egypt and supported by customs export declarations (VAT Law Article 22); (ii) offshore services supplied to non-residents and fully consumed outside Egypt; and (iii) cross-border e-commerce sales of baby and maternity goods where overseas consumers are invoiced and goods are shipped under the Egyptian customs export procedure. For online sales, export proceeds must be credited to an authorized Egyptian bank account; PayPal/Stripe settlement amounts onward-transferred into Egypt are acceptable if the bank certificate identifies the foreign sales. Deliveries into the Suez Canal Economic Zone (outside customs territory) are equally zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (C24) / Electronic Export Certificate issued by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank currency certificate or SWIFT MT103) evidencing foreign currency credited to an Egyptian authorized bank
  • Commercial invoice, proof of the cross-border sales contract, and e-commerce platform / fulfillment carrier reports matching customer remittances to shipped orders

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance: every VAT-registered LLC issuing B2B or B2G invoices must generate e-invoices in JSON format via the ETA platform or an ETA-approved provider; each invoice receives an ETA-validated UUID, hash and QR code before recognition as a taxable supply. B2C online sellers are phased into the mandatory e-receipt system, under which sales to consumers must be cleared through the ETA in real time. Invoices failing ETA clearance do not support input VAT deduction and trigger administrative penalties (up to EGP 100,000 for first offenders) plus possible suspension of the Tax Card.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt systems cross-check export declarations, e-invoices, customs data and foreign currency receipts. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers reversal of zero-rating to 14% VAT, disallowance of input VAT credits, statutory late-payment interest (approximately 14% per annum), and administrative fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000. Fraudulent export claims involving fictitious shipments or forged certifications additionally attract double the evaded tax, imprisonment, freezing of bank accounts, and suspension of the Tax Card pursuant to Law 67/2016 Articles 37, 42 and 44.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since 6 March 2024, the CBE has operated a fully liberalized, market-determined exchange-rate regime, and the Egyptian Pound is effectively convertible for current-account transactions. Bona fide trade payments, service payments, and profit/dividend remittances can be executed through licensed banks at the free interbank rate without prior CBE approval, subject only to documentary verification. Capital-account transactions, including equity contributions, intercompany loans, and portfolio inflows, remain regulated: they must be registered with the participating bank under the CBE dealing system and matched against the original inward transfer to support future conversion and repatriation. Repatriation of profits by the EG LLC is legally guaranteed by Article 131 of Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, but remitting banks require (i) the audited financial statements for the fiscal year concerned, (ii) an Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax clearance certificate showing full settlement of corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax for the profit year, (iii) a verified capital registration certificate matching the original inbound FDI flows, and (iv) confirmation that no outstanding domestic credit is being financed by the same profits. Practical hurdles are documentary and timing-based rather than blocking: conversion requests are queued at the bank treasury, the beneficiary and the ultimate shareholder must pass sanctions and AML screening, and the LLC must demonstrate that its outstanding local bank borrowings do not exceed the net asset position supporting the distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE's unified foreign-exchange instructions, including the circulars issued contemporaneously with the 6 March 2024 exchange-rate liberalization, export proceeds from the LLC's maternity and infant commodity sales must be repatriated to a licensed Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the on-board date of the bill of lading or the airway bill; service export receipts must be received within 90 days from the invoice date. CBE-authorized extensions of up to a further 90 days may be granted for justified commercial causes, and the full amount of proceeds must be credited to the exporter's foreign-currency account (FCA) at a licensed bank. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio has been abolished: exporters are no longer required to sell a fixed percentage (historically 25-50%) of their proceeds; the LLC may retain 100% of repatriated export receipts in an FCA and convert to EGP at the free interbank market rate at its sole discretion. To certify compliance, the bank requires the export customs declaration filed through the Egyptian Single External Trade Window, the underlying sales contract and commercial invoice, the transport document, and the FX settlement advice; failure to repatriate within the 90-day prescription exposes the LLC to CBE reporting as a foreign-exchange violation, bank-level facility suspension, and statutory penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), established and governed by Law No. 194 of 2020 (the Central Bank and Banking System Law). The CBE is the sole monetary, banking, and foreign-exchange supervisory authority; its General Administration for Foreign Exchange, the Licensing Department, and the Dealing System Section administer the unified Egyptian foreign-exchange regime, the licensing of authorized dealers, the balance-of-payments classification of cross-border payments, and the foreign-direct-investment (FDI) registration system for capital inflows and repatriation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for any CBE-regulated clearing or settlement function in Egypt. Stripe does not operate in Egypt and cannot settle EGP to an Egyptian merchant; PayPal does not provide Egyptian-registered business merchant receiving accounts that credit EGP, and historical PayPal access for Egypt is limited to personal send-side functions. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital from the non-resident shareholder must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) in USD or EUR to the LLC's designated capital-escrow account at the chosen Egyptian bank, before the bank issues the capital deposit certificate under the Egyptian Companies Law. The incoming wire must carry the standard International Banking Transaction message fields identifying the ultimate originator, the sending bank, and a precise narration referencing the entity ('EG__EG__EG_LLC'), the Egyptian Commercial Registration number, and the phrase 'Share Capital Subscription - Foreign Direct Investment Equity'. The bank will map the transfer to the Egyptian balance-of-payments classification code for foreign direct investment in Egypt - equity capital, register the inflow in the CBE dealing system, and issue an FDI registration and repatriation-rights certificate enabling future dividend and liquidation remittances. The documentary package must include the notarized Articles of Association, the foreign shareholder's board resolution approving the contribution, a legal opinion confirming unlawful incorporation, and proof of authorization of the account signatories; once the subscription is paid, the capital escrow account is converted by bank mandate to the LLC's operational EGP/FCY account, and the capital deposit certificate is stamped and replaced by free-use current accounts.For import settlements covering maternity and infant commodities from overseas suppliers, the LLC will use two primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account and advance-payment structures, and (ii) irrevocable, confirmed documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) for larger purchase orders where the Egyptian bank issues the credit and the beneficiary bank confirms at sight or usance terms. Prior to each outward conversion, the Egyptian bank will require the signed import contract or pro-forma invoice, the final commercial invoice, the NAFEZA Advance Cargo Information Declaration (ACID) number for sea and air cargo, and, after arrival, the customs import release documentation; for L/C disbursements, the bank will process compliance-checks of the shipping documents, including the transport document, insurance certificate, weight/survey certificates, and the LLC's valid tax registration certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for any CBE-regulated clearing or settlement function in Egypt. Stripe does not operate in Egypt and cannot settle EGP to an Egyptian merchant; PayPal does not provide Egyptian-registered business merchant receiving accounts that credit EGP, and historical PayPal access for Egypt is limited to personal send-side functions. Cross-border digital EMI platforms such as Payoneer and Wise are not licensed by the CBE as foreign-exchange dealers or payment processors in Egypt; they can function only as a pre-collection B2C 'wallet' for the maternity-supplies e-commerce business, with funds subsequently repatriated by SWIFT MT103 to the LLC's Egyptian FCA. The controlling legal constraints are Article 213 of the CBE Law (exclusivity of licensed banks and exchange companies for foreign-exchange dealing), the unified payment-system regulations restricting unlicensed processors from maintaining EGP settlement accounts, and the CBE's anti-money-laundering circulars requiring resident exporters to settle export receipts through a licensed Egyptian bank within the statutory 90-day window.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard CIT rate for resident companies under Law No. 91 of 2005 (Art. 8), as amended by Law No. 6 of 2020; 40% statutory rate applies only to banks, petroleum exploration, and Suez Canal activities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends distributed by a resident Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders (Art. 111 Law 91/2005); the rate is applied to gross distributions without deduction at the corporate level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million in aggregate related-party/controlled transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory preparation and submission of the Master File, Local File, and the annual transfer pricing disclosure form (Ministerial Decree No. 547/2020 and ETA TP Guidelines); multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EGP 20 billion or more must additionally file a Country-by-Country Report.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not impose a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to the UAE regime; however, the ETA applies general anti-avoidance rules, substance-over-form doctrine, and the OECD BEPS beneficial-ownership test. A non-resident-owned LLC without premises, employees, board activity, or fulfilment operations in Egypt is recharacterized as a shell; treaty and investment-law benefits are then revoked and payments reclassified as taxable Egyptian-source income at full statutory rates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – E-commerce Revenue Understatement

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of EGP 10,000–50,000 under Art. 129 Law 91/2005; civil penalty of 200% of the evaded tax; e-invoice/e-receipt violations carry additional fines up to EGP 50,000; enforced asset seizure and court-ordered closure of the online storefront.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general manager are jointly and severally liable for the LLC's tax debts where default arises from their fault; the ETA may impose travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, and refer culpable directors for criminal prosecution leading to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty relief, refusal of Investment Law incentives, and revocation of SME regime eligibility; recharacterization of cross-border payments as taxable Egyptian-source income; reassessment at 22.5% CIT plus 10%–20% withholding and 200% delinquency penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations commit a false-affidavit offence; civil restitution of the evaded taxes, personal travel bans, disqualification from management of Egyptian entities, and imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EGP 100,000 for documentation failure under Art. 133 as applied through TP regulations; discretionary TP adjustment to arm's-length income; assessed underpayment accrues monthly surcharges of 2% plus interest on the principal underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false TP computations are jointly liable for the additional tax and interest; submission of forged TP benchmark studies triggers criminal proceedings and personal imprisonment for fraud against the ETA.
AML Non-Compliance and Unlicensed Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Fines and licence suspension under AML Law No. 80 of 2002 and Central Bank regulations; freezing of PSP and bank balances; criminal forfeiture of proceeds where funds are linked to tax evasion, money laundering, or illegal digital-commerce activity.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally obligated to report suspicious transactions; failure to do so carries imprisonment of 3–7 years, personal fines, and permanent disqualification from serving as directors or managers of any Egyptian company.
