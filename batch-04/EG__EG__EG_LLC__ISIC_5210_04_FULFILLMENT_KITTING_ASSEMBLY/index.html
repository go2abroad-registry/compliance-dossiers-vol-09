Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity intending to make taxable supplies in Egypt must appoint an Egyptian tax representative, who is jointly and severally liable for VAT obligations. The representative files the VAT registration application with the competent ETA office via the unified electronic portal, attaching the entity's constitutional/power-of-attorney documents, certified incorporation instruments, and the tax representative's legal identification. Upon verification, ETA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the entity is registered for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016. The same TIN is used for withholding tax, payroll tax, and e-invoicing enrollment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident foreign entity intending to make taxable supplies in Egypt must appoint an Egyptian tax representative, who is jointly and severally liable for VAT obligations. The representative files the VAT registration application with the competent ETA office via the unified electronic portal, attaching the entity's constitutional/power-of-attorney documents, certified incorporation instruments, and the tax representative's legal identification. Upon verification, ETA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the entity is registered for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016. The same TIN is used for withholding tax, payroll tax, and e-invoicing enrollment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian tax law does not provide check-the-box or general hybrid-mismatch elections. An Egyptian LLC is per se a taxable resident juridical person subject to entity-level corporate tax under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 as unified by Law No. 26 of 2020. No elective pass-through status exists for an LLC. Group filing may be available only where expressly permitted under the tax law; hybrid mismatch treatments are not elective and are constrained by general anti-avoidance rules and transfer pricing substance requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory registration when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; persons making zero-rated export supplies may register voluntarily regardless of turnover, and other below-threshold businesses may optionally register if they perform taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Egypt to outside the customs territory are zero-rated provided the export is completed and evidenced within the period prescribed by ETA. Services rendered to non-residents qualify for zero-rating when supplied for direct use and benefit outside Egypt, subject to documentary evidence of offshore consumption and receipt of foreign currency. The zero-rating applies only to genuine exports; domestic supplies of goods and services remain taxable at the standard rate absent a specific statutory exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of foreign currency repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin

Egypt mandates electronic invoicing and e-receipts for VAT-registered taxpayers on a phased basis. Invoices must be issued through ETA-certified systems, transmitted to the ETA portal near real time via secure API integration, validated by ETA, and printed with a machine-readable QR code. No physical fiscal stamping is used; digital validation by ETA serves as fiscal clearance. Non-compliance triggers fines under the VAT Law and underlying executive regulations.

ETA may audit export files retroactively and deny or claw back zero-rated VAT, refunds, and credits where documentation is defective or where the purported export is found to be fraudulent. Penalties include a VAT shortfall assessment of 100% of the due tax, additional monetary fines escalating with the period of non-payment, interruption of e-invoicing clearance, and suspension of tax certificates. Criminal liability, including imprisonment, may attach to submitting forged customs or foreign-inflow documentation under the VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 and customs legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP operates under a CBE-managed flexible market-determined exchange-rate regime (unified rate since March 2024) with no official parity; current-account transactions for genuine trade and services are broadly convertible, but every FX operation must be intermediated by a CBE-licensed bank and evidenced by underlying commercial documentation (contracts, invoices, and, for goods, the ACI/ACID customs declaration via NAFEZA). Capital-account convertibility remains restricted: inward equity injections and outward profit remittances require GAFI registration, bank intermediation, and full tax compliance, and non-resident shareholders face a domestic 10% dividend withholding tax (treaty reduction available) plus presentation of audited financial statements, a valid tax card, and an ETA tax-clearance certificate. The post-2024 flexible regime has essentially removed explicit FX rationing, but each remittance is still subject to bank-level compliance review of 3-10 business days, and mismatched purpose codes or undocumentary flows can trigger CBE penalties, administrative fines, and CIT adjustments under Law 194/2020.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion of hard-currency export receipts into EGP is currently imposed; EG_LLC may retain 100% of proceeds from goods export or cross-border fulfillment/kitting services in USD or EUR accounts at an authorized Egyptian bank. The statutory repatriation obligation requires all such proceeds to be credited to the LLC's account with a CBE-licensed bank within 180 days from the bill-of-lading date or service-completion date, supported by the underlying sales/fulfillment contract, commercial invoices, customs/ACI declarations, and bank credit advice. Failure to repatriate or unjustified delays must be explained to the bank and the CBE and may expose the unrepatriated balance to 22.5% corporate income tax adjustment risk and administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE; Arabic: البنك المركزي المصري) acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 on the Central Bank and the Banking System, being the sole monetary authority, regulator of all FX transactions, and supervisor of licensed banks; co-regulators for treasury flows are GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) for FDI registration and the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) for profit-remittance tax clearance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary or regulated settlement rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise, Payoneer) hold no CBE banking licence, cannot open a domestic Egyptian account, cannot settle in EGP, and cannot lawfully collect Egyptian trade receivables under Law 194/2020, which channels all deposit-taking, FX execution, and payment clearing through CBE-licensed banks. EMIs remain viable only as supplementary non-resident wallets for disbursing non-EGP vendor payments (software, freight, marketing) from offshore funds; every compliant inbound receipt and EGP-denominated obligation must clear through the licensed Egyptian bank, and EMI statements should be retained merely as payment evidence rather than as regulatory KYC documents.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) before remittance, EG_LLC opens a non-operational capital-collection account at the chosen CBE-licensed bank using its incorporation pack; (2) the foreign shareholder issues an English/Arabic board or shareholder resolution stating the exact USD or EGP amount, share allocation, and GAFI registration number; (3) the remitter executes a SWIFT MT103 from its own corporate account with a strict beneficiary reference of 'EG_LLC share capital subscription - GAFI No. [XXXX]' and the CBE inward-transfer purpose code for direct investment/capital contribution; (4) the receiving bank verifies the GAFI commercial registration, articles of association, passports, and tax card, credits the capital account, and reports the inflow to the CBE through its electronic reporting platform; (5) funds are released to the operational account only after commercial and tax registration are conclusive. No prior CBE approval is required for standard FDI inflow, but the bank may request a source-of-wealth declaration for transfers above approximately USD 1 million; all MT103 confirmations, purpose-code annotations, and bank credit advices must be retained as treasury audit evidence.For import settlements (packaging materials, kitting machinery, inventory) the LLC must first obtain an ACI/ACID declaration number through the NAFEZA single window before shipment; banks will then execute either a commercial letter of credit (preferred for large capex), documentary collection, or direct SWIFT MT103 with underlying invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and insurance certificate attached, with no blanket LC mandate post-2024. For non-import service payments (freight, software, third-country subcontracting), the rail is SWIFT MT103/202 with contracts and invoices, subject to ETA withholding tax (up to 20% on service and royalty fees or applicable treaty relief) and to the 10% dividend withholding tax on profit distributions to non-residents. Domestic EGP operating payments (wages, rent, utilities, local suppliers) should use the CBE Egypt ACH (EG-ACH) direct-credit rails; Airwallex-type EMIs may be used only for premium-conversion non-EGP vendor payments and are expressly excluded as the onshore settlement rail for Egyptian receivables.Deliverable onshore FX forward (USD/EGP, EUR/EGP, etc.) for documented trade and fulfillment exposures, maturities typically up to 12 months, requiring underlying LC, invoice, or purchase-order support and 10-20% initial margin.FX swap (spot-forward combination) for short-term EGP liquidity management across month-end, tax, and payroll cycles.Cross-currency swap among USD/EGP notional for multi-year asset or equipment financing, subject to CBE-licensed bank approval and documented exposure.FX options (structured strategies such as collars) to hedge contingent foreign-currency exposures, permitted only with bank-approved option strategies and real trade documentation.Offshore NDFs (USD/EGP non-deliverable forwards) are generally inaccessible to resident non-bank corporates; they are available mainly through international bank windows for portfolio investors and are not recommended for EG_LLC.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividend distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to its shareholders, including non-resident shareholders, under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended. This withholding tax represents the final tax on distributed profits and is withheld by the Egyptian company at the time of distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Form No. 100 must be submitted no later than four months after the end of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the statutory deadline is 30 April of the following year. Quarterly advance payments are due by the last day of the month following each calendar quarter, and the fourth quarter payment must accompany the annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate related-party controlled transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EGP 500,000,000, and Country-by-Country Reporting is triggered when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 800,000,000 (or the EQE comparable threshold of EUR 750,000,000). The documentation must be retained and supplied to the ETA within 30 days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the UAE/GCC Economic Substance Regulations, but Egyptian law and the ETA articulate a strong substance-over-form doctrine. A fulfilment/kitting LLC must evidence real premises, employees carrying out assembly/kitting operations, inventory management, and decision-making capacity. The ETA may disregard a legal entity transaction or recharacterise an arrangement under Law No. 91 of 2005 Article 30 if the entity has no economic substance, is purely interposed between the foreign investor and the Egyptian activity, or functions as a conduit for treaty shopping.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the full underpaid tax, delay surcharge of 2% per month capped at 20%, additional administrative penalties, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion. Courts may impose imprisonment, confiscation of equipment and bank balances, and fines equal to the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who sign false returns or direct the evasion may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes. They face personal civil restitution, travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment under the Egyptian Income Tax Law and Penal Code. Directors cannot hide behind the LLC's limited liability.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the corporate entity or recharacterise the transaction under the substance-over-form doctrine, deny treaty withholding relief, and assess tax at 22.5% on the reattributed Egyptian-source income along with late-payment surcharges and transfer pricing penalties. The entity may also be struck off the commercial register and denied free-zone or investment-law incentirates.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for the full amount of tax avoided through sham entities; they may face a departure ban, prohibition from managing companies in Egypt for up to five years, and criminal liability if the arrangement is adjudicated as deliberate evasion.
Transfer Pricing Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The ETA may adjust taxable income to arm's length, disallow excessive deductions, deny double tax relief, and apply a 22.5% tax on the upward adjustment plus 2% monthly delay surcharge and TP-specific penalties. Persistent violations may be referred to the ETA audit and dispute collection board.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length agreements without economic/financial justification may be jointly liable for the TP adjustment amount, undergo criminal proceedings for fraudulent tax reduction, and be exposed to arrest warrants and seizure of personal assets where the adjustment exceeds the capacity of the company to pay.
AML and Payment Rail Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Violations of Egyptian AML Law No. 80 of 2002 carry fines up to EGP 10 million and imprisonment, separate from tax sanctions. The ETA may reopen closed tax assessments upon discovery of unreported payment rails, impose double withholding, and blacklist the entity from government licences and customs facilities.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable under AML law for knowingly using false invoices or payment descriptions. Personal sanctions include criminal imprisonment, director disqualification, freezing of bank accounts, and civil confiscation of amounts received through misclassified payment rails. The corporate veil is pierced when the payment system is used for money laundering.
