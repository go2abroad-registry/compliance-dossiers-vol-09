Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; online services portal: eta.gov.eg; for customs: Egyptian Customs Authority (e-customs portal) shaheenalbetaa. The mandatory e-invoicing system is administered through ETA's integrated electronic invoice portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without an Egyptian permanent establishment making taxable supplies in Egypt must register for VAT through the ETA online portal (or by postal filing) using: (1) copy of incorporation certificate from country of residence, (2) passport copies of directors and authorised signatories, (3) certified power of attorney appointing a local tax representative, and (4) evidence of the Egyptian business address or P.O. box. The ETA issues a unique Tax Identification Number (TIN) and a VAT Registration Certificate within up to 40 working days. Entities incorporated as an Egyptian LLC must obtain a tax card (بطاقة ضريبية) from the local tax office and register for income tax, VAT, payroll tax (if employees), and stamp duty subsequent to incorporation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity without an Egyptian permanent establishment making taxable supplies in Egypt must register for VAT through the ETA online portal (or by postal filing) using: (1) copy of incorporation certificate from country of residence, (2) passport copies of directors and authorised signatories, (3) certified power of attorney appointing a local tax representative, and (4) evidence of the Egyptian business address or P.O. box. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists; Egypt imposes entity-level corporate income tax on Egyptian LLCs regardless of foreign ownership. No pass-through or partnership-equivalent election is available for LLCs. Certain investment incentive elections under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 (e.g., deduction of investment costs over staged periods, free-zone status election) may be available for qualifying projects. Egypt does not currently provide hybrid mismatch elections; OECD hybrid mismatch rules are not domestically enacted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (Law No. 67 of 2016, as amended by Law No. 8 of 2023 and Law No. 23 of 2024); foreign suppliers with economic activity in Egypt or making distance sales to Egyptian consumers above this threshold must register. Exports are subject to 0% VAT and do not trigger cancellation of registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is allowed for (i) exported goods provided that the goods leave Egypt within a statutory timeframe and the exporter retains customs proof of export; (ii) services supplied to non-residents where the benefit of the service is effectively consumed abroad and the service qualifies as an exported service under the VAT Implementing Regulations; and (iii) supplies related to international transport (subject to bilateral exemptions). For DTC webstore exports, zero-rating is available if the seller holds valid supplier code, issues an export invoice, and receives payment from outside Egypt in freely convertible currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document processed by Egyptian Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight) or Air Waybill (for air freight)
  • Commercial invoice showing 0% VAT and export destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing transfer of foreign currency from the foreign buyer/consumer into Egypt
  • Proof of foreign currency settlement to the Central Bank of Egypt (where applicable)
  • Contract or purchase order evidencing the export transaction

Mandatory integrated e-invoicing and e-receipt system administered by ETA. Registered taxpayers must issue electronic XML invoices through approved systems using API integration (ETA's online clearance platform) in near-real time prior to delivery/acquisition. Invoices receive a Unique Invoice Identifier and QR code after fiscal clearance; failure to clear invoices triggers administrative penalties (EGP 20,000–100,000 per non-cleared invoice). B2C sales are covered by the e-receipt mandate for specific industries, including e-commerce and retail; will be extended nationwide. Taxpayers must use Treasury or ETA-approved electronic signature/certificates.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to heightened documentary review and ETA cross-matching with customs export records and CBE repatriation reports. A false or fraudulent zero-rating claim exposes the taxpayer to: reassessment at 14% VAT on the full export value; reversal of input VAT credits claimed on export-related purchases; pecuniary penalties up to twice the amount of tax evaded; criminal fines and imprisonment under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 (Articles 46–55); and a 5% withholding tax certification penalty for incorrect invoices. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline renders the zero-rating claim void and the VAT output tax becomes due with late payment interest at 18% per annum.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified managed-float foreign-exchange regime following the March 2024 unification of the official and parallel exchange rates. The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is a convertible trade settlement currency but is not freely convertible outside the licensed banking system. Current-account transactions (DTC import/export of retail goods and cross-border digital services) are settled by CBE-licensed banks upon documentary verification; capital-account movements (share-subscription injections, dividend remittances, and liquidating distributions) are permitted but require bank-level registration, Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax clearance, and practical priority treatment within the bank's USD liquidity allocation. For an Egyptian LLC engaged in Shopify DTC retail, after-tax profit remittance is lawful and requires audited financial statements, a board dividend resolution, ETA confirmation of no outstanding tax arrears, and the CBE inward-investment registration; residual hurdles are operational timing delays during international reserve stress rather than statutory prohibition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE rules, export proceeds must be fully repatriated into the Egyptian banking system within 120 days from the date of shipment (bill of lading date) or from the date of the customs declaration, with waiver extensions available for specified capital-goods transactions. Under the post-2024 unified-exchange-rate regime, the CBE does not publish a fixed statutory conversion percentage for surrendered proceeds; the absolute obligations are full repatriation through a CBE-licensed bank, retention in an Egyptian foreign-currency account, and priority offering of conversion to the relationship bank to fund essential-import demand. In practice, a Shopify DTC exporter may be requested by its bank to convert a portion of received USD (commonly 25-50% of net settlement) into EGP for local operating liquidity. All Shopify Payments, Stripe, and PayPal settlements must flow to the LLC's Egyptian bank account within the statutory window, and the PSP payout statements must be retained as the export-receipt evidence trail.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Banking Supervision and Foreign Exchange Operations Sector

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for direct treasury settlement and Low for export-proceeds clearing. Offshore digital EMIs (Payoneer, Wise, Airwallex, Neat) are not licensed by the CBE as banks and cannot lawfully receive or settle Egyptian export proceeds in place of a CBE-licensed bank; routing export sales value through an unlicensed EMI exposes the LLC to AML review and breaches CBE repatriation requirements. Their permissible role is restricted to facilitating the collection of Shopify/PayPal/Stripe retail proceeds in USD and automatically sweeping funds to the LLC's Egyptian corporate account through the PSP's partner banking network. Licensed Egyptian fintechs and payment service providers (e.g., e-finance, Fawry, Valu) may be used for domestic EGP collection and acquiring services, but they do not provide FX conversion or cross-border treasury clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity injection must be remitted via SWIFT MT103/202 to the LLC's CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account, referencing the CBE Balance-of-Payments purpose code 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' and, where applicable, the GAFI investment certificate issued under Investment Law No. 72/2017. The receiving bank conducts AML verification, validates the share-subscription resolution and amended commercial-registry extract, issues the inward-remittance credit note, and updates the CBE foreign-investment register; funds are released to the EGP operational ledger after source-of-funds clearance. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of taxable net profit under the Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 10% on dividends distributed by an Egyptian company, subject to a possible 5% rate for qualifying listed-share investments and statutory conditions. Treaty rates may be lower and depend on the relevant Egypt double-tax agreement, ownership threshold, holding period, beneficial ownership, and anti-abuse provisions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation commonly requires a Local File where aggregate related-party transactions exceed EGP 8 million during the relevant financial year, subject to the applicable transaction categories and current ETA rules. A Master File is generally relevant to Egyptian members of multinational enterprise groups meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly EGP 3 billion or more. Country-by-Country reporting obligations may also apply at the multinational-group level, generally using the EGP 3 billion consolidated-revenue threshold and applicable filing or notification rules. Thresholds and administrative requirements must be reconfirmed for the filing year.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not generally operate a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable to every holding, finance, or IP entity. Nevertheless, substance is relevant under transfer-pricing rules, general anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse provisions, permanent-establishment rules, deductibility rules, and Egyptian commercial and tax administration. The LLC should maintain Egyptian decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or documented operating arrangements, local books, bank and payment controls, inventory and fulfillment evidence, customer and supplier contracts, and commercially credible functions and risks. A nominal Egyptian entity receiving foreign group charges without personnel, control, benefit, or documentary support is high risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, additional tax, civil penalties, and criminal tax-evasion sanctions under the Egyptian Income Tax Law, potentially including imprisonment and a fine linked to the tax evaded. The precise sanction depends on the offense, amount, intent, repetition, and applicable amendments.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, authorized signatories, accountants, or beneficial owners who participate in intentional evasion may face personal criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, civil restitution, asset measures, and possible travel restrictions by competent judicial or prosecutorial order. Corporate limited liability is not a defense to personal criminal conduct.
Failure to withhold or remit tax

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC may become liable for the unwithheld tax, additional tax, penalties, reassessment, and related procedural sanctions. Deliberate retention or misappropriation of withheld tax can create criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and signatories may be personally exposed where they authorized, directed, or knowingly permitted the failure. Travel restrictions and imprisonment require competent legal process and are not automatic consequences of an ordinary computational error.
VAT evasion and invoice non-compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, denial of input credit, additional tax, administrative fines, closure or other administrative measures where legally available, and criminal sanctions for deliberate fraud or evasion.

Director personal liability: Managers and authorized representatives may face personal liability for knowing participation in fraudulent invoicing or deliberate VAT evasion, including fines, restitution, and imprisonment under the applicable criminal provisions.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: ETA adjustment to arm's-length income, denial or limitation of deductions, additional corporate tax, withholding-tax exposure, penalties, interest, and documentation-related consequences.

Director personal liability: Personal liability generally arises from fraud, deliberate falsification, or knowing authorization rather than from a good-faith pricing disagreement. Evidence of engineered losses or fabricated comparables materially increases criminal and civil risk.
Shell company, nominee ownership, or beneficial-ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, denial of treaty benefits, anti-money-laundering investigation, confiscation or asset-freezing measures where authorized, corporate and licensing consequences, and criminal sanctions for fraud, forgery, tax evasion, or money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors, nominees, ultimate beneficial owners, and professional facilitators may face personal criminal liability, fines, imprisonment, restitution, asset restraint, and judicially ordered travel bans. Limited liability does not protect a person who uses the company as an instrument of fraud.
Money laundering and proceeds of crime

Statutory sanction: Egyptian anti-money-laundering legislation may impose imprisonment, substantial fines, confiscation, asset freezing, and other measures, with enhanced consequences for organized or predicate-offense conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance personnel may be personally liable for knowing participation, assistance, concealment, or intentional failure to report suspicious activity where a reporting obligation applies. Travel restrictions and pretrial or precautionary measures may be imposed through competent authorities.
Foreign-exchange, banking, and payment-processor circumvention

Statutory sanction: Potential banking, foreign-exchange, customs, tax, AML, and payment-services sanctions, including fines, transaction restrictions, seizure, account action, and criminal prosecution depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and controllers may face personal investigation, fines, imprisonment, restitution, asset restrictions, and travel limitations where they authorized or knowingly benefited from unlawful payment arrangements.
Customs and import misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, duties, VAT, fines, seizure, forfeiture, delayed release, and criminal customs liability in cases of deliberate smuggling or fraudulent declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors, customs representatives, and beneficial owners may be personally liable for knowing false declarations, smuggling, or document forgery, including criminal sanctions and asset or travel measures.
Corporate records and accounting falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax assessments, rejection of deductions, administrative penalties, audit escalation, and criminal sanctions for forgery, fraud, or intentional obstruction of tax administration.

Director personal liability: Directors, accountants, and authorized representatives who approve or use falsified records may face personal criminal and civil liability, including restitution, fines, imprisonment, and judicial precautionary measures.
Treaty abuse and conduit arrangements

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, application of domestic withholding tax, additional tax, penalties, interest, and potential anti-avoidance or fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Personal exposure arises where directors knowingly implement sham arrangements, false residence representations, or fraudulent documentation. Good-faith reliance on properly verified treaty documentation is materially different from deliberate treaty shopping.
Permanent-establishment and unregistered business risk

Statutory sanction: Potential registration, corporate income tax, VAT, payroll, withholding, customs, and interest assessments, together with penalties for non-registration or non-filing.

Director personal liability: Personal liability is principally triggered by deliberate concealment, false filings, or obstruction. Directors may face fines and criminal proceedings where the facts meet statutory offense elements.
