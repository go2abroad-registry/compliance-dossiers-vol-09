Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; electronic services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg and https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration with the Egyptian Tax Authority using the company's commercial registration certificate, power of attorney for the legal representative, the founders' and managers' ID documents or passport copies, lease or ownership proof of the head office, and the audited opening balance sheet. The application is submitted through the ETA portal or in person at the competent tax office within three months of incorporation; issuance of the electronic tax ID card generally occurs within one to two weeks after confirmation of the registered principal place of business.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must apply for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration with the Egyptian Tax Authority using the company's commercial registration certificate, power of attorney for the legal representative, the founders' and managers' ID documents or passport copies, lease or ownership proof of the head office, and the audited opening balance sheet. The application is submitted through the ETA portal or in person at the competent tax office within three months of incorporation; issuance of the electronic tax ID card generally occurs within one to two weeks after confirmation of the registered principal place of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election is available under Egyptian tax law; an LLC is per se treated as a taxable corporation subject to corporate income tax. Tax incentives are granted only through administratively approved investment schemes or special economic zones (e.g., Suez Canal Economic Zone). Egypt applies hybrid mismatch alignment rules consistent with internationally agreed anti-avoidance standards, and affected taxpayers must file informational disclosures. No unilateral entity-classification election is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods or services; mandatory VAT registration is required within 60 days of exceeding the threshold, while non-resident suppliers making distance sales to Egyptian consumers may be subject to simplified registration rules

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Egyptian VAT zero-rating applies to the export of tangible goods physically transported out of Egypt, provided the exporter submits reliable customs documentation and retains proof of payment in foreign currency. Services supplied to non-residents and effectively off-shore services are also zero-rated if they are not deemed to be physically rendered inside Egypt and meet the ETA's supporting documentation conditions. The exporter must be registered for VAT, issue e-invoices stamped by the ETA, and maintain a complete documentation trail within the statutory records retention period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (formal export entry approved by the Egyptian Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent Air Waybill / Truck Waybill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Justified Commercial Invoice showing 0% VAT and HS code alignment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX advice proving settlement of export proceeds by the overseas buyer
  • Signed proof of delivery or delivery confirmation from the importer/carrying agent

All ETA VAT-registered B2B taxpayers are required to issue electronic tax invoices and electronic receipts through the ETA e-invoicing and e-receipts platform. The system uses an API-based validity model with encrypted QR codes, digital stamping of invoice UUIDs, and real-time submission to the ETA for validation and authorization codes. Taxpayers must integrate certified ERP or billing software with the ETA reporting gateway and retain deterministic invoice serials. Enforcement is phased by sector and turnover, with non-compliance penalties for missing or incorrect e-invoice clearance.

The ETA and Egyptian Customs cross-check export entries, e-invoice archive data, foreign currency receipts, and shipping manifests. False or forged zero-rating claims trigger repayment of the under-assessed VAT plus late-payment interest, administrative fines of up to the full amount of evaded tax, potential criminal penalties including imprisonment under the VAT Law and Anti-Corruption framework, suspension or cancellation of VAT registration, and customs prosecution for export misdeclaration. In addition, exporters failing to repatriate foreign currency within the applicable Central Bank of Egypt window may face exchange-control measures and loss of the preferential zero-rating benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1997 and formally maintains current-account convertibility for trade in goods, services, royalties, and dividends; the March 2024 shift to a permanently flexible exchange-rate regime unified the official and market rates and eliminated the parallel FX market. Capital-account convertibility remains restricted in form: all inbound equity, shareholder loans, and outbound dividends or profit repatriations must flow through an authorized bank, be recorded on CBE unified dealing forms, and be reconciled against documentary proof that the foreign currency originally entered the Egyptian banking system. Profit remittance by the Egyptian LLC requires audited Egyptian financial statements, a tax-clearance certificate, a board resolution, and evidence of the original capital entry; residual USD-liquidity pressures in the interbank market may cause short sequencing delays, and netting of intra-group receivables/payables without CBE authorization is not permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, 100 percent of proceeds must be repatriated to an authorized Egyptian bank within five months from the date of export (bill-of-lading/shipment date) under the CBE's amended export-repatriation regime effective 29 December 2024, replacing the earlier three-to-six-month discipline. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into EGP; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in its foreign-currency account, sell it to the bank at the prevailing interbank rate, or apply it to documented import payments. Late repatriation proceedings may trigger statutory penalties calculated on the EGP equivalent, and the bank must be presented with the import/export dealing form and customs declaration. Service-export receipts, including franchise royalties receivable by the Egyptian master franchisee, must also be credited through authorized banks and are subject to the CBE's documentary declaration regime, although the strict five-month goods timeline is not automatically extended to services.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary and foreign-exchange supervisory authority; the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) administers inbound foreign-investment registration; foreign-exchange dealing is permitted exclusively through CBE-licensed authorized banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The sole lawful route is a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer from the Chinese parent's bank to the Egyptian LLC's USD capital-escrow account at a CBE-licensed authorized bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state: 'EG-LLC [COMPANY NAME] - CAPITAL INCREASE - GAFI REGISTRATION NO. [XXX]'; the remitter name in Field 50 must be the Chinese investing entity itself, not an unrelated intermediary or third-country shell. The beneficiary Egyptian bank verifies the credit against the GAFI capital-account application, files the CBE Unified Dealing Declaration of Purpose form, and issues the capital-deposit certificate, which serves as the legal basis for GAFI's issuance of the foreign-investment certificate.
