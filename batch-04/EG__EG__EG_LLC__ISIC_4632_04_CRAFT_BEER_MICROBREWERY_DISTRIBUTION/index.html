Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; official national portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic-invoice and e-receipt platform operated by the ETA with open REST/XML APIs under the same portal. The ETA has exclusive competence over corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty, payroll tax, electronic invoicing, and export VAT refunds for the EG_LLC.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: Step 1 — incorporate the LLC in the Commercial Registry and obtain the commercial register extract; an Egyptian LLC may be 100% foreign-owned and has no statutory minimum paid-in capital. Step 2 — within 30 days of commencing activity, apply at the competent ETA office or through the ETA e-portal for a unified Tax Card, which issues the single Tax Identification Number covering corporate income tax, VAT, payroll tax, and stamp duties under Tax Procedure Law No. 88/2023. Step 3 — file mandatory VAT registration because annual taxable turnover exceeds the EGP 500,000 threshold; a non-resident person supplying taxable goods or services into Egypt must appoint an ETA-accredited tax representative, while a resident Egyptian LLC with non-resident shareholders is itself the taxpayer and must maintain a local address and a responsible compliance officer. Step 4 — onboard to the ETA e-invoicing API by obtaining API credentials and a digital signing certificate and by completing sandbox testing before issuing live invoices. Step 5 — secure the statutory alcohol wholesale/distribution license required for beer distribution, register the license particulars with the ETA, and activate the customs ASYCUDA World export clearance profile for export consignments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Egyptian LLC is an opaque resident juridical person. It is taxed as a separate taxpayer on worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 30/2023; no pass-through or partner-level attribution of profit applies, and members are not taxed on undistributed LLC income. Non-resident counterparties are taxed only on Egyptian-source income. For indirect tax, the LLC is the VAT taxable person and is solely responsible for collecting output VAT and claiming input VAT credit.

Available election forms

Egyptian law provides no check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election; an Egyptian LLC cannot elect fiscal transparency. Available statutory elections include: (i) consolidated corporate income tax filing where a parent owns at least 95% of an Egyptian subsidiary; (ii) voluntary VAT registration for persons below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (iii) election of the accounting period aligned with the Egyptian fiscal year; and (iv) administrative election between simplified deemed-profit accounting and actual audited accounting for eligible small taxpayers. A VAT group election, partnership pass-through election, and OECD Pillar Two or GLoBE elections are not available in Egypt.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 aggregate annual value of taxable supplies of goods and services; registration is compulsory above this threshold under Law No. 67 of 2016. Beer exporters and alcohol wholesalers are in practice required to register as a condition of exercising licensed activity, and non-resident persons making distance or electronic supplies into Egypt must register irrespective of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of craft beer and kegs by the registered EG_LLC are zero-rated at 0% VAT where: (i) the exporter holds valid VAT registration and the alcohol export license; (ii) the goods physically leave Egypt under the customs export declaration within the time limit accepted by the ETA; (iii) the export invoice and e-invoice identify the foreign buyer, state the 0% rate, and do not charge domestic VAT; (iv) the goods are exported for valuable consideration in foreign currency; and (v) the foreign-currency proceeds are received into an Egyptian authorized bank account. Corresponding input VAT on kegs, cold-chain logistics, malt, hops, and distribution overhead is creditable and refundable through the ETA export refund cycle once the export documentation is matched. Offshore services supplied to non-residents may equally be zero-rated where the supply is outside Egypt under the place-of-supply rules.

Closed-loop e-invoicing is mandatory. Every B2B invoice must be issued as an XML e-invoice through the ETA e-invoicing API in near real time, digitally signed and carrying a QR code. The ETA automatically validates each invoice against XML schemas, taxpayer registration status, and the reusable unique invoice identifier, and returns Accepted or Rejected status; only accepted invoices confer input VAT deduction rights on the buyer. E-receipts are mandatory for B2C sales. Invoices not transmitted through the ETA e-invoicing channel are treated as not issued for VAT deduction purposes, and no alternative paper-invoice channel exists.

The ETA export-refund and audit directorates cross-check every zero-rating claim against the ASYCUDA World customs database, shipping manifests, inward remittance certificates issued by authorized Egyptian banks, and the accepted e-invoice archive. Where the goods did not leave Egypt, the declared value is inflated, or the export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the transaction is re-characterized as a domestic supply: 14% VAT is assessed on the contract value, the input VAT previously refunded is clawed back, and a monthly late-payment surcharge runs from the original refund date. Administrative evasion penalties apply at statutory percentages of the tax evaded, with discretionary fines scaled by gravity; forged customs or bank certificates expose the entity and its managers to criminal prosecution, and repeated violations trigger suspension of VAT registration and forfeiture of export zero-rating privileges for a period set by the ETA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-float regime and maintains current-account convertibility. Routine current payments, trade flows, and distributions may be effected through CBE-licensed banks without a general prior no-objection, provided the underlying transaction is supported by standard commercial documents. Capital-account inflows, including direct equity contributions by non-resident shareholders, require GAFI registration and bank-level verification; profit remittances are permitted after certification of distributable reserves by the statutory auditor, a valid tax clearance, an updated commercial registration, and a board resolution. In practice, CBE-designated banks apply a documentary gate rather than discretionary rationing, but funds for transactions with sanctions-related counterparties or insufficient import/export documentation are routinely rejected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE instructions, the full invoice value of exported goods must be repatriated to Egypt through a licensed bank within the statutory window, commonly four months from the date of shipment, or such extended period as the CBE approves on a case-by-case basis. There is no mandatory conversion/surrender of export receipts into Egyptian pounds: proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account of the exporting LLC at an Egyptian bank. Any exception permitting disposal or retention of export proceeds abroad prior to repatriation requires a written CBE authorization. For craft-beer export transactions, the bank will require the relevant customs export declaration, the sales contract, and a delivery/pro-forma invoice to confirm the foreign-currency receivable before clearing.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Banking Supervision and Foreign Exchange Control Departments under Law No. 194 of 2020, as applied together with Law No. 80 of 2002 on Anti-Money Laundering and the CBE's successive exchange-control circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs and e-money platforms are not licensed by the CBE to hold EGP deposits, operate domestic IBANs, or execute onshore FX settlement; they therefore cannot be used as primary treasury rails for an Egyptian operating LLC. Restricted use is feasible only as an auxiliary payment interface for small-volume commercial cards or foreign-currency procurement, with the underlying clearing always performed by a CBE-licensed correspondent bank. The compliance burden is disproportionate to the utility, and the decision is therefore disfavored for the fully regulated treasury objective.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from non-resident sponsors are wired in USD, EUR, or GBP to the Egyptian LLC's CBE-licensed bank account under the payment narrative 'Foreign Direct Investment – Share Capital / Shareholder Loan'. The corporate account must be pre-verified by the bank against the GAFI certificate, the LLC registration, and the board resolution authorizing the capital increase. The remitting bank will apply a capital-account purpose code; the receiving Egyptian bank will issue a capital inflow certificate / bank receipt for use in statutory audits, tax reporting, and profit repatriation calculations. Pass-through through third-country EMIs or intermediary wallets must be avoided; all funds must land in the onshore account of the named Egyptian entity. The usual clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with rigorous matching of the beneficiary ID and collection of a unique CBE-level transaction code.Outbound settlements for hops, malt, kegs, cold-chain equipment, and distribution-related services are made primarily by SWIFT MT103 wires or MT202 cover payments for operational invoices, and by commercial letters of credit via SWIFT MT700 for larger capital purchases. The local clearing leg for domestic distributor collections is handled through the CBE retail/clearing system or normal local credit transfers under the Egyptian E-Invoice and Unified Banking Data repository frameworks.
