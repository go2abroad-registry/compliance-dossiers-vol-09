Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), operating under the Ministry of Finance. Official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg. Mandatory electronic invoicing and e-receipt clearance portal: https://www.invoice.eta.gov.eg. The ETA administers the Integrated Tax Code comprising income tax (Law 91/2005), VAT (Law 67/2016), stamp duty, and the Unified Tax Procedures Law (Law 25/2020). For private charitable foundations, the Ministry of Social Solidarity co-grants the founding licence under Law 149/2019 on Non-Governmental Organizations and Private Foundations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned LLC establishing in Egypt must: (1) notarize the memorandum and articles of association and register with the Commercial Registry of the Ministry of Trade and Industry; (2) obtain the charitable foundation licence from the Ministry of Social Solidarity under Law 149/2019 where the LLC operates as a private charitable endowment vehicle; (3) file the unified tax registration application with the ETA, attaching certified constitutional documents, board resolution appointing a resident tax representative, registered address evidence, passport copies of shareholders/managers, and legalized incorporation documents of any non-resident corporate founder; (4) upon verification, receive the unified 9-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) covering income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and the e-invoicing profile. Separate VAT registration is required once annual taxable supplies reach EGP 500,000 (or voluntarily from EGP 250,000). Non-resident suppliers without an Egyptian establishment are not eligible for VAT registration and instead trigger the Egyptian resident recipient's reverse-charge obligation. The TIN is a precondition for bank account opening, customs clearance, and issuance of fiscally valid e-invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide taxable income at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5% for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2023. LLC earnings are subject to entity-level CIT; distributions are not deductible. A licensed private charitable foundation is eligible for a limited-purpose income tax exemption under Article 114 of Law 91/2005 where the ETA confirms that the endowment income is applied exclusively to philanthropic, scientific, or public-benefit grant-making purposes. Unrelated commercial or investment income not deployed for the approved charitable objects remains taxable at the entity level. Egypt does not recognize pass-through taxation for LLCs; the classification is fixed by commercial legal form.

Available election forms

Egypt does not offer US-style check-the-box or hybrid pass-through elections; entity classification is determined by legal form. Available statutory mechanisms include: (1) VAT group registration for related Egyptian persons under Article 16 of Law 67/2016, allowing consolidated VAT filing and netting of input/output VAT before the ETA upon prior approval; (2) voluntary VAT registration for persons with annual taxable supplies between EGP 250,000 and EGP 500,000; (3) Advance Pricing Agreements with the ETA for cross-border related-party arrangements; (4) annual application for charitable income tax exemption under Article 114 of Law 91/2005, subject to ETA listing, audited accounts, and renewal; (5) voluntary self-correction/disclosure procedures with reduced penalties under Law 25/2020. Hybrid mismatch anti-abuse is addressed through general anti-abuse rules, Egyptian CFC provisions, thin-capitalization limits, and beneficial-ownership/economic-substance audit practice rather than formal elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies to any person whose taxable supplies of goods and services exceed EGP 500,000 over any rolling 12-month period. Voluntary registration is permitted for taxable supplies above EGP 250,000 per annum. Exempt non-profit activities are excluded from taxable turnover for threshold measurement. Non-resident suppliers with no Egyptian fixed establishment have no registration threshold and the Egyptian recipient must self-assess VAT via the reverse-charge mechanism on imported services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 23 of VAT Law 67/2016, exports of goods and services are zero-rated. Goods qualify when they physically depart Egyptian customs territory under a valid customs export declaration, are sold to a non-resident buyer, and are shipped within the statutory export period. Services qualify when supplied to a non-resident recipient with no Egyptian establishment and the service is consumed wholly outside Egypt. International transport and directly related services are also zero-rated. For a private charitable foundation, pure grant disbursements and endowment donations are outside the VAT charge as non-economic transfers; however, management fees invoiced to offshore endowment funds and scientific grant-administration services supplied to non-residents consumed abroad may qualify for 0% VAT provided the foreign recipient-beneficiary test, foreign-currency invoicing, and offshore-use substantiation are met. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is creditable and produces refundable positions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (certified customs release proving physical departure of goods from Egypt, matching commodity code, quantity, and value)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (or multimodal transport document naming a consignee outside Egypt and confirming shipment out of Egyptian territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign currency within the prescribed repatriation deadline

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance applies to all VAT-registered businesses. Taxpayers must transmit electronic invoices (B2B) and electronic receipts (B2C) to the ETA through the official portal or certified ERP integration in JSON format. Each invoice is digitally signed with an ETA-issued e-certificate (SHA-256 digest and RSA signature) and receives a unique UUID and submission code from the ETA before it acquires fiscal validity. Rejected invoices are invalid for input VAT recovery and expose the issuer to penalties. Rollout phases for e-invoicing and e-receipts are fully operative; the ETA enforces compliance by suspending TIN credentials and e-invoicing profiles for non-compliant registrants.

The ETA may re-open export zero-rating claims and refund positions for the statutory assessment period despite completed refunds. Fraudulent or fictitious zero-rated exports, including sham transport documents, false foreign-currency invoicing, or non-repatriation of proceeds, trigger retrospective clawback of the VAT refund and related input credits, plus late-payment additional tax at 2% per month or fraction thereof. Administrative penalties per invoice, criminal fines, imprisonment for wilful tax evasion, strike-off from the exporter priority-refund registry, suspension of the TIN, and joint liability of foundation principals and responsible officers may be imposed. Documentary integrity, matching of the customs export declaration with the e-invoice submission, and CBE-compliant foreign-currency repatriation are the primary audit focal points.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates an overall managed float; current account transactions are largely liberalized, while capital account operations remain subject to CBE registration and approval. Profit remittance to non-resident investors is permitted through the receiving licensed bank, contingent on CBE registration of the original inward capital injection, audited financial statements, local tax clearance, and compliance with Law 194/2020. For a private charitable foundation, 'profit' is limited to income derived from managing its endowment; donor grants are not distributable as profit, and any conversion or onward remittance of foreign-currency grants is subject to enhanced source-of-funds and purpose-of-gift reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt within 90 days from the date of bill of lading or due shipment date unless a CBE-approved extension is obtained. Under the current liberalized regime, no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed; exporters may retain foreign currency in a CBE-approved export proceeds account. The foundation itself, being an endowment-management entity with no export trade, remains subject to the same statutory framework if it undertakes any incidental export activity. Failure to repatriate within the 90-day window exposes the entity to penalties under the CBE Banking and Credit Law No. 194 of 2020 and associated sanctions regulations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – Foreign Exchange Operations Sector and Banking Supervision Sector

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut, etc.) do not possess a CBE license and cannot serve as principal treasury rails for an Egyptian-domiciled LLC/foundation. CBE regulations require all corporate FX and cross-border settlement to be executed through banks licensed under Law 194/2020. Such EMIs may only be used as ancillary collection/payment tools where they ultimately route funds through a CBE-supervised bank, but the foundation must maintain its primary operational and escrow accounts at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank for statutory and compliance reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections (endowment transfers, grants, donations) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to a CBE-licensed bank account in the foundation's name. The remitter must include a purpose code corresponding to 'transfers for social/charitable services' or 'capital endowment injection' as defined in CBE transaction classification guidelines. The foundation must file the mandatory foreign-funding declaration with the Ministry of Social Solidarity under Law 149/2019, and obtain a CBE no-objection confirmation for any intended conversion to EGP. All inbound funds must be matched to the foundation's CR (commercial registry) extract and Egyptian tax card; no third-party pass-through is permitted.Outbound disbursements, whether grant distributions or payments for cross-border goods/services, are executed via SWIFT MT103 in the designated settlement currency through the foundation's CBE-supervised bank. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under Egyptian Companies Law No. 159/1981 as amended by Law No. 4/2016, an LLC must appoint an independent certified auditor when: (i) the Articles of Association or shareholders' agreement mandates audit; (ii) the company's paid-up capital exceeds EGP 250,000; (iii) annual turnover exceeds EGP 5,000,000; (iv) the number of shareholders exceeds ten; or (v) a regulatory authority — in this case the charitable-endowment licensing authority — requires audit as a license condition. A private charitable foundation managing endowment assets is always subject to statutory audit because it administers third-party beneficiary funds.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the close of the fiscal year (Article 36, Law No. 91/2005); a calendar-year foundation must file no later than 30 April of the following year. Quarterly self-assessed advance instalments apply during the year under Articles 27-29.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million aggregate controlled transactions per fiscal year triggers the Local File; the Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenues exceed EGP 1.5 billion (ETA Transfer Pricing Decisions Nos. 457-458 of 2020).

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the Article 51 exemption, Article 30 GAAR and substance-over-form practice impose equivalent conditions: the foundation must hold genuine board meetings in Egypt, maintain a local grant pipeline, employ substantive staff, keep segregated charitable bank accounts and document each financing arrangement. A letter-box charitable vehicle loses the exemption and attracts back taxes and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by false declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Articles 133-136 of Law No. 91/2005 as amended: imprisonment generally up to five years, fines scaled to the evaded tax, compulsory civil restitution, 1.5% monthly delay surcharge and revocation of the charitable license.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil in fraud and sham structures; directors and signatories are jointly and severally liable for the foundation's tax debt, face travel bans and asset freezes by ETA collection units, management disqualification and custodial sentences.
Shell charitable entity / sham economic substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA retroactively revokes the Article 51 exemption, taxes accumulated endowment returns at 22.5%, imposes delay surcharges and fines, and invokes Article 30 GAAR to recharacterise the structure; license cancellation under Law No. 149/2019 may follow.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners are identified through anti-avoidance disclosure; directors are jointly liable for restored tax, named in criminal referrals and subject to travel bans while the liability remains unpaid.
AML / counter-terrorist financing and NGO licensing

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80/2002 and counter-terrorism Law No. 94/2015: fines up to EGP 10,000,000, confiscation and freezing orders, imprisonment up to 15 years for responsible directors, and blacklisting of the foundation by the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors bear individual criminal liability for wilful blindness in grant disbursements, are subject to travel bans and deportation measures, and face personal asset freezing where foundation funds were used to conceal illicit proceeds.
Withholding tax and DTA abuse on repatriation

Statutory sanction: The LLC remains liable as debtor for the un-withheld tax (Article 59) plus the 1.5% monthly delay surcharge and a fine up to EGP 100,000; false TRC usage triggers criminal fraud charges, restitution and recapture of treaty benefits with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign withholding declarations are personally liable for uncollected amounts, face civil prosecution for misapplication of company funds, and may be banned from cross-border travel until tax clearance is issued.
