Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – official name: Misr El-Mehwar for Tax Administration; corporate and VAT registration portal: https://www.incometax.gov.eg; e-invoicing portal: https://www.invoice.bambooroute.eta.gov.eg; VAT returns portal: https://eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

The foreign entity must incorporate as an Egyptian LLC (branch or standalone), obtain a commercial record (CR) from the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), then register with the Egyptian Tax Authority within 30 days of commencing business to obtain the Tax Card (TIN) and VAT registration certificate. Required documents include the CR, share register, lease contract, power of attorney, passport copies of shareholders, board resolution, and proof of office address. Tax ID is issued in paper form and activated in ETA’s e-system; VAT registration must be completed before issue of tax invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The foreign entity must incorporate as an Egyptian LLC (branch or standalone), obtain a commercial record (CR) from the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), then register with the Egyptian Tax Authority within 30 days of commencing business to obtain the Tax Card (TIN) and VAT registration certificate. Required documents include the CR, share register, lease contract, power of attorney, passport copies of shareholders, board resolution, and proof of office address. Tax ID is issued in paper form and activated in ETA’s e-system; VAT registration must be completed before issue of tax invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists for Egyptian LLCs. Corporations are taxed at the entity level only; shareholders are taxed on dividends through withholding. There is no domestic hybrid-mismatch election, and Egypt does not apply US-style check-the-box rules. However, a small or micro business may elect simplified taxation under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 (as amended by Law 14/2018) if turnover is below EGP 20 million. No other material elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (Art. 19, VAT Law 67/2016). A foreign person opening a permanent establishment or performing taxable economic activity in Egypt must register irrespective of turnover if supplies are deemed Egyptian.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 32 of Law 67/2016 zero-rates exported goods where title passes to a non-resident, the goods are physically transported out of Egypt within the statutory period, and export occurs under a valid customs export declaration. Offshore services and related supplies physically performed and used outside Egypt may be zero-rated when received by a non-resident. The exporter must hold documentary evidence of actual export and hard-currency proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill/CMR)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRS issued by Egyptian bank proving FX proceeds received)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Purchase/supply contract with foreign buyer
  • ETA zero-rating evidence record

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance through the Egyptian Tax Authority’s (ETA) e-invoice platform. All registered taxpayers must integrate business systems with the ETA API, upload invoice XML/JSON files before dispatch, and receive a unique tax invoice ID and two-dimensional barcode (QR) validated by the ETA. Invoices without ETA clearance and QR stamp are considered invalid evidence for tax deduction. E-receipt requirements apply to retail sellers. Phased implementation has reached large and medium taxpayers; full mandatory application to all VAT registrants is enforced.

The ETA may conduct post-export desk audits with full inspection of customs, shipping, and bank remittance records. If zero-rating is disallowed, the taxpayer must pay the 14% VAT with late additions from the date of export; a penalty not exceeding 100% of tax is applicable under Law 67/2016. Fraudulent export-zero-rating claims may trigger criminal sanctions including imprisonment, fines of up to EGP 1 million, and forfeiture of rights to export relief. Retrospective assessment limitation is generally 5 years, or 6 years in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations since 1997, so current-account transactions, including profit and dividend remittances, are formally unrestricted. Following the 6 March 2024 unification of the exchange rate, the EGP/USD rate is determined by the interbank market. Convertibility is subject to administrative documentation and bank-side compliance rather than CBE pre-approval. Profit remittance requires audited financial statements demonstrating distributable retained earnings, a valid tax clearance certificate, and the instructing bank's sanctions, PEP, and source-of-funds screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign-exchange instructions and the Executive Regulations of Law No. 194 of 2020, export proceeds must be received into an Egyptian bank account within 180 days of the bill-of-lading date. Following the March 2024 float, the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; exporters may retain proceeds in foreign-currency accounts or convert at the prevailing interbank rate. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window triggers CBE penalties, suspension of trade facilities, and customs export-compliance review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Al-Bank al-Markazi al-Misri; competent supervisory authority for all foreign-exchange operations under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, acting through its Foreign Exchange Committee.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — no passporting right exists for foreign e-money institutions in Egypt. Under Law No. 194 of 2020, only CBE-licensed banks and licensed payment service providers may hold funds, operate payment accounts, or execute EGP/USD conversion in the onshore market. Offshore digital EMIs cannot access the CBE clearing systems (RTGS/EgySI) or maintain settlement accounts. A licensed local PSP partnership (for example Paymob or Fawry) is feasible only for low-value retail collections and is not suitable for wholesale trade settlement, letter-of-credit disbursements, or export proceeds repatriation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for aggregate controlled transactions exceeding EGP 10 million with a related party; Master File required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 500 million; CbC report required if group turnover exceeds EGP 25.6 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egyptian tax law applies substance-over-form and GAAR; entities claiming treaty benefits or free-zone exemptions must demonstrate a real business presence including physical warehouse, Egyptian bank accounts, transfer of title/inventory, and decision-making in Egypt. Absent economic substance, transactions can be re-characterized and tax privileges stripped.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Under-reporting Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Law 91/2005, evaded tax is collected plus a 50% administrative penalty (doubled for repeat offending); criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the fraudulent return or knowingly omit income become jointly and severally liable for the tax, civil penalties, and risk travel bans and personal imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Distributions

Statutory sanction: The paying entity is held liable for the unrecovered WHT, a 2% monthly delay surcharge on the underpaid amount, and a civil fine up to EGP 20,000 per return.

Director personal liability: Any director or senior officer accountable for the payment is personally liable for the withheld amount if it was deducted but not remitted; asset freezing and travel bans are commonly applied.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficit

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make an upward TP adjustment, impose a 10% additional tax on the adjustment amount, and apply a monthly delay penalty on the extra assessment.

Director personal liability: Officers who fail to ensure contemporaneous documentation are deemed primary tax violators under GAAR and can be jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalty. In extreme cases, imprisonment for up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Artificial Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Treble damage: denial of treaty benefits, retroactive revocation of free-zone tax holiday, re-qualification of transactions as taxable, and a fraud penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing under commercial and tax law; shareholders/directors can be ordered to pay corporate debts and taxes from personal assets, and face criminal sanctions under Egyptian Companies Law.
Money Laundering / AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: AML Law No. 80/2002 imposes a fine up to EGP 1,000,000 and imprisonment from 3 to 7 years; the funds and property derived from laundering are confiscated.

Director personal liability: In addition to criminal imprisonment, compliance directors face personal confiscation orders, disqualification from corporate office, and reputational travel bans upon indictment.
