Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended by Law No. 4 of 2022, including the provisions applicable to Limited Liability Companies; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations; Commercial Registration Law provisions relating to registration of companies with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association (Aqd Al-Shirkah) submitted electronically through GAFI, culminating in the Certificate of Incorporation (Shahadat Al-Qaid Al-Tijari).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the amended Companies Law, an LLC is not subject to a prescribed statutory minimum capital. The founding members must declare an initial capital in the memorandum, which must be fully paid into a temporary blocked escrow bank account in the company's name and held until the Certificate of Incorporation is issued, after which the funds are released to the company treasury.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the General Administration of Civil Defense; structural load clearance for floor loading; workplace occupational safety inspection by the competent labor authority; emergency lighting, evacuation signage, and fire extinguisher servicing under the Egyptian Fire Code.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — the unified national revenue agency of Egypt; primary portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoice, e-receipt and e-filing platform: https://www.invoice.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the foreign-owned LLC with GAFI (General Authority for Investment) and obtain the Commercial Registry certificate. 2) Submit the tax registration application (Tax Card/TIN) to the ETA territorial tax office under the Unified Tax Procedures Law 206/2020, including the certified Arabic incorporation deed, commercial registry extract, leasing/business-address evidence, passport copies of members, board resolution appointing an Egyptian tax representative, and opening bank confirmation. 3) File a separate VAT registration application on the ETA portal once actual or expected annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000, or elect voluntary VAT registration when exporting zero-rated services. 4) Activate the ETA e-invoicing account and obtain the PKI digital-signature certificate for FATORA clearance. Trackable turnaround typically 10–20 business days after complete submission.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed independently under Income Tax Law 91/2005 (standard CIT 22.5%); no pass-through eligibility for LLC members.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in Egypt. Statutory elections available: (a) voluntary VAT registration for exporters below the EGP 500,000 compulsory threshold (Law 67/2016, Art. 78); (b) group tax consolidation for wholly-owned Egyptian subsidiaries upon ETA approval (Law 91/2005, Art. 60); (c) treaty relief/advance pricing applications for cross-border engineering service income; (d) free-zone or Special Economic Zone status (e.g., SCZone) election if the LLC relocates operations into an approved zone; (e) anti-hybrid-related deduction restrictions under the GAAR clauses of Law 91/2005 (Art. 59) disallow payments to tax-exempt or hybrid-entity related persons.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 aggregate annual taxable supplies for compulsory VAT registration (Law 67/2016, Art. 78); no threshold for non-resident digital or e-commerce service suppliers; exporters may voluntarily register below the threshold to claim refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to export of goods and to services supplied to a non-resident recipient where the service is wholly performed or consumed outside Egypt, the consideration is received in convertible foreign currency remitted into an Egyptian bank account, and the offshore utilization is evidenced; for EG_LLC's offshore BIM/CAD deliverables, the contract must specify exclusive use of outputs outside Egypt, the recipient must be non-resident, and delivery/utilization must occur outside Egyptian territory.

  • Certified export contract naming the non-resident client and stating offshore delivery of CAD/BIM services
  • Egyptian Customs export declaration (SAD) for any physical deliverables or media shipped abroad
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Egypt when applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or authenticated SWIFT MT103 proving the foreign-currency consideration entered an Egyptian bank account
  • ETA zero-rating substantiation file: serialized commercial invoice matching the cleared e-invoice XML, offshore service acceptance certificate, non-resident tax-residency certificate of the client, and utilization logs (IP access, deliverable handover) confirming consumption abroad

Mandatory ETA-governed e-invoicing and e-receipt regime per Ministerial Decrees 188/2021, 123/2022 and 127/2022, phased for all VAT-registered enterprises; invoices/receipts must be transmitted in real time to the FATORA platform (https://www.invoice.eta.gov.eg) in ETA-compliant XML, digitally signed with ETA-issued PKI certificates, validated synchronously via API, and stamped with a unique QR code; paper or non-cleared invoices are legally null and attract administrative penalties.

ETA desk and field audits may re-characterize improperly documented exports as taxable local supplies, resulting in retroactive 14% VAT assessment plus delay tax at 2% per month or fraction thereof up to the statutory cap (100%), fines ranging from EGP 5,000 to EGP 100,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years under Law 67/2016 Arts. 73 and 76 for fraudulent zero-rating claims; invalid export documentation also nullifies the Egyptian recipient's input-credit entitlement and exposes the exporter to refund clawback plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates under a partial liberalization regime. Current account transactions are broadly convertible, while capital account remittances such as dividends, loan repayments, and capital repatriation require prior CBE registration, documentary substantiation, and passage through a licensed Egyptian bank under the post-2016 flexible exchange-rate framework. Profit remittances are freely permitted once audited financial statements and a tax clearance certificate are submitted. The underlying foreign-direct investment must be registered with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), and the incoming capital must be certified by the receiving bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be credited to an Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the date of shipment and are subject to a mandatory surrender ratio of 100% to the local bank unless the CBE grants a specific exemption, such as financing inputs or repayment of existing foreign currency obligations. The exporting company must submit the customs-related export declaration and the applicable CBE foreign-exchange form to evidence the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Payoneer are not licensed by the CBE and cannot maintain Egyptian pound accounts, receive EGP-cleared payments, or provide local virtual IBANs. Their use is restricted to inbound and outbound remittances in convertible currencies where the underlying funds originate from or settle to a licensed Egyptian bank. For treasury operations, a direct CBE-licensed bank account is legally required for all local-currency and EGP-denominated settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity capital must be remitted from the shareholder's bank account to the entity's CBE-licensed bank in Egypt via SWIFT MT103. The transfer reference must state the company identifier and the purpose code for FDI equity. The receiving bank will complete the CBE foreign-exchange declaration, verify against GAFI registration, credit the capital escrow account, and issue a foreign-exchange certificate evidencing the conversion to EGP for subsequent profit-repatriation calculations. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the close of the fiscal year. For a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 30 April; for fiscals ended 30 June, 31 October.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Gross engineering/CAD/BIM service revenue and other business receipts arising from Egypt and foreign clients attributable to the Egyptian LLC. Non-resident shareholders — not the LLC itself — should not file this form in their own names unless they carry on a permanent establishment (PE) in Egypt. Where the LLC is owned by a foreign investor, all Egyptian-source revenue of the LLC is reported here; if the LLC has no PE and is merely collecting contracted fees for an offshore principal, such fees should instead be subject to withholding and not entered as LLC revenue. Late filing attracts a periodic fine not exceeding EGP 5,000 plus an incremental penalty for each month of default. Late payment of assessed CIT accrues a monthly surcharge of 0.5% of the unpaid amount. Aggravated assessments after a tax investigation can impose up to 20% additional tax on the understated tax, plus fines and imprisonment where evasion is proven.
Box 2 Direct costs of service delivery: payroll of architects/engineers, software and platform expenses, subcontracted drafting services, depreciation of computers and technical equipment. Only deductible if incurred for generating taxable revenue and evidenced by bank transfers, contracts, and e-invoices. Intra-group service charges paid to foreign affiliates must satisfy transfer pricing documentation and Egyptian economic substance principles before deduction. Late filing attracts a periodic fine not exceeding EGP 5,000 plus an incremental penalty for each month of default. Late payment of assessed CIT accrues a monthly surcharge of 0.5% of the unpaid amount. Aggravated assessments after a tax investigation can impose up to 20% additional tax on the understated tax, plus fines and imprisonment where evasion is proven.
Box 3 General administrative expenses: rent, commercial registration renewals, professional fees, marketing, utilities, and insurance. Non-resident owner's general and administrative costs cannot be recharged to the Egyptian LLC unless supported by an intercompany agreement and an arm's-length markup. Without Form No. 41 declarations and TP documentation, the ETA may disallow the deduction. Late filing attracts a periodic fine not exceeding EGP 5,000 plus an incremental penalty for each month of default. Late payment of assessed CIT accrues a monthly surcharge of 0.5% of the unpaid amount. Aggravated assessments after a tax investigation can impose up to 20% additional tax on the understated tax, plus fines and imprisonment where evasion is proven.
Box 4 Taxable income before adjustment: Box 1 minus Box 2 and Box 3, adjusted for non-deductible provisions, donations, and intercompany payments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions in a fiscal year triggers mandatory Egyptian Transfer Pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) under the ETA Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. However, the ETA applies the OECD BEPS Action 6 beneficial-ownership and substance requirements to deny DTA and withholding relief. A CAD/BIM outsourcing LLC must demonstrate live Egyptian management decisions, a physical office, local technical staff and payroll, and real operational bank accounts in Egypt. Failing this, the structure may be disregarded, treaty benefits denied, and all revenue treated as Egyptian-source without cost deductions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Omission of Egyptian-Source Revenue

Statutory sanction: Additional tax up to 25% of the hidden tax payable, fines under Law 91/2005 of up to EGP 50,000, and imprisonment for a term starting at two years. The ETA may also compulsorily reassess gross revenue at 22.5% CIT and disallow all contested deductions pending criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who authorized concealment are jointly liable for the underpaid tax, interest, and fines; the tax authority can issue travel bans on the directors and the general manager pursuant to the Tax Collection Law and seek sequestration of personal assets, followed by imprisonment of controlling shareholders for criminal evasion.
Sham/Shell Company Lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the intercompany contracts and treat the LLC as fiscally nonexistent, reattributing 100% of the net profit to the foreign shareholder as Egyptian-source distributions subject to 10% dividend WHT and additional tax of up to 20% of the underassessed amount. The company's trade license may be suspended by GAFI, and all tax exemptions and incentives are revoked retroactively.

Director personal liability: On veil-piercing under Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 2020, directors and shadow managers are personally liable for the company's tax debt if they stripped assets or maintained fictitious capital. Courts may impose a travel ban, civil restitution of unpaid tax, and imprisonment for the de facto manager for issuing false invoices and obstructing tax inspection.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violation of Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002: fines up to EGP 200,000 and imprisonment for up to three years, with confiscation of the laundered amounts and underlying assets. Licensed service providers involved in the transaction may be penalized with revocation or suspension, and the FIU may freeze corporate bank accounts immediately.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure customer due diligence and beneficial ownership disclosure can be held personally liable for corporate AML fines and civil restitution. In aggravated cases involving cross-border AEDT-style engineering contracts, the public prosecutor can impose travel bans and pretrial detention on the directors while funds in Egyptian banks are sequestered.
