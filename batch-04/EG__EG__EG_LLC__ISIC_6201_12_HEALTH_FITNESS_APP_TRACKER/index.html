Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None available. Egypt does not recognize check-the-box or hybrid entity elections. LLC is treated as a separate taxable entity under unified corporate income tax law. A mandatory consolidated tax return can apply for subsidiaries under common control, but no entity classification election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for resident/established persons; non-residents supplying taxable goods or services in Egypt are subject to mandatory registration through a tax representative irrespective of turnover; voluntary registration possible at EGP 250,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of tangible goods exported outside Egypt and services performed and consumed entirely outside Egypt, including digital health app licensing/subscriptions delivered to non-resident users, are eligible for 0% VAT provided the supplier retains uncontroverted proof of export, receipt of foreign currency, and the economic ownership of the exported goods/service effective use lies abroad. The service supplier must permanently possess objective evidence that the customer is a non-resident and that no effective use or enjoyment of the service occurs in Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACD / Cargo Tracking Number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for physical goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit/confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds in an Egyptian bank account
  • Service-specific export evidence: executed overseas consumption documentation, non-resident customer contract, IP/subscription user domicile proof, online telemetry export logs

Mandatory centralized e-invoicing clearances. Taxpayers must transmit machine-readable invoices (JSON/XML) to the ETA, cryptographically sign them and request an ETA-generated UUID acceptance before issuing to customer. Real-time validation via REST API/SDK; invoices must include QR code in offline scenarios. E-receipts (B2C) phase-in by turnover bracket is mandatory; non-compliance blocks issuance and subjects to penalties.

ETA and Customs conduct document-intensive refund audits. Failure to prove zero-rating or late repatriation of export proceeds triggers: (1) retrospective clawback of VAT at 14%, (2) late payment penalties up to 15% per year, (3) fraud penalties ranging from 50% to 100% of VAT evaded, (4) administrative fines up to EGP 5,000,000 and imprisonment for felony-level tax evasion. Misdescribed of export documentation and artificial export transactions are treated as tax evasion and may expose the taxpayer and authorized officers to criminal liability and permanent loss of export incentive status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the 6 March 2024 currency reform, Egypt operates a market-driven flexible exchange-rate regime in which the EGP-USD reference price is determined by interbank supply and demand with CBE intervention limited to disorderly-market conditions. Current-account transactions, including software and application licensing, digital-health subscription revenue, technical services, royalties, and profit remittances, are freely convertible upon presentation of the underlying contract, commercial invoice, platform settlement statements, and tax documentation to an authorized dealer bank. Capital-account convertibility is restricted: inward foreign investment must be recorded under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and the CBE inbound-capital registration framework to activate the statutory repatriation guarantee; outward dividend and liquidation remittances require audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, proof of the original inward remittance, and the CBE registration validation number. Fitness-app revenue remitted from Apple App Store In-App Purchase and Google Play Billing is classified as service-export income and is convertible at the free interbank rate with no preferential spread.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All exporters of goods and services are required to repatriate proceeds through a CBE-licensed bank: goods within six months from the bill-of-lading date and services within three months from the invoice date. A mandatory currency-conversion tier applies to repatriated foreign currency; as a standing rule, 50% of export proceeds must be converted into EGP within 30 days of availability in the exporter's Egyptian bank account, with the ratio adjustable by CBE circular (historically raised to 100% during liquidity stress and relaxed after the March 2024 float). The undistributed remainder is held in an Export Proceeds Foreign-Currency Account and may be applied to permissible working-capital, import, and debt-service obligations, each supported by underlying invoices and CBE forms.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), established under the Central Bank, Banking Sector and Monetary System Law No. 88 of 2003, and exercising exclusive regulatory power over the Egyptian banking system, foreign-exchange licensing, monetary policy, and cross-border payment and clearing operations pursuant to Foreign Exchange Law No. 38 of 1977 and the Unified Banking Law.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Egyptian law does not recognize foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) as corporate deposit custodians or clearing participants; only CBE-licensed banks may hold resident corporate deposits and execute EGP clearing. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, and Mercury are usable solely as offshore collection and invoicing conduits for outbound disbursements; every inbound capital item (equity subscription, shareholder loan, export proceeds) must ultimately be received in a licensed Egyptian bank account to satisfy the CBE repatriation, surrender, and AML obligations under Law No. 80 of 2002 and FinTech Law No. 194 of 2020. EGP balances held outside Egypt are prohibited, rendering any unlicensed EGP-clearing arrangement illegal.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% (Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended by Law No. 176 of 2020)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on dividends and in-kind distributions made by Egyptian LLCs to both resident and non-resident shareholders under Article 23 of Law No. 91 of 2005, unless reduced by an applicable Double Taxation Agreement. The 10% is final for individually resident shareholders and a credit or refund for resident corporate shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing and final settlement are due within four months after the end of the tax year. For a calendar-year LLC, the deadline is 30 April of the following year. A 60-day extension may be administratively granted by the ETA on request only for objective reasons.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory when the taxpayer's related-party transactions in a given tax year exceed EGP 10 million in aggregate. A Master File is additionally required where the same threshold is met and the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 1 billion. A Country-by-Country Report is required when the group's consolidated revenue reaches EGP 12 billion. All documentation must be contemporaneous and submitted with the annual return or upon ETA notice, in Arabic or English.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE; however, Article 30 of Law No. 91 of 2005 enshrines substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and general anti-abuse principles. An Egyptian LLC holding the health-fitness app IP but with no board meeting, no Egyptian employees, no server or activity coordination, and no commercial decision-making will be treated as a conduit for the foreign shareholder. The ETA may disregard the entity, deny treaty benefits, re-assign income to the true foreign owner, and impose branch-level WHT and penalties. For AML purposes, the LLC must file beneficial ownership data in the commercial registry; absence of economic substance is a red flag for both GAFI and the ETA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Article 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005, imprisonment for 3 to 5 years and a fine of 100% to 300% of the evaded tax; if the taxpayer is a legal person, the fine is doubled; assets may be seized by coercive administrative execution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the false return or direct the concealment are criminally co-liable; civil liability attaches to their personal property for the unpaid tax, delayed penalties, and restitution; courts may issue travel bans and impose custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: ETA may disregard the shell entity, re-characterise gross receipts as foreign profit, deny DTA benefit, impose 20% WHT on diverted royalties, and apply penal interest and 2% monthly additional tax on the under-assessment; Commercial Registry may be struck off under Companies Law.

Director personal liability: The doctrine of beneficial owner is applied, and directors as de facto managers are personally accountable for refusing to file accurate disclosure; travel links to the shell entity trigger GAFI financial embargoes; repeated abuse leads to personal penal liability for the shadow principal.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA adjustment of taxable income, 2% monthly interest until payment, administrative fine under Executive Regulation of Law 91/2005 for defective TP documentation, and potential re-qualification of payments as fees subject to 20% WHT; failure to file CbC report invokes additional EGP fines and enhanced audit.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to penalty as same-day managers if they intentionally certify an inaccurate TP policy; the ETA can pierce the corporate veil when proof shows that the LLC exists solely to avoid tax, making directors jointly and severally liable for the additional tax plus uncapped late-payment interest.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 imposes imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines up to EGP 100,000; Beneficial Ownership Register violations carry corporate fines and cancellation of the commercial registration under GAIF directives; the ETA can additionally block the filing of a valid CIT return until AML compliance certificate is remediated.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and the nominated beneficial owner may receive personal fines, travel bans and freezing orders; where the proceeds of suspect health-data transactions are found to be criminal gains, the court may order the seizing of the director's personal bank accounts and assets.
