Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Cairo; unified tax e-services portal https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing clearance portal https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg; customs controls are administered separately by the Egyptian Customs Authority through the Nafeza National Single Window for Foreign Trade.

E-government filing portal

As a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC (Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, no statutory minimum capital), the company first obtains its Commercial Registration Certificate (CRN) and the maritime operating license issued by the Egyptian Maritime Transport Authority, which authorizes deep-sea towage, salvage and cross-border vessel assistance. Within 30 days of commencement, the LLC's legal representative (or an appointed resident tax agent under a notarized power of attorney) files the unified tax registration application at the competent ETA office, attaching the notarized incorporation contract, CRN extract, registered-office lease or title deed, passport/ID copies of directors and managers, the maritime license, and licensed Egyptian bank account details. The ETA issues a unified Tax Card bearing one Tax Identification Number (TIN) that simultaneously covers corporate income tax, VAT and withholding obligations; the same TIN is later activated for the Nafeza customs-registration profile and the e-invoicing user account. Because the operating vehicle is a resident Egyptian LLC, the non-resident VAT-registration route is unavailable; full resident registration is mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-transparent elections for LLCs; a resident LLC is automatically taxed at entity level on its worldwide gains under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005. Available statutory elections include: (i) a formal application to treat a parent and its 50%-or-more-owned subsidiaries as one economic unit for consolidated income tax purposes; (ii) an application for an Advance Pricing Agreement covering cross-border towage and salvage margins; and (iii) election, where the licensed activity operates in a free zone or the Suez Canal Economic Zone, of the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 regime substituting the annual 1% duty or the zone-specific incentives for corporate tax. Hybrid mismatch elections are not statutorily available; the OECD MLI instruments and Egyptian general anti-avoidance principles govern such outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is triggered once annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000 (Article 15 of Law No. 67 of 2016). Voluntary registration is permitted below this level, and the ETA operates a simplified compliance regime for small suppliers. An exporter of zero-rated towage and salvage services must nevertheless register immediately upon the first taxable event and cannot rely on the turnover threshold because export-oriented activity is registrable at the incorporation stage.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 23 of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, exported goods and services, including international maritime transport of passengers and goods, are taxable at 0%. A deep-sea towage and emergency salvage contract qualifies for zero-rating when: (i) the Egyptian-resident LLC performs the service as a foreign-going maritime operation for a non-resident shipowner, head charterer, hull underwriter or P&I club; (ii) the tug's voyage documents, AIS records and the master's service statement demonstrate that the assisted vessel's movement is international or that the salvage assistance is incidental to an international voyage; and (iii) the consideration is settled from abroad in freely convertible currency credited to the LLC's licensed Egyptian bank account. Domestic towing, cabotage, and salvage wholly performed in Egyptian internal or territorial waters for Egyptian cargo are excluded from zero-rating and attract the standard 14% VAT rate.

  • Customs export declaration lodged through the Nafeza National Single Window with the Egyptian Customs Authority identifying the towed or salved vessel and any ship's stores or goods on board; for goods-free towage, the coastal/port authority vessel movement certificate and the tug's notice of departure confirming the international voyage.
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill supplemented by the signed towage/salvage service order and the master's signed services receipt showing the port of loading or assistance and the foreign destination; for salvage operations, the Lloyds Standard Form or equivalent SCOPIC/arbitration record where applicable.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the LLC's licensed Egyptian bank confirming receipt in freely convertible currency within the applicable CBE export-proceeds period, matched to the invoice and the foreign payer's bank reference.

The Egyptian electronic invoice system (ETA Decision No. 601 of 2020, as amended) requires every taxable person issuing sales invoices to generate e-invoices in the ETA-approved XML/JSON schema, sign them with the taxpayer's electronic certificate, transmit them via the public REST API or the ETA web portal, and obtain an ETA clearance verdict together with a UUID, QR code and digital stamp before the invoice becomes legally valid. Non-integrated suppliers face invoice rejection, penalties and suspension of the TIN. The sister e-receipt regime imposes real-time clearance for qualifying B2C supplies. Export invoices for offshore towage and salvage must carry the export/zero-rating reason code and cross-reference the port-authority movement certificate and the Nafeza customs export number.

The ETA's Export Refund Unit and the Large Taxpayer Center apply document-level and bank-remittance audits to every zero-rating claim and may reopen a period within the statutory assessment window, generally 5 years, extended where fraud is shown. A claim is clawed back retroactively where the documentation bundle is incomplete or contradictory, where the ultimate recipient is Egyptian, or where repatriation of the convertible proceeds exceeds the applicable CBE deadline; the clawback carries the unpaid VAT plus a late-payment supplement of 2% per month. Under Article 59 of Law No. 67 of 2016, deliberate false zero-rating or forged export documentation constitutes tax evasion punishable by imprisonment and/or a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 1,000,000 in addition to the entire tax evaded, while e-invoice signature misuse and non-integration trigger administrative penalties and suspension of the TIN, with personal criminal exposure for the tax representative and the managing director.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the 6 March 2024 FX unification, Egypt operates a single, bank-driven interbank market for the Egyptian pound. Current-account receipts and payments, including cross-border towage and maritime salvage fees, are generally convertible in Egypt if routed through authorized banks with full underlying documentation. Capital-account movements remain controlled: equity and shareholder loans require registration with CBE/GAFI and must be booked through a licensed Egyptian bank. Profit and dividend remittance are permitted after audited financial statements, settlement of Egyptian tax liabilities or valid withholding relief, and confirmation of the GAFI investment file. Original capital repatriation requires CBE no-objection and GAFI deregistration procedures. The Egyptian pound is thus best characterized as 'partially convertible': current-account convertibility is broad, but capital-account convertibility is conditional and documentary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian service exporters, including an EG_LLC conducting offshore towage/salvage, must repatriate the full value of foreign currency export proceeds to an account with a licensed Egyptian bank no later than 180 days from the service invoice date or the contractual completion certificate date, whichever is earlier. Under current CBE practice, no fixed percentage of gross export proceeds is compulsorily sold for EGP at the moment of receipt; the statutory obligation is full repatriation to the licensed banking system. The exporter may hold up to 100% of the received foreign currency in a USD operational account for a maximum retention period of six months, during which the balance may be applied to admissible current payments (bunkers, port/channel expenses, P&I insurance, towage subcontractors, and trade settlements). Any unconsumed balance remaining after the expiry of the permitted retention period must be sold to a licensed bank at the prevailing interbank rate unless the bank and CBE have approved a rollover justified by a firm, underlying trade commitment.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Foreign Exchange Operations Sector, exercising statutory powers under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and the CBE's successive foreign-exchange circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail for the Egyptian legal entity. Offshore EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under CBE Law No. 194 of 2020 and may not hold an EGP account, issue local IBANs, or provide onshore escrow capacity. They are acceptable only as ancillary collection channels outside Egypt that originate a SWIFT MT103 credit to a licensed Egyptian Tier-1 bank. Any domestic wallet or fintech wanting to clear salvage/towage proceeds in Egypt must hold a payment-facilitation licence from the CBE under Law No. 18 of 2019. For statutory capital, shareholder loans, and salvage-award settlement, direct licensed-bank clearing is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions from the non-resident shareholder must be injected by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's own bank account into a designated USD capital account held by the Egyptian LLC at a Tier-1 licensed Egyptian bank. The remittance narrative must quote the GAFI investment file/certificate, identify the remitter as the registered shareholder, and state the purpose as 'foreign direct investment - equity subscription'. Inward equity remittances must match a contemporaneous capital increase registered in the Egyptian commercial registry; the receiving bank will issue a foreign-currency receipt and file the transaction with CBE's reporting system. Shareholder loans must be supported by an executed facility agreement, must be registered as an intra-group liability with the CBE/GAFI if required, and must be drawn by SWIFT MT103 using the loan purpose designation agreed with the receiving bank. No digital EMI or third-party wallet may be interposed in the statutory equity path, and no conversion is made before the bank confirms compliance eligibility.For operational disbursements (bunker suppliers, subcontracted tugs, P&I premiums, legal/advisory, port charges, and salvage-award payments to foreign arbitrators or secured parties), the primary clearing rail is an outward SWIFT MT103 from the Egyptian LLC's USD account, cross-referenced to the underlying contract and CBE maritime-transport purpose code. Bank-to-bank cover payments must use MT202 COV to avoid intermediary-name mismatch. For high-value capital disbursements, such as the purchase or long-term bareboat charter of a tug/salvage vessel, an irrevocable confirmed commercial documentary letter of credit is the preferred rail to protect the Egyptian buyer and the foreign seller. Every outward payment must be accompanied by the supplier invoice, service certificate, notarized contract, tax card, and, where required, the Egyptian General Department of Customs/Port Authority approval. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax applied to profit distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to its shareholders, including non-resident shareholders, unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable Double Taxation Agreement. The 10% is a final tax for non-resident shareholders unless treaty relief is granted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian TP documentation (Local File) is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds EGP 10 million in a fiscal year. The Master File must be prepared when the group’s consolidated annual revenues exceed EGP 100 million. Controlled transactions below EGP 10 million still require a self-sufficient transfer-pricing summary table in the corporate tax return.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute on the Gulf model. The ETA, however, applies the ‘economic reality / substance over form’ principle embedded in Article 89 of Law 91/2005 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) against artificial arrangements. For maritime towage and salvage, a corporate entity claiming free-zone or treaty benefits must demonstrate actual local substance: genuine decision-making at the board level, real presence of vessel crew, maintenance and operational management in Egypt, active registration of tugs under the Egyptian flag or in the SCZone maritime registry, and performance of non-resource economic activity from that location. An offshore shell that merely invoices a towage contract while operations are physically through a foreign tug and crew is treated as lacking substance and is likely to be disregarded under the GAAR; the income will be recharacterised as a taxable PE/source income in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion — Article 133 of Law 91/2005

Statutory sanction: Imposition of the full unpaid tax plus an administrative penalty of 20% of evaded tax. Criminal sanction includes imprisonment for a term not less than 3 months and not exceeding 5 years, and a fine not less than EGP 1,000 and not exceeding EGP 1,000,000, doubled on repeat. The ETA may also freeze the vessel, the corporate bank accounts, and the towage equipment pending the final tax court judgment.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners of the EG_LLC are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax if they signed knowingly false returns or diverted invoice proceeds. The ETA can obtain a presidential decree-appointed seizure order on their personal bank accounts and assets, impose a travel ban on the manager for any tax debt above EGP 10,000, and, on conviction, the responsible director faces actual imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Disregard

Statutory sanction: The ETA will disregard the corporate shell under GAAR and reattribute the salvage revenue to the actual ‘permanent establishment’ of the foreign operator. Additional penalties of 20% of the omitted tax, plus late fees of 2% per month up to 30%, are automatically applied. The corporate entity may be subject to administrative dissolution by the Egyptian General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI).

Director personal liability: Where the directors are the ultimate beneficial owners of the shell, the recharacterised taxable profit is collected personally from assets of the directors to the extent of the shell’s unpaid liabilities. The Corporate Law (Law 159/1981) enables piercing the corporate veil where the company is a façade; in severe cases, a court may impose a ban on forming another Egyptian company for 5 years and order repayment to the currency exchange authority for laundered salvage proceeds.
Money Laundering / False Beneficial Ownership (AML Law No. 80/2002)

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing Law imposes imprisonment between 6 months and 7 years and a fine up to EGP 1,000,000 for intentional concealment of beneficial ownership. All proceeds and towing equipment are subject to confiscation. The maritime operator’s licence may be suspended for 6 months by the Egyptian Ministry of Transport, and the corporate bank account may be closed under court order.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false BO declaration are criminally liable jointly with the entity. The court may order a personal fine equal to the amount laundered, up to the value of the towage contracts in question; a travel ban is imposed by the Anti-Money Laundering and Combating Terrorism Financing Unit (AMLU) pending investigation, and any director found personally liable for AML crimes is barred from board membership in Egyptian stock-exchange companies for 5 years.
