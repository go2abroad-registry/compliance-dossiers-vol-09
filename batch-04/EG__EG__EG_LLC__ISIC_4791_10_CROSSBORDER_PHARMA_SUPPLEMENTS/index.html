Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; official electronic portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (unified taxpayer registry, CIT/VAT filing, and e-invoicing/e-receipts fiscal clearance gateway).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC obtains its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and unified tax card by: (1) completing incorporation with the Commercial Registry (Al-Sijil Al-Tijari) and, where foreign shareholding applies, the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI); (2) submitting to the ETA the certificate of incorporation, certified articles of association, commercial registry extract, registered-office lease, and designation of the general manager or local tax representative; (3) applying separately for VAT registration under Law 67/2016 once annual taxable supplies reach or are reasonably expected to exceed EGP 500,000, with voluntary registration permitted below that threshold; (4) obtaining an electronic signature certificate and activating ETA e-portal credentials for CIT, VAT, withholding, and e-invoice transmission; and (5) for card-acquiring e-commerce merchants, presenting the payment-licence evidence to the ETA so that the taxpayer's profile is correctly flagged for e-invoicing and e-receipt obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC obtains its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and unified tax card by: (1) completing incorporation with the Commercial Registry (Al-Sijil Al-Tijari) and, where foreign shareholding applies, the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI); (2) submitting to the ETA the certificate of incorporation, certified articles of association, commercial registry extract, registered-office lease, and designation of the general manager or local tax representative; (3) applying separately for VAT registration under Law 67/2016 once annual taxable supplies reach or are reasonably expected to exceed EGP 500,000, with voluntary registration permitted below that threshold; (4) obtaining an electronic signature certificate and activating ETA e-portal credentials for CIT, VAT, withholding, and e-invoice transmission; and (5) for card-acquiring e-commerce merchants, presenting the payment-licence evidence to the ETA so that the taxpayer's profile is correctly flagged for e-invoicing and e-receipt obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Egyptian system does not provide check-the-box pass-through or hybrid-mismatch transparency elections; the LLC is an opaque resident corporation taxed under Law 91/2005 at the standard CIT rate of 22.5%. Available statutory elections include: (i) an accounting fiscal-year-end election requiring ETA pre-approval; (ii) the small- and medium-enterprise presumptive revenue-based tax regime under Law 6/2018 for eligible enterprises below the statutory turnover ceiling; (iii) the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) investment election under Law 83/2002, which permits qualifying export-oriented licensees to obtain a 0% CIT/privileged fiscal treatment certificate; and (iv) an accrual-basis versus cash-basis election for VAT may be available to small registered taxpayers under the ETA executive regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services constitutes the mandatory VAT registration trigger under Law 67/2016 and its executive regulations; registration is optional below this economic turnover threshold, and a non-resident supplier selling to Egyptian consumers may be required to appoint a fiscal representative where Egyptian-source supplies arise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to (i) dietary-supplement goods physically exported from Egypt and discharged under a definitive NAFEZA-automated customs export declaration; (ii) services rendered to non-resident customers for use wholly outside Egypt where the direct benefit is located abroad; and (iii) internationally related transport services, provided in each case that the consideration is paid in freely convertible foreign currency and the export proceeds are received in Egypt within the mandatory repatriation window. The exporter must evidence a genuine non-resident buyer, enforceable sales contract, actual cross-border movement, and proper e-invoicing with the 0% export code so that underlying input VAT on raw materials and fulfilment costs remains creditable and refundable from the ETA.

  • Customs Export Declaration (El-Bayane El-Goomrukee) signed by the licensed clearing agent and matched to the NAFEZA single-window release record
  • Commercial invoice (final/definitive) stating the foreign-currency export value and the 0% VAT code
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from Egyptian customs territory to the non-resident buyer
  • Signed purchase order or export contract reflecting terms of sale, Incoterms, and payment of convertible currency
  • Foreign-exchange inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds into an Egyptian bank account
  • Certificate of origin and, where applicable, Egyptian Drug Authority supplementary clearance records for the nutraceutical product

A compulsory real-time fiscal clearance regime applies: the LLC must issue all sales invoices in ETA-approved XML/JSON format, transmit them through the ETA e-invoicing platform via direct API or a licensed service provider, and obtain a unique digital UUID and QR-code stamping before the invoice becomes legally valid for input VAT deduction. E-receipts are separately mandated for point-of-sale and online B2C transactions. Invoices not cleared in real time are rejected by the system, attract statutory fines, and cannot sustain an input VAT credit.

The ETA reconciles zero-rated export claims against customs export declarations, bank FX records, and e-invoice clearances. Where export zero-rating is found to be unsupported, fictitious, or inflated, the ETA reverts the supply to the 14% standard VAT rate, disallows and claws back associated input VAT credits, and imposes the statutory default increment of 2% per month on the outstanding tax, together with civil penalty multipliers applicable to tax evasion. Proven fraudulent export invoicing or forged customs/FX documentation carries criminal exposure under the VAT Law, including fines linked to the evaded tax and imprisonment of the responsible directors or tax representatives, while the Central Bank of Egypt may additionally suspend export clearance privileges and initiate sanctions for unrepatriated foreign-exchange proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt applies current-account convertibility for trade, services and investment income under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194/2020, but foreign-currency flows must pass through authorized banks and be supported by underlying contracts, corporate documents and CBE declarations. Capital-account transactions such as equity injections, shareholder loans and intra-group financings are not freely convertible and must be documented for CBE/GAFI registration. Profit remittance to a foreign shareholder is lawful, but the remitting bank will require current audited financial statements, proof of dividend approval and payment of Egyptian corporate and withholding taxes, an inward remittance trail for the original foreign capital, and a bank-completed CBE transfer/undertaking form. The practical hurdles are not the formal exchange-control license but bank-level KYC, source-of-funds reconciliation and foreign-currency liquidity on the interbank market.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE exchange-control rules, export proceeds must be repatriated to a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank within 180 calendar days from the date of shipment, normally evidenced by bill of lading/airwaybill. The proceeds must be credited to the exporter's foreign-currency account and the exporter must execute the standard export-proceeds surrender undertaking. The default mandatory conversion ratio is 100% of export proceeds into EGP at the bank's prevailing interbank rate, subject to any CBE-approved foreign-currency retention quota, which in current practice may allow up to 50% of proceeds to be retained for verified import payments for the LLC's own trade operations. A new LLC without an approved retention facility should plan on 100% surrender and conversion. Non-compliance may trigger CBE fines, denial of trade facilities and referral to the Egyptian AML/authorization enforcement regime.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for an offshore EMI/neobank as the primary treasury rail; MEDIUM only as an ancillary offshore collection node if all funds ultimately land in a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Paysera or white-label neobanks cannot lawfully hold EGP, clear EGP, or operate as local banks in Egypt unless licensed by the CBE under Law 194/2020 and the payment system regulation. Using such EMIs as the principal account creates a KYC/AML blocker because the bank cannot verify the ultimate source of funds and the exchange control repatriation route is broken. A compliant structure is to use CBE-licensed fintech acquirers or PSPs in Egypt, such as Paymob or Fawry for local card acquiring, with daily settlement into the onshore NBE or Banque Misr operational account.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual self-assessment return must be filed within four months after the close of the corporate fiscal year; final CIT payment falls due on the filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the aggregate annual value of related-party transactions exceeds EGP 10 million; Master File is required where the Egyptian entity belongs to a group with consolidated global turnover above EGP 3 billion or related-party transactions above EGP 300 million; CbC reporting threshold aligns with OECD EUR 750 million consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation. Nonetheless, the ETA applies the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR introduced by Law No. 211/2020), substance-over-form jurisprudence, beneficial-ownership requirements under tax treaties, and Central Bank AML licensing/KYC standards. A letterbox LLC lacking premises, employees, and operational control in Egypt will be re-characterized as a dummy vehicle and may lose treaty, free zone, and investment-law incentives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years, criminal fine of 25%–100% of the evaded tax, confiscation of goods and funds, full recovery of unpaid tax plus the monthly 1% delay surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and may be subject to criminal prosecution, travel bans, asset attachment, and disqualification from board service; the LLC veil is pierced where the director is the actual controller of the evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Retroactive denial of Investment Law 72/2017 tax holidays and free-zone exemptions; re-characterization of the entity as non-existent for tax purposes with reassessment and penalties; deregistration of the company; fine for false declarations up to EGP 100,000.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for unpaid tax and third-party e-commerce refunds; non-resident directors may be banned from entering Egypt and subject to contempt orders until final judgment is satisfied.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: TP audit adjustment increasing Egyptian taxable income, plus a monthly 1% delay surcharge on the additional tax; an additional fine of up to 100% of the tax difference for fraudulent documentation; ETA may impose a penalty of 5% of the value of undocumented controlled transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve TP policies can be named as jointly liable for the TP adjustment; ETA referral to the Public Prosecutor may lead to criminal sanctions for intentional under-declaration and personal seizure of offshore assets.
Money Laundering / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002, imprisonment up to 7 years, fine ranging between EGP 10,000 and EGP 1,000,000 per offense, freezing of Egyptian bank and merchant accounts, revocation of the commercial license, and confiscation of assets linked to laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally prosecutable; additional penalties include deportation (if non-resident), travel bans, direct personal attachment of funds, and being barred from serving in any Egyptian commercial entity.
