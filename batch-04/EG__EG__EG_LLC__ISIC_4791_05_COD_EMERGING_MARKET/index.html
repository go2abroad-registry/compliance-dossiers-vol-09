Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an LLC under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; founders freely determine the authorized capital in the Memorandum of Association. The subscribed capital must be fully paid within 30 days of commercial registration and evidenced by a bank payment receipt or certified fund clearance certificate. No compulsory escrow account or notarized capital deposit applies to a non-banking mail-order e-commerce LLC.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire Safety Clearance (Shahadat Al-Waqaya Al-Madaniya) from the Civil Protection Department, Ministry of Interior, must be obtained for storage areas exceeding 200 m2, including approved fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and battery/charging station fire controls. Municipal engineering endorsement confirming structural load-bearing capacity of slab and racking configuration is required. The workplace must be registered with the Ministry of Manpower under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003, with an occupational health and safety plan and labor office notifications completed for all staff before operational commencement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) operating under the Ministry of Finance; primary taxpayer portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains its Tax Identification Number (TIN) by first registering with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the competent commercial registry, then filing the electronic Tax Registration Form (Form 1) on the ETA portal accompanied by certified articles of association, foreign incorporation certificate, board resolution appointing a local tax representative, proof of registered office address, and passport copies of managers. Upon approval, the ETA issues the Tax Card (Wathiqat al-Darb) containing the 9-digit TIN. If taxable turnover exceeds the VAT threshold, the LLC must separately register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 and obtain a VAT certificate; non-resident e-commerce sellers making taxable supplies through local platforms are required to appoint a resident VAT representative before commencing sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Egyptian tax law. The LLC is statutorily taxed as a separate corporate entity under Unified Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, and no flow-through status is available. Investment incentives under Law No. 72 of 2017 allow eligible entities to elect between a 10-year fixed rate regime or a 50% investment cost deduction; companies located in the Suez Canal Economic Zone may negotiate special SEZ tax treatment with the competent authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services; voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 10(1) of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 provided the goods physically leave Egypt within 90 days of supply and the exporter retains transport documentation. Offshore services are zero-rated only where entirely used outside Egypt and rendered to a non-resident; the benefit-of-the-place-of-use test must be evidenced. Cash-on-delivery cross-border e-commerce sellers must substantiate last-mile delivery outside Egypt through logistics records and trace the payment received via approved payment service providers to the specific export invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) stamped by Egyptian Customs confirming the goods left the country
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or road consignment note evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian bank, or a payment service provider trace report, showing receipt of export proceeds attributable to the declared invoices

Mandatory e-invoicing applies to all VAT-registered businesses pursuant to ETA Decision No. 161 of 2020 as progressively rolled out and mandatory since 2022. Registrants must transmit invoices through the ETA-integrated electronic invoicing platform either via direct API or approved integrator using the ETA-prescribed REST/JSON/XML schema, digitally sign each invoice with an ETA-issued certificate, and print/embed an ETA-generated QR code in the GS1 standard. B2C operators must additionally implement the e-receipt system. Invoices not cleared through this real-time fiscal process are stripped of input VAT deductibility and expose the seller to non-compliance penalties.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict ETA documentary audit and digital cross-matching of e-invoices, customs declarations, and bank remittance data. Any misstatement or fraudulent export claim triggers retrospective clawback of all input VAT credited, late payment interest penalties, and fines under Article 73 of the VAT Law of twice the tax evaded plus possible imprisonment up to three years; under Law No. 91 of 2005 tax evasion penalties include fines equal to the deficient corporate tax and up to five years' imprisonment for repeat offenders. False customs declarations additionally attract confiscation or penalties under the Customs Law, and failure to receive export proceeds within the stipulated repatriation window may result in loss of input credits, exchange-control sanctions, and audit-triggered investigation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed float exchange rate regime. Current account transactions are generally free and convertible through authorized banks, while capital account transactions are subject to CBE approval. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted provided the company has proper audited financial statements, tax clearance, and underlying transaction evidence. Cash-on-delivery (COD) businesses must document the entire order-to-cash cycle to support the validity of remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt within 5 months from the date of shipment. A minimum of 100% of the export invoice value must be surrendered into an authorized bank in Egypt. The exporter may retain foreign currency earnings in a CBE-permitted account only if future payments are contractually justified; otherwise, mandatory conversion to Egyptian pounds applies.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with binding legal constraints. Egyptian law requires any entity providing payment services or collecting cash on behalf of merchants to be licensed as a payment service provider by the CBE. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Airwallex) cannot operate standalone for COD settlement inside Egypt without a CBE license or a formal local partnership with a licensed bank or payment aggregator. The COD model requires on-ground cash collection via CBE-licensed logistics or banking partners. Offshore EMIs are viable only for inbound international payment acceptance or settlement of supplier invoices, not for local COD cash collection.

22.5% flat corporate income tax on commercial and industrial profits; a temporary 5% additional levy applies to the portion of net profit exceeding EGP 100 million, raising the top marginal rate to 27.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends and profit distributions from an Egyptian LLC to non-resident members. Distributions to resident Egyptian corporate shareholders are exempt under the participation exemption; resident individuals are also subject to the 10% final WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within 4 months after the close of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year LLC, the deadline is 30 April of the following year. Advance CIT installments fall due in quarterly windows set by the ETA against the prior year's tax liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million aggregate annual value of controlled transactions triggers the mandatory Related-Party Disclosure Form attached to Form 19 and obliges the LLC to prepare and submit a Local File and, where the group-wide consolidated revenue threshold is met, a Master File within 60 days of an ETA audit notice. Category-specific scrutiny applies to financing transactions (EGP 10 million), goods flows (EGP 15 million), and services or intangibles (EGP 5-10 million).

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but economic substance is enforced through: (i) the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Law No. 91/2005, which empowers the ETA to disregard transactions lacking commercial rationale; (ii) Law No. 83/2020 requiring Egyptian companies to maintain an auditable beneficial-ownership register with real local management, office premises, payroll, and banking substance; (iii) Central Bank of Egypt AML/KYC rules conditioning account operation on verified physical existence and local decision-making; (iv) the place-of-effective-management test used to determine Egyptian tax residency. A letterbox LLC with foreign decision-makers and no local substance will fail deductibility, treaty claims, and VAT/GST registrations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealed COD Cash Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under Articles 133-134 of Law No. 91/2005: imprisonment for 3-5 years and fines of EGP 20,000-500,000; mandatory civil restitution of the evaded tax; delay surcharge of 2% per month (capped at 100% of the tax due); asset freeze, precautionary seizure of bank accounts, and travel ban issued by the economic court pending criminal trial.

Director personal liability: Directors, the general manager, and any signatory of the false return are personally and jointly liable for the evaded tax and unpaid surcharges. Criminal conviction leads to automatic travel bans, disqualification from managing any Egyptian company, and potential imprisonment of the controlling person.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA application of GAAR to re-characterize and re-allocate the full revenue and profit to the Egyptian entity; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; revocation of the ETA tax card; de-registration and striking-off under Companies Law No. 159/2020; Central Bank of Egypt mandate to terminate the entity's bank accounts for failure to update the beneficial-ownership register under Law No. 83/2020.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly liable for all corporate withholding taxes and unpaid CIT where the company is deemed a sham; courts may pierce the veil and order personal contribution to the corporate tax debt; directors face a direct ban on new commercial registrations and travel bans for the entire audit period.
AML / CTF Breach in Cash-Handling Operations

Statutory sanction: Law No. 80/2002 as amended by Law No. 154/2019: imprisonment of 3-7 years and fines up to EGP 1,000,000; confiscation of the laundered funds, goods, and delivery vehicles; suspension of the commercial registration and banking facilities; placement on the CBE watchlist.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior compliance officers are personally prosecutable for failure to implement AML controls; personal assets and accounts are attachable in asset-recovery actions; a conviction carries a mandatory travel ban and a 10-year prohibition on holding any financial or management position in Egypt.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer-pricing adjustment to arm's-length results with re-computation of the tax due; delay surcharge of 2% per month (capped at 100%); administrative fines up to EGP 100,000 for the missing disclosure form; a 40% penalty on the under-declared tax difference in cases of gross or repeated misstatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the inter-company agreements and TP disclosures are personally liable for the penalty additions if they knowingly certified false pricing; civil restitution orders may attach personal bank accounts; in aggravated cases, criminal charges of tax evasion may be joined.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: The payer LLC is the primary debtor for the unremitted WHT; collection enforced with the 2% monthly delay surcharge (capped at 100%); deliberate failure constitutes tax evasion and triggers criminal imprisonment of 3-5 years and fines of EGP 20,000-500,000; the ETA may attach the LLC's Egyptian bank accounts for the full withheld amount.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, chairman, and general manager are statutorily jointly and severally liable for all WHT amounts the company failed to withhold and remit; the ETA routinely issues travel bans and personal asset freezes against directors in withholding-default cases.
