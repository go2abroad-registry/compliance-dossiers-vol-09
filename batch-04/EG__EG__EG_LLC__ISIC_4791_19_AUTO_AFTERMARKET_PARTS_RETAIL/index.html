Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Law No. 83 of 2022 for LLCs; however, the share capital must be expressly stated in the Articles of Association and be sufficient to achieve the objects of the company. Monetary contributions must be deposited into a temporary blocked bank account at a licensed Egyptian bank prior to registration. The bank retains the funds until GAFI issues the Certificate of Incorporation, after which the capital is released to the company’s active bank account in the name of the LLC.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; centralized e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoice/receipt clearance platform operated by ETA for all VAT registrants.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) Appoint an ETA-recognized tax representative if no local manager is resident; (2) file registration application for the Tax Card (Bitaqa Daribiya) and VAT certificate via ETA e-services or in person at the competent tax office, attaching certified constitutional documents, POA, lease or proof of premises, board resolutions, and ID copies of shareholders/managers; (3) await ETA inspection and issuance of the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number, generally within 5–15 working days; (4) activation of e-invoicing onboarding and PIN credentials is mandatory at registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable: Egypt applies entity-level taxation and has no US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election mechanism. Available statutory elections include: (i) consolidated corporate income tax filing for a parent holding at least 100% of one or more wholly-owned Egyptian subsidiaries; (ii) election to carry forward tax losses for up to 5 years; (iii) election to apply the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZONE)/Free Zone preferential exemption regime for qualifying export-oriented licensed activities; (iv) option for quarterly VAT settlement for small taxpayers below the mandatory monthly frequency; (v) election to document transfer pricing via local/master file set for advance certainty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual turnover of taxable goods or services; mandatory registration applies from the first day of taxable activity once the threshold is met or exceeded. Exceptional regime: dealers in scheduled luxury goods (including certain automobiles) and non-resident suppliers of electronic services may be required to register irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) for the exported automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories applies if: (i) the buyer is a non-resident and the goods are physically exported outside Egypt within 90 days of the supply date; (ii) there is a direct contractual link between the Egyptian LLC and the foreign buyer; (iii) invoices are issued in foreign currency; (iv) export proceeds are received in Egypt and surrendered through an Egyptian bank per Central Bank of Egypt instructions; (v) the taxpayer holds an approved customs export declaration. Ancillary services provided to non-resident clients (e.g., tuning configuration, after-sales remote support) also qualify for 0% if performed offshore, benefited outside Egypt, and fully documented; services consumed in Egypt are subject to 14% VAT.

Mandatory full electronic invoicing and electronic receipts integrated with the ETA central clearance platform. Invoices and receipts must be transmitted in near-real time via ETA-approved APIs using JSON/XML through an ETA Mapper or certified ERP connector, or through the ETA web portal. Each submitted invoice is validated, assigned a unique ID, UUID, and cryptographic QR/stamp, and cleared before the supply can be recorded; non-compliant invoices are rejected automatically. There is no separate physical stamping; B2C retail (including online auto parts sales) is subject to the e-receipt mandate once the registrant is onboarded by ETA wave.

ETA audits apply enhanced scrutiny to export zero-rating claims; failure to substantiate physical export or average FX repatriation within the CBE deadline results in full clawback of the 14% VAT plus monthly delay penalty of 2% (or fraction) capped at 100% of the assessed tax. Fraudulent claims carry a criminal fine of EGP 10,000–50,000, possible imprisonment under VAT Law No. 67/2016, and a 10-year assessment window for falsified documentation. Customs violations on export declarations may trigger separate customs penalties and bank disqualification for repeat offenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-flexible exchange rate regime implemented through the CBE interbank system. Current account transactions (imports of automotive spare parts, supplier payments, logistics service fees, platform commissions, dividends on ordinary trade, and after-tax profit remittances to non-resident shareholders) are in principle free of prior individual CBE licensing, but must be executed through a CBE-licensed bank with correct statement purpose codes and valid underlying documentation. Capital account transactions (share capital injection, shareholder loans, equity disinvestment, loan repayments not arising from trade) remain administratively controlled by the CBE and are banked through the CBE's electronic foreign exchange registration and verification procedures for foreign direct investment. Practical convertibility hurdles include: prioritization of hard-currency allocation to essential goods during FX shortages; time drag on FX liquidity during acute external deficits; mandatory documentation of goods receipts and customs declarations (ACI/ACID) before trade-related FX conversion; and the requirement for dividend remittances to be supported by audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), and a certificate from the company's bank confirming the legality of the underlying profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign exchange circulars applicable to Egyptian exporters, 100% of export proceeds for goods shipped from Egypt must be repatriated to a local bank account in Egypt within 5 months from the date of the bill of lading (airway bill for e-commerce parcels), extendable for up to another 5 months only with the prior authorization of the exporter's bank and on demonstrable commercial grounds. Receipts generated through Stripe, PayPal, and other payment gateways for online sales to non-resident buyers must be swept into an Egyptian bank account in the same foreign currency or converted to EGP through the banking system within the same statutory window. The exporter must execute a declaration to the bank covering the value of shipments and the expected currency of proceeds; untimely repatriation triggers bank-level blocking of future export finance, escalation to the CBE, and administrative penalties under Law No. 194 of 2020. Proceeds not offset by verified import payments for the exporter's own business must be sold to the banking system when the CBE imposes surrender ratios; as of the current regime, the statutory framework requires full repatriation but permits hold-back in foreign currency accounts subject to CBE instructions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE; Arab Republic of Egypt), operating principally under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), with exclusive authority over monetary policy, foreign exchange regulation and administration, bank licensing and supervision, payment and settlement systems, and the enforcement of capital movement and anti-money laundering rules affecting Egyptian corporate accounts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW for the Egyptian legal entity acting as principal treasury principal. Offshore digital EMIs (Stripe Treasury, Payoneer, Airwallex, Wise Business) cannot operate as the sole clearing and settlement bank for an Egyptian-incorporated exporter because the CBE mandates that all domestic receipt, conversion, and outward payment operations for foreign exchange be conducted through licensed banks in Egypt under Law No. 194 of 2020. Such EMIs are legally viable only as auxiliary collection concentrators for inbound e-commerce receivables (Stripe/PayPal/razorpay), with automated daily or weekly sweeps by SWIFT transfer to an Egyptian bank, or as treasury vehicles for a separate offshore sales affiliate. Structural constraints: EMIs have no Egyptian banking license, cannot hold EGP settlement accounts, cannot issue Egyptian import letters of credit, and cannot authenticate ACI customs declarations; product outages and FX conversion spreads may exceed bank rails; therefore the corporate treasury must not rely solely on EMI rails for liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital for EG_LLC must arrive by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the foreign investor's bank to the Egyptian bank, using the corporate account name and the unique company Commercial Registry number in the beneficiary line. The remittance should cite the CBE purpose code covering foreign direct investment in a limited liability company (capital subscription), reference the GAFI investment certificate issued under Law No. 72 of 2017, and be supported by the Egyptian bank's inward remittance declaration. The bank will verify the corporate registration, the UBO resolution, and the legality of the funds; funds are typically credited to a USD or EUR capital escrow account and then converted to EGP available for operations only after CBE registration confirmation is obtained. For shareholder loans, the routing file must contain an executed loan agreement, an interest schedule at arm's-length rates, CBE loan notification, and the borrower's tax identification number; the loan must be reported to the CBE and will be subject to the 5% withholding tax on interest payments at disbursement of each coupon.Outbound payments for auto-parts inventory (thermal components, filters, bearings, brake discs, electronics, tuning accessories) from suppliers in China, Germany, UAE, and Turkey will be routed over two primary rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 clean payments for low-value, low-risk repeat orders up to the CBE/bank-set trade threshold, provided the supplier is not on a restricted list and the import is matched to a valid ACI/ACID declaration in the NAFEZA single-window system; and (2) documentary letters of credit (commercial LC) issued via SWIFT MT700 for higher-value purchase orders, with the Egyptian bank acting as confirming or advising bank through a corresponding offshore bank. For both rails, the company must maintain an importer registration card issued by the General Organization for Export and Import Control (GOEIC), the supplier commercial invoice, packing list, ocean/airway bill, certificate of origin, and the ACI cargo tracking number. The local bank will apply the current CBE foreign exchange formalities, verify that the outbound payment amount matches the customs declaration, and will reject payments where the importer's name, ACID, or product HS code is inconsistent; this verification must be completed before FX conversion to hard currency.Deliverable over-the-counter (OTC) FX forward contracts (EGP against USD, EUR, and GBP) executed with a CBE-licensed Egyptian commercial bank, coverable strictly against underlying trade receivables and supplier payables, with maximum tenor aligned to the statutory 5-month export proceeds window or the trade LC maturity, where the forward is non-speculative and fully documented.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) structures on EGP are permissible only when documented through an offshore non-resident bank counterparty outside Egypt; Egyptian-resident entities may not book speculative NDFs locally, and the arrangement requires a back-to-back underlying trade exposure, CBE-reportable documentation, and board-approval memorandum setting out the hedging rationale.Cross-currency swap contracts (USD/EGP or EUR/EGP) permitted for longer-term treasury management of intercompany debt and shareholder loans, executed with a licensed Egyptian bank or an approved international counterparty, subject to CBE maturity-mismatch notification and the company's internal derivatives policy; the swap must be designated as a cash-flow hedge under IFRS 9 with hedge documentation maintained for tax and CBE compliance.Sight and usance letters of credit with flexible currency netting and banker's acceptances, used as an implicit hedging and financing rail by fixing the FX rate at LC issuance for the future payment date.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: LOW for the Egyptian legal entity acting as principal treasury principal. Offshore digital EMIs (Stripe Treasury, Payoneer, Airwallex, Wise Business) cannot operate as the sole clearing and settlement bank for an Egyptian-incorporated exporter because the CBE mandates that all domestic receipt, conversion, and outward payment operations for foreign exchange be conducted through licensed banks in Egypt under Law No. 194 of 2020. Such EMIs are legally viable only as auxiliary collection concentrators for inbound e-commerce receivables (Stripe/PayPal/razorpay), with automated daily or weekly sweeps by SWIFT transfer to an Egyptian bank, or as treasury vehicles for a separate offshore sales affiliate. Structural constraints: EMIs have no Egyptian banking license, cannot hold EGP settlement accounts, cannot issue Egyptian import letters of credit, and cannot authenticate ACI customs declarations; product outages and FX conversion spreads may exceed bank rails; therefore the corporate treasury must not rely solely on EMI rails for liquidity.
