Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official portal: www.eta.gov.eg; electronic services platform: https://egypttax.gov.eg (including e-invoicing and e-receipts portals)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must register with the Egyptian Tax Authority upon commencement of business or upon exceeding VAT registration thresholds. Procedure: (1) obtain Commercial Registry (Ministry of Trade and Industry) certificate and investment license, (2) submit the tax registration application via the ETA e-portal or in-person at the competent tax office, (3) file Charter/Articles of Association, certificate of incorporation, lease or ownership proof of business premises, board resolution appointing the tax representative, and passport/commercial registration copies of non-resident shareholders, (4) obtain the Tax Card (Faith Tax Card) recording the Consolidated Tax Number (TIN), which also serves as the VAT identification number, (5) register for payroll tax (monthly income tax withholding), stamp duty, and WHT registration with the same TIN. The LLC may also obtain a separate customs number (I/C code) from the Egyptian Customs Authority for import/export activity. Registration must be completed within 30 days of incorporation or before commencing taxable activities.

Procedure: (1) obtain Commercial Registry (Ministry of Trade and Industry) certificate and investment license, (2) submit the tax registration application via the ETA e-portal or in-person at the competent tax office, (3) file Charter/Articles of Association, certificate of incorporation, lease or ownership proof of business premises, board resolution appointing the tax representative, and passport/commercial registration copies of non-resident shareholders, (4) obtain the Tax Card (Faith Tax Card) recording the Consolidated Tax Number (TIN), which also serves as the VAT identification number, (5) register for payroll tax (monthly income tax withholding), stamp duty, and WHT registration with the same TIN. Registration must be completed within 30 days of incorporation or before commencing taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Egyptian law; an Egyptian LLC is mandatorily taxed at the entity level as a juridical person under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005. Profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to withholding tax at source. The only available administrative elections relate to the simplified presumptive tax regime for small businesses (turnover below EGP 10 million, with an irrevocable election for a 3-year period), cash-basis election for certain taxpayers, and tax treaty relief by advance application. Egyptian transfer-pricing rules incorporate hybrid mismatch concepts only via the OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) minimum standards; no domestic check-the-box hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services (mandatory registration); EGP 90,000 threshold for partial registration of exempt persons; voluntary registration permitted below threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated under Article 30 of Law 67/2016 where the goods are transported out of Egypt or the services are performed and consumed wholly outside Egypt, including transport, insurance, and directly related services for exported goods. A revenue-based financing fund providing cross-border royalty or advisory services may claim zero-rating only upon documentary proof that the recipient is a non-resident, the service is not used by an Egyptian establishment, and the fee flows from outside Egypt. Intra-group offshore services with no Egyptian consumption also qualify if the supply qualifies as an 'exported service' under ETA guidance.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Bill of Exit (Electronic Customs Clearance System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign currency bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency
  • Signed export sale contract or proforma invoice
  • ETA e-invoice with zero-rated supply code
  • Copy of the registered export registry (export file) maintained by the taxpayer
  • Proof of deconsolidation of goods at the port of final destination (for re-export scenarios)

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt regime administered by the ETA. Taxpayers above the specified turnover threshold (phased implementation; currently mandatory for entities with annual turnover above EGP 50 million) must issue invoices and receipts exclusively through ETA-approved electronic channels. The ETA operates a real-time clearance model (API-based integration with taxpayer ERP systems, SOAP/REST, and fiscal device digital stamping). Each invoice receives an internal registration number and unique QR 2D barcode; documents must be reported to the ETA e-invoicing platform within 24 hours of issuance. Fiscal stamping of cash registers (e-receipts) is mandatory in the retail sector. Non-compliance triggers suspension of VAT refunds and blockage of the taxpayer's digital fiscal identity.

The ETA may retroactively claw back zero-rated treatment and assess full 14% VAT plus late payment penalties (currently up to 2% per month on the tax due) if export documentation is deficient, goods are diverted to domestic consumption, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) 180-day deadline. False zero-rating claims constitute VAT evasion under Law 67/2016 Articles 51-52, punishable by a fine of no less than 100% of the tax due and up to 1000% in case of fraudulent documentation, plus imprisonment of both the legal representative and the foreign entity's local tax agent. The ETA also applies an automatic 1.5x assessment multiplier and criminal referral where fake export receipts or forged inward remittance certificates are detected. For services, the ETA can recharacterize an offshore service as Egyptian-source and disallow the zero-rating if the local fund performs management or financing functions in Egypt or invoices in EGP to a domestic entity. Export VAT refunds are subject to pre-refund audit; the refund may be suspended until the CBE confirms FX repatriation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt accepted IMF Article VIII (full current-account convertibility) in 1991. The capital account is comparably open: foreign investors registered with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 enjoy unrestricted repatriation of capital, dividends, and loan proceeds through CBE-licensed banks. Following the March 2024 currency-unification decision, the CBE removed the previous prior-approval queues, LC-only import rule, and FX margin caps; banks now execute conversions for any declared underlying transaction, subject to document-based verification. Practical hurdles for profit remittance are limited to (i) submission of audited financial statements and tax-clearance certificates, and (ii) momentary intra-day EGP liquidity tightness, which does not normally trigger a denial.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds. The former surrender regime rooted in Law No. 38 of 1977 was abolished in the 2003 liberalization package and was not revived by the 2020 banking law. Corporate exporters may retain 100% of export and royalty receipts in onshore foreign-currency accounts or convert them at will; the only foundational requirements are declaration to the receiving bank using the CBE/BPM-6 purpose codes and retention of the underlying contract for five years for the bank's audit trail.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange administrator, exercising statutory powers under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020. The CBE licenses all onshore dealer banks, operates the interbank FX market, sets the EGP fixing methodology, and supervises payment infrastructure including RTGS, ACH, and the InstaPay instant-payment network.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM. A regulated offshore digital EMI is viable only as a first-mile collection originator, not as a substitute for the onshore bank account. A foreign EMI must be duly licensed in its home jurisdiction (e.g., FCA-authorized UK EMI or EU e-money institution) and may collect ACH and direct-debit payments from digital-brand merchants, subsequently wiring the monthly aggregate to the Egyptian LLC via SWIFT MT103. However, the CBE does not recognize offshore EMIs for the purpose of statutory corporate settlement accounts, EGP clearing, tax evidence, or FX hedging; the LLC must maintain its treasury core with a CBE-licensed bank and ensure that all contractual documentation expressly disclaims deposit-taking activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant sequence: (1) the foreign investor remits a SWIFT MT103 from its bank to the LLC's USD Capital Escrow Account at the chosen CBE-licensed bank, quoting the BPM-6 direct-investment purpose code for share capital or equity transactions, with Field 70 references containing the GAFI registration number, the current commercial registration extract, and the board resolution increasing capital; (2) the receiving bank verifies the GAFI no-objection letter and the CBE Capital Declaration, then posts the account; (3) treasury converts only the EGP-denominated liquidity tranche through the interbank market, preserving the USD balance for the royalty fund's capital base; (4) the investor's shareholding and the transferred amount are recorded in the Commercial Registry within the statutory filing period and added to the capital-base evidence monitored by the bank.EGP-denominated obligations, including royalty payments to Egyptian digital brands and local operating costs, are cleared via CBE RTGS for high-value payments and the InstaPay instant-payment network for micro-settlements, both keyed off the national 29-character IBAN; repeated low-value collections are executed through Direct-Debit ACH mandates under standard clearing rules. Cross-border USD payments to investors, foreign suppliers, and IP royalties are cleared via SWIFT MT103 through the correspondent banking chain, with MT202 COV or MT202 used for bank-to-bank treasury positioning; import-related settlements by clients are structured as irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP600 opened by the local bank. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on distributions of profits to all shareholders (Article 56, Law No. 91/2005). The withholding is a final tax for resident and non-resident individuals and is creditable or refundable for corporate shareholders according to their domestic law.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within 4 months from the fiscal year-end. Default fiscal year is the calendar year (deadline: 30 April); a different fiscal year is permitted only with prior ETA approval, and the 4-month rule applies relative to that year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where annual controlled transactions with related parties exceed EGP 8,000,000. Master File: mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 200,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: mandatory where group consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 15,000,000,000, subject to the Egyptian Tax Authority notification deadlines.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute, but economic substance is enforced through four horizontal pillars: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Art. 106, Law No. 91/2005) allows the ETA to disregard transactions or arrangements lacking a valid commercial purpose or business substance; (ii) the Egyptian TP Guidelines require a complete functional, asset, and risk (FAR) analysis such that a royalty/financing fund LLC without employees, premises, and local decision-making in Egypt is treated as a pure conduit; (iii) the beneficial ownership doctrine under DTAs denies treaty relief on payments to bare-shell recipients; (iv) the AML Law No. 80/2002 obliges identification of the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) of the fund. An Egyptian LLC acting solely as a pass-through financing vehicle will therefore be recharacterized and taxed on an arm's length basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 133 of Law No. 91/2005: imprisonment of 2 to 5 years and a fine equal to 100% to 200% of the tax evaded; additional assessment, delay interest at 2% per month (capped at 100%), taxpayer blacklisting, and forced collection by bank attachment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the erroneous return are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, delay surcharges, and fines. They face criminal imprisonment, civil restitution from personal assets, and an administrative travel ban.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The ETA denies treaty relief and applies the full statutory WHT (20% on royalties and interest); the arrangement is recharacterized under the GAAR (Art. 106), thin-capitalization rules reclassify the debt, and fines and delay interest are imposed on the entire adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and authorized signatories are exposed to civil restitution, travel bans, and personal liability for uncollected withholding tax. The foreign ultimate beneficial owner may be deemed resident in Egypt and assessed personally for the avoided corporate tax.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of up to EGP 100,000; the return is treated as incomplete; the ETA makes a mandatory upward adjustment to the arm's length price, with delay interest at 2% per month up to 100% of the adjustment.

Director personal liability: The signing director is personally liable for the transfer pricing adjustment, penalties, and surcharges. In cases of intentional mispricing, criminal tax evasion charges and a travel ban apply to the responsible officers.
Withholding Tax Non-Remittance

Statutory sanction: Immediate demand for the unremitted WHT plus delay interest at 2% per month (capped at 100%); administrative fine of up to EGP 20,000; enforcement by attachment of bank accounts; refusal to issue the company's tax clearance certificate.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted withholding tax, and statutory collection tools include seizure of personal assets and a travel ban against the responsible directors.
AML and Funds Flow Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Egyptian AML Law No. 80/2002 (as amended): imprisonment of 2 to 7 years and a fine of EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000; freezing of accounts; administrative sanctions including license revocation or closure of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers knowingly involved in money laundering are criminally liable, subject to civil confiscation of laundered assets, and face international and domestic travel bans.
