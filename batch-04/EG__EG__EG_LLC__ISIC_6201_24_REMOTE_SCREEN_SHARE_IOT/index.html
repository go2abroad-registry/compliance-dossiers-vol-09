Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital for an LLC. Shareholders set the capital in the memorandum. Any cash contribution above zero must be deposited into a blocked temporary escrow account in the company's name and released after the incorporation certificate is issued; in-kind contributions require certified valuation. No escrow deposit is mandatory when the LLC is formed with zero or symbolic capital.

Funding & unlock path: For the registered office and engineering/back-office facilities, obtain Civil Defense fire-safety approval for the premises, a structural conformity letter from the building owner/engineer where required, and an occupational health and safety clearance from the Ministry of Manpower. No environmental permit is required where no physical server or manufacturing floor exists.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; online e-services portal at www.eta.gov.eg and e-invoicing platform at invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign LLC that acquires Egyptian tax nexus must apply for VAT registration and a consolidated Tax Card (TIN) through the ETA electronic registration workflow. The applicant submits certified incorporation documents, board resolution, power of attorney, local fiscal representative appointment (where required), bank account evidence, and contact details. ETA validates the file and issues the national TIN/VAT ID and Tax Card.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign LLC that acquires Egyptian tax nexus must apply for VAT registration and a consolidated Tax Card (TIN) through the ETA electronic registration workflow. The applicant submits certified incorporation documents, board resolution, power of attorney, local fiscal representative appointment (where required), bank account evidence, and contact details. ETA validates the file and issues the national TIN/VAT ID and Tax Card.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not permit check-the-box or hybrid pass-through elections. An LLC is mandatorily taxed as a resident juridical entity under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005. The only relevant elections are a non-calendar fiscal year approved by ETA and voluntary VAT registration for suppliers below the compulsory registration threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services in any 12-month period; imports of taxable goods or services require registration regardless of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods leaving Egypt are zero-rated upon proof of physical export. Services, including remote desktop access, screen sharing, and IoT telemetry software, are zero-rated when rendered to a non-resident recipient and the benefit is consumed entirely outside Egypt. Substantiation must demonstrate the non-resident status of the recipient, the foreign place of use, and the receipt of foreign currency consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • For pure remote software/IoT services: ETA-approved zero-rated e-invoice, executed subscription agreement, server logs proving usage outside Egypt, and bank SWIFT credit advice showing inward remittance.

Egypt mandates electronic invoicing under Law No. 206 of 2020. Registered VAT taxpayers must transmit invoice data in real-time via the ETA Enterprise Integration Platform (EIP) using approved APIs; ETA validates the data and returns a unique QR code / invoice clearance ID before the supplier can present the invoice to the buyer. No physical stamping is required, but complete XML/JSON audit files must be retained.

ETA may audit zero-rating claims and refund packets. Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT assessment plus late-payment interest, administrative fines under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, and criminal sanctions for deliberate tax evasion or forgery. Refund claims remain subject to clawback for the statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed float regime for the EGP. Current account transactions are generally free, but capital account movements require CBE approval via authorized banks. Profit remittances are permitted after settlement of corporate tax liabilities and submission of audited financial statements; however, banks apply enhanced scrutiny to large outward transfers, and access to hard currency can be rationed during periods of acute liquidity stress. Non-resident LLC shareholders may remit dividends net of the 10% distribution tax, subject to documentary proof of the underlying investment via the approved bank. The CBE has progressively relaxed surrender requirements but retains oversight over conversion timing to stabilize reserves.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters must receive proceeds through an authorized bank and deposit into a foreign currency account within 30 days of the shipment date. Prior to the 2024 liberalization, 100% of export proceeds had to be surrendered to the banking system within 90 days; current regulations require conversion of at least 50% of the proceeds to EGP within 30 days, with the remainder retainable for trade-related current transactions, subject to CBE circulars. Software and digital service exports are treated as export receipts and must follow the same timelines, with invoicing supported by service contracts and customs or e-export documentation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA or EU-licensed e-money institutions) cannot be used as the primary operating bank for the Egyptian LLC. The CBE does not recognize foreign EMIs for domestic payroll, tax payments, or FX conversions. They may only serve as ancillary payment gateways for collecting global subscription revenue, but settlement to Egypt must route via SWIFT to a CBE-licensed bank. Any payment card issuing or local acquiring arrangement requires a partnership with a CBE-licensed payment service provider.

After CBE's automated settlement and issuance of a Foreign Investment Certificate, funds become unrestricted for operating expenses. Failure to attach Form 20 triggers the presumption of an unsubstantiated related-party arrangement: the ETA may impose an income adjustment, deny cost recovery, apply the 2% monthly surcharge on additional tax, and charge a documentation penalty for absence of a required contemporaneous Local File.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions per fiscal year triggers the mandatory Local File/TP documentation and Form 20 disclosure; MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EGP 12,000,000,000 are additionally subject to Master File and Egyptian CbCR notification/filing obligations.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime; substance is enforced through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 33, Law 91/2005), Egyptian CFC rules, and the requirement that tax incentives, free-zone status, and treaty benefits are conditional on real activity in Egypt (employees, premises, management decisions, core revenue generation). A remote-support software LLC must evidence local R&D, contractual authority, and decision-making substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 139 of Law 91/2005, imprisonment from six months to five years and a criminal fine of multiples of the evaded tax (statutory range by court calibration), plus collection of the evaded tax with the 2% monthly delay surcharge and enforced administrative attachment.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who willfully file or approve false returns are prosecuted personally; the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent conduct, exposing personal assets to civil restitution, freezing orders, and travel bans during the criminal tax investigation.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: ETA invalidation of the artificial arrangements under Article 33 GAAR, disallowance of all deducted charges, recapture at 22.5% with the coupled 2% monthly surcharge, unpaid WHT on cross-border fees (20%), commercial registry deregistration, and revocation of the tax card.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or create fake invoices face civil recovery of the denied deductions, criminal forgery sanctions, and personal liability for the tax shortfall where the veil is pierced; the ETA may apply account freezes and director disqualification orders.
AML & Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law 18/2021, fines up to EGP 1,000,000, freezing of bank accounts, suspension of the LLC's electronic payment/collection capability in Egyptian banks, and criminal prosecution of the designated AML officer and directors.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer incur personal criminal liability if suspicious cross-border transactions are recklessly processed; individual asset freezing, travel bans, and confiscation of monies connected to unlawful proceeds may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Thin Capitalization Failure

Statutory sanction: Compensatory TP adjustment of taxable income, disallowance of excess interest/royalty deductions, additional 22.5% CIT, a 2% monthly delay surcharge on the resulting tax, and penalties for failure to present contemporaneous documentation; treaty benefits may be refused for non-resident group members.

Director personal liability: Directors attesting the Form 5 and Form 20 disclosures assume direct responsibility for material omissions; willful misapplication of TP rules is prosecuted under the tax evasion article, and the ETA can seize company bank balances and place personal liability on directors where the documentation defect conceals deliberate profit stripping.
