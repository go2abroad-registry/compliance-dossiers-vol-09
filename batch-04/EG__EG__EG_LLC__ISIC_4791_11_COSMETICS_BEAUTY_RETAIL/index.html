Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-Taxation services via the unified federal e-invoice and e-filing platform.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing an Egyptian LLC must complete commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Egyptian Commercial Registry, then obtain a Tax Card from the ETA. The LLC must subsequently register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 via the ETA e-Tax portal. Required documents include the Articles of Association, notarized incorporation forms, legal representative identification, proof of registered office, commercial registration certificate, board resolutions, and bank account details. The ETA issues a unified Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) applicable for corporate income tax, VAT, payroll tax, and stamp duty.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establishing an Egyptian LLC must complete commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Egyptian Commercial Registry, then obtain a Tax Card from the ETA. The LLC must subsequently register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 via the ETA e-Tax portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt imposes entity-level taxation on LLCs; no check-the-box or general pass-through election exists. Available statutory elections include voluntary VAT registration below the mandatory threshold, appointment of a fiscal representative by non-resident VAT vendors, the small taxpayer simplified taxable base regime under ETA guidelines, and certain election-based transfer pricing documentation reliefs. No hybrid mismatch pass-through election is available under Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover; however, any person importing or exporting goods in the course of business is subject to mandatory VAT registration regardless of turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods is zero-rated (0% VAT) under Article 19 of the Egyptian VAT Law, provided the goods are physically exported under customs supervision, the supplier holds a valid VAT registration, and the export is completed within the statutory timeline. Certain offshore/intangible services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated where the recipient is outside Egypt, the service is actually consumed outside Egypt, and documentary evidence supports the offshore character of the supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Sader/Hawiya) issued by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercially issued export invoice (matching the ETA-validated e-invoice)
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and/or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Valid VAT Tax Card and Export Contract/L/C documentation
  • Transport evidence (port/container records or CMR) where applicable

Egypt mandates real-time electronic invoicing (e-invoice) and electronic receipts (e-receipt) through the ETA's integrated clearance platform. Taxpayers must transmit B2B and B2G invoices and credit/debit notes in JSON/XML format via API authentication; each accepted invoice is digitally stamped with a validation code and QR code linked to the ETA real-time fiscal database. Phased enforcement is based on annual turnover thresholds, and non-compliance triggers invoice rejection, tax clearance certificate suspension, and fines.

The ETA may conduct an export substantiation audit for up to five years, review both inventory and bank submission trails, and retrospectively claw back input VAT offsets together with any uncollected zero-rated output VAT. Penalties include monthly delay tax of 2% on outstanding amounts, an additional fine up to 150% of the tax amount for false export documentation or fraud, and administrative fines up to EGP 50,000 independent of monetary penalties, plus criminal referral for tax evasion under Egyptian tax law. Unjustified export refunds are recoverable with full clawback and compounded penalty interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a market-managed floating exchange-rate regime restored by the CBE decision of 6 March 2024, which unified the official and parallel rates and eliminated mandatory forced conversion. Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services) are freely convertible through licensed banks with documentary verification only; no pre-shipment exchange approval is required. Capital-account items (FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, dividend remittances, and branch remittances) are convertible subject to underlying documentation and balance-of-payments registration at the receiving bank. Practical profit-remittance hurdles: (i) dividend remittance requires audited annual financial statements proving distributable reserves, a tax clearance certificate issued by the Egyptian Tax Authority, and settlement of the 10% dividend withholding tax (treaty-reduced rates require a tax residence certificate); (ii) banks will demand proof that the paid-in capital has been reflected in the Commercial Registry and the CBE statistical system before any distribution is processed; (iii) liquidity queues may still arise for USD at smaller banks, but the main institutions (CIB, NBE, Banque Misr) clear legitimate corporate asks within ordinary business days; (iv) all conversions must occur at the published interbank market rate and no parallel-market premium may be recorded in the statutory accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign-exchange instructions issued pursuant to Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, exporters must repatriate and credit the full value of export proceeds to a licensed Egyptian bank within 180 days following the shipping/customs-release date. Historically, 100% of gross export receipts had to be converted into EGP upon receipt; following the 6 March 2024 unified market-rate decision, the mandatory EGP conversion was removed, and exporters may retain proceeds in a foreign-currency export-proceeds account (FCA) and deploy those balances for trade-related foreign-currency payments (supplier imports, logistics, insurance) without prior CBE approval, or convert them at the prevailing interbank rate. The 180-day repatriation deadline remains a hard statutory requirement: any foreign-held balance, including funds resting in digital wallets or offshore EMI accounts, must be physically credited into the Egyptian banking system within that window. Non-compliance exposes the exporter to administrative sanctions, ranging from compulsory conversion at punitive rates, bank-level transaction restrictions, and suspension of the export registration. Extensions are granted only for documented force majeure or carrier delays and require exceptional CBE/account-bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sovereign monetary authority and foreign-exchange administration acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law). The CBE exclusively licenses banks and payment service providers and governs all inward and outward cross-border fund transfers, export-proceeds repatriation, and hedging transactions in Egypt.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Conditional / Medium when used solely as a secondary collection corridor, but Low as a standalone treasury solution. Legal constraints: (i) the CBE export-repatriation regime requires all export proceeds to be credited to a licensed Egyptian bank within 180 days; an offshore digital EMI (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Modulr) is not a CBE-licensed bank and cannot satisfy the repatriation rule if funds remain abroad; (ii) Egyptian card-network and national payments rules require e-commerce settlement to enter through an Egyptian acquirer or through an Egyptian-licensed payment service provider linked to the local bank account; (iii) Egyptian accounting and tax records must recognise the EMI only as an intermediary, not as a treasury counterparty; (iv) a compliant operating pattern is: PayPal/Shopify settlement → offshore EMI for collection and currency aggregation → immediate onward SWIFT MT103 (within 7 days or less) to the Egyptian beneficiary bank, with the local bank fully notified that the EMI is a mere fronting corridor and with all commercial invoices, shipment proofs, and platform payout statements attached to each remittance; (v) any prolonged accumulation of export proceeds in an offshore wallet is a compliance red flag and will be treated by the Egyptian bank as a breach of the exporter's repatriation undertaking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Funds may enter Egypt only through an Egyptian-licensed bank using the SWIFT system (MT103 for direct credits; MT202 for treasury interbank legs). The remitting foreign shareholder must be named exactly as in the GAFI investment registration, and the beneficiary must be the LLC's EGP and/or USD capital-contribution account at the onboarding bank. The purpose must be classified as equity capital for direct investment under the BPM6 balance-of-payments code 'Direct Investment – Equity', and the MT103 field 72 narrative must state 'FDI Share Capital under Unified Investment Law No. 72 of 2017'. The Egyptian bank will not credit the capital account until the full compliance file is verified: (i) GAFI investment-registration certificate; (ii) notarised and apostilled Memorandum of Association including the capital schedule and shareholding split; (iii) Commercial Registry extract sealing the managers' authority; (iv) UBO register; (v) sanctions/PEP screening results; and (vi) source-of-funds evidence. After credit, the bank issues an FDI deposit confirmation and registers the inflow in the CBE's electronic balance-of-payments statistics system. The paid-in capital must then be notarised into the MOA within the statutory filing period; in-kind capital contributions are not wired and require a separate bank-approved valuation protocol.Clearing rails for trade settlement: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for supplier prepayments, post-shipment balances, and reimbursement of marketing/logistics services; the paying bank requires the commercial invoice, proforma invoice, underlying contract, and Nafeza customs import entry once goods arrive, and the transfer must be made in the invoice currency without split payments. (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) issued via SWIFT MT700 are preferred for first-time international suppliers and high-value purchases of packaging and raw materials; payment is made against compliant shipping documents (bill of lading, invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and EDA product registration where applicable). (iii) Documentary collections (documents against payment or acceptance) are suitable for trusted repeat suppliers at lower cost. (iv) Domestic EGP disbursements use the CBE RTGS system and Meeza corporate payment rails. Every outward foreign-currency remittance must be accompanied by the underlying trade documentation and the bank's CBE-mandated FX declaration forms, ensuring consistency with the exporter's balance-of-payments reporting and customs records.Deliverable USD/EGP FX forwards with tenors generally up to 12 months, executed under an ISDA/IFXMA master agreement and supported by verified underlying export or import exposure; settlement occurs onshore at the prevailing interbank market rate.USD/EGP FX swaps combining same-day and forward legs, used for temporary EGP liquidity management and the orderly roll-over of forward positions.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP and EUR/USD/EGP combinations) permitted for medium-term balance-sheet hedging of the LLC's foreign-currency liabilities, including interest-payment legs.Vanilla USD/EGP foreign-exchange options offered by major private banks (CIB, AAIB, HSBC Egypt), subject to the bank's underlying-trade-exposure test, ISDA documentation, and CBE counterparty limits; premiums may be paid in EGP or USD.Offshore EGP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not an onshore CBE-permitted product for Egyptian licensed entities; a non-resident parent treasury may execute EGP NDFs offshore strictly for consolidation hedging, but such contracts fall outside the Egyptian regulatory perimeter and are treated as one-way offshore transactions rather than bank-permissible hedging instruments.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Conditional / Medium when used solely as a secondary collection corridor, but Low as a standalone treasury solution. Legal constraints: (i) the CBE export-repatriation regime requires all export proceeds to be credited to a licensed Egyptian bank within 180 days; an offshore digital EMI (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Modulr) is not a CBE-licensed bank and cannot satisfy the repatriation rule if funds remain abroad; (ii) Egyptian card-network and national payments rules require e-commerce settlement to enter through an Egyptian acquirer or through an Egyptian-licensed payment service provider linked to the local bank account; (iii) Egyptian accounting and tax records must recognise the EMI only as an intermediary, not as a treasury counterparty; (iv) a compliant operating pattern is: PayPal/Shopify settlement → offshore EMI for collection and currency aggregation → immediate onward SWIFT MT103 (within 7 days or less) to the Egyptian beneficiary bank, with the local bank fully notified that the EMI is a mere fronting corridor and with all commercial invoices, shipment proofs, and platform payout statements attached to each remittance; (v) any prolonged accumulation of export proceeds in an offshore wallet is a compliance red flag and will be treated by the Egyptian bank as a breach of the exporter's repatriation undertaking.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 for aggregate controlled transactions in a fiscal year; Master File required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 1,000,000,000; a transfer pricing disclosure schedule is mandatory in all cases where related-party transactions exceed EGP 2,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the ETA invokes substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance rules, and beneficial ownership requirements under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended; treaty relief and tax planning structures are disregarded where the recipient lacks real economic substance such as premises, personnel, and operational activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Intentional Underreporting)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under Law No. 91 of 2005 and Law No. 11 of 2020: fine up to EGP 50,000, imprisonment up to five years, and civil collection of the evaded tax plus a penalty of up to 100-150% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who sign or approve false returns are jointly and severally liable; ETA may impose asset seizure, travel bans, passport retention, exclusion from public works, and criminal forfeiture of the illicitly suppressed sales proceeds.
Shell Company / Artificial Procurement Structure

Statutory sanction: Non-recognition of the intermediary, re-characterization of the transactions, upward assessment at the Egyptian 22.5% CIT rate, additional 20% withholding on reclassified royalty or service payments, and late-payment surcharges under Law No. 11 of 2020.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in the artificial structure face personal assessment for the additional tax, director disqualification, travel restrictions, and potential criminal fraud liability if the arrangement is proven to be a sham to mislead the ETA.
AML and Payment Settlement Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002: imprisonment from three to fifteen years and a fine of EGP 1,000,000-5,000,000; subsidiary sanctions include freezing orders, forfeiture of funds, and suspension or revocation of the commercial registration and e-commerce licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may be personally prosecuted; sanctions include arrest, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as directors in Egypt or any other ETA-supervised entity.
