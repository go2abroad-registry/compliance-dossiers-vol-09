Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Commercial Registry Department / One-Stop-Shop (OSS); General Department of Commercial Registry, Ministry of Trade and Industry.

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended by Law No. 94 of 2005 (single-member LLC provisions), Articles 10-21 (Limited Liability Companies); Law No. 72 of 2017 (Investment Law) and its Executive Regulations, Chapter II (GAFI One-Stop-Shop); Commercial Registry Law and Executive Regulations, Ministry of Trade and Industry.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Contract and Articles of Association ('Aqd at-Ta'sis), notarized before a Notary Public and filed through GAFI's one-stop shop, culminating in the Commercial Registry Certificate (Qayd Tigari).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Egyptian LLCs (abolished by Law No. 94/2005); declared capital is divided into nominal quotas and may be paid in cash or in kind. The cash portion must be placed in a temporary capital escrow deposit account at a licensed Egyptian bank before incorporation, and a bank certificate of deposit is required; in-kind contributions require a certified expert valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety clearance from the General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) is required for the registered office, together with a licensed-engineer structural/occupancy load confirmation for the leased floor; workplace registration under Law 12/2003 (Occupational Safety and Health) covers emergency exits, first-aid, electronic accident registers, and the occupational clearance certificate for the software workforce.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Egyptian LLCs (abolished by Law No. 94/2005); declared capital is divided into nominal quotas and may be paid in cash or in kind. The cash portion must be placed in a temporary capital escrow deposit account at a licensed Egyptian bank before incorporation, and a bank certificate of deposit is required; in-kind contributions require a certified expert valuation report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - Ministry of Finance; central e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

After incorporation with GAFI, the Egyptian LLC files the unified tax registration application (Form 1) through the ETA portal within 30 days. Required supporting documents include the certified Arabic incorporation deed, commercial register abstract, registered office proof, and identification/authorisation of directors and shareholders. The ETA issues the Tax Card containing the Tax Identification Number (TIN) within 5-15 working days. A separate VAT registration application must be filed through the same portal when annual taxable turnover equals or exceeds EGP 500,000; voluntary VAT registration is allowed below the threshold. Non-resident foreign entities deriving Egyptian-source income must obtain a temporary TIN through the non-resident registration facility and may also need to register for electronic services VAT regardless of turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch classification election. The relevant statutory elections are: (1) small business simplified tax election under the Unified Tax Law 153/2022 - eligible taxpayers with annual gross turnover not exceeding EGP 20 million may annually elect a single 5% final tax on gross turnover in lieu of corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty and real estate tax; (2) voluntary VAT registration for taxable persons below the EGP 500,000 threshold; and (3) election of a 12-month fiscal year that differs from the calendar year, subject to prior ETA notification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for resident suppliers (compulsory registration); non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services to Egyptian consumers are required to register for VAT through the ETA simplified non-resident registration module regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported services, including SaaS subscriptions, are zero-rated under Article 28 of the VAT Law and implementing regulations when: (i) the customer is a non-resident with no Egyptian permanent establishment; (ii) the service is enjoyed or used wholly outside Egypt; (iii) delivery is evidenced by offshore cloud infrastructure logs and contract terms; (iv) payment is received in convertible foreign currency and repatriated in compliance with Central Bank of Egypt rules; and (v) the exporter maintains full documentary evidence of export intent. Supplies consumed by Egyptian residents are subject to 14% VAT and are reported through the e-receipt system.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for physical exports); for SaaS, the validated service export invoice together with the service delivery/performance certificate evidencing non-Egyptian enjoyment
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (for physical goods); for digital SaaS, the offshore cloud hosting contract, data-centre logs and access reports showing utilisation outside Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from an Egyptian bank or settlement report from the approved payment gateway (Paddle/Lemon Squeezy) evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance is in force across Egypt. The Micro-SaaS billing engine must issue ETA-compliant XML/JSON invoices and receipts and transmit them through the official ETA e-invoicing API/web portal; each document is cleared by the ETA and receives an authenticated hash and QR digital stamp before it becomes fiscally valid. Real-time validation covers schema, tax registration status, VAT rate application and uniqueness. The use of an ETA-authorised intermediary is acceptable. Non-submission, late submission, or alteration after clearance attracts statutory penalties per document and may lead to temporary suspension of the tax card or registration.

The ETA may retrospectively review export zero-rating files within the general five-year tax assessment period (extended where no timely return is filed). Insufficient documentation leads to reassessment at 14% VAT with late-payment interest and administrative penalties up to the amount of the tax evaded. In cases of fraud or false statements, penalties may increase up to double or higher, and criminal sanctions (fine and imprisonment) may be imposed under Law 153/2022. Any improperly refunded VAT is clawed back with interest, and the taxpayer's VAT registration may be suspended or cancelled.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) operates under a managed float with de jure partial convertibility. Current-account transactions, including cross-border software and SaaS service exports, are fully convertible subject to documentary support (invoice, settlement report, tax registration). Capital-account items, including inbound capital injections and outward dividends profit remittances, are permitted only through authorized banks and require GAFI registration, audited financial statements, board minutes, the annual capital-base confirmation, and a tax clearance certificate issued by the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) before repatriation. Residual hurdles include CBE priority checks during balance-of-payments stress, bank-level internal limits on USD conversions, and the practical requirement that remittances be debited from EGP after a documented conversion, which links remittance timing to daily bank liquidity. Since the March 2024 FX unification and the CBE/IMF liberalization programme, no parallel-market penalty is imposed, but banks maintain a 15-day hold on unusual inbound credits before release.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Software/SaaS exporters domiciled in Egypt must ensure all export proceeds are received through an authorized Egyptian bank in accordance with CBE instructions; the CBE reporting window requires the full value of the invoice to be credited to the exporter's Egyptian foreign-currency account within 15 business days from the contractual due date, and in no event later than 30 days after the end of the export shipment month. There is no mandatory percentage conversion ratio currently in force; exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currency, or may voluntarily sell them for EGP at the market-led interbank rate. A de facto conversion trigger applies where the exporter repatriates proceeds for EGP capex or operational expenditure: the bank effectuates conversion at the prevailing daily reference rate. Delayed realization triggers CBE-qualified penalties and may restrict the exporter s access to import financing, so the proceeds must arrive via SWIFT from Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, with a platform settlement report evidencing net SaaS subscription revenue.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) acting as the sole monetary, foreign-exchange and banking-regulatory authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), together with the CBE s Foreign Exchange Transactions Regulations and the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) for capital-flow registration. The CBE administers the interbank FX market, calculates the official EGP reference rate, and supervises all authorized dealers, including exchange bureaus and banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW for the primary treasury layer, MEDIUM as an ancillary settlement conduit. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Payoneer, Wise Platform, Mercury) are not licensed to establish deposit-taking or payment-institution activities in Egypt under Law No. 18 of 2019 (Regulating Non-Cash Payment Systems) and may not open EGP-denominated accounts or route CBE-regulated clearing. They can, however, receive USD collections from Paddle/Lemon Squeezy and forward net proceeds by SWIFT into the Egyptian LLC's onshore bank account. The Egyptian treasury stack must therefore be anchored by a domestic authorized dealer bank; offshore EMIs are only a pre-clearing collection convenience and must be cleared of funds before each FW/CBE reporting cut-off. Reliance solely on an offshore EMI without onshore repatriation is non-compliant and exposes the entity to CBE enforcement and ETA export-proceeds penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound route is a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the Egyptian LLC's foreign-currency capital account at an authorized dealer (CIB New York nostro for USD); the wire must articulate an explicit purpose code of the CBE balance-of-payments classification for direct investment in service enterprise capital, together with a GAFI approval reference. Remittance field 70 must carry the shareholder resolution date, GAFI registration number, and scheduled capital-increase amount. The receiving bank will verify the UBO against CBE watchlists, mandate a source-of-funds certificate for amounts exceeding USD 100,000, and credit the capital account; EGP conversion follows on the LLC's FX order at the interbank rate, with the converted balance credited to the EGP operational account. The injection must be declared on the CBE Form M and in the next periodic ETA direct investment return; failure to register with GAFI/CBE may cause a forced reversal of the credit.Standard clearing rail for payments is the SWIFT MT103 message executed through the Egyptian bank's internet corporate channel after ETA clearance; such payments include subscription cancellation refunds to customers (refund pool), vendor developer payments, cloud-hosting fees, and platform commissions to Paddle/Lemon Squeezy, all supported by purpose-coded invoices. For physical IT hardware or procurement of infrastructure, a documentary commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700) is preferred, with the Egyptian importing company posting 100% margin in EGP at the CBE rate and the beneficiary shipping documentation (B/L, commercial invoice, packing list). Domestic EGP legs are settled real-time through the Egyptian Banking Settlement System (RTGS) in Cairo, while USD cross-border settlement is cleared via correspondent CHIPS/Fedwire in New York and reconciled to the Egyptian bank's nostro balances. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of taxable income for ordinary Egyptian companies, including limited liability companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 10% on dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company. A 5% rate may apply to qualifying listed shares or qualifying ownership situations under the applicable Egyptian rules. A treaty may reduce the rate, subject to the relevant treaty conditions, beneficial-ownership requirements, anti-abuse rules, and procedural compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return is generally due within four months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year taxpayer, the normal deadline is 30 April of the following year. The return must generally be filed even where the company has no taxable profit or has incurred a tax loss.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation generally includes a Local File and Master File where the applicable related-party transaction and group thresholds are exceeded. The commonly applied threshold is EGP 8 million for each relevant transaction category for Local File purposes, including transactions involving goods, services, financing, or intangible property. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are generally relevant where the multinational group meets the EGP 3 billion consolidated-revenue threshold, subject to Egyptian filing and notification rules. Thresholds, categories, and filing deadlines must be checked against the current Egyptian Tax Authority instructions for the relevant year.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not currently operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable to every corporate entity. Nevertheless, substance remains material for corporate residence, permanent-establishment analysis, beneficial ownership, treaty access, transfer pricing, deductibility, anti-avoidance, and the validity of intercompany arrangements. The Egyptian LLC should maintain locally appropriate management, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure, books and records, decision-making evidence, customer and supplier records, and commercial rationale. A foreign IP, financing, or platform company with no meaningful functions, assets, risks, or decision-making capacity may be challenged as a conduit or sham arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, recovery of the unpaid tax, statutory additional tax, administrative penalties, and potential criminal tax-evasion proceedings. Tax-evasion offenses may carry imprisonment generally ranging from six months to five years and a fine linked to the evaded tax, subject to the applicable Tax Law, Tax Procedures Law, amendments, and judicial determination.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, authorized signatories, and persons who ordered, enabled, or knowingly participated in the conduct may face personal criminal exposure, fines, restitution of evaded tax, asset-seizure measures, and restrictions connected with criminal proceedings. Piercing of limited liability is fact-specific and is not automatic. Travel restrictions may be imposed by competent authorities or courts in connection with criminal investigations, unpaid public debts, or enforcement orders; imprisonment is possible only under applicable criminal law and judicial process.
Failure to register, file, or maintain tax records

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, estimated assessment, loss of evidentiary protection, additional tax, audit escalation, and possible criminal consequences where the conduct is deliberate or conceals tax.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and authorized representatives may be fined or prosecuted where their personal acts or omissions caused the violation. The LLC's limited liability does not protect a director from personal liability for statutory offenses, fraud, obstruction, or knowingly inaccurate filings.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to arm's-length income, secondary tax consequences where applicable, corporate tax, withholding tax, interest or additional tax, documentation penalties, and potential denial of deductions. Material concealment may support tax-evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers may face personal exposure for deliberate misrepresentation, falsified documentation, or participation in a fraudulent pricing arrangement. Ordinary commercial disagreement over method selection does not by itself establish personal criminal liability.
Improper treaty access and beneficial-ownership abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding-tax reassessment at the domestic rate, additional tax, interest, penalties, and potential anti-avoidance or tax-evasion proceedings where the documentation was knowingly false.

Director personal liability: Signatories and advisers who knowingly submit false certificates or conceal the beneficial owner may face civil restitution, administrative fines, and criminal exposure. Travel restrictions or detention measures may arise only under competent legal process.
Unlawful profit diversion and disguised distributions

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as dividends, salary, related-party payment, or taxable benefit; corporate tax and withholding-tax assessments; denial of deductions; penalties; and possible breach of company-law or insolvency duties.

Director personal liability: Directors may owe civil restitution to the company or creditors and may be liable for breach of fiduciary, accounting, or insolvency duties. Fraudulent diversion may create criminal exposure and can support attachment or travel measures under judicial or enforcement procedures.
Anti-money laundering and payment-platform misuse

Statutory sanction: Account freezing, transaction rejection, reporting to competent authorities, confiscation or forfeiture, regulatory penalties, tax reassessment, and criminal sanctions under Egyptian anti-money-laundering and related criminal legislation. Sanctions depend on the offense, knowledge, proceeds, and involvement.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, compliance officers, and employees who knowingly facilitate laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, or concealment may face personal criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and asset-restraint measures. Limited liability does not shield knowing participants.
Unlicensed or misclassified digital activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, license denial or revocation, fines, tax reassessment, service blocking, and criminal sanctions where the activity falls within a regulated or prohibited category.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly conduct an unlicensed regulated activity may face personal fines, prosecution, and orders affecting travel or assets under applicable sector legislation and judicial procedure.
Payroll, social insurance, and contractor misclassification

Statutory sanction: Back taxes, social-insurance contributions, additional tax, penalties, employee claims, audit adjustments, and potential criminal sanctions for deliberate withholding or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and payroll signatories may be personally liable for withheld amounts not remitted and for deliberate evasion or false records. Civil claims and enforcement against responsible officers may arise independently of the LLC's liability.
Corporate insolvency and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Transaction avoidance, director disqualification where applicable, civil damages, insolvency proceedings, tax recovery, asset tracing, and potential criminal liability for fraudulent bankruptcy or creditor prejudice.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil restitution, contribution to creditor losses, asset attachment, and criminal prosecution. Travel restrictions and imprisonment depend on the applicable offense and court order; they are not automatic consequences of ordinary business failure.
