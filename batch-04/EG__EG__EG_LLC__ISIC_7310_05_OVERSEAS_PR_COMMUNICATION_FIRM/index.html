Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under the amended Companies Law; capital is fixed by the AOA. The declared paid-up capital must be deposited in an Egyptian licensed bank, which issues a capital deposit certificate submitted with the GAFI incorporation file. Funds remain in the LLC's pre-registration bank account and are released upon issuance of the Commercial Registry certificate; no separate escrow or trust arrangement is statutorily imposed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Civil Defense Directorate (Ministry of Interior) requires verified extinguishers, smoke detectors, evacuation signage, and a fire-drill protocol; Ministry of Manpower Workplace Clearance (Form OHS-2) requires an OHS plan, first-aid kit, and NOSI enrollment for staff. Structural load suitability of the leased office is certified by a licensed structural engineer, and the lease must be registered in the LLC's name with the municipal licensing office.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Egyptian-incorporated LLC is a resident taxpayer under Income Tax Law 91/2005 Art. 17 and is taxed at 22.5% on worldwide income; no pass-through is available. Non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Egyptian-source income and distributions. Free-zone / SCZone Tax_Exempt_Zone status is available only by express GAFI licensing and is not a registration default.

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or hybrid classification election; entity classification follows mandatory statutory rules under the Civil Code and Income Tax Law. Available administrative elections include: (i) depreciation method election; (ii) group loss utilization election under Art. 54 of Law 91/2005; (iii) Investment Law incentive and free-zone election through GAFI; (iv) VAT input-tax allocation method election (direct attribution vs. proportional formula); (v) CFC and transfer-pricing safe-harbor elections under Law 30 of 2023. Hybrid mismatch treatment is statutory only and grants no taxpayer option.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable supplies (mandatory registration threshold); voluntary registration is permitted from EGP 50,000; zero-rated exports to non-residents count toward the threshold, while the Egypt-based PR agency must include all invoices for overseas clients when determining the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Law 67/2016 Art. 23, exported direct services - including public relations, media relations, and communication consultancy performed by the EG LLC for non-resident clients - qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the following conditions are met: (i) the recipient is a non-resident and the service is contractually and actually performed for overseas use; (ii) the invoice is issued in foreign currency with a clear 'zero-rated export services' statement; (iii) the consideration is received in hard currency through a licensed Egyptian bank within the CBE repatriation window; and (iv) full documentation evidencing offshore performance is retained. The related input VAT on local costs is creditable and refundable through the ETA refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Cargo Release Number on the NAFIZA Single Window) - required only for any physical exports; for services, the NAFIZA export declaration is replaced by the ETA Export Services Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR (for goods exports) or signed overseas service contract, deliverables, media plans, and campaign performance reports evidencing offshore use (for the PR firm)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / CBE foreign-exchange form showing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency and the matched booking to the export invoice
  • Original VAT export invoice bearing the zero-rate indicator and QR code, matches with the ETA e-invoicing system
  • Bank exchange account statement and SWIFT/MT103 evidence of settlement; completion of the Egyptian customs 'Export Accounting' verification

Mandatory real-time national e-invoicing is fully in force for all VAT-registered entities. Every invoice must be submitted to the ETA e-Invoicing API in bilingual Arabic-English JSON/XML format, digitally signed with an ETA-issued certificate, encoded with a QR stamp, and cleared by the ETA before the supply is deemed legally complete. The e-receipt module is also mandatory for B2C transactions. Non-compliance triggers fines of EGP 100 to EGP 50,000 per invoice, plus TIN suspension for repeated violations.

The ETA may retrospectively deny zero-rating and claw back input VAT credits within the 5-year audit window under Law 67/2016 Art. 63. Deficient or fraudulent export substantiation triggers: (i) reassessment at 14% VAT with interest at 2% per month; (ii) administrative fines under Arts. 72-73 ranging from EGP 1,000 to EGP 50,000 per violation and up to EGP 100,000 for invoice integrity breaches; (iii) criminal penalties under Art. 76 including imprisonment up to 5 years and fines of up to 5 times the evaded tax for deliberate falsification; and (iv) automatic inclusion in the CBE non-performing exporter list with suspension of export privileges. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days additionally subjects the exporter to monthly CBE fines up to 2% of the uncollected amount and immediate tax audit escalation under Law 194 of 2020.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is convertible for permissible current-account transactions, including service fees, management fees, royalties, dividends, and profit repatriation, where the underlying transaction is monetarily genuine and fully documented. Capital-account operations are subject to more intensive bank intermediation: non-resident equity contributions into an Egyptian LLC may enter freely through an authorised bank, but subsequent outward capital repatriation (liquidation distributions, sale of participation, repayment of shareholder loans) must be supported by audited financial statements, evidence of lawful original entry, current tax clearance, and bank records. Egypt operates a bank-intermediated managed float rather than full offshore EGP convertibility; EGP cannot be settled or exchanged outside the authorised banking system meaningfully. For an overseas PR-communication firm, profit remittance is feasible but document-heavy: the LLC must provide audited standalone accounts, a board distribution resolution, proof that the profit is tax-paid, valid commercial registration, and a CBE-compliant direct investment inflow record. Temporary FX liquidity tightness may cause banks to queue high-value remittances, but this is a settlement delay, not a legal prohibition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Egyptian resident exporters of services, including cross-border public-relations retainers, the gross invoice proceeds must be repatriated through an authorised Egyptian bank. Under prevailing CBE export-proceeds practice, the foreign exchange must be received by the bank within 90 calendar days from the service invoice due date, unless the bank agrees to a documented extension for valid commercial reasons. Once received, the funds must be posted to a designated foreign-currency collection/retention account or converted into EGP at the interbank market price. A mandatory 100% conversion presumption applies in the absence of a CBE-recognised foreign-currency expenditure plan; the entity may lower the conversion requirement to zero by submitting a rolling foreign-currency cash-flow forecast covering overseas subcontractors, media placements, cloud/subscription fees, and other genuine external liabilities. Every service export must be accompanied by an electronic declaration in the bank’s export system, the signed PR service contract, purchase order, performance statement, and the correct CBE balance-of-payments purpose code for business/public-relations services. Late collection, split-receipting, or the use of an unlicensed offshore payment intermediary to settle the proceeds directly into EGP outside the banking system would constitute a breach of Egyptian exchange-control rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange regulator in Egypt, acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, the Egyptian Exchange Control Law No. 80 of 1947 (as amended), and CBE Foreign-Exchange Department circulars. The CBE licenses all commercial banks, administers the official interbank FX market, receives all mandatory balance-of-payments and export-proceeds reporting, and supervises AML/CFT compliance through the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Egypt does not recognise foreign electronic-money institutions or digital-only cross-border banks as settlement banks for resident corporate accounts. Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, and similar offshore EMIs may be used strictly as front-end collection/payment tools, but all statutory service export proceeds must be swept into an authorised Egyptian bank account before EGP conversion or any CBE-compliant disbursement. No passporting or EMI equivalence regime exists in Egypt; an offshore EMI cannot issue a CBE-recognised export proceeds receipt and cannot satisfy the physical corporate-KYC mandate. Locally licensed payment fintech companies in Egypt operate under CBE e-payment licences and still require a settlement account at a licensed bank; they do not replace commercial banking rails.

Defense evidence: Present a bank treasury-side attestation that the Egyptian USD/EGP collection account is the contractual settlement account; provide gateway and EMI statements demonstrating that enterprising funds are automatically or daily swept into the authorised Egyptian bank before any EGP conversion; reconcile every invoice to a unique wire reference and purpose code; file the required CBE export-proceeds declarations showing full repatriation; execute a compliance memorandum committing that no crypto-currency or shell payment mechanism will be used; and supply the bank with a copy of the negative-screening report for the EMI provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for a foreign shareholder injecting equity into EG_LLC is a direct SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder’s bank account to the LLC’s USD/EUR account held at an authorised Egyptian bank. The wire must name the beneficiary as EG_LLC, contain the shareholder legal name in field 50, and use a clear remittance reference such as 'FDI-EQ-CAP-EG-LLC' or a GAFI investment-file reference. The receiving bank will assign the transaction the CBE statistical/BOP purpose code for direct investment equity capital (commonly 'Direct Investment – Share Capital into an Egyptian Resident Company'; the exact statistical code is selected in the bank’s E-boarding system). the bank enters the inflow into the CBE’s external investment and capital position recording; if the contribution is made in USD and the LLC requires EGP operating capital, the bank converts the funds at the interbank rate through the Egypt Electronic Foreign Exchange System (EFES) and issues a capital-inflow certificate. Supporting documents for verification include the LLC’s constitutional documents, GAFI incorporation/amendment certificate, non-resident shareholder board resolution, source of funds, passport copies, and compliance declarations. The capital must be used only for the LLC’s stated corporate purposes, not for speculative FX positions.For overseas trade and service payments, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct credit to a nominated beneficiary, with an optional MT202 COVER where the bank settles through a correspondent. For high-value or documentary transactions, the LLC may open an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit (LC) confirmed by a reputable international bank, transmitted by SWIFT MT700/MT710 subject to UCP600. The outbound remitting bank must receive an underlying invoice, contract, service completion evidence, and the correct CBE payment purpose code; disbursements can be funded either from the LLC’s retained foreign-currency export-proceeds account or by purchasing EGP for USD at the prevailing market rate. Where the payment is to the entity’s own overseas affiliate, an additional transfer-pricing file must be maintained to support the amount and timing. All outbound wires should be pre-screened through the bank’s sanctions filter and comply with Egypt’s anti-money-laundering reporting thresholds.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP forward contract with an authorised Egyptian bank, concluded only for an actual or anticipated retainer-receivable or operating-payable exposure and carrying strict non-speculative documentation; settlement occurs at maturity through the EFES rate with physical EGP delivery.Onshore FX swap (swap-in/swap-out of USD and EGP) with a licensed bank, used for temporary liquidity alignment between a foreign-currency receipt and an imminent EGP expenditure, executed as two matched legs with a zero or transparent spread.Cross-currency swap between USD and EGP with an authorised bank, used to hedge balance-sheet exposures arising from a long-term foreign-currency shareholder loan or multi-year overseas retainer contract, with periodic net payments matched to the underlying cash-flow profile.Zero-cost collar / range forward involving USD and EGP, available on a relationship-approved treasury basis to cap conversion cost and set a floor for expected export-proceeds conversions, supported by audited receivables forecasts and CBE-compliant underlying documentation.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) in EGP/USD is not generally permissible for the Egyptian resident LLC onshore; it may be considered only where the Egyptian bank and CBE provide an explicit exception, typically for a non-resident affiliate or where the hedging need cannot be settled in EGP; as a rule, the LLC should avoid NDFs unless CBE clearance in writing is obtained.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW. Egypt does not recognise foreign electronic-money institutions or digital-only cross-border banks as settlement banks for resident corporate accounts. Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, and similar offshore EMIs may be used strictly as front-end collection/payment tools, but all statutory service export proceeds must be swept into an authorised Egyptian bank account before EGP conversion or any CBE-compliant disbursement. No passporting or EMI equivalence regime exists in Egypt; an offshore EMI cannot issue a CBE-recognised export proceeds receipt and cannot satisfy the physical corporate-KYC mandate. Locally licensed payment fintech companies in Egypt operate under CBE e-payment licences and still require a settlement account at a licensed bank; they do not replace commercial banking rails.
