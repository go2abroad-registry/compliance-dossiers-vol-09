Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — the unified national tax administration established under Law No. 92 of 2020 by merger of the former Income Tax Authority and Sales Tax/VAT Authority; official digital portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg, providing the taxpayer registration console, e-filing, e-invoicing clearance, e-receipt reporting, and the TaxtoGo integrated e-services interface.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested Egyptian LLC must obtain a statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN; tax file number) through the ETA single-window registration module. Procedure: (1) file the unified VAT and income tax registration declaration (Form 1) via the ETA e-portal; (2) upload the certified Arabic and English versions of the Articles of Association, the Commercial Registry extract (Al-Sijil Al-Tijari), and the board resolution designating the Egyptian registered office and appointing an accredited tax representative; (3) attach the registered office lease or usufruct deed, the opening audited balance sheet, the bank certificate for the LLC's account at an Egyptian licensed bank, and the ultimate beneficial owner declaration under AML Law No. 80 of 2002; (4) receive the ETA Tax Card and VAT registration certificate, normally issued within 7 to 10 business days. Resident tax status attaches at Commercial Registry incorporation, so the LLC is enrolled immediately in income tax, payroll, withholding, e-invoicing, and VAT compliance modules upon TIN issuance; a non-resident founder structure is permitted without minimum capital under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or foreign-entity reclassification election; an Egyptian LLC is statutorily a resident juridical person under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, taxed at the 22.5% corporate headline rate on globally sourced income, and can never elect pass-through treatment. The available statutory elections and mechanisms for the EG LLC (ESOP administration services) are: (i) election to compute taxable profits from certified audited financial statements rather than an ETA-assessed deemed profits basis; (ii) election to carry forward net operating losses for 5 consecutive years without indexation; (iii) election to use the ETA consolidated e-filing and e-invoicing compliance channels; (iv) election under the advance pricing agreement route in the Egyptian transfer-pricing framework for the intercompany trust-administration service fee; (v) election to claim reduced withholding tax on cross-border payments by lodging the beneficial ownership and tax residency certificate under the applicable double tax treaty; and (vi) application of the Multilateral Convention (MLI) principal purpose test and limitation-on-benefits filters, which override hybrid entity arrangements lacking economic substance (Egypt is an MLI signatory and applies the PTT to covered tax agreements).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover of goods or services in Egypt (mandatory VAT registration threshold under Article 20 of Law 67 of 2016); voluntary registration is permitted where deemed taxable activity begins at EGP 50,000; non-resident suppliers of digital and electronic services achieve taxable nexus in Egypt and must register through the simplified non-resident registration path once Egyptian-source turnover exceeds the EGP 500,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 5 of Law No. 67 of 2016, the 0% VAT rate applies where: (a) goods are exported outside Egyptian customs territory under an accepted Customs Export Declaration; or (b) services are supplied to a non-resident client for use wholly outside Egypt, including ancillary transport, handling, freight-forwarding, insurance, and international transport services. For the EG LLC administering an employee stock ownership trust (ESOP): the 0% rate will stand where the service agreement is executed with the non-resident ESOP trustee or foreign plan sponsor, the share-trust accounting, payroll administration, and equity settlement mechanics are performed against an overseas beneficiary ledger, and the consideration is received in convertible foreign currency in the Egyptian LLC's licensed bank account. Services supplied to, or recharged by, a domestic branch, a resident group company, or for local employees are standard-rated at 14% and do not qualify as export services. The taxpayer may recover input VAT attributed to zero-rated exports through the monthly VAT return offset regime or via the ETA export-input refund protocol.

  • Customs Export Declaration transmitted through the ACI/NAFIZA single window, stamped by Egyptian Customs with the final export release number (for goods); for services, the notarized offshore-service contract specifying the place of performance and foreign economic use
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or International Carrier's Receipt evidencing delivery outside Egypt; for ESOP/administrative services, a time-sheet or performance registry proving that the processes were executed by the Egyptian establishment for the non-resident principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the receiving Egyptian licensed bank, showing the exact foreign-currency amount, transaction reference, and conversion record, together with the Central Bank of Egypt electronic collection notification
  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice bearing the 0% rate, the ETA E-Invoice code/QR, contract reference, and INCO terms, with the full credit note history where applicable
  • Input-VAT allocation and cost build-up working papers demonstrating that deductions relate proportionately to the zero-rated export supplies
  • Corporate and economic substance file: board minutes, commercial correspondence, beneficial ownership disclosure, and the export-proceeds follow-up ledger reconciling each export dispatch to its corresponding wire of proceeds

Egypt operates a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance regime: every VAT-registered taxpayer must transmit B2B invoices to the ETA centralised E-Invoicing platform, either via certified API/ERP integration (XML or JSON) or through the ETA web portal, and the ETA validates the invoice data, applies a unique digital E-Invoice code and QR identifier, and returns the cleared invoice to the issuer within seconds. Post-clearance editing is prohibited; corrective credit and debit notes must be separately cleared through the same flow. Business-to-consumer transactions are captured by the separate e-Receipt (fiscal receipt) system phased in since 2021. Non-cleared invoices lose input VAT deductibility and expose the issuer to administrative fines and monthly adjustment penalties on unreported output VAT.

The ETA, jointly with Egyptian Customs and the Central Bank of Egypt, applies intensive export-audit controls: export files must be retained for 5 years from the end of the relevant financial year, and any zero-rated invoice not supported by the Customs export declaration, the shipping or performance record, and the inward remittance received within the 180-day mandatory repatriation cycle is automatically re-characterized as a domestic standard-rated supply. Consequences include: retrospective VAT claw-back at 14% with reversal of all input VAT credits previously offset against the zero-rated output; statutory delayed-tax additions of up to 3% per 30-day period under Articles 26 and 27 of Law 67 of 2016; administrative fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 for false export documentation under Article 67; criminal referral with imprisonment of up to 5 years and confiscation of instruments for manufacturing or knowingly presenting forged export documents; and, for recidivist evasion, an additional penalty not exceeding double the evaded VAT, plus attachment of bank accounts through the Egyptian Economic Court. Voluntary self-correction within 60 days of an ETA inspection letter, with full payment before assessment, may abate a portion of the interest additions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP is fully convertible for current-account transactions as liberalized since 2016, including trade in goods and services, profit and dividend remittances, interest, royalties, and eligible employee transfers, provided the underlying transaction is documented via CBE-endorsed verification forms (referable to CBE Form 10/11 for services and invisible transactions). Capital-account transactions—foreign equity injections, cross-border corporate loans, portfolio inflows, and repatriation of disposal proceeds—remain subject to mandatory CBE registration and, for certain structures, prior approval; no general ex-ante exchange control permit exists for ordinary current items, but profit remittance requires a valid Tax Clearance Certificate under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 Art. 50, audited financials confirming accumulated distributable reserves, and GAFI verification of contributed capital. Practical friction arises from EGP float volatility, periodic US-dollar liquidity cycles, CBE-mandated documentation discipline, and the need to book each flow through a CBE-licensed authorized bank rather than offshore wallets or unauthorized PSPs. A foreign-owned EG_LLC administered ESOP trust would face remittance scrutiny on any cross-border transfer to non-resident option holders; therefore every outward transfer must be matched to signed equity plan documents, tax certificates, and CBE purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations, all export proceeds must be repatriated through an authorized bank in Egypt within 90 days from the date of shipment or dispatch, extendable to 180 days for certain sectors with CBE approval; since deregulation in 2011, mandatory surrender of hard currency is abolished, but 100% of repatriated proceeds must be credited to an EGP or registered foreign-currency corporate account and can only be used for permitted outflows (import settlements, corporate obligations, or sale to the banking system). Non-compliance triggers banking facilities freeze, customs clearance block, and CBE penalty lists, and requires the exporter to retain the full original documentary set (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration, beneficiary bank credit advice). For the subject entity (ISIC 6430 ESOP trust administration with no direct merchandise exports), the mechanism is legally N/A for trust service receipts; however, all income from employee/investor contributions must be classified as 'services or invisible transactions' supported by the Trust Deed, ESOP plan, contribution memos, and audited statements, and repatriated within the same deadline logic if any service export fee is charged to a foreign employing group company.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) is the sole monetary, banking, and foreign-exchange regulatory authority, exercising statutory control under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, the Banking Sector Law, and binding CBE circulars, decisions, and administrative instructions. The General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Egyptian Exchange act as ancillary bodies for investment registration and capital-flow verification.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW. Offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA-authorised or EU e-money institutions) and cross-border fintech platforms have no legal authority to hold EGP corporate accounts, convert EGP, or settle employee payroll obligations in Egypt under CBE Law No. 194 of 2020, the CBE's 2021 Payment Services Regulation, and CBE AML directives; all local payment initiation and FX conversion are restricted to CBE-licensed banks and CBE-licensed payment service providers. Offshore USD/EUR treasury accounts at major international banks are operationally permissible to facilitate external ESOP contributions and foreign option-holder payouts, but cannot substitute for mandatory local CBE bank accounts, Egyptian payroll rails, tax withholding deposition, or CBE reporting; reliance on unlicensed EMIs would additionally trigger AML/CFT red flags with the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit and jeopardize GAFI/CBE good standing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires (1) the foreign investor/ESOP settlor wires funds from the investor's own named bank account via SWIFT MT103 to the Egyptian LLC's CBE-registered foreign-currency capital account at an authorized bank; (2) the MT103 reference field must carry the EG_LLC GAFI registration number, the beneficiary bank SWIFT BIC, and the CBE purpose code for capital contribution (per the CBE's Uniform Nomenclature of Transactions, specifically 'P01-capital injections' or as instructed by the receiving bank's compliance), and (3) the local bank files CBE Form 33 (investment registration) so the flow is booked as 'capital and other investment liabilities' with a Registration Approval Number (RAN). Loan financing from non-resident parent/option plan sponsors must be reported to the CBE under the borrowing framework and, if exceeding regulatory thresholds, requires the CBE registration of the external loan before disbursement; all amounts must be supported by board resolutions, a notarized share subscription/capital increase contract, and GAFI end-of-year capital verification certificates. Domestic EGP contributions from resident employees routing into the trust administrator's EGP operational account must be credited through local RTGS/ACH with purpose code 'domestic corporate transfer' and retained with the underlying ESOP subscription forms for the 5-year CBE record-keeping window.Primary clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for all cross-border settlements—repurchase of exercised shares from non-resident option holders, payments of ESOP service fees to foreign group entities, and reimbursements of professional fees; each MT103 must be accompanied by CBE Form 10/11 documentation, a tax clearance certificate for profit-like remittances, and matching contract/invoice sets; (ii) commercial standby/documentary letters of credit (MT700) where the ESOP trust treasury acquires liquid assets or pays suppliers of plan administration infrastructure, subject to CBE import/LC rules; (iii) domestic payroll disbursements routed through the Egyptian RTGS and Instant Payment Network (IPN) from a segregated EGP payroll account; and (iv) for shareholder distributions, an Egypt-specific return-of-equity remittance code validated by CBE investment records and audited distributable reserves. Any EGP/USD conversion for outbound payments must be executed at the dealing rate of a CBE-licensed bank, with the bank issuing a conversion slip retained for audit; forward cover must correspond to a real underlying receivable/payable with supporting invoices to avoid speculative FX classification.Onshore deliverable FX forwards (tenor up to 360 days) between the EG_LLC and a CBE-licensed authorized bank, hedged strictly on underlying payroll and share-repurchase exposure with invoice/plan documentation; the bank must book the forward as a genuine hedging transaction under CBE dealing rules.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP) and cross-currency interest-rate swaps executed through CBE-licensed banks for long-term foreign-denominated shareholder loans or bond proceeds, each requiring CBE notification/approval and designation of the underlying foreign-currency liability.Transactional limit-cover (pre-purchase) contracts for contracted ESOP contributions and committed foreign disbursements, documented by invoice, vesting notices, or contribution memos, and booked on a cost basis without leverage.Offshore EGP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are NOT permitted for onshore Egyptian entities; onshore access is prohibited by the CBE, and participation by the EG_LLC in offshore NDFs would constitute a regulatory breach.Onshore FX options and exotic derivatives are generally not offered to corporates; any use would require separate express CBE licensing approval and is considered impermissible for a standard ESOP administration vehicle.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard corporate income tax rate for Egyptian juridical persons under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended; special rates apply to certain oil and state-owned enterprise categories (40.55%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends and profit distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to shareholders and beneficiaries; resident corporate shareholders are within the Egyptian CIT net or exemption system, while non-resident shareholders receive a 10% WHT deduction unless a Double Taxation Agreement (DTA) provides a lower rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return and final self-assessment must be filed within four months after the close of the LLC's 12-month fiscal year (e.g., a 31 December year-end is due by 30 April of the following calendar year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Egyptian Local File documentation is mandatory when the aggregate value of controlled transactions with a related party exceeds EGP 15 million in a fiscal year; the Master File is required for controlled transactions above EGP 30 million or where the Egyptian entity is part of an MNE group exceeding the equivalent gross revenue threshold set by the ETA executive regulations. The ETA retains power to request documentation below these thresholds when facts indicate a risk of income shifting.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime similar to offshore financial centers. However, under Law No. 91 of 2005 and the ETA's general anti-abuse and substance-over-form powers, the tax authority may disregard a juridical arrangement if the LLC administering the ESOP trust lacks genuine local offices, staff, decision-making, board meetings, or real economic activity. The entity must demonstrate that the administration and trust services are carried out in Egypt and that the shares and underlying investments are beneficially held in a legitimate fiduciary structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for a term of up to 5 years and a fine of not less than EGP 5,000 and not exceeding EGP 50,000, in addition to payment of the entire evaded tax and a monthly delay surcharge of 2%; the court may order closure of the registered office for up to one year.

Director personal liability: Directors, trustees, and managers who sign false returns or knowingly participate in concealment are jointly and severally liable for unpaid CIT; criminal conviction can trigger asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Gap

Statutory sanction: ETA recharacterization of the controlled transaction at arm's length with additional tax, an additional assessment penalty of up to 30%-90% of the underpaid tax, delay surcharges, and monetary penalties for non-submission of TP disclosure forms.

Director personal liability: The CFO and designated tax officer can be held personally accountable for the assessed additional tax where they witness and approve false TP documentation; criminal liability arises for forgery of benchmarking studies or submission of false declarations.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA is entitled to disregard the legal personality of the entity, tax all net income as attributable to the parent or beneficial owners, deny CIT deductions, and impose nondeductible 100% additional tax penalties in repeated cases of artificial arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors and nominee shareholders who lend their names to the shell vehicle may be treated as de facto managers; they face civil recovery of unpaid taxes, asset freezing, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for facilitating tax evasion.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The company is personally responsible for the withheld amount; ETA collects the tax from the LLC as agent, adds a 2% monthly delay surcharge, imposes a fine of up to EGP 20,000, and may prosecute the officers for failure to remit tax maintainable for public funds.

Director personal liability: Officers who cause the company to remit gross dividends or royalties become personally liable for the WHT debt and any bankruptcy losses; prison sentences of up to 3 years can be imposed for intentional non-remittance of collected tax.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of not less than 5 years and a fine of EGP 500,000-1,000,000; proceeds and related property are subject to confiscation and freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Senior management of the LLC and trustees of the ESOP are deemed obligated-persons under the AML law; knowing participation leads to personal criminal liability, civil restitution to affected employees, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from serving as directors.
