Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Egypt Ministry of Finance; e-Tax portal at www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipt system at einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must first register with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) to obtain incorporation approval and commercial registration. The LLC must then file an ETA Tax ID issuance application (Tax Card form) through the e-Tax portal within 30 days of commercial registration, submitting the company contract (MOA), commercial registration certificate, board resolutions, legal attorney appointment, office lease proof, passports of managers/owners and financial year data. The ETA issues a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and a Tax Card showing the VAT, withholding tax and stamp tax registrations. Non-resident owners are not required to obtain a separate TIN for the LLC; a non-resident entity with Egyptian-source income is registered only when a withholding obligation or permanent establishment is identified. VAT registration is mandatory once annual taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed EGP 500,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Egyptian tax law. A resident Egyptian LLC is a per se taxable entity subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income; there is no shareholder-level pass-through. Limited special regimes are available by election or by license under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, including the Free Zone regime (subject to ETA/GAFI approval), Special Economic Zones under Law No. 83 of 2002, and certain investment incentives; these do not convert an LLC into a pass-through entity. No hybrid mismatch entity election is recognized in Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for VAT registration; mandatory ETA Tax Card registration applies from the commencement of the LLC's business activity regardless of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services consumed outside Egypt qualify for 0% VAT. For executive search and headhunting services, the following criteria are audited: the service is supplied to a non-resident client not registered under the Egyptian VAT law; the service is performed for purposes of the client's business outside Egypt; the benefit, use, and placement result occur outside Egypt; and payment is received in freely convertible foreign currency through a licensed Egyptian bank. The LLC must maintain transparent evidence linking the invoice to an actual offshore service and must reflect the export zero-rating in the e-invoicing system.

  • Customs Export Declaration and export manifest for physical goods (not applicable to pure service exports, but required when headhunting transactions include bundled goods or promotional materials)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or equivalent transport document for physical goods (not required for pure executive search services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement issued by a CBE-authorized bank confirming receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Executed executive search/headhunting service agreement with the non-resident client, including scope, fee structure and jurisdiction
  • ETA e-invoice with the zero-rated export code and QR digital stamp evidencing real-time fiscal clearance
  • Documented service performance evidence: candidate search, interview coordination, placement acceptance, timesheets and final placement reports
  • Bank wire via retainer or Wise B2B record showing the foreign payer matched to the invoice and the legally required foreign exchange repatriation

Egypt has a mandatory real-time e-invoicing and e-receipt regime administered by the ETA. B2B and B2G invoices must be transmitted in structured XML or JSON via ETA-approved APIs, cleared in real time, and stamped with a unique QR digital stamp before issuance. The e-receipt system is progressively mandated for B2C transactions, including executive search and headhunting services. All zero-rated export invoices must also be submitted through the e-invoicing system with the appropriate export VAT code. Noncompliance with e-invoicing, e-receipt, or digital stamping rules triggers fines, invoice rejection, and enhanced audit risk.

The ETA and the Egyptian Central Bank actively audit export zero-rating claims. A claim will be clawed back if the foreign proceeds are not repatriated or evidenced within the applicable period, if the service is found to be consumed in Egypt, or if documentation is missing. Penalties include retrospective VAT assessment at 14%, late-payment interest (currently 2% per month or part thereof, with minimum penalties), financial fines of up to EGP 5 million, and criminal sanctions for tax evasion under Law No. 206 of 2020. In serious cases, the tax evasion offense is punishable by imprisonment, disqualification from GAFI benefits, and nullification of export incentives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a generally convertible regime for current account transactions under the CBE Banking Law and Law No. 194 of 2020, while capital account operations require documentary support, bank verification and tax/regulatory clearance. For the EG_LLC executive-search business, service fees received from non-resident clients are current account items and may be remitted abroad after AML/KYC verification, applicable withholding of Egypt corporate income tax and participant tax compliance. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation to non-resident shareholders are permitted under the Investment Law and CBE foreign-investment regulations provided the LLC submits audited financial statements, settles all taxes, and obtains the relevant CBE investment attestation. Practical hurdles arise mainly from hard-currency liquidity in the Egyptian banking market, documentary delays in CBE reporting and periodic manual review of large inbound foreign receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service compensation earned from non-resident clients constitutes service export revenue for Egyptian foreign-exchange purposes. In accordance with CBE regulatory guidance, the full amount of such service export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt through a licensed bank within 90 days from the invoice date or, if later, final delivery of the executive-search service. A mandatory conversion ratio of 15% of each service-export receipt applies by default; that portion must be converted to Egyptian pounds at the prevailing interbank rate, and the remaining 85% may be retained in the LLC's foreign-currency account for approved transactional purposes. The CBE may allow a lower conversion ratio or a temporary waiver upon documented hedging, reinvestment or financing needs.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for peripheral collections only. Offshore digital EMI services such as Wise and Payoneer are not licensed by the CBE to open EGP-denominated business accounts, effect domestic clearing in Egypt, or provide statutory account evidence for Egyptian corporate tax and FX filings. Such EMIs may be used as an auxiliary collection channel for low-value initial retainers in hard currency, but the EG_LLC must route all statutory-exposure settlement, export proceeds and shareholder distributions through a CBE-licensed bank. Legal constraint: under the Egyptian Banking Law, an onshore entity's business treasury and outward payments must be conducted through an authorized Egyptian bank; foreign EMI balances do not benefit from Egyptian depositor protection or CBE oversight.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A foreign parent or shareholder capital injection must be transmitted through a CBE-licensed bank using SWIFT MT103 with the clear purpose code for FDI equity participation and a reference to the LLC's foreign-currency account. The remitting bank must include the full legal name, UBO identification where required, and a payment narrative referencing the Articles of Association and board resolution approving the contribution. The receiving Egyptian bank will perform sanctions screening, AML KYC verification, and CBE investor-registration checks, then issue an inward remittance advice and credit the proceeds to the LLC's foreign-currency account. If the injection is made in Egyptian pounds directly, the transfer should use the same FDI purpose code and the bank should complete CBE inward investment reporting so that future dividend repatriation is legitimate.For operating trade settlements under executive-search contracts, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 bank-to-bank payment against a valid commercial invoice and retainer agreement. For domestic payments to subcontractors and placement consultants, CBE-cleared EGP transfers or EGP account-to-account credits are appropriate. Commercial letters of credit should not be used for intangible headhunting services; they are only relevant if the LLC imports physical recruitment or IT hardware, in which case a documentary LC requires full import documentation, shipping evidence, and CBE-form-specific review. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of net taxable profits under the Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company are generally subject to 10% withholding tax. A 5% rate may apply to qualifying dividends related to listed shares or listed securities under the applicable statutory conditions. Treaty rates may reduce the rate where the recipient qualifies as the beneficial owner and all procedural requirements are met.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and tax payment are generally due within four months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year company, the ordinary deadline is 30 April of the following year. A company using an approved non-calendar financial year must apply the four-month rule by reference to its own year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Taxpayer identification section Tax registration number, legal name, legal form, registered address, tax office, activity code, contact details, and return period. A non-resident shareholder or parent is not substituted for the Egyptian LLC. Report the Egyptian LLC's own tax registration details and disclose non-resident ownership and related-party relationships where requested. Late or non-filing may trigger administrative penalties under the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 206 of 2020, generally including monetary penalties that increase for repeated or serious failures. Failure to submit a required return may attract a penalty commonly ranging from approximately EGP 3,000 to EGP 50,000, with higher penalties for repeated violations or specified serious breaches. Late payment attracts additional tax calculated under the statutory late-payment formula, generally linked to the Central Bank credit and discount rate plus the statutory margin and calculated by month or part of a month; it is not normally expressed as a simple fixed daily surcharge. Material understatement, concealment, false records, or deliberate tax evasion can create criminal exposure in addition to tax, interest, and administrative penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing documentation requirements generally apply where related-party transactions exceed the applicable ETA threshold. The commonly applied threshold for Local File documentation is EGP 8 million per related-party transaction category during the relevant fiscal year, subject to the current executive regulations and ETA guidance. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report may additionally be required for Egyptian members of multinational enterprise groups meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, commonly associated with EGP 3 billion or its prescribed equivalent for the reporting group. Thresholds, filing dates, and format requirements must be verified for the relevant tax year.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Nevertheless, substance is relevant under transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance principles, beneficial-ownership analysis, permanent-establishment rules, deductibility provisions, and treaty application. An executive-search LLC should maintain Egyptian management authority, personnel or demonstrable operational capacity, client and candidate records, office or suitable operating infrastructure, local decision-making, bank and accounting controls, contracts, evidence of service delivery, and commercially rational intercompany arrangements. A company with no meaningful functions, assets, risks, or local decision-making may face denial of deductions, transfer-pricing adjustments, treaty-relief denial, permanent-establishment recharacterization, or sham-transaction allegations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, additional tax, interest, administrative penalties, and potential criminal prosecution under the Egyptian Income Tax Law and Unified Tax Procedures Law. Tax-evasion offenses may result in imprisonment and fines, with the amount depending on the offense, tax loss, repetition, and judicial findings.

Director personal liability: Managers, authorized signatories, accountants, and other responsible persons may incur personal criminal liability where they participated in, directed, approved, or knowingly facilitated the violation. Civil recovery may include unpaid tax, penalties, interest, and restitution. Travel restrictions or asset measures may be imposed through applicable judicial or enforcement procedures; they are not automatic merely because a person is a director.
Unregistered activity and non-filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines for non-registration, non-filing, inaccurate registration data, or late filing; tax assessment based on available information; late-payment additional tax; and possible escalation for repeated or deliberate conduct.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may be personally exposed to statutory penalties for acts or omissions attributable to the representative. Directors do not automatically become liable for all company tax debts solely by holding office, but personal exposure increases where there is consent, participation, fraud, obstruction, or failure to comply with a specific legal duty.
Improper withholding-tax administration

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the tax that should have been withheld, additional tax and interest, filing penalties, and potential penalties for inaccurate or missing withholding returns. The payer may bear the economic cost if it failed to deduct the tax from the payment.

Director personal liability: Authorized representatives and responsible finance personnel may face personal liability where the failure was deliberate, fraudulent, or specifically attributable to them. Treaty documents obtained after payment may not automatically cure an earlier failure to withhold.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to Egyptian taxable income, denial of unsupported deductions, additional tax, interest, documentation penalties, and possible corresponding-adjustment disputes with the foreign jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal exposure if they knowingly approved fabricated documentation, sham arrangements, or deliberate tax evasion. Ordinary transfer-pricing disagreement generally creates corporate assessment exposure rather than automatic director imprisonment.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reallocation of income, denial of deductions, permanent-establishment or beneficial-ownership findings, penalties for inaccurate filings, and potential sham-transaction or tax-evasion prosecution where intent and deception are established.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil restitution, enforcement against assets, and criminal liability for deliberate concealment or fraudulent arrangements. Travel bans or imprisonment require the relevant statutory and judicial basis and are not automatic consequences of low substance alone.
Value-added tax and invoicing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, denial of input-tax credits, late-payment charges, administrative penalties, electronic-invoicing sanctions, and potential criminal consequences for deliberate false invoicing or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The company is ordinarily the primary debtor, but responsible officers and signatories may face personal sanctions where they knowingly issued false invoices, obstructed inspection, or participated in deliberate VAT evasion.
Payroll, social insurance, and employment misclassification

Statutory sanction: Back payroll tax, social-insurance contributions, penalties, interest, employment-law assessments, and possible sanctions for false payroll records or non-registration.

Director personal liability: Responsible managers may be personally liable for deliberate concealment, false payroll records, or failure to make required deductions. Company-level liability remains primary for ordinary arrears.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership failures

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting consequences, account restrictions, confiscation or asset-freezing measures, administrative sanctions, and criminal penalties under Egyptian anti-money-laundering legislation for money laundering or related offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, beneficial owners, and employees may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or failure to perform legally required controls. Imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and travel restrictions depend on the offense and judicial process.
Unlicensed recruitment or labor-supply activity

Statutory sanction: Regulatory closure, license suspension or cancellation, administrative fines, disgorgement or restitution, and possible criminal sanctions under applicable labor and recruitment legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly operate without authorization may face personal fines or criminal liability. Corporate limited liability does not protect a person from liability for their own unlawful conduct.
Obstruction of tax inspection or records destruction

Statutory sanction: Separate administrative or criminal penalties, adverse assessment based on available information, additional tax, and loss of evidentiary credibility.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and any person responsible for obstruction may face personal fines or imprisonment where provided by law. Corporate status does not shield an individual who destroys records or obstructs an investigation.
