Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate EG_LLC with GAFI One-Stop-Shop and obtain the Commercial Register certificate. Step 2: File the Income Tax file opening form (Form 202) at the competent ETA tax office to obtain the Tax Card (TIN). Step 3: Apply for VAT registration under Law No. 67/2016 and obtain a separate VAT Registration Number (mandatory within 60 days of exceeding EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies; voluntary registration permitted below threshold). Step 4: Onboard to the ETA e-invoicing platform: register authorized signatories, obtain MFA credentials and digital signing certificates, and validate XML/JSON invoice schema. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — opaque resident taxpayer. EG_LLC is subject to Egyptian corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended. No pass-through treatment; shareholders are taxed separately on distributions. Non-resident entities are taxed on Egypt-source income only. Not within a tax-exempt zone regime.

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or hybrid entity classification election; statutory legal form is conclusive for tax classification. Available elections include: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the mandatory threshold; (ii) selection of the fiscal year with ETA approval; (iii) simplified quarterly VAT registration and filing for non-resident digital-supply suppliers; (iv) Advance Pricing Agreement applications for transfer-pricing certainty. Hybrid mismatch elective regimes under OECD BEPS Action 2 have not been enacted in Egypt.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies. Voluntary registration: available for taxable persons below the mandatory threshold. Non-resident suppliers of digital services (including streaming and in-app downloadable content) are subject to a simplified ETA registration and quarterly filing regime when their taxable supplies consumed in Egypt exceed the prescribed threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of services are zero-rated under Article 13 of VAT Law No. 67/2016 where the service is rendered to a non-resident recipient having no permanent establishment in Egypt and the service is performed and consumed outside Egypt. Voice-streaming and audio-chat revenues derived through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing qualify as exported services when the applicable storefront and billing territory are outside Egypt and proceeds are received in foreign currency. Egyptian-storefront sales are subject to 14% VAT (platform merchant-of-record collection may apply).

  • Customs Export Declaration (statutory for goods; for pure digital services the ETA accepts zero-rated service export invoices and annual digital-service export declarations in lieu of customs filing)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (goods) — replaced for streaming exports by Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console payout reports evidencing digital delivery to non-resident storefronts and gross sales per territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian bank, or equivalent FX credit advice, evidencing hard-currency settlement from Apple/Google credited to an Egyptian bank account

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices are transmitted in real time to the ETA focal-clearance system via RESTful APIs in XML/JSON; each invoice receives a unique UUID and QR code and is cleared before legal issuance. B2C in-app transactions through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing must be supported by e-receipts where the end user is in the Egyptian storefront. Non-compliance suspends invoice clearing and attracts penalties of EGP 20,000–100,000, potential business closure, and revocation of e-invoicing privileges.

The ETA may audit zero-rating claims within the statutory assessment period; deficient substantiation triggers reversal of the 0% rate, assessment of 14% VAT, a monthly late-payment addition of 2% of the unpaid VAT under Article 90 of Law No. 67/2016, and fines of EGP 5,000–50,000 for documentation violations. Knowingly false export documentation constitutes tax evasion under Law No. 177 of 2022, carrying imprisonment and a fine of double to five times the evaded tax, cancellation of VAT registration, and inadmissibility of the purported inward remittance as export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is not freely convertible in the manner of a fully open capital-account currency. Current-account payments, including legitimate imports, exported services, software and technology payments, dividends, interest, royalties, and other business remittances, are generally processed through licensed Egyptian banks subject to documentary evidence, sanctions screening, tax compliance, and available foreign currency liquidity. Capital-account transactions, including foreign investment, capital reductions, shareholder loans, and intercompany funding, require enhanced documentation and may require GAFI, CBE, bank, corporate-registry, or sector-specific approvals. A foreign-owned LLC may generally receive foreign capital and remit distributable profits, but remittance is not automatic: the bank normally requires audited or approved financial statements, shareholder or board resolutions, tax-clearance or tax-compliance evidence, evidence that the profits are legally distributable, proof of payment of applicable Egyptian withholding tax, and evidence of the original investment or funding trail. Dividend and service-fee remittances can be delayed by foreign-currency availability, correspondent-bank controls, incomplete beneficial-owner information, inconsistent commercial invoices, transfer-pricing concerns, sanctions screening, or unresolved tax filings. Mobile-app receipts from Apple and Google are cross-border service or platform receipts and should be supported by platform statements, contractual terms, settlement reports, bank advices, and accounting records. No assumption should be made that local revenues may be converted or transferred offshore without bank review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt does not generally impose a universal statutory requirement that all export proceeds be surrendered to the banking system at a fixed mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters are expected to use licensed banks for foreign-exchange transactions and to satisfy applicable customs, banking, and trade-documentation requirements. For goods, export proceeds and customs records must be capable of reconciliation, and the collecting bank may require evidence of shipment, invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and receipt of funds. For digital services and app-platform receipts, the company should retain platform contracts, monthly settlement statements, invoices or equivalent revenue records, remittance advices, and evidence of the underlying service. Applicable CBE instructions, bank procedures, customs rules, and future emergency measures may impose reporting, repatriation, or documentary requirements; therefore, no fixed statutory repatriation deadline or mandatory conversion percentage should be recorded without confirmation from the relationship bank for the relevant transaction. Foreign currency may generally be retained in an authorized foreign-currency account, subject to bank policy and applicable CBE rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020. The CBE regulates licensed banks, foreign-exchange dealers, payment systems, payment service providers, and foreign-exchange transactions. The Egyptian Customs Authority, the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), and the Egyptian Tax Authority also control documentation relevant to cross-border capital, trade, and tax remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to Low for primary treasury banking and Low for unlicensed offshore EMI substitution. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully provide Egyptian payment, deposit-taking, local collection, local acquiring, or domestic settlement services merely because the company holds an offshore account. Payment services in Egypt are regulated under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and related CBE rules; local payment acceptance, wallet services, acquiring, payment aggregation, and domestic settlement generally require a CBE-licensed bank or payment-system/payment-service provider. Offshore EMIs may be used for limited corporate treasury functions where the Egyptian bank, the EMI, and the correspondent banks accept the structure, but the company must preserve a licensed Egyptian bank account for statutory payments, payroll, taxes, local expenses, capital documentation, and regulated FX transactions. Apple and Google settlement arrangements require reconciliation to the Egyptian entity's books and may be challenged if the payee entity, contracting entity, tax residence, or beneficial owner is inconsistent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity through a CBE-regulated Egyptian bank into a designated foreign-currency or capital account using the exact legal name of the Egyptian LLC and the originating shareholder's account. Before transfer, provide the bank with the incorporation documents, commercial-register extract, GAFI investment file, shareholder resolution, capital-increase or funding agreement, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, expected amount and currency, and intended purpose. The bank should assign the applicable CBE or internal transaction-purpose classification; no invented purpose code should be used where the bank has not confirmed the code. The SWIFT message should identify the investment or shareholder-loan purpose accurately and avoid generic descriptions such as 'consulting' or 'payment.' For a capital increase, complete the required corporate and registry filings and preserve the bank certificate evidencing receipt of capital. For a shareholder loan, execute the loan agreement before funding, document interest and maturity, analyze Egyptian withholding tax and transfer pricing, and confirm whether any GAFI, CBE, registry, or bank notification is required. Reconcile the incoming amount to the accounting ledger, foreign-investment records, and beneficial-owner file. Do not route capital through Apple, Google, unrelated affiliates, personal accounts, cash, or unlicensed payment intermediaries.Use the Egyptian LLC's licensed-bank account and pay the named contractual beneficiary through a bank-supported SWIFT wire, normally an MT103 or its current ISO 20022 equivalent, with a precise payment purpose, invoice number, contract reference, beneficiary banking details, and charge allocation. For higher-value or documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit issued or advised through a licensed bank, with invoice, packing list, transport document, insurance document, certificate of origin, customs documentation, and inspection documents where applicable. Documentary collection, open-account settlement, and advance payment may be available subject to bank policy, counterparty risk, import and customs rules, and CBE documentation requirements. For software, cloud, hosting, app-store, advertising, content, and professional-service payments, retain the agreement, invoice, proof of performance, tax analysis, withholding-tax evidence, and beneficiary KYC; these payments are not automatically 'trade imports' merely because they are cross-border. The bank may request import or service documentation, tax forms, sanctions screening, and evidence of foreign-currency availability. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to Low for primary treasury banking and Low for unlicensed offshore EMI substitution. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully provide Egyptian payment, deposit-taking, local collection, local acquiring, or domestic settlement services merely because the company holds an offshore account. Payment services in Egypt are regulated under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and related CBE rules; local payment acceptance, wallet services, acquiring, payment aggregation, and domestic settlement generally require a CBE-licensed bank or payment-system/payment-service provider. Offshore EMIs may be used for limited corporate treasury functions where the Egyptian bank, the EMI, and the correspondent banks accept the structure, but the company must preserve a licensed Egyptian bank account for statutory payments, payroll, taxes, local expenses, capital documentation, and regulated FX transactions. Apple and Google settlement arrangements require reconciliation to the Egyptian entity's books and may be challenged if the payee entity, contracting entity, tax residence, or beneficial owner is inconsistent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5 million: aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions trigger a mandatory Local File. EGP 50 million: aggregate annual related-party transactions trigger a mandatory Master File. These thresholds apply under ETA Resolutions No. 51/2019 and subsequent amending resolutions; the annual CIT return must include a transfer pricing disclosure schedule identifying controlled transactions, counterparties, methods selected and the arm's length outcome.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute comparable to GCC regimes. Nevertheless, the ETA applies substance-over-form principles and a general anti-avoidance rule under Article 111 of Law No. 91/2005, requiring that the Egyptian LLC demonstrate real commercial activity, local management, staff and independent decision-making. A foreign streaming principal with significant local digital activity, servers or dependent agents may be deemed to have a taxable permanent establishment even absent a registered branch. Free-zone subsidiaries must maintain genuine activity to justify continued 0% CIT treatment and may lose the benefit upon a sham-substance finding.
