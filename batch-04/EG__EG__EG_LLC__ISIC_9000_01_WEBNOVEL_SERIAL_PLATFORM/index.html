Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (Inland Revenue Department for income tax; VAT Department for value-added tax; e-invoicing system access via https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the ETA unified registration portal (or in-person service center) before commencing taxable activities. Non-resident entities without a permanent establishment in Egypt must appoint a resident tax representative/fiscal agent who files VAT and income tax returns. Required documents include certified incorporation certificate (legalized), passport copies of directors, memorandum of association, board resolution appointing local representative, lease agreement for any Egyptian office, and proof of bank account. The ETA issues the VAT certificate and Tax Card (SIN number) within 10–15 business days. Electronic registration is mandatory for entities created after January 1, 2021; a temporary registration number is issued immediately upon submission of the preliminary form.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: Worldwide income for Egyptian-resident LLCs; foreign entities are taxed on Egyptian-source income under the general rules, and must register for VAT if making taxable supplies in Egypt regardless of permanent establishment status. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: Worldwide income for Egyptian-resident LLCs; foreign entities are taxed on Egyptian-source income under the general rules, and must register for VAT if making taxable supplies in Egypt regardless of permanent establishment status. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable to the standard LLC structure, unless the entity is licensed as a free-zone company inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) or a public free zone, in which case certain VAT/CIT exemptions apply. The described standard LLC is presumed to operate outside free-zone regimes.

Available election forms

No check-the-box entity classification election is available in Egypt; the taxation classification follows the legal form. A Limited Liability Company is always a separate taxable person for Egyptian Corporate Tax (currently 22.5% under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended) and is a VAT payer if the threshold is exceeded. Hybrid mismatch elections are not separately available; the entity may choose to apply the modernized thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing rules voluntarily, but no affirmative election is required. A foreign LLC may elect to be treated as a branch of its foreign parent for tax purposes, but that does not alter entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 for goods and services; EGP 1,000,000 annual turnover threshold for simplified registration (Law No. 67 of 2016, as amended). Non-resident digital service providers are required to register for VAT from the first Egyptian pound of taxable revenue without threshold if they supply electronic services to non-registered persons in Egypt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated for VAT (Article 22 of Law No. 67 of 2016). Digital web novel platform services are treated as exported services when supplied to non-resident persons outside Egypt who are not VAT-registered in Egypt; must be provided directly and digitally from Egypt, and the consideration must be received in foreign currency. The service must be actually performed, no Egyptian consumption, and the recipient must have no establishment in Egypt. Supporting contracts, end-user IP addresses, payment gateway records, and annex of subscriber-country reports are required to evidence the foreign nexus. A written shipment attestation is sufficient for goods; for digital services a certificate of the tax representative and service delivery logs are acceptable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form 1/12 or electronic export declaration through NAFIZA)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Electronic delivery manifest (for digital exports, a server log extraction report is accepted as delivery evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (issued by an Egyptian licensed bank proving receipt of foreign currency proceeds)
  • Original or certified copy of the export contract
  • Foreign currency transfer receipt from an Egyptian bank, showing the actual value repatriated

Mandatory e-invoicing (Markaz) is phased-in since November 15, 2020, and e-receipting since July 2022. All VAT-registered taxpayers must integrate their accounting/invoicing systems with the ETA real-time validation platform using government APIs (JSON/SOAP). Approved integration models: server-to-server (XML submission), ETL file upload, or enterprise service bus. The ETA validates every invoice against the VAT rules, assigns a unique Max unique ID and two-dimensional barcode (QR), and returns an acceptance code; rejected invoices are blocked and trigger tax audit flags. Penalties for non-compliance: EGP 50,000 to EGP 2,000,000; repeated non-submission can lead to suspension of the tax card and denial of input VAT credits.

The ETA may reassess zero-rating claims up to five years from the date of the tax return, and up to eight years in case of fraud, intentional evasion, or non-substantiation. Late or non-repatriation of export proceeds triggers automatic VAT reversal plus penalties under Customs and Tax Laws: a VAT clawback of the claimed input VAT, an additional 100% tax penalty on the tax due (up to 300% for willful evasion Law No. 91 of 2005, Article 133), and interest of 1% per month on the underpaid amount (with a minimum of 0.5% per month). Criminal fines for false invoicing range from EGP 100,000 to EGP 5,000,000; prison terms may apply for repeated violation. The tax card may also be suspended, triggering a corporate tax audit and freezing of bank accounts.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a liberalized current-account regime for trade in goods and services, subscription royalties, and cross-border digital-content fees, provided the underlying transaction is evidenced and routed through a CBE-authorized dealer bank. The capital account is liberalized for inward foreign direct investment and properly registered shareholder loans, but outward capital movements such as dividend remittances, share-sale proceeds, capital reduction, and shareholder-loan repayments require documented source-of-funds, audited accounts, GAFI/CBE registration, tax clearance, and bank-level compliance approval. Profit remittances are legally permitted once the Egyptian limited liability company has valid commercial registration, a corporate tax card, audited financial statements showing distributable reserves, a shareholder resolution declaring the dividend, and a clean Egyptian tax position. In practice, banks may refer borderline transactions to their AML/FX compliance committees for escalation, which creates timing rather than substantive blockage. The unified exchange-rate regime adopted by CBE permits banks to price EGP freely, so licensed corporate transactions receive market-conform settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds derived from goods or services, including the Egyptian LLC's serialized web-fiction subscriptions sold through Apple App Store and Google Play, must be repatriated to Egypt through a CBE-licensed bank within the CBE-prescribed payment and collection period, which in normal documentary and electronic-service flows is the invoice maturity or contractual payout cycle and in no case materially exceeds the maximum period established by CBE instructions for trade and service export receivables. There is no permanent mandatory percentage conversion to EGP under the current unified-rate framework; the exporter may retain foreign currency in a licensed foreign-currency account, use it for permitted external obligations, or convert it to EGP in the interbank market at the prevailing bank rate. The mandatory conversion ratio applicable at the reporting date is 0%, unless CBE adopts a temporary prudential circular requiring partial conversion. The bank will classify the receipt under the balance-of-payments purpose code for exports of services and will record the receipt in the exporter's CBE foreign-exchange file; overdue repatriation, if unjustified, may expose the entity to CBE penalties, bank counterparty downgrade, or restrictions on future outward remittances.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting through its Banking Supervision and Foreign Exchange Operations functions under Law No. 194 of 2020, the Central Banking and Banking System Law, and the CBE Foreign Exchange Circulars; the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) is the administrative counterpart for inbound foreign investment registration and capital repatriation evidence.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a substitute for a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account; Medium as a supplementary non-resident collection gateway. Offshore digital EMIs and e-money institutions are not authorized to provide core corporate treasury and statutory capital accounts in Egypt. A CBE-licensed bank remains mandatory for share capital injections, Egyptian tax payments, statutory audit trails, profit remittances, and documentary clearing. An offshore EMI may lawfully collect inbound app-store revenue outside Egypt, but those balances must be swept via SWIFT MT103 into the Egyptian LLC's licensed bank account on a regular cycle; any use of an EMI as the primary treasury rail will undermine GAFI/CBE compliance and audit protection. Legal constraints arise under CBE's exclusive licensing powers over payment services and Egypt's capital-control mapping; the Egyptian operating entity should never maintain corporate liquidity on an unlicensed digital wallet outside Egypt.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The corporate treasury should execute outbound disbursements through the CBE-licensed bank's electronic banking platform using SWIFT MT103 for cross-border payments, and MT202/COVER where interbank transfers require a separate cover instruction. For large procurement or content-licensing transactions, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit is available where counterparties demand documentary security, though ordinary author royalties and platform infrastructure costs are more efficiently settled on open account with invoice and service confirmation. For domestic EGP vendor and statutory payments, the LLC may use the Meeza instant-payment network and EG-ACH domestic clearing rails to reduce friction and traceable records. Every outbound FX transfer must be accompanied by the correct CBE purpose code, e.g., 'royalties and license fees', 'management and professional services', 'software/cloud infrastructure', or 'distribution of profit to non-resident shareholder', plus the underlying invoice, contract, withholding-tax certificate, and the LLC's corporate tax card.Onshore deliverable Egyptian-pound foreign-exchange forwards with a CBE-licensed authorized dealer bank, limited to the notional amount and tenor of a documented export receivable, import payable, dividend exposure, or shareholder loan.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on EGP where a non-resident treasury counterparty requires settlement outside Egypt; the hedge must be economically matched to the LLC's repatriation timing and disclosed in financial statements.Cross-currency swaps between the Egyptian LLC and a bank licensed or approved by CBE, with physical exchange of EGP and freely convertible currency at determined intervals to manage re-invoicing and liquidity risk.Plain-vanilla FX swaps between EGP and USD/EUR with an authorized dealer bank for tenor-level liquidity management, provided the transactions do not introduce speculation and are supported by the LLC's board-approved treasury policy.
