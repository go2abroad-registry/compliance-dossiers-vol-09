Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to LLC formation under Law No. 159 of 1981; however, the capital stated in the Articles must be fully subscribed at formation. For REIT portfolio activities, the Financial Regulatory Authority requires a minimum paid-up capital of EGP 10 million (approximately USD 200,000) evidenced by a bank capital-deposit certificate issued after escrow deposit in a licensed Egyptian bank.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; taxpayer portal and e-invoice clearance platform at https://tax.gov.eg and https://einvoice.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must first incorporate with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), obtain commercial registration, and then apply to the ETA for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the tax card (binding tax card). The application is filed through the ETA taxpayer portal or at the competent tax office and requires: certificate of incorporation and Memorandum of Association, commercial registration certificate, notarized board resolution appointing the local tax representative, specimen of authorized signatories, proof of registered office, opening bank certificate, and for non-resident shareholders a tax residency certificate or adequate legalized documentation. The TIN/tax card must be obtained before commencing activity or within statutory registration deadlines. VAT registration with the ETA is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed the annual threshold; the LLC must submit the ETA Form for VAT registration and receive a VAT certificate number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or general passthrough election exists under Egyptian tax law. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed at the corporate level on worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005. There is no statutory REIT passthrough election; a real estate investment trust operating as an LLC remains subject to corporate income tax. Taxpayers may elect the fiscal year and certain depreciation and inventory methods subject to ETA approval, and may elect grouped VAT registration with related taxable persons in limited circumstances. Egypt does not provide hybrid mismatch elections domestically; relief is only by treaty tie-breaker provisions where applicable.

14%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Egyptian VAT Law, exports of goods and services are zero-rated if the supplier complies with the documentary evidence requirements of the ETA. For goods, zero-rating requires actual physical export from Egypt evidenced by a customs export declaration discharged by Egyptian Customs. For services, the service must be supplied to a non-resident recipient, used and benefitted from entirely outside Egypt, and not recoverable in Egypt; services directly related to real estate located in Egypt, including leasing or rental of Egyptian property, are not zero-rated and remain subject to VAT at the standard rate where applicable. The zero-rated claim requires full foreign-exchange collection through the banking system and preservation of transport, customs, insurance, and payment documents.

  • Customs export declaration registered in the Egyptian Customs system and stamped by the discharging customs office
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR land transport document, or equivalent transport document showing export from Egypt
  • Inward remittance certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in freely convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order or sales contract matching the declared quantities and values
  • Transportation documents proving the goods left Egypt and arrived at the foreign destination, and any customs declaration at destination (where available)
  • Certificate of origin (where required by import destination or to secure preferential treatment)

Egypt enforces mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt filing through the ETA. VAT-registered businesses must issue real-time electronic invoices using ETA-certified APIs or the taxpayer portal. Every invoice/tax invoice must be submitted to the ETA clearance engine before delivery to the buyer, and a QR code and digital unique identifier must be embedded on the invoice. The e-invoice system requires structured XML with ETA-validated code; discrepancies trigger automatic rejection and blocking of the transaction read. Businesses above the threshold must integrate their accounting/ERP systems with the ETA API or use the ETA web portal. Penalties apply for late e-invoice filing, incorrect data fields, issuing paper invoices instead of e-invoices, or accepting VAT without eligible electronic documentation.

ETA and Egyptian Customs jointly audit zero-rated transactions. A failed claim, missing export proof, or failure to obtain e-invoice clearance results in immediate reversal of the zero rate and assessment of 14% VAT, late interest, and penalties. Tax evasion penalties under Law No. 67 of 2016 can range up to 50 percent of the due tax plus daily delay interest; false documentation and fraudulent e-invoice registration carry criminal exposure, imprisonment, confiscation of assets, and prohibition from contracting with government entities. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day Central Bank of Egypt period may also trigger foreign exchange violations and loss of tax incentives. The ETA may reassess invoices for up to five years after filing date for incorrect zero-rating, and the taxpayer bears the burden of proving the export through the full documentary chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the exchange-rate unification of 6 March 2024, the CBE operates a flexible, market-determined EGP/USD regime under the IMF Extended Fund Facility: the Egyptian pound is fully convertible for all current-account transactions, including dividend and profit remittances, with no quantitative ceilings for licensed-bank payments. Capital-account convertibility is conditional on documentation: inbound foreign-direct-investment equity injections must be registered with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) under Law No. 72 of 2017, using the Unified Economic Registration (UER) certificate and the CBE's inward-declaration electronic form. Profit-remittance hurdles are no longer systemic after the clearing of the 2022-2023 FX backlogs, but the distributing vehicle must present audited IFRS financial statements, a board dividend resolution, tax withholding proof (10% dividend WHT under Law No. 91 of 2005, subject to treaty relief), and traceability of the original USD injection through the same authorised dealer bank. The REIT is treated as a non-exporter generating Egyptian-pound rental income; thus current-account convertibility is unrestricted, whereas any future sale of EGP into USD must be based on a genuine underlying distribution or investment-repatriation transaction. Administrative transitory measures, including corporate FX withdrawal caps, remain subject to CBE circulars; treasury policy must monitor CBE dealing-room directives on a daily basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt's statutory export regime imposes repatriation, not surrender, of export receipts: 100% of the export value must be credited to the exporter's foreign-currency account at an Egyptian licensed bank within 180 days from the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date unless an extended collection tenor has been approved by the CBE. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%; the pre-2016 surrender bands (100%/75%/50%) were abolished with the 2016 float, and the March 2024 reform permits free retention of the proceeds in FCY accounts with unrestricted use for current-account payments. The ISIC 6499 REIT does not engage in merchandise export; incidental service receipts, such as property-management fees or administrative recharges collected from non-resident tenants or sponsors, must be characterised in the treasury ledger as non-export service income to avoid triggering the 180-day ranking or the CBE services-repatriation declaration. If the REIT ever sells a stabilised property to a non-resident buyer, the sale proceeds are classified as capital-account FDI disinvestment and are subject to the GAFI repatriation certificate regime rather than the export-proceeds rules.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Al-Bank al-Markazi al-Masri; the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) exercises supplementary licensing and anti-money-laundering oversight for the real-estate investment vehicle under its capital-market remit.

Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the assessed tax per month of delay, up to a maximum of 20% of the tax due, plus a fixed fine of up to EGP 500 for administrative failure. Late payment triggers a separate surcharge of 1% per month (or fraction) of the unpaid amount, also capped at 20%. Intentional failure to file may result in criminal fines equal to 50% of the evaded tax, and in egregious cases imprisonment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions in the fiscal year triggers mandatory Egyptian Local File documentation. The Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 2,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not have a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute. Nevertheless, the General Anti-Avoidance Rules under Article 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005 permit the Egyptian Tax Authority to disregard or recharacterise structures lacking genuine commercial substance. A REIT must maintain a physical office in Egypt, substantiate effective board management and investment decisions, employ qualified local property managers, and keep real estate books in Egypt; otherwise, treaty entitlements can be denied and profits re-allocated to a deemed Egyptian PE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Suppression)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction: imprisonment for up to 2 years, fine equal to 100% of the evaded tax plus a public penalty of up to 50% of the assessed deficiency; in serious cases the ETA may also impose a monthly late-payment surcharge and administrative fine of up to 100% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the evaded corporate tax if they signed false returns or directed the falsification. They may face civil restitution of the tax deficiency plus prejudgment interest, a travel ban on leaving Egypt, and personal imprisonment for up to 2 years after final criminal judgment.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The GAAR orders recharacterisation of the entity as a domestic PE; all income is taxed at the 22.5% head office rate, treaty relief is denied, and interest on the under-assessed amount accrues from the original filing date. A civil penalty of 40% of the re-allocated tax may be imposed for abusive filings.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the directors funnelled payments without commercial substance; directors are jointly liable for the full tax deficiency and face asset freezing orders and a five-year ban from managing any Egyptian entity.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The ETA makes a primary TP adjustment to an arm's length profit; sloppy documentation leads to a 10% penalty on the overstated deductions. The unpaid amount bears default interest at 1% per month from the original due date, with no cap on the TP adjustment itself.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's-length transactions or submitted false TP documentation are personally liable for the adjusted corporate tax and may be sued for civil damages by minority investors; in aggravated cases, criminal penalties apply for falsified records.
AML / Unreported Foreign Transfers

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80 of 2002 (Anti-Money Laundering), fines range from EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000 per violation plus confiscation of the underlying funds. Repeat offenders are exposed to imprisonment up to 5 years, and the entity's trading licence is suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are criminal employees under the AML law; they face joint and several civil liability for the unreported amounts, a personal travel ban, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment up to 5 years if money laundering is proven.
