Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election in Egypt. A foreign LLC may elect to register as a branch of the foreign parent or as a separate Egyptian subsidiary; wholly-owned Egyptian subsidiaries may apply for consolidated/group tax filing under Article 61 of the Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91/2005. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available under domestic law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies to movable goods physically exported outside Egypt evidenced by customs export declarations, and to services rendered to non-residents or directly in connection with exported goods, provided legal ownership passes to the recipient abroad and consideration is receivable in foreign currency.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from an Egyptian licensed bank

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing with ETA clearance for all B2B transactions. Invoices must be transmitted via ETA-approved APIs in XML/JSON format, digitally signed, and stamped with a unique QR code. ETA's e-receipt system applies to B2C transactional sales. Non-integrated suppliers are subject to penalties and inability to deduct input VAT.

ETA denies zero rating where export documentation is incomplete or misdeclared, clawing back all VAT waived plus late penalties of up to 100% of unpaid tax. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract criminal prosecution under Egyptian tax fraud provisions, monetary fines, and potential debarment from e-invoicing/export facilities. ETA matches e-export declarations against customs data and bank foreign currency credit records in automated audit sweeps.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-float exchange rate regime with partial- but administratively liberalized- convertibility. Current-account transactions (trade in goods, services, royalties, technical fees) are fully convertible upon documentary verification by CBE-authorised banks under Article 54 of Law No. 194 of 2020. Capital-account transactions, specifically equity injection, intercompany loans, and liquidation proceeds, require either prior CBE approval or registration under Egyptian Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Profit and dividend remittance by non-resident shareholders is legally permissible but operationally conditioned on audited financial statements, proof of corporate tax settlement, an ETA tax clearance certificate, and execution through an authorised bank at the prevailing interbank rate; temporary FX liquidity shortages can introduce settlement delays but do not constitute a formal legal prohibition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory percentage conversion ratio into EGP; the earlier surrender regime was rescinded as part of the 2016 and subsequent CBE exchange reforms. Exporters must repatriate all export proceeds into a foreign-currency account held with an authorised Egyptian bank within 180 days from the date of shipment (airway bill or bill of lading), subject to CBE-granted extensions. Collected proceeds may be retained in USD/EUR, applied to pay import suppliers, or voluntarily sold to the bank. Failure to credit proceeds within the prescribed timeline constitutes a CBE compliance breach, resulting in possible restrictions on import financing, referral to the CBE violations committee, and rejection of future export-credit facilities for the defaulting entity.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), supported by the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for investor registration and the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) for tax-clearance-linked foreign remittances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore fintech and electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed by the CBE and cannot hold Capital Escrow Accounts, statutory capital, export proceeds, or execute import settlements for an Egyptian onshore entity; Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves deposit-taking, payment, and money-service activity for CBE-licensed banks and licensed Egyptian payment service providers. A UAE or EU EMI may be used only for incidental operational expenditure (software subscriptions, digital tooling) and must never be presented as the primary treasury channel; any substantial cross-border treasury flow passing through an unlicensed EMI would trigger CBE exchange-control and AML violations, resulting in account freezing and administrative penalties against the LLC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection from the foreign parent must be routed exclusively through a CBE-authorised Egyptian bank. The parent initiates a SWIFT MT103 wire with the beneficiary name identical to the LLC's Commercial Registry entry, references the GAFI project number, and states the balance-of-payments purpose code for Direct Investment in Egypt (equity capital / share capital increase). The receiving bank lands the funds into a Capital Escrow Account and performs UBO verification, tax-card validation, and commercial-register screening before releasing funds to the LLC operating account. The bank then issues a Certificate of Inward Remittance which the LLC must preserve for subsequent profit repatriation, debt-to-equity conversions, and liquidation clearances. All underlying corporate documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum of association, board resolution, parent financial statements, passport copies, and Commercial Registry extract) require notarisation, legalisation/apostille, and certified Arabic translation before first drawdown.For the import of PLC modules, SCADA servers, MV switchgear, and substation automation hardware, the primary clearing rail is an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit confirmed by a reputable overseas correspondent bank, with settlement via SWIFT MT103 / MT202 cover. Services, engineering fees, and low-value subcontract disbursements below the bank's LC threshold are paid through direct SWIFT MT103 operations from the LLC's USD or EGP account. The CBE requires fully documented invoices, and all goods entering Egypt require customs Form 4 / ACI pre-registration referencing the underlying trade contract. A dedicated trade file per transaction must include bill of lading or air waybill, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, packing list, supplier invoice, and site acceptance documentation to satisfy both banking due diligence and future ETA audit queries.Onshore USD/EGP commercial forward contracts, tenor up to 180 days, strictly back-to-back with verified import or export documentation and not for speculative use.USD/EGP exchange-traded futures contracts on the Egyptian Exchange (EGX), settled through an approved brokerage and used to hedge confirmed procurement orders.Cross-currency swaps between EGP and USD executed by a CBE-authorised bank treasury desk for project-financing cash-flow match, subject to CBE no-objection and underlying trade/investment exposure.FX swaps (spot purchase with simultaneous forward re-sale) employed solely to rebalance working capital between EGP and USD across committed supply-chain obligations.Offshore USD/EGP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not permissible for onshore Egyptian corporates; excluded by CBE settlement rules requiring actual delivery of funds through the onshore interbank market.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended, applicable to juridical persons including an Egyptian Limited Liability Company engaged in electrical installation works (ISIC 4321).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final dividend withholding tax under Article 56 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 on profit distributions by an Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders; the 10% is applied at source before remittance at the time of dividend approval.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 aggregate value of related-party transactions in a tax year triggers mandatory preparation of the TP-1 declaration attached to Form 19, full Local File documentation and, for group-wide reporting, a Master File (Ministerial Decree No. 1202 of 2018 as amended).

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted standalone Economic Substance Regulations comparable to Gulf Cooperation Council regimes; however, general anti-avoidance rules under Article 99 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, substance-over-form doctrines and transfer pricing provisions empower the ETA to disregard artificial or sham transactions; entitlement to treaty benefits requires demonstrable economic activity, personnel, premises and operational substance in the residence jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of not less than 6 months and up to 5 years, plus a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000 under Articles 133–134 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005; mandatory recovery of evaded tax, a 35% additional assessment premium and a 2% monthly late-payment surcharge; bank account freezes and precautionary asset seizure by the ETA.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax shortfall, fines and surcharges; the ETA may impose a travel ban by placing the director on the embarkation embargo list and may attach personal assets where the corporate veil is pierced on grounds of fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment by the ETA applying best-judgment methodologies with a 35% additional premium and 2% monthly surcharge; administrative fines of up to EGP 100,000 under the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 25 of 2020; denial of deductions for undocumented intra-group charges.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution of the underpaid corporate tax and penalties; where TP documentation is knowingly falsified, criminal liability for tax evasion attaches, including imprisonment, travel bans and disqualification from board positions.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Disregard or recharacterization of the arrangement under the Article 99 GAAR; denial of DTA benefits; re-assessment of taxable income at 22.5% with the 35% additional premium; cancellation of the tax card, commercial register dissolution and fraud penalties under Companies Law No. 159 of 2020.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced when the LLC is used as a mere façade; directors become personally liable for evaded corporate tax, fines and damages, and are subject to criminal prosecution, travel bans and potential imprisonment for fraud.
Withholding Tax Fraud and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Withheld tax becomes recoverable directly from the legal representative; a 2% monthly surcharge applies; criminal embezzlement penalties apply for non-remittance; AML violations under Law No. 80 of 2002 result in imprisonment of up to 5 years, a fine of EGP 50,000 to EGP 500,000 and asset freezing measures.

Director personal liability: Directors act as statutory withholding agents and are personally liable for the unremitted WHT amounts, surcharges and criminal fines; principals face imprisonment, departure bans, asset attached execution and personal bankruptcy exposure where fraudulent non-remittance is established.
