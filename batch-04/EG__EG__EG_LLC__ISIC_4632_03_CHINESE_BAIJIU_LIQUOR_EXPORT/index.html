Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — Ministry of Finance; national portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC obtains its unified TIN through the ETA's integrated tax registration workflow: (1) submit the Unified Tax Registration Statement at the competent ETA tax office in the district of the registered office; (2) attach the legalised memorandum of association and trade registry extract, proof of office premises, board resolution appointing a local tax representative, statutory accounting records, and bank certificate evidencing the Egyptian corporate account; (3) a non-resident without a permanent establishment must appoint a licensed Egyptian fiscal representative. The ETA issues a single electronic tax card containing the TIN/VAT registration number; the taxpayer must then activate its e-invoicing account and obtain the digital stamping/QR-signing credentials before issuing any commercial invoice.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident juridical person taxed in Egypt on worldwide income at the standard corporate income tax rate; no pass-through status is available to corporate members.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists under Egyptian law, and hybrid mismatch treatment is regulated by BEPS Action 2-aligned anti-hybrid provisions without a free-form election. Available elected regimes include: (a) SME presumptive CIT on annual revenue under Law No. 22 of 2023 at progressive rates from 0% up to 15% for turnovers up to EGP 100 million, elected in lieu of the standard 22.5% accounting-based tax; (b) special investment-incentive schedules and periodic CIT exemptions for licensed SCZone and Golden License enterprises under Law 83/2002 and Investment Law 72/2017; (c) cash-basis accounting election for eligible small taxpayers; (d) formal fiscal-representative election by non-resident taxpayers for VAT and withholding purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies to any taxable person whose annual value of taxable supplies exceeds EGP 500,000 (Art. 15, VAT Law 67/2016); non-resident suppliers of digital and electronic services are subject to compulsory registration regardless of threshold through an Egyptian fiscal representative.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including Chinese baijiu spirits, are zero-rated under Art. 11 of VAT Law 67/2016 where: (a) the goods physically leave Egypt through an approved export port within the statutory shipment window; (b) the customs export declaration names the EG LLC as exporter; (c) ownership passes to a non-resident buyer under a duly executed sale contract; and (d) the sales proceeds are received through an Egyptian Authorised Dealer bank in freely convertible foreign currency. Ancillary freight, handling, insurance and commission services supplied for the exported goods are also zero-rated. The exporter recovers attributable input VAT through the streamlined ETA export-refund mechanism, subject to substantive verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Jumruki) carrying the ETA and customs exit certification
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing lawful outbound shipment from Egyptian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency credit advice issued by the Egyptian Authorised Dealer bank
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and signed sales contract/purchase order correlated line-by-line with the customs declaration
  • Letter of Credit or SWIFT payment instruments and beneficial-owner/export declaration for AML and fiscal cross-verification

A phased national mandate now covers all ETA-registered taxpayers: every B2B/B2G case invoice and applicable B2C e-receipt must be transmitted in ETA-compliant XML/JSON to the national clearance portal (https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg), pass real-time schema, VAT-ratio, counterparty-eligibility and anti-fraud validation, and receive an ETA-issued UUID, QR code and digital stamp before being recognised for the buyer's input-VAT deduction. The exporting LLC must maintain an API-enabled e-invoicing account, digital signing certificate and structured counterparty acceptance/rejection workflow; invoices lacking ETA clearance are treated as not issued for tax purposes, attract per-invoice fines typically ranging from EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000, and can trigger tax-card suspension.

The ETA automatically cross-matches customs exit data, e-invoiced VAT returns and Central Bank of Egypt FX-flow records to verify real export substance. If zero-rating lacks the required documentation or hard-currency proceeds are not credited to the Egyptian bank account within the 180-day repatriation deadline (or an approved extension), the ETA retroactively assesses VAT at 14% on the invoice value, adds monthly delay penalties of 0.5% of the assessed tax, and imposes fines of 30%–100% of the under-declared tax. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims constitute VAT-evasion offences punishable by imprisonment for up to 5 years, full clawback of refunds, customs criminal liability, disqualification of the managing officers, and suspension or blacklisting of the exporter in the ETA tax-card, customs broker and foreign-trade operator registers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange rate regime for the Egyptian Pound (EGP). Current and capital account operations are largely liberalized through CBE-licensed banks, and the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 guarantees repatriation of invested capital and profits. Practical convertibility hurdles include mandatory CBE reporting, economic substance disclosures, tax clearance certificates, delayed FX availability during periods of liquidity stress, and restrictive documentary scrutiny on remittance purposes. Profit remittance is permissible for Egyptian LLCs provided that audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance, and a board-authorized remittance resolution are presented to the maintaining bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE external trade instructions, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt and credited to the exporter's Egyptian bank account within six months from the date of the underlying shipping declaration. There is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage under the current managed float; exporters may retain foreign currency revenue in approved foreign-currency accounts, but the CBE retains authority to impose, by circular, dedicated conversion ratios or temporary surrender obligations to support foreign exchange reserves. For alcoholic beverages such as Chinese baijiu, the exporter must submit the customs export declaration, CBE receipt statement, and original shipping documents as proof of performance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Foreign Exchange, Banking Operations, and External Trade Compliance Directorate

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility rating: Egyptian law restricts full banking, e-money, and foreign exchange services to institutions licensed or expressly recognized by the CBE. Offshore fintech/EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, neobank platforms) cannot lawfully hold Egyptian clearing accounts, open local EGP accounts, or act as the primary liquidity settlement rail for an Egyptian treasury. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Transfer Pricing Local File is required where annual controlled transactions with a single related party exceed EGP 15,000,000, or when the Egyptian taxpayer has gross revenue exceeding EGP 30,000,000 and controlled transactions exceed EGP 5,000,000. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated global revenue exceeds EGP 100,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting thresholds apply separately under ETA decree.

Economic substance scope: There is no codified Economic Substance Regulation identical to the UAE ESR regime. However, by virtue of Article 33 of the Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and the Tax Procedures Law No. 174 of 2018, the ETA applies a robust substance-over-form and GAAR analysis. A Baijiu export LLC must maintain real premises, personnel, logistics contracts, supplier and customer files, board minutes, bank accounts, and decision-making records in Egypt. Where staff and management are effectively located abroad, the ETA may disregard the Egyptian entity as artificial, re-allocation profits to the foreign parent or treating the Egyptian LLC as a permanent establishment of a foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the evaded tax at 150% of the under-declared amount; criminal fine of up to EGP 500,000 and imprisonment for a term not less than five years under Article 89 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended; revocation of the tax card and customs export registration; seizure of goods and bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Courts routinely pierce the corporate veil in fraud cases: directors and finance officers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax plus penalties, their personal assets may be attached, and travel bans are issued during criminal investigation or enforcement.
Shell Company and Sham Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: ETA disallows the contract under the general anti-abuse rule, recharacterizes the transaction, and recalculates the Egyptian taxable profit at 22.5%; additional 20% withholding tax on deemed service or royalty flows may apply; monthly late penalty of 5% up to 40% is added. Criminal filing of false declarations also subjects the entity to fines of EGP 200,000 up to EGP 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the false transfer pricing declaration face personal civil liability for the additional corporate tax, disqualification from board service in Egypt, and criminal liability for submitting false official statements.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Violations of Egypt AML Law No. 80 of 2002 attract criminal fines up to EGP 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to ten years, as well as asset freezing and confiscation. The Ministry of Investment may suspend, revoke, or refuse the commercial license and export privileges.

Director personal liability: Board members and the Compliance Officer may be held personally and criminally liable where negligence or knowledge is proven; civil third-party liability is unlimited and travel bans are imposed to guarantee presence during investigation and restitution.
Unreported Cross-Border Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: The ETA will assess the unpaid withholding tax, impose a delay surcharge, and add a non-compliance fine that may reach 100% of the withheld amount. The underlying payment may be disallowed as a deductible expense in the Egyptian corporation's tax calculation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the non-whthheld remittance become personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and fines; the corporate veil is pierced to recover the amount from the directors' personal bank accounts, and a personal travel ban can be issued until full settlement.
