Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — Ministry of Finance; official taxpayer portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and digital-stamping platform: https://einvoice.eta.gov.eg; TIN validation gateway: https://id.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate at the GAFI one-stop shop and obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate (CR). Step 2 — File the ETA general tax registration (Form 1 for corporate taxpayers) within 30 days of the CR date, submitting the Articles of Association, head-office title or lease, board-resolution extract appointing the tax representative, and identity documents of shareholders and directors; the ETA issues the unified Tax Card bearing the statutory 9-digit TIN. Step 3 — Register for VAT under Law No. 67/2016 once taxable supplies exceed or are expected to exceed the EGP 500,000 mandatory threshold; the ETA then issues a separate VAT ID linked to the TIN. Step 4 — Complete e-invoicing activation by uploading the entity's digital signature certificate, validating the ERP system against the ETA's API test environment, and receiving the e-invoice clearance UUID code. Step 5 — File monthly VAT returns by the 20th of the month following each tax period, quoting the TIN and VAT ID in all filings; foreign-owned LLCs must simultaneously maintain the Central Bank-compliant FX repatriation file for non-resident fee receipts.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid-mismatch elections; an EG LLC is per se opaque and taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the corporate income tax rate. Available elections are limited to: (1) GAFI Investment Law No. 72/2017 incentive election (flat-rate tax bands, customs and VAT reliefs for qualifying projects inside designated zones); (2) consolidated income tax filing election for Egyptian group companies under Article 57 of Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 (ministerial approval); (3) SME presumptive-profit election under the Unified Tax Law amendments (Law No. 1/2022) where turnover bands do not exceed EGP 20 million; and (4) e-invoice validation-mode election (real-time API vs. web-portal batch clearance). None of these alters the entity-level liability of the LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies for mandatory VAT registration; EGP 50,000 for voluntary registration (small traders subject to simplified schedule); e-invoicing is compulsory for all VAT-registered persons irrespective of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to services that are genuinely exported, including cross-border commercial litigation, arbitration, and asset-tracing services, provided: (i) the service recipient is a non-resident person holding no Egyptian permanent establishment; (ii) the contractual seat of performance (arbitral or judicial forum) and the legal benefit of the service are located outside Egypt; (iii) the deliverables are transmitted offshore; and (iv) consideration is paid in foreign currency by external remittance into an Egyptian bank account. Services enjoyed or consummated inside Egypt (e.g., Egyptian court hearings, enforcement of Egyptian judgments, onshore discovery) do not qualify and are taxable at 14% unless exempt under a specific schedule.

  • Customs Export Declaration (AVR Form) — required only where goods accompany or proceed from the litigation (e.g., inspection samples); for pure services the zero-rated services declaration on the VAT return operates in its place
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — evidencing physical export of any documentary or evidential exhibits; for digital legal services, the ETA e-invoice transmission record and off-platform delivery logs satisfy the export-evidence rule
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian receiving bank, CRR-verified, proving receipt of foreign-currency consideration and matched by FX conversion records
  • Signed cross-border legal services agreement with non-resident client plus seat-of-dispute or arbitration clause outside Egypt
  • ETA e-invoice export clearance record bearing the zero-rating code and the official ETA UUID
  • Performance dossier: foreign arbitration or court docket numbers, foreign counsel retainers, and off-Egypt delivery reports

Egypt operates a centralized fiscal clearance mandate, not merely an e-reporting obligation. Every B2B invoice (and, in phased sectors, B2C e-receipts) must be composed in the ETA's XML/JSON schema, digitally signed, transmitted in real time at the moment of issuance, and validated by the ETA's clearance engine. The engine verifies the VAT ID white-list, buyer identity, invoice legal rules, mandatory Arabic/English fields, and the applicable zero-rating code; upon acceptance the ETA returns the official invoice UUID and QR-style digital stamp, and only this ETA-stamped invoice is legally valid for VAT deduction and export zero-rating. Rejected invoices are deemed not issued from a fiscal-law perspective, exposing the taxpayer to Article 74–76 penalties. Machine-to-machine API, sFTP batch, and web-portal channels are permitted with OAuth2-enabled digital certificates; the export zero-rated invoice requires a separate ETA zero-rating code and reconciliation with the monthly return schedule.

The ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt jointly audit export-service proceeds and remittance evidence. If foreign-currency receipts are not repatriated to Egypt within the 180-day statutory deadline, the zero rating is retrospectively reversed, the supply must be re-characterized as a domestic taxable supply at 14%, and the ETA may reopen the preceding five fiscal years to claw back unpaid VAT with statutory late-payment interest and a fine of not less than the evaded tax. Deliberate or fraudulent zero-rating claims (false remittance certificates, fictitious offshore seats, nominee foreign entities) constitute tax evasion causing imprisonment and monetary fines under Articles 74–76 of Law No. 67/2016, cancellation of e-invoicing clearance privileges for up to two years, public registration on the ETA tax-evasion list, and suspension of GAFI investment incentives; the ETA also notifies the Egyptian AML unit and the competent professional licensing board (Ministry of Justice) for disciplinary debarment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, guaranteeing current-account convertibility. Since the 6 March 2024 FX liberalization, the CBE unified the official and parallel exchange rates under a flexible EGP regime; current-account service receipts (legal fees, arbitration disbursements, settlement proceeds) are freely convertible and transferable through authorized banks without CBE pre-approval. Capital-account transactions (inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, share-transfer proceeds, liquidation distributions) remain subject to CBE documentation-based validation and bank-level balance-of-payments coding under BPM6. Profit and equity remittance hurdles: the remitting bank must verify the original inflow trail (GAFI investment certificate, CBE foreign-currency inflow registration), confirm the entity holds a valid commercial registration and tax card, and obtain an automatic tax-clearance acknowledgment from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA). No centralized profit-repatriation moratorium exists post-2024; however, banks retain documentary discretion and enhanced due diligence for remittances exceeding EGP 1 million equivalent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio applies to export or service receipts. Since the 2016 float, and confirmed by the March 2024 unified-rate reform, exporters and service providers (including cross-border law firms) may retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in FX accounts at authorized banks without surrender to the CBE. Statutory repatriation timeline: goods exports – proceeds must be credited to an Egyptian bank within six months from the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date, extendable to twelve months upon documented CBE-approved bank justification; services exports – proceeds must be received within ninety days from the invoice date unless a longer contractual credit period is evidenced and bank-approved. For EG_LLC, legal-service export receivables must flow through the Egyptian banking system within the ninety-day window; late repatriation triggers CBE administrative sanctions under the Central Bank Law and is treated by EMLCU as an AML red flag.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – البنك المركزي المصري, established under Law No. 163 of 2022 issuing the new Central Bank and Banking System Law (replacing Law No. 88 of 2003). The CBE holds exclusive jurisdiction over foreign-exchange licensing, banking supervision, capital-flow registration, and monetary policy. The CBE coordinates with the Egyptian Money Laundering Combating Unit (EMLCU), the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA), and the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for cross-border treasury, AML, and investment-entry controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, MultiPass) to hold IOLTA-style client balances in Egypt. A non-resident EMI cannot accept deposits from an Egyptian-licensed LLC or provide segregated trust accounts without a CBE e-money institution license under the Central Bank Law; the CBE has not recognized any foreign EMI as an authorized custodian of client money. Offshore EMIs may operate only as ancillary inbound/outbound corridor rails (e.g., receiving a USD remittance and forwarding via SWIFT/MT103) provided funds do not remain parked on the EMI ledger. Domestic fintechs (Fawry, Paymob, MNT-Halan, Contact by EFG Hermes) are rated Medium for operational collections and invoice reconciliation but are legally barred from operating interest-bearing client trust accounts; they may serve as parallel collection rails under a merchant agreement with automatic daily sweep to a Tier-1 bank escrow account. Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS) front-end onboarding is permitted only where the custody ledger resides with a CBE-licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for foreign shareholder capital injection into EG_LLC: real-time SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank repositioning) from the foreign shareholder's bank to the EG_LLC USD/EGP account at a CBE-licensed bank. The receiving bank must code the credit under BPM6 'Direct Investment – Equity Injection' (CBE account classification 'AE-1 Capital Contribution'); the remitting bank must quote the purpose code 'Capital Investment' under the CBE Balance-of-Payments Instruction and insert the GAFI investment file number in SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information). Before the first credit, EG_LLC must file a Foreign Currency Inflow Declaration with the CBE through its account bank, attaching: GAFI license, commercial register extract, tax card, shareholders' resolution, and a notarized capital certificate. Enhanced due diligence applies where the injection exceeds USD 100,000 equivalent, requiring source-of-funds confirmations from the remitting bank, UBO passport copies, and proof of the foreign bank's regulatory licensing. Non-cash contributions (receivables or IP) require a CBE-approved valuation report by the General Authority for Investment.Primary clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 with full correspondence for court filing fees, expert witness fees, overseas counsel, arbitrator fees, and settlement distributions; each MT103 must reference the invoice or award number and quote a CBE 'invisible current transaction' service-export code for automatic clearing. Commercial LC alternative: Egyptian banks issue sight or term documentary letters of credit only where the underlying is a goods trade or a law-firm milestone reimbursement tied to a sealed settlement agreement; a standby LC may be used for cross-border escrow margin obligations. Documentary gate and thresholds: post-2024 deregulation removes statutory per-transfer caps, but banks apply enhanced review at USD 50,000 equivalent and require for settlement payments above USD 100,000 a certified copy of the final award or settlement agreement approved by the competent court. Egyptian Tax Authority validation: outbound service remittances are gate-checked against the 15–20% withholding regime on foreign service fees unless a treaty relief certificate under a tax treaty (e.g., Egypt-UK, Egypt-France, Egypt-UAE DTAAs) is presented; the paying bank must obtain a stamped tax-clearance acknowledgment to release the wire.Onshore deliverable FX forwards (USD/EGP, EUR/EGP) with tenors up to 12 months, permitted only where EG_LLC evidences an underlying retainer-receivable or payable-invoice exposure to the dealing bank (underlying-exposure test).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on EGP for tenors up to 24 months, executed offshore or with CBE-approved onshore banks, cash-settled in USD with no EGP delivery; a documented hedging rationale is required for onshore booking.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD, USD/EGP) under ISDA/CSA agreements where the firm carries multi-currency costs (EUR arbitration-seat expenses, USD court disbursements), subject to board-approved treasury policy and a bank credit-line approval.FX options with capped-cost structures as a permissible overlay for settlement-contingency exposure, classified as non-speculative and evidenced in the firm's treasury policy.Currency-swap-linked escrow structures permitting USD-denominated collateral substitution where client funds are held pending award enforcement, eligible at Tier-1 banks holding CBE derivative licenses and subject to full asset-liability documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, MultiPass) to hold IOLTA-style client balances in Egypt. A non-resident EMI cannot accept deposits from an Egyptian-licensed LLC or provide segregated trust accounts without a CBE e-money institution license under the Central Bank Law; the CBE has not recognized any foreign EMI as an authorized custodian of client money. Offshore EMIs may operate only as ancillary inbound/outbound corridor rails (e.g., receiving a USD remittance and forwarding via SWIFT/MT103) provided funds do not remain parked on the EMI ledger. Domestic fintechs (Fawry, Paymob, MNT-Halan, Contact by EFG Hermes) are rated Medium for operational collections and invoice reconciliation but are legally barred from operating interest-bearing client trust accounts; they may serve as parallel collection rails under a merchant agreement with automatic daily sweep to a Tier-1 bank escrow account. Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS) front-end onboarding is permitted only where the custody ledger resides with a CBE-licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations as in Gulf Cooperation Council states; however, the ETA applies the substance-over-form doctrine, beneficial ownership tests, and DTA anti-abuse rules. A foreign parent or treaty claimant must demonstrate actual commercial activity, local management, and substantive decision-making in its jurisdiction of residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, 2% monthly surcharge up to 100%, fine of up to EGP 100,000, and criminal prosecution for felony tax evasion under Art. 133 of Law No. 91/2005.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax; Egyptian courts routinely issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, and impose prison terms of 3–5 years upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Mis-Documentation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment at full corporate rate, solvency of a 30% penalty on the adjusted amount if documentation is incomplete, loss of deduction for the undocumented cross-border payment.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign false transfer pricing disclosures face personal civil liability for the tax differential, disqualification from board positions, and criminal referral for knowingly providing misleading documentation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Circumvention

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, retroactive imposition of 20% dividend WHT on all distributions, deemed profit taxation, and revocation of the tax-registration certificate.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced; directors are personally assessed for withheld taxes plus statutory penalties and may be banned from forming future entities in Egypt.
AML / Unlicensed Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EGP 1 million, freezing of IOLTA/trust accounts, confiscation of proceeds, and imprisonment of 5–10 years if linked to tax-evasion predicates.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are criminally liable; personal travel bans, seizure of personal property, and prohibition from financial services are commonly imposed.
