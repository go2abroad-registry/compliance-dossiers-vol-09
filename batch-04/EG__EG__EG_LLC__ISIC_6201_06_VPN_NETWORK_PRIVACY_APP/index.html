Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian LLC under Law No. 4 of 2025; the Members must declare an Authorized Capital sufficient for the corporate purpose, deposit the subscribed paid-up portion in cash into a provisional incorporation bank account opened in the Company's name 'under formation' at a licensed Egyptian bank, and submit the bank certificate together with an auditor's confirmation to GAFI before issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Directorate fire-safety certification comprising approved extinguishers, alarm systems, two egress routes, and an assembly-point plan; structural load inspection for light-office occupancy (design live load of 2.0-2.5 kPa); and Ministry of Manpower occupational health and safety clearance confirming the non-hazardous IT category of the workplace.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - unified national tax administration; electronic portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (Fatoora e-invoicing portal: https://www.invoicing.eta.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing an Egyptian LLC must (1) incorporate with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) to obtain Commercial Registration; (2) file the Tax Registration application with the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) within 30 days of commercial commencement; (3) obtain the Income Tax Card ('Tax Card') and VAT registration certificate through the ETA unified portal or in-person Tax Office, submitting the Commercial Registration, power of attorney to a local tax agent, signed contracts, lease agreement, and board resolutions; (4) non-resident digital service suppliers without a fixed establishment are separately required to register for VAT through the ETA's simplified portal before supplying electronic services to Egyptian customers when annual taxable supplies exceed the VAT threshold. The tax card must be renewed annually and used for customs, banking, and invoicing purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election; classification is fixed by law. An Egyptian LLC is a taxable person subject to corporate income tax at standard rates and VAT as principal taxpayer. Statutory election mechanisms limited to: (1) consolidated tax filing for qualifying groups under the unified tax procedure framework; (2) advance pricing agreements for intercompany transactions; (3) formal VAT grouping does not exist; (4) no hybrid mismatch election is operationalized under Egyptian law, so cross-border hybrid instruments are generally recharacterized by tax treaty anti-abuse rules rather than elective regimes.

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and services are zero-rated under Egypt VAT Law when the supplier provides documentary proof that goods/services left Egypt (or are performed offshore) and that the consideration has been received in freely convertible foreign currency. Digital network security SaaS services exported to a foreign recipient qualify where the recipient is located outside Egypt, payment arrives in hard currency via formal banking channels, and the relevant contract and beneficial ownership documentation is maintained. Domestic air/ship supplies, transit trade, and internationally transported goods also qualify.

Mandatory e-invoicing (B2B) and e-receipting (B2C) through the ETA Fatoora platform with phased mandatory rollout for all ETA-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be generated in the ETA-approved XML schema, digitally signed with the taxpayer's certificate, and submitted via API for near-real-time post-issuance clearance; each compliant invoice receives a Unique Identifier (UUID) and embedded QR Code (digital stamping). The integration must use the ETA's high-volume merchant API endpoints with HLN/HMAC-based authentication and monthly declarative reconciliation.

Export zero-rating is examined through the ETA risk engine targeting overclaimed VAT refunds. Missing or fraudulent export/fx proof triggers repayment of the zero-rated VAT credit together with interest and a penalty of up to 100% of tax evaded under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016; repeated violations can escalate to criminal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years. Currency repatriation must occur within 180 days; failure without valid ETA permission causes full VAT clawback and exchange-control non-compliance under the Central Bank of Egypt rules, in addition to customs penalties for forged export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since March 2024, Egypt operates a unified, bank-intermediated, managed-float foreign-exchange regime with no imposed dual exchange rates. Current-account convertibility is practically unrestricted: software license fees, SaaS subscription export proceeds, royalties, hosting costs, and service payments may flow through Authorized Banks at the free interbank rate without case-by-case CBE approval, subject only to standard AML/CFT documentation and e-invoicing compliance. Capital-account convertibility is more measured. Shareholder capital injections, shareholder loans, and dividend remittances require: (i) a certified board resolution; (ii) CBE balance-of-payments purpose-code classification; (iii) for dividends, audited financial statements, legal-reserve certification under Articles 499–506 of the Egyptian Companies Law, an approved corporate income tax return via the Unified Tax Platform, and a final tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority; and (iv) documentary evidence of the underlying foreign investment registered with GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones). During the 2022–2023 USD scarcity, repatriation queues and bank-side deferrals of dividend remittances were material; the March 2024 devaluation and the IMF Extended Fund Facility substantially restored interbank liquidity, though residual procedural friction remains for first-time remittances. Profit remittance for a VPN/SaaS LLC is structurally lawful but non-automatic: the bank must confirm the company has no overdue taxes, the foreign exchange was generated from qualifying export receipts, and no stale unsettled import documentary collections exist.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Egyptian export-proceeds regulations (implemented via CBE departmental instructions and Law No. 207 of 2020 on Customs) the resident exporter must: (i) attach a digital Export Declaration to the customs NAFEZA platform for movement of software out of Egyptian jurisdiction, or in the case of pure digital services, maintain a service-registration record with the CBE's foreign-exchange declaration system; (ii) collect the full invoice value through an Authorized Bank in Egypt within 180 days of the export shipment date or the service performance date, whichever later triggers the export contract; (iii) credit the foreign currency into a designated USD/EUR foreign-currency account of the exporting LLC; and (iv) confirm to the bank the receipt of proceeds via the CBE's export proceeds tracking window. Since 2017, the former mandatory fixed conversion quota of 50% of export proceeds into EGP has been rescinded; exporters may lawfully retain export proceeds in foreign currency, use them to settle import/expense obligations, or convert them to EGP at the prevailing interbank rate at their discretion. However, the CBE retains macro-prudential authority to issue temporary general directives altering the retention or conversion treatment of export proceeds in emergency balance-of-payments circumstances, so treasury policy should maintain a dual-book strategy: retain 70–80% of USD proceeds offshore-accessible within Egypt and convert a tactical minimum to EGP for local tax and payroll obligations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the supreme monetary and exchange-control authority, exercising delegated powers through Authorized Banks under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law). The CBE's Financial Markets Affairs and Banking Operations Sectors administer licensing, balance-of-payments statistics, foreign-exchange dealing, and corporate treasury instrument approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW-MEDIUM for direct onboarding of the Egyptian-resident LLC to offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Mercury, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business). These platforms typically require the entity to be incorporated in a jurisdiction they directly support or to operate through a foreign treasury subsidiary. Legal constraints under Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE circulars require a resident exporter to maintain its principal treasury relationship, export-proceeds repatriation account, and collection accounts within Egypt; opening a foreign-currency account abroad by an Egyptian resident requires prior CBE approval, which is rarely granted unless the entity demonstrates a genuine operational need and obtains a ministerial nod from the Ministry of Investment. Global correspondent-bank de-risking in MENA further reduces the practical viability of offshore EMIs as primary clearing rails. The viable architecture is therefore a hybrid: the Egyptian LLC collects subscription/revenue onshore, remits surplus USD to an offshore-regulated subsidiary (e.g., a UAE DMCC holder) for global card-processing and software-infrastructure purchases, with a service/royalty agreement between the two entities satisfying transfer-pricing and export-proceeds repatriation rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol is a fully traceable SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder to the LLC's designated capital account at an Authorized Bank. Required components: (a) Purpose code 'GDFI – Foreign Direct Investment in Egyptian Limited Liability Company' (or the CBE's balance-of-payments code for equity inflows); (b) a reference string matching the board resolution and GAFI incorporation file number; (c) supporting documents — articles of association, GAFI certificate of incorporation, board capital-call resolution, share-allocation ledger, and source-of-funds bank letter. The receiving bank credits the funds to a segregated Capital Escrow Account, issues an Inward Transfer Advice (ITA), registers the flow with the CBE's balance-of-payments infrastructure, and reports the operation in the investor's electronic file. No prior CBE exchange-control approval is required for lawful FDI in convertible currencies; conversion to EGP, if needed, occurs at the free interbank rate. Structuring the injection as equity (not shareholder debt) avoids Article 51 thin-capitalization restrictions and preserves clean future dividend remittance documentation.For operating expenditures (hosting, bandwidth, marketing, software licenses from overseas vendors), the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 from the local foreign-currency account, accompanied by the vendor's invoice, signed services agreement, and a purchase-order reference; the bank performs an FX and AML sanity check before releasing USD/EUR. For high-value hardware/server purchases (e.g., EDR appliances, datacenter networking) the bank executes an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (sight or 90–180 days usance) under UCP 600, or Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) as a cost-optimized alternative. Dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders uses the dedicated profit-repatriation rail: audited financial statements, legal-reserve certification above the statutory floor, corporate income tax return and final tax-clearance, a board resolution authorizing distribution, and then a single SWIFT MT103 with dividend purpose code; the bank releases EGP-converted or original-currency proceeds (preferred: original USD from export-proceeds accounts) and files the outflow with the CBE. For critical cloud-infrastructure spend, the LLC may additionally execute a non-resident bank-guarantee-backed trade service to leverage liquidity without mobilization of collateral.Onshore FX Forward Contracts — bank-offered for documented export receivables and import payables, typically up to 12-month tenors, with margin or collateral line and mandatory underlying-trade documentation.FX Swaps — plain-vanilla EGP/USD or USD/EUR swaps matching recurring liquidity cycles, permissible for hedgers with a genuine current-account exposure.Cross-Currency Swaps — permitted where the LLC carries foreign-currency debt or long-dated licensing liabilities; requires ISDA/CSA documentation and risk-assessment by the bank's market-risk unit.FX Options — available on a restricted, case-by-case basis under IFEMA documentation, principally as zero-premium collared strategies for large upcoming infrastructure contracts.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permissible only where the reference currency cannot be delivered onshore due to CBE-temporary restriction or for offshore synthetic hedging through a foreign treasury subsidiary; speculative NDFs referencing the EGP are prohibited onshore.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW-MEDIUM for direct onboarding of the Egyptian-resident LLC to offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Mercury, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business). These platforms typically require the entity to be incorporated in a jurisdiction they directly support or to operate through a foreign treasury subsidiary. Legal constraints under Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE circulars require a resident exporter to maintain its principal treasury relationship, export-proceeds repatriation account, and collection accounts within Egypt; opening a foreign-currency account abroad by an Egyptian resident requires prior CBE approval, which is rarely granted unless the entity demonstrates a genuine operational need and obtains a ministerial nod from the Ministry of Investment. Global correspondent-bank de-risking in MENA further reduces the practical viability of offshore EMIs as primary clearing rails. The viable architecture is therefore a hybrid: the Egyptian LLC collects subscription/revenue onshore, remits surplus USD to an offshore-regulated subsidiary (e.g., a UAE DMCC holder) for global card-processing and software-infrastructure purchases, with a service/royalty agreement between the two entities satisfying transfer-pricing and export-proceeds repatriation rules.
