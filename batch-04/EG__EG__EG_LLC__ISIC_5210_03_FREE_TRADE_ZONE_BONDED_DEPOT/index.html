Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered address in Egypt is required for company registration and official service. A serviced or virtual office may be accepted as a registered administrative address if the provider, lease, premises evidence and governmental inspection requirements are acceptable to GAFI, the Commercial Registry, tax authority and licensing bodies. It is not sufficient for the operational activity of a bonded warehouse, customs warehouse, depot or free-zone logistics facility. The company must have an approved physical warehouse or depot, customs controls, appropriate lease or title evidence, fire and safety approvals, inventory systems, responsible personnel and any SCZone, GAFI, customs or port authority authorization applicable to the location.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; official portals: www.eta.gov.eg and the ETA e-invoicing/e-tax platform at invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must first complete commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Ministry of Trade and Industry, then file the unified tax file (maalf al-sharika) with the competent ETA office. Submit legalized articles of association in Arabic, commercial registry extract, contract/lease for registered premises, board resolution appointing a fiscal representative, director passport copies, power of attorney, and opening balance sheet. The ETA issues a single Tax Identification Number (TIN) used for income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and payroll. For a free-zone bonded depot, the operator additionally obtains a free-zone license from GAFI/SCZONE and receives a free-zone project file number; a separate customs bonded-warehouse license is issued by the Egyptian Customs Authority. The ETA account is then activated for e-filing and e-invoicing API credentials.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian-resident limited liability company is a juridical person taxed at entity level on Egyptian-source income and worldwide income if fully resident; free-zone bonded-depot projects are, by special statute, treated as taxable under the Investment Law free-zone fee regime rather than ordinary CIT/VAT for approved physical operations.

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election or hybrid mismatch election. Available statutory elections include: (i) election through GAFI/SCZONE to operate a project in a free trade zone, replacing full corporate income tax, VAT, customs and stamp duties with an annual gross-revenue fee (generally 1%) on free-zone operations; (ii) election of the simplified small and medium enterprise (SME) gross-receipts tax regime where annual turnover is within the statutory threshold; (iii) election to file on a fiscal-year basis by obtaining ETA approval; and (iv) for groups, electing consolidated tax filing where all legal conditions are met. Pass-through treatment is not an available election for an LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory ETA VAT registration is triggered when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is possible above EGP 250,000 annual turnover. A licensed free-zone bonded depot is not subject to ordinary VAT registration for in-zone/re-export operations; it must instead be entered in the ETA free-zone project fee registry and register for import VAT when goods are cleared into the national customs territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported outside Egypt and to certain international transport and related services. For free-zone bonded depots, goods entering the licensed depot for storage and subsequent re-export are treated as being outside the Egyptian VAT/customs territory; in practice the movement out of Egypt, or the supply into a free-zone bonded warehouse, is considered an export supply on which the 0% VAT rate (or full out-of-scope treatment) applies. The operator must prove that the goods actually left Egypt, that they entered customs-bonded storage under customs supervision, and that they were not diverted into the domestic market.

  • Customs Export Declaration (final SAD / export declaration form)
  • Free-zone entry declaration or customs receipt evidencing deposit into the bonded depot
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / multimodal transport document)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin, insurance certificate, and customs warehouse stock ledger extracts (when applicable)

Egypt mandates real-time e-invoicing and e-receipts through the Egyptian E-Invoice System (EIS). All registered taxpayers must issue B2B invoices as structured XML or PDF through ETA-certified ERP/API integrations, obtain an ETA-generated unique identifier (UUID) and QR 'fiscal stamp' before releasing invoices to customers, and transmit the invoice to the ETA for automated clearance. The ETA cross-matches invoice data with customs declarations and banking transfer records. Free-zone companies selling into the national market, or issuing documents for the annual fee calculation, must comply with the same e-invoicing protocols; failure to clear invoices in real time invalidates input VAT deduction and incurs statutory penalties.

The ETA and Egyptian Customs jointly audit export declarations, free-zone stock reports, and banking confirmations. Any unjustified zero-rating or input VAT refund obtained through false export declarations, forged documents, failure to re-export within the free-zone timeline, or diversion to the domestic market is clawed back with VAT plus late payment interest at 2% per month, a fine of 100% to 300% of the evaded tax, and potential criminal liability including imprisonment. The free-zone license may be suspended or revoked, and the 1% annual gross-revenue fee may be reassessed on the full value of goods cleared for local consumption.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a market-determined, flexible exchange-rate regime following the March 2024 liberalisation, with the interbank USD/EGP rate set daily by CBE-authorized dealers. Current-account transactions (trade settlements, service fees, dividend remittances) are generally permitted through the banking system against underlying documentation. Profit remittance requires audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate confirming settlement of corporate tax and withholding tax, and CBE-registered foreign capital evidentiary documents. Capital-account movements (intercompany loans, guarantees, outward investment) require CBE registration or approval. Free-zone bonded-depot operators enjoy streamlined treatment on trade-linked currency flows, but full convertibility is conditional on documentary compliance and the absence of foreign-currency rationing during periods of balance-of-payments pressure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to an Egyptian licensed bank and credited within 90 days from the bill-of-lading date (extendable to 180 days with CBE approval for specific sectors). Under the post-March-2024 floating regime, 100% of export receipts must pass through the domestic banking system; the CBE instructs banks to mandatorily convert the proceeds into EGP at prevailing market rates, with retention in foreign currency permitted only up to documented future import or hard-currency tax/expense obligations and subject to bank compliance approval. The bonded warehouse must therefore structure receivable collections to clear via Egyptian authorised banks, with conversion timing aligned to weekly inventory-turnover cash flow to minimise translation exposure.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law); CBE Board circulars govern the foreign exchange market, authorised dealer banks, and balance-of-payments reporting. The General Authority for Investment (GAFI) supports the foreign-investment file, while customs-duty and bonded-depot supervision sit with the Egyptian Customs Authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for settlement-grade treasury purposes. Non-resident digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) hold no CBE banking licence and cannot maintain corporate EGP accounts, issue letters of credit, or provide clearing rails into the Egyptian RTGS system. Under Law 18 of 2019 (Payments Law) and Law 194 of 2020, deposit-taking and foreign-exchange dealing are restricted to CBE-licensed banks. A non-resident EMI may only function as a front-end payment aggregator partnered with a licensed Egyptian bank, and is unsuitable as the primary clearing or FX execution platform for this bonded-depot entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign equity injection must be routed by SWIFT MT103 over the correspondent network into the LLC's CBE-licensed beneficiary bank. The remitter must annotate the wire with the CBE balance-of-payments purpose classification for foreign direct investment (equity capital) and the GAFI investment-file number. The beneficiary bank opens a Capital Escrow Account, verifies the underlying subscription agreement and board resolution, and files the inward FDI declaration with the CBE statistical department so that future dividend repatriation and capital liquidation can be sourced from documented FDI proceeds. Supporting verification comprises apostilled certificate of incorporation of the foreign investor, UBO disclosure, source-of-funds evidence, and the Egyptian LLC's commercial registration and certificate of commencement; settlement through an unlicensed intermediary, cryptocurrency, or non-correspondent shell account is not permitted.Trade settlements for the bonded depot's import and working-capital payments are executed via SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier payments and via MT700/MT707 commercial documentary credits under UCP600 for goods requiring bank confirmation. All Egyptian goods imports require registration on the NAFIZA platform with a valid ACID number and a bank-approved CBE import form or trade-finance instrument before remittance. Domestic EGP obligations (customs duties, municipal fees, local logistics) clear through the CBE RTGS system and the Instant Payment Network (IPN). USD liquidity is obtained on the interbank FX market at market rates, with same-day USD settlement available for confirmed trade invoices. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for settlement-grade treasury purposes. Non-resident digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) hold no CBE banking licence and cannot maintain corporate EGP accounts, issue letters of credit, or provide clearing rails into the Egyptian RTGS system. Under Law 18 of 2019 (Payments Law) and Law 194 of 2020, deposit-taking and foreign-exchange dealing are restricted to CBE-licensed banks. A non-resident EMI may only function as a front-end payment aggregator partnered with a licensed Egyptian bank, and is unsuitable as the primary clearing or FX execution platform for this bonded-depot entity.
