Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; unified national tax administration and e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing and receipt validation platforms operated by the ETA at https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must first complete incorporation with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and the Commercial Registry (Ministry of Trade and Industry), then register with the ETA within the statutory registration period (before commencing activity or within 30 days of the start of operations) to obtain a unified Tax Card and VAT registration certificate. Procedure: (1) submit the commercial registration certificate, Articles of Association, lease or title deed, board resolution/power of attorney, and bank certificate through the ETA e-services portal or in person at the competent tax office; (2) receive the Tax Card and VAT certificate; (3) onboard to the ETA e-invoicing system through API registration and authentication; (4) register with the Customs Authority to obtain the exporter customs code used in export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Egypt is a unitary civil-law jurisdiction and does not offer U.S.-style check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. An LLC is always treated as a separate taxable juridical person under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and VAT Law No. 67 of 2016. Only limited statutory choices are available, such as cash-basis accounting for small taxpayers and the option to apply for VAT registration before the threshold is reached.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable turnover under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, Article 16. VAT registration is compulsory once actual annual turnover exceeds this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of used construction excavators and machinery is zero-rated where the seller is VAT-registered, title passes to a non-resident customer, and the goods are physically exported from Egypt under a customs export declaration within the statutory export period. Deemed exports to qualified free zones and specified offshore services rendered to non-residents may also qualify. The taxpayer must maintain full documentary proof of export and must reconcile the export declaration with the ETA and Customs Authority records; input VAT attributable to export sales is creditable and refundable.

  • Customs export declaration (Bayan) duly stamped by the Customs Authority and the ETA
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR evidencing physical departure of the goods from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian bank, or SWIFT MT103/MT202 confirmation, evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, sale contract, packing list, certificate of origin, and any valve/inspection records required for used heavy machinery

Mandatory phased e-invoicing and e-receipt regimes apply to ETA-selected taxpayers. Issuers must integrate via ETA-approved APIs or the taxpayer portal, submit invoice data in real time, and obtain machine validation with a clearance decision (accepted or rejected). Each valid invoice bears a unique ETA scan code/QR element, and clearance is a legal precondition for input VAT deduction and for using the invoice in customs or export documentation. Non-integrated taxpayers are subject to administrative penalties and suspension of tax clearance certificates.

The ETA and Customs Authority conduct pre- and post-clearance audits and cross-check export declarations against bank remittance records. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger reassessment of output VAT at 14%, late payment interest at approximately 1.25% per month, penalties under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 Articles 61–65 ranging from fines equal to double the tax evaded to imprisonment for tax fraud, revocation of exporter and e-invoicing privileges, and retrospective clawback of input VAT refunds already paid, together with civil liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is assessed as partially convertible with a liberalized current account and a regulated capital account. Current account transactions, including trade settlements, freight, insurance, and profit remittances, are generally permitted subject to documentary verification and CBE-established bank due diligence. Profit and dividend repatriation is legally feasible for non-resident shareholders of an Egyptian LLC provided that audited financial statements are filed, the corporate tax clearance certificate is current, and the remitting bank confirms that the underlying foreign exchange was acquired through a licensed Egyptian bank. Capital account operations (equity injections, shareholder loans, portfolio inflows, and external debt) require CBE registration, a licensed bank intermediary, and compliance with balance of payments balance-of-payments reporting codes. Following the 2022 and 2024 unified flexible exchange-rate reforms, prior import usance restrictions were largely dismantled, but large outbound transfers above CBE notification thresholds require supporting documentation and may be subject to the bank's enhanced due diligence and the national electronic foreign-currency declaration platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters must repatriate foreign currency proceeds from export sales into a bank account held at an authorized CBE bank within statutory timeframes established by CBE Foreign Exchange Circulars and prevailing the prevailing trade finance directives. For used construction machinery and heavy equipment exports (ISIC 4659), the applicable repatriation window is typically 120 days from the date of the export bill of lading, with an expectation that the full invoice value is credited through the banking system. As of the most recent liberalization, the mandatory surrender-to-EGP ratio has been relaxed to effectively zero for most exporters, but exporters must maintain foreign currency receipts in registered accounts, complete the CBE export declaration form, and present supporting customs documentation (export customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and packing list). Non-repatriation or late repatriation constitutes a CBE enforcement violation that can trigger bank account restrictions or administrative penalties; routes that route export proceeds directly to offshore accounts without CBE approval are prohibited.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary, banking, foreign exchange, and payment system regulator in Egypt, operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Banking and Monetary System Law) and the Banking Law No. 194 of 2020.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs acting as the primary treasury rail. Egypt operates a closed banking regulatory perimeter under Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE payment-service licensing rules; no non-bank foreign electronic money institution (EMI) may provide deposit-taking, payment-account, or foreign-exchange clearing services to an Egyptian LLC without a CBE license. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Revolut Business can serve only as auxiliary payout or receivable-concentration tools, and any onward transfer to an Egyptian bank account remains subject to CBE FX declaration and anti-money-laundering screening by the receiving bank. Consequently, the corporate onboarding blueprint relies exclusively on licensed Egyptian commercial banks for primary collections and settlement, with offshore EMI rails used solely for low-value contingency disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders intending to capitalize the Egyptian LLC must route the capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a CBE-licensed correspondent bank into the LLC's foreign-currency account at an authorized Egyptian bank. The wire must quote the Egypt-balance-of-payments statistical purpose code: direct investment - equity capital. The receiving bank requires, in advance or alongside the wire, the LLC's valid commercial registry extract, the articles of association, a shareholders' resolution approving the capital injection amount and currency, the share-subscription instrument, and a source-of-funds statement from the remitting shareholder. The sending bank is required to include true and accurate originator and beneficiary details in compliance with FATF Recommendation 16; incomplete or ambiguous remittance data will cause CBE bank rejection. Inbound capital amounts above USD 100,000 trigger mandatory wire-level CBE electronic declaration and elevated enhanced due diligence, including confirmation that the foreign shareholder is not a PEP or sanctioned person; amounts exceeding USD 1 million may be deferred pending CBE balance-of-payments validation.Trade settlements for used construction excavators from international sellers are executed through (a) commercial letters of credit issued by the Egyptian bank via SWIFT MT700/MT740 series, with presentation of commercial invoice, insurance certificate, bill of lading, packing list, and independent inspection certificate as the preferred cleared rail for high-value sup-above USD 250,000 transactions; and (b) SWIFT MT103 direct wires for open-account balances and spare-part purchases, provided the buyer presents the customs import notification or a confirmed goods-arrival statement to the bank and quotes the correct balance-of-payments settlement code. For payments expected before the goods arrive, Egyptian banks require either a usance LC or a prior approval from the CBE's trade-finance unit, given residual documentary oversight for import payments above USD 20,000. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% flat CIT on net taxable income of resident LLCs (Law No. 26/2020, replacing the 20% rate under Law No. 91/2005); no ordinary local surtax for wholesale trading LLCs outside special economic zones.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final dividend distribution WHT on the gross amount of profits distributed by the Egyptian LLC to shareholders, resident or non-resident alike (Law No. 91/2005, Art. 52; reaffirmed under Law No. 26/2020).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the aggregate value of related-party controlled transactions in a fiscal year exceeds EGP 5 million; Master File prepared where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 750 million; a Transfer Pricing Disclosure Form must accompany Form No. 26 for any material related-party flow, regardless of size.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (Law No. 91/2005, Arts. 30–33), the beneficial-ownership doctrine in DTA applications, and ETA audit practice requiring physical premises, personnel, bank accounts, real trade risk, and title-holding functions in the used-machinery merchant trade. Free-zone and SCZONE privileges lapse automatically where the entity fails to perform genuine inspection, freight, and inventory-resale functions from Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Restoration of the full evaded tax plus a 40% penalty, escalated to 100% if forged documents are proven; criminal monetary penalties of EGP 50,000–5,000,000 and imprisonment up to five years under Law No. 2/2021.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the full evaded amount where non-payment is caused by their willful default; the ETA may impose travel bans, attach personal bank accounts, and obtain criminal convictions leading to professional debarment.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entire net profit as a deemed distribution attracting the 10% WHT, disallowance of all cross-border expenses under GAAR, and revocation of commercial registration under Companies Law No. 72/2017 as a frustrated shell.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors signing knowingly false substance declarations face criminal exposure for tax evasion and money laundering; civil claw-back of management fees is available in insolvency proceedings.
AML / Sanctions Exposure

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for 3–15 years and fines up to EGP 5 million per AML violation; confiscation of excavators and transaction proceeds; asset-freeze orders can paralyze the LLC's banking lines pending review.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners are personally liable for financing illicit transactions; travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition under bilateral judicial assistance treaties may follow.
Withholding and Repatriation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA re-assessment of the unpaid WHT plus 2% monthly delay fines and 5% late-filing surcharges; contractual gross-up clauses generate a second deemed-distribution tax layer; bank accounts and equipment receivables may be seized.

Director personal liability: Unsatisfied WHT attaches permanently to the LLC's tax file; upon liquidation the ETA has priority claim, and directors who paid a dividend while taxes were due incur personal restitution obligations.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Documentation fine of EGP 50,000–500,000; ETA adjustment to the full arm's-length margin with additional 22.5% CIT; the adjusted difference may be reclassified as a disguised dividend attracting a further 10% WHT, plus monthly delay fines.

Director personal liability: A TP adjustment rendering the company insolvent exposes directors to liability for trading while insolvent; the ETA can collect the uncollected difference from board members under the executive collection law.
