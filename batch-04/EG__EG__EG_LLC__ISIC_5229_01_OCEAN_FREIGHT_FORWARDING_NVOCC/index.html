Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Egyptian LLC; the authorized capital stated in the Memorandum of Association must be fully subscribed, and cash subscriptions must be evidenced by a capital deposit certificate issued by a bank licensed by the Central Bank of Egypt or paid into GAFI's designated account prior to filing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - Maslahat al-Dara'ib al-Misriyah; official digital portal https://www.eta.gov.eg; VAT e-invoicing and e-receipts clearance portal https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; governed by Tax Procedures Law No. 206 of 2020 and e-invoicing framework decrees.

E-government filing portal

Under Tax Procedures Law 206/2020, the LLC must self-register via the ETA unified e-services portal within 60 days of commercial commencement. Required submissions include notarized LLC bylaws, Commercial Registration extract, social insurance number, legal representative POA, and registered office evidence. The ETA issues a single Unified Tax Registration Number (UTRN) and tax card covering corporate income tax, VAT, payroll, and stamp duty. The LLC must also complete separate VAT registration once the statutory threshold is met and obtain an e-invoicing digital certificate before issuing taxable invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is a juridical taxpayer under Income Tax Law 91/2005; profits taxed at 22.5% corporate rate; no attribution to owners until distribution). — Under Tax Procedures Law 206/2020, the LLC must self-register via the ETA unified e-services portal within 60 days of commercial commencement. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is a juridical taxpayer under Income Tax Law 91/2005; profits taxed at 22.5% corporate rate; no attribution to owners until distribution).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists. Statutory elections include: (1) group taxation consolidation for connected Egyptian companies under Income Tax Law 91/2005; (2) ETA-approved cash or accrual accounting basis elections for long-cycle service contracts; (3) investment incentive registration under Investment Law 72/2017 for eligible zones/activities; dividend distribution policy does not alter entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods and services (VAT Law 67/2016 as amended; registration mandatory upon crossing the threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods physically exported from Egypt evidenced by customs export declarations and to exported services supplied to non-resident principals, including NVOCC ocean freight forwarding and box brokerage directly tied to export carriage and settled in foreign currency. Internationally rendered freight services qualify under the international transport zero-rating provisions; purely domestic logistics, warehousing, and Egyptian-port despatch services remain standard-rated at 14%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Shaka) duly cleared in the Egyptian customs single window
  • Bill of Lading (Master or House) evidencing the export ocean leg
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from an Egyptian bank evidencing foreign currency repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list signed by the declaring exporter
  • Freight forwarding service contract or booking note with the non-resident principal
  • ETA cleared e-invoice bearing the validated UUID for the zero-rated supply

Mandatory real-time clearance of e-invoices and e-receipts on ETA's digital stamping platform. The taxpayer integrates via ETA REST APIs, transmitting JSON documents with digital signature and QR code; each invoice receives an ETA clearance UUID before issuance to the client. Scanned documents without validated QR are non-compliant; customs exports are cross-referenced to cleared e-invoices in the customs single window.

ETA performs post-export desk audits reconciling customs manifest data against e-invoices, bills of lading, and bank inward remittance certificates. If zero-rating is unjustified, the ETA recomputes 14% VAT on the full invoice value and imposes a 2% monthly late-payment surcharge plus administrative fines. Fraudulent documentation triggers tax evasion penalties under VAT Law 67/2016, including imprisonment and fines ranging from EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000, multiples of tax evaded, suspension of e-invoicing API access, and customs licensing blacklisting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the March 2024 exchange-rate liberalization, the CBE operates a unified, market-determined float for the EGP against hard currencies; current-account transactions (trade settlement, freight invoices, agency fees) are freely executable through authorized banks at interbank or bank-offered rates without prior CBE licensing. Capital-account flows are convertible in principle under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, but remain documentary-heavy: inbound equity subscriptions require a GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) filing, an attested LLC contract, and a bank capital-attestation deposit; profit and dividend remittance requires audited financial statements, a valid tax card, tax filing and payment clearance, and source-of-funds documentation. In practice, bank-level compliance review and interbank FX availability create 1-5 business-day settlement friction, and effective USD liquidity tightens during CBE reserve-stabilization periods. For the NVOCC service model, ocean freight and brokerage fees qualify as invisible earnings and current-account services, repatriable once the receiving Egyptian bank holds the complete shipping file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt's current flex regime imposes no fixed mandatory surrender percentage: exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in USD or EUR in onshore FX accounts or convert at market rate without selling a statutory quota to the CBE. Repatriation timelines remain mandatory: proceeds from goods exports must be credited to a licensed Egyptian bank within 9 months from the bill-of-lading or export-customs date (Import/Export Law No. 118 of 1975, Article 46 and implementing CBE instructions); proceeds from service exports, including NVOCC freight, brokerage, and ancillary charges, must be collected within the contractual payment term, normally not exceeding 9 months from invoice date unless a CBE waiver is obtained. Each receipt must pass through the bank's foreign-exchange statistical system with the underlying contract, invoice, and cargo documents attached; non-repatriation, offshore parking of proceeds, or delayed conversion can trigger CBE sanctions, account restriction, and tax audit exposure.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary, supervisory, and foreign-exchange authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and its Executive Regulations (CBE Board Decision No. 2192 of 2021, as amended).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

LOW as a primary clearing rail; CONDITIONAL as an ancillary offshore collection pipe. Non-CBE-licensed digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut) cannot hold EGP or clear through Egypt's SWIFT/RTGS ecosystem; Egyptian receiving banks will flag, delay, or reject credits routed from an unlicensed EMI unless the full underlying freight document set is pre-submitted. The practical approach is to use an EMI authorized by a reputable regulator (FCA, CySEC, or equivalent) only for collecting small overseas brokerage commissions from low-risk jurisdictions, then sweep funds weekly to the onshore NVOCC USD account with the carriage documentation embedded in the wire reference.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders fund the Egyptian LLC by a SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own bank account to the capital-attestation (escrow) account opened at the selected CBE-licensed bank. The remittance must carry a purpose of payment such as 'Inward Direct Investment - Equity Capital' together with the applicable CBE statistical purpose code for FDI equity, the LLC name, and the GAFI investment file number. The bank's compliance file must hold the LLC contract (Articles of Association), GAFI registration notification, copy of the capital-deposit certificate issued by the notary or bank, board or shareholder resolution on officer authorization, passport copies of UBOs, and the company's written request for capital attestation. Upon matching the remittance to the attested amount, the bank issues the capital certificate and transfers the funds to the operational account, registering the transaction with CBE statistics. The treasury verification step is to obtain the bank's irrevocable inward-remittance confirmation before GAFI stamps the share certificates.Outgoing settlements of NVOCC obligations, including ocean carrier freight, container deposits, port charges, overseas agency commissions, and slot purchases, are executed predominantly by SWIFT MT103 direct transfer because Egypt's free-float regime removes prior CBE approval for service transactions. The bank requires the carrier's commercial invoice and debit note, the underlying booking or service contract, and confirmation of sufficient EGP/USD liquidity; recurring payments to core carriers may be covered by a standing-order facility after one full KYC and trade-file review. For high-value charter or slot-purchase commitments, an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) can be issued against CBE-reportable documentation; for smaller contracted maritime agents, SWIFT MT103 with trade-purpose coding remains the most efficient rail. On the Egyptian side, the CBE's real-time gross settlement system for EGP and the SWIFT network for USD/EUR are the formal clearing layers; the Arab Regional Payment System (Buna) is a secondary regional alternative for EGP, AED, and SAR corridors.Onshore outright delivery forwards (USD/EGP and EUR/EGP) with documented underlying freight and trade cash flows, tenors typically up to 12 months, executed at a CBE-licensed bank under Law No. 194 of 2020.FX swaps (USD/EGP) for short-dated treasury rebalancing and for covering mismatched freight collection and payment cycles, available as spot/forward or tom-next structures up to 6 months.Cross-currency swaps for long-dated USD/EGP commercial liabilities, available on a case-by-case exemption and approval basis; offshore NDFs in EGP are not permitted onshore and should be avoided to prevent bank compliance flags.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends and profit distributions paid by Egyptian resident companies to non-resident shareholders (Law 91 of 2005).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the end of the fiscal year (Law 91 of 2005, Article 31). Extension available upon prior approval for consolidated or foreign-source accounting files.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File required when the aggregate value of controlled transactions in a tax year exceeds EGP 30 million. Master File required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime akin to the UAE, but the Egyptian Tax Authority applies general anti-abuse rules and substance-over-form principles under Law 91 of 2005. An Egyptian NVOCC LLC must maintain physical staff, operating bank accounts, board management, and decision-making records for local operations; a shell with no genuine freight forwarding activity faces reclassification and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imposition of the avoided tax amount plus a 100% penalty on the evaded tax (Law 91 of 2005, Article 133). Criminal fines of up to EGP 50,000 and/or imprisonment for the responsible officers may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they approved false accounts. Egyptian courts permit piercing the corporate veil where fraud is proven; officers face personal civil restitution, travel bans by blacklisting with tax authority, and imprisonment of up to three years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of claimed expenses, recharacterization of profits under general anti-avoidance rules, and a penalty of up to 30% of the adjusted tax amount. The Tax Authority may also deem the LLC to be a permanent establishment of its parent if substance is lacking.

Director personal liability: Directors face restoration orders to make good any unpaid taxes arising from artificial profit allocation; travel bans may be issued by the ETA for non-payment, and criminal charges for facilitation of tax evasion can extend to imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ETA may upwardly adjust the taxable income and apply a 30% adjustment penalty on the additional tax due. Underestimated TP documentation penalties range from EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000 for failure to submit a Local File.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve TP documentation containing false figures may be personally obliged for the adjusted tax and penalties; criminal sanctions arise if willful intent to evade tax is demonstrated, including travel bans and imprisonment.
Unpaid Withholding Tax on Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent (Egyptian LLC) is liable for the full WHT amount plus monthly delay surcharge of 2% per month (capped at 50% of tax). Violations may trigger a service-provider payment ban and administrative blocking of corporate filings.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers can be held personally liable for unremitted WHT where they controlled the distribution process. Egyptian law permits travel restrictions and asset attachment for delinquent officers; in severe cases, imprisonment for misappropriation of withheld taxes.
