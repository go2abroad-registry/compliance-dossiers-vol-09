VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover, per Article 17 of VAT Law 67/2016; mandatory registration for resident and non-resident persons carrying out taxable supplies through an Egyptian fixed establishment. Persons with turnover below the threshold may register voluntarily for refund purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside Egypt qualify for VAT zero-rating, including regardless of payment of foreign currency proceeds, provided the exporter maintains documentary evidence proving the goods left Egypt. Services supplied to non-residents for direct use outside Egypt, transport services, and services related to exported goods may also be zero-rated. The export must comply with the ETA's rules on 'unjustified forfeiture of tax refunds' and the taxpayer must retain originals or electronic certified files.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ECS form approved by Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of foreign currency at a licensed Egyptian bank
  • Purchase order/Contract and sales invoice
  • ETA zero-rating worksheet and sworn declaration that goods were exported and delivered outside Egypt

Egypt has a mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt regime operated by the ETA's Electronic Invoicing System. Taxpayers registered on or after January 2021 must integrate their ERP/billing systems with the ETA's REST API and upload invoices in real time. Each invoice receives a unique QR code and UUID; the ETA performs fiscal validation before integration. Failure to report invoices electronically may render them non-deductible and subject to penalties.

Under VAT Law 67/2016 and Article 28 of the Executive Regulations, a taxpayer claiming unjustified zero-rating or failing to provide the required export documentation shall be subject to VAT at 14% plus statutory delay fines and penalties equal to EGP 500 to EGP 5,000 per invoice for electronic invoicing non-compliance. In case of fraudulent zero-rating evidenced by forged documents, penalties include imprisonment under the Tax Evasion Law No. 202 of 2020 and a fine not exceeding 200% of the tax evaded. The ETA may retroactively claw back VAT refunds and apply the general 15% annual delay interest for a period of up to 5 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since 6 March 2024, Egypt has operated a flexible, market-determined exchange rate regime; the EGP is freely convertible for current-account transactions (import/export trade flows, freight, insurance, and related services) provided the underlying commercial documentation is presented to an authorized bank. Capital-account inflows (equity subscriptions and shareholder loans) are permitted after submission of notarized corporate resolutions, Commercial Registry updates, and tax registration confirmation; outbound profit and dividend remittances require audited financial statements, evidence that distributable profits arise from Egyptian operations, and a tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA). Sulfur mineral-ore trading under ISIC 4669/HS 2503 is treated as a standard commodity transaction rather than a dual-use or controlled goods category, reducing documentary friction; however, all FX transactions must pass through CBE-licensed banks and be classified under CBE balance-of-payments purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of non-oil goods are required under CBE instructions to bring 100% of export proceeds into Egypt through an authorized bank within the CBE-mandated repatriation window, customarily four to five months from the bill-of-lading date. Following the March 2024 FX liberalization, the mandatory conversion of export receipts into EGP was abolished; proceeds may be held in foreign-currency accounts with the collecting bank, and the statutory 'surrender' obligation is limited to repatriation into Egypt via banking channels rather than conversion into EGP. Failure to repatriate within the prescribed period triggers CBE administrative penalties, suspension of trade-finance limits, and heightened ETA audit scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the sole monetary and banking supervisory authority operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and its executive regulations, together with CBE foreign-exchange circulars governing authorized-dealer banks, balance-of-payments reporting, and trade-related FX operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW as the primary treasury venue; permissible only as an AUXILIARY payment channel. Egypt's regulatory perimeter under Law No. 194 of 2020 confines corporate account-taking, FX clearing, and documentary credit issuance to CBE-licensed banks; offshore EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Corpay) hold no Egyptian banking license and therefore cannot open EGP accounts, execute domestic EGP payments, collect Egyptian export proceeds, or issue letters of credit. Such EMIs may support ancillary foreign-currency payouts (freight, marine insurance, surveyor fees), but they are not authorized dealers for CBE statistical reporting and cannot discharge ETA invoicing obligations, customs-duty settlements, or LC undertakings.

10% on gross dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders under Law 91/2005. Egyptian corporate shareholders are exempt under participation exemption.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months following the close of the fiscal year. Tax year is generally the calendar year unless otherwise approved. For a December 31 year-end, filing deadline is April 30.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 4,000,000 annual related-party transaction threshold for mandatory Local File and Master File. Penalties for noncompliance up to EGP 1,000,000. Related-party transactions must be disclosed in Annual TP Declaration Form.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no formal ESR framework but requires genuine substance and actual commercial activity. Arrangements lacking commercial substance may be disregarded under General Anti-Abuse Rules and Article 32 of Law 91/2005. The ETA may adjust profits based on functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for tax evasion (up to 5 years) and fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 500,000; additional administrative fine up to 100% of tax understatement. Criminal proceedings under Law 91/2005.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally participate in tax evasion before personally liable for fines and penalties; may face imprisonment, travel bans, property seizure, and civil liability for unpaid corporate taxes under tax law veil-piercing provisions.
Filing / Compliance Violations

Statutory sanction: Late filing fines (5% per month up to 100% tax), late payment fines (5% per month up to 100% tax), fixed penalties up to EGP 50,000 for failure to submit required forms, and revocation of tax registration licenses in repeated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly cause noncompliance. In extreme cases, criminal fines against responsible managers under ETA enforcement; no imprisonment for purely civil late filing unless fraud is present.
Transfer Pricing Adjustments and Penalties

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalty equal to 25% of the TP adjustment amount (per ETA TP Guidelines); late payment fines and interest; failure to submit TP documentation may result in additional 10% penalty. Potential aggregate fine up to 100% of adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for implementing related-party arrangements may be personally liable for additional tax if they acted beyond authority or misrepresented facts. Criminal liability only when TP manipulation is part of a tax evasion scheme.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: GAAR may deny benefits; ETA may ignore the shell entity and assess tax against foreign principal; sanctions include revocation of tax registration, fines, and denial of treaty relief. Under Egyptian law, shell companies misusing commercial register may face administrative penalties and deregistration.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to travel bans and personal liability for taxes if they act as nominee directors for sham entities. Involvement in circular transactions or fraudulent invoices could give rise to criminal liability for facilitating tax evasion.
AML and Trade Finance Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Violations under Egyptian AML Law No. 80 of 2002: imprisonment up to 15 years and fines from EGP 200,000 to EGP 10,000,000. Corporate fines may reach EGP 5,000,000; freezing of accounts, assets, and cargo; revocation of license.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally and criminally liable for AML violations. Civil liability for damages; travel bans and asset freezes; in serious cases, imprisonment and extradition. Negligent failure to obtain necessary licenses is also culpable.
