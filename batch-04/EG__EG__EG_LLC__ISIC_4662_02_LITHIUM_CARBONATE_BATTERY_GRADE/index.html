Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Egyptian LLCs have no statutory minimum capital requirement under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981. The issued capital must be stated in the incorporation contract and fully paid at incorporation. No statutory escrow account is generally required for an LLC; the banks and GAFI may require evidence of payment or a capital deposit account under current procedural practice. Capital contributions in kind require valuation and supporting evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egyptian pound is partially convertible under a managed floating regime. Current account transactions for genuine trade (imports/exports), services, and profit remittances from registered entities are supported by the interbank market when backed by contractual, customs, and tax documentation. Capital account flows such as direct investment, shareholder loans, portfolio investments, and offshore hedging must be pre-approved or notified through CBE's Balance of Payments reporting system; profit repatriation is subject to an annual audited financial statement, tax clearance, and Board approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE's foreign exchange circulars, all export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt within 90 days from the date of the bill of lading, using an authorized bank. A mandatory surrender ratio of 50% of export proceeds applies unless a sector-specific waiver is obtained; the surrendered portion is converted to EGP at the applicable interbank rate and the balance can be retained in an export FX account and applied toward genuine current account obligations. The CBE may revise the surrender ratio via circulars during emergency foreign exchange conditions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) / Egyptian Foreign Exchange Administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low direct viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI accounts (Wise, Mercury, Airwallex) cannot receive Egyptian export proceeds or hold EGP onshore, because Egyptian exchange control law requires all export proceeds and shareholder capital injections to enter through an Egyptian authorized bank unless a special CBE approval is obtained for maintaining a foreign account. Offshore EMIs may only be used for incidental pre-financing or documentation payments that are ultimately re-routed to Egypt. A resident LLC opening an offshore EMI account must report it under CBE Law No. 194/2020 and will face enhanced due diligence at every Egyptian counterparty bank due to the unknown flow of foreign funds.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Newly formed LLC with foreign shareholders and no domestic operational footprint

Root cause: Newly formed LLC with foreign shareholders and no domestic operational footprint

Defense evidence: Commercial Registry extract; tax card; lease or ownership certificate for the registered office; GAFI incorporation certificate; detailed board resolution explaining purpose and authority; notarised opening balance sheet or audited combined financial statements; UBO biometric/notarised passport copy; source of funds warranty; expected transaction matrix.
Cross-border trade in high-risk commodity (battery-grade lithium carbonate) with no visible physical storage or processing

Root cause: Cross-border trade in high-risk commodity (battery-grade lithium carbonate) with no visible physical storage or processing

Defense evidence: Complete trade documentation set: proforma/commercial invoice, supplier KYC and corporate registration, transport contracts, bill of lading, marine/cargo insurance, warehouse receipts, independent inspection reports (SGS/Ctac/Bureau Veritas), customs declaration, NAFEZA import registration and bank transfer instructions showing shipment mandate.
Payment to a supplier that is not the same entity named on the Letter of Credit or supplier invoice

Root cause: Payment to a supplier that is not the same entity named on the Letter of Credit or supplier invoice

Defense evidence: Written know-your-supplier policy, sanction/OSINT screening reports on each beneficiary, supplier board resolution and registration certificates, valid sales/purchase agreement with anti-touting clause, full payment trail from downstream customer to upstream supplier, and management undertaking explaining any back-to-back trade settlement.
High reliance on shareholder debt rather than paid-up equity creating a thin-capitalisation concern

Root cause: High reliance on shareholder debt rather than paid-up equity creating a thin-capitalisation concern

Defense evidence: Formal debt-to-equity policy, executed shareholder loan agreement with arm's length commercial interest rate, consolidated financial statements, solvency certificate, CBE capital registration record, tax auditor report, board resolution allocating the funding between equity and shareholder loan, and a cash-flow forecast for debt service.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injection must be routed from the beneficial owner's account by SWIFT MT103 through correspondent clearing into the LLC's capital escrow account at the licensed Egyptian bank. The remittance must carry the Egypt Balance of Payments purpose code '401 – Direct Investment Equity' for equity contributions or '402 – Direct Investment Loan' for shareholder loans. The receiving bank will require the GAFI-certified incorporation documentation, board resolution authorising the capital increase, source-of-funds warranty executed by the foreign shareholder, sanctioned-entity checks, and the executed escrow account undertaking. Funds are credited only after full verification; any conversion to EGP for local operating expenses is handled at the CBE interbank rate subject to permissible balance limits.For lithium carbonate supplier settlement, the primary clearing rail is a commercial letter of credit opened through an Egyptian authorized bank, either at sight or with a deferred payment tenor up to 90 days, against presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin, packing list, quality certificate, and insurance policy. After arrival and customs release, open-account balances and L/C reimbursements are settled via SWIFT MT103 through the Egyptian bank's nostro account. Every outward payment must be matched with a valid import registration form and an approved CBE import ID; foreign exchange release is conditional on verification against the National Single Window (NAFEZA) customs records. Cash-in-advance telegraphic transfers are permitted for low-value urgent spare parts but subject to additional CBE scrutiny.Authorized bank-provided USD/EGP forward contracts up to 180 days, strictly against confirmed import/export trade documentation and cannot be rolled over without proof of the underlying trade flow.Institutional offshore NDF contracts for USD/EGP are not accessible to resident Egyptian corporates as a standalone hedge; NDFs may be incorporated only in cross-border shareholder loan agreements through an offshore vehicle with clear treasury justification and CBE approval.Cross-currency swaps between EGP and USD for debt service on onboarding shareholder loans are permissible after CBE registration, provided the hedging is limited to the principal and interest exposure of the approved loan and does not create open speculative positions.

Box 8 Amount payable with the return or refund due. Any balance of Egyptian CIT after credits must be paid by the statutory final due date; refund claims follow an ETA assessment process. Late filing triggers a monthly delay surcharge of 2% on the unpaid corporate income tax, statutory fines ranging from EGP 1,000 to EGP 20,000 for the return, and possible seizure of the company's bank accounts and goods. Repeated or intentional non-filing may lead to criminal proceedings against the legal representative and an order restricting travel.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Ashared Local File must be maintained when the annual aggregate value of connected-party transactions exceeds EGP 10,000,000; a Master File is required when the multinational group entity's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 200,000,000 (or equivalent in hard currency), in accordance with Ministry of Finance transfer-pricing guidelines and the executive regulations of Law 91 of 2005.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style ESR as a standalone code, but the Egyptian Tax Authority applies anti-abuse, substance-over-form, and PE-recognition doctrines under Law 91. Free-zone and investment-incentive entities are required to maintain real business premises, adequate staffing, and substantive operational decision-making in Egypt; without actual economic substance, tax exemptions may be revoked and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: CIT reassessment with an 18% annual interest charge, statutory fines of up to EGP 50,000, and imprisonment for up to five years under Law 91 of 2005; Customs violations may add additional penalties if receipts were underpriced for duty evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who knowingly participate in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall, face asset freezing and travel bans during adjudication, and may be sentenced to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, reclassification of income as Egyptian-source, application of the full 20% or 10% statutory withholding rates, back-assessment of 22.5% CIT on net profit, plus monthly delay surcharges and fines of up to EGP 20,000.

Director personal liability: Officers and shareholders who knowingly organize a sham entity are personally liable for the unpaid taxes and interest; they may also face criminal conspiracy charges and travel bans under Egyptian tax and penal legislation.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian Tax Authority may disregard the pricing, adjust taxable income to arm's length, impose a 30% penalty on the disputed tax amount, and levy a 2% monthly delay surcharge; unsupported invoices are disallowed and cross-border payments treated as dealings with third parties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign misleading transfer-pricing documentation are jointly liable for the resulting tax deficiency and penalties; where the arrangement is found to be an intentional sham, they may be subject to personal criminal prosecution.
Disguised Profit Repatriation / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the fee or royalty, recharacterization as a deemed dividend subject to 10% withholding, imposition of 20% WHT on any recharacterized interest or royalty, plus late-payment surcharges and fines up to 100% of the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Board members who approve fictitious service or licensing agreements are personally liable for the unreported withholding tax and associated penalties; if the arrangement was designed to evade tax, imprisonment is possible.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law 80 of 2002, intentional trade-based money laundering carries imprisonment of up to 15 years, fines of up to EGP 500,000, and confiscation of laundered assets; the Central Bank of Egypt may also impose enforcement measures on financial institutions handling the funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers have personal liability for failure to file a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) or for enabling the laundering scheme; their personal assets may be frozen, travel banned, and they are subject to criminal imprisonment.
