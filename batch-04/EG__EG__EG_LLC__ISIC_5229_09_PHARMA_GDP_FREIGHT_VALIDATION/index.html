Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian LLC. The founders set the capital in the Memorandum of Association. Paid-up cash capital must be deposited in a licensed Egyptian bank account before GAFI registration; the amount remains restricted/escrowed pending issuance of the incorporation certificate and commercial registration profile. In-kind capital contributions must be valued and acknowledged in the Memorandum.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Civil Defense Certificate from the Civil Defense Authority (Ministry of Interior) covering the warehouse and office, structural load and electrical safety certificate signed by a licensed engineer under Law 119 of 2008, and an Occupational Safety and Health clearance from the Ministry of Manpower for workplace ventilation, emergency exits, fire suppression, and worker training records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), unified online tax portal at https://eta.gov.eg, with mandatory electronic e-invoicing and e-reporting platforms at https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity or newly formed Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA before or within the VAT statutory deadlines. The registration package in Arabic includes: certified commercial registry extract and incorporation contract, lease or ownership proof of the registered office, nationality documents or passports of directors/managers, power of attorney for the local tax representative where applicable, and proof of appointment of the responsible tax accountant. Upon ETA review, the taxpayer receives a unified Tax Card (TIN), VAT registration number, and electronic filing credentials. Foreign entities not having a fixed Egyptian establishment but carrying out taxable economic activities in Egypt must appoint a legally recognized tax representative and register through Form 1 of the VAT law; the ETA may also require bank guarantee or annual VAT returns to monitor compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident entity or newly formed Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA before or within the VAT statutory deadlines. The registration package in Arabic includes: certified commercial registry extract and incorporation contract, lease or ownership proof of the registered office, nationality documents or passports of directors/managers, power of attorney for the local tax representative where applicable, and proof of appointment of the responsible tax accountant. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election is available for an Egyptian LLC; the LLC is an opaque entity assessed at entity level under Egypt's Corporate Income Tax and VAT regimes. Egypt does not currently adopt hybrid mismatch or fiscal transparency elections for domestic LLCs, and no territorial entity classification election exists beyond the mandatory statutory classification. Head office expenses, thin capitalization, and controlled foreign company rules are applied irrespective of an entity-selection election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is triggered by a historical or expected annual taxable turnover exceeding EGP 500,000; a non-resident supplier without Egyptian fixed establishment but performing taxable supplies in Egypt is also required to register for VAT, either directly or through a VAT representative, regardless of a turnover threshold where supplies consist of executed services in Egypt

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of tangible goods out of Egypt and to services rendered in direct connection with duly exported goods, including international transportation and logistics services (including GDP pharma freight services), provided that the VAT registrant can evidence (i) the physical departure of the goods from an Egyptian customs jurisdiction, (ii) the supply of the services to a non-resident client or as part of an export supply chain, and (iii) the receipt of foreign currency in Egypt through licensed banking channels. The exporter must possess the original export contract, customs cleared export declaration, and compliant transport documents proving the goods exited Egypt within the prescribed statutory timelines.

  • Customs Export Declaration registered and stamped by Egyptian Customs through the Nafeza single-window system
  • Original or certified true copy of the Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from an Egyptian sea or air port
  • Commercial invoice and signed export contract identifying the buyer or overseas principal
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank foreign-currency receipt confirmation from an Egyptian licensed bank
  • Goods discharge or dispatch confirmation from the freight forwarder or GDP logistics operator evidencing movement of product from Egypt to the stated destination

Real-time e-invoicing and fiscal clearance is mandatory for all B2B transactions under the ETA's electronic invoicing framework. Tax invoices and credit/debit notes must be transmitted in ETA-approved JSON/XML format through the certified API integration or web portal, digitally signed with the taxpayer's e-seal, and submitted to the ETA for stamping and real-time validation before an invoice is considered legally complete. The ETA assigns a unique fiscal number and performs automated schema, mandatory-field, and value validation; invoice acceptance is rejected unless the digital stamp is obtained. A GDP pharma freight operator must therefore integrate its enterprise billing system with the ETA clearing portal and maintain real-time connectivity, including continuous adaptation to staged tax types and gradual mandatory e-invoice roll-out for all transaction values.

Egypt's ETA can reassess zero-rated export transactions within the general five-year tax limitation period; failure to substantiate an export zero-rating claim or any misrepresentation of an offshore supply results in assessment at the standard 14% VAT rate with late-payment interest calculated from the original supply date. Where fraud or intent to evade tax is established under Law 67 of 2016, penalties include a fine from EGP 20,000 to EGP 200,000, imprisonment of the responsible persons or legal representative, double payment of the evaded tax amount (100% additional tax penalty), clawback of all input VAT previously credited in the export refund, and cancellation or suspension of the export-related tax registration. For GDP logistics, the ETA additionally cross-matches Nafeza customs clearances against Central Bank foreign-exchange remittance records; any mismatch or delayed repatriation beyond the 180-day statutory deadline triggers audit scrutiny, potential tax reassessment, and export refund denial.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since 6 March 2024, Egypt applies a flexible exchange-rate regime with the interbank EGP rate as the single official reference. Current-account transactions (merchandise imports, freight and logistics fees, service fees, dividends and profit remittances) are practically convertible through licensed banks upon submission of underlying invoices and CBE BOP e-declarations, without case-by-case CBE approval. Capital-account transactions remain registrable: non-resident equity injections require a GAFI capital filing and an inbound remittance certificate; shareholder loans exceeding threshold amounts require external-debt registration with CBE. Profit-distribution remittances require audited financial statements, board/shareholders' distribution resolution and an Egyptian Tax Authority tax-clearance certificate. The 2022-2023 FX rationing and parallel-market premium were eliminated by the 2024 float; practical convertibility for EG_LLC pharmaceutical freight fee flows is HIGH, though EGP-denominated cost centers should be covered with rolling 6-12 month hedging.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. The CBE 6 March 2024 reform repealed the June 2022 instruction requiring 100% surrender of export proceeds. EG_LLC may retain all service receipts in foreign-currency current accounts (FROA/FCY), use them directly to pay overseas freight and cold-chain suppliers, or sell them voluntarily on the interbank market. The statutory expectation remains that export and cross-border service proceeds enter the Egyptian banking system within 180 days of the invoice or transport-document date and are recorded in the CBE BOP e-declaration; unexplained non-arrival may be treated as an exchange-control violation. Each pharma freight receipt should be matched with the freight invoice, AWB/BL, EDA GDP licence number and EFFA registration to satisfy bank income-matching and customs ACI controls.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Law No. 194 of 2020; the General Department for Foreign Exchange Operations administers FX licensing, balance-of-payments (BOP) classification and import-export proceeds supervision. The General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) manages corporate registration and inward-investment certification; the Egyptian Drug Authority (EDA) under Law No. 151 of 2019 licenses pharmaceutical GDP logistics; the Egyptian Customs Authority operates the ACI (Advance Cargo Information) manifest regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW (15/100) for domestic EGP treasury, inbound capital compliance and statutory account purposes; MEDIUM (50/100) only as an offshore treasury overlay used by the non-resident parent to pay overseas transporters and pharmaceutical suppliers. Egypt does not recognize foreign EMI or digital-bank licences: under Law No. 194 of 2020, only CBE-licensed banks may issue current accounts, operate e-money or access national clearing and instant-payment rails. A non-Egyptian EMI cannot hold EGP accounts, cannot settle with Egyptian residents and may not serve as the statutory settlement venue of EG_LLC. Legal constraint: routing Egyptian-source revenue into an offshore EMI without a CBE-licensed bank intermediary would constitute unlicensed payment activity; therefore the EMI remains confined to the parent's offshore treasury column.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route: non-resident shareholder's own offshore account to EG_LLC's FCY account at the Tier-1 Egyptian bank via SWIFT MT103. The bank verifies that the remitter matches the GAFI-registered LLC member list, records the BOP narrative 'direct investment equity capital in non-financial corporations', and issues an inward remittance certificate. For injections above EGP 25 million equivalent, the CBE General Department for Foreign Exchange Operations receives a capital-increase file with the GAFI amendment. MT103 field 70 must state: 'EG_LLC - capital subscription - GAFI file no. [ ] - Law No. 72/2017'. The bank opens an internal pass-through sub-account or escrow ledger pending issuance of the amended commercial-registry certificate; after registration, funds may be converted to EGP at the interbank rate for working capital. Intercompany shareholder loans follow the same MT103 rail with 'related-party loan - external debt registration' notation, a signed loan agreement and CBE notification to secure future debt-service remittances.Freight and logistics disbursements are cleared by SWIFT MT103 (GPI) from EG_LLC's FCY account, using BOP purpose code 'transportation services - freight' with the EDA GDP licence reference in the beneficiary message; genuine service payments do not need prior CBE approval. Where EG_LLC co-imports pharmaceutical products for GDP distribution, settlement executes by irrevocable at-sight Commercial Letter of Credit or documentary collection against the full GxP package: commercial invoice, cold-chain B/L or AWB, temperature-log certificate, EDA import permit, certificate of analysis, packing list and cargo insurance.
