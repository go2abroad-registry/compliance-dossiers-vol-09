Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an Egyptian LLC. The capital is determined in the Article of Association and must be fully subscribed. Cash subscriptions may be evidenced by a bank deposit certificate, but a bank escrow account is not statutorily compulsory; in-kind contributions require an independent valuation report appended to the incorporation file.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense fire safety certificate covering fire extinguishers at the storage bay, emergency exits, and compliance with the Egyptian Fire Protection Code; structural load clearance for cathode pallet racking or reinforced floor slabs; occupational health and safety approval from the Ministry of Manpower, including mandatory PPE, lifting-equipment inspection, and employee training for manual cathode loading operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; electronic tax portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity exporting goods from or to Egypt must first be registered with the Egyptian Tax Authority. The standard route is: (1) form an Egyptian LLC or registered branch through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain commercial registration; (2) file the ETA tax card application to obtain the taxpayer identification number (TIN); (3) file the VAT registration declaration if taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold or if zero-rated export refunds are sought; (4) register in the ETA e-invoicing platform; and (5) appoint an Egyptian tax representative or fiscal representative for all tax filings, correspondence, and service of notices where the company has no Egyptian management place.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation An Egyptian LLC is a juridical person under Egyptian law and is taxed at entity level on its worldwide income unless an income-tax treaty source rule applies. The LLC is not a pass-through entity, and no Egyptian statutory regime treats an LLC as fiscally transparent. Physical copper cathode trading conducted through an Egyptian LLC is therefore subject to Egyptian corporate income tax on net profits and VAT on local supplies; only a separately licensed free-zone entity would enjoy the free-zone exemption regime.

Available election forms

Egypt does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election or a general hybrid mismatch election. The tax characterization of an Egyptian LLC follows its legal form and central-management-and-control facts. Available administrative elections are limited to the selection of fiscal year, the adoption of the simplified SME tax computation where eligibility thresholds are met, and certain VAT cash-accounting or invoice-dating elections prescribed by the ETA. Egypt relies on general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and transfer-pricing adjustments to recharacterize hybrid mismatches; a foreign parent cannot elect a different Egyptian tax treatment for a wholly owned Egyptian LLC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is available from EGP 100,000 of annual taxable supplies; exporters must register irrespective of the threshold when they need to invoice at 0% and recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physically delivered refined copper cathodes from Egyptian customs territory to a foreign destination are eligible for a 0% VAT rate, subject to: (i) the exporter being VAT-registered; (ii) the goods passing into the ownership or control of a non-resident buyer; (iii) the goods being physically exported under customs supervision through a formal export declaration; (iv) the exporter holding acceptable evidence of means of transport from Egypt to the overseas destination; and (v) foreign currency consideration being received in Egypt through an authorized Egyptian bank. Offshore trading transactions under LME warrants that do not involve physical movement of goods through Egypt are outside the Egyptian zero-rating chain; such offshore warrant transfers do not justify Egyptian export VAT claims.

  • Customs export declaration validated by Egyptian Customs through the NAFEZA single window, with HS code 7403 for refined copper cathodes
  • Bill of lading, multimodal transport document, or air waybill showing dispatch from Egypt to the foreign buyer's destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or foreign currency credit advice issued by an Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Egypt has phased in mandatory electronic invoicing for VAT-registered businesses, including exporters. Each electronic invoice must be structured in ETA-compliant JSON/XML, digitally signed, submitted in real time through ETA-approved APIs or certified middleware, and cleared by the ETA before issuance. The system assigns each invoice a unique UUID and QR code; digital stamping or clearance failure blocks the transaction and is subject to fines. Businesses selling copper cathodes to Egyptian industrial consumers must also integrate their ERPs with ETA e-invoice and, where applicable, e-receipt infrastructure.

The ETA routinely audits export zero-rating claims and refund applications with enhanced scrutiny for physical-trader invoices, customs release records, and bank settlement trails. If the export is not genuinely completed, the zero rating is reversed, the VAT becomes payable at the standard 14% rate, all refunded input VAT is retrospectively clawed back, and late-payment surcharges apply. Claiming export zero-rating on missing, forged, or non-matching export documentation may be treated as VAT fraud, resulting in criminal liability, fines, imprisonment under the VAT Law and the Egyptian Penal Code, denial of future refunds, customs liabilities, and personal liability for responsible officers where wilful concealment is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is generally convertible for bona fide current-account transactions through licensed banks, subject to documentary verification, sanctions screening, banking liquidity, and applicable CBE controls. Current-account payments such as imports, trade services, freight, insurance, interest, and dividends may generally be processed when supported by commercial documents and tax compliance evidence. Capital-account transactions, shareholder funding, intercompany loans, capital reductions, acquisitions, and foreign-investment exits are subject to bank-level review, source-of-funds verification, corporate approvals, registration evidence, tax clearance where applicable, and documentary proof of the underlying transaction. There is no unrestricted guarantee of immediate foreign-currency availability. Profit and dividend remittances are generally feasible through an authorized bank after audited financial statements, shareholder resolutions, tax evidence, retained-earnings confirmation, and proof of lawful ownership and capital funding are provided. The company should maintain a documented treasury policy, avoid informal FX markets, and obtain written bank confirmation before executing material repatriations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt does not generally impose a universal statutory requirement that all export proceeds be converted into Egyptian pounds at a fixed mandatory percentage. Export proceeds must nevertheless be collected, documented, and processed through authorized banks in accordance with CBE, customs, and banking procedures. Banks commonly require repatriation or collection of export proceeds within the applicable regulatory or bank-prescribed period, commonly six months from shipment for ordinary exports, subject to transaction-specific rules, extensions, and exceptions. The exporter should obtain the servicing bank's current written confirmation of the applicable deadline, permitted foreign-currency retention, and any required CBE or customs reporting before shipment. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign currency or converted through the banking system, subject to bank controls and current regulations. No fixed mandatory conversion ratio should be assumed without confirmation of the latest CBE circular and the bank's trade-finance procedures.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Bank and Banking System Law promulgated by Law No. 194 of 2020, together with applicable banking, customs, anti-money laundering, and foreign exchange instructions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for ancillary payment services and Low for primary treasury banking. Egypt does not provide a generally available offshore-EMI passporting regime. An offshore digital EMI cannot ordinarily open or operate local Egyptian corporate accounts, collect local funds, execute regulated payment services, or bypass CBE foreign-exchange and AML controls without an appropriately licensed Egyptian bank, payment-service provider, or other authorized institution. The LLC should use a CBE-regulated bank for foreign-currency receipts, import payments, export proceeds, capital injections, dividend remittances, and FX transactions. Offshore platforms may be used only for permitted services after legal review, contractual due diligence, data and AML assessment, sanctions screening, and confirmation that the platform is not conducting unauthorized local payment activity.

Before remittance, provide the bank with the constitutional documents, commercial registration, ownership chart, shareholder or board resolution, subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, and any required investment or corporate-registration documentation. The bank should confirm the required purpose code, supporting documents, reporting treatment, and whether the funds must be credited to a capital account or may be credited to an operating foreign-currency account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of net taxable profits for ordinary Egyptian companies, including an Egyptian LLC, subject to specific statutory exceptions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by an Egyptian resident company are generally subject to 10% withholding tax. A 5% rate may apply in specified circumstances, including qualifying listed shares, subject to statutory conditions. A tax treaty may reduce the rate where the recipient qualifies and all procedural and beneficial-ownership requirements are met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egypt’s transfer-pricing documentation rules generally use an EGP 8 million related-party transaction threshold for Local File requirements and an EGP 3 billion consolidated-group revenue threshold for Master File requirements, subject to the applicable rules, aggregation, exclusions, and tax-year guidance. Confirm current thresholds and filing obligations with the ETA for the relevant year. Country-by-Country reporting may apply to qualifying multinational groups meeting the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not generally operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, anti-avoidance principles, permanent-establishment analysis, treaty entitlement, and the factual assessment of whether functions, assets, risks, management, and commercial decision-making are genuinely located where asserted. A copper-trading LLC should maintain evidence of personnel and decision-making, trading authority, inventory and title, logistics, financing, insurance, and control of market and credit risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment, recovery of tax, statutory additional tax and administrative penalties, and potential criminal fines or imprisonment where the conduct satisfies the applicable tax-crime provisions. The precise sanction depends on the act, amount, intent, and governing law.

Director personal liability: Managers and authorized signatories may face personal criminal exposure for their own knowing participation, false certification, or other statutory involvement. Corporate limited liability does not immunize individual offenders. Civil recovery, travel restrictions, or imprisonment are not automatic and require a legal basis and applicable procedure.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, additional tax, applicable administrative penalties, and potential documentation-related consequences under Egyptian transfer-pricing and tax-procedure rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be exposed for intentional fraud, knowing false filings, or other personal statutory breaches. An adjustment or documentation failure alone does not automatically pierce the LLC’s limited-liability protection.
Improper treaty or withholding-tax relief

Statutory sanction: Recovery of underwithheld tax, additional tax and penalties, and possible criminal consequences if fraud or deliberate falsification is established. The Egyptian payer may remain responsible for withholding and remittance.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may incur personal exposure for knowing non-remittance, false certifications, or other statutory violations; liability is fact-specific and is not automatic merely because a treaty claim is denied.
Customs, origin, and trade-document fraud

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, duties and associated charges, administrative penalties, seizure or confiscation where authorized, and potential criminal sanctions for fraud or smuggling under applicable customs law.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct, authorize, or knowingly participate in customs fraud may face personal criminal or civil consequences under applicable law. Corporate status does not protect a participant from liability for personal misconduct.
AML, sanctions, and suspicious trade flows

Statutory sanction: Account restrictions, transaction rejection, reporting and investigation, asset measures where legally authorized, regulatory penalties, and potential criminal prosecution under applicable Egyptian AML, sanctions, and criminal laws.

Director personal liability: Directors or officers who knowingly facilitate, conceal, or fail to perform duties imposed on them may face personal regulatory or criminal exposure. Freezing, travel restrictions, and imprisonment depend on the applicable legal authority and process.
Corporate-registration and beneficial-ownership non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Regulatory or administrative penalties, correction orders, tax or AML scrutiny, and potential criminal sanctions where false statements or concealment constitute an offence.

Director personal liability: Managers and signatories may be personally liable for knowing false statements or breaches of duties imposed directly on them. Limited liability is not a defense to an individual’s own unlawful conduct.
