Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - One-Stop Shop (OSS) for Company Establishment, Ministry of Investment and International Cooperation, in coordination with the Commercial Registry Division.

Governing statute

Law No. 159 of 1981 (Egyptian Companies Law), as amended by Law No. 4 of 2018, Limited Liability Company chapter (arts. 85-126), read with Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and GAFI Executive Regulations; notarization is governed by Authentication and Audit Law No. 68 of 1947.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association (Aqd at-Ta'sis) and Articles of Association (an-Nizam al-Asasi), executed before a licensed Notary Public and deposited through the GAFI One-Stop Shop Company Registration Application (OSS Form 2).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an Egyptian LLC under Law 159/1981 as amended; however, the allotted quotas must be fully subscribed, and GAFI requires a Capital Deposit Certificate from a Central Bank of Egypt-licensed bank showing the full deposit in the LLC's under-formation account. No mandatory escrow account exists; funds are released to the company's free operating account after the incorporation certificate issues. In-kind contributions are permitted subject to independent valuation, notarization, and inclusion in the founding contract.

Funding & unlock path: The General Administration of Civil Defense (Ministry of Interior) must issue a fire-safety certificate for the registered office, conditioned on fire extinguishers, emergency exits, signage, and an approved evacuation plan; the Governorate buildings directorate must certify structural load adequacy for the occupied floor; the Ministry of Manpower performs occupational safety and health inspection under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003, requiring a workplace registration statement and, for offices above 50 employees, a dedicated safety officer.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an Egyptian LLC under Law 159/1981 as amended; however, the allotted quotas must be fully subscribed, and GAFI requires a Capital Deposit Certificate from a Central Bank of Egypt-licensed bank showing the full deposit in the LLC's under-formation account. No mandatory escrow account exists; funds are released to the company's free operating account after the incorporation certificate issues. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official registration, e-filing and e-invoicing portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg (unified tax portal; e-invoicing platform at https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a single unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) through ETA's consolidated registration procedure: (1) incorporate with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain commercial registry extract from the Ministry of Commerce; (2) execute a notarized power of attorney appointing a resident general manager or statutory representative; (3) submit through the ETA unified registration portal the certified articles of association, commercial registration, proof of address, bank account details, and board/owner resolutions; (4) complete income tax, VAT, payroll (salary tax), stamp tax and real estate tax registrations simultaneously; (5) receive the consolidated TIN certificate within approximately ten business days of a complete submission; non-resident principals with a taxable presence in Egypt must also register through a designated tax representative or fixed establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a single unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) through ETA's consolidated registration procedure: (1) incorporate with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain commercial registry extract from the Ministry of Commerce; (2) execute a notarized power of attorney appointing a resident general manager or statutory representative; (3) submit through the ETA unified registration portal the certified articles of association, commercial registration, proof of address, bank account details, and board/owner resolutions; (4) complete income tax, VAT, payroll (salary tax), stamp tax and real estate tax registrations simultaneously; (5) receive the consolidated TIN certificate within approximately ten business days of a complete submission; non-resident principals with a taxable presence in Egypt must also register through a designated tax representative or fixed establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not apply US-style check-the-box elections. The Egyptian LLC is mandatorily classified as a resident juridical person taxed at the entity level under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as substantially amended by Law No. 27 of 2022 (headline CIT rate 25% for resident companies). Available statutory elections include: (i) group consolidation election for wholly-owned Egyptian subsidiaries, subject to ETA pre-approval; (ii) joint venture election under Law No. 27 of 2022 to tax an undivided venture as a standalone entity; (iii) branch versus subsidiary primary establishment election for foreign enterprises; and (iv) advance pricing agreements for transfer pricing certainty. No hybrid mismatch check-the-box election is available under Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies (mandatory VAT registration threshold for local suppliers; non-resident persons making taxable supplies in Egypt through a fixed establishment or reliant on the reverse-charge mechanism are also required to register regardless of turnover)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) goods actually exported from Egypt and customs-cleared for exit; (b) services rendered for non-resident customers and consumed wholly outside Egypt, including electronically supplied, digital, and MMORPG online game-publishing services delivered via app store platforms to non-resident players; (c) services, financial, or digital supplies supporting export transactions. Claimant must prove that the service is performed and delivered outside Egypt, that payment is received in foreign currency from the non-resident purchaser, that the buyer is not registered for Egyptian VAT, and that no economic use of the supply occurs in Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayaniya) with actual exit stamp
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing foreign-exchange repatriation
  • Export contract or digital services subscription/payout agreement
  • App store financial statements (Apple App Store and Google Play payout reports) linked to the non-resident purchase trail
  • ETA zero-rated e-invoice with QR/clearance stamp
  • Summary of export transactions attached to the periodic VAT return (Form 24 equivalent)

E-invoicing and e-receipts are mandatory on a phased basis for taxpayers meeting ETA turnover thresholds, including digital platform operators and software publishers. Invoices must be generated through ETA-approved software or direct API integration, digitally signed with the ETA seller certificate, transmitted to the ETA e-invoice platform in near real-time using JSON/XML fiscal records, validated by the ETA engine, and stamped with an ETA-generated QR or clearance code before issuance to the buyer. An invoice failing to obtain ETA clearance is legally treated as an unissued invoice and attracts delay fines plus non-deductibility of input VAT.

ETA audit scrutiny extends to export documentation for the five-year statutory limitation period and longer in cases of fraud. Penalties include a VAT evasion fine equal to double the tax evaded with a statutory minimum, incremental delay fines for late or incomplete e-invoice clearance, fines of EGP 3,000 up to EGP 50,000 per non-cleared e-invoice, and criminal liability risk including imprisonment under Articles 71 and 73 of Law No. 67 of 2016. Export zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back with interest where foreign currency is not received within the 180-day repatriation deadline or where the underlying customs, remittance, or payout documentation is forged or materially inaccurate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-determined exchange rate (managed float) since the CBE alignment decision of 6 March 2024, which removed the previous parallel-market spread and is anchored by Egypt's IMF Extended Fund Facility. Current-account transactions — software and digital-gaming service exports, in-app purchase proceeds, foreign-currency operating receipts, and dividends/profit remittances — are freely convertible through authorized banks, subject to documentary verification (platform payout reports, ETA tax clearance, audited financials, and CBE balance-of-payments purpose codes). Profit remittance hurdles include obtaining an ETA tax clearance certificate, evidencing the original USD inflow by bank advice, and ensuring the benefiting bank account matches the Apple/Google payout beneficiary name. Capital-account transactions are not automatically convertible: inward share-capital injections, shareholder loans, and acquisition of Egyptian assets must be routed through authorized banks under declared CBE purpose codes, and outward repatriation of original capital requires proof of the originating inflow, updated Commercial Registry/GAFI filings, and audited statements. In practice, banks apply net-open-position discipline, EGP liquidity constraints, and require Arabic-notarized corporate documentation before releasing large FX conversions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Digital-gaming and software-export proceeds (classified under CBE balance-of-payments services exports) must be repatriated through an authorized Egyptian bank within 180 days from the date of the export invoice/shipment per CBE foreign-exchange instructions issued under Law No. 194 of 2020. Following the 6 March 2024 liberalization, the CBE no longer prescribes a fixed mandatory conversion ratio: exporters may retain the full foreign-currency amount in an onshore foreign-currency account at the receiving authorized bank or convert into EGP at the interbank rate; alternatively, proceeds may be netted against documented import or credit obligations with bank approval. The collecting bank must record the inflow in the CBE reporting framework, the exporter must file the applicable proceeds declaration within 30 days of receipt, and any retention offshore, unreported offset, or conversion outside the authorized banking channel constitutes a CBE violation. Consequently, the EG_LLC must direct all Apple App Store and Google Play payouts via SWIFT directly to its Egyptian USD account and maintain a complete audit trail from Store Connect payout reports to bank credits.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole national monetary and foreign-exchange authority under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Promulgating the Central Bank and Banking System Law), its Executive Regulations, and the supporting CBE foreign-exchange circulars. All foreign-currency dealings by resident Egyptian entities must be executed exclusively through CBE-authorized banks; unofficial market FX transactions or offshore settlement rings for Egyptian export proceeds are statutorily prohibited and criminally sanctionable. The Ministry of Finance, the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), and the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) are the ancillary regulators for tax and capital-account matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW — legally constrained for the EG_LLC as a resident Egyptian entity. Resident legal entities are not permitted to use unlicensed or offshore electronic-money institutions (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Revolut Business, or similar) as the primary collection or settlement rail for service-export proceeds. All export receipts must be repatriated through an authorized CBE bank; interposing an offshore EMI ring-fence would breach the CBE foreign-exchange instructions under Law 194/2020, distort the export-proceeds reporting obligation, and materially increase the risk of corporate account rejection or regulatory inquiry. Locally licensed Egyptian fintech payment providers (licensed by the CBE under the retail-payment regulatory framework) may support EGP merchant collection and payout automation, but they do not replace a Tier-1 bank for USD/EGP cross-border clearance. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions: foreign shareholders must remit by SWIFT MT103 only, using the bank's incoming-wire details, with the beneficiary field naming the EG_LLC and the payment reference stating share capital contribution. The receiving Egyptian bank posts the credit to the operational USD account and records the CBE balance-of-payments purpose code for direct-investment equity in a resident company. The LLC must then file the corresponding capital increase with the Egyptian Commercial Registry and, if GAFI-registered, with GAFI within the statutory filing window (generally 30 days from the shareholders' resolution), presenting the updated Commercial Registry extract to the bank. Shareholder or subordinated loans: remit as intercompany financing under the appropriate CBE purpose code, attaching the executed loan agreement, commercial interest-rate evidence, and repayment schedule; the bank registers the exposure in CBE monitoring, and later principal and interest repayment requires an outward remittance referencing the original inward advice. All injections must be supported by the remitter's bank reference, the LLC board resolution, and the shareholders' resolution; the bank performs expected-use verification and retains the transfer receipt for future profit repatriation matching.Operational disbursements (cloud infrastructure, ad networks, licensing, contractors, influencers): settled by SWIFT MT103 in USD/EUR/GBP via the authorized Egyptian bank's correspondent network, each payment carrying the applicable CBE purpose code (computer/software services, royalties, marketing) and supporting invoices; the bank applies liquidity and net-open-position limits with CBE post-transaction reporting. For physical procurement (merchandising, hardware, colocation equipment), the LLC may open an Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit under its import-finance line, with commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin presented; for pure digital MMORPG content, direct SWIFT MT103 vendor payments with a comfort-letter framework are standard and faster than LC processing. Dividend and profit remittances to foreign shareholders: executed by outward SWIFT MT103 under the profit-repatriation purpose code, conditional on audited financial statements, an ETA tax clearance certificate, confirmation that the distribution corresponds to declared net after-tax reserves, and a board resolution; any EGP conversion occurs at the interbank rate inside the bank. The recommended architecture is direct SWIFT settlement from Apple/Google to the onshore Tier-1 bank; an offshore parent entity, if any, may use EMIs only for incidental non-Egyptian treasury functions, never for the EG_LLC's export proceeds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 per annum in aggregate related-party transactions triggers mandatory Egyptian Local File documentation; the Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 500,000,000; group CbCR filing applies at the ultimate parent threshold established by Egyptian law (OECD-equivalent).

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no separate Economic Substance Regulations regime in the UAE sense; however, the Income Tax Law, ETA practice, and judicial anti-abuse principles require the EG LLC to demonstrate genuine commercial substance: real staff, management and control in Egypt, actual business risk, and substantive game development or publishing functions. A shell LLC claiming DTA benefits or deducting large royalty flows without substance is vulnerable to recharacterisation, denial of treaty benefits, and imposition of Egyptian-source withholding taxes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Egyptian Income Tax Law 91/2005: fine of up to 100% of the evaded tax, monthly delay surcharges, imprisonment up to five years, asset seizure, and revocation of tax registration.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and signatories who knowingly participate are jointly and severally liable; the court may issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, and impose criminal imprisonment notwithstanding the LLC's limited liability shield.
Shell company / absence of commercial substance

Statutory sanction: ETA may disregard the shell, treat the foreign parent as having a deemed Egyptian permanent establishment, deny DTA benefits, impose 20% dividend/royalty withholding, and make transfer pricing adjustments with penalties.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and foreign beneficial owners are exposed to veil-piercing: civil restitution, travel bans, freezing of foreign and domestic assets, and potential criminal liability for abuse of legal form.
Transfer pricing misstatement / documentation failure

Statutory sanction: ETA will recompute the controlled transaction at arm's length; additional CIT is assessed with monthly delay surcharge; failure to submit TP documentation results in evidence-limiting penalties and potential assessment based on available information.

Director personal liability: CFO, tax director, and members of the board who approve false TP documentation can be named as defendants in criminal tax proceedings; personal fines and imprisonment apply where the misstatement is intentional.
AML / financial sanctions / platform payout misuse

Statutory sanction: Egyptian AML Law as amended by Law 18/2021: imprisonment of three to ten years, fines from EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000, freezing orders, and closure of the payment account; Apple and Google developer accounts may also be terminated.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for wilful AML violations; travel bans, bank freezes, personal fines, and confiscation orders can be issued against individuals irrespective of the corporate entity's liability.
