E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a Tax Card (TIN) by registering with the Egyptian Tax Authority using the company commercial registry extract, lease/power-of-attorney, board resolution, and financial statements. VAT registration is mandatory if annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; application is filed online through the ETA portal, followed by issuance of a VAT certificate and activation of the e-invoice account.

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for export of tangible goods outside Egypt evidenced by customs export entry, and for offshore services supplied to non-residents with economic use outside Egypt. The exporter must hold a valid VAT registration certificate and an exporter code issued by the General Authority for Export and Import Control; goods must physically leave Egypt within the legally prescribed timeframes.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACID). Bill of Lading / Airway Bill. Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency. Bank debit/credit advice and FX repatriation proof. Copy of the export VAT zero-rating invoice. Certificate of Origin where required.
  • Supplier/registration copies evidencing VAT ID and exporter code.

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing and e-receipts through ETA's e-invoice portal. All VAT-registered taxpayers must integrate their billing systems with the ETA API for immediate clearance of each invoice; invoice data is digitally signed, stamped with a QR code, and validated in real time before issuance to buyers. Non-compliance blocks issuance and triggers automated penalty assessment.

The ETA may deny zero-rating and recover VAT at 14% plus interest from the date of supply if the export documentation is incomplete or inconsistent; fraudulent zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, daily late payment penalties (usually 2% per month for the understated VAT), criminal prosecution, and possible customs and foreign-exchange penalties. ETA and the Central Bank of Egypt also cross-check export invoices against inward remittance bank records, triggering clawback for uncollected export proceeds within the 180-day deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified managed-floating exchange rate regime following the 5-6 March 2024 liberalisation under an IMF Extended Fund Facility; the interbank USD/EGP market determines the official price and the pound is current-account convertible. Trade payments, service fees, royalties, dividends, and branch profit remittances are transferable through authorised dealing banks (AD banks) upon presentation of underlying contracts, commercial invoices, customs ACID declarations, and proof of tax settlement. Capital-account openness is partial: inbound foreign direct investment (FDI) is welcomed and must be registered in a CBE Investment Registration File (IRF), while repatriation of registered FDI principal, liquidation proceeds, and dividends is permitted only with audited financials and a Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) clearance. Practical remittance hurdles for a grocery-retail LLC include hard-currency liquidity cycles, bank-side EGP conversion requirements for local expenses, and documentary duplication across Customs, ETA, and CBE forms. Portfolio outflows and resident outward capital transfers remain prohibited or strictly conditioned on CBE approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign-exchange circulars read with Unified Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 (Arts. 91-94), an exporter must transfer the full gross export proceeds to its account maintained with an authorized bank in Egypt no later than 90 days from the bill-of-lading date for merchandise exports, extendable to 180 days for documented deferred-payment trade. Since the November 2016 FX liberalisation, no fixed mandatory conversion ratio into EGP is imposed on general merchandise exporters: 100% of the hard-currency proceeds may be retained in the exporter's foreign-currency Export Proceeds Account provided the 90-day repatriation deadline is met. The CBE retains reserved powers to order conversion of a percentage of export receipts in a balance-of-payments emergency or for exports settled under bilateral non-convertible-currency agreements. Non-repatriation is an exchange-control violation attracting criminal sanctions, fines, and bank-relationship termination under Law 194/2020 rather than a mere tax default.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Foreign Exchange Department, acting with the CBE Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Unit (AMLFU) and the Egyptian Customs Authority (ACID platform) for trade-linked foreign exchange control enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail, MEDIUM-AUXILIARY only for incidental low-value refunds or petty disbursements. Egypt does not authorise offshore cross-border electronic money institutions (EMIs) as bank counterparties; the CBE, under Law 194/2020 and the AMLFU AML framework, licenses domestic payment service providers (Meeza-branded neopay firms and a small set of fintechs) that cannot clear SWIFT MT103 corporate import flows on a standalone basis. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) may not hold EGP balances, are not admitted to Egyptian ACH or RTGS clearings, and inbound USD received from such EMIs is treated as a third-party transfer requiring full invoice and CBE form support. Reliance on an offshore EMI as the corporate settlement account would frustrate CBE import settlement registration, ACID customs declarations, and IRF reconciliation, and is therefore legally and operationally non-viable as the main clearing channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) open a Capital Escrow / Investment Registration File (IRF) account at the lead AD bank (for example National Bank of Egypt, SWIFT BIC NBEGEGCX) designated exclusively for FDI equity; (2) the foreign shareholder wires funds via SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 reciting the IRF number and the narrative 'FDI equity injection - subscription of shares in EG_LLC', instructing the remitting bank to use USD as interbank value currency with all charges borne by the sender; (3) the beneficiary AD bank credits the Capital Escrow account, books a Capital Inward Transfer, issues the Investment Registration Credit Note, and electronically notifies the CBE foreign-exchange reporting system; (4) the portion required for local working capital is converted at the unified interbank USD/EGP rate into the operational EGP account against proof of incorporation and tax registration, while unutilised USD remains in the USD operational account. Future dividend and liquidation remittances will be permitted only up to the cumulative registered IRF balance, therefore the MOA filed with the Egyptian Commercial Registry should state the injected amount as paid-up capital and the contribution should be recorded in the ETA tax card.Principal clearing rails for supplier payments: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer on open-account terms for small and high-frequency items such as condiments and packaged goods; (ii) direct documentary collections (D/P or D/A) through the AD bank for mid-value consignments; and (iii) commercial irrevocable letters of credit at sight, issued in USD by the AD bank, typically requiring 100% cash cover for a newly incorporated LLC. Mandatory conditions: filing of the Egyptian Customs ACID advance cargo declaration, CBE electronic import settlement registration (successor to the paper Form D import declaration), and presentment to the AD bank of the final commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, packing list, insurance certificate, certificate of origin, and food-safety import permits for plant-origin products. The bank will debit the EGP/USD account only after full verification of the underlying trade documentation. Egyptian domestic instant-payment rails (IPS) are not applicable to cross-border USD or LC clearing but may be used for intra-Egypt settlement of customs duties, VAT, and logistics costs.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP outright forwards with tenors of 1 to 12 months written by the AD bank against documented committed import payables, subject to collateral or margin of 10-15% for new clients, with no speculative rollover beyond the underlying trade schedule.USD/EGP foreign-exchange swaps with the AD bank for temporary conversion of retained export proceeds or USD import sinking funds into EGP working capital and subsequent clawback into USD at the same bank on a booked trade-flow basis.Natural hedge structure utilising a designated USD Import Sinking Fund sourced from retained hard-currency sales and registered export proceeds, preserving an offset against future USD payables with zero speculative trading; exchange-traded options and offshore non-deliverable forwards referencing the EGP are not authorised instruments for onshore retail corporates under current CBE practice.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% on net taxable income (Law 91/2005, Art. 51, as amended by Law 75/2015); no separate municipal corporate profit tax for onshore retail entities

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on cash and in-kind dividend distributions made by Egyptian LLCs to resident and non-resident shareholders (Law 91/2005, Art. 5, as amended by Law 83/2015); the tax is levied at the source on the gross distribution without imputation credit

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and Master File are mandatory where aggregate controlled transactions with any single related party exceed EGP 30,000,000 per fiscal year; simplified TP documentation and disclosure through the TP Information Form attached to the annual CIT return are required where controlled transactions with a related party exceed EGP 6,000,000 annually

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but economic substance is enforced through the GAAR in Law 91/2005, Art. 59, place-of-effective-management analysis, PE deconstruction practices, deemed-profit estimation powers, and anti-conduit interpretations in treaty application; the Asian supermarket must evidence operational substance through physical store premises, committed personnel, POS sales records, audited stock holdings, Egyptian bank accounts, and local management autonomy in order to survive ETA substance review and tax-treaty benefit audits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Concealment or False Records

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years and fines of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000 together with recovery of evaded tax under Law 91/2005; administrative surcharge of 6% per month on the concealed tax; statute of limitations extended to ten years for fraudulent assessments

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who sign false declarations face criminal prosecution, personal civil liability for evaded tax and surcharges, attachment of personal assets, travel bans, and blocking of outward remittances by ETA enforcement order
Shell Company / Sham Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: ETA disregard of the corporate form under the GAAR, retroactive deemed-profit assessment at 22.5% CIT, monthly 6% delay surcharge, fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 20,000 for non-maintenance of accounting books, and revocation of the commercial registry license

Director personal liability: Individuals named as officers may be personally enforced for corporate tax debt through civil attachment; courts have imposed asset freezes and immigration watchlist measures; nominee directors face criminal liability for false attestation in commercial registration filings
AML Violation / Unlicensed Remittance Conduct

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to EGP 100,000 per violation under AML Law 80/2002 as amended; EY and Financial Intelligence Unit freezing orders; imprisonment of one to seven years where money-laundering predicates are established; cancellation of the grocery retail license

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are exposed to criminal prosecution even when the LLC is the facilitation instrument; travel bans are routinely imposed during FIU investigations; confiscation of proceeds and laundering instruments may reach personal assets of the officers
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Dividend WHT Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Upward TP adjustment to the arm's-length result with an additional margin penalty of 25% of the adjustment; disallowance of disguised fees and interest; application of the 20% withholding framework on recharacterized distributions; fines for non-filing of the TP Information Form up to EGP 50,000

Director personal liability: Officers who execute circular intercompany documentation are jointly liable for the withheld dividend tax, recharacterization assessments, and surcharges; evidence of intentional fee circularization supports prosecutions for tax evasion; statutory representatives may be barred from holding tax clearance certificates, thereby freezing access to banking facilities and procurement contracts
