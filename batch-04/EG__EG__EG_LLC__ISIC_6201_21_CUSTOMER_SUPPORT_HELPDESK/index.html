Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - One-Stop Shop / Integrated Services Office, acting with the Commercial Registry of the Ministry of Trade and Industry.

Governing statute

Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 (as amended), together with the Executive Regulations issued by Prime Minister's Decree No. 96 of 1982; limited liability companies are further subject to the Commercial Registration Law and the unified VAT and tax registration rules administered by the Egyptian Tax Authority.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Contract (Eaqd al-Tasis) and annexed Articles of Association, submitted through the GAFI company registration electronic portal, with registration effected in the Commercial Register (Qayd al-Sharikat).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 42 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an LLC under the Investment Law; the capital must be divided into quotas, fully subscribed at incorporation and paid up within the statutory period not exceeding five years; a bank certificate confirming the capital deposit or escrow account in Egypt is required by GAFI before registration.

Funding & unlock path: The LLC must obtain a fire safety inspection certificate from the General Administration for Civil Defense of the Ministry of Interior confirming working fire extinguishers, emergency exit signage, smoke detectors and evacuation arrangements; a structural/occupational clearance of the leased floor shall be issued by a licensed engineering office verifying floor loading for IT equipment; the Ministry of Manpower provides an occupational health and safety clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 covering workstations, ergonomics, first aid kits, and electrical safety for the helpdesk staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an LLC under the Investment Law; the capital must be divided into quotas, fully subscribed at incorporation and paid up within the statutory period not exceeding five years; a bank certificate confirming the capital deposit or escrow account in Egypt is required by GAFI before registration.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) The founders, having subscribed to the Company's entire share capital, shall cause such capital to be deposited in the designated bank account opened in the name of the LLC under formation; (2) The Company adopts its registered office and legal address at the premises located in Cairo, Egypt, as specified in the incorporation contract, and the General Manager is authorized to execute the lease agreement and register the address with the competent authorities; (3) The General Manager, Financial Manager, and Company Secretary are appointed, and the General Manager is authorized to execute all formation documents and complete filings before the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones, the Egyptian Tax Authority, the Commercial Registry, and any other governmental offices.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable - the Helpdesk SaaS activity is classified as a non-industrial Information Technology and IT-Enabled Service; no manufacturing or industrial operating license from the Industrial Development Authority (IDA) is required; the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MCIT) supervises the ICT sector generally.

Category: None required as a statutory industrial permit; however, if the LLC processes the personal data of end users or client firms, the company should consider filing the voluntary/required Data Controller Registration with the Data Protection Authority under the Personal Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020; a consumer assistance helpline serving Egyptian consumers may also require a service-practice notification to the Consumer Protection Agency.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — unified TIN/tax card registry and VAT administration; official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipt platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg. Company incorporation and tax registration are co-administered with GAFI (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones) through the one-stop shop.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Form EG_LLC with GAFI (100% foreign ownership allowed, no minimum capital) and complete Commercial Registry filing. Step 2: File the ETA establishment declaration and issue a power of attorney to a local accredited tax representative within 90 days of commercial registration to obtain the unified tax card (TIN). Step 3: Register for VAT on mandatory Form No. (1) VAT within 30 days of the date taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000, or earlier if voluntary registration is opted; ETA issues the VAT certificate under the same TIN coding. Step 4: Enroll in the ETA e-invoicing master data file and obtain API credentials/golden certificate before issuing any B2B/B2G invoice. Step 5: For export zero-rating, register the company in the ETA exporters register, open the export file, and provide the Egyptian bank account details for Central Bank of Egypt FX tracking.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident EG_LLC taxed on worldwide income at 22.5% CIT; non-resident sellers without PE taxed only on Egyptian-source income via withholding/deemed-profit rules; SCZone/Free-Zone licensed entities can elect exemption windows but pure SaaS helpdesk operations normally remain in the ordinary 22.5% regime)

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election; ETA applies source rules and the general anti-avoidance rule (Art. 37 Income Tax Law 91/2005). Available statutory elections: (i) deemed-profit election under Art. 63 for non-resident service providers without PE (15% of gross as taxable profit); (ii) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (iii) cash-basis or invoice-basis VAT accounting election where bookkeeping standards permit; (iv) Free Zone/SCZone investment exemption election under Investment Law 72/2017 — not advantageous for B2B export services because the 0% VAT export regime already eliminates indirect tax leakage; (v) export incentive and penalty-relief election under the 2025 Tax Facilitation Package (Law 12/2025) preserving FX-based export benefits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies (VAT Art. 15). Foreign non-resident digital/e-services suppliers selling into Egypt without a local establishment must register for VAT irrespective of threshold under ETA Decree No. 633/2021; imports of the foreign parent SaaS platform into Egypt by EG_LLC trigger 14% reverse-charge VAT on the intercompany fees.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies under VAT Law Art. 24/1 when the helpdesk/SaaS service is supplied by EG_LLC to a non-resident recipient, is consumed entirely outside Egypt (determined by customer location and benefit, not call-agent location), is paid in foreign currency, and is evidenced as an export in the ETA exporters register. Mixed-use or onshore assist elements such as serving Egyptian end-customers remain standard-rated at 14% and must be separately invoiced. Intercompany trials, dormant environments, or non-FX settlements will be recharacterized as taxable domestic supplies.

  • Signed export service contract or master helpdesk subscription agreement with the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice and ETA-cleared e-invoice marked Export (0%) with ETA QR code and clearance UUID
  • ETA export register/export file registration confirmation for the service transaction
  • Bank credit advice or Foreign Currency Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from an Egyptian authorized bank evidencing FX receipt
  • Central Bank of Egypt export proceeds repatriation confirmation (FX credited/surrendered to EG_LLC Egyptian bank account)
  • Service delivery and off-shore usage evidence: IP logs, non-resident customer contact details, usage analytics, and a certificate confirming non-Egyptian consumption
  • Goods-export annex (only when SaaS is embedded or ancillary to physical goods): customs export declaration and Bill of Lading/Airway Bill

Mandatory real-time B2B e-invoicing and B2C e-receipts phased in by ETA (large taxpayers 2021; all taxpayers in subsequent tranches through 2023-2024). Every invoice/receipt must be generated via ETA-approved systems, submitted within seconds to the ETA clearing platform (einvoicing.eta.gov.eg) through REST/API integration authenticated with the golden certificate, and bear an ETA-generated UUID, QR code and digital tracking stamp to be legally valid. Zero-rated export invoices must carry the Export transaction-type code, foreign customer TIN/country code, and the applicable FX rate per the ETA schema. Offline invoicing is prohibited; rejected invoices are legally void, force output VAT recovery, and block input VAT deduction.

ETA cross-matches zero-rated invoices against bank FX inflows; if foreign-currency proceeds are not credited within the CBE window (typically 180 days for service exports), the 0% claim is retrospectively clawed back at 14% plus 2% monthly delay interest without goodwill. Fraudulent or false export claims trigger VAT evasion penalties (Art. 71-74, Law 67/2016): minimum fine EGP 20,000, maximum EGP 200,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, forfeiture of input VAT credits, and joint personal liability of the tax representative and directors. E-invoice clearance misuse adds fines up to EGP 50,000 per invoice under Decree 501/2022 plus suspension of the exporter registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the 6 March 2024 exchange-rate unification and the adoption of a market-determined, durably flexible exchange-rate regime under the IMF Extended Fund Facility, the Egyptian pound (EGP) is freely tradable through CBE-licensed banks within a managed float. Current-account convertibility is substantially liberalized: cross-border payments for services, including SaaS subscriptions, cloud-hosting fees, intercompany service charges, and customer-support helpdesk software licenses, may be executed by authorized banks upon submission of the underlying commercial documents and a CBE balance-of-payments (BOP) purpose-code declaration. Capital-account convertibility remains administratively controlled: inbound equity injections require registration through an authorized bank under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and General Authority for Investment (GAFI) procedures, whereas outbound profit remittances and capital repatriations require audited financial statements, an Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) clearance certificate, and proof that the foreign currency was lawfully acquired through the onshore banking system. Key practical hurdles include bank-level FX liquidity windows, enhanced purpose-code scrutiny on large invoices, and the need to maintain a clean, auditable FX origination trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE rules implementing Law No. 194 of 2020, all export proceeds from services and goods must be received into an Egyptian authorized bank and repatriated to Egypt within 90 days of the transaction effective date for services, extendable to 180 days with documented justification and bank approval. After the March 2024 liberalization, no mandatory conversion ratio applies: the prior emergency surrender requirement was suspended, and exporters may retain 100% of repatriated proceeds in onshore foreign-currency export or operational accounts or convert them at interbank rates. Compliance requires the LLC to: (i) submit software-export contracts and invoice cohorts to the receiving bank; (ii) tag each inbound digital-services receipt with the correct CBE BOP services code; (iii) route all proceeds exclusively to a CBE-licensed onshore account, because receipts held at offshore EMI or PSP accounts are not recognized as valid repatriation; and (iv) retain the complete CBE FX transaction file for at least five years for audit.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), exercising exclusive jurisdiction under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 and the accompanying foreign-exchange regulations, with delegated authority over authorized dealing banks, balance-of-payments reporting, capital-account administration, and all cross-border settlement currency approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM for ancillary use only; LOW as a primary rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Currencycloud are not recognized by the CBE as banks or licensed payment-service providers and may not maintain EGP-denominated deposits or clear into the Egyptian interbank EGP system. They may operate solely as collection and aggregation gateways downstream of Stripe Billing and PayPal, provided the merchant agreements are in the LLC's name, funds are swept at least weekly via SWIFT MT103 to the CBE-licensed onshore operational account, and the EMI is not contractually designated as the CBE-recognized repatriation account. Any unlicensed foreign entity offering payment services to Egyptian residents would face CBE enforcement. Accordingly, the compliant architecture requires an onshore licensed bank as the sole liquidity hub; EMIs are permitted only as accelerant rails for aggregator receipts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder shall subscribe equity via SWIFT MT103 to the LLC's onshore foreign-currency account at an authorized bank. The payment order must include the correct CBE BOP purpose code for inward direct investment in equity, a narrative field stating 'Capital subscription to [LLC name], Commercial Register [number], Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 registration [IFD number]', and a reference to the capital-increase board resolution to preempt unidentified-inflow flags. The bank will verify the investor's GAFI-filed incorporation file, apostilled board resolutions, the UBO/shareholding list, and the LLC's commercial-register capacity to receive the amount; no minimum capital requirement blocks the subscription. Shareholder loans must be routed under the CBE intercompany-lending purpose code with a signed term sheet to prevent recharacterization as equity or deemed distribution. For any future exit or dividend repatriation, the LLC must present audited financials, ETA clearance, and the bank-issued evidence of registration of the initial inflow to activate the CBE repatriation allowance.SaaS and trade disbursements are settled via SWIFT MT103 for single outbound payments, or, where bank-requested security applies, through a commercial letter of credit issued by the authorized bank; LCs are primarily relevant to goods rather than digital services. Each outbound MT103 must carry the correct CBE outbound services purpose code, such as computer and data-processing services, royalties, license fees, or import of services, and be supported by the underlying contract or order, the supplier invoice, and non-resident beneficiary withholding documentation where applicable under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 or an applicable double-tax treaty. Banks will not release FX above their internal invoice-clearing threshold without ETA remittance clearance. For recurring payouts to global cloud and helpdesk sub-processors, the LLC should implement an approved aggregate-invoice sweep arrangement, processing weekly or monthly batch MT103s against one statement of work, to minimize redundant KYC on each debit. The onshore FX account should maintain a minimum 45-day disbursement runway to avoid forced conversions in illiquid interbank windows. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM for ancillary use only; LOW as a primary rail. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Currencycloud are not recognized by the CBE as banks or licensed payment-service providers and may not maintain EGP-denominated deposits or clear into the Egyptian interbank EGP system. They may operate solely as collection and aggregation gateways downstream of Stripe Billing and PayPal, provided the merchant agreements are in the LLC's name, funds are swept at least weekly via SWIFT MT103 to the CBE-licensed onshore operational account, and the EMI is not contractually designated as the CBE-recognized repatriation account. Any unlicensed foreign entity offering payment services to Egyptian residents would face CBE enforcement. Accordingly, the compliant architecture requires an onshore licensed bank as the sole liquidity hub; EMIs are permitted only as accelerant rails for aggregator receipts.
