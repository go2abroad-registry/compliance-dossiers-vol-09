Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; unified e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing/e-receipts platform: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC, the manager must first obtain incorporation certificate from GAFI and the Commercial Register (no statutory minimum capital). The MFO must also hold its FRA capital-markets concession before commencing activity. Within 30 days of commercial registration the entity files with ETA for a Tax Card (unified TIN) providing the notarized memorandum of association, commercial registration, premises lease, board resolution, beneficial-owner declarations and director identity papers. VAT registration under VAT Law 67/2016 is separate: registration is compulsory when taxable supplies reach EGP 500,000 per annum, or earlier when the company intends to claim input VAT credits, export zero-rating, or import with VAT deferral; the VAT ID is stamped onto the same Tax Card. A non-resident without an Egyptian branch must appoint a resident fiscal representative jointly and severally liable. Final step: activation of the ETA e-invoice account and API integration for billing software.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident legal person subject to corporate income tax on worldwide profits at the standard CIT rate of 22.5% under Law 91/2005; no pass-through treatment exists.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through election is available; LLC treatment is mandatory under Egyptian law. Permitted elections are limited to: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (ii) election to classify designated service contracts as exported services for 0% VAT; (iii) for licensed projects, election to operate under a General Free Zone or Suez Canal Economic Zone regime (annual fee in lieu of customs/VAT; 0% CIT on export profits); (iv) treaty-based relief elections for foreign investors; (v) irrevocable e-invoicing integration go-live elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 aggregate annual value of taxable supplies; importers are also liable to register from the first import, regardless of turnover. Voluntary registration is allowed below the threshold, mainly to recover input VAT and support export zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated under VAT Law Art. 26(1) when physically exported outside Egypt within the timeframe fixed in the Executive Regulations and in any event within the CBE's 180-day FX-repatriation window, supported by customs release and conversion of proceeds in convertible currency through an Egyptian bank. Services are zero-rated under Art. 26(2) when supplied to a non-resident recipient for use and performance wholly outside Egypt, paid from abroad in foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system. For this domestic-licensed MFO, only genuinely offshore discretionary advisory mandates meeting those tests qualify as exported services; portfolio management and custody provided to resident or non-resident clients within Egypt fall under the VAT-exempt financial-services list, which suppresses output VAT and blocks input VAT recovery.

  • ETA-matched Customs Export Declaration (Egyptian customs port release/exit statement) reflecting exact invoice value and tariff classification
  • Original negotiable Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR border export document naming the Egyptian LLC as consignor with Egypt as origin
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign-Currency Proceeds Certificate evidencing full consideration credited to the LLC's Egyptian bank account within the statutory 180-day repatriation deadline
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list in the ETA e-invoice format carrying the ETA-validated QR code and the statutory zero-rating export clause
  • Executed international sale contract or advisory engagement agreement setting out Incoterms for goods, or the performance location and non-Egyptian utilization clauses for offshore services
  • For offshore MFO services: SWIFT interbank credit evidence (Custody_Wire rail), updated KYC and beneficial-ownership file of the non-resident client, engagement/portfolio advisory mandates, and minutes or digital records proving delivery outside Egyptian territory
  • Copy of the ETA zero-rating request/acknowledgment and import-export registration file where the exporter was required to obtain prior approval of the export classification

All VAT registrants must integrate their billing systems with ETA REST APIs and transmit each B2B invoice in the ETA XML/JSON schema (UBL 2.1-aligned, Arabic-first) at the moment of issuance for real-time centralized validation. ETA assigns a unique invoice identifier and digitally stamps the invoice with an RSA-SHA256 QR code (ISO/IEC 18004); the seller must render that QR stamp on the invoice copy sent to the buyer. An invoice without an ETA-validated code is legally non-compliant and cannot support input VAT deduction or zero-rating. E-receipts are mandatory for B2C transactions; customs and banking records are automatically reconciled with the e-invoice dataset.

ETA automatically reconciles every zero-rated e-invoice against customs export declarations, bank FX-receipt records and corporate e-filings; credit is withdrawn if the export window is missed, proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, documents are falsified, or the service is found to be consumed in Egypt. Fraudulent claims trigger immediate clawback of the VAT refund, reassessment at 14% VAT with statutory late-payment surcharges, and the evasion penalties of VAT Law 67/2016 (imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines). Repeated or organized falsification leads to suspension of the ETA e-invoice clearance account, blacklisting in the Customs risk engine, and referrals for money-laundering scrutiny under AML Law 80/2002.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-floating EGP with full current-account convertibility for genuine trade, services, dividends, and interest remittances, subject to documentary checks at CBE-licensed banks; the capital account remains partially convertible and every inbound equity or shareholder-loan injection must pass source-of-funds verification, GAFI company-registry capitalization, and CBE balance-of-payments purpose-code classification. Profit-remittance hurdles include: (i) an electronic tax-clearance certificate demonstrating settled corporate income tax, VAT, and stamp duties; (ii) bank-level review lasting five to ten business days triggered by the ISIC 6430 high-risk financial classification; (iii) exact beneficiary matching in MT103 field 59 and Remittance Information field 72; and (iv) the periodic scarcity of USD/EUR interbank liquidity, during which the CBE priorities framework may defer discretionary outbound foreign-currency transfers. EGP is not deeply deliverable offshore, so hedgeable foreign exchange is concentrated in onshore forwards, FX swaps, and offshore NDF reference pricing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE Foreign Exchange Regulations require 100% of goods or service export proceeds to enter Egypt through a licensed domestic bank: the statutory repatriation window is 7 business days from the maturity/due date of the collection document and in all cases no later than 30 calendar days from the bill-of-lading date for goods, or 30 days from the invoice due date for services and advisory fees. The mandatory conversion/surrender ratio to EGP is 0% after the November 2016 exchange-rate liberalization, meaning the exporter or the Multy-Family Office receiving service fees may credit export receipts to a foreign-currency account without forced conversion to EGP, but the receiving bank must report the proceeds to the CBE within 7 working days of credit. Failure to repatriate within the timeline triggers penalty interest, denial of new import facilities, and liabilities under Law No. 194 of 1980 as amended by Law No. 80 of 2015.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) as the principal exchange-control authority, acting jointly with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) on inbound capital registration and with the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) on portfolio-advisory licensing of the Multi-Family Office; the Egyptian Money Laundering Combating Unit (EMLCU) performs AML/CFT pre-clearance and suspicious-transaction oversight for the High_Risk_Special_License entity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary domestic clearing host; Medium as a foreign-currency staging corridor. Egypt's Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and the CBE non-bank licensing regime reserve the interbank clearing rails (EGYPS/RTGS and instant payment systems) to CBE-licensed banks and licensed fintechs admitted to the CBE Regulatory Sandbox. Offshore EMIs, including FCA-regulated UK EMIs and EU PSD2 payment institutions, cannot hold EGP-denominated client floats, cannot onboard Egyptian residents as local payment-account holders, and must not be presented as the host bank of the EG_LLC in any CBE filing. The permissible use is limited to collecting non-EGP family subscriptions under custody wrappers and initiating SWIFT transfers to the onshore escrow account; the EMI must not serve as clearing counterparty for the Egyptian entity's domestic operating expenses.

The permissible use is limited to collecting non-EGP family subscriptions under custody wrappers and initiating SWIFT transfers to the onshore escrow account; the EMI must not serve as clearing counterparty for the Egyptian entity's domestic operating expenses.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions (goods, services, intangibles, financial) exceed EGP 15,000,000 per tax year; Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 1,000,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting notification is triggered for groups with consolidated revenue above EGP 10,000,000,000. Even where thresholds are not met, the MFO must complete the transfer-pricing disclosure annex, retain contemporaneous benchmarking documentation, and file it with Form 19.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance discipline is enforced through: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) recharacterizing artificial or sham arrangements; (ii) OECD-aligned beneficial-ownership and permanent establishment tests applied by the ETA to all DTA relief and free-zone claims; (iii) CFC and branch rules; and (iv) sectoral licensing by the FRA and Central Bank of Egypt requiring physical premises, resident directors, risk and strategy committees meeting in Egypt, and a locally employed compliance staff. A shell Egyptian MFO with all decision-making offshore, no payroll, and no substantive local control will be denied treaty benefits, fully re-assessed on the advisory fee base, and exposed to license revocation and deemed-profit taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Income Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 91 of 2005: imprisonment of up to 5 years plus a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000; the ETA recovers the full understated tax plus the 2%-per-month delay surcharge (capped at 100%), and the taxpayer's tax-clearance certificate is suspended, freezing all profit repatriation, banking clearances, and government transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors and the actual officer in charge are jointly and severally liable for the assessed tax, surcharges, and administrative fines; deliberate concealment triggers criminal prosecution, travel bans, seizure of personal accounts, and disqualification from managing licensed financial entities.
Shell Company / DTA Abuse (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: The ETA recharacterizes the arrangement under the GAAR: denial of treaty relief, reclassification of the entire fee base as Egyptian-source income, statutory WHT assessment at 10%-20%, transfer-pricing adjustments, delay surcharges of 2% per month (capped at 100%), and revocation of any free-zone or SCZONE status obtained on fictitious productivity claims.

Director personal liability: Directors signing treaty-relief and substance declarations face criminal liability for false attestations under Article 134 of Law 91 of 2005; the actual manager is personally exposed to civil restitution for off-shored fees re-sourced to Egypt, bank account freezes, and travel bans while the ETA's fraud investigation proceeds.
AML / Licensing Breach (High-Risk MFO)

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002: imprisonment of 3 to 7 years and fines of EGP 50,000 to EGP 1,000,000 for laundering-linked conduct; administrative sanctions of up to EGP 5,000,000 by the FRA; immediate license revocation, mandatory liquidation, and referral to the ETA for deemed-profit assessment on unexplained turnovers.

Director personal liability: Criminal prosecution of directors and the AML compliance officer; personal asset seizure, travel bans, revocation of 'fit and proper' status, and a 5-to-10-year prohibition from managing any financial, investment, or portfolio-advisory company in Egypt.
Improper Profit Repatriation / WHT Under-Withholding

Statutory sanction: The ETA will apply debt-equity recharacterization, restating wired funds as dividends, and impose the 10% WHT plus the 2%-per-month delay surcharge (capped at 100% of the tax); administrative fines for non-filed Form 41 statements follow, and the tax-clearance certificate remains suspended until full settlement of principal, surcharges, and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered non-withheld distributions are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted WHT and penalties; if offshore routing documentation exists, criminal evasion charges attach personally; travel bans and personal asset execution are instruments available to the ETA's recovery unit.
