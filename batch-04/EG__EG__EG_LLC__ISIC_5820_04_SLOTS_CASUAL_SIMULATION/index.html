Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Company Establishment Department and One-Stop Shop (OSS), Cairo, in coordination with the Commercial Registry (Ministry of Trade and Industry) and the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA).

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 (as amended by Law No. 4 of 2009), Chapter III governing Limited Liability Companies; Executive Regulations issued by Prime Ministerial Decree No. 2526 of 2010; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 (Arts. 38-40) establishing One-Stop Shop incorporation procedures.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association (Aqd wa Nizam al-Taasis), notarized before the Ministry of Justice Notary Public, together with the GAFI incorporation application form and supporting annexes.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to limited liability companies; share capital is fixed at founders' discretion and stated in the Articles of Association. Capital must be fully subscribed and fully paid at incorporation; cash contributions must be deposited in a licensed Egyptian bank account and evidenced by a no-withdrawal Capital Deposit Certificate nameable in favor of GAFI, while in-kind contributions require independent valuation under the Executive Regulations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under Ministry of Finance; official e-services and clearance portals: www.eta.gov.eg (registration, returns, Tax Card issuance) and einvoicing.eta.gov.eg (mandatory e-invoice/e-receipt fiscal clearance).

E-government filing portal

Within 30 days of commencing activity, the foreign-owned LLC must: (i) complete GAFI/commercial registry incorporation; (ii) file the ETA Tax Registration Application at the competent tax office where the head office is located, attaching the certified commercial register extract, MOA/AOA, board resolution appointing the local manager, POAs, passports of beneficial owners, and lease/title deed of the registered office; (iii) obtain the Tax Card (unified taxpayer ID) and automatically be assigned to the ETA 'large/medium/small taxpayer' category; (iv) file a separate VAT registration declaration under Law 67/2016 when taxable annual turnover reaches EGP 500,000, or apply voluntarily below that threshold; (v) obtain e-invoicing credentials (ETA sandbox production registration) and register an authorized integrator/software for API clearance. Non-resident entities may complete registration through a duly appointed resident tax representative; no minimum capital is statutorily required for the LLC to obtain TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Egyptian LLC is a resident company taxed on worldwide income at 22.5% corporate income tax under Law 91/2005; no pass-through/check-the-box available; foreign shareholders taxed only on Egyptian-source distributions/dividends subject to treaty relief; digital export receipts qualify as Egyptian-source income subject to entity-level tax with corresponding foreign tax credit where applicable).

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide check-the-box or hybrid entity elections. Available statutory elections include: (i) unified tax group filing for 100%-owned Egyptian subsidiaries (Law 91/2005 Art. 52); (ii) VAT cash-basis accounting election for taxpayers below EGP 10 million annual turnover; (iii) participation exemption upon election and statutory holding conditions for dividends from Egyptian and treaty-country subsidiaries; (iv) loss carry-forward set-off election against subsequent profits without expiry, subject to ownership continuity and MoF approval; (v) DTA relief election for reduced withholding rates; (vi) installment/mediation election under the Unified Tax Procedures Law 90/2022 for settlement of disputed assessments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods and/or services, determined cumulatively per calendar year, after which mandatory VAT registration is triggered; voluntary registration permitted below threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies when physical export is evidenced by the Egyptian customs export declaration. Services: 0% applies when (i) the service is performed/consumed entirely outside Egypt, (ii) the recipient is a non-resident person, and (iii) consideration is invoiced in foreign currency and fully remitted through an Egyptian bank account. For simulated social casino/slots digital content distributed via Apple IAP/Google Play Billing, zero-rating is available as an export of digital services only if the platform settlement reports identify non-resident end-user purchases and the net proceeds (after platform commissions) are repatriated in convertible foreign currency; Egyptian-market or Egypt-card purchases remain subject to 14% VAT. The ETA requires proof that the virtual content is not supplied through a local payment gateway, local branch, or local promotion channel.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASED/AES automated export release for the period if physical goods involved)
  • ETA-cleared commercial VAT invoice (with clearance code and QR)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, or platform settlement report (App Store Connect / Google Play Console aggregate financial statements) as transport/delivery evidence for digital export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a CBE-licensed bank evidencing full foreign-currency receipt and credit to the LLC's Egyptian account
  • Apple/Google payout statements matching the invoiced amount, net of platform commissions, for the applicable accounting period

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance for all B2B e-invoices and B2C e-receipts: the taxpayer's ERP/accounting system must transmit standardized XML/JSON invoices to the ETA Fiscal Clearance API (or the ETA web portal for low-volume filers), undergo validation and digital signing, and receive a unique ETA clearance code and QR code before the document is issued to the counterparty. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlement documents issued to the foreign digital storefronts must be supported by ETA-cleared e-invoices/credit notes; failure to clear triggers per-invoice fines (EGP 20,000–50,000), disallowance of input VAT deduction, and cascading penalty interest.

ETA audits export zero-rating strictly: if the taxpayer fails to close foreign-currency receipts within the repatriation deadline or the documentation set is incomplete/inconsistent, the ETA retroactively assesses 14% VAT on the gross export proceeds, disallows the associated input VAT recovery, and adds late-payment interest at 1.5%–2% per month under the Unified Tax Procedures Law 90/2022. Fraudulent export substantiation (false customs declarations, circular remittances, fabricated platform reports) triggers fines of EGP 50,000–1,000,000, imprisonment exposure up to 5 years for tax evasion, joint personal liability of the site director/manager, and revocation of the export registration code; the standard retrospective assessment window is 5 years, extending to 6 years for evasion cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-floating exchange rate system for the Egyptian pound. Current-account convertibility is broadly permitted for licensed trade, services, and profit remittances subject to bank validation of underlying documents; capital-account transactions remain carefully administrated, requiring CBE registration and documentary substantiation for equity injections, loans, and repatriations. Practical hurdles include bank-level scrutiny of foreign exchange purposes, potential dollar liquidity shortages that delay settlement, and strict CBE oversight of invoice matching and deferred foreign-exchange obligations in order to deter parallel-market speculation. Profit remittance is legally permitted with audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and an underlying statutory basis, but a foreign investor may encounter queuing or allocation constraints in times of balance-of-payments pressure. A Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) license can ease some administrative burdens for qualifying export-oriented or logistics-linked entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Foreign Trade Regulations and Banking Law No. 194/2020, export proceeds from physical and commercial exports must be repatriated to an Egyptian bank account within five months from the date of shipment unless a longer period is expressly approved by the CBE. All proceeds must be credited in a convertible foreign currency; the mandatory conversion to Egyptian pounds was abolished after the November 2016 currency float. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in a CBE-licensed foreign-currency account or convert at the interbank rate according to their liquidity needs. There is no fixed statutory surrender percentage; however, any balance remaining unconverted must be fully declared and auditable, and proceeds used to settle CBE-approved external obligations require separate authorization.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Foreign Exchange Operations and Financial Institutions Supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low rated for direct operation. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer are not licensed to hold customer balances or perform domestic clearing in Egypt. Payment services must be delivered through CBE-licensed banks or through approved Payment Service Providers under the CBE FinTech Regulatory Sandbox. A non-resident EMI seeking onshore operation would need a separate CBE payment institution license, a physical compliance presence in Egypt, and full AML/KYC integration with the national payments switch. For cross-border liquidity purposes, an EMI domiciled in an EU or UK jurisdiction can only act as an upstream settlement facility for the Egyptian treasury; all final EGP clearing must occur through a correspondent bank relationship and the Egyptian bank's SWIFT account.

Equity capital is routed by a foreign parent as a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer to the Egyptian bank, with the remittance information field clearly labeled using the CBE statistical purpose code for 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Contribution' followed by the four-digit company identifier.
