Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, 15 Lateef El-Barghary Street, Nasr City, Cairo; unified e-services portal https://www.eta.gov.eg with integrated e-invoice/e-receipt, e-filing, and customs single-window (ASYCUDA World) access.

E-government filing portal

The foreign equity provider must first secure the CBE payment-gateway licence (National Payment Council Law 18/2019) and GAFI incorporation of EG_LLC under Companies Law 159/1981. Statutory TIN procurement: (1) file the ETA registration application (Unified Tax Law No. 85/2020) within 30 days of commercial registration at the competent ETA office; (2) attach GAFI incorporation certificate, MOA, commercial register extract, notarised power of attorney, and the directors'/shareholders' ID documentation; (3) obtain the Permanent Tax Card (Permanent TIN) unifying income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and e-invoicing identifiers; (4) activate ETA e-invoice/e-receipt digital certificates. VAT registration is compulsory within 60 days once taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 per annum, and voluntary below the threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - EG_LLC is an Egyptian resident juridical person (incorporated in Egypt, central management in Cairo) taxed on worldwide income at the 22.5% corporate rate (Law 91/2005 as amended; 25% for banks/oil, 40% for exploration). No pass-through is available for an LLC; dividends remitted to non-resident shareholders bear 10% withholding tax. A foreign branch would instead be taxed on a deemed 65% taxable base with an additional 5% remittance tax - not applicable to EG_LLC.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Egyptian tax law; classification follows the registered civil-law form. Available/notable mechanisms: (i) branch of foreign company may apply the deemed 65%-of-profits taxable-base computation instead of full cost allocation; (ii) ETA discretionary VAT group registration for integrated entities; (iii) mainland LLC has no free-zone 0% regime election; (iv) consolidated/group income tax filing is not permitted, each entity files independently; (v) tax treaty relief (double-taxation treaty network) requires prior ETA authorization for reduced withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory); voluntary registration is available below threshold; foreign service providers importing services are subject to 14% reverse-charge VAT irrespective of threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export goods (VAT Law 67/2016 Art. 24) and services physically/economically rendered to non-residents qualify for 0% VAT, including cross-border acquiring, clearing, and payment-processing services provided to foreign merchants, provided the merchant of record is a non-resident and the benefit is used outside Egypt. The PSP must evidence the foreign counterparty, issue an ETA-cleared zero-rated e-invoice, and show full foreign-currency receipt via Egyptian banks; services partly rendered to Egyptian merchants are apportioned and standard-rated.

  • Customs export declaration (ECS) processed through ASYCUDA World / the Egyptian Single Administrative Window
  • Bill of lading or air waybill (or truck manifest) evidencing physical departure from Egypt for goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian bank evidencing credit of the export proceeds in convertible currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin issued to the foreign buyer
  • Foreign merchant contract / PSP merchant agreement demonstrating non-resident status of the beneficiary
  • ETA-cleared zero-rated e-invoice carrying the QR fiscal code
  • CBE-licensed PSP operator approval and AML/CTF (Law 80/2002) compliance records for the transaction

ETA e-invoicing regime (Ministerial Decision 161/2020 and successive roll-out phases 1-7, 2021-2025): all resident companies must integrate their ERP/billing systems with the ETA e-invoice platform through public REST/SOAP APIs; each zero-rated export or domestic invoice is transmitted in near-real-time and validated before issuance (fiscal clearance). The ETA returns a digitally stamped invoice bearing a unique QR-code fiscal code; non-clearance blocks issuance and exposes the taxpayer to fines. The PSP must implement e-receipt (B2C settlement receipts) in parallel under the e-receipt mandate, and maintain real-time merchant-settlement reporting in the CBE-approved scheme.

ETA can reopen assessments within the five-year limitation (extended in fraud/evasion cases). Incorrect or fraudulent zero-rating gives rise to VAT clawback at 14% plus statutory delay interest, per-return fines (EGP 20,000-100,000) for false e-invoicing, and, under Law 91/2005 Art. 134 / Law 67/2016 penalties, fines of one to three times the tax evaded with imprisonment for wilful documentation falsification; customs authorities may concurrently claw back any export refunds and the CBE may blacklist repeat FX-repatriation violators.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and a flexible exchange-rate regime introduced in March 2024, with interbank rates determined by market forces and no parallel official rate. Foreign direct investment under Law 72/2017 is protected by statutory guarantees of profit and capital repatriation. In practice, outbound remittances of dividends and invested capital require presentation of audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, valid commercial registration, and underlying transaction documents to an authorized dealer bank. Capital-account transfers remain subject to documentary scrutiny and, occasionally, foreign-currency liquidity timetables, but are not subject to blanket blocking or prior CBE licensing for non-banking entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated through the Egyptian banking system. Under CBE rules, proceeds are normally required to be received within six months from the date of shipment or within thirty days of the stated collection date, depending on the underlying letter of credit or collection instrument. There is no mandatory conversion percentage under the current flexible regime; exporter may retain proceeds in foreign currency accounts or sell them on the interbank market, provided the receipt is traced through an authorized bank and the exporter can demonstrate settlement of underlying obligations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium for fully offshore digital EMI providers. Egypt's Payment Systems Law (Law 18/2019) and CBE licensing regulations require any payment service provider (PSP) operating in Egypt or processing EGP-denominated merchant payments to obtain CBE authorization or act strictly through a licensed local PSP. Offshore EMIs cannot directly hold EGP settlement accounts or perform local currency clearing; they may integrate as white-label technical infrastructure providers but must not undertake treasury conversion, settlement, or merchant acquiring without a CBE-approved arrangement with an onshore licensed bank or PSP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to a capital sub-account maintained at an authorized dealer bank in Egypt. The supporting wire instruction must include a clear purpose code equivalent to CBE/Egyptian economic classification for direct investment or equity subscription; the LLC must complete the statutory investment affidavit and submit the board resolution, updated commercial register, and passport copies of all UBOs to the bank. For USD-denominated capital, conversion to EGP is performed at the interbank market rate on value date; foreign capital is credited to a blocked or escrow sub-account pending delivery of the Egyptian General Authority for Investment (GAFI) certificate of data entry and completion of the local corporate registration formalities.Outbound treasury disbursements are settled via SWIFT MT103/PAYMENT orders for genuine trade or service obligations, documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 for goods transactions, and clean telegraphic transfers supported by tax registration and customs declarations. USD transactions ultimately clear through the correspondent network in New York via CHIPS/Fedwire; EUR transactions clear via TARGET2. EGP-denominated payments are cleared on the CBE Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system. Any outbound remittance exceeding CBE/regulatory thresholds must be backed by the underlying contract, an Egyptian tax card, a valid withholding tax receipt, and, for imports, the import registration and customs Bill of Lading or cargo release; settlement to non-related third parties is generally prohibited unless substantiated by a signed commercial agreement and beneficial-owner certification.Plain-vanilla FX forward contracts with CBE-licensed banks for bona fide import, export, dividend, and capital-repatriation programsFX swaps arranged with authorized banks to manage short-term liquidity mismatches between EGP collections and USD or EUR obligationsCross-currency swaps for long-term cross-border finance and direct investment exposuresOffshore Egyptian-pound non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) used by non-resident financial counterparts for synthetic EGP hedging without onshore settlement

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when related-party transactions exceed EGP 5 million per fiscal year; Master File mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated global revenue above EGP 500 million; related-party transaction disclosure form required even below the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not sustain a full standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE. General anti-abuse rules, tax recharacterisation powers, and investment licence requirements require genuine commercial activity, local decision-making, employees, and expenditure for an Egyptian payment gateway entity to preserve tax benefits and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for 3 to 5 years, fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000, payment of the avoided tax plus 2% monthly delayed penalties and 18% annual interest, prohibition on government contracts, and revocation of e-filing credentials

Director personal liability: Directors and statutory representatives who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt; the ETA can levy personal assets, impose travel bans, and refer the case to criminal prosecution
Shell Company / Artificial Treaty Shopping Entity

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of the entire fee flow as taxable income in Egypt, 22.5% corporate income tax, 10% dividend withholding tax on deemed distributions, penalties, and interest; cancellation of SEZ/licence incentives and disqualification from future tax benefits

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil, hold directors personally liable for the additional tax, freeze personal bank accounts, and impose custodial sentences for using sham legal structures to evade tax
AML / Payment Service Provider Licence Violation

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the payment institution licence, fine of EGP 1,000,000 to EGP 5,000,000 or more, criminal prosecution of responsible officers, seizure of processing funds, and blacklisting from the banking payment network

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners can be personally prosecuted, barred from financial services roles, subjected to asset freezes and travel bans, and imprisoned under the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law and Central Bank rules
