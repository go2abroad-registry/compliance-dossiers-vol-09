Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A serviced-office or business-centre address physically located in Egypt is generally accepted for a low-risk freight-forwarding LLC where the activity does not require a special licence, provided a valid lease or service agreement is filed with GAFI and the company maintains a dependable mail-receiving and legal-address infrastructure in Egypt. A purely virtual mailbox with no physical Egyptian presence is not acceptable for commercial registration, tax domicile, or bank account opening. For intermodal rail freight expediting, the registered office must also be able to receive notices from the Egyptian Tax Authority, the Commercial Registry, and any customs or transport counterparties; full virtual-only office models are therefore disallowed.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to LLCs under Law No. 4 of 2018; the members freely fix the capital in the Articles, but the entire cash capital component must be deposited in an Egyptian bank account, evidenced by a bank capital deposit certificate annexed to the notarized Articles before GAFI registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate issued by the Civil Defense Directorate; structural load and occupancy inspection under Building Law No. 119 of 2008; worker health and safety clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003, including fire extinguishers, exit signage, occupational health registration of employees, and coverage for occupational disease insurance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to LLCs under Law No. 4 of 2018; the members freely fix the capital in the Articles, but the entire cash capital component must be deposited in an Egyptian bank account, evidenced by a bank capital deposit certificate annexed to the notarized Articles before GAFI registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – e-Services and Public Portal: eta.gov.eg (official e-filing and e-invoicing access points).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must file a registration application with the competent ETA Tax Office (including the large-taxpayer office, depending on turnover). The applicant submits: certified copy of constitutional documents (e.g., LLC memorandum/statutes) translated into Arabic and legalized in the country of origin and by Egyptian consulate; board resolution appointing the legal representative/tax agent in Egypt; commercial registry extract (when hiring/having a branch or representative office); proof of premises address or electronic registration for non-residents; and power of attorney to the local tax facilitator. After verification, the ETA issues the Tax Registration Card ('Tazkira') bearing the TIN, and, if the entity is subject to Value Added Tax, a separate VAT Registration Number (VRN) under VAT Law No. 67/2016. Registration is mandatory within 30 days of the earlier of commencement of commercial activity, establishment of a branch, or exceeding the VAT threshold.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must file a registration application with the competent ETA Tax Office (including the large-taxpayer office, depending on turnover). The applicant submits: certified copy of constitutional documents (e.g., LLC memorandum/statutes) translated into Arabic and legalized in the country of origin and by Egyptian consulate; board resolution appointing the legal representative/tax agent in Egypt; commercial registry extract (when hiring/having a branch or representative office); proof of premises address or electronic registration for non-residents; and power of attorney to the local tax facilitator. After verification, the ETA issues the Tax Registration Card ('Tazkira') bearing the TIN, and, if the entity is subject to Value Added Tax, a separate VAT Registration Number (VRN) under VAT Law No. 67/2016. Registration is mandatory within 30 days of the earlier of commencement of commercial activity, establishment of a branch, or exceeding the VAT threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt's income tax system does not provide check-the-box pass-through elections; resident companies are taxed at entity level at the standard corporate rate (currently 22.5%). Available elections include applying for tax and customs incentives under Investment Law No. 72/2017 (e.g., Free Zone project status, Suez Canal Economic Zone/SCZone special provisions), pursuing voluntary registration under the ETA’s digital e-invoicing framework, and adopting specific transfer pricing documentation methods for related-party transactions in accordance with Egyptian TP regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services (mandatory registration); no threshold for importer registrations

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to export of goods where the exporter proves physical exportation and transfer of ownership to a non-resident, and to international transportation and ancillary services (including rail intermodal multimodal freight and expedition) supplied by a resident to a non-resident and for the transportation of goods in transit or outside Egypt. The supplier must obtain documentary evidence within the tax return period in which the supply is made.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / Electronic Export Certificate)
  • Bill of Lading (or international through-bill for rail/intermodal multimodal transport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of receipt of export proceeds through Egyptian banking channels)

Egypt implemented a mandatory e-invoicing system (E-Invoice) for B2B and B2C/receipt transactions. All taxable persons must interconnect to the ETA’s national electronic invoicing platform through certified application programming interfaces (APIs), transmit invoices in near real-time, obtain unique invoice IDs, and include QR codes on all fiscal documents. The system performs real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping/validation; invoices not cleared through ETA are treated as invalid and no input tax deduction is granted. Electronic invoices must be issued for all transactions between registered persons; exemptions and zero-rated supplies must also flow through the platform.

The ETA conducts rigorous post-shipment audits that cross-match customs export declarations, transport documents, foreign exchange receipts, and bank correspondence. Unsubstantiated zero-rated exports are recharacterized as domestic taxable supplies, attracting retroactive VAT at 14%, late payment penalties (5% per week of tax due, capped at 100%), monetary fines, and criminal liability for tax evasion (including imprisonment of responsible officers in aggravated cases).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-determined floating exchange rate for the Egyptian pound (EGP) since March 2024. Current-account transactions (cross-border freight forwarding, intermodal rail services, payment of subcontractors, and service fees) are fully convertible through CBE-licensed authorized banks without prior CBE approval. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity subscription, direct foreign investment financing and portfolio flows, remain subject to the usual bank-level due diligence and CBE reporting/disclosure codes. Profit remittance hurdles persist in practice: remittable dividends require audited Egyptian financial statements, an Egyptian Tax Authority No-Tax-Dues certificate, re-perpetrated source-of-funds documentation, and execution through an authorized bank with foreign currency availability; banks may apply internal liquidity buffers and transaction prioritization, causing episodic settlement delay. Freight forwarding and logistics service fees are current-account items and generally clear without the documentary LC burden applicable to merchandise imports.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign exchange and AML instructions, export proceeds from goods must be repatriated and credited within an Egyptian authorized bank in 120 days from the date of shipment, extendable up to 180 days for CBE-approved documentary delays; proceeds from services and freight-forwarding activities must be received within 90 days from the date of invoice/service completion. The mandatory surrender ratio is 100% repatriation: all export/freight proceeds must be brought back to Egypt via the authorized bank route and either credited to the exporter's foreign-currency account or sold to the bank at the prevailing market rate; no permission exists for exporters to retain proceeds fully abroad beyond the statutory window. The proceeds may, however, be retained in foreign currency (USD/EUR/GBP/CNY) at the exporter's discretion without forced conversion into EGP, provided an available intra-year CBE record reflects full receipt.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the sovereign monetary and foreign exchange authority established under Law No. 88 of 2003 and now governed by Law No. 194 of 2020 promulgating the Central Banking and Monetary System Regulation; the CBE also administers Egypt's consolidated foreign exchange position through its authorization of dealing banks.
