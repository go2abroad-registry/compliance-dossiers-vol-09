Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official e-services and e-invoicing portals: https://www.eta.gov.eg and https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; customs export administration is handled by the Egyptian Customs Authority.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity engaged in taxable Egyptian supplies must apply to the ETA for a Tax Card/TIN and VAT registration before commencing the taxable activity. The foreign juridical person must submit a certified and Arabic-translated certificate of incorporation, passport copies and powers of attorney for legal representatives, and, when no Egyptian fixed establishment is maintained, appoint an Egyptian tax representative. The application is lodged through the ETA electronic registration portal or the competent tax office; the ETA issues a TIN/VAT registration number within the statutory timeframe. Thereafter, the entity must become a participant in the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt system, obtain an ETA-approved digital certificate and API credentials, and certify any invoicing software or use the ETA-hosted portal. A foreign e-commerce seller without a physical presence in Egypt is still required to register where it makes taxable remote supplies to Egyptian consumers above the applicable threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and is taxed at the corporate income tax rate of 22.5% on its worldwide income; it is not eligible for pass-through or tax-transparent classification. Territorial scope: resident companies are taxed on worldwide income with a foreign tax credit; non-resident suppliers are taxed only on Egyptian-source income. Free-zone or special economic zone exemption status is not automatic and requires a separate license or qualifying election.

Available election forms

Egyptian tax law does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch classification election; an LLC is always treated as a separate taxable person. Available elections include the selection of a financial year with ETA approval; eligible micro and small enterprises may elect the presumptive tax regime under Law No. 152 of 2020 instead of the regular corporate tax computation; investment projects may elect available incentives under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; and a qualifying free-zone or special economic zone entity may elect export-oriented income tax exemptions. Such elections do not override or replace VAT registration, e-invoice, or customs export compliance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies in Egypt for mandatory VAT registration; EGP 250,000 for voluntary VAT registration. Non-resident suppliers of e-commerce and digital services are also required to register when their taxable remote supplies to Egyptian consumers exceed the Egyptian-pound annual threshold on a rolling 12-month basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent VAT applies to goods exported from Egypt where the VAT-registered supplier transfers ownership to a non-resident buyer, the goods are physically exported under a valid customs export declaration, and the export proceeds are received in convertible foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system. Services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated where the service is performed outside Egypt, is for direct use outside Egypt, or is a listed ancillary export service. The exporter must issue a 0% export VAT invoice and retain all shipment, customs, and foreign-currency receipt evidence to support the input VAT refund claim.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic customs entry) showing the Egyptian point of departure and the foreign destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note evidencing actual export
  • Commercial export invoice issued at 0% VAT and matching the customs declaration
  • Inward remittance certificate from a licensed Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Packing list and certificate of origin where required by the bank, customs, or the foreign buyer

All VAT-registered entities must issue invoices through the ETA centralized e-invoicing platform, either by direct API integration or through the ETA-hosted portal. The invoice must be presented in the ETA-prescribed JSON or XML format, digitally signed with an ETA-issued certificate, and transmitted before recognition for VAT deduction and audit purposes. The ETA validates the invoice and returns an acceptance with a unique invoice UUID; each invoice must display a verifiable QR code as the digital fiscal stamp. For B2C retail transactions, including TikTok Shop sales, ETA e-receipt rules require real-time issuance of validated receipts. Private offline invoices without ETA acceptance are not recognized as valid fiscal documents for input VAT credit, export zero-rating, or audit protection.

The ETA cross-matches zero-rated export invoices against customs exit records, e-invoice acceptance data, and bank statements showing inward foreign-currency remittance. If the export documentation is missing, inconsistent, or the foreign proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day window, the supply is recharacterized as a taxable domestic supply at 14%, the claimed input VAT credit is clawed back, and delay interest and statutory evasion penalties are imposed. Deliberate submission of forged customs declarations, bills of lading, or inward remittance certificates constitutes tax evasion and may lead to enhanced fines, criminal liability, disqualification from the e-invoice/e-receipt system, and cancellation of the exporter’s VAT registration privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) is managed under a floating exchange-rate regime in principle, but capital account transactions remain subject to material convertibility restrictions. Current-account payments for permitted trade in goods and services are generally admissible if supported by authentic commercial documentation and valid import/export registration. Capital account operations, including foreign-direct-equity capital injection, shareholder loans, and profit repatriation, require CBE compliance, declaration forms, and validation through licensed banks; dividend remittance is permitted on the basis of audited financials, tax clearance, and documented distributable reserves. Social-commerce entities relying on cross-border digital platforms must evidence the underlying transaction chain and source-of-funds documentation to avoid re-characterization of payments as untraceable portfolio flows. Practical hurdles include bank-level FX liquidity constraints, mandatory prioritization of essential imports, and time lags in obtaining purchase-order-related FX allocations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must receive export proceeds through an authorized bank in Egypt within 120 days from the date of export shipment; the entire proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt in foreign currency or EGP in accordance with CBE regulations. There is no generalized statutory percentage mandate requiring conversion of export receipts into EGP on the same day; however, if the exporter voluntarily sells the foreign currency to a licensed bank, the transaction is executed at the prevailing interbank market rate. The exporter must furnish shipping documentation, customs declarations, and bank transfer evidence to justify the receipt. Failure to repatriate or excessive delay without CBE approval can trigger penalties, suspension of the exporter's import/export card, and referral to the money-laundering reporting regime. For cross-border digital retail revenue, the exporter/seller is expected to route offshore payment sweepings directly into the Egyptian bank account and to preserve platform settlement statements to evidence export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — also operating as the foreign exchange regulator; market trading, licensing, and settlement are administered pursuant to the Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE circulars.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability for offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) as a standalone banking rail. Egypt does not recognize foreign EMI e-money licenses as a substitute for a licensed Egyptian bank account; inbound settlement from TikTok Shop and Wise B2B must ultimately land in an Egyptian bank account for customs, VAT, and CBE recordkeeping. Offshore EMIs may be used for pre-collection convenience, but they cannot perform Egyptian pound clearing, issue EGP-denominated accounts, or disburse to local suppliers without a local banking partner. CBE requires all payment-service providers handling Egyptian residents to be licensed or registered with the CBE; unlicensed cross-border sweepers present potential currency-control and AML risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The standard compliant rail is a SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent bank to the Egyptian LLC's capital-escrow/operational account at a licensed Egyptian bank. Wire transfer must reference the CBE foreign-investment number, the GAFI registration certificate, and a corporate board resolution. For the first injection, the sender must also issue a bank-to-bank proof-of-title and a copy of the audited annual financial statements of the parent entity, if available. Commercial invoices or share subscription agreements are attached to the receiving bank for CBE recording. For recurring working capital top-ups, each transfer must be correspondently recorded in the CBE's external asset declaration platform as a loan or equity contribution; otherwise the bank may treat it as a deemed dividend or suppressed export receipt. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividend distributions made by the Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders under Article 57 of Law No. 91 of 2005. The 10% tax is applied at the date of distribution on the gross cash dividend; in-kind distributions are valued at fair market value. Egypt-source profits distributed to foreign entities are not subject to any additional branch tax for the LLC itself.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax year is the fiscal year approved by the ETA, normally the calendar year. The annual Form No. 9 return and final settlement are due within 4 months after fiscal year-end (by 30 April for calendar-year taxpayers). Quarterly advance payments approximating the current-year CIT liability are payable by the end of April, July, and October; the residual balance is paid with the annual return. For fiscal years other than the calendar year, the deadlines shift to the last day of the fourth month following the selected year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Ministerial Decree No. 686 of 2019 (as amended by Decree No. 230 of 2020): Local File is mandatory when the annual value of related-party transactions in any single category (goods, services, intangibles, financial transactions) exceeds EGP 15 million. Master File is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EGP 400 million. Country-by-Country Report is mandatory when the group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 20 billion. The annual related-party disclosure annex to Form No. 9 is required regardless of the threshold; ETA may request a Local File on a risk basis below the threshold, particularly for e-commerce companies relying on foreign platform agreements.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the ETA enforces substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance provisions under Law No. 91 of 2005. An Egyptian LLC claiming SCZone or free-zone CIT incentives must demonstrate physical business premises, actual local payroll, real management and control in Egypt, and substantive procurement/logistics functions. A remote TikTok Shop operation routed through a nominee-structured LLC with no Egyptian substance will be disregarded for tax purposes, and profits will be re-attributed to the actual economic operator. No separate ESR notification filing is required for an Egyptian LLC; substance is examined through CIT audits, transfer pricing files, e-invoicing data, and bank account analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 133-140 of Law No. 91 of 2005: imprisonment of 3-8 years, criminal fines of 2-5 times the evaded tax, additional assessment at 22.5% plus the 2.5% State Development Tax, a 2% monthly late-payment surcharge, and asset seizure orders. E-invoice fraud adds fines of up to EGP 50,000 per invoice and mandatory tax-card suspension.

Director personal liability: The general manager, financial director, and beneficial owner who signed or directed the fraudulent return are personally and jointly liable for the unpaid CIT and surcharges; the ETA may impose preventive travel bans, freeze personal and corporate bank accounts, seize movable and immovable property, and, upon conviction, the court may impose imprisonment for the controlling manager and shareholders who participated in the evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length result at the 22.5% rate plus 2.5% State Development Tax where applicable; recharacterization of the excessive payment as a constructive dividend subject to 10% WHT; 2% monthly surcharge on the adjusted tax; documentation penalties of up to EGP 200,000 and denial of the deduction for the entire related-party payment when the file is not submitted.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the non-arm's-length payments are jointly liable for the disallowed deduction, the additional WHT, and surcharges; the ETA may assert civil restitution against personal assets where funds were transferred outside Egypt; travel bans and bankruptcy proceedings may follow if the adjustment renders the LLC insolvent.
Shell Company and Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The ETA will disregard the shell entity, re-attribute its profits to the true Egyptian economic operator or, in cross-border cases, to a taxable permanent establishment of the foreign parent; denial of all DTA benefits and incentive exemptions; fines under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 and Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 of EGP 100,000-500,000; de-registration of the commercial license; publication of the administrative decision.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal criminal liability for false statutory declarations, including imprisonment of up to 5 years; the corporate veil is pierced for the sham, making nominees and undisclosed beneficial owners jointly liable for all CIT, WHT, and AML penalties; the ETA will issue travel bans and freezing orders against personal accounts, and courts may order personal asset forfeiture.
Money Laundering and AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended): imprisonment of 5-15 years and a fine equal to 1-3 times the laundered amount; freezing and comprehensive forfeiture of all TikTok Shop receivables, bank balances, inventory, and vehicles acquired with the proceeds; publication of the conviction sentence; the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit issues blocking orders to all banks and payment institutions.

Director personal liability: Directors, shareholder-managers, and beneficial owners who order, participate in, or silently benefit from the cash funneling are criminally liable as co-perpetrators, including imprisonment; personal travel bans and passport surrender are mandatory during investigation; personal assets are subject to civil restitution and asset forfeiture; the corporate veil is entirely pierced for any sham transaction, and the LLC's bank mandate may be transferred to a court-appointed liquidator.
