Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Egyptian LLC; the founding quota-holders may determine initial capital, which must be subscribed in full and paid in cash or in-kind. If the partners elect to use a notarial escrow arrangement, funds must be deposited into a blocked bank account until GAFI registration approval is obtained.

Funding & unlock path: Not mandatory for pure online retail with no physical premises; once any office or warehouse is procured, the Egyptian Civil Defense Authority must inspect and certify fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and electrical-safety plans, and the local labor authority must issue an occupational fitness and workplace safety clearance before staff are deployed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Egyptian LLC; the founding quota-holders may determine initial capital, which must be subscribed in full and paid in cash or in-kind. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official e-services portal https://www.eta.gov.eg; dedicated e-invoicing clearance platform https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing the Tax Card application (with the Unified Public Registration form) with the ETA within 30 days of commencing activity, either electronically through the ETA e-services portal or at the competent tax registration office. The statutory dossier comprises certified constitutional documents (Articles of Association), Commercial Registry extract, proof of legal address in Egypt, board resolution appointing a fiscal representative resident in Egypt, and KYC/AML identification of all non-resident beneficial owners. For VAT purposes the LLC files the VAT registration declaration under Article 15 of Law No. 67 of 2016 in parallel, after which the ETA issues a single Unified Tax Registration Certificate covering income tax, VAT, and stamp duty. Non-resident e-commerce operators without a local establishment must appoint an accredited Egyptian tax representative and obtain an ETA e-portal credential before making any taxable supply; however, this composite key assumes an Egyptian-incorporated LLC as the registered taxpayer.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The LLC is a resident juridical person under Law No. 91 of 2005, taxable at the standard 22.5% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income (foreign tax credit available), with no pass-through eligibility. The LLC is a separate taxable person for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016. All Amazon marketplace revenue (ISIC 4791 cross-border retail) is booked as corporate income in Egypt. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The LLC is a resident juridical person under Law No. 91 of 2005, taxable at the standard 22.5% corporate income tax rate on worldwide income (foreign tax credit available), with no pass-through eligibility. The LLC is a separate taxable person for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016. All Amazon marketplace revenue (ISIC 4791 cross-border retail) is booked as corporate income in Egypt. Territorial scope applies only to non-resident principals; no special economic zone or tax-holiday status is assumed for this general-case composite key.

Available election forms

Egypt has no US-style 'check-the-box' election. Available mechanisms: (i) fiscal unity consolidated filing election under Article 48 of Law No. 91 of 2005 for resident companies with at least 95% common beneficial ownership, subject to ETA approval; (ii) VAT grouping is not permitted; each legal entity files independently; (iii) Double Tax Treaty relief election under Article 56 of the Income Tax Law for treaty-eligible foreign counterparties, subject to Egyptian substance and beneficial-ownership conditions; (iv) hybrid mismatch arrangements are re-characterized under Egyptian general anti-avoidance rules (Article 30(h) of Law No. 91 of 2005); (v) quarterly CIT instalment elections and an annual VAT refund settlement election are available for cash-flow management.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods and services for mandatory VAT registration (Article 15, Law 67/2016); registration due within 30 days of crossing the threshold; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under Article 22 of Law No. 67 of 2016 for (i) tangible goods that physically leave Egyptian customs territory within 90 days of delivery/invoice date, and (ii) services performed within Egypt for a non-resident recipient whose benefit is not enjoyed in Egypt. For the Amazon FBA cross-border model, goods consigned by the Egyptian LLC to foreign fulfillment centers or directly to foreign consumers qualify provided the export contract, foreign-currency commercial invoice, and customs export declaration are all in the LLC's name and the LLC retains title and risk documentation. Recovered input VAT is first offset against output VAT; any excess refund is settled by the ETA within 45 days of documentary inspection per the VAT Executive Regulations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Egypt Customs Authority) with ACI-compatible manifest link
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill / e-commerce logistics manifest evidencing physical removal from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Egyptian bank foreign-currency purchase voucher documenting repatriation of export proceeds

Egypt mandates real-time fiscal clearance of all B2B tax invoices through the ETA e-invoicing gateway, and e-receipts for B2C marketplace sales. The LLC must integrate its ERP with the ETA API (XML/JSON schemas), transmit every invoice/receipt for validation, obtain a unique ETA UUID and digital stamp, and affix the ETA-generated machine-readable QR code to paper copies. Invoices failing validation (invalid TIN, misclassified merchandise, missing nexus data) are automatically rejected and deemed non-issued, attracting statutory fines and audit penalties under Law No. 67 of 2016 as amended by Law No. 20 of 2023. The regime is fully digital; private invoice registers are not recognized for tax deduction purposes.

The ETA operates a data-matching audit desk that reconciles customs manifests, e-invoicing clearance data, bank remittance logs, and VAT returns. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day statutory window automatically reverses the 0% rate, triggering assessment of 14% VAT plus late-payment interest and a 100% additional-tax clawback (double VAT) for fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims under Article 76 of Law No. 67 of 2016, in addition to criminal fines of EGP 20,000–100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for willful evasion. The retrospective assessment window is five years from the end of the relevant tax year; incomplete export documentation, absence of FX remittance certificates, or diversion of goods to local market consumption is treated as presumptive evidence of unlawful zero-rating, exposing the LLC to summary assessment, suspension of its e-invoicing license, and loss of VAT refund eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP operates as a market-determined managed float with onshore interbank convertibility; after the March 2024 CBE decision to liberalise the exchange-rate regime, the US dollar is freely traded against EGP inside the licensed banking system at the interbank price. Current-account flows — trade proceeds, e-commerce export receipts, commissions, and service fees — are executable at licensed banks with correct purpose-code documentation. Capital-account items — equity injections, external borrowings, dividend and interest remittances — are permitted but require documentary compliance: GAFI investment registration, bank FX files, ETA tax-clearance certificate for profit repatriation, and CBE inward/outward reporting. Residual hurdles: temporary bank-level USD cover constraints for unusually large outflows, mandatory execution through authorised banks, and absence of an offshore deliverable EGP market; genuine Amazon FBA export earnings, once collected through the authorised banking channel, are remittance-eligible without balance-of-payments restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation: 100% of Amazon and e-commerce export proceeds must be credited to an Egyptian licensed bank within 6 months from the date of shipment, extendable by up to an additional 6 months upon documented request to, or acceptance by, the CBE; the 6-month clock runs from the bill-of-lading date for goods and from the payment date for digital marketplace services. Conversion ratio: no fixed mandatory CBE surrender ratio remains after the 2016 float and the March 2024 liberalisation — exporters may lawfully hold receipts in foreign currency in a USD operational account or convert to EGP at the interbank rate. In practice, the receiving bank will match the export proceeds against the customer's FX demand file as a liquidity-discipline measure, not as a statutory conversion quota. Every Amazon payout must be reported by the bank under the CBE Balance-of-Payments export purpose code for merchant retail trade over the internet, and the bank maintains an export-proceeds file containing the e-invoices, Amazon settlement statements, and the credited dollar amount.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary authority and bank prudential supervisor in Egypt; foreign-exchange activity is regulated under Law No. 194 of 2020 (the Foreign Exchange and Banking Law), CBE Board of Directors Decree No. 232 of 2021 (AML/CFT), and the CBE consolidated prudential rulebooks and periodic FX circulars. The CBE licenses all banks, approves correspondent relationships, operates the foreign-exchange interbank market, and receives all balance-of-payments reporting for export proceeds and capital flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM as a settlement rail, LOW as a treasury depository for EG_LLC. Foreign fintech EMIs — Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Connect, GKash — may collect Amazon marketplace payouts offshore in USD or EUR, but they are not licensed under Egyptian banking law (Law No. 194 of 2020 and Law No. 18 of 2019 on digital payment regulation) and cannot hold EGP domestic balances, open nostro accounts with Egyptian banks, or issue local Egyptian IBANs. Legally feasible architecture: offshore EMI commercial account for marketplace payouts, periodic sweeps via SWIFT MT103 to the Egyptian bank USD Non-Resident Foreign-Currency Account under purpose code 'e-commerce export proceeds', then conversion inside the interbank band. The 6-month CBE repatriation clock runs from each Amazon payment date, so the EMI balance must be swept at least monthly. Not feasible: using the EMI balance as the only treasury without local bank settlement, or paying Egyptian suppliers directly from an unlicensed EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for EG_LLC equity injection: (1) the non-resident shareholder instructs an SWIFT MT103 from its home bank to the Egyptian bank's New York or London correspondent, naming EG_LLC as beneficiary and placing 'Equity contribution — incorporation capital' in the purpose field, together with the CBE Balance-of-Payments direct-investment equity code; (2) the Egyptian bank credits a temporary USD Capital Blockage account and issues a capital-deposit certificate enabling GAFI to issue the commercial register; (3) after GAFI registration, the USD is either converted to EGP at the bank's daily interbank rate for operating needs or retained in the USD operational account; (4) the bank files the inward foreign-investment report with CBE and opens the treasury relationship for future guarantee and repatriation procedures. Compliance cautions: the wire amount must match the capital pledged to GAFI; any shareholder loan apart from equity requires CBE registration and debt-cover declaration forms; cash deposits above regulatory thresholds are unacceptable; all transfers require the full KYC dossiers described above.For the FBA general model, the operative clearing-rail hierarchy is: (A) Cross-border supplier payments — SWIFT MT103 via the Egyptian bank's correspondent, debiting either the USD Foreign-Currency Account or EGP converted at interbank, recorded under CBE BoP purpose code for goods imports; each USD payment must be matched against documented FX source (Amazon export proceeds) in the bank's FX files. (B) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP-600 — issued by an Egyptian Tier-1 bank for inventory purchase orders above USD 50,000, giving documentary risk mitigation and predictable FX planning; recurring small FBA lots are better served by direct wire because LC issuance cost outweighs benefit. (C) Domestic EGP payments — warehouse, freight-forwarding, local suppliers, and Egyptian tax payments — executed exclusively through EBS-RTGS (Egyptian Banks Society Real-Time Gross Settlement) and CBE-interfaced current accounts; domestic transactions must not be settled in USD. (D) Amazon platform fees, FBA fulfilment charges, and ocean/airfreight deductions — settled directly by Amazon from marketplace proceeds outside the Egyptian banking system and therefore not CBE-reportable outflows. All outbound FX disbursements are subject to the bank's FX-liquidity cover and the export-proceeds matching file.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP outright forward — maturity up to 12 months, executed with an Egyptian licensed bank against documented trade exposure, physically delivered in the Egyptian interbank market.USD/EGP FX swap — spot versus forward legs with a licensed local treasury counterparty, permitted for cash-flow hedging of expected e-commerce export proceeds and import payables.Cross-currency interest-rate swap — available in combination with USD or EUR bank credit facilities to convert floating-rate external debt exposure into a covered EGP-equivalent hedging structure.Exchange-listed USD/EGP futures — cash-settled contracts on the Egyptian Exchange (EGX, launched 2021), providing an exchange-cleared hedge instrument for resident corporates.Offshore EGP NDF (negative boundary) — unavailable to Egyptian resident treasuries; Egypt maintains no onshore NDF market under CBE law, so EG_LLC must confine hedging to the deliverable instruments above, while non-resident counterparties may transact EGP NDFs only offshore.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: MEDIUM as a settlement rail, LOW as a treasury depository for EG_LLC. Foreign fintech EMIs — Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Connect, GKash — may collect Amazon marketplace payouts offshore in USD or EUR, but they are not licensed under Egyptian banking law (Law No. 194 of 2020 and Law No. 18 of 2019 on digital payment regulation) and cannot hold EGP domestic balances, open nostro accounts with Egyptian banks, or issue local Egyptian IBANs. Legally feasible architecture: offshore EMI commercial account for marketplace payouts, periodic sweeps via SWIFT MT103 to the Egyptian bank USD Non-Resident Foreign-Currency Account under purpose code 'e-commerce export proceeds', then conversion inside the interbank band. The 6-month CBE repatriation clock runs from each Amazon payment date, so the EMI balance must be swept at least monthly. Not feasible: using the EMI balance as the only treasury without local bank settlement, or paying Egyptian suppliers directly from an unlicensed EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; a Transfer Pricing Information Schedule must accompany the annual CIT return whenever related-party transactions exceed EGP 1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no separate Economic Substance Regulations like Gulf jurisdictions. However, Article 77 of Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 empowers the ETA to disregard transactions or entities that lack commercial substance and are designed purely for tax avoidance. An e-commerce LLC must maintain a physical office, operational staff, genuine stock and logistics monitoring, dated board minutes, a dedicated bank account, and complete accounting ledgers; otherwise it can be recharacterized as a conduit or sham and its profits re-attributed to the foreign parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting FBA Revenue

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 2-5 years and/or a fine of not less than EGP 10,000 and not more than twice the tax evaded; additional 2% monthly delay tax up to 40%; automatic turnover-based tax assessment; seizure of sale proceeds and commercial registration suspension.

Director personal liability: Directors, CFO and tax preparer are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; personal assets are attachable; ETA may issue travel bans; a criminal conviction may preclude holding a director position in Egypt.
Shell Company and Artificial Conduit

Statutory sanction: ETA invokes Article 77 of Law 91/2005 to disregard the entity; re-attributes 100% of the B2C e-commerce profit to the foreign shareholder; assesses 22.5% CIT plus 20% deemed dividend withholding tax and monthly delay tax.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution for the full avoided CIT and WHT; corporate veil piercing makes directors personally liable; assets are frozen, travel restrictions are imposed and active participants face up to two years' imprisonment for tax evasion.
Undocumented Cross-Border Royalty and Interest Payments

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the deduction; mandatory assessment of 20% withholding tax on the gross payment; up to 100% penalty on the unpaid WHT; transfer pricing adjustment can trigger an additional 30% penalty under TP regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors become co-obligors under Articles 58 and 60 of Law 91; personal assets can be attached if the LLC is insolvent; travel bans are issued; deliberate under-withholding results in criminal referral and possible imprisonment.
AML / Informal Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80/2002, fines from EGP 100,000 to 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for laundering commercial proceeds; court-ordered freezing and confiscation of all linked bank balances; permanent revocation of commercial registration.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecuted even after corporate dissolution; passports are surrendered; civil disgorgement of funds routed through informal systems is ordered; travel declarations are restricted and imprisonment is possible upon conviction.
