Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official electronic portal: www.eta.gov.eg. Investor-facing incorporation and registration coordination is also provided by the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC first obtains its statutory incorporation certificate from GAFI, registers in the Commercial Registry, and obtains any sectoral license (for marine surveying, the approval of the Financial Regulatory Authority / Insurance Oversight authorities). The LLC then files ETA Taxpayer Registration Form requesting the Tax Identification Number (TIN), supported by the incorporation deed, commercial registration extract, management passports, board resolutions, head-office address proof, and the maritime insurance surveying license. The ETA issues a single TIN; VAT registration is effected through the same ETA registration process if the annual taxable turnover equals or exceeds the statutory threshold or the LLC wishes to register voluntarily.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian LLC is a juridical person and is treated as a separate taxable entity under Egypt's Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and the unified tax procedure framework. Resident LLCs are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident taxpayers are taxed on Egypt-source income. Egypt does not recognise pass-through or tax-transparent treatment for LLCs.

Available election forms

Egyptian tax law has no US-style check-the-box or pass-through election. An LLC is compulsorily taxed as an entity-level corporation. Limited statutory elections exist only in specific circumstances, such as applying for tax group treatment among qualifying resident corporations, or for non-resident branches to be taxed on a deemed-profit basis. No hybrid mismatch election is available under Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable turnover from supplies of goods and services. Importers and providers of certain taxable services may have separate registration requirements; foreign persons making taxable supplies through Egyptian nexus must register with the ETA even if the turnover threshold is not met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from Egypt under a customs export declaration; to international transport services; and to services directly connected with exported goods, or services rendered and wholly consumed outside Egypt for non-resident recipients. For a marine insurance surveyor, zero-rating may be claimed when the inspection/survey service is supplied to a non-resident underwriter, insurer or cargo owner and the service is either performed wholly outside Egypt or is directly connected with cargo being exported from Egypt. The taxpayer must maintain documentary proof of the foreign recipient, foreign-currency payment, and the actual export/survey transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Manifest bearing Egyptian customs export clearance stamps
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill where applicable) evidencing departure of goods from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a bank in Egypt showing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial export invoice issued in foreign currency
  • Marine survey report and service contract identifying the non-resident underwriter or principal

The ETA mandates a centralised electronic invoicing and electronic receipts clearance system. Registered taxpayers subject to implementation phases must integrate their accounting/ERP systems with ETA-approved APIs or certified fiscal devices, submit invoice and receipt data in structured JSON/XML in near-real time, receive a unique electronic validation code and QR stamp, and issue no valid invoice before fiscal clearance. Non-compliance results in rejection of invoices for input VAT recovery and administrative penalties.

The ETA audits export zero-rating claims by matching e-invoice data with customs export records, bill-of-lading details, and inward remittance certificates. If the export documents are incomplete, false, or the foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated within the applicable deadline, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 14% VAT. Retrospective assessments may be raised for up to five years, accompanied by late-payment delay tax, monetary fines, and criminal penalties for tax evasion under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016. Fraudulent zero-rating claims or forged export documents may result in imprisonment, fines equal to the tax evaded, and personal liability of company directors and signatories.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt adheres to IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility; since the CBE's unified floating exchange rate regime (March 6, 2024), trade in goods, services, insurance premiums, and claim settlements are freely transferable through licensed banks at the interbank market rate. Capital-account transactions (equity injection, shareholder loans, capital repatriation) are permitted under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 but require GAFI registration, CBE-form declaration, and source-of-funds documentation. Profit and remittance flows are not subject to prior CBE approval, yet operational delays may arise during episodic USD liquidity tightness. Cross-border marine survey fees are current-account invisibles and are remittable without pre-authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign-exchange regulations, marine surveyors (services exporters) must repatriate 100% of foreign-currency receivables to a licensed Egyptian bank within 180 days of invoice maturity; no mandatory conversion into EGP exists after the 2024 float, but repatriated funds must be credited to a domestic FX account and can be freely sold at the interbank (market) rate. Non-compliance triggers CBE penalty circulars and bank review of the client's credit facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Banking and Central Bank Law No. 194 of 2020; competent AML/CFT authority is the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU). Insurance-sector licensing is separately administered by the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA), not the CBE.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% on taxable profits of juridical persons for tax periods commencing on or after 1 January 2020 (Article 45, Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax (WHT) on cash or in-kind profit distributions made by an Egyptian company, including distributions to non-resident shareholders, under Article 56(1) of Law No. 91/2005.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed on or before 30 April for a company with a 31 December year-end; generally within 4 months of the fiscal year-end date used in the commercial register. Any taxes shown payable are due on the filing date; the return must be filed even if no tax is payable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and TP Disclosure Form required when aggregate related-party transactions in the Egyptian tax year exceed EGP 8,000,000; Master File and CbCR threshold: multinational group consolidated revenue of approximately EGP 4,000,000,000 (Egyptian documentation thresholds under ETA TP Regulations and Decree No. 415 of 2021).

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no separate Caribbean-type ESR filing for standard marine surveyors; however, substance is enforced through: (a) the general anti-avoidance rule in Law 91/2005 allowing the ETA to disregard or recharacterize arrangements lacking genuine commercial purpose; (b) CFC rules applicable to Egyptian residents controlling low-tax offshore insurers or shell survey firms; (c) Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) licensing and fit-and-proper requirements, which require marine insurance surveyors to maintain a real office, maritime professional staff and actual forensic and damage inspection capacity; and (d) treaty benefits requiring the recipient to demonstrate substantive activity in the residence state. A desk-only shell with no real survey marine capability is prima facie challengeable.
