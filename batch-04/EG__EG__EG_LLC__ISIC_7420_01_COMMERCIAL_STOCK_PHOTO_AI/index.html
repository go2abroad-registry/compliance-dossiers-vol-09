Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA / Maslaha al-Dara'ib al-Misriyya), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; principal portals: https://www.eta.gov.eg (tax registration, Tax Card, returns) and https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg (e-invoice/e-receipt real-time clearance).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC via GAFI or the Commercial Registry and obtain the Commercial Registration certificate. Step 2: Within 30 days of incorporation, file with the ETA the Tax Card application (articles of association, CR, authenticated shareholder passports, registered office lease) to obtain the permanent Tax Card number (the primary TIN). Step 3: File VAT registration concurrently if projected annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; a non-resident parent or digital platform shareholder must separately register under the ETA simplified non-resident digital-services VAT module (no Egyptian notarization; filing by home-country TIN, passport copy, and an Egyptian tax representative or designated online channel). Step 4: Complete ETA e-invoicing onboarding and obtain the two digital signature certificates (signature key and encryption key) for fiscal clearance. Step 5: Open a CBE-licensed bank account and establish the exporter's foreign-currency proceeds register for FX receipt tracking.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income at the standard CIT rate of 22.5% (Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005); non-resident shareholders are subject to Egyptian-source taxation only (dividend WHT 10%, royalty WHT 20% as applicable). No pass-through or transparent treatment exists for LLCs. Qualifying exporters of digital services may additionally apply the partial CIT reduction on export profits under Article 29(1) of Law 91/2005, subject to ministerial conditions and the Export Development Fund rules. — Step 1: Incorporate the LLC via GAFI or the Commercial Registry and obtain the Commercial Registration certificate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income at the standard CIT rate of 22.5% (Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005); non-resident shareholders are subject to Egyptian-source taxation only (dividend WHT 10%, royalty WHT 20% as applicable). No pass-through or transparent treatment exists for LLCs. Qualifying exporters of digital services may additionally apply the partial CIT reduction on export profits under Article 29(1) of Law 91/2005, subject to ministerial conditions and the Export Development Fund rules.

Available election forms

Not applicable to LLC classification: Egypt does not operate check-the-box or comparable flow-through elections; LLCs are mandatorily taxed at the entity level. Available statutory elections are limited to: (i) selection of fiscal year (calendar year or other ETA-approved 12-month period); (ii) VAT grouping of related resident companies with ETA prior approval; (iii) simplified non-resident digital VAT registration; and (iv) sector-specific export procedure reconfirmation. There is no domestic hybrid mismatch election; treaty benefit and LOB analysis is applied contractually rather than by elective filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies for resident taxable persons (Article 22, VAT Law 67/2016); there is NO de minimis threshold for non-resident suppliers of electronically supplied services (digital content, stock media, SaaS, AI-generated visual assets) — registration is triggered upon the first supply to an Egyptian consumer under the ETA simplified non-resident digital VAT module.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 16 of VAT Law 67/2016: zero-rating applies to services genuinely exported from Egypt — supplied to a non-resident customer, consumed outside Egypt, consideration received in freely convertible foreign currency, and statutory export evidence retained. For the stock-photography/generative visual assets LLC: sales of licenses, subscriptions, API deliveries, and AI-generated visual content to non-resident end-users qualify for 0% VAT where the contract is with a non-resident, payment is received in FX into an Egyptian bank account, and download/use occurs outside Egypt. Supplies to Egyptian residents or where the digital asset is enjoyed in Egypt are standard-rated at 14%; intra-Egypt distribution is not zero-rate eligible.

  • ETA-cleared zero-rated export e-invoice (valid QR code, export/digital services code)
  • For physical media only: Customs Export Declaration plus Bill of Lading or Airway Bill; for pure digital export: non-customs digital-supply evidence (server logs, licensee geolocation, download attestation, AI-platform usage record)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) / CBE bank foreign-exchange receipt evidencing receipt of the export consideration in convertible currency
  • Executed license agreement, purchase order, or API subscription record with the non-resident customer and IP address/geolocation data of the delivery endpoint
  • CBE export proceeds register entry confirming FX repatriation within the statutory 180-day window

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance: B2B invoices must be issued in valid JSON/XML and transmitted to the ETA e-invoicing platform (https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg) for validation; the ETA returns a Unique Identifier and a cryptographic QR stamp before the invoice is legally effective. B2C transactions are covered by the parallel e-receipt system requiring fiscal integration of ERPs/cash registers with the ETA API. Enrollment is phased by cumulative turnover brackets; connections use OAuth2-secured APIs with digital certificates and the ETA e-invoicing JSON schema. Invoices failing clearance are treated as non-issued and input VAT deduction is denied; penalties up to EGP 50,000 per invoice may apply.

ETA may re-audit zero-rated exports for 5 years (10 years for suspected fraud). Failure to produce the full documentation bundle within 90 days of an ETA request retroactively revokes zero-rating, triggering assessment at 14% VAT plus monthly late-payment interest of 2% (or the CBE discount rate, if higher) and forfeiture of the refund claim. Proven evasion attracts an additional penalty of 100%–200% of the tax due (Articles 42–43, VAT Law) and imprisonment under Articles 72–75. Non-repatriation of export FX within the CBE 180-day deadline denies the export proof, imposes a 10% administrative penalty on the un-repatriated amount under CBE Foreign-Exchange Regulations, and disqualifies the exporter from the refund fast-track and Export Development Fund support.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by the Central Bank of Egypt. Current account transactions are convertible with standard documentation requirements. Capital account transactions, including equity contributions and profit remittances, require prior Central Bank of Egypt approval and are subject to repatriation ceilings, tax withholding, and documentation verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be surrendered to an authorized Egyptian bank within six months from the date of shipment. The surrendering bank must receive full commercial documentation including invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin. Non-surrender constitutes a regulatory violation subject to penalties and account restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for offshore electronic money institutions. CBE regulations prohibit unlicensed cross-border digital payment services and impose strict AML/KYC obligations on any foreign EMI operating within Egypt.
