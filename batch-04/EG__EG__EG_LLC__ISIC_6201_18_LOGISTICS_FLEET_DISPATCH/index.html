Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA — El-Maslahah El-Daribeyah); official e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipts portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the LLC via the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) one-stop shop and obtain the Egyptian Commercial Registry extract, with a notarized Arabic-language power of attorney issued to the local manager. Step 2 — Apply to the competent ETA tax district for the tax card (Bitaqa Daribeyya), i.e., the 10-digit unified Tax Identification Number (TIN); this TIN is a precondition for bank accounts, customs, VAT, and payroll filings. Step 3 — Register for VAT under Law 67/2016 via the ETA e-services portal within 30 days of exceeding EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover, submitting the commercial registry extract, tax card, constitutive documents, board resolution, and head-office/service-agreement evidence. Step 4 — Register as a withholding agent for salary tax and social insurance when Egyptian employees are engaged. Step 5 — For physical fleet-hardware or telematics-device exports, register with the General Organization for Export and Import Control (GOEIC) and obtain the CBE importer/exporter registry code used for ACID customs declarations. ETA processing generally completes within 10–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch look-through election exists under Egyptian law; a domestic LLC is always a separate corporate taxpayer subject to CIT at 22.5% on worldwide income (with foreign tax-credit relief) and is a distinct VAT person. Available elections: (i) choose the fiscal year end subject to ETA approval; (ii) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold to obtain input-VAT recovery; (iii) elect Investment Law 72/2017 incentives (deduction of up to 30%–50% of investment cost) or the free-zone regime inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone (0% income tax and VAT-neutral treatment for zone-to-zone supplies), such election being made in the GAFI/SCZone setup agreements; (iv) quarterly VAT export-refund cycle election; and (v) annual re-submission of an Egyptian tax-residence certificate issued by the ETA to claim reduced treaty withholding rates on cross-border payments. Hybrid-mismatch rules are not codified and no elective transparency is available for LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services (mandatory threshold per VAT Law Art. 15); voluntary registration permitted below the threshold, and exporters of services are encouraged to register irrespective of turnover to secure zero-rating and input-VAT refunds

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods and services is zero-rated at 0% VAT under Law 67/2016 and its Executive Regulations. For the logistics-fleet SaaS/telematics scope, the ETA requires: (i) the supplier is a VAT-registered Egyptian entity; (ii) the services are contractually supplied to a non-resident beneficiary located outside Egypt; (iii) the services are effectively consumed offshore, e.g., fleet data processed or utilized abroad — domestic usage by an Egyptian freight operator would disqualify zero-rating; and (iv) the export consideration is received in foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is creditable, and refund claims follow the quarterly ETA export-refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD Entry / ACID-linked declaration) — required for physical fleet-hardware and telematics-device exports; for pure SaaS there is no customs entry and the ETA accepts the service-export evidence set instead
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (or equivalent electronic transport document) evidencing physical export movement where freight equipment is shipped
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC / bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice) evidencing receipt of export consideration in Egypt
  • Signed international software/telematics service agreement and commercial invoice naming the non-resident customer and stating that the service is performed and consumed outside Egypt
  • ETA zero-rating declaration / export-refund statement as filed in the ETA e-services module

Mandatory near real-time e-invoicing (B2B) and e-receipts (B2C) under ETA Decision No. 369/2020 and the successive phase-in schedules, currently covering essentially all VAT-registered taxpayers including logistics-technology SaaS providers. Invoices are transmitted in JSON/XML to the ETA Electronic Invoicing System (EIS) through ETA-certified middleware or direct REST/SOAP APIs, signed with the taxpayer's ETA digital certificate; the ETA validates each document in near real time and returns an acceptance code plus a unique digital-stamp hash and QR code that must appear on the invoice. Non-compliant issuance invalidates the document for input-VAT deduction and attracts fines of up to EGP 50,000 per invoice, with business-interruption risk for rejected transmissions.

The ETA may reassess export zero-rating claims within the general 5-year limitation period (6 years where fraud or evasion is alleged). Upon disallowance — e.g., evidence that the service was consumed by an Egyptian resident, that the invoice was fictitious, or that FX proceeds were not repatriated through the banking system — the ETA will claw back the 14% VAT that would have been due, plus a delay penalty of 2% per month (capped at 100% of the principal tax), and statutory evasion penalties commonly ranging up to 100% and escalating to 300% of the evaded tax for deliberate fraud, with criminal referral of responsible officers. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the Central Bank of Egypt deadline (5 months for goods; the 180-day administrative benchmark for services) also exposes the exporter to customs and foreign-exchange sanctions and loss of input-VAT refund carry-forwards. The ETA maintains risk-scoring analytics on digital exporters; missing or irreconcilable IRC/SWIFT documentation is the most common trigger for clawback and frozen refund balances, and the general manager and board members may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-floating exchange-rate regime and has subscribed to IMF Article VIII, making the Egyptian pound convertible for current international transactions, including export service receipts, dividends, interest, and profit remittance, provided the underlying documents (contract, audited statements, tax clearance, board resolution) are verified by the administering bank. The capital account is open but documented: foreign-direct-equity injection and repatriation must be reported to the CBE through the bank and referenced to the GAFI investment certificate, and banks apply full AML/KYC scrutiny to every conversion. In practice, profit remittance is impeded not by statutory ceilings but by operational hurdles, chiefly delays in obtaining tax clearance, bank-level internal compliance reviews, and the unwillingness of banks to process large conversions during periods of EGP scarcity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE board instructions, exporters must repatriate 100% of the invoice value into Egypt through a licensed bank within 30 days of the date of export (date of shipment for goods; date of invoice or client payment in the case of cross-border SaaS/logistics-telematics services). There is no mandatory surrender ratio to Egyptian pounds: since the 2016 float and the 2022-2024 IMF reform sequence, exporters may keep all proceeds in a CBE-licensed foreign-currency account or voluntarily convert them at the interbank market rate. Balances retained abroad (e.g., in offshore EMI wallets) beyond the 30-day window are treated as unrepatriated export proceeds and expose the LLC to fines, customs sanctions, and the denial of future import/export authorisation; free-zone companies and long-term installment contracts require prior CBE approval for deviation.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole regulatory and supervisory authority for foreign exchange, banking, and payment systems under Law No. 194 of 2020; the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) administers investment approvals, and the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) supervises non-bank financial services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for any offshore EMI used as the principal treasury rail; Medium only as an ancillary collection gateway. Legal constraints: offshore digital banks and EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Mercury, Payoneer) have no licence to operate in Egypt; EGP-denominated accounts, local clearing, or payment-processing to Egyptian counterparties require a CBE licence or a partnership with a licensed Egyptian bank under the CBE Payment Systems regulation (Law 194 of 2020). An LLC may use such EMIs solely to receive hard-currency SaaS subscriptions from foreign clients, but the accumulated balances must be flowed to the Egyptian bank within 30 days to satisfy export-proceeds repatriation, and the account must be declared in the CBE transfer documentation; otherwise, the structure creates an unrepatriated-proceeds exposure.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% on taxable corporate profit (Law No. 91 of 2005, Income Tax Law, Article 48, as amended; uniform rate for Egyptian LLCs and foreign-owned LLCs).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends distributed by an Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders (Law 91 of 2005, Article 56, as amended); the 5% branch-remittance tax applies separately to foreign branch after-tax profits, not to shareholder dividends.
