Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), under the Ministry of Finance. Taxpayer registration, tax-file administration, VAT registration, electronic invoicing, electronic receipts, declarations, and payment services are administered through the ETA electronic tax portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg and related electronic platforms.

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian LLC must obtain a unified tax registration number and open an income-tax file with the competent ETA office based on its registered address. The standard process is: (1) complete incorporation and obtain the Commercial Register and required business-license documentation; (2) obtain the company tax card or electronic tax-registration certificate from the ETA; (3) submit the constitutional documents, Commercial Register, company address evidence, articles of association, identification of managers or authorized representatives, and power of attorney where applicable; (4) obtain VAT registration when the statutory threshold is exceeded or voluntary registration is accepted; (5) complete ETA portal enrollment, authorized-user verification, digital certificate and electronic-signature procedures where required; and (6) obtain an electronic-invoicing profile and integrate approved ERP, API, or portal issuance channels. A non-resident foreign entity with an Egyptian taxable activity, permanent establishment, Egyptian-source income, or VAT obligations must register with the ETA through its Egyptian tax representative or authorized representative and provide certified constitutional, tax-residence, authority, and business documents, with Arabic translation and legalization or apostille where required. Non-resident registration does not by itself create an Egyptian legal entity; an Egyptian branch, subsidiary, permanent establishment, or other taxable presence may require separate commercial and regulatory registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box election for an Egyptian LLC. An Egyptian LLC is ordinarily taxed as a separate legal person for corporate income tax and VAT purposes. No general statutory hybrid-mismatch election changes the LLC's default classification. Separate applications or eligibility conditions may apply for investment-law incentives, free-zone treatment, special economic-zone treatment, tax treaty relief, or approved tax-accounting arrangements; these are incentive or relief regimes and not entity-classification elections. A branch or permanent establishment of a foreign company is generally taxed under the rules applicable to a non-resident enterprise and is not interchangeable with an Egyptian LLC merely by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover, subject to the statutory turnover calculation and applicable regulatory rules. Registration may also be required without relying on the threshold for specified activities, imports, taxable supplies subject to special rules, or other statutory cases. Importers and non-residents may have registration or collection obligations independently of the domestic turnover threshold. Voluntary registration may be available subject to ETA conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Egypt to a destination outside Egypt may qualify for VAT at 0% if the transaction satisfies the VAT Law, executive regulations, customs requirements, and ETA documentary conditions. The exporter must substantiate the actual export, identify the goods and purchaser, retain the commercial and customs records, and establish that the goods left Egyptian territory. Certain services supplied to non-residents may qualify for zero rating where the statutory conditions are met, including qualifying offshore use or benefit outside Egypt and compliance with documentary and payment evidence requirements. Services performed or consumed in Egypt generally do not become zero-rated merely because the customer is a non-resident or an international airline. Meals prepared and delivered in Egypt to an aircraft at an Egyptian airport should generally be treated as a domestic taxable supply unless a specific customs, bonded, export, or special-zone rule applies and is fully documented. Export VAT refunds or input-tax recovery require valid purchase invoices, customs evidence, VAT filings, proof of payment and export proceeds where requested, and reconciliation of output and input tax.

  • Egyptian Customs export declaration or equivalent validated export record
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, customer, goods, quantities, values, currency, VAT treatment, and destination
  • Packing list and transport document, including bill of lading, airway bill, or other carrier evidence as applicable
  • Evidence that the goods physically exited Egypt and were accepted by customs at the export point
  • Certificate of origin where required by the destination, customs authorities, trade arrangement, or customer
  • VAT invoice or electronic invoice and supporting sales ledger entry
  • Bank evidence of export proceeds or other approved payment evidence where required by the ETA, banking, customs, or foreign-exchange rules
  • Contracts, purchase orders, delivery records, airway or ramp-delivery records, and evidence linking the exported goods to the relevant customs declaration
  • For bonded or airport supplies, applicable bonded-warehouse, customs-control, duty-free, or special-zone authorization and inventory reconciliation records

Egypt operates a mandatory electronic-invoicing regime for in-scope business-to-business transactions and other categories brought into scope by ETA decisions and implementation phases. Taxpayers must register on the ETA electronic-invoicing platform, obtain or use an approved electronic signature, issue structured electronic invoices containing prescribed supplier, customer, tax, item, value, and unique-identification data, and transmit the invoice through the ETA portal or an approved API or ERP integration. The ETA assigns or validates a unique invoice identifier and may electronically validate or reject submissions. Electronic receipts apply to covered business-to-consumer transactions under the applicable implementation timetable. Paper invoices generally do not satisfy electronic-invoicing obligations once the relevant taxpayer and transaction are in scope. In-flight catering suppliers should assess whether invoices to airlines, airport operators, and other corporate customers fall within mandatory electronic invoicing, and whether ramp delivery, bonded-kitchen, import, customs, or airport-security systems impose additional documentation controls.

The ETA and Customs Authority may deny zero rating, disallow input VAT recovery, recover incorrectly refunded or unpaid VAT, assess the applicable VAT retrospectively, and impose statutory late-payment interest and administrative penalties. Scrutiny commonly addresses false or incomplete customs declarations, mismatch between invoices and export declarations, failure to prove physical export, fictitious customers or transactions, invalid electronic invoices, unsupported input VAT, related-party pricing, missing bank evidence, and diversion of bonded goods into the domestic market. Fraudulent or deliberately evasive conduct may trigger tax-evasion charges, substantial fines, criminal liability for responsible individuals, suspension or cancellation of tax registration, customs sanctions, seizure or forfeiture, and loss of export or bonded privileges. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation period should be treated as a compliance control for export proceeds, subject to applicable Central Bank of Egypt, banking, customs, sectoral, and approved-extension rules; non-compliance may result in banking or export-control consequences and can undermine refund substantiation. The business should maintain a transaction-level export file and reconcile customs, ETA, accounting, airline-contract, inventory, and bank records for the statutory retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP is convertible on current-account transactions in line with Egypt's IMF Article VIII commitments and the Unified Banking Law No. 194 of 2020. Trade payments for goods and services, royalty payments, and routine overseas expenses may be executed through authorised banks at market-determined interbank rates without item-by-item CBE licence. Capital-account convertibility is partial: inward equity subscription in an Egyptian LLC and registered shareholder loans are protected transfers once declared to the receiving bank; subsequent dividend remittance, loan servicing and capital repatriation may be effected without pre-approval, but each outward transfer is subject to documentary validation. Key profit-remittance hurdles are: (i) the bank must hold audited financial statements, a dividend board resolution, and proof that the 10% withholding tax (or treaty-reduced rate, where applicable) on dividends was filed and paid to the Egyptian Tax Authority; (ii) an ETA tax-clearance certificate is normally demanded for large or recurring outward dividend wires; (iii) in periods of USD liquidity stress, CBE prioritisation can impose queuing of T+2 to T+5 on large outbound transfers; and (iv) EGP is not deliverable offshore, so all foreign-currency liabilities must be covered from foreign-currency balances or converted onshore. Overall, the regime is a managed, trade-liberal system — safe for registered FDI trade flows, but not to be treated as unrestricted capital-account convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The catering LLC must repatriate the full foreign-currency value of export proceeds, including bonded in-flight meal uplifts recognised as customs exports, so that the funds are credited to an account at an authorised Egyptian bank within 90 calendar days of the customs export declaration or airline uplift document date. The obligation is 100% repatriation, not 100% mandatory conversion into EGP: under the post-December-2022 flexible-exchange-rate framework, the CBE has not imposed a forced conversion ratio at receipt, and proceeds may be retained in the invoice currency in a foreign-currency account for onward import settlement, debt service or treasury deployment. To close each export file, the LLC must file with its bank the NAFEZA single-window export declaration, commercial invoice, airway bill or airline uplift manifest, the IATA flight schedule reference, and the CBE export-monitoring registration reference. The bank matches the inbound FX credit to the export documents and releases funds to the treasury account. Non-repatriation within the statutory window can trigger CBE monetary penalties, downgrading of the exporter's banking file, suspension of future import FX allocations, and mandatory AML reporting to the Egyptian Money-Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Combating Unit.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Foreign Exchange Operations Sector and Banking Control Sector. The CBE is the sole monetary, foreign-exchange and banking control authority under the Unified Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, operating the interbank EGP/USD trading platform, regulating authorised FX dealers, administering import/export trade-payment documentation, monitoring export proceeds repatriation, and licensing all deposit-taking institutions in the Arab Republic of Egypt. No separate foreign-exchange administration exists outside the CBE.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

LOW — offshore digital EMIs are not a legally viable primary banking rail in Egypt. The Unified Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves deposit-taking, foreign-currency dealing and payment-account services to CBE-licensed banks and their licensed payment-technology subsidiaries. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business or Currencycloud cannot hold EGP, cannot satisfy the 90-day export-proceeds repatriation rule, and are not recognised for capital injection or dividend remittance clearance. The CBE requires all export receipts to land via SWIFT/RTGS in an Egyptian licensed bank account. An EMI may be used only as an auxiliary collection tool for non-Egypt, non-export ancillary receivables; any such funds must be swept onward to the Egyptian anchor bank and will trigger enhanced due diligence. The recommended architecture is a single Tier-1 Egyptian bank as treasury anchor, supplemented, only if an airline counterparty insists, by a USD concentration account at a recognised foreign branch of a CBE-licensed bank rather than an EMI.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% dividend withholding tax on profit distributions by Egyptian LLCs and joint stock companies; a reduced 5% rate applies to distributions by companies listed on the Egyptian Exchange subject to listing and holding-period conditions. Non-resident airline parent companies may invoke a DTA to lower or eliminate the rate, typically to 5% or 0% under an eligible substantial-holding article.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months after the close of the fiscal year; for a calendar-year taxpayer, no later than 30 April of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due by the end of the month following each tax quarter: 30 April, 31 July, 31 October, and 31 January for a calendar-year taxpayer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required for taxpayers whose total annual gross revenues exceed EGP 20 million and whose controlled related-party transaction values exceed EGP 10 million. The Transfer Pricing Disclosure return must accompany the annual Form No. 19 whenever the related-party transaction exceeds the regulatory threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a separate GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, the ETA applies the PE definition aligned with OECD and UN treaty practice, supported by General Anti-Avoidance Rules under Law 91/2005. An airline catering company claiming treaty benefits must show real decision-making, operational staff, physical kitchen and refrigeration presence, dispatch records, and a local board or management authority; otherwise the ETA may recharacterize or deny PE and treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Assessments on deemed profit of 15%-20% of gross catering turnover where books are rejected; incremental tax penalty of 25% of the evaded tax, monthly interest of 2%, fines of 100%-200% of tax evaded under Law No. 91 of 2005, and referral to the Economic Prosecution for criminal tax evasion. Imprisonment may range from two to five years, with publication of the conviction and revocation of the catering license.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false returns or approved fake invoices are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties. Civil restitution is supported by attachment of personal assets, bank accounts, and aviation contracts; travel bans may be ordered by the execution court, and a criminal conviction disqualifies directors from managing food supply or airline-related companies for up to five years.
Shell Company / Sham PE

Statutory sanction: The ETA may disregard the shell, reattribute all Egypt-source inflight catering income to a permanent establishment of the foreign parent, apply a deemed-profit assessment on gross catering service fees, disallow all management and royalty deductions, and impose estate-of-debt sanctions including fines and double penalties for abusive tax avoidance. The company's operating license may be revoked by the Civil Aviation and catering licensing authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who formed or maintained a sham entity face veil-piercing judgments for all unpaid Egyptian corporate tax, social insurance, and VAT-type obligations. Courts may impose civil restitution, personal travel bans, seizure of directors' residential properties, and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment of up to five years for misuse of corporate legal personality.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA makes adjustments using TNMM or Profit Split based on Egyptian food-service benchmark comparables, assesses arm's-length interest on the deficiency, levies an understatement penalty of up to 25%-50% of additional tax, and applies monthly 2% interest. The failure to provide TP documentation also blocks evidentiary rebuttal and triggers a higher-margin deemed assessment without entitlement to deduction.

Director personal liability: Where a director or tax supervisor deliberately refuses TP documentation requests, the personal liability regime applies to the resulting additional tax and penalties in proportion to fault. Courts may order the recovery of underpaid profits from directors' bank accounts, impose travel bans during tax subpoena proceedings, and prohibit the director from serving on any Egyptian tax-compliant board for a regulatory period.
AML and Undocumented Cash Flows

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended, sanctions include fines of up to EGP 200 million for entities, imprisonment of senior managers for three to seven years, freezing of bank accounts, confiscation of funds equal to the laundered amount, and cancellation of the customs bonded-airport license held by the catering operator.

Director personal liability: Every director, compliance officer, and general manager who knowingly or negligently allowed hawala, ghost payroll, or unreported cash circulation is personally liable for fines and restitution. The criminal court may issue international arrest warrants, impose domestic travel bans, order asset seizure, and disqualify the individual from holding future catering licenses or duty-authority privileges in Egyptian airport zones.
