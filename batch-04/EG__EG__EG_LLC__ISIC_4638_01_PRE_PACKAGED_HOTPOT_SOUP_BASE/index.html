Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA / المصلحة المصرية للضرائب), Ministry of Finance; unified portal: www.eta.gov.eg (tax card, CIT, VAT, e-invoicing/e-receipts platforms). Customs-side counterpart: Egyptian Customs Authority (ECA) operating the NAFEZA single window / ACI (Advanced Cargo Information) system; corporate incorporation: General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the LLC before GAFI or the local Commercial Registry; obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate and company file number. Step 2: Obtain an Import/Export Card (exporters register with the Ministry of Trade and Industry / GOEIC) before undertaking export distribution of the hotpot soup base product. Step 3: File corporate tax registration with ETA within one month of commercial registration; ETA issues the Tax Card (بطاقة ضريبية) reflecting the unified tax ID and certifying the LLC as subject to CIT, VAT and stamp duties. Step 4: Register for VAT with ETA (portal or competent tax office) once taxable annual turnover exceeds EGP 500,000, or voluntarily at incorporation, and obtain a distinct VAT registration number. Step 5: Complete mandatory e-invoicing onboarding: procure the ETA client X.509 certificate, connect the ERP to the ETA REST API (v1.3+) or use the ETA web portal, and link export transactions to NAFEZA/ACI customs declarations. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a per se corporate tax resident under Law No. 91 of 2005, subject to CIT at 22.5% on worldwide income; no pass-through, flow-through or check-the-box recognition. Distributions to non-resident shareholders are generally subject to a 10% dividend withholding tax, treaty-reduced where applicable. SCZone/Investment Zones may offer special incentive regimes under Law 72 of 2017 and Suez Canal Economic Zone protocols, but the default statutory classification remains entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box regime; LLC tax classification is statutory. Available elections relevant to the export LLC: (i) consolidated corporate income tax filing for a fiscal group under Article 51 of Law 91/2005 where the parent holds at least 90% of capital and voting rights and both entities adopt the same fiscal year; (ii) immediate claiming of input VAT credits attributable to export zero-rated supplies (refund election under Article 30 of Law 67 of 2016) instead of carrying credits forward; (iii) tax loss carryforward election under Article 34 of Law 91/2005 for a maximum of five years, with no general carryback; (iv) advance pricing agreements and mutual agreement procedures under Egypt's treaty network for transfer-pricing and hybrid mismatch resolution. No hybrid mismatch 'reverse hybrid' election is recognized in Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration threshold: EGP 500,000 annual turnover of taxable supplies under Law 67/2016 (Article 16). Exporters may register voluntarily before reaching that threshold in order to claim input VAT refunds; non-resident digital-service suppliers are subject to deemed registration with no threshold, and Egyptian business buyers self-assess on reverse charge for qualifying imported services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot base bricks, are zero-rated (0% VAT) under Law 67 of 2016 where the goods are physically exported and delivered to a non-resident buyer within the statutory export windows (generally 30–90 days from supply, subject to customs extensions). Offshore services are zero-rated only when rendered to a non-resident recipient, effectively consumed outside Egypt, and supported by a written contract and foreign-currency payment; no domestic Egyptian use may occur. Input VAT directly attributable to the zero-rated export stream is refundable under Article 30 of Law 67 of 2016 through the electronic refund workflow and is subject to a documentary audit against customs and banking data.

  • ETA-cleared e-invoice (XML, acceptance UUID and QR code) for the export supply
  • Certified commercial invoice and export sales contract (Arabic or bilingual)
  • Customs Export Declaration as released on the NAFEZA/ACI single window, showing the statistical value and applicable HS classification (2103.x/2106.x)
  • Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air) or CMR (road) naming the exporting LLC as shipper and the foreign customer as consignee
  • Packing list, certificate of origin (if claimed) and weight/delivery list
  • Inward remittance certificate or Swift MT103 evidencing foreign-currency payment credited to the LLC's Egyptian bank account
  • Central Bank of Egypt foreign-currency export declaration confirming timely repatriation of export proceeds

Mandatory e-invoicing with real-time fiscal clearance: every export supply invoice must be submitted to ETA in the official ETA XML/JSON schema, digitally signed with the ETA-issued X.509 client certificate, and transmitted through the REST API (v1.3+), a licensed middleware product, or the ETA portal; ETA validates and returns an acceptance UUID and QR hash within seconds. Without clearance, the invoice is legally deemed unissued, export refunds are suspended, and penalties accrue. E-receipts (B2C transactions) and NAFEZA Advance Cargo Information (ACI) registration are mandatory in phases, with all VAT-registered taxpayers covered through the January 2025 rollout; physical export additionally requires the ACI export message, a customs statistical declaration and the ETA frozen transaction codes for refund processing.

ETA cross-matches e-invoice clearance data with NAFEZA customs releases, CBE remittance records and bank MT103 evidence. Any discrepancy triggers suspension of the refund, reversal of input VAT credited and re-issuance of the export invoice at 14% VAT. For fraudulent zero-rating, Articles 71–72 of Law 67 of 2016 impose repayment of the evaded VAT, a fine of EGP 20,000 to EGP 5,000,000, imprisonment of between six months and five years, and interest at 14% per annum from the original due date. Under Law 91 of 2005 (Article 133), willfully false export documentation in CIT refund claims carries a penalty up to 300% of the evaded tax and criminal liability; the exporter is also disqualified from the refund program and e-invoicing clearance for up to three years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a market-driven managed float regime (post-March-2024 FX reform) in which the EGP is fully convertible at interbank market rates for current account purposes; import/export payments, services and profit remittances are free of quantitative ceilings when supported by the required documentary file (commercial contract, customs declarations, tax clearance). Capital account transactions remain registration-based: foreign direct investment inflows must be recorded with GAFI (Law 72/2017) so that the future repatriation of capital and 100% disposal of after-tax net profits can be executed without CBE approval; in practice, CBE FX liquidity pressure periodically creates a settlement queue for non-essential repatriations, so the EG-LLC treasury must maintain a 30-to-45 day USD liquidity buffer and pre-position dividends in a USD account when the interbank spread is attractive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE export-currency rules, 100% of the invoice value of non-oil export proceeds must be repatriated through the Egyptian banking system within 90 days of customs release, extendable to 180 days for deferred-payment export contracts upon written approval. The former mandatory 75% surrender ratio (Decree 380/2004) was abolished in 2016, and under the current flexible regime there is zero mandatory EGP conversion ratio: the exporter may credit proceeds to a USD/EUR/CNY foreign-currency account with an authorised dealer bank or convert at the interbank rate at its chosen timing. Nevertheless, the CBE requires submission of the customs export release certificate (via CargoX/ACI platform), the shipment export declaration and a bank receipt-of-value certificate; any non-repatriated or late-repatriated proceeds are reported by the bank to the CBE, triggering administrative penalties under Law 194/2020 and potential suspension of the exporter's customs identification number.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020; the General Department for Foreign Exchange Operations and the Financial Stability/Anti-Money Laundering Unit jointly administer exchange-control enforcement, authorised-dealer licensing, FX position limits and cross-border reporting for all Egyptian resident entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for ancillary outlays only. Egypt's Law 221/2020 on Fintech allows CBE-licensed non-bank financial companies to operate digital payment wallets, but no offshore fintech EMI (Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business, or similar) holds a CBE banking licence enabling resident corporate deposit-taking, LC issuance or export-proceeds collection. Offshore EMIs may be used for small service payments (software, marketing, remote contractors) and for receiving sponsor/partner micro-funding, but they cannot serve as the primary cross-border treasury rail or fulfil Egyptian Customs/import-form requirements; all substantive FX and liquidity flows must clear through CBE-licensed dealer banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections should be executed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the foreign parent to the EG-LLC's USD account at the chosen Egyptian authorised-dealer bank, using the CBE Balance-of-Payments purpose code for direct investment equity and including the GAFI investment certificate reference in the narrative field for auto-classification. The funds are credited without prior CBE approval only when the GAFI registration material (board capital-increase resolution, amended shareholding structure and Ministry of Investment form) is pre-filed with the receiving bank; the bank subsequently issues an inward credit verification letter that serves as the legal basis for future dividend repatriation. For amounts above USD 250,000, supporting evidence of the parent's foreign corporate tax residence and a certified copy of the parent's constitution must be deposited; shareholders' loans must be expressly subordinated and declared on the bank's external-debt form to prevent reclassification as equity and to protect the future annual-interest remittance.Outbound disbursements run on two primary rails: (a) SWIFT MT103/MT202 via the Egyptian bank's correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire in New York for USD) for open-account TTs, service fees and advance settlements, each instruction matched with the CBE importer declaration and the approved FX budget; and (b) Irrevocable commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) issued under UCP600 for tallow and bulk-material purchases, with exporter presentations advised via MT754 and sight reimbursement executed against compliant documents; local EGP disbursements (customs duty, port charges, freight and payroll taxes) are cleared on the CBE Real-Time Gross Settlement system. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10 million aggregate related-party transactions per year for the Local File; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 300 million; a simplified transfer-pricing disclosure form must be filed for cross-border related-party transactions exceeding EGP 2 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt follows a substance-over-form doctrine and applies a general anti-abuse rule (GAAR). The ETA may disregard an entity or arrangement that lacks commercial justification and is principally tax-motivated. Free-zone companies must demonstrate actual premises, qualified staff, real decision-making, and substantive operations in the free zone; failure to do so results in loss of the tax holiday and re-assessment at 22.5%. All related-party transactions, including export sales of pre-packaged hotpot soup base bricks, must be supported by contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and benchmark analyses.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of twice the evaded tax; in addition, the full tax plus a 100% late-payment surcharge is collected, and the company's tax card is suspended.

Director personal liability: Where directors sign false returns or participate in fictitious invoicing, the corporate veil is pierced; directors face civil restitution of the evaded tax, travel bans, and imprisonment under Law 91 of 2005.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA disregards the legal form, applies GAAR to re-assess 22.5% tax on deemed Egyptian-source profit, revokes the free-zone exemption, and levies an understatement penalty of up to 40% of the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for the re-determined tax and subjected to travel bans; criminal fraud charges are available if the misrepresentation is intentional.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under Law 80 of 2002, imprisonment of up to 15 years and a fine equal to 5%–10% of the laundered amount; bank accounts are frozen and the proceeds confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors personally face criminal liability, asset freezing, and pecuniary restitution; extradition is available under Egypt's bilateral criminal treaties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may adjust the taxable profit, disallow cross-border related-party deductions, impose an additional tax equal to 10% of the audited shortfall, and levy a fixed punitive fine of EGP 100,000 for non-filing of the disclosure form.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return while aware of the missing transfer-pricing file can be personally liable for the tax shortfall; a court-ordered travel bar may be issued during enforcement.
