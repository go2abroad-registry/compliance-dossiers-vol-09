Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Egyptian Ministry of Finance. Main portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing portal: https://einvoice.invoicing.eta.gov.eg; e-receipt portal: https://ereceipt.invoicing.eta.gov.eg. Unified national tax administration; no sub-national entity-level tax authorities.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the LLC at the Commercial Registry (Ministry of Trade and Industry) to obtain the Commercial Registration (CR) number and certificate of incorporation. Step 2: file the ETA Tax Card Application (Form 1) with the competent district tax office, attaching the CR extract, authenticated memorandum/articles of association, board resolution, lease agreement, copies of manager/shareholder IDs, and bank confirmation; the ETA issues the unified tax card containing the 14-digit Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration references. Step 3: register for VAT on the same ETA file once annual taxable turnover reaches or is projected to reach EGP 500,000 (mandatory within 30 days); voluntary registration is allowed below the threshold and is advisable for export-oriented businesses to recover input VAT. Step 4: enroll in the ETA e-invoicing system by obtaining an ETA-accredited digital-signature certificate, completing portal onboarding, and activating production API credentials. Step 5: complete Social Insurance Authority (SISI) registration for payroll and open the foreign-currency account with an Egyptian authorized dealer bank for export proceeds. A foreign-owned LLC must maintain a resident tax representative/manager for ETA correspondence; non-resident pure digital service providers without an Egyptian establishment may instead use the ETA simplified foreign e-services registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Egyptian LLC is a juridical tax resident under Income Tax Law 91/2005 and is subject to corporate income tax at 22.5% on worldwide net income. No pass-through or check-the-box classification is available; the LLC is taxed at entity level, and profit distributions are subject to 10% dividend withholding. Treaty and unilateral foreign-tax-credit relief may apply to foreign-source income, but no general territorial exemption exists. The software/fintech services profile does not change the statutory classification.

Available election forms

Egypt does not recognize US-style check-the-box elections; classification follows statutory corporate form. Available elections: (a) Law 20/2020 SME presumptive-tax regime for annual turnover up to EGP 20 million, with final revenue-based cash-basis rates of 0.5%, 0.75%, 1% and 1.5% by turnover bracket, elected by formal ETA notification before the first return; (b) depreciation and inventory valuation method elections declared in the first CIT return; (c) voluntary early VAT registration before crossing the EGP 500,000 threshold; (d) advance pricing agreements (APA) with the ETA transfer-pricing unit for cross-border service contracts; and (e) treaty-based withholding-rate reductions claimed through ETA double-tax relief forms. No fiscal-unity (tax grouping) election and no hybrid-mismatch unilateral election exist under Egyptian domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 per annum of taxable turnover; mandatory registration within 30 days of crossing the threshold (Article 28 of VAT Law 67/2016). Registration is also required without threshold for goods importers and for entities seeking input-VAT recovery on exports. Non-resident suppliers of digital services to Egyptian consumers are required to register through the ETA foreign-supplier e-portal regardless of the EGP 500,000 threshold; services imported from non-residents by Egyptian businesses remain subject to reverse-charge withholding at 14%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for (a) tangible goods physically exported through Egyptian customs, and (b) services supplied by an Egyptian VAT-registered supplier to a non-resident recipient where the benefit is wholly consumed abroad, including software-as-a-service, cloud infrastructure, and financial portfolio tracking applications. The exporter must evidence the non-resident status of the recipient (overseas tax/CR certificate), maintain a written export contract addressed abroad, issue a foreign-currency commercial invoice, and channel proceeds through an Egyptian authorized dealer bank. For app-store based software exports (Stripe and Apple IAP rails), platform payout statements, end-user IP/geolocation evidence, and overseas subscriber contracts are accepted as supplementary proof that the service is consumed outside Egypt. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is refundable under Article 23 of VAT Law 67/2016.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Egyptian Single Window (NAFEZA) identifying the exporter as legal owner and specifying the HS code and physical exit record
  • Export contract and commercial invoice showing FOB/CIF value, foreign-currency payment terms, and recipient address outside Egypt
  • Original Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill (air), or CMR/road manifest naming the Egyptian exporter as consignor/shipper
  • Packing list and container/seal or terminal exit receipt evidencing departure from Egyptian territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice from the Egyptian authorized dealer confirming foreign-currency receipt and conversion within the CBE repatriation window
  • VAT return e-file schedule of zero-rated export supplies together with the cleared e-invoice marked with the export indicator
  • For digital services: platform payout statements (Stripe/Apple IAP), IP-geolocation data of end users, and the overseas subscriber contract

Mandatory. Since the phased introduction beginning 15 November 2020, all ETA-registered enterprises are progressively required to submit e-invoices (B2B/B2G) and, under the e-receipt program, B2C retail receipts in real time. Documents are transmitted in XML (UBL 2.1) or JSON to the ETA e-invoicing API (Rased central platform), which performs XSD schema validation, XAdES digital-signature verification, TIN/CR cross-checks, and business-rule validation. Upon clearance the ETA returns a unique digital stamp code (UUID and QR block) that must be embedded in the invoice; a document that fails validation or lacks the stamp is treated as never issued and cannot support input-VAT deduction. API integration uses OAuth 2.0 client-credentials authentication with HMAC-signed payloads and an active on-boarded digital certificate. Non-compliance creates a presumption of undeclared revenue and may result in risk-engine blacklisting and business suspension orders.

The ETA reconciles the zero-rated export schedule against NAFEZA customs exit data, CBE foreign-exchange records, and bank IRCs. Failure to substantiate the export within the 180-day repatriation window triggers immediate reassessment at 14% VAT, a monthly 2% late-payment addition (Article 30 of VAT Law 67/2016), and complete clawback of any input VAT previously refunded. Where export documents are falsified or transactions are circular/reversed, Article 66 imposes fines of up to EGP 2,000,000 and imprisonment, extends the five-year assessment period, and the CBE may block the exporter's commercial registration and trade-finance facilities. The Law 4/2023 tax-penalty reconciliation facility permits early settlement of late additions at reduced rates but does not extinguish criminal liability for forged export evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime following the March 2024 national FX unification. Current-account transactions, including cross-border software-service receipts, are freely convertible, and registered profit/dividend repatriation is permissible after ETA issuance of a tax-clearance certificate and completion of annual statutory audit. Capital-account items — equity injections, shareholder-loan conversions, and money-market investments — remain subject to CBE balance-of-payments reporting and bank-level compliance checks. Practical hurdles include interbank USD liquidity volatility, mandatory inward-declaration forms for foreign investment, and CBE scrutiny of whether the underlying revenue represents genuine Egyptian service exports rather than passive investment income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Circular 31 (as amended through the 2016 float and 2024 unification), exporters of goods and intangible services must repatriate export proceeds into an Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the contractual export/settlement date (180 days for permitted categories). For a fintech software exporter in ISIC 6201, the shipment-evidence trigger is the signed SaaS/subscription contract and e-invoice rather than customs declarations. There is no mandatory conversion ratio today — exporters may retain USD proceeds in foreign-currency accounts or sell them voluntarily at the interbank rate. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline exposes the exporter to suspension of its CBE export file, denial of FX trade facilities, and bank-imposed documentary penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary, banking-licensing, and foreign-exchange authority; the Ministry of Finance and General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) govern entity registration, while the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) administers tax-clearance gates for capital and profit remittances.

Stripe Fit: High

VIABILITY RATING: LOW as a full banking substitute; LIMITED SAFE-HARBOR as a collection-only intermediary. Physical-goods imports are cleared via commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) where the vendor requires documentary protection or the transaction value exceeds the bank's open-account threshold; the CBE imposes no mandatory LC requirement in the current regime, but the bank's electronic payment order and customs-form linkage must show a valid import purpose code. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: VIABILITY RATING: LOW as a full banking substitute; LIMITED SAFE-HARBOR as a collection-only intermediary. CBE Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 requires any entity that handles EGP payments, issues payment instruments, or provides payment-account services to Egyptian residents to hold a CBE-issued license. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, etc.) hold no Egyptian banking or PSP license and therefore cannot maintain EGP settlement accounts, receive interbank EGP transfers, or function as a primary clearing rail. Their permissible role is confined to collecting USD proceeds from merchant-of-record rails (Stripe, Apple IAP) and executing an onward SWIFT MT103 credit to the EG_LLC USD account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, with full UBO and sanctions reporting completed through the receiving bank's KYC channel.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% corporate income tax (CIT) on taxable commercial profits of the EG_LLC under Law No. 91 of 2005 (Income Tax Law), as amended by Law No. 2 of 2018 and subsequent finance laws.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax (WHT) on gross dividends distributed by the Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders under Article 56 of Law No. 91 of 2005; distributions to resident corporate shareholders holding 25% or more may claim the domestic participation exemption, but distributions to foreign portfolio shareholders remain subject to 10% unless a treaty reduces the rate.
