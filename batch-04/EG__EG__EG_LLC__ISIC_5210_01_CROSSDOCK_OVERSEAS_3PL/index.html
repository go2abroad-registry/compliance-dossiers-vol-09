Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for an Egyptian LLC; however, if the founders declare a capital in the Articles, the amount may be deposited into an Egyptian bank account as a precaution against non-completion, and the bank certificate is filed with GAFI when using the pre-incorporation deposit procedure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official ETA digital portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg, with integrated e-invoicing, e-receipt, taxpayer registration and tax card services.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC or a foreign LLC branch engaged in cross-dock/3PL fulfillment: (1) complete corporate existence/investment registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Commercial Registry; (2) legalize the foreign incorporation/charter, power of attorney and board resolutions in Arabic and obtain the required apostille/consular legalization; (3) file the LLC or branch registration documents with GAFI and obtain the investment/commercial registration number; (4) submit the ETA taxpayer registration application at the competent tax office with the GAFI certificate, constitution/charter, local lease or service address evidence, local statutory representative passport, and board resolution, after which the ETA issues the unified Tax Card carrying the Tax Identification Number (TIN); (5) apply separately for VAT registration under VAT Law 67/2016 as soon as the mandatory registration threshold is met or before making the first taxable supply, and activate the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt account with a certified digital signature. A foreign entity without a branch cannot ordinarily obtain a standalone TIN/VAT ID; the in-country LLC/branch is the statutory Egyptian taxpayer.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned Egyptian LLC or a foreign LLC branch engaged in cross-dock/3PL fulfillment: (1) complete corporate existence/investment registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and the Commercial Registry; (2) legalize the foreign incorporation/charter, power of attorney and board resolutions in Arabic and obtain the required apostille/consular legalization; (3) file the LLC or branch registration documents with GAFI and obtain the investment/commercial registration number; (4) submit the ETA taxpayer registration application at the competent tax office with the GAFI certificate, constitution/charter, local lease or service address evidence, local statutory representative passport, and board resolution, after which the ETA issues the unified Tax Card carrying the Tax Identification Number (TIN); (5) apply separately for VAT registration under VAT Law 67/2016 as soon as the mandatory registration threshold is met or before making the first taxable supply, and activate the ETA e-invoicing/e-receipt account with a certified digital signature. A foreign entity without a branch cannot ordinarily obtain a standalone TIN/VAT ID; the in-country LLC/branch is the statutory Egyptian taxpayer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or pass-through election for LLCs: an Egyptian LLC is a per se corporation for income tax purposes and a separate VAT person. No hybrid mismatch or entity-classification election is available. Limited administrative elections exist: voluntary VAT registration from the EGP 250,000 turnover level, VAT grouping or consolidated VAT registration where the ETA recognizes common control, investment incentive regimes in Special Economic Zones/free zones, and depreciation method elections under Law 91/2005.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration where annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is available where annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 250,000. The ETA may also require registration before the threshold where a taxpayer makes an importation or first taxable supply, and foreign digital/e-commerce suppliers making taxable Egyptian supplies may have separate registration obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported outside Egypt and to internationally connected services (international transport, handling, packing, commission and port services) supplied to or paid by non-residents, provided the exporter is VAT-registered; the goods physically leave Egypt within the statutory export window (generally 90 days from invoice, extendable on ETA/Customs approval); a customs export declaration is filed in the exporter’s name; the sale is made to a non-resident under a foreign-currency contract; the goods are not diverted to domestic consumption; and input VAT invoices are properly matched to the export supply. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is creditable and refundable through ETA after documentary matching.

  • Customs Export Declaration and officially stamped terminal/port release records evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR or equivalent transport document naming a consignee outside Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from an Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign exchange

Mandatory ETA e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance applies to VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted from certified ERP/billing software through the ETA API in prescribed XML/JSON schema, digitally signed with the taxpayer’s ETA certificate, and validated by the ETA before becoming fiscally valid. Accepted invoices receive a unique ETA acceptance code/QR digital stamp; B2B/B2G invoices are cleared in near-real-time, while B2C e-receipt clearance is phased in. Non-compliant invoices are rejected and can deny input VAT deduction. For cross-dock/3PL activity, inventory transfers to the warehouse are not taxable supplies but must be documented consistent with the principal/seller invoicing arrangement to preserve export zero-rating.

ETA can re-audit export transactions retrospectively and reverse zero-rating where documentation is missing, mismatched or backdated, requiring output VAT plus reversal of input VAT credits/refunds. Late payment surcharges accrue on assessed tax, and administrative fines under VAT Law 67/2016 apply for invoice, declaration and documentation violations. Fraudulent export claims, including forged customs declarations, bills of lading or inward remittance certificates, circular foreign-currency receipts, or fabricated cross-dock movements, can result in disgorgement of refunded VAT, additional fines calculated by reference to evaded tax, suspension or blacklisting of the taxpayer’s TIN/e-invoicing account, and criminal prosecution of the entity and responsible officers. A cross-dock/3PL operator must avoid holding export documents merely for a related principal while recording local fulfillment services, which may be re-characterized as taxable Egyptian service revenue and trigger clawback of the principal’s export zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP is freely convertible for all current-account transactions — import/export settlement, 3PL and logistics service fees, dividend and profit remittance, and intra-group service payments — through CBE-authorized banks at the prevailing interbank rate; capital-account transactions, including foreign-currency borrowing by Egyptian residents, issuance of guarantees abroad, and outbound portfolio investment, remain restricted and require CBE approval or mandatory registration with an authorized bank; non-resident shareholders of an EG LLC may remit dividends and liquidation proceeds after satisfying Egyptian withholding tax (10% standard, treaty-reduced where applicable), submission of audited financial statements, ETA tax clearance, and production of the GAFI investment-file registration; practical remittance hurdles are documentary rather than administrative, including transfer-pricing review of intercompany fees and transient USD liquidity conditions, though the 2025 normalization of foreign-currency availability has materially reduced delay.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters of goods and services, including cross-border e-commerce fulfillment and cross-dock 3PL operators, must repatriate 100% of proceeds to Egypt through a CBE-authorized bank within 180 days from the date of customs release (goods) or from the contractual invoice/settlement date (services); the exporter must file the export declaration, commercial invoice, customs manifest, and marketplace settlement statements to enable the bank's underlying-trade review; the mandatory conversion-to-EGP surrender percentage was rescinded in March 2024, allowing proceeds to be retained in foreign-currency accounts and converted at the interbank rate only when EGP operational liquidity is required; the CBE retains emergency authority to reinstate surrender ratios, and non-compliance triggers penalty interest at the statutory banking rate, suspension of FX services, and delisting from CBE watch reports.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — sole monetary and exchange-control regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), as amended; CBE licenses all authorized banks and non-bank FX bureaus, operates and supervises the interbank foreign-exchange market, issues binding FX circulars, enforces AML/CFT obligations under AML Law No. 80 of 2002, and administers Egypt's unified flexible-exchange-rate framework adopted in March 2024 and fully liberalized in December 2024; the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) handles investment registration but exercises no independent FX regulatory authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary banking channel and Not Permitted as a substitute for an Egyptian licensed bank: foreign digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Payoneer, Currencycloud) hold no Egyptian banking license, cannot maintain EGP nostro accounts, and cannot perform CBE-authorized FX; they are viable only as offshore collection and payout layers for marketplace receipts (Amazon, Noon, Shopify, AliExpress) in USD/EUR/GBP, with onward SWIFT MT103 routing to the EG LLC's onshore Egyptian bank account; all domestic EGP payments — payroll, VAT/ETA settlement, supplier disbursements — must settle through a licensed Egyptian bank under AML Law No. 80 of 2002 and CBE payment-oversight rules; Airwallex Global Accounts may receive cross-border e-commerce payouts, but onboarding must be combined with a physical Egyptian bank account and the EMI's anti-money-laundering and sanctions-screening standards must meet CBE/FATF expectations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 4 months after the close of the fiscal year, per Article 85 of Law 91 of 2005; for a calendar-year taxpayer, no later than 30 April of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory deadline as Form 19; the TP disclosure must be filed within 4 months after the fiscal year-end or no later than the extended filing date granted by the ETA.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Self-assessed advance corporate tax instalments are payable on the ETA-prescribed quarterly dates relative to the fiscal year, generally within 30 days after each quarter-end, with the final settlement made on the annual Form 19.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Identification and Tax Card Data Legal name, commercial registration number, tax card number, 10-digit TIN, address of the Egyptian warehouse, fiscal year start and end dates, and activity code under the ETA business register. A non-resident principal operating through the Egyptian 3PL warehouse as a permanent establishment must complete this box using the Egyptian PE registration details, not the offshore head office details. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the total tax due for each commenced month of delay, an administrative fine up to EGP 50,000, denial of tax clearance certificates, and risk of enforced collection, bank attachment, or warehouse asset seizure.
Box 2 - Gross Revenue Total invoiced revenue for cross-dock handling, storage, order fulfilment, repacking, last-mile coordination, and any separately billed ancillary services. Include only Egypt-source revenue attributable to the Egyptian PE. Revenue booked remotely by the foreign head office for services performed entirely outside Egypt should be excluded and separately supported. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the total tax due for each commenced month of delay, an administrative fine up to EGP 50,000, denial of tax clearance certificates, and risk of enforced collection, bank attachment, or warehouse asset seizure.
Box 3 - Deductible Costs and Expenses Cost of goods sold, wages, warehousing rent, depreciation on material handling equipment, utilities, shipping and freight costs, IT platform expenses, customs brokers, and other P&L items satisfying the deduction rules. Head-office recharge allocations are deductible only where the corresponding services are actually rendered, beneficial to the Egyptian PE, and supported by transfer pricing documentation at arm's length. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the total tax due for each commenced month of delay, an administrative fine up to EGP 50,000, denial of tax clearance certificates, and risk of enforced collection, bank attachment, or warehouse asset seizure.
Box 4 - Taxable Income Before Loss Set-Off Accounting profit adjusted for disallowed expenses, provisions, timing differences, and any transfer pricing adjustments mandated by the ETA. For a non-resident principal with an Egyptian PE, taxable income is limited to profits economically attributable to the PE; no overseas group overheads may be deducted without a compliant TP analysis. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the total tax due for each commenced month of delay, an administrative fine up to EGP 50,000, denial of tax clearance certificates, and risk of enforced collection, bank attachment, or warehouse asset seizure.
Box 5 - Tax Losses Carried Forward Declared prior-year tax losses utilized against current-year taxable income, subject to the 5-year carryforward limitation and continuity-of-activity rules. Cross-border loss pooling is not permitted; losses generated by the Egyptian PE are only available to offset future Egyptian PE income. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the total tax due for each commenced month of delay, an administrative fine up to EGP 50,000, denial of tax clearance certificates, and risk of enforced collection, bank attachment, or warehouse asset seizure.
Box 6 - Advance Payments and Tax Credits Quarterly corporate tax instalments already paid, plus any final withholding tax credits, deducted from the total computed tax. Only credits statutorily available to the Egyptian PE may be claimed; foreign home-country notional taxes or head-office losses cannot be credited. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the total tax due for each commenced month of delay, an administrative fine up to EGP 50,000, denial of tax clearance certificates, and risk of enforced collection, bank attachment, or warehouse asset seizure.
Box 7 - Tax Due Computation Taxable income multiplied by the 22.5% statutory corporate income tax rate, reduced by eligible credits and offset by installed payments, yielding the final balance payable. The flat 22.5% rate applies to the PE's Egyptian attributable income; dividend withholding tax on downstream remittances is not settled through this box. Late filing triggers a monthly surcharge of 2% of the total tax due for each commenced month of delay, an administrative fine up to EGP 50,000, denial of tax clearance certificates, and risk of enforced collection, bank attachment, or warehouse asset seizure.
Box A - Covered Controlled Transactions Identification of all related-party transactions, including intercompany warehouse services, logistics fees, management charges, software licences, interest, and recharges. Non-resident groups must disclose any cross-border arrangement in which the Egyptian PE pays or receives value from group entities, including cost-sharing or global service fee schemes. Failing to file the TP disclosure or Local File triggers fines in the range of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000, upward TP adjustments plus annual interest at 18.5%, and may escalate to criminal tax evasion treatment for wilful concealment.
Box B - Pricing Methodology Selection of the transfer pricing method, comparable data source, financial ratios, and arm's-length range calibration. Provide the benchmarking or comparability study for the Egyptian fulfilment functions and risk profile; state the tested party and rationale clearly. Failing to file the TP disclosure or Local File triggers fines in the range of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000, upward TP adjustments plus annual interest at 18.5%, and may escalate to criminal tax evasion treatment for wilful concealment.
Box C - Certification Signed declaration by the legal representative and certified tax accountant confirming the accuracy of the TP disclosures. The Egyptian PE general manager or authorised power-of-attorney holder must sign; remote electronic signature by the foreign CFO is not sufficient without an Egyptian tax representative. Failing to file the TP disclosure or Local File triggers fines in the range of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000, upward TP adjustments plus annual interest at 18.5%, and may escalate to criminal tax evasion treatment for wilful concealment.
Quarterly Instalment Computation Line Estimated taxable profit for the fiscal year, applied to the 22.5% rate, divided into advance instalments, with declared actual quarter profit for any variance required by the ETA. Non-resident PEs must instal based on realistically projected Egypt-source profits; using zero instalments while generating Egyptian fulfilment revenue will attract delay surcharges. Each unpaid or late quarterly instalment attracts a monthly 2% surcharge of the amount due and can trigger administrative freezing of bank accounts until settlement.
Instalment Payment Reference Unique payment reference number, TIN, and fiscal year designation of each quarter paid; linkage of payments to the annual return. Foreign treasury teams must remit each instalment through an Egyptian-licensed bank using the PE tax account reference; cross-border payments without CBE authorisation are unlawful. Each unpaid or late quarterly instalment attracts a monthly 2% surcharge of the amount due and can trigger administrative freezing of bank accounts until settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 in aggregate controlled transactions within a tax period triggers mandatory Local File documentation; Master File and CbC notification requirements arise for multinational groups with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding EGP 15,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, Article 31 of Law 91 of 2005 contains a general anti-abuse rule empowering the ETA to disregard transactions lacking bona fide commercial substance. For treaty relief and CIT purposes, the EG LLC must demonstrate actual staff, warehouse capacity, inventory control systems, decision-making records, and risk assumption in Egypt; a shell or letterbox structure claiming DTA benefits will be attacked under the substance-over-form doctrine and treaty anti-abuse provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years, a fine of up to EGP 50,000, double collection of the evaded tax, monthly delay surcharges of 2%, administrative attachment of bank accounts and warehouse assets, and suspension of commercial registration and tax clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors, tax managers, and signatories of false returns may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties; travel bans, passport stays, asset freezes, and arrest warrants may be issued personally, and the corporate veil can be pierced where fraud is proven.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length result, annual interest on the underpayment at 18.5%, fines between EGP 10,000 and EGP 100,000, disallowance of intercompany deductions, and referral for criminal evasion charges for deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally engineered artificial intercompany pricing face personal civil claims for restitution; in severe cases, criminal liability attaches and travel bans or property attachment are invoked to enforce tax debt.
Illegal Profit Repatriation and Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the 10% dividend WHT plus 2% monthly delay penalties; fines under the Central Bank of Egypt and foreign exchange framework ranging from EGP 1,000,000 to EGP 10,000,000, imprisonment from 6 months to 5 years for unlicensed FX dealing, and freezing of corporate and beneficiary bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors with operational knowledge of unlawful FX remittance schemes face criminal prosecution, personal fines, asset seizure, travel bans, and possible extradition; board approval receipts will not shield individual culpability.
Money Laundering and Shell Company Misuse

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian AML Law 80 of 2002, imprisonment from 3 to 7 years, fines up to EGP 500,000 or twice the laundered amount, confiscation of warehouse inventory and bank balances, forfeiture of corporate assets, and blacklisting from export and re-export facilitation programs.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate predicate laundering acts are personally criminally liable; personal assets are subject to seizure, passports are lifted, and Interpol red notices may be issued for fugitive principals.
Digital Filing and E-Invoice Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Monthly 2% surcharge on the unpaid tax for each commenced month of default, administrative fines up to EGP 50,000, nullity of deduction claims for undocumented expenses, suspension of the legal representative's tax signature, and inclusion in the public ETA defaulters list.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and certified accountant who signed non-compliant digital filings are exposed to administrative fines in their personal capacity; directors who ignore ETA recovery orders may face property attachment and travel restrictions until the corporate debt is serviced.
