Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Egyptian Ministry of Finance; official filing portal: Egypt E-Invoice / E-Tax Portal at https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg and https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA after incorporation and issuance of the commercial registry certificate. Submission of incorporation documents, articles of association, board resolution appointing local tax representative, proof of registered address, and shareholder identification. The ETA issues a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT certificate. For bonded trucking, the LLC must be licensed as a customs carrier and obtain an authorized economic operator (AEO) status where applicable. Registration may be initiated online through the ETA portal followed by physical verification at the tax office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Egypt does not apply US-style check-the-box elections. A domestic LLC is automatically taxed as a separate entity under Law No. 91 of 2005 and Law No. 67 of 2016 (VAT). Available statutory elections include the election to join or leave a corporate tax group (fiscal unity) subject to ministerial conditions, the election to apply the simplified Cash-Basis regime for small taxpayers below the EGP 10 million turnover threshold, and available hybrid mismatch alignment elections permitted by local transfer-pricing documentation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and services; mandatory VAT registration is required for any person engaged in importing or exporting goods regardless of turnover, and for any person contracting with government entities where such supplies are subject to VAT

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to physical export of goods outside Egypt, directly exported or deemed exported, and to international transport services directly related to exported goods, when the service provider is a registered taxable person and the supply is evidenced by customs documentation. For customs bonded road feeder services that move airport cargo under customs seal, zero-rating is recognised if the transport is in connection with an exportation of goods and the operator retains the customs bonded movement document proving exit from Egypt. Services ancillary to transport within Egypt are in principle subject to VAT unless supplied directly to an exporter as part of an export transaction satisfying documentary tests.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/Data from NACIS containing export invoice number, HS codes, and net masse)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (often accompanied by Customs Bonded Movement Register / TIR Carnet where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Currency Bank Statement proving payment for exported goods has been received in Egypt

Egypt mandates phased continuous electronic invoicing and e-receipt for taxpayers above specified revenue thresholds. Invoices for B2B supplies must be issued, transmitted, and digitally stamped through the ETA e-invoicing platform in the required JSON/XML schema. Clearance is real-time using a unique QR code and digital signature; integration is required through an ERP-to-ETA API web service or approved tax authority portal. Customs bonded trucking operators must synchronise electronic tax invoices with Customs System (NACIS) and the e-invoicing system to evidence exempt/zero-rated export transactions and customs bonded movements.

Export zero-rating is strictly scrutinised by the ETA using cross-validation of customs export declarations, international transport documents, banking settlement data, and the e-invoicing system. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to clawback of the entire VAT input credit claimed, an additional tax of 14% on the under-declared supply, a fine of not less than 50% and not exceeding 150% of the due tax, late payment interest, criminal sanctions for forging customs or remittance documents, and possible AEO licence revocation plus blacklisting from all customs bonded operations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains IMF Article VIII convertibility for all bona fide current-account transactions, including cross-border service-export earnings of the GPS-sealed bonded truck fleet business; such inflows are freely creditable to Egyptian accounts and no prior CBE approval is required for current-account settlement. Capital-account convertibility is treaty-protected and legally available for foreign equity, shareholder loans, dividend repatriation, and capital disinvestment, but is subject to documentary gates: GAFI investment registration, an authorized-dealer banking channel, audited financial statements, and a final tax-clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority. Practical hurdles persist in foreign-currency priority queues during periodic liquidity tightening and strict bank-level verification of the underlying commercial documentation; however, the 2024 liberalization, the settlement of approximately USD 2.1 billion of overdue investor FX arrears, and the IMF Extended Fund Facility have materially removed prior convertibility rationing, and profit remittance is now processed at the unified market rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Post-March 2024 deregulation: the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% — the earlier 50% to 75% surrender circulars of 2022-2023 were repealed, and exporters may lawfully retain export earnings in foreign currency in Egyptian bank accounts or convert at the unified market rate without CBE-imposed conversion quotas. Statutory repatriation timeline: export and service-export proceeds must be routed through an authorized dealer bank in Egypt within a maximum of 5 months from the date of shipment or service performance; deferred-payment arrangements may extend to 8 months only with prior CBE approval evidenced in the bank file. For bonded road-feeder operations to international airports, all freight and logistics service income is treated as invisible export proceeds and must be accompanied by CMR/AWB documentation so that the receiving bank can verify the export nexus and record the inflow in CBE reporting systems.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary and foreign-exchange authority under Law No. 88 of 2003 and the Exchange Control Law No. 80 of 1947; supervises all authorized dealer banks, administers the EGP unified flexible exchange-rate regime adopted on March 6, 2024, and enforces Egypt's IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary inbound settlement or repatriation rail; Medium only as an ancillary disbursement channel. Legal constraints: a non-licensed offshore EMI cannot hold EGP, cannot act as an authorized dealer for Egyptian service-export receipts, and cannot satisfy the CBE obligation to repatriate invisible earnings through an Egyptian banking institution; using an offshore EMI as the collecting account for cross-border transport fees would constitute a circumvention of the Exchange Control Law No. 80/1947 and the CBE Payment Systems Law No. 18/2019 licensing regime. Permitted usage is limited to post-tax operational disbursements such as paying non-resident software or contracted services, provided the underlying invoices are booked in Egypt and the outbound payment is executed through an authorized bank with proper tax documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward shareholder equity injections must flow exclusively through the banking system: the foreign investor initiates a SWIFT MT103 transfer to the Egyptian authorized dealer bank's correspondent account, and the Egyptian bank credits the company's USD sub-account and files the CBE inward-remittance statistical return. The company must simultaneously register the inflow with GAFI (FDI project registration or share-capital amendment) and with the CBE investment-registration records, using the purpose designation 'Contribution to share capital of Egyptian LLC, GAFI registered', accompanied by the Commercial Registration number and Egyptian IBAN. The company must retain for the statutory five-year period the inward-remittance advice, the MT103 message, and the bank conversion certificate if the USD was converted to EGP. There is no physical fungible exchange; settlement is cleared via SWIFT USD/EUR/GBP correspondent clearing through the CBE, with value dating as instructed.Outbound settlement uses three coordinated rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 customer transfers for recurrent operating disbursements such as customs-bonded tolls, runway-side service fees, spare parts, and fuel, subject to bank-validated commercial invoices and CBE advance-cargo-information filing; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 for high-value capital procurement including tractors, air-ride dollies, and telematics hardware, funded on 100% cash cover for imported capital goods; and (iii) documentary collections on documents-against-payment for low-value consumables. All outbound flows must mirror the National Single Window / NAFEZA customs declarations and CMR documentation, and each foreign-currency outflow is settled through the CBE-authorized interbank FX market and reported on the bank's CBE returns at T+1.Deliverable USD/EGP forward contracts with authorized dealer banks, supported by underlying service-export invoices or import obligations and settled on actual flows;Spot-forward FX swap programmes with CBE-licensed banks to match airport-cargo monthly working-capital cycles;Currency-neutral trade-receivables financing including USD-denominated factoring and forfaiting of air-cargo invoices to avoid EGP convertibility friction;European-style FX options with CBE-authorized banks, with premium payable in EGP and optionality restricted to genuine economic exposure;Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts are not generally permissible onshore against EGP except with prior CBE approval for specific interbank applications; Egyptian corporates should treat NDFs as a restricted instrument requiring specialized bank and central bank authorization.

Box 3 Net profit before tax after deducting allowable expenses. For a non-resident taxpayer, this is the PE attributable profit under Art. 38 of Law 91/2005 after applying transfer-pricing adjustments. Late filing: statutory fixed fine of EGP 5,000 – EGP 20,000; additional monthly delay fee of 2% of tax due (maximum 30% of the total tax amount); late payment interest at central bank rate plus surcharge; intentional non-filing triggers criminal sanctions under Art. 104 and Art. 107 of Law 91/2005.
Box 4 Tax losses carried forward from prior years. A non-resident's PE may offset its own prior losses within the 5-year carryforward limit, but treaty losses may not be imported to Egypt. Late filing: statutory fixed fine of EGP 5,000 – EGP 20,000; additional monthly delay fee of 2% of tax due (maximum 30% of the total tax amount); late payment interest at central bank rate plus surcharge; intentional non-filing triggers criminal sanctions under Art. 104 and Art. 107 of Law 91/2005.
Box 5 Net taxable income after loss offset and statutory adjustments. Taxable base is subject to 22.5% corporate rate; treaty PE determination must be disclosed if the foreign company is claiming no PE. Late filing: statutory fixed fine of EGP 5,000 – EGP 20,000; additional monthly delay fee of 2% of tax due (maximum 30% of the total tax amount); late payment interest at central bank rate plus surcharge; intentional non-filing triggers criminal sanctions under Art. 104 and Art. 107 of Law 91/2005.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 1,000,000 annual aggregate controlled transactions for mandatory Local File; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated global revenue exceeding EGP 2,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in UAE or Bermuda. However, the Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 contains general anti-abuse provisions (Art. 98) and substance-over-form principles. The Egyptian LLC must maintain real management, offices, staff and physical truck operations in Egypt. Failing this, the ETA may recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, and deem a taxable Egyptian PE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 107 of Income Tax Law No. 91/2005: imprisonment for a term of 3 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 10,000 – EGP 50,000; plus assessed tax at 22.5%, late surcharges, delay interest, and seizure of trucks and logistics assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who participate in or knowingly permit fraudulent accounting are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax; criminal conviction imposes travel bans, passport seizure and potential prison term until the tax debt is settled.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under the general anti-avoidance rule (Art. 98) and transfer-pricing adjustment; the entire profit is deemed attributable to an Egyptian permanent establishment, taxed at 22.5%, with penalties up to 30% of adjusted tax and customs bond license cancellation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for reclassified taxes if they executed an artificial scheme known to be unlawful; veil piercing is granted by courts on proof of fraud, nominalism or abuse of legal personality; travel restrictions and administrative freezing orders may issue.
AML / Customs Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 15 to 25 years and a fine between EGP 50,000 and EGP 5,000,000, confiscation of freight vehicles and funds; customs penalties up to 10 times the duty value for false declarations; licence and customs bond status permanently revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face pretrial detention, freezing orders, travel bans, and confiscation of assets traced to laundering; directors cannot escape liability by resigning if they had knowledge or control during the violation.
