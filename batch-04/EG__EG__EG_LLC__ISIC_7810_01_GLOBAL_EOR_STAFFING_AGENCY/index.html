Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA | مصلحة الضرائب المصرية), a body of the Ministry of Finance; primary portal: www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipts gateway: invoicing.eta.gov.eg; governed by the Unified Tax Procedure Law No. 206/2020 and its Executive Regulations.

E-government filing portal

Within 30 days of commercial registration at the GAFI One-Stop Shop, the LLC files Uniform Tax Registration Form No. 1 with the competent ETA office (online via ETA portal). Required bundle: legalized Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, Commercial Registry extract, statutory books, board resolution appointing a tax representative/statutory auditor, notarized power of attorney, and IDs of managers/shareholders. A non-resident owner must appoint an Egyptian fiscal representative. The ETA issues a single unified 14-digit TIN covering CIT, VAT, payroll tax and stamp tax. VAT registration is made on the same file within 30 days after the relevant VAT threshold is crossed; the taxpayer is then onboarded for e-invoice/e-receipt integration and obtains an electronic signature certificate.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope / Tax_Exempt_Zone. The Egyptian LLC is a resident entity taxed at 22.5% CIT on worldwide income (Law 91/2005, art. 19 amended by Law 14/2020); non-resident and branch operations are taxed on Egyptian-source income only; no pass-through election. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope / Tax_Exempt_Zone. The Egyptian LLC is a resident entity taxed at 22.5% CIT on worldwide income (Law 91/2005, art. 19 amended by Law 14/2020); non-resident and branch operations are taxed on Egyptian-source income only; no pass-through election. A company licensed in a Free Zone or the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) under Law 83/2002 may qualify as Tax_Exempt on export profits (0% effective CIT) where export conditions and customs supervision are met.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Egyptian law; entity classification follows the Civil Code and CIT Law. Available statutory choices: (1) Free Zone/SCZone licence election for 0% tax on export sales (Law 83/2002); (2) Investment Law No. 72/2017 incentive election (e.g., 30% deduction from investment costs for eligible sectors); (3) election to treat a foreign branch as a separate permanent establishment with transfer-pricing documentation; (4) VAT treatment election for reimbursable payroll advances to be excluded from taxable consideration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (Executive Regulation Decree No. 66/2017); voluntary registration: EGP 50,000; non-residents supplying taxable services to Egyptian consumers are registrable irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) goods physically exported from Egypt; (ii) services rendered for non-residents and effectively consumed outside Egypt (Law 67/2016, art. 15). The EOR agency can zero-rate global contractor payroll, HR administration and compliance services where the client is a non-resident, the payroll execution and reporting occur from Egypt but the service is used and delivered to the client abroad, and the services are not attributable to a permanent establishment of the client in Egypt. Salary/social-insurance reimbursements are excluded from VAT when invoiced as recoverable advances with vouchers. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is refundable in cash (Export Facilitation Program; refund timeline 30-90 days for certified exporters).

  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier consignment note evidencing physical departure (goods)

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance of all B2B e-invoices and B2C e-receipts through the ETA interoperability gateway (REST APIs, XML/JSON payloads, HMAC signing, e-signature certificates). Invoices are submitted at issuance and must receive an ETA acceptance/cleared status before accounting effect; printed invoices carry a QR digital stamp. EOR platforms must integrate licensed payroll/ERP systems with the ETA API; non-accepted invoices result in disallowed input VAT, blocking of export refunds, and separate fines per invoice.

ETA may reassess and claw back zero-rated VAT within the general 5-year statute of limitation (Law 206/2020). Delayed tax carries 2% monthly surcharge; deliberate evasion triggers fines EGP 50,000 to EGP 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (VAT Law 67/2016, art. 72 as amended); forged or inflated export documentation attracts additional tax equal to 100% of the tax evaded, criminal referral, loss of export subsidy entitlements, and CBE penalties for non-repatriation of export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a fully liberalized, market-determined exchange rate regime for the Egyptian Pound as of March 2024 under the IMF Extended Fund Facility arrangement. Current account convertibility is broadly available across authorised dealers, including for service imports and remittance flows, subject to presentation of underlying invoices, tax compliance evidence, and standard bank documentation. Capital account transactions are convertible but subject to documentary verification, CBE administrative surveillance, and periodic liquidity conditions. Profit remittances from an Egyptian LLC require audited financial statements, a validated foreign exchange return form submitted through the dealer bank, dividend General Assembly resolutions, and an Egyptian Tax Authority no-objection/clearance certificate. Practical remittance hurdles include temporary interbank EGP liquidity tightness, hard-currency prioritization for essential imports, and heightened scrutiny of round-tripping where foreign shareholder loans are repaid within 30 days of inbound funding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of March 2024, the CBE abolished all mandatory export proceeds surrender ratios and statutory conversion timelines. Under the pre-liberalization regime, 50-75% of export proceeds had to be surrendered within 90 days of collection; that regime no longer applies to invoiced EOR staffing service revenues. The Egyptian LLC is permitted to retain 100% of service export proceeds in a foreign-currency operating account with a licensed dealer bank or convert at prevailing onshore interbank rates. Nonetheless, the dealer bank must be in receipt of the underlying EOR service contract, delivered invoice, and a funds-received confirmation within 10 business days of settlement, and the company must maintain full CBE-form reporting to substantiate the source of funds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under the Central Banking and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, the official custodial authority for the Egyptian Pound, the sole licensing authority for authorized dealers, and the primary regulator of foreign exchange operations through the Foreign Exchange Trading System and the Banking Supervision Sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability is LOW for an offshore EMI acting as the principal treasury bank. Egypt does not recognize foreign electronic money institution licenses, and the CBE requires all EGP-denominated treasury activity and related foreign exchange reporting to be transacted with a domestic CBE-licensed authorized dealer bank. Wise is usable as a purely supplemental belt for cross-border contractor payout corridors and for collecting small remittance balances from non-Egyptian clients, but it cannot hold the operational EGP corporate account, cannot perform CBE-mandated FX filings, and must be wired-through the licensed commercial bank account of the Egyptian LLC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign direct investment and intercompany working capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 through a CBE-licensed dealer bank to the LLC's capital or operational account. The instruction fields must reflect the appropriate Egyptian FX purpose code (foreign direct investment equity code for paid-in capital, intercompany loan or service-advance code for operational funding), together with the transferring entity's name, country of origin, and a narration referencing the investment registration number or underlying service agreement. The receiving bank will file the prescribed CBE form and generate an inward remittance certificate that must be kept for GAFI, CBE, and corporate tax records. The LLC must then register the inflow with GAFI under Investment Law 72 of 2017, and for any conversion into Egyptian Pounds it must deal with the onshore open market through the dealer bank. Rebate or excess payments from offshore clients must be classified with the correct service-trade purpose code and supported by the corresponding EOR invoices.Local payroll and contractor disbursements in Egypt are executed via the CBE-operated Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system, the automated clearing house (ACH) rail, or Meeza card scheme where contracting parties hold Meeza prepaid/in-payment credentials; each disbursement requires a pre-uploaded payroll file and a valid direct debit mandate. Foreign-currency payments for subcontractors, healthcare admin, or regional vendor settlements follow SWIFT MT103 rules with full COR/COR beneficiary-account formatting, based on reimbursement or disbursal invoices, and with the supporting FX application (outward report) required by CBE circulars. For larger or extended payment terms to non-Egyptian suppliers, a Commercial Letter of Credit (CL/C) pursuant to UCP 600 or a Standby Letter of Credit may be issued by the Egyptian dealer bank as primary settlement rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File requirement arises when the aggregate value of related-party transactions exceeds EGP 10 million in a tax period; a Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 150 million or when the annual value of related-party transactions with Egyptian entities exceeds EGP 30 million. Simplified documentation (summary TP form) applies for transactions between EGP 5 million and EGP 10 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code similar to UAE/Bahrain, but the Egyptian Tax Authority applies the OECD BEPS substance-over-form principle, the anti-abuse clause in Article 56 of Law 91/2005, and GAAR measures to deny treaty benefits to shell or letter-box companies. A PE of a foreign EOR platform must demonstrate actual people, physical premises, payroll records, bank accounts, and decision-making capacity in Egypt; free-zone companies must meet minimum substance thresholds in the relevant investment zones under Investment Law 72/2017 and may be subject to annual 'economic presence' checks. Failure of substance tests leads to reclassification of profit as fully Egyptian-source and disqualification from DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Fines equal to 200% of the underpaid tax plus double the tax due; criminal imprisonment for up to 5 years under Article 391 of Law 91/2005 and the Criminal Code; the tax authority may issue an administrative seizure of bank accounts and assets.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Egyptian LLC are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's unpaid tax debts where the under-declaration was intentional or caused by gross negligence; courts may issue travel bans and asset freezes; personal imprisonment of officers (up to 3 years) is available if criminal intent is proven.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance in the Egyptian PE

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entire EOR fee as Egyptian-source profit subject to 22.5% CIT plus 10–20% WHT; additional 50% ex officio assessment under Article 49 of Law 91/2005; denial of treaty benefits and double exclusion or inclusion of profits with penal interest at 2% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the additional tax and penalties if they sign or cause to be signed declarations misrepresenting the PE's substance; administrative enforcement includes entry bans for pure-letter-box directors found to have exercised control from Egypt.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of excess interest and service fees; TP adjustment back-calculated using Egyptian comparables; penalty of 10–40% of the adjusted tax difference; exposure to APAs and mutual agreement relief is revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability for the resulting tax shortfall if they knowingly approved the transfer-pricing arrangements; repeated violations trigger disqualification from board positions in Egyptian companies and police travel bans during tax ex officio proceedings.
AML / Suspicious Cross-Border Payments Evading Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian Central Bank AML rules and Criminal Procedure Code, financial transactions in excess of EGP 100,000 per movement are reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU); deliberate evasion is punishable with imprisonment (3–7 years) and fines up to EGP 500,000; the underlying corporate transaction is nullified and the withheld tax becomes immediately due.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are individually liable for failing to prevent money laundering; the Egyptian Central Bank may revoke the EOR entity's payment/remittance license, and directors face seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and extradition under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Late Filing and Non-Payment of Interim Corporate Tax

Statutory sanction: Monthly 1% penalty on tax due (capped at 50%) plus 2% monthly delinquency surcharge; interest accruing at the Central Bank discount rate + 2%; remedial seizure of accounts and property through ETA collection departments after 60 days of non-payment.

Director personal liability: If the company remains in default and the directors had personal knowledge of the accrued tax liability, they may become personally liable for the unpaid amount under the 'piercing the veil' doctrine in Article 25 of Law 91/2005, and the competent court may issue a travel suspension.
