Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — unified taxpayer portal eta.gov.eg; e-filing via e-ta.egypt.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipts via einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign LLC must (1) if conducting activity through an Egyptian establishment, complete commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and obtain a commercial register; otherwise appoint an Egyptian tax representative. (2) Submit the unified Tax Registration Form (Form No. 1) to the competent ETA Taxpayer Service Center together with certified articles of association, legalized power of attorney, passport/ID copies, proof of address and, where applicable, municipal license. (3) ETA issues a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and, where annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000, a VAT registration certificate. (4) Complete e-invoicing/e-receipts system onboarding and obtain ETA digital signature certificates and API credentials.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: Resident LLC taxed on worldwide income; non-resident entities taxed only on Egypt-source income. Pass-through not eligible. Tax-Exempt_Zone treatment only if a special investment incentive agreement under dedicated economic zone legislation is obtained.

Available election forms

None. Egypt does not maintain a check-the-box regime, hybrid mismatch election, or entity classification election. LLCs are mandatorily corporate taxpayers. Foreign investors may alternatively elect to operate through a branch, representative office, or contractual joint venture; a joint venture without separate juridical personality is taxed at the participant level. Transfer-pricing advance pricing agreements and statutory investment incentive elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration at EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover; voluntary registration available from EGP 50,000 annual taxable turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside Egypt within the applicable customs time limits are zero-rated. Services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use and consumption wholly outside Egypt — including virtual performance hosting, digital ticketing, and metaverse streaming services — are zero-rated where the recipient is not registered for Egyptian VAT, the economic benefit is realized abroad, and the foreign-currency consideration is received into an Egyptian licensed bank account. Conditions include a valid export contract, supporting invoices, customs/exiting evidence for goods, and bank documentation proving actual receipt of export proceeds in hard currency.

  • Egyptian Export Customs Declaration (Nafeza/CargoX e-customs statement)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from an Egyptian licensed bank confirming foreign-currency receipt
  • Export contract, commercial invoice, and non-resident consumer or offshore-use evidence for digitally delivered services

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing with ETA fiscal clearance before invoice delivery to the customer; B2C e-receipts also mandatory. Taxpayers must integrate certified billing systems with the ETA e-invoicing portal via REST/SOAP APIs using ETA-defined XML schemas; each invoice is validated in real time, digitally signed, and stamped with an ETA-generated QR code and unique tracking code. Non-compliant invoices are legally invalid for input VAT deduction.

ETA cross-checks zero-rating claims against Central Bank of Egypt foreign-exchange receipt records, Customs exit data, and e-invoicing clearance files. Failure to substantiate exports within the statutory timeframe or fraudulent over-claiming triggers reversal of input VAT recovered, retrospective clawback, reassessment, penalty equal to the evaded VAT, administrative fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 1,000,000, customs exposure under Customs Law No. 207 of 2020, and imprisonment of up to five years pursuant to Articles 71–76 of the VAT Law No. 67 of 2016.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-flexible exchange rate regime; the EGP is not fully convertible in the strict sense because all FX operations must be executed through CBE-licensed banks at the interbank market rate. Current account operations including service fees, royalties, distribution fees, and profit remittances are generally permitted after submission of the underlying transaction documents, audited financial statements, and a tax clearance certificate. Capital account operations such as shareholder loans, equity injections, and repatriation of capital are permitted but subject to stricter bank-level due diligence, GAFI registration where applicable, and documentary proof of the original source of the inward remittance. Profit remittance hurdles are manageable but require verified transferable profit from audited accounts, full tax settlements and filed corporate tax returns, and presentation of the CBE-approved remittance form. For virtual concert and metaverse operations, the recurring cross-border revenue streams from ticket tokenisation and platform licensing are treated as current-account service exports, and therefore enjoy convertibility for trade-related payments, provided contracts, invoices, and tax receipts are properly evidenced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under prevailing CBE regulations and Law 194/2020, all export proceeds derived by an Egyptian juridical person from virtual performance services and digital event ticket sales must be repatriated to Egypt and credited to a CBE-licensed bank account in the name of the exporting Egyptian LLC. The statutory expectation is that proceeds are received within 180 days from the date of invoice or from the completion of the virtual concert, unless the underlying commercial contract establishes a longer credit term or the CBE grants a case-specific extension. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% in the sense that the LLC may retain foreign currency in an approved foreign-currency account rather than force-converting to EGP; however, any EGP settlement must be executed at the prevailing day interbank market rate set by the CBE. Full 100% of the export value must be documented by the bank through the trader's currency account, with all receipts traced via wire confirmations, commercial invoices, and the Egyptian tax authority's digital receipting environment.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 on the Regulation of the Monetary and Banking System, the Egyptian Foreign Exchange Law, and the Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002. The CBE supervises all foreign exchange operations, licensing of banks and payment service providers, and the clearing and settlement of cross-border transfers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 19,000,000

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the Egyptian Tax Authority applies substance-over-form principles under Article 84 of the Income Tax Law and can disregard artificial or sham arrangements lacking real economic activity. A virtual concert platform generating digital ticket revenue through an Egyptian LLC must demonstrate local staff, contract negotiation, IP risk-bearing, and substantive payment functions to avoid PE treatment or recharacterization of conduit royalty structures.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False Revenue Reporting

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for a term from 2 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000, plus criminal restitution of tax evaded; assessment at 40% on undeclared income may be imposed, and e-invoicing non-compliance carries a EGP 50,000 administrative penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in falsifying records are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; the ETA may impose travel bans and freeze personal bank accounts; a criminal conviction leads to disqualification from managing any joint-stock company and imprisonment of the responsible officer.
Shell Company / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions under Article 61 (transfer pricing) and Article 84 (tax avoidance); additional CIT at 22.5% plus late payment surcharge of 2% per month; possible penalty equal to 30% of the adjusted gross income.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell LLC may be held personally accountable for abusive tax avoidance if they sign false transfer pricing documentation; civil damages may be recovered from their assets, and a travel ban is commonly issued during tax evasion investigation.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Violations

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EGP 5,000,000 under Law No. 80/2002 (as amended by Law No. 22/2022); imprisonment of 3 to 7 years for money laundering; assets are subject to freezing and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners bear personal criminal liability where the entity is used as a laundering vehicle; the court may impose travel bans, asset freezing, and personal seizure of director assets up to the aggregated laundered value, and imprisonment may not be suspended for repeat offenders.
