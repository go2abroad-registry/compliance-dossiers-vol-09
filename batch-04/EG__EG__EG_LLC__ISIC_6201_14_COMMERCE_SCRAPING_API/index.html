Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Cairo — official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance platform: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must obtain a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and an official Tax Card from the ETA within 30 days of commercial registration or of the commencement of taxable activity. The LLC must appoint a tax representative registered with the Egyptian Accountants Syndicate under a notarized power of attorney, and file through the ETA's integrated e-filing portal. Required documentation: incorporation certificate from the Commercial Registry (under Companies Law No. 4 of 2018 and, where applicable, GAFI registration), articles of association evidencing unlimited foreign ownership, activity license or a written confirmation that no license is required for the web-scraping/proxy-API activity, address and lease proof, opening balance sheet, and identity documents of directors. VAT registration, which is separate from the income-tax file, is mandatory once annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000, with the VAT certificate accessible in the same portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 and is taxed on worldwide net income at the 22.5% corporate headline rate. There is no pass-through regime, no check-the-box treatment, and no territorial remittance exemption for Egyptian-source derived foreign revenue; losses may be carried forward for five years with no carryback.

Available election forms

None comparable to US check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections: entity classification is statutory and non-elective. Available elections are administrative and tax-filing options only: (i) voluntary VAT registration before reaching the EGP 500,000 threshold; (ii) adoption of the simplified/small-taxpayer presumptive income regime under the Tax Procedures Law No. 206 of 2020 where the statutory turnover cap is met; and (iii) formal submission of the Article 12 VAT zero-rating declaration for each export service contract, which is accepted by the ETA on a contract-by-contract basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory VAT registration within 30 days of exceeding the threshold; voluntary registration permitted below it; the threshold is measured on Egyptian-supply turnover, not export turnover).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Egyptian LLC may zero-rate exported services under Article 12 of the VAT Law and its Executive Regulations where all cumulative conditions are satisfied: (i) the recipient is a non-resident with no Egyptian permanent establishment; (ii) the scraping/API-data services are wholly consumed outside Egypt and are not linked to Egyptian taxable supply; (iii) a written export service contract defines the offshore recipient and jurisdiction of use; and (iv) payment is received in freely convertible foreign currency through an Egyptian licensed bank. The ETA requires either the ITIDA software/IT export certificate or an equivalent service-dispatch dossier (delivery logs, IP evidence, cloud data-center location) to confirm genuine offshore consumption. Note that Stripe-based settlement must ultimately be credited (net of PSP fees) to the LLC's Egyptian bank account and evidenced by a bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate; direct offshore Stripe holding or USDC/crypto settlement is not a recognized repatriation corridor and will defeat the zero-rating claim.

  • Export e-invoice issued through the ETA EIS carrying the 'EX' zero-rating code and QR smart stamp, cross-referenced to the export contract
  • Signed export service contract and service-level agreement identifying the non-resident customer and defining offshore consumption of the scraping-API gateway
  • NAFEZA Customs Export Declaration — required only where physical media, servers, or co-located hardware accompany the service supply; for pure digital service exports, replaced by the ITIDA software/IT export certificate
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for tangible payloads) or, for digital services, hosting-provider delivery logs and IP-address evidence demonstrating consumption outside Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by an Egyptian authorized bank evidencing foreign-currency receipts in USD/EUR and crediting to the LLC's Egyptian account
  • Stripe payout/settlement reports reconciled to the IRC, board resolution approving the export pricing, and the LLC's VAT export registration record in the ETA exporter database

The ETA Electronic Invoicing System (EIS) is mandatory for VAT registrants: every B2B invoice must be digitally signed using the taxpayer's ETA-issued certificate, submitted for real-time validation through the EIS REST/JSON API, and retained with the ETA smart-code/QR digital stamp. Non-conforming XML/JSON is rejected before issuance, meaning the invoice number is legally ineffective for VAT deduction. Export invoices must carry the 'EX' type code and be correlated with the NAFEZA customs declaration or, for pure software/API services, with the ITIDA IT-export certificate; B2C sales are progressively covered by the mandatory e-receipt system.

The ETA may reassess zero-rated supplies for five years (ten years in cases of fraud or deliberate concealment). Where the offshore-consumption or foreign-currency repatriation trail cannot be substantiated, the export is reclassified as a domestic taxable supply at 14% VAT, the refund is clawed back, and the tax is collected with the statutory late-payment addition. Fraudulent zero-rating claims carry severe legal consequences: criminal prosecution under the VAT Law with imprisonment (up to five years) and monetary fines scaled to the evaded amount, mandatory re-issuance of corrected e-invoices, removal from the exporter VAT-refund fast-track, and reputational referral under Egypt's anti-money-laundering framework. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the CBE deadline also exposes the LLC to a deemed-distribution adjustment at 22.5% corporate income tax, and USDC/cryptocurrency settlement is not a valid method of satisfying Egyptian exchange-control and VAT-documentation requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has operated a CBE-supervised flexible exchange-rate regime since the 6 March 2024 currency unification, with the EGP price formed by interbank supply and demand. Current-account flows (software subscriptions, API access, and data services) are convertible for bona fide commercial purposes if the underlying contract, commercial invoice, and economic substance satisfy the receiving bank's documentary review. Capital-account flows, including dividend remittances and capital repatriation, are permitted only after inward FDI has been registered with the CBE/GAFI and the company demonstrates current tax clearance (Tax Law No. 91 of 2024, VAT Law No. 67 of 2016) and audited financial statements. Practical profit-remittance hurdles include heightened AML documentation, a statutory prohibition on cryptocurrency-denominated settlement inside Egypt, and bank-level FX-liquidity allocation, which can delay large on-demand USD transfers during periods of balance-of-payments stress.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Exchange Control Law No. 97 of 1976, export proceeds — including digital, data-service, and licensing revenue — must be repatriated into a licensed Egyptian bank account in the original invoice currency, within the contractual credit period generally capped at 180 days. There is currently no statutory percentage surrender requirement: the temporary mandatory conversion ratios (in the range of 80–100% of proceeds) applied during the 2022–2023 FX-scarcity episodes were withdrawn by the CBE's 6 March 2024 flexible-exchange-rate reform. Exporters may retain the full balance in foreign-currency current accounts for permitted operational, hedging, or liquidation purposes, subject to CBE transaction-code filings and documentary retention by the account-holding bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — primary regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and the Foreign Exchange (Exchange Control) Law No. 97 of 1976, as amended.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 for controlled transactions (Local File requirement); Master File required when Egyptian entity participates in a multinational group whose consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 500,000,000; Country-by-Country Report required where consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EGP 8,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not administer a GCC-style ESR regime. Instead, economic substance is assessed under Law 91/2005 anti-avoidance provisions, specifically Article 103 on sham transactions, and under the Tax Procedure Law 206/2020. A recipient of treaty benefits must satisfy the beneficial-ownership and active-business-substance tests. For free-zone licensed LLCs, substantive local activity (employees, premises, decision-making, and revenue generation) must be observable; otherwise the tax authority may reclassify free-zone income as taxable head-office income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Assessment of evaded tax plus a fine equal to 100% of the evaded amount; penalty under Tax Procedure Law 206/2020 can reach EGP 2,000,000 for corporate entities; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of controlling officers for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who sign false returns or direct the concealment can be made jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt. Travel bans and asset freezing orders are routinely issued under the law when evasion exceeds substantial thresholds; repeat offenders face imprisonment without the option of a fine.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: A reclassification of the entity as a non-taxable sham or transparent conduit; recapture of the 0% free-zone rate with CIT at 22.5% on all accumulated profits plus late payment surcharge of 2% per month; penalties up to EGP 500,000 for submission of false substance declarations.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Law 91/2005 and Companies Law No. 4/2018 when the entity is used to evade tax or manipulate creditor positions. Each director who knowingly facilitated the sham may be ordered to pay evaded tax personally, and the commercial registrar may disqualify them from board positions in Egypt for up to three years.
Fictitious Intercompany Loans and Interest Stripping

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the entire interest deduction; back-taxes at 22.5%; 2% monthly late-payment surcharge; additional penalty equal to 10% of the disallowed interest under TP rules; potential WHT assessment at 20% on the interest paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved artificially high interest rates can be held aggregation liable for the resulting tax under the anti-abuse regime; if the structure is deemed a disguised distribution, a 10% dividend WHT is applied to the shareholder-beneficiary, and a director may receive a fine up to double the amount paid by the company.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Integration

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to EGP 5,000,000 for corporate entities and seizure of compromised assets; supervisory penalties from the Financial Regulatory Authority; prohibited access to the Egyptian banking system for the LLC. Suspicious transaction filings not made to the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Unit (EMC) trigger separate administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to establish a beneficial-ownership registry or who knowingly process proceeds of unlicensed scraping activity through crypto rails may be prosecuted personally, with imprisonment from one to seven years and confiscation of personal assets obtained from the offence. Travel bans are standard during the investigation phase.
