Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Companies Law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Egyptian LLC; the founders freely determine the capital in the Articles of Association. The entire subscribed capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account maintained in the name of the future LLC at a licensed Egyptian bank and must remain blocked until registration; the deposit certificate is a mandatory attachment to the GAFI filing, and the block is released on issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: The General Directorate of Civil Defense of the Ministry of Interior must issue a final Fire Safety Certificate after on-site inspection, verifying functional fire alarms, extinguishers, emergency exit signage, evacuation drills, and sprinkler zoning. The district engineering administration must certify structural load capacity for the racking and mezzanine systems, an insurance load inspection certificate included. The Ministry of Manpower Occupational Safety and Health Department must issue a workplace clearance confirming completion of a risk assessment, first-aid stations, PPE audit, and a certified fire safety officer on staff as required for facilities employing more than 20 workers.

Paid-in capital rule: The Companies Law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Egyptian LLC; the founders freely determine the capital in the Articles of Association. The entire subscribed capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account maintained in the name of the future LLC at a licensed Egyptian bank and must remain blocked until registration; the deposit certificate is a mandatory attachment to the GAFI filing, and the block is released on issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - National portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipt platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; integrated customs single window: NAFEZA (CargoX platform).

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor first registers the Egyptian LLC in the Commercial Register (within 30 days of commencement) and obtains an activity license from the competent governorate. The entity then files unified tax registration with the ETA using Form No. 1 at the tax office having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office, attaching Arabic-translated and notarised constitutional documents, foreign registry extract, board resolution, lease contract, bank account confirmation and financial statements. The ETA verifies the file and issues the unified Tax Card containing the TIN for income tax, payroll withholding, VAT and stamp tax. Foreign companies that only render remote services without a permanent establishment are not eligible for a TIN and settle VAT under the recipient's reverse charge mechanism.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A resident Egyptian LLC is taxable on worldwide net profits at the standard corporate income tax rate of 22.5% (Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005). No pass-through or look-through treatment is available to shareholders; dividends distributed to foreign shareholders are subject to the 10% dividend withholding tax unless reduced by treaty.

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification or hybrid mismatch election exists in Egypt. Limited elections and rulings available include: (i) treaty permanent establishment attribution for foreign companies operating through an Egyptian branch; (ii) deemed-profit taxation for international transport; (iii) voluntary VAT registration below the compulsory threshold; (iv) advance pricing agreements (APAs) for intra-group transactions; (v) tax rate stabilization commitments included in investment approvals under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; and (vi) an irrevocable election between an Egyptian subsidiary or branch form at the time of entry.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual turnover of taxable goods and services; mandatory VAT registration within 30 days of crossing the threshold (VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, Art. 27). Foreign suppliers providing cross-border digital or logistics services are generally taxed under reverse charge unless the services are zero-rated as exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods are zero-rated when they physically leave Egyptian customs territory and are sold to a non-resident purchaser, supported by a completed customs export declaration and shipping documents. Services are zero-rated when rendered to a non-resident recipient located outside Egypt and used wholly outside Egypt; this expressly covers logistics services connected to exports and transit, including parcel consolidation, sorting, warehousing, handling, packing and direct-injection linehaul for e-commerce parcels. The zero rate is denied if the exporter fails to prove the export within the statutory time limit or if foreign currency proceeds are not repatriated to Egypt. Input VAT recovery on zero-rated export costs is allowed only after the export documentation package is substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic NAFEZA/CargoX export entry for each consolidated parcel shipment)
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill evidencing physical exit of goods from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation evidencing hard-currency repatriation of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated VAT invoice carrying the e-invoice UUID and QR code matching the customs declaration
  • Packing list, parcel manifests, purchase orders and foreign currency account statements

Mandatory e-invoicing (B2B) and e-receipts (B2C) phased in by ETA decrees from 2021 to 2024 for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted in real time to the ETA e-invoicing platform (einvoicing.eta.gov.eg) via certified M2M API integration (JSON/XML schema) or the ETA web portal. Every accepted invoice receives an ETA-issued UUID, unique invoice code and QR code digital stamp; invoices that fail fiscal clearance are legally treated as not issued for VAT purposes. The e-invoicing system is interoperable with the NAFEZA Customs single window for export declarations.

The ETA conducts cross-matching audits within the five-year statutory limitation window, reconciling e-invoice zero-rated outputs, VAT returns, Customs export entries in NAFEZA and Central Bank of Egypt banking data. Failure to produce the full documentation bundle, missing FX repatriation evidence, or late receipt of proceeds triggers retrospective reversal of the zero rate and assessment of 14% VAT on the exporting entity with delay fines and interest. Deliberate submission of forged declarations or counterfeit proof of export constitutes tax evasion under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 and Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, exposing the taxpayer to criminal prosecution, imprisonment, monetary fines, immediate refund clawback with accrued penalties, suspension of the Tax Card, removal from the exporter registry and blacklisting from e-invoicing clearance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP is a managed float with substantive current-account convertibility for trade, services, profit repatriation, and dividend remittances, provided the underlying economic purpose is evidenced to a CBE-licensed authorized dealer bank. The capital account is subject to CBE authorization; equity capital inflows into an Egyptian LLC are freely transferable through licensed banks when accompanied by the share subscription agreement, registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), and the inward transfer certificate. Profit and dividend repatriation by the LLC requires audited financial statements, a dividend distribution resolution of the shareholders, tax clearance certificates confirming CIT and stamp duty settlement, and conversion at the prevailing interbank market rate. Practical hurdle: recurring USD/EGP liquidity tightness may delay hard-currency payouts, and treasury operations must avoid parallel-market channels entirely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For cross-border logistics, parcel consolidation, and linehaul services invoiced in foreign currency, export/service proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt through a CBE-licensed bank within 180 days from the date of the final invoice or service completion. The standing baseline under the current flexible exchange-rate regime does not impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio; proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account and converted at market rate when EGP disbursements arise. However, CBE has from time to time issued temporary administrative instructions mandating conversion of a percentage of export proceeds (e.g., 50–100% during balance-of-payments stress), so the treasury function must monitor CBE foreign-exchange circulars on a rolling basis. Non-repatriation within 180 days triggers bank reporting to CBE, cancellation of interbank buying-rate access, and administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), governed by Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law); foreign exchange authorization and licensing of dealing banks are further regulated by CBE Foreign Exchange Circulars and Law No. 636 of 1975, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs, including Airwallex, are rated 'Medium' feasibility: they are not CBE-licensed payment institutions in Egypt, and Law No. 194 of 2020 reserves money-transfer and payment services to CBE-licensed entities. They cannot lawfully collect local-card, InstaPay, or Meeza settlements on behalf of the Egyptian LLC without a local sponsor bank. Their legally viable role is confined to outbound vendor disbursements, multi-currency settlement with non-Egyptian counterparties, and netting of freight payments, while all inbound capital, export proceeds, and statutory corporate accounts must reside in an Egyptian licensed bank. The recommended architecture is a sponsor-bank-anchored overlay in which Airwallex functions as a treasury execution layer rather than a regulated local account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File is required when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions exceeds EGP 8 million, or as amended by ETA ministerial instructions; Master File and country-by-country reporting are separately triggered for large multinational groups above prescribed consolidated revenue thresholds. A TP disclosure form is required with the annual return for all related-party transactions above the de minimis amount.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the ETA and tax courts apply an economic-substance and beneficial-ownership concept when denying DTA relief, revoking free-zone exemptions, and recharacterizing sham structures. A pure shell Egyptian LLC run from abroad without local management, operational staff, contract control, or risk management is a fatal risk for tax exemptions and treaty rates; the entity must show genuine sorting, storage, dispatch, billing, and decision-making functions in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Income Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Collection of evaded tax, delay surcharge of 2% per month, and fine up to twice the evaded tax, plus other monetary penalties under Law No. 91/2005 as amended; criminal prosecution and imprisonment may be imposed where fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Where a director knowingly signs or directs a false return, conceals revenue, or misuses corporate accounts, the ETA can pierce the corporate veil, impose joint civil liability, issue a travel ban, freeze bank accounts, and pursue criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing / Controlled Transaction Abuse

Statutory sanction: ETA restates the transaction at market value, assesses additional taxable income at 22.5%, applies monthly delay surcharges, and imposes fixed penalties for non-submission of transfer pricing documentation; unsupported transfer pricing studies are rejected and adjusted.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute intercompany arrangements without business substance or knowingly sign misleading transfer pricing schedules may be held jointly and severally liable in sham or abusive-circumvention cases; precautionary asset seizures and travel restrictions may apply.
Shell Company / Abuse of Free-Zone Incentive or DTA

Statutory sanction: License cancellation by GAFI, retrospective clawback of corporate income tax and customs duties avoided, statutory surcharges, and denial of treaty benefits under the principal-purpose test and MLI anti-abuse rule.

Director personal liability: Managers and legal representatives who certified false economic-substance information are personally liable for clawed-back duties and fines; enforcement includes travel bans, asset seizure, and criminal sanctions for false affidavits.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Failure in Payment Systems

Statutory sanction: Violation of Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended entails criminal fines, confiscation of funds, licence suspension, and imprisonment for knowing involvement in money laundering; the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit may impose preventive freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers who fail to detect suspicious parcel-settlement activity may be prosecuted personally, fined, imprisoned, placed on travel-ban lists, and held civilly liable for laundered moneys.
