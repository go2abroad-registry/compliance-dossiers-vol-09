Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No pre-registration paid-up capital escrow is required for a service LLC whose capital is fixed at nominal value without cash contribution; if the founders elect an initial capital of EGP 50,000 under Article 82 of Law 159/1981, the cash portion must be deposited into a blocked bank escrow account in Egypt and released only after issuance of the commercial registration certificate. In-kind contributions must be fully assessed by an accredited valuer and legally transferred at registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and civil defense approval must be obtained from the General Administration of Civil Defense under Law No. 12 of 1983, covering automatic extinguishers, exit signage, and an approved evacuation plan for the office premises, together with a structural-load occupancy certificate from the municipal engineer. Occupational clearance is issued by the Ministry of Manpower after an establishment occupational safety and health file has been opened, a periodic work environment measurement plan for lighting, noise, and chemical exposure has been lodged, and the annual employee medical examination protocol has been contracted under Article 230 of the Labor Law No. 12 of 2003.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities and local branches must obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) by registering with the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) through the unified electronic registration portal. The procedure requires submission of certified constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, appointment of a resident tax representative or local branch manager, commercial register extract, and a valid power of attorney. ETA issues a unified tax ID card integrating income tax, VAT, and stamp tax registration. Non-resident entities deriving Egyptian-source income must also register for withholding tax purposes and, for VAT, must register if conducting taxable supplies in Egypt, subject to the statutory threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt's tax law does not provide a check-the-box regime. Corporations and limited liability companies are subject to corporate income tax at the entity level. Certain joint-stock and LLC structures may elect to be treated as investment funds or special-purpose vehicles only where explicitly permitted under the Investment Guarantees and Incentives Law and the Income Tax Law, subject to ETA approval. Hybrid mismatch rules per the OECD BEPS framework are recognized only through tax treaties and domestic anti-avoidance provisions; no elective mismatch regime applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and services; EGP 1,000,000 annual threshold for services-only suppliers under Executive Regulations of VAT Law No. 67 of 2016, as amended.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services from Egypt are generally zero-rated for VAT purposes. The eligible exporter must be a VAT-registered person, must maintain a valid export contract, and must prove that the goods left Egypt or the services were performed and consumed outside Egypt. Services physically rendered in Egypt to a non-resident for use outside Egypt, including inspection and quality-control testing services performed on goods destined for export, may qualify for zero rating if the foreign recipient bears the cost and the benefit is realized abroad, subject to ETA guidelines.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ACID / Export Manifest)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of foreign currency transfer showing settlement in the exporter's Egyptian bank account
  • Copy of the underlying export contract and purchase order
  • ETA-approved e-invoice with zero-rated VAT code
  • Certificate of Origin where required by destination customs

Egypt has implemented a mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt system administered by the ETA. Taxable persons registered under VAT must integrate their billing systems with the ETA's electronic invoice portal through secure APIs (either direct API integration or certified ERP providers). Invoices must be transmitted in real time for fiscal clearance and digital stamping; the ETA assigns a unique QR code and invoice UUID. Failure to integrate or submit required data fields results in rejection and administrative penalties.

Egyptian customs and tax authorities conduct rigorous post-clearance audits of export zero-rating claims. Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims trigger repayment of input VAT recovered, additional tax at the standard VAT rate with late-payment interest, and monetary penalties under the VAT Law and Income Tax Law. Criminal penalties apply for forged export documents or false declarations, including imprisonment and fines. The Minister of Finance and customs authorities may impose retrospective clawbacks for claims older than five years where fraud or gross negligence is proven, with no time limit for fraudulent acts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt exited the managed-peg exchange system on 6 March 2024 and now operates a CBE-supervised flexible exchange-rate regime; current-account convertibility (inspection/testing fee income, subcontractor remittance, trade settlements) is liberalized subject to underlying-documentation verification by the executing bank. Capital-account convertibility is restricted but workable: foreign equity injections, dividend remittances, and parent-loan repayments require GAFI registration under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, a tax-clearance certificate, and compliance evidence lodged with CBE-licensed banks; no CBE pre-approval is required post-flexible regime, but bank-led transaction monitoring (GOAML reporting) and ETA foreign-currency purchase checks apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE revised circulars effective 2024 abolish mandatory conversion and surrender of export receipts; exporters may lawfully retain 100% of proceeds in domiciled foreign-currency accounts. Statutory repatriation timeline: service-export proceeds (including pre-shipment inspection and quality-control fees) must be credited to a CBE-licensed bank within 180 days from invoice/service-completion date; a bank-approved extension of up to 90 additional days is available, and uncollected balances must be reported to CBE, subjecting the entity to Law 88/2003 penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary authority and banking supervisor exercising exclusive foreign exchange regulation under Law No. 88 of 2003 (Central Bank, Banking Sector, and Monetary System Law) as amended by Decree-Law No. 162 of 2022; all FX transactions must be routed through CBE-licensed banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM as an ancillary collection channel. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) may collect inspection fee receivables from non-resident clients, but they cannot hold EGP and cannot function as the Egyptian treasury account without violating the CBE licensing regime under Law 88/2003. All EGP conversion and inbound/outbound settlement must be finalized in the name of the EG LLC through a CBE-licensed bank; credits arriving via Wise are treated as ordinary MT103 payments and are fully subject to KYC checks, GOAML reporting, and the Egyptian sourcing rules for foreign-currency purchases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions per fiscal year to trigger mandatory Master File and Local File preparation; Country-by-Country Report required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of EGP 20,000,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; however, general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and substance-over-form doctrines under Income Tax Law 91/2005 require the LLC to maintain real premises, employees, contracts, independent decision-making, and commercial substance in Egypt. Beneficial ownership is mandatory for treaty relief; shell entities with no genuine economic activity are disregarded and recharacterized by the ETA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Income Tax Crimes)

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution before the Economic Courts; imprisonment of 1 to 5 years; fines up to EGP 50,000; confiscation of the evaded tax amounts; reinstatement of the full CIT plus monthly 2% delay surcharges and late-filing penalties.

Director personal liability: The ETA pierces the corporate veil: directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of evaded tax, subject to travel bans, asset freeze orders, passport seizure, and imprisonment for the responsible officers.
Shell Company / Sham Artificial Arrangements (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: ETA disregards the artificial arrangement under GAAR, recharacterizes related-party transactions, disallows artificial deductions, reassesses CIT at the 22.5% rate, imposes administrative penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax, and if applicable denies tax treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Civil veil-lifting under the Egyptian Companies Law and Civil Code; directors personally liable for unpaid corporate tax debts, mandated travel bans, asset sequestration, and debarment from serving as directors in Egypt.
AML and Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EGP 20,000,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years under AML Law 80/2002 as amended by Law 154/2019; confiscation of the funds linked to the offense; freezing of corporate and personal bank accounts; publication of enforcement measures.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed AML compliance officer bear personal criminal liability, face travel bans and passport suspension, asset forfeiture orders, and permanent disqualification from corporate management in Egypt.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure and Mis-Pricing

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing audit adjustment to the arm's-length profit level; additional CIT at 22.5%; dedicated TP penalty of 20% of the adjusted tax; monthly 2% delay surcharge on all late-paid amounts; official publication of the adjustment order.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified the related-party disclosure and financial statements as accurate are personally fined for intentional non-disclosure, held civilly liable for restitution of the TP penalties, and subjected to travel bans until the assessed liabilities are settled.
