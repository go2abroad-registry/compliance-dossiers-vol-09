Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or mail-forwarding address does not satisfy Egyptian statutory requirements. GAFI and the Unified National Commercial Registry require a physically identifiable registered office in Egypt evidenced by a lease contract, usufruct, or a licensed serviced-office agreement. A serviced-office arrangement with a licensed business center is accepted as physical presence, provided the LLC's name plaque is posted, records and management files are accessible at the address, and the center maintains reception and mail-handling capacity during statutory working hours. The company must also hold the minimum operational indicia of business substance appropriate to a financial-newswire content aggregator, including data processing infrastructure, a management presence, and bank documentation.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

For an LLC there is no statutory minimum capital under the Investment Law; capital is set at the founders' discretion in the Articles of Association and is expressed in EGP. Cash quotas must be deposited into an Egyptian licensed bank before incorporation; in-kind quotas must be substantiated by a certified valuation. Escrow and repatriation rules for foreign-currency capital follow the Investment Law's exchange controls.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; corporate and VAT registration portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing portal: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian LLC incorporated through GAFI is automatically enrolled with the Egyptian Tax Authority for corporate income tax, obtaining a tax card and 14-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN). A foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in Egypt must appoint an Egyptian tax representative, submit constitutional documents, board resolution, power of attorney, evidence of Egypt-source taxable activities, and apply for TIN and VAT registration through the ETA non-resident registration desk. VAT/e-invoicing registration additionally requires obtaining an ETA digital signature certificate and integration with the ETA e-invoicing platform.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An Egyptian LLC incorporated through GAFI is automatically enrolled with the Egyptian Tax Authority for corporate income tax, obtaining a tax card and 14-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN). A foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in Egypt must appoint an Egyptian tax representative, submit constitutional documents, board resolution, power of attorney, evidence of Egypt-source taxable activities, and apply for TIN and VAT registration through the ETA non-resident registration desk. VAT/e-invoicing registration additionally requires obtaining an ETA digital signature certificate and integration with the ETA e-invoicing platform.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box regime or hybrid mismatch election. Statutory elections relevant to an Egyptian LLC include: election to file a consolidated income tax return by wholly owned Egyptian corporate group members; election for voluntary VAT registration below the mandatory threshold; selection of acceptable transfer pricing methodology; and election to utilize deductible provisions/bad debt allowances subject to ETA conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable supplies is the mandatory VAT registration threshold; EGP 100,000 permits voluntary registration; importers and non-resident suppliers of taxable services/electronic services into Egypt are required to register without regard to turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods and services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Egypt are eligible for the 0% VAT rate. Eligibility requires the exporter to be VAT registered, to hold valid export documentation, to physically ship goods out of Egypt within statutory timelines, and to receive the foreign-currency export proceeds into an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the date of export. Input VAT attributable to export sales is creditable and refundable after ETA verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Foreign Currency Remittance / Bank Credit Confirmation
  • Original Export Contract or Sale Order
  • Certificate of Origin
  • ETA VAT Export Statement / Zero-Rated Supply Schedule

Egypt mandates real-time e-invoicing for B2B supplies and e-receipts for B2C supplies through the ETA E-Invoice System. Taxpayers must integrate their ERP/billing software using ETA-approved APIs and JSON schema, obtain an X.509 digital certificate, cryptographically sign every invoice, and transmit each invoice for real-time clearance. ETA assigns a unique UUID and QR code; invoices failing ETA clearance are legally invalid and disallow input VAT recovery.

ETA performs risk-based audits by matching customs export declarations, bank FX credits, bills of lading, and e-invoice records. Deficient or fraudulent documentation reverses the 0% rate, triggers VAT at 14% plus late-payment interest from the date of supply, and claws back input VAT credits. Administrative fines range from EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 per invoice, and additional penalties up to 100%–200% of the tax evaded may be imposed. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating/refund claims may result in fines up to EGP 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years. The statutory audit limitation period is generally 5 years from the end of the relevant tax period, permitting retrospective assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the CBE exchange-rate unification effective March 2024, current-account transactions, including cross-border service fees, software licensing, inter-affiliate royalties, and profit remittances supported by a valid tax clearance certificate, are freely convertible through authorized banks at the unified market rate. Capital-account transactions, comprising foreign equity injections, shareholder loans, and portfolio investments, remain subject to documentary substantiation, beneficial-ownership declarations, and CBE balance-of-payments purpose-code verification; external debt registration triggers additional CBE economic substance checks. No general restriction applies to the repatriation of legitimate newswire and data-feed subscription revenue provided the Egyptian LLC maintains current tax filings, obtains annual tax clearance, and routes all payments through licensed banks in accordance with CBE AML/CFT requirements under Law No. 80 of 2002.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current CBE framework, export and service-export proceeds, including data-feed subscription and syndicated-finance newswire license fees, must be repatriated to Egypt through a licensed bank within the contractually agreed collection period, generally not exceeding six months from invoice date. Proceeds may be credited in foreign currency to the exporter's FCY account or converted into EGP at the unified market rate. The former mandatory conversion ratio was abolished with the March 2024 liberalization, but the statutory repatriation obligation through the banking system remains in force; banks verify the Beneficial Ownership attestation and the underlying commercial contract for every inbound receipt.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 concerning the Central Bank and the Banking System, together with the CBE Foreign Exchange Committee and licensed dealing banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and offshore digital-only cross-border fintechs are not licensed by the CBE to hold EGP accounts, clear domestic payments, or execute FX conversions for Egyptian corporate treasury operations. Offshore EMI onboarding is feasible only for EUR/GBP-denominated collections outside Egypt; all onshore EGP treasury activity, FX conversion, and outbound wire execution must be performed through CBE-licensed banks. The CBE regulatory sandbox currently does not authorize EMI-style multi-currency treasury accounts for closed-loop B2B newswire settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed via SWIFT wire from the foreign shareholder's account to the Egyptian LLC's EGP or FCY settlement account at a CBE-licensed bank. The receiving bank books the credit as a direct-investment inflow and requires completion of the CBE Declaration of Purpose of Transfer using balance-of-payments code 601 (equity participation in an Egyptian company). Verification materials include the notarized Articles of Association showing subscribed capital, the current Commercial Registry extract, the valid Tax Card, and a Board resolution specifying the per-share subscription amount; banks also confirm that the LLC's registered capital is not exceeded without a corresponding capital-amendment filing.Outbound disbursements for the ISIC 6391 newswire and API model comprise subscription and licensing fees paid to offshore data providers, cloud-hosting fees, administrative costs, and dividend or shareholder-loan remittances. All are settled via SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers from the LLC's EGP or FCY accounts after FX conversion at the unified market rate. Open-account settlement against a commercial invoice is the standard clearing rail; a documentary remittance with invoice verification is applied where the payee requires guaranteed payment. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% (Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended; standard corporate rate for commercial and industrial companies)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed by Egyptian companies to non-resident shareholders (Law No. 91 of 2005, Article 56); reduced to 5% or exempt under DTA where conditions and minimum shareholding thresholds are met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where the taxpayer's gross revenue exceeds EGP 20 million and aggregate related-party transactions exceed EGP 5 million; Master File required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 500 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 applies a general substance-over-form principle and a business-purpose doctrine. A non-resident entity (including fintech newswire companies receiving Egypt-source subscription income) must demonstrate substantive decision-making, contracts, and operational activity in Egypt or in its treaty residence to avoid re-characterization of commercial income as a permanent establishment and to secure treaty benefits. Where revenue is routed through an intermediary shell company in a zero-tax jurisdiction, the ETA may pierce the structure and attribute profits to a deemed Egyptian establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Unpaid tax recovered immediately with a penalty equal to 100% to 200% of the evaded amount; criminal fine from EGP 5,000 to EGP 500,000 and imprisonment for up to five years; publication of the judgment in the commercial registry.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who sign false returns or knowingly participate in stamping of false invoices are personally assessed for unpaid tax and civil restitution; travel bans are issued for tax arrears exceeding EGP 1 million; criminal sentences may include imprisonment and disqualification from board service.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Refusal to operate as a non-resident entity; recharacterization as a permanent establishment; imposition of unpaid corporate income tax on gross turnover (or deemed margin), plus a 100% penalty; denial of treaty benefits; deregistration from the ETA commercial registry.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for taxes that cannot be recovered from the sham entity; administrative travel bans may be issued; in egregious placement of third-party funds, charges of money laundering under Egyptian AML Law No. 80 of 2002 may result in imprisonment from 3 to 15 years and asset confiscation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed fine of EGP 1,000 per day of delay up to a maximum of EGP 100,000; adjustment of the taxable income upward by 30% or more for the undocumented transaction; potential secondary adjustment for under-payment of dividends and deemed distribution taxes.

Director personal liability: Chief financial officers and managing directors are personally liable for the penalty if they deliberately omit documentation; criminal liability arises if the omission masks tax evasion; courts may impose imprisonment for habitual default and issue personal asset freezes.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EGP 1 million per AML violation under Egyptian Money Laundering Law; additional administrative fines and freezing orders from the Egyptian Economic Security Authority; where funds are linked to terrorism, imprisonment for up to 15 years and criminal confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Responsible compliance officers and board directors are personally prosecutable for inadequate sanctions screening; court-mandated travel bans and passport seizure apply to havaperatus of accused directors; civil restitution to the injured party may be enforced against personal assets.
