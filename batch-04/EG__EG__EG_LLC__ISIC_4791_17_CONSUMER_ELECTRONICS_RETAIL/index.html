Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA - Maslahat al-Dara'ib al-Misriyyah), Cairo; e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipt platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; business registration through GAFI One-Stop-Shop: https://www.gafi.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned EG LLC: (1) incorporate via GAFI/Commercial Registry and obtain the Commercial Registry Extract (sak al-tagheel) plus the published Official Gazette notice; (2) file the ETA tax registration kit within 60 days of commercial establishment: authenticated Articles of Association, Commercial Registry Extract, board resolution appointing the director(s), passports and valid residence proof for managers/shareholders, notarized power of attorney (if remote) and the Egyptian office lease title; (3) ETA issues the unified 9-digit TIN, which is concurrently the corporate income tax, VAT and withholding tax registration number, and issues the Tax Card (validity subject to ETA renewal policy); (4) onboard the mandatory E-Invoicing / E-Receipt API with the ETA digital-certificate enrolment; (5) complete the social-insurance registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate check-the-box or hybrid-entity elections: an LLC is irrevocably treated as a resident entity-level taxpayer under Law No. 91/2005. Available statutory mechanisms: (a) Free-zone/special-zone regime approved by GAFI under Law No. 72/2017 - exempts export-oriented free-zone profits from CIT and grants VAT customs treatment, but local-market retail sales remain fully taxable; (b) MSME presumptive taxation under Law No. 152/2020 - an eligible small LLC may elect the ETA-approved simplified fixed-margin tax basis in lieu of accounting profits; (c) investment incentive certificates for qualifying industrial/logistics projects in underdeveloped or economic zones - not generally available to pure online retailers; (d) accelerated depreciation under Art. 47 of Law 91/2005; (e) foreign hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized by the non-deductibility of payments to preferential-regime jurisdictions (Art. 66 of Law 91/2005) and by treaty substance-over-form tests. Consolidation/fiscal unity is not permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover for domestic businesses (Law 67/2016, Art. 29). No threshold applies to non-resident suppliers of digital/electronic-commerce services: VAT registration is compulsory from the first supply to an Egyptian consumer, regardless of turnover (Art. 30 and Exec. Reg. Art. 30).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (1) export of goods where the buyer is a non-resident, title passes to the buyer, and the goods physically leave the Egyptian customs territory within 90 days of the export invoice date (ETA extension possible); (2) services to non-resident persons where the benefit is used wholly abroad and the service is directly linked to exported goods (Exec. Reg. Art. 53); and (3) supplies to licensed free-zone projects confirmed by Egyptian Customs. Producers and merchants must mark the export invoice in the E-Invoicing system with export type code 'EX'; the claim fails if goods are diverted to the local market. A licensed online consumer-electronics exporter retains full input-VAT recovery attributable to the export sales, but pure domestic B2C sales remain taxable at 14%.

  • Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) / electronic Export Declaration through ASYCUDA and the Advance Cargo Information (ACI) system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the export shipment and destination outside Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) / Foreign Exchange Transfer Certificate issued by an Egyptian authorized bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • QR-validated zero-rated export invoice issued through the ETA E-Invoicing system (export type code 'EX')
  • Signed export contract or foreign purchase order and corresponding payment evidence
  • Customs packing list and HS/ECC classification certificate for consumer electronics

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance: all registered taxpayers must integrate through the ETA E-Invoicing API (JSON/XML schema with certified digital signatures), transmit invoices and B2C e-receipts for central validation prior to issuance, and print the ETA-generated UUID and QR stamp on the final fiscal document. Rollout is phased by turnover tiers; merchants selling through Stripe/PayPal must still generate and transmit a QR-coded Arabic e-receipt for each Egyptian consumer purchase in real time. Non-compliance results in suspension of the Tax Card, blocking of customs ACI privileges, and administrative fines of EGP 20,000-50,000 per violating transaction.

The ETA, the Egyptian Customs Authority and the CBE exchange ACI/ASYCUDA, e-invoice and banking data to corroborate each export zero-rating claim, including physical exit, buyer residency and repatriation of hard currency within the 180-day CBE deadline. A false export declaration is VAT evasion under the penalty provisions of Law No. 67/2016: imprisonment up to five years and a fine between EGP 100,000 and EGP 1,000,000 (or not less than twice the tax evaded), together with mandatory restitution of the refund plus CBE credit-rate interest, and delay penalties of 2% per month (capped at 100%). The ETA may open and retroactively claw back refunds within the five-year assessment window; the exporter carries the full burden of proof. Repeated violations trigger cancellation of VAT registration, revocation of the e-invoice 'EX' privilege, customs export blacklisting and referral to public prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the March 2024 flotation, the EGP is a market-determined, broadly convertible currency for current-account flows, including e-commerce receivables, import payments, commissions, royalties, and ordinary profit remittances, all handled through CBE-licensed banks without quotas or surrender certificates. Capital-account flows (equity injection, shareholder loans) require bank-level CDD, GAFI/BoP coding, and underlying documentation, but no separate CBE approval is required for an LLC's share-capital deposit. The practical hurdles are documentary: (i) no outward profit remittance is executed without the ETA income-tax clearance and tax card; (ii) import settlements require the Commercial Register, tax card, and GOEIC importer-eligibility file; (iii) any non-bank PSP (Stripe/PayPal) collection must be swept into the licensed bank to be recognized as statutory repatriation; (iv) financial statements must follow EAS/IFRS and reflect the BoP classification of all cross-border transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters, including cross-border e-commerce sellers, must repatriate the full invoice value through an authorized bank within four months of the shipping date per successive CBE foreign-exchange circulars; the proceeds must be credited to a CBE-bank account, and any receivable held in a foreign PSP wallet or offshore bank materially violates this repatriation duty. The compulsory EGP-conversion share historically applied to export proceeds (about 50%) was suspended under the March 2024 flexible-exchange-rate instruction, so the EG_LLC may retain export receipts in USD/EUR and convert at the interbank rate when funding import payables. For a predominantly import-based retail legal entity, the repatriation obligation is triggered only when the company resells cross-border; for import purchases the bank applies the reverse documentary discipline, verifying settlement against pro forma invoices, customs declarations, and the importer's GOEIC status.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole regulator of banks, foreign-exchange operations, and payment systems under Law No. 194 of 2020; the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) administers FDI registration and capital-formation documentation for Egyptian LLCs; the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) issues the tax-clearance certificates that condition any outward foreign-currency remittance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury layer, AUXILIARY only for B2C collection. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: (1) execute the LLC's constitutive instrument and complete GAFI pre-registration; (2) open the share-capital escrow/subscription account at the chosen CBE-licensed bank presenting the Commercial-Registry extract and the partners' resolution; (3) the non-resident shareholder's overseas bank sends a SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR) to the Egyptian bank's USD-correspondent clearing chain (e.g., beneficiary BIC CIBEEGCX for CIB, NBEGEGCX for NBE), naming the beneficiary precisely as 'EG_LLC' and stating the payment purpose in field 70 as 'Direct investment — share capital injection into Egyptian LLC'; (4) the receiving bank applies CDD to the remitter and records the BoP item 'Direct Investment / Equity'; (5) upon credit and any agreed EGP conversion at the interbank rate, the bank issues a capital-deposit certificate evidencing the paid share capital; (6) the LLC completes final GAFI registration and reflects the deposit in annual financial statements. Cash deliveries, third-party intermediary wiring without corporate-name identity, and unlicensed PSP wallets are not acceptable for the capital-formation route. Any inbound shareholder loan instead of equity must be booked through the bank, coded as 'Direct Investment / Debt', and is subject to Egyptian thin-cap and interest-withholding rules.Import settlement rails for consumer-electronics purchases: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers from the company's USD account for advance-payment or documents-against-payment terms — accepted under the post-2023 relaxed regime provided the bank holds the pro forma invoice, the supplier's GOEIC registration proof, and the company's tax card, and the disbursement is filed against the import ledger; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit via SWIFT MT700 (issuance) and MT707 (amendment) for batch wholesale purchases, typically payable at sight against shipping documents and settled through the Egyptian bank's trade desk under the CBE import-documents verification framework; (iii) documentary collections (D/P or D/A) reintroduced in 2023 as a permitted alternative. For local Egyptian suppliers, disbursements use the CBE-operated national RTGS/ACH clearing rails. All outbound USD wires require linkage to the importer's Commercial Register, tax card, GOEIC registration, and eventual customs clearance; speculative overnight EGP/USD positioning by corporates is not permitted.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP FX forwards, purchased USD vs EGP, executed directly with a CBE-licensed corporate bank against documented import exposure (pro forma invoice or LC); tenors generally 1–12 months, extendable toward 24 months with bank credit approval and initial margin/collateral of 10–15% of notional.Offshore USD/EGP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only through the Egyptian bank's parent/affiliate for non-deliverable hedging of the EGP leg; permitted solely against genuine underlying commercial flows, never for speculative positions, and disfavored after the 2024 float in favor of the cheaper deliverable onshore market.USD/EGP cross-currency swaps and FX swaps, executed on bank treasury approval to align the currency and tenor of import payables with EGP current-account revenues, supported by written trade contracts and cash-flow projections.Purchased (long) USD-call/EGP-put options for hedging the e-commerce import-payment stream, available from major onshore banks where the option premium is economically justified; writing or selling options is not granted to standard retail trade corporates.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury layer, AUXILIARY only for B2C collection. Legal constraints: CBE Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Egyptian Payments Law No. 18 of 2019 reserve account-keeping, clearing, and issuance of e-money to CBE-licensed banks and licensed PSPs; offshore digital EMIs (Stripe, PayPal, Wise, Payoneer) are not licensed in Egypt, cannot open EGP accounts, cannot be designated as a repatriation bank, and any proceeds resting in an EMI wallet are not recognized as statutory receipt. Stripe does not currently onboard Egyptian-domiciled merchants; PayPal is only a limited receive/withdrawal channel in Egypt and cannot provide settlement rails. Consequently, the EG_LLC must use a CBE-licensed local acquiring bank/PSP (e.g., CIB merchant services, NBE gateway, Paymob, Fawry, Telda) for card settlement, and any PayPal/Stripe-adjacent international collection must be swept daily to the licensed EGP/USD account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8 million aggregate annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory TP Disclosure Form and Local File; group consolidated turnover above EGP 500 million also requires Master File and CbCR positions.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not run a separate ESR notification system, but the ETA enforces substance through GAAR, beneficial ownership rules and business-purpose doctrines. Free-zone/SCZone concessions require actual employees, premises, books and decision-making in Egypt. A shell Egyptian LLC interposed between offshore suppliers and consumers, holding only the e-commerce contract while all real functions are performed abroad, will be disregarded or recharacterised, with profits reassessed at the Egyptian level and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Additional CIT assessment, late-payment surcharge at 2% per month capped at 100%, criminal fine up to EGP 20,000 or three times the evaded tax, imprisonment up to five years, and cancellation/renewal denial of the tax card.

Director personal liability: Directors and the UBO who signed or caused the false return can be prosecuted jointly; courts may pierce the LLC veil and impose civil restitution, asset freezing and travel bans under Egyptian criminal tax procedure.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: ETA will reconstruct the transaction under the arm's length standard, deny deductions for undocumented imports/service fees, apply penalty amounts for failure to submit TP documentation and charge the 2% monthly late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved non-arm's length invoicing may be assessed as responsible persons for collected taxes; in severe cases, fraudulent invoicing leads to criminal tax penalties and travel restrictions.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: ETA can recharacterise the arrangement, attribute the full retail profit to the Egyptian LLC, deny treaty benefits, impose 22.5% CIT plus penalties, and withhold at 20% on any amount treated as a technical fee or royalty.

Director personal liability: Because the corporate form is abused, veil piercing is available; directors can be held jointly liable for the tax gap and may face travel bans and deportation/residence restrictions in Egypt.
AML and Digital Payment Misuse

Statutory sanction: Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law 80/2002 sanctions inadequate AML controls, typically fines and imprisonment up to five years for deliberate money laundering; settlement accounts may be frozen by CBE and platform payouts paused.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal prosecution, personal fines, and seizure of assets; bank mandates and travel restrictions may be ordered in pending AML investigations.
