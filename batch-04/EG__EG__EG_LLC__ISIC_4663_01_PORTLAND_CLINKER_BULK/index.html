Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) — One-Stop Shop for Incorporation and Commercial Registration, Cairo Headquarters and governorate branches.

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 as amended by Law No. 4 of 2018, Executive Regulations issued by Presidential Decree No. 146 of 2019 (Limited Liability Company formation and registration formalities), the SME Law No. 152 of 2020 for the small-company streamlined regime, and Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 as the optional incentive and dispute-resolution framework.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Application and Articles of Association (Aqd Tasis) submitted electronically through the GAFI Egypt Business Center one-stop-shop portal, followed by notarization and Commercial Registry registration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No de jure minimum paid-up capital applies; founders declare a nominal share capital (market benchmark EGP 50,000). The entire declared capital must be deposited into a blocked capital escrow account at a Central Bank of Egypt licensed commercial bank, and the Capital Deposit Certificate must be presented to GAFI before issuance of the incorporation certificate; funds are released only by joint instruction of the authorized signatories following Commercial Registration.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Authority (Ministry of Interior) certificate confirming fire-fighting equipment, NFPA-compliant stack spacing, and escape routes in the storage facility; structural load inspection certificate from a licensed engineer verifying yard and silo bearing capacity for bulk clinker storage; Ministry of Manpower Occupational Safety and Health clearance covering workplace risk assessment, dust exposure controls, and periodic medical examination records.

Paid-in capital rule: No de jure minimum paid-up capital applies; founders declare a nominal share capital (market benchmark EGP 50,000). The entire declared capital must be deposited into a blocked capital escrow account at a Central Bank of Egypt licensed commercial bank, and the Capital Deposit Certificate must be presented to GAFI before issuance of the incorporation certificate; funds are released only by joint instruction of the authorized signatories following Commercial Registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministry of Finance — Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA); official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipts clearance portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity exporting from Egypt must obtain a statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing the ETA's unified tax registration form, via the ETA taxpayer portal or the competent ETA office, after completing commercial registration (Companies Law 159/1981 or Investment Law 72/2017). The registration file must contain the notarized certificate of incorporation/articles of association, commercial registry extract, an Egyptian resident tax representative's power of attorney, board resolution, proof of premises, and bank account details. The ETA issues a single TIN that integrates income tax, stamp tax, withholding tax and VAT registration; the taxpayer is then activated on the mandatory e-invoicing system with digital signature credentials. Non-resident suppliers deriving taxable revenues from Egyptian nexus must register through an appointed Egyptian tax representative; processing is typically 5–20 business days. Exporting companies must also obtain the CBE import/export code from their bank and, if applicable, register on the NAFEZA single-window customs platform.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide profits at the 22.5% standard corporate income tax rate (Law 91/2005 as amended); shareholders are not pass-through participants, and dividends are subject to separate dividend withholding tax. Branches of foreign LLCs are equally entity-level taxpayers on Egyptian-source income. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide profits at the 22.5% standard corporate income tax rate (Law 91/2005 as amended); shareholders are not pass-through participants, and dividends are subject to separate dividend withholding tax. Branches of foreign LLCs are equally entity-level taxpayers on Egyptian-source income. A free-zone/SCZone entity remains entity-level taxed but may obtain territorial VAT/customs relief for inter-zone and export supplies.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch entity election; an LLC is a per-se corporate taxpayer. Available elective mechanisms relevant to this structure are limited to: (i) GAFI Free Zone or Investment Zone licensing and SCZone designation, altering the VAT/customs territorial treatment of supplies while retaining entity-level CIT; (ii) selection of the ETA's simplified small-taxpayer accounting/registration for turnover below the statutory ceiling (not applicable to the export wholesale volume assumed here); and (iii) appointment of an Egyptian resident agent/tax representative for foreign entities. CFC, permanent establishment and general anti-avoidance rules apply automatically and cannot be elected away.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 1,000,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods and services (mandatory VAT registration); voluntary registration is permitted once annual taxable turnover reaches EGP 500,000. Registration must be filed within 30 days of exceeding the threshold. A non-resident carrying on an economic activity with Egyptian nexus must register (through a resident tax representative) for VAT irrespective of the threshold when the activity is not otherwise exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods, including bulk Portland clinker/cement loaded on ocean vessels, are zero-rated under VAT Law 67/2016 where the goods are physically removed from Egypt and exported under a valid customs declaration; the supplier must retain a coherent export file evidencing the sale to a non-resident, the transport documents, and receipt of the sale proceeds in convertible foreign currency. Directly connected services (e.g., international sea freight of the exported goods, handling and international transport services performed for the exporter) are also zero-rated/exempt where supplied to and paid by a non-resident. A free-zone/SCZone exporter is likewise entitled to 0% on exports with simplified territorial verification. Input VAT attributable exclusively to the exported supply is refundable after ETA verification of the export and banking file; exporters with clean compliance histories may access the ETA's accelerated 45-day VAT refund procedure.

  • Egyptian Customs electronic export declaration (NAFEZA single-window clearance record) confirming physical export of the goods from Egypt
  • Signed international sales contract/order and commercial invoice with the foreign buyer, including vessel-loading terms for bulk clinker
  • Bill of lading or sea waybill evidencing loading at an Egyptian port and discharge at a destination outside Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-currency credit confirmation (SWIFT credit note) proving export proceeds were received into the exporter's Egyptian bank account
  • ETA zero-rated e-invoice bearing the ETA-issued UUID and QR stamp, with the VAT input invoice list and refund application when an input VAT refund is claimed
  • Certificate of origin for the cement/clinker where required under the letter of credit or destination-market rules

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing (B2B) and e-receipts (B2C) applies to all VAT-registered enterprises under ETA Decision No. 357/2021 and phased implementation decisions. Suppliers must generate invoices in the ETA-prescribed JSON schema, digitally sign them using ETA-issued cryptographic credentials, and transmit every invoice to the ETA clearance platform (integrated billing system API or ETA invoicing portal at https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg); the ETA returns a unique UUID and QR stamp after real-time validation, and only then may the supplier deliver the invoice to the purchaser. Cleared invoice data must be archived for 5 years. Invoices without ETA clearance are invalid for input VAT deduction, and separate monetary penalties apply for each non-compliant or late transmission.

The ETA and Central Bank of Egypt cross-match export declarations, bills of lading, contract pricing, and banking FX credits on a national database; auditors verify that export proceeds were repatriated to an Egyptian bank account within 180 days of shipment. A defective or fraudulent zero-rating file triggers: reversal of 0% and reassessment at 14% VAT; refusal and retrospective clawback of input VAT refunds with statutory delay additions (2% per month or part of a month under Law 67/2016); monetary fines and loss of eligibility for the accelerated-refund and e-invoicing compliance regimes; administrative customs penalties, including goods confiscation, for false export declarations; and criminal tax-evasion liability — imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of not less than EGP 5,000 and not exceeding EGP 50,000 (VAT Law as amended), in addition to the tax and delay additions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-floating exchange rate regime. Current account transactions are generally freely convertible for trade in goods and services; capital account operations, including foreign direct investment entry and profit remittance, are subject to CBE licensing, registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), and bank-mediated documentary compliance. Profit and dividend remittance is permissible after settlement of corporate tax due and submission of audited financial statements through the local custodian bank, but residual administrative hurdles exist including bank liquidity availability, priority allocation for essential imports, and periodic surrender constraints that can delay outward remittance timing. Bilateral investment treaty protections and GAFI guarantees mitigate convertibility risk for registered FDI.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated through an Egyptian bank within 60 days of the bill of lading date (extendable to 120 days with CBE approval for deferred payment trade terms). A mandatory conversion ratio of 100% of the repatriated export proceeds applies, requiring conversion to Egyptian Pounds (EGP) within 5 business days of receipt unless the exporter obtains an exemption under CBE's designated commodity list or qualifies for foreign currency retention accounts for pre-approved operational needs, such as covering imports of raw materials or servicing hard-currency debt. Failure to comply subjects the exporter to sanctions, fines, and suspension of banking facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and the Ministry of Finance's Customs and Foreign Trade Control authorities acting under the Foreign Exchange Law No. 194 of 1980 and CBE Circulars (including Circular 4/2022 and subsequent FX liberalisation measures)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. CBE prohibits unlicensed and offshore financial technology companies and electronic money institutions (EMIs) from providing cross-border payment services or holding Egyptian client funds without obtaining a CBE e-payment licence and establishing a local legal presence. No-license 'digital EMI' onboarding for operating accounts is not viable for an Egyptian LLC; any cross-border payment tokenisation must route through CBE-approved Payment Service Providers (PSPs) or banks, which are subject to full AML/CFT supervision under Law No. 80 of 2002 and CBE's payment card and fund transfer regulations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (final withholding tax on dividend distributions made by Egyptian companies to non-resident shareholders, imposed under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 4 months after the end of the tax year; for a calendar-year LLC, the statutory deadline is 30 April. A tax year other than the calendar year is permitted with prior ETA approval; the deadline is nevertheless 4 months after that tax year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Advance corporate income tax installments are due at intervals during the tax year as determined by the ETA's schedule for the relevant fiscal year; the final annual reconciliation is performed in the Model No. 10 return due 4 months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the aggregate value of related-party transactions during the tax year reaches or exceeds EGP 15,000,000 (Egyptian pounds). Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EGP 1,000,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational enterprises meeting the higher ETA-published consolidated revenue threshold. The Egyptian LLC importing Portland clinker from a related foreign supplier or paying management, interest, or royalty fees to related parties above these thresholds must prepare contemporaneous documentation before the filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the Income Tax Law and the Unified Tax Procedures Law contain general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and substance-over-form doctrines. The ETA may disregard arrangements lacking commercial rationale, recharacterize transactions, and reallocate profits to the Egyptian entity where the LLC is a paper relay with no real office, no operational management, no warehouse or physical logistics for bulk clinker, and no assumption of commercial risk. Free-zone and Investment Law incentives likewise require demonstrable operational substance, local employment, audited books, and active fulfillment of activity; a shell LLC interposed to strip margins through inflated management fees or excessive interest will trigger recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and penalty assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False CIT Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution by the ETA: imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 500,000, plus mandatory restitution of the evaded tax with monthly late-payment interest of 1.5% on the unpaid amount. In aggravated cases the financial penalty may be up to double the evaded tax and the tax authority may seal the premises.

Director personal liability: Under Law No. 91/2005 and Law No. 206/2020, the president of the board, managing director, or LLC manager who signs the false return is jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties. Criminal conviction attaches directly to that individual, enabling personal bankruptcy proceedings, attachment of personal assets, and a travel ban (prevention from departure) issued by the public prosecutor for the duration of the investigation and execution of liabilities.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: ETA recharacterization of the structure under the GAAR, reallocation of profits to the Egyptian taxable entity, full disallowance of the related-party payments, additional tax up to 100% of the under-assessed amount, monthly interest of 1.5%, and rejection of treaty relief. The entity may also be struck off the commercial register for pursuing an activity different from that declared.

Director personal liability: The ETA may pierce the corporate veil where the LLC is a facade: directors become personally liable for the company's tax debts and for undistributed withholding obligations. Individual officers may face criminal sanctions, prohibition from managing companies for up to 5 years, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets to serve the corporate tax debt if corporate funds are insufficient.
Withholding Tax Negligence / Unreported Distributions

Statutory sanction: Assessment against the Egyptian paying LLC of the full unpaid WHT amount plus monthly late-payment interest of 1.5%; additional fine ranging from EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000 and criminal liability for deliberate non-withholding under Article 137 of Law No. 91/2005.

Director personal liability: The withholding obligation falls personally on the LLC's statutory representative (manager/director), who is the designated withholding agent. A director who authorizes an unreported foreign remittance is jointly liable to the tax authority for the full withheld sum and may incur an interdiction from travel, a freezing order on personal bank accounts, and imprisonment for up to 2 years where the non-withholding was fraudulent.
AML / Money Laundering (Law No. 80/2002 as amended by Law No. 72/2021)

Statutory sanction: Independent criminal sanctions separate from tax penalties: imprisonment of up to 15 years and fines up to EGP 5,000,000 for the entity; freezing and confiscation of the laundered funds and the assets of the entity; prohibition from using Egyptian bank facilities and cancellation of import/export privileges.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and compliance officers are personally indictable for failure to implement AML policies. Conviction results in imprisonment, disqualification from board positions, confiscation of personal assets traceable to the funds, and a mandatory travel ban while the criminal case is pending; the EMLCU may also issue a domestic asset freezing order against all officers involved.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Price Manipulation of Imports

Statutory sanction: ETA TP adjustment based on its own comparables analysis, additional corporate tax, monthly late-payment interest of 1.5%, and an administrative fine for documentation failures ranging from EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000. Where the mispricing also affects customs value, customs duty and VAT reassessments follow, together with penalties under the Unified Customs Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the TP documentation or the tax return affirming the arm's-length character of the related-party transactions become personally liable for the tax adjustment in the event of ETA recharacterization. Individual liability includes civil restitution of the evaded tax, travel bans during active audit or execution procedures, and referral to criminal prosecution if the TP documentation is found to be fabricated or fraudulent.
