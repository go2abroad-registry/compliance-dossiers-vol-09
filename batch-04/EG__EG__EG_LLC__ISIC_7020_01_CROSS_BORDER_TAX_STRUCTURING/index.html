Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for an Egyptian LLC. The founders fix the nominal capital, which must be divided into equal quotas. The entire cash portion of the capital must be deposited before registration into a temporary corporate bank account in Egypt; the bank issues a capital deposit certificate. Full cash payment is mandatory at incorporation. In-kind contributions must be independently valued and fully delivered. The deposit is released to the company after issuance of the Commercial Registration Certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire Safety Certificate issued after inspection of the registered office, covering fire extinguisher placement, emergency exits, smoke detection, and evacuation signage; structural load inspection endorsed by a licensed engineering office confirming adequate office floor loading; occupational health and workplace safety clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 covering ventilation, lighting, electrical installation safety, restrooms, and first-aid facilities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA); e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoice portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), the Egyptian LLC must register with the ETA to obtain a tax card containing the income tax ID and, if VAT-registered, the VAT registration number. Registration is completed using the commercial registration certificate, constitutional documents, director and shareholder identification, lease or ownership evidence of the head office, and the signed ETA registration forms; non-resident shareholders are generally not required to obtain an Egyptian TIN solely by holding shares unless they create a permanent establishment in Egypt.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; an Egyptian LLC is an opaque resident entity subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income under Law No. 91 of 2005 and to VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 irrespective of dividend distribution.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available in Egypt. The only relevant statutory election is the tax grouping election under Law No. 91 of 2005, whereby an Egyptian parent company holding at least 95% of the share capital and voting rights of an Egyptian subsidiary may elect grouped taxation. Hybrid mismatch elections, foreign entity classification elections, and most OECD-type entity classification elections are not recognized in Egyptian domestic tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover; mandatory registration also applies to a single taxable supply exceeding EGP 500,000, and registration is immediately effective once the threshold is met

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and services are zero-rated for VAT when: (i) goods are physically delivered to a non-resident and leave Egypt within the statutory period; or (ii) services are provided to a non-resident person who uses or consumes the services wholly outside Egypt, including cross-border tax and transfer-pricing consultancy in respect of non-Egyptian entities. The exporter must possess contemporaneous evidence showing the export nature of the transaction, receipt of foreign currency in an Egyptian bank account, and consistency between the reported supply and the actual services or goods delivered.

  • Customs Export Declaration (for goods, with ACI number in the Egyptian customs single-window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for physical exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming receipt of convertible foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice or service agreement clearly identifying the non-resident recipient and the offshore nature of the services
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice with QR code evidencing zero-rated supply

All VAT-registered taxpayers must use the ETA e-invoicing system through approved clearing centers; invoices must be submitted in real time via the ETA API in the prescribed JSON/XML format and receive ETA clearance with a unique UUID and QR code before being treated as valid tax invoices. E-invoice clearance is mandatory for all B2B and B2G transactions, and e-receipt clearance is phased in for B2C sales; invoices without ETA clearance are invalid for input VAT deduction and legal documentation.

Unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to ETA reassessment at the 14% standard VAT rate, late-payment surcharges, statutory daily penalties, cancellation of the export certificate, and retrospective clawback of any refunded VAT. The ETA audits export documentation and foreign-exchange repatriation records; in cases of deliberate false documentation, criminal prosecution and fines are available under the Egyptian VAT Law and banking regulations, including restitution of the tax amount plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, ensuring current-account convertibility: ISIC 7020 consultancy fees, royalties, and management charges earned by EG_LLC may be invoiced, converted, and remitted to non-resident parties without exchange-control prohibition once the underlying service is documented and any applicable Egyptian tax is settled or exempted. Since the 6 March 2024 FX reform, the EGP is managed as a flexible currency priced by CBE-authorized banks on a daily interbank basis; no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio applies to current-account exports. The capital account remains regulated: inbound equity, shareholder loans, capital repatriation, liquidation balances, and profit remittances require documentary verification through a CBE-authorized bank, filing on the CBE integrated transfer platform, and reference to the GAFI investment file where applicable. Profit-remittance hurdles are procedural rather than prohibitive: audited Egyptian financial statements, tax clearance from the Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) or a valid exemption certificate, correct beneficiary ownership declaration, sanctions screening by the remitting bank, and, for dividends, payment of the 5% or 10% dividend withholding tax as applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Circular No. 12 of 2019 as amended by later circulars and the 2024 liberalization package, service-export proceeds for ISIC 7020 consulting contracts must be repatriated to a CBE-authorized Egyptian bank within 90 days from the invoice due date; a bank-approved extension to 120 days may be granted for contracts with inherently long settlement terms. The proceeds must be credited in the original hard currency to a designated Egyptian foreign-currency account and declared on the CBE integrated transfer reporting system. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain 100% of proceeds in USD or EUR and convert to EGP at the prevailing interbank rate at its own election. The former 50% mandatory surrender that applied to selected goods-export categories before 2024 is not applicable to professional services provided the LLC maintains an executed client agreement, a milestone-dated invoice, and a matching inward SWIFT credit to the compliance account. Late repatriation disables access to the LLC's hedging lines, triggers automatic debit of proceeds toward credit facilities, elevates EMLCU reporting, and may block future CBE approvals for foreign-currency accounts or trade finance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE); the CBE is the sole exchange-control authority and, through the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU), the national financial intelligence unit. The General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) also administers FDI-related registration and investment-file compliance for the LLC under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Triple, and other foreign digital EMI platforms are not licensed by the CBE and may not provide CBE-supervised accounts to Egyptian corporates. An Egyptian LLC is prohibited under Article 233 of Law No. 194 of 2020 from maintaining offshore operational accounts without a prior, purpose-limited CBE authorization. Foreign EMIs may therefore be used only as peripheral payout-collection channels for low-value, non-resident client fees, with funds swept monthly into the onshore CBE-authorized compliance account. Routing equity injections, intercompany balances, or profit distributions through an unlicensed EMI is a definitive anti-money-laundering red flag, invalidates tax-clearance proof for remittances, and exposes the LLC's signatories to criminal exposure under Egypt's AML Law No. 80 of 2002. Onshore Egyptian fintech rails such as Fawry, Telr, and bank e-wallets are acceptable only for EGP-denominated local collections and cannot substitute for cross-border SWIFT settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's foreign bank to the EG_LLC escrow or operational account at a CBE-authorized Egyptian bank. The wire must carry the CBE inward-direct-investment classification for the acquisition of equity in an existing LLC and reference the GAFI investment-file number in SWIFT field 70, together with the corporate name, purpose, and invoice-equivalent capital-constitution reference. The beneficiary bank verifies the credentials against the commercial registry, tax card, and investment certificate, credits the designated exchange-control account, and converts FX to EGP at the interbank rate if the capital has been subscribed in EGP; otherwise it holds the hard currency in a legacy capital account. The subscribed capital must remain unencumbered until the commercial-registry amendment and statutory publication in the Official Gazette are completed. A shareholder remittance that is not equity must be submitted under the CBE inward-loan purpose code so that principal and interest can be serviced abroad under the applicable debt/equity rules; failure to classify accurately may lead to recharacterization, denial of interest deductibility, and withholding exposure. Every inbound leg is reported by the receiving bank to the CBE integrated transfer platform, and EMG/EMLCU sanctions screening is executed before credit.Outbound settlements for subcontractors and non-resident service providers must be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the CBE-authorized Egyptian bank using the appropriate CBE current-account services-import purpose code and supported by the signed agreement, itemized invoice, proof of performance, and ETA withholding-tax certificate or exemption. For physical-goods procurement or software licenses on behalf of advisory clients, the standard clearing rail is a sight documentary letter of credit governed by UCP600 and opened through the Egyptian bank's trade desk, usually with 100% cover or an approved working-capital limit. Profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are a distinct gate: the remitting bank must receive audited financial statements, the ETA tax-clearance certificate, evidence of payment of dividend withholding tax, and the board resolution authorizing distribution; the wire must include the CBE dividend-repatriation purpose code. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW. Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Triple, and other foreign digital EMI platforms are not licensed by the CBE and may not provide CBE-supervised accounts to Egyptian corporates. An Egyptian LLC is prohibited under Article 233 of Law No. 194 of 2020 from maintaining offshore operational accounts without a prior, purpose-limited CBE authorization. Foreign EMIs may therefore be used only as peripheral payout-collection channels for low-value, non-resident client fees, with funds swept monthly into the onshore CBE-authorized compliance account. Routing equity injections, intercompany balances, or profit distributions through an unlicensed EMI is a definitive anti-money-laundering red flag, invalidates tax-clearance proof for remittances, and exposes the LLC's signatories to criminal exposure under Egypt's AML Law No. 80 of 2002. Onshore Egyptian fintech rails such as Fawry, Telr, and bank e-wallets are acceptable only for EGP-denominated local collections and cannot substitute for cross-border SWIFT settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions per fiscal year triggers mandatory transfer pricing disclosure and local file preparation

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist in Egypt, but Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 incorporates antiavoidance principles, substance-over-form, and general anti-abuse rules. The ETA may disregard artificial structures, recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, and impose penalties if the Egyptian LLC lacks substantive activity such as a physical office, qualified staff, margin-bearing functions, and actual decision-making in regard to consultancy and tax structuring services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty equal to 150%–200% of the evaded tax; interest and delay surcharges at 2% per month up to 30%; criminal fine and imprisonment up to five years under Egyptian Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who approve false filings are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax, surcharge, and penalties; the corporate veil may be pierced upon proof of fraud, resulting in seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: ETA application of general anti-abuse rules to disregard the entity and reallocate income; denial of treaty relief; tax reassessment with 22.5% CIT, late-payment surcharges, and penalty up to 200% in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who knowingly use a shell structure face joint liability for the entire tax deficiency, administrative fines, and criminal sanctions for tax evasion; travel bans and asset freezes are available under Egyptian execution law.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Upward TP adjustment, denial of deduction for unsubstantiated related-party charges, administrative penalty for non-disclosure, and monthly delay surcharge of 2% on the tax deficiency, capped at 30%.

Director personal liability: The tax director and managing directors who sign false TP declarations may be personally assessed for surcharges and penalties; criminal liability may attach when misstatements are fraudulent.
Money Laundering & AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002, penalties include imprisonment up to 15 years, fines up to EGP 1,000,000, confiscation of funds, and freezing of corporate accounts; financial institution de-risking may also occur.

Director personal liability: Senior management, directors, and beneficial owners who knowingly or negligently facilitate money laundering are exposed to direct criminal prosecution, travel bans, asset seizure, and disqualification from serving as directors.
