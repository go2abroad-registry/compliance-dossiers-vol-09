Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; e-Tax and e-Invoice portal administered at www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors establishing an Egyptian LLC must submit the standard incorporation and business license documents to the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Ministry of Trade and Industry; after incorporation, the LLC applies for a Tax Card and unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) at the competent ETA office by filing Form 113 (or equivalent registration form), attaching the Commercial Registry extract, power of attorney, articles of association, and proof of registered office. VAT registration is made separately on the same ETA portal; the LLC receives a VAT/TIN certificate evidencing capacity to issue valid tax invoices. Non-resident foreign entities without an Egyptian branch or fixed establishment generally are not locally registered for TIN/VAT unless they maintain a permanent establishment, supply taxable services in Egypt, or import into Egypt.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a tax resident juridical entity taxed on worldwide income under Law No. 91 of 2005; for indirect tax purposes the LLC is a taxable person subject to Egyptian VAT rules; no automatic pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or statutory hybrid mismatch elections. An LLC can elect its financial year (calendar year default), elect to carry forward tax losses (companies generally carry forward up to five years), and, if qualifying for group registration, may apply for consolidated tax filing. No entity classification election under Egyptian VAT law exists; VAT recovery is governed by standard input/output VAT mechanics.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supply turnover (mandatory registration for importers regardless of threshold; voluntary registration is available below threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the export of goods where actual physical export outside Egypt occurs within the statutory deadline, supported by customs export documentation. For cross-border services, including CRO services to a non-resident sponsor outside Egypt, the service must be supplied to a recipient outside Egypt, the beneficiary must not consume the service in Egypt, and the CRO service must demonstrate that the taxable person's export evidence meets ETA guidance. If clinical trial management services are performed in Egypt for Egyptian patients and consumed locally, even for a foreign sponsor, those local portions are normally standard-rated; only services benefitting and used entirely outside Egypt qualify at 0%.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

VAT-registered businesses above the e-invoicing implementation threshold must issue electronic invoices and e-receipts in a structured XML/JSON format submitted to the ETA's central clearance platform in near-real time. Each e-invoice must carry a valid TIN/VAT ID, encoded product/service code, QR code, and clearing reference issued by the ETA. The system rejects non-compliant invoices and requires API integration through approved communication channels.

The ETA may audit export zero-rating claims for up to five years and restrospectively reassess VAT at the standard rate if export conditions are not satisfied or goods/services are diverted to local consumption. Penalties include the VAT amount due, late payment penalties, and administrative fines; false declarations or fraudulent documentation may trigger 100% additional tax, criminal penalties, and suspension from the e-invoicing system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian Pound has been convertible under a unified floating exchange-rate regime since 6 March 2024, supported by the IMF Extended Fund Facility. Current-account transactions, including CRO clinical-trial service fees and milestone payments from non-resident pharmaceutical sponsors, are freely convertible and may be retained in foreign currency. Capital-account convertibility is available for registered foreign direct investment, but only if the inward equity injection is made through an authorized Egyptian bank and registered with the CBE via the bank plus the General Authority for Investment (GAFI); subsequent profit and capital repatriation requires audited financial statements, evidence of Egyptian corporate income-tax payment or a tax-clearance certificate, corporate resolutions, and an arm's-length milestone billing trail matching the clinical-trial contracts. Practical hurdles for an offshore-owned LLC CRO include decentralized bank-level due diligence, mandatory valid tax-card status, potential branch-level delays in currency availability, and strict rejection of any settlement routed through non-authorized intermediaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service-export proceeds, including CRO revenue from foreign sponsors, must be repatriated and credited to an account at an authorized bank in Egypt within 30 days of the contractual invoice due date and within a hard statutory ceiling of 180 days from invoice issuance. The mandatory conversion (surrender) ratio is 0% under the post-float regime, meaning 100% of export receipts may be retained in a foreign-currency account with an authorized bank. Off-shore set-off arrangements, cross-border netting outside banks, or collection through foreign EMI wallets is a statutory exchange-control violation exposing the CRO and its managers to penalties imposed under CBE enforcement powers.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary and exchange-control authority; all foreign-exchange and cross-border treasury operations must be executed through CBE-licensed authorized dealer banks, acting under the Unified Banking Act (Law No. 194 of 2020), CBE foreign-exchange circulars, Egyptian Exchange Control Law (Law No. 80 of 1947), and Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, which guarantees the free transfer of invested capital and profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed EGP 5,000,000. Master File is mandatory when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 15,000,000,000. Country-by-Country Report is mandatory at the same EGP 15,000,000,000 consolidated group turnover threshold. Below the documentation thresholds, a simplified transfer pricing disclosure statement annexed to Form 19 remains compulsory, with fixed penalties for omission.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime comparable to GCC jurisdictions. However, Law 91/2005 Article 30, as amended by Law 211/2020, empowers the ETA to disregard arrangements lacking economic substance, apply the substance-over-form doctrine, and recharacterize intercompany transactions that do not reflect commercial reality. A clinical-trial CRO must demonstrate substantive local operations: physical office premises, qualified clinical staff, local ethics committee approvals, trial master files, and active decision-making on protocol execution. Absence of substance may trigger deemed distributions, full CIT assessment on artificial profits, WHT reclassification, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment plus a fine of up to three times the evaded tax under Law 91/2005 Article 133; compulsory payment of the evaded tax with a 2% monthly surcharge; revocation of tax clearance and VAT certificates; exclusion from public tenders.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and legal representatives who participate in, order, or conceal evasion are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, fines, and surcharges. Travel bans, asset freezes, and confiscation orders may be imposed; criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA disregards the corporate form under the general anti-abuse rule; intercompany deductions are disallowed; the LLC is assessed at 22.5% CIT on deemed reconstructed profit; unpaid WHT is recaptured with 2% monthly late penalties; treaty relief is denied.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud or abusive reliance on legal personality, directors are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and withholding amounts; civil restitution orders and suspension from board service apply.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA transfer pricing adjustments under Article 30; additional CIT at 22.5% on adjusted profit; administrative penalty of up to 5% of the adjusted transaction value; 2% monthly late payment surcharge; fixed penalties for failure to submit TP documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP declarations are jointly liable for the aggregate adjustment, the additional CIT, and the resulting penalties; automatic disqualification from obtaining tax clearance and government approvals.
Withholding tax default / profit stripping

Statutory sanction: A penalty equal to the uncollected WHT, plus a 2% monthly surcharge for each month of delay; the ETA may sequester bank accounts and offset any due refunds against the unpaid WHT liability.

Director personal liability: Article 141 of Law 91/2005 imposes joint and several liability on legal representatives for the tax that should have been withheld and remitted; personal civil restitution and travel bans follow continued non-payment.
Anti-Money Laundering violations

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law 80/2016, as amended: fines up to EGP 5,000,000, asset forfeiture, compulsory dissolution of the entity, and criminal prosecution of the compliance officer and board members.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines, imprisonment of up to 5 years, seizure of personal assets, and prohibition from leaving the country through immigration travel bans at the request of the Money Laundering Prosecution.
