Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box, fiscal transparency, or pass-through election is available under Egyptian tax law. An Egyptian LLC is a per se corporate taxpayer under Law No. 91 of 2005; it cannot be disregarded as a branch of its foreign parent. No hybrid mismatch election is provided by domestic law. The only available elections are ordinary income-tax accounting elections, depreciation method and period elections, and application of the unilateral foreign tax credit for foreign-source reinsurance income; a captive reinsurer does not qualify for small-business simplified regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is available from EGP 150,000. Reinsurance premium income is treated as an exempt supply under the VAT Law and is not itself chargeable to VAT; nevertheless, the LLC should maintain tax registration and e-invoicing capability as a licensed captive reinsurer and employer.

Egypt operates a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system. VAT-registered entities and other notified legal persons must issue invoices in the ETA-approved XML/JSON schema and transmit each invoice immediately through the ETA API. The ETA validates the transaction, assigns a unique UUID, and returns a QR code/digital stamp before the invoice has legal effect. E-receipts for consumer transactions are phased in through the same clearance mechanism. Any alteration, late submission, or bypass of the API disqualifies the invoice for input VAT deduction, corporate tax deduction, customs export substantiation, and export refund purposes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed float of the Egyptian pound (EGP) with no comprehensive exchange-control restrictions on current-account transactions, including trade, services, reinsurance premiums and claims arising in the ordinary course of business. Capital-account items such as equity injections, share subscriptions, dividend repatriation, intercompany loans and liquidation proceeds remain subject to documentary verification by Egyptian banks and CBE/ITRS reporting. Profit remittance is permissible after due payment of Egyptian corporate tax and withholding tax and, where relevant, submission of tax-clearance certificates or electronic tax-filing confirmations issued by the Egyptian Tax Authority. In practice, full convertibility is conditional on satisfactory documentation, the absence of unresolved commercial-bank FX rationing, and compliance with current CBE directives. The post-2024 stabilization framework has materially relaxed convertibility, but treasury must monitor any temporary CBE emergency measures during external-account stress.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Pursuant to CBE banking instructions, export proceeds must be received into an account maintained with an Egyptian-licensed bank and credited to the exporter within the statutory window. Under the current liberalized framework, the mandatory surrender ratio into EGP is 0% for goods proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts and deploy it for lawful trade, reinsurance, claims and service payments. The statutory repatriation timeline requires export proceeds to be credited to the Egyptian bank account within the later of 90 days from the bill-of-lading date or the maturity of the underlying documentary credit, and in no event later than 180 days after shipment unless a longer period is pre-approved by the CBE. For reinsurance and other services, proceeds must be repatriated upon contractual settlement due date or within not more than 90 days after invoice/maturity. CBE retains authority to reintroduce temporary conversion or surrender ratios in circumstances of acute foreign-exchange shortage, and treasury must not rely on a permanent 0% ratio for regulatory-capital or debt-service planning.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) established under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020; CBE is the sole licensing, supervisory and regulatory authority for banks, foreign exchange transactions and balance-of-payments reporting, and administers the Foreign Exchange Act and AML/CFT Law No. 80 of 2002.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. An offshore electronic money institution (EMI) or fintech payment provider may be used as an ancillary payment channel but should never replace a CBE-licensed bank as the primary treasury rail. Egyptian AML/CFT law and CBE regulations reserve deposit-taking, foreign-exchange dealing and local-currency payment settlement to licensed banks and licensed payment-service providers. An offshore EMI cannot independently provide final settlement into EGP, execute Egyptian tax payments, or produce CBE-reportable cross-border clearing evidence. The captive reinsurance SPV may nominate an EMI for operational receivables acceleration, provided the EMI holds funds in segregated accounts at a licensed bank and all material premium, claims and capital flows route through the Egyptian Tier 1 bank for full audit and regulatory trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection into the Egyptian captive SPV should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident parent to the SPV’s account at a CBE-licensed Tier 1 Egyptian bank. The remittance narrative must state equity contribution/direct investment in the Egyptian LLC and reference the applicable IMF BPM6 direct-investment purpose code used in Egypt. The wire must include the SPV legal name, entity identification, SWIFT/BIC, and a compliance reference to the board resolution approving the injection. The receiving bank will apply standard CBE KYC, verify the source of funds, check the UBO structure, and request certificate of incorporation, share register, tax identification, board resolution and proof of economic purpose. Funds should be booked into a dedicated capital escrow or foreign-currency operational account; any conversion to EGP must occur on the interbank market with a retained currency-transaction form for CBE inspection.Outbound payments from the Egyptian SPV—reinsurance premium cessions, claim settlements, fronting fees, ceding commissions and operating disbursements—should settle through SWIFT MT103 payment orders executed via the Egyptian bank’s correspondent network. For structured transactions, commercial documentary letters of credit (MT700/MT710) or documentary collections will be used where presentation of invoices, customs forms and underlying evidence is required. All outbound transfers must be supported by the underlying reinsurance or service contract, quoted CBE balance-of-payments reporting codes, and retained bank confirmations. The Form No. 8 return must be filed within four months after the end of the fiscal year. Except where the ETA has approved a different accounting period, the fiscal year is the calendar year ending on 31 December. Taxes due must be settled by the same statutory deadline assessed on the self-declared taxable income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transaction threshold for mandatory Local File preparation; Master File is required where consolidated group turnover exceeds EGP 100,000,000 or at ETA request. Captive reinsurance tailoring and premium arrangements above this threshold require contemporaneous benchmarking and Local File filing together with the annual return.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substances Regulation, but the tax administration invokes substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance rules, and OECD BEPS Action 5/6 principles. A captive reinsurance SPV must demonstrate genuine underwriting and risk management in Egypt: paid-up regulatory capital, qualified actuaries and insurance officers, board meetings and decisions in Egypt, substantive claims and reserve control, routed bank accounts, and physically occupied premises. Without economic substance, tax treaty benefits can be denied, captive profits may be recharacterized as taxable dividends or group service income, and the captive may be treated as a shell vehicle subject to license revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Return

Statutory sanction: Ex-officio ETA assessment at 22.5% plus monthly delay fees up to 100% of unpaid tax; criminal imprisonment up to five years and financial penalties; confiscation of assets derived from evaded tax; blacklisting from Egyptian tax clearance processes.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or knowingly prepared false returns are severally liable for evaded tax, civil restitution, criminal sanctions, asset freezes, and travel bans pending investigation or enforcement.
Shell Company and Unlicensed Reinsurance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of Financial Regulatory Authority insurance or reinsurance license, cancellation of Egyptian tax residence claimed by the SPV, recharacterization of all premiums as parent company income, and fines under Egyptian Insurance Law No. 10 of 1981.

Director personal liability: Nominee and shadow directors may face personal liability to creditors and the tax authority, civil restitution of diverted premiums, disqualification from insurance management, travel bans, and freezing of personal bank accounts.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: EAT transfer pricing adjustment increasing Egyptian taxable profits, 22.5% corporate tax on the upward adjustment, matching administrative penalties, monthly delay fees, and disallowance of retrocession/claim deductions where no valid commercial purpose exists.

Director personal liability: CFOs and board members who approve false TP policies or fail to maintain contemporaneous documentation become joint and several debtors for the TP adjustment; deliberate under-reporting can be prosecuted as tax fraud with travel bans and imprisonment.
AML and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Enforcement action by the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit, seizure and confiscation of reserve and investment assets, fines, and imprisonment of money laundering principals; revocation of the captive's license and removal of designated officers.

Director personal liability: Directors implicated in AML breaches face personal asset freezes, travel bans, criminal indictment, and permanent debarment from insurance and financial positions in Egypt; civil claims by harmed creditors may follow.
Unauthorized Profit Repatriation / WHT Evasion

Statutory sanction: ETA recapture of unpaid withholding tax, a delay fee of up to 100% of the WHT, denial of expense deductions for disguised distributions, and criminal penalties for deliberate concealment of non-resident tax liability.

Director personal liability: Board members authorizing unlawful repatriation are jointly and severally liable to the ETA, can be ordered to restore the distributed amount to the SPV, and may face travel bans, property precautionary seizure, and criminal liability for fraudulent distributions.
