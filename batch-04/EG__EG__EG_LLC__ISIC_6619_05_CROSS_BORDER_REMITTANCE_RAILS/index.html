Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported physical goods and qualifying international services, including cross-border remittance processing services provided to non-residents, are zero-rated subject to documentary proof that the goods or services left Egypt's territorial scope and were supplied to a recipient outside Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming foreign currency receipt
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Bank transfer credit note evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

All B2B transactions require e-invoicing with real-time fiscal clearance via the ETA's API; each invoice must be digitally signed and transmitted within 24 hours; B2C e-receipts are mandatory for eligible traders; QR code stamping is required on simplified invoices and receipts.

Zero-rating claims are subject to ETA audit covering export records, forex receipts, and supporting contracts; false or unsupported zero-rating triggers VAT assessment at 14%, additional late penalties up to 100% of tax due, and administrative fines ranging from EGP 50,000 to EGP 5,000,000; fraud cases may impose criminal penalties and full clawback with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-float exchange-rate regime for EGP. Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services) are generally convertible without prior licence. Capital-account transactions (profit repatriation, dividend distribution, intercompany loans, and investment liquidation) require documentary proof of underlying commercial purpose, CBE registration of foreign investment, audited financial statements, tax clearance, and compliance with CBE Circular No. 25/2016 (as amended). Repatriation of profits by an EG_LLC is permitted only after the annual audit is filed with GAFI and the relevant tax authorities; funds must be remitted through an authorised bank in the original currency or converted at the prevailing market rate. For remittance-service fees classified as invisibles, CBE reporting thresholds apply and the full amount of collected foreign currency must be credited to the company's licensed account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current CBE regulations, proceeds from exports of goods must be repatriated to Egypt through an authorised bank within 180 days from shipment date unless an extension is approved; for services (including money transmission commissions) the applicable receipt window is 90 days from service completion. There is no mandatory forced conversion ratio in Egypt since the 2016 FX liberalisation; however, 100% of the export/remittance value must be received into the Egyptian banking system. For a remittance agent, incoming cross-border settlement flows must be credited to the designated CBE-licensed collection account and reported through the electronic transfer system on a same-day basis. Residual EGP balances from conversion are subject to CBE liquidity-management guidelines, and any failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline triggers a compliance review and potential fines.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank Law) and Law No. 88 of 2003 (Bank and Credit Law). CBE operates the Foreign Exchange Trading System (FETS) and supervises all currency and payment operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability: LOW to MEDIUM. Off-shore fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Payoneer) do not hold a CBE electronic payment-service-provider (PSP) licence and therefore cannot act as the primary settlement gateway for Egypt-domiciled remittance flows. They may be used only for ancillary supplier payments where a local regulated bank account remains the principal treasury vehicle. Legal constraints under CBE payment regulations mandate that all Egypt inbound and outbound transaction settlement must pass through a licensed bank or a CBE-approved sub-processor; non-bank entities do not have direct SWIFT access. A licensed local electronic payment company may be engaged as a technical rails aggregator, but the core custody of client funds must remain with a commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections (share capital, shareholder loans, and licence contributions) must be remitted via SWIFT to the company's CBE-licensed bank account. The overseas remitter must include the appropriate ISO 20022 purpose code (e.g., 'ECUR' for equity capital) and the bank will verify economic substance against the investment registration certificate issued by GAFI or the relevant supervisory authority. For remittance agents, settlement funds from overseas correspondents must be credited to the designated collection account and reconciled with CBE's electronic transfer reporting portal on a trade-date basis. Cash funding is strictly prohibited; bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 confirmation is mandatory and must be supported by an indemnity covering source of funds and purpose of the transfer.Outbound trade and operating disbursements are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border bank transfers. Local cash-pickup settlements will be executed through the CBE Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system and the Egyptian inter-bank mobile wallet platform (InstaPay) for last-mile distribution. Commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) are opened through the same corporate bank using UCP600 standard rules and require pro-forma invoices, certificates of origin, and customs declarations where applicable. All outbound foreign-currency payments must be filed with CBE within the prescribed reporting period and meet the underlying business-activity test; supporting contracts and tax-compliance certificates (e.g., withholding tax receipts) must be maintained on file for four years.Onshore outright forwards (FX forwards) in EGP/USD and EGP/EUR - executed with authorised banks only after demonstrating a genuine underlying remittance or trade exposure, documented by contract and forecast cash flow.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) - permissible for liquidity and treasury management when paired with a corresponding commercial transaction, subject to CBE compliance and reporting obligations.Exchange-traded FX futures on the Egyptian Exchange (EGX) - available for EGP/USD hedging with approved local brokerage counterparties, subject to position limits.Option contracts (calls and puts on EGP) - may be offered by qualified banks to corporate clients with approved treasury policies; must be settled physically and cannot be used for speculative purposes.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) - not generally permitted for onshore Egyptian corporates; only non-resident counterparties with CBE-approved hedging programmes may access NDFs through international banks, and any settlement involving an Egyptian entity requires prior central-bank approval and subsequent reporting.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1: Sales/Revenue Gross operating revenue from cross-border remittance services, transaction fees, commissions, exchange spreads, and ancillary payment processing income, net of discounts and returns. Non-resident owned LLC must report all Egypt-source revenue from cross-border money transmission on an accrual basis, including income cleared through SWIFT and local cash pickup rails. Late filing triggers a fixed penalty of EGP 1,000 plus a monthly surcharge of 2% of tax due (minimum EGP 500), capped at 40% of assessed tax. Underpayment penalties apply to omitted tax. Material false declarations may expose criminal tax evasion sanctions with imprisonment.
Box 2: Deductible Operating Expenses Direct and indirect expenses: payroll, bank charges, SWIFT costs, cash pooling fees, regulatory licence amortisation, rent, utility costs, insurance, and depreciation determined under Egyptian tax rules. Payments to non-resident affiliates are deductible only where supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, beneficial ownership evidence, and proof of withholding tax deposit. Late filing triggers a fixed penalty of EGP 1,000 plus a monthly surcharge of 2% of tax due (minimum EGP 500), capped at 40% of assessed tax. Underpayment penalties apply to omitted tax. Material false declarations may expose criminal tax evasion sanctions with imprisonment.
Box 3: Net Taxable Income / Tax Loss Tax-adjusted profit after adding back non-deductible amounts, applying special deductions, and deducting carryforward tax losses (maximum 5 years). If the LLC reports a loss, the loss certificate must be retained. Carryforward relief is limited to 5 years and cannot be claimed against branch profits unless a treaty provides otherwise. Late filing triggers a fixed penalty of EGP 1,000 plus a monthly surcharge of 2% of tax due (minimum EGP 500), capped at 40% of assessed tax. Underpayment penalties apply to omitted tax. Material false declarations may expose criminal tax evasion sanctions with imprisonment.
Box 4: Related Party Transaction Disclosure (TP-1) Disclosure of all transactions with related parties, including nature, volume, pricing method, and economic substance, as required under Egyptian transfer pricing rules. Non-resident controlled LLCs with related-party transactions exceeding EGP 8 million must attach Local File/Master File and obtain ETA acknowledgement before filing deadline. Late filing triggers a fixed penalty of EGP 1,000 plus a monthly surcharge of 2% of tax due (minimum EGP 500), capped at 40% of assessed tax. Underpayment penalties apply to omitted tax. Material false declarations may expose criminal tax evasion sanctions with imprisonment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8 million aggregate annual value of related-party transactions triggers mandatory TP disclosure, Local File and Master File preparation. A Master File is also required where the MNE group records consolidated revenue above EGP 350 million. Egypt applies CbCR notification thresholds consistent with OECD base erosion rules for large groups.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the ETA applies substance-over-form principles, CFC provisions, and anti-abuse doctrines to disregard shell LLCs. A remittance LLC must maintain premises, staff, independent management, board minutes, bank accounts, and transaction decision-making within Egypt to evidence real economic activity and obtain treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 91 of 2005, articles 133–135: imprisonment up to 3 years, a fine of EGP 1,000 to EGP 5,000 or up to twice the evaded tax, and payment of all evaded tax plus penalties (up to 100%). Criminal prosecution may be brought before the Economic Offences Court.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties if the corporate veil is pierced. Travel bans, asset freezes, and seizure of personal property may be imposed, with imprisonment up to 3 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: ETA may disregard the LLC, recharacterise the activities of the foreign parent or shareholder, recapture withholding tax, impose deficiency assessments, and revoke the company's remittance licence under Law No. 72 of 2017.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held responsible for the entire tax deficiency under veil-lifting; criminal liability may attach for assisting a sham arrangement, leading to travel bans, disqualification, and imprisonment for tax evasion.
AML & Remittance Licensing Violation

Statutory sanction: License revocation, administrative fines starting from EGP 200,000 per violation, forfeiture of funds, and suspension from clearing and settlement systems. Criminal AML conviction can result in imprisonment up to 7 years and fines up to EGP 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Senior management are personally liable for deliberate or negligent failure to file STRs/CDD under AML law. Personal bank accounts may be frozen; directors may face travel bans, strikes-off from the commercial register, and imprisonment of up to 7 years.
