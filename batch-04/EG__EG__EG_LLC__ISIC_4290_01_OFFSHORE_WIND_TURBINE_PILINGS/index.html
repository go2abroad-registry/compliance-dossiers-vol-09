Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — official national tax authority; portal: www.eta.gov.eg (electronic services for TIN issuance, VAT filing, and e-invoicing).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Egypt must first obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the Egyptian Tax Authority by filing the standard registration application, submitting constitutional documents (e.g., certificate of incorporation, foreign registration certificates), notarized Arabic translations, evidence of the Egyptian permanent establishment or local branch registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), and any required commercial registry extracts. The ETA issues the TIN after verification; VAT registration is effected simultaneously when the entity exceeds the statutory threshold or voluntarily elects registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity conducting taxable activities in Egypt must first obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN) from the Egyptian Tax Authority by filing the standard registration application, submitting constitutional documents (e.g., certificate of incorporation, foreign registration certificates), notarized Arabic translations, evidence of the Egyptian permanent establishment or local branch registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), and any required commercial registry extracts. The ETA issues the TIN after verification; VAT registration is effected simultaneously when the entity exceeds the statutory threshold or voluntarily elects registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections. LLCs are taxed at the entity level under Law No. 91 of 2005 (Income Tax Law). Elections available include free-zone status with qualifying tax exemptions under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, special economic zone (SEZ) incentives, and treaty-based permanent establishment determinations through the competent authority procedure. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are available under the tax rules aligned with BEPS Action 2 where the law permits recharacterization.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration (non-resident suppliers with an Egyptian economic presence are also required to register).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside Egypt are generally zero-rated for Egyptian VAT provided they leave the country under customs supervision within the legally permitted timeframes. Offshore services supplied to non-residents for use wholly outside Egypt, including marine construction and installation services for offshore wind turbine foundations, may qualify as zero-rated services when the benefit is enjoyed outside Egypt and the exporter retains contemporaneous evidence. A written export declaration and compliance with the ETA’s export documentation rules are required; services must be performed physically outside Egypt or directly in relation to exported goods to be eligible.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Egypt operates a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing and clearance system administered by the ETA. B2B invoices must be transmitted to the ETA’s e-invoicing portal via secured API integration in a specified JSON/XML schema; each invoice receives a unique UUID and QR code after validation. The system performs real-time fiscal clearance and rejects non-conforming invoices. Foreign entities with Egyptian VAT obligations must integrate through approved SDKs or service providers.

The ETA may conduct post-export audits up to ten years from the filing date in cases of fraud. False zero-rating claims trigger retrospective VAT assessments at 14%, an additional penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, disciplinary fines under VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 (as amended) of not less than EGP 25,000 and up to EGP 500,000, and potential criminal liability including imprisonment. Where foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day CBE directive, tax authorities may deny the foreign currency certificate, invalidate zero-rating substantiation, and claw back the VAT plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a controlled but generally current-account convertible regime under Law No. 194 of 2020 encompassing the Foreign Exchange Regulation Law. The Egyptian Pound (EGP) is not freely convertible offshore; onshore convertibility is maintained through CBE-licensed banks. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections, are convertible upon submission of legally required documentation and CBE reporting, but remain subject to prudential gates and anti-money laundering checks. Profit remittances are permitted for legally established foreign-owned entities, provided the company has audited financial statements, a valid tax clearance certificate from the Egyptian Tax Authority, proof of capital entry through a bank account, and no outstanding unresolved exchange-control violations. Dividend and profit transfers are processed at the prevailing market rate with straightforward administrative review; however, historical periods of FX scarcity have resulted in queueing for hard currency, so pre-approval and early application are recommended for large distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE rules, all export receipts must be repatriated to Egypt and credited to an Egyptian bank account within a maximum of 90 days from the date of shipment or customs clearance, as indicated on the export documents. Exporters must submit customs declarations, invoices, and proof of ship arrival to the bank. There is currently no blanket statutory mandatory conversion of export proceeds into EGP under the liberalized FX regime following the 2016 float; exporters may retain foreign currency in approved accounts or convert to EGP at the market rate. However, the CBE reserves the right to introduce temporary surrender requirements (historically ranging from 50% to 75%) during foreign exchange stress periods, and banks may impose their own internal conversion thresholds for consolidation of liquidity. Full surrender obligations are typically triggered if the exporter utilises subsidised financing or concessional credit lines.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and the Egyptian Exchange Control Authority (within CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Egypt does not recognise offshore digital EMI (Electronic Money Institution) licenses. Any entity wishing to provide payment or e-money services must obtain a license from the CBE under the E-Money Regulation Law and the Payment Systems Law. An offshore EMI cannot open a corporate bank account or gateway to Egyptian clearing systems without a local licensed partner or a CBE-approved branch. For a single-purpose EG LLC, the incremental cost and time of establishing a licensed EMI are unjustified. Consequently, all treasury operations must be routed through regulated Egyptian commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign capital contributions and shareholder loans are routed through an onshore Egyptian bank using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire transfers. The remitting bank must include a purpose code describing the transaction as either 'Foreign Direct Investment – Share Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan' in accordance with CBE's Institutional Framework for Foreign Exchange Declaration. Prior to sending funds, the EG LLC must obtain a CBE-approved inward remittance number from the receiving bank by submitting the board resolution, signed subscription agreement, and the company's commercial registration. For amounts exceeding USD 100,000 or the EGP equivalent, a CBE foreign exchange declaration form must be filed by the receiving bank within 30 days. Funds are initially credited to the capital escrow account unless an operational account designation is permitted. Transfer instructions must specify the beneficiary bank's SWIFT code, account number, and reference 'OFFSHORE_WIND_PILINGS_EG_LLC_CAP2024'. The underlying project agreement with a recognised Ministry or utility should be annexed for smooth compliance.Trade payments for procured raw steel, marine equipment, and subcontracted installation services must be executed using SWIFT MT103 (for direct supplier payments) and documentary Letters of Credit (Commercial LC) issued by an Egyptian commercial bank upon presentation of shipping and customs documents. For LC issuances, EG LLC must provide the commercial invoiced value, transport documents, and proforma invoice to the issuing bank. The bank will then issue an irrevocable, at-sight LC under ICC Uniform Customs and Practice (UCP 600) terms. Outbound trade payments require CBE Form 103/104 filings and are scrutinised for currency conversion requests; EG LLC can request EGP conversion at the market rate or use the foreign currency from the company's retained export proceeds account.
