Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official portal: eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance portal: invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

The TIN must appear in all commercial, customs, and electronic invoicing records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not applicable. An Egyptian LLC is treated as a per-se corporation and is not eligible for pass-through or check-the-box treatment. Egypt does not recognize hybrid mismatch elections or domestic fiscal unity elections for LLCs; entity classification can only be changed by altering the legal form of incorporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies of goods and/or services during a 12-month period; non-resident taxable persons with an Egyptian taxable establishment apply the same threshold, while non-residents supplying specified digital or services with in-country use may have reverse-charge or registration obligations irrespective of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to goods physically exported to a non-resident under a valid customs export declaration, and to services supplied from Egypt to a non-resident where the place of supply and effective use and enjoyment are outside Egypt. The taxpayer must be VAT-registered, flag the relevant e-invoice as zero-rated, report the export transaction in the periodic VAT return, and preserve documentary proof of export and receipt of foreign currency proceeds in Egypt. Offshore email scanning, mail forwarding, and virtual-office administrative services may qualify where the recipient is foreign, the output is delivered outside Egypt, and the service is not consumed in-country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (final/approved export discharge) for tangible goods and physical mail items
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or postal export receipt proving physical dispatch from Egypt to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in Egypt within the prescribed repatriation period

Mandatory ETA e-invoicing and phased e-receipt regime. Registered taxpayers must generate invoices in XML/JSON and transmit them through the official ETA API for near-real-time validation. Each approved invoice receives an ETA-validated QR code, unique document number, and digital stamp; unvalidated invoices are non-compliant for input VAT deduction and may trigger administrative penalties. Fiscal clearance is performed electronically, and no non-digital invoice may be issued by a covered taxpayer.

The ETA cross-matches zero-rated VAT returns, e-invoices, customs declarations, and bank remittance records. Missing, contradictory, or false substantiation results in reassessment at 14% VAT, reversal of any claimed refund, late-payment interest, administrative fines, and potential tax-evasion prosecution with criminal penalties extending to the legal representative. Retrospective clawback may apply from the date the zero-rating was originally claimed, and the exporter may also lose customs facilitation status and face suspension of future zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII, with the Egyptian pound (EGP) traded in a managed-float interbank market. Capital account transactions remain restricted: inbound direct equity investment must be routed through a CBE-licensed bank with GAFI registration, while outbound dividend remittances require tax clearance certificates, CBE-approved purpose codes, and verified underlying documents. For a non-resident-owned LLC engaged in virtual office and scanning services, profit repatriation is lawful after corporate tax, VAT, and stamp tax compliance; the CBE applies enhanced scrutiny to foreign currency transfers exceeding intraday transaction thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE circulars issued pursuant to Law No. 194 of 2020, export proceeds must be fully repatriated to an Egyptian bank account within the commonly imposed timeline of 90 days from the date of shipment or negotiation of the exporting documents; CBE may temporarily reduce the window to 30 days during liquidity-stress periods. As of the current policy, there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio of export proceeds into EGP; exporters may retain foreign currency in CBE-licensed bank accounts and convert at the prevailing interbank market rate, subject to banks' monitoring and reporting obligations. Failure to meet the repatriation timeline triggers CBE penalties, bank account restrictions, and loss of access to import finance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and its Foreign Exchange Control Unit pursuant to Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and CBE AML/CFT regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and non-CBE-licensed payment institutions have low viability as a primary corporate treasury rail. Egyptian law requires all EGP deposits and clearing activity to be conducted through licensed banks or licensed fintech entities operating under CBE sandbox or Payment Systems Law (Law No. 18 of 2019). Foreign EMIs are not recognized as banks and cannot provide statutory corporate bank accounts, foreign exchange services, or CBE reporting. Their use is limited to ancillary card-issuing functions integrated with a CBE-licensed bank sponsor.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The receiving bank will issue a Foreign Investment Deposit Certificate and register the inflow with the CBE; where applicable, the LLC must also register the investment with GAFI. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends and profit distributions paid by an Egyptian LLC to both resident and non-resident shareholders, imposed under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005; no further Egyptian taxation applies to a non-resident recipient, but treaty rates may reduce the 10% rate to 5% to 15% depending on shareholding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed and the full tax paid within four months after the fiscal year-end; a calendar-year LLC must file by 30 April of the following year. Extensions apply only with ETA pre-approval and do not suspend interest accrual.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments are due by 30 April, 31 July, 31 October, and 31 January for calendar-year taxpayers, corresponding to the month following each quarter-end.

Line 20 - Total tax credits and final CIT payable Aggregation of quarterly provisional instalments, foreign tax credits for treaty countries, and withholding-credit balances, followed by application of the 22.5% statutory rate and computation of the balance payable. Any dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders must be cross-referenced in the WHT annex, and the 10% dividend distribution tax must be declared and paid by the LLC before remittance. Late filing triggers a progressive fine of EGP 1,000 to EGP 20,000 plus an additional tax of 2% per month or fraction on the unpaid CIT. Late payment incurs the same 2% monthly surcharge with automatic ETA collection, bank garnishment, and suspension of tax clearance certificates. Persistent non-filing may result in deemed assessment and criminal referral.
QRTR-Box 1 - Instalment period and tax base indicator Declaration of the quarter reference period and the provisional computation basis selected by the LLC. Non-resident shareholders have no direct instalment obligation, but the LLC must fund instalments from operating cash flow before effecting external dividend distributions. Underpayment of quarterly instalments is subject to the 2% monthly additional tax on the shortfall, and repeated missed instalments trigger ETA enforcement action against the LLC's bank accounts and administrative attachment of the licensed office equipment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Egyptian TP guidelines, a Local File is mandatory when controlled transactions with any single foreign related party exceed EGP 8,000,000 in a fiscal year; a Master File is required when aggregate controlled transactions exceed EGP 40,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting applies where the multinational group consolidated revenue is at least EGP 12,000,000,000. A TP disclosure annex must be filed with the annual CIT return even below the documentation thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the ETA applies a substance-over-form anti-abuse principle under Article 30 of Income Tax Law No. 91/2005, a beneficial-ownership test for treaty relief, reinforced AML KYC requirements, and TP documentation rules. A mail-scanning CMRA must maintain licensed premises, engaged local staff, actual client contracts, an operational bank account, and independent decision-making records; a letterbox-only arrangement without staff or local management is exposed to denial of tax clearance, treaty benefits, and commercial-registration revocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Additional tax of 2% per month on unpaid amounts, penal assessment of 100% to 200% of the evaded tax, criminal fines up to EGP 1,000,000, and imprisonment of up to five years under Law No. 91/2005 as amended by Law No. 51/2023; assets and bank accounts may be attached by the Egyptian Tax Authority.

Director personal liability: Directors and general managers who certified false accounts are jointly and severally liable for unpaid CIT; enforcement courts may issue travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, and place names on the police blacklist under Law No. 25/1996, with imprisonment imposed on officers who engineered fraud.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commercial Registry and Investment Authority may dissolve the LLC, cancel its establishment license, and liquidate its assets; the ETA may re-assess taxable profits on a deemed-turnover basis and impose the 22.5% CIT with 2% monthly surcharges; fictitious-company provisions of Companies Law No. 159/1981 permit nullification of the entity.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil when the LLC is used as a cover: directors become personally liable for unpaid tax and third-party debts, may be sequestrated or declared bankrupt, and travel bans and asset freezes are routinely issued under the civil enforcement regime.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Egyptian AML Law No. 80/2002 as amended by Law No. 8/2015 imposes imprisonment of up to 15 years and fines of up to EGP 5,000,000 for money laundering participation; FIU Egypt may freeze assets, the CMRA license is subject to administrative suspension, and the ETA may coordinate criminal seizure of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign hosting agreements, approve KYC bypasses, or fail to submit required reporting and suspicious-transaction reports bear criminal liability jointly with the LLC and are exposed to travel bans, personal account freezes, and prolonged investigation detention.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may adjust taxable income to an arm's-length result, apply the 22.5% CIT on the adjustment, add 2% monthly delay surcharges, and impose documentation penalties under the Egyptian TP regulations; the assessment period is extended for non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: The general manager who signed inflated intercompany invoices may be made personally liable for the resulting tax deficiency under the doctrine of corporate waste; repeated TP misstatements additionally expose directors to criminal referrals for false bookkeeping.
