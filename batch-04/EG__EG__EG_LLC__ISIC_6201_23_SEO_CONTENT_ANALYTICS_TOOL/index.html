Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; official portal https://www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing clearance portal https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) incorporate and obtain an Arabic-notarized and apostilled certificate of incorporation and board resolution; (2) register for a tax card (statutory TIN) with the ETA via the unified TAX platform or the competent tax office, submitting the entity contract, legal representative POA, Egyptian registered address, and bank account evidence; (3) mandatorily register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 if annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000, or voluntarily register to claim input VAT and export refunds; (4) onboard to the ETA e-invoicing system and obtain the unique ETA registration certificate and QR/API credentials; non-resident LLCs without a fixed establishment but deriving Egyptian-source income must also register for tax and file under the withholding regime.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax at the statutory rate of 22.5% on worldwide net taxable income, with no pass-through to shareholders; distributions to foreign shareholders may trigger additional withholding tax. — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) incorporate and obtain an Arabic-notarized and apostilled certificate of incorporation and board resolution; (2) register for a tax card (statutory TIN) with the ETA via the unified TAX platform or the competent tax office, submitting the entity contract, legal representative POA, Egyptian registered address, and bank account evidence; (3) mandatorily register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 if annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000, or voluntarily register to claim input VAT and export refunds; (4) onboard to the ETA e-invoicing system and obtain the unique ETA registration certificate and QR/API credentials; non-resident LLCs without a fixed establishment but deriving Egyptian-source income must also register for tax and file under the withholding regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax at the statutory rate of 22.5% on worldwide net taxable income, with no pass-through to shareholders; distributions to foreign shareholders may trigger additional withholding tax.

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; LLC income is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available statutory mechanisms: (1) voluntary VAT registration for exporters below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (2) VAT group treatment for closely related exporting entities; (3) consolidated income tax filing for Egyptian groups with ownership of at least 90%; (4) small-taxpayer simplified regime for annual turnover below EGP 10,000,000; (5) foreign tax credit elections and treaty treaty-based relief applications; Egypt has not enacted OECD hybrid mismatch legislation, so no domestic hybrid-mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies for mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration is permitted for exporters and zero-rated suppliers; the e-invoicing mandate applies to all VAT-registered taxpayers

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 16 of the Egyptian VAT Law applies to: (1) goods physically exported outside Egypt and supported by official customs export documentation; (2) services, including MarTech SaaS and SEO analytics subscriptions, supplied to a non-resident recipient and wholly consumed outside Egypt, provided the place of supply is treated as outside Egypt and the consideration is received in foreign currency through a licensed Egyptian bank; (3) international transport services and ancillary services; the exporter must maintain a complete evidentiary file and clear the invoice through the ETA e-invoicing platform.

  • ETA-cleared export e-invoice with export code, QR validation, and full detail of the SaaS/SEO analytics service scope
  • Signed commercial contract or subscription agreement with the non-resident customer evidencing offshore place of supply and foreign-currency consideration
  • Customs export declaration with matching export invoice (required when any hardware, data pipelines, or physical media are bundled with the software service)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export of any bundled goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a licensed Egyptian bank evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Central Bank of Egypt export proceeds confirmation/repatriation statement and annual reconciliation listing

The ETA operates a real-time fiscal clearance e-invoicing and e-receipt regime; every business-to-business invoice and relevant receipt must be transmitted in JSON/XML to the ETA clearance platform before issuance, validated digitally, and stamped with a QR code and unique UUID; integration must use the ETA REST APIs from certified ERP/accounting systems; export zero-rated invoices must include the export indicator and supporting evidence codes, and invoices not cleared by the ETA are fiscally invalid and may be disallowed for input credit and refund purposes.

ETA audit teams scrutinize zero-rating claims through invoice-level reconciliation, bank FX certificates, and customs records; fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating triggers reassessment of VAT at the standard 14% rate on the gross consideration, a monthly delay penalty of 2% on unpaid VAT until full settlement, administrative fines up to EGP 500,000, retrospective clawback of refunded amounts with a 100% surcharge, suspension of refund rights for up to five years, and imprisonment penalties of up to five years for tax evasion under Article 86 of Law No. 67 of 2016 as amended.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed float regime for the EGP. Current account transactions are generally freely convertible and supported by banks, but capital account transactions require CBE approval or registration. Profit and dividend remittances are permissible after corporate tax settlement and audited financial statements, but may encounter administrative delays and prioritization of essential imports during periods of FX scarcity. In practice, full convertibility for services/SaaS revenue is subject to CBE circulars and bank-specific compliance filters.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated into Egypt within the statutory period (generally up to 90 days from the date of shipment or notification, with limited extensions by the CBE). Proceeds are required to be sold or converted into EGP in the local banking market in full unless a specific exemption or non-surrender allowance is granted under CBE Circular No. 38 of 2023 and related instructions, which impose mandatory surrender ratios of up to 100% for most exported services, including technology and SEO content analytics tools.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 as amended, and the Foreign Exchange Control regulations issued by the CBE

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMI-only banking. Offshore electronic money institutions and virtual IBAN providers are not licensed or supervised by the CBE. They cannot maintain settlement accounts in Egypt or provide commercial banking services to an Egyptian LLC without obtaining a CBE branch or e-payment license. Foreign EMIs may only serve as informational invoicing tools, but all actual EGP and FX settlement must occur through CBE-licensed banks. Use of non-compliant offshore EMI rails creates AML and exchange-control violation risks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for an Egyptian LLC must be routed through a CBE-licensed bank in the name of the Egyptian company. The bank will require a board resolution, shareholder invoice or contribution statement, a purpose code aligned with CBE's Balance of Payments manual for foreign direct investment (FDI), and proof of corporate existence. The receiving bank will map the wire to capital suspense, then convert or register the contribution in EGP following CBE rules. SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank is the standard channel. Verification includes KYC/QI checks and registration with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or banks must register the inward remittance via CBE's Electronic Reporting System.Trade disbursements and international software-related vendor payments are executed through CBE-licensed banks using SWIFT MT103 for open-account foreign currency payments. For high-value or high-risk transactions, commercial letters of credit (MT700) may be required, especially where goods or services involve Egyptian import and tax documentation. Outbound payments require underlying contracts, commercial invoices, tax registration (tax card), taxpayer identification, and evidence of withholding tax compliance. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% headline corporate income tax (CIT) rate for resident LLCs under Law No. 91 of 2005; non-resident branch profits additionally attract a 10% branch remittance tax on repatriated after-tax profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions by Egyptian resident LLCs to shareholders (Article 52, Law No. 91 of 2005); the rate applies on the gross dividend and is retained and remitted by the LLC to the ETA.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form No. 21 must be filed within four months after the end of the tax year (Article 87, Law No. 91 of 2005); for a calendar-year LLC, the deadline is April 30 of the following year; any tax payable is due simultaneously with the return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 aggregate annual related-party transaction value; exceeding this ETA threshold mandates the preparation and submission of a comprehensive Transfer Pricing Disclosure with the annual CIT return, plus full Local File and Master File documentation prepared contemporaneously; Country-by-Country reporting applies at consolidated group revenue thresholds under Egyptian decrees.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, Law No. 91 of 2005 Article 103 (general anti-abuse rule), the Unified Tax Procedures Law, and the beneficial ownership register requirements impose a comprehensive substance-over-form standard: the LLC must demonstrate active management, real employees, local decision-making, and genuine commercial activity to secure treaty benefits, avoid PE creation, and prevent recharacterization of intercompany arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine equal to at least the amount of tax evaded plus imprisonment under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended; civil recovery of evaded tax, late-payment surcharge of 2% per month, and a further 10% administrative penalty on the understated tax; ETA may seize bank accounts and freeze payment gateways.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and beneficial owners who signed false returns or directed evasion are personally and jointly liable for evaded tax; courts may impose asset attachment, travel bans, aviation and land exit bans, and prison sentences of up to several years, and the corporate veil will be pierced where funds were diverted to owners without legal distributions.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: ETA recharacterization of the entity as a fiscal fiction; full 22.5% CIT imposed on deemed Egyptian-source profit, denial of all notional management fee deductions, disallowance of treaty advantages, and application of Article 103 GAAR with additional 20% adjustment penalty and interest.

Director personal liability: The sole nominee director and non-resident beneficial owner face piercing of the corporate veil where no formal shareholder resolution or arm's-length compensation was recorded; personal civil exposure for the full tax, interest and penalties; criminal liability for knowingly structuring a sham company to escape Egyptian tax.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA TP audit requiring an upward adjustment to the Egyptian entity's taxable income, a 20% accuracy-related penalty on the understated tax, late-payment interest of 2% per month, and potential re-qualification of royalty payments as hidden profit distributions subject to 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: CFO and directors who approved defective TP documentation may be held liable for the additional tax and penalties under the responsible-officer doctrine; in flagrant cases of deliberate transfer of profits, imprisonment and travel restrictions apply alongside personal restitution orders.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended) ranging from EGP 50,000 to EGP 500,000 for UBO registration violations; criminal sanctions of up to seven years' imprisonment for participating in concealment, money laundering or financing arrangements, plus confiscation of the LLC's assets and closure of local bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and the compliance officer bear personal criminal liability for failure to file UBO disclosures; travel bans and asset freezes are routinely imposed during investigation; piercing of the corporate veil permits recovery of laundered funds from directors' personal estates where corporate records are adjudged artificially manipulated.
