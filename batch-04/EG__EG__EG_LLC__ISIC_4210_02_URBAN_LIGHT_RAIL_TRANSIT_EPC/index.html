Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Egyptian LLC is a juridical person and full Egyptian tax resident taxed on worldwide income under Law 91/2005, as amended by Law 26/2020, at 22.5% corporate income tax on taxable income up to EGP 80 million and 32.5% on the excess; no pass-through or US-style disregarded-entity treatment is available, and free-zone classifications apply only where an explicit GAFI/SCZone investment license is granted.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box election; juridical classification is fixed by statute. Available elections include: (i) fiscal-year election — adoption of any 12-month tax period with ETA approval, defaulting to the calendar year; (ii) SME presumptive tax regime election under Law 152/2020 for annual turnover not exceeding EGP 20 million, replacing corporation tax, stamp duties, and public levies with turnover-based brackets; (iii) free-zone/SCZone license election substituting 0% corporate income tax during the licensed period for an annual board fee in foreign currency; (iv) unincorporated joint-venture election under Law 91/2005 to treat jointly-owned project structures as a single taxable unit; and (v) no hybrid-mismatch organized election — Egypt's CFC rules and general anti-avoidance clause override artificial arrangements lacking economic substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies of goods or services triggering mandatory VAT registration under Law 67/2016 and Executive Regulations; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, and non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in Egypt must register and appoint an Egyptian tax representative

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT under Law 67/2016 applies to: (i) export of goods — title passes to a non-resident buyer, goods physically leave Egypt under a valid customs export declaration within 180 days from the invoice date, and proceeds are credited to an Egyptian authorized bank in freely convertible foreign currency; and (ii) exported services — services supplied to a non-resident, performed and effectively used outside Egypt, and covered by the Executive Regulations' list of zero-rated offshore services (including engineering, design, telecommunication, transportation, and ancillary logistics). For the Urban Light Rail Transit EPC scope, only separable offshore design-engineering and offshore procurement segments may qualify for 0%; on-site track laying, catenary installation, and local construction within Egypt remain standard-rated at 14%, so each zero-rated invoice must be traceable to a distinct offshore work-package in the EPC contract.

  • Customs export declaration (ECS/C88 document) stamped by the Egyptian Customs Authority evidencing physical exit of the goods from Egypt within the statutory 180-day window
  • Original Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note consigned to the non-resident buyer and cross-referenced to the invoice, contract, and customs export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT/telex credit advice from an authorized Egyptian bank evidencing receipt and conversion of the export proceeds in freely convertible foreign currency

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing applies to VAT-registered businesses under ETA Decree No. 420/2020 and subsequent amendments. Every tax invoice must be issued through the ETA centralized clearance platform or a certified ERP-to-API integration; the ETA validates invoice data in near real time and returns a digital stamp comprising a unique invoice UUID and QR code, which is embedded before issuance to the buyer. Un-cleared invoices are legally invalid and the transaction is treated as undocumented; since 2022, the parallel B2C e-receipt system is mandatory in phased waves. System non-compliance results in blocked invoice issuance, fines under the VAT law, and referral to the ETA audit division.

The ETA audits export files within the five-year assessment period. If the customs exit proof, shipping document, or FX inward remittance is defective or the 180-day repatriation deadline is missed, the 0% rate is reversed retroactively and 14% output VAT is assessed from the original invoice date, plus a delay penalty of 1.5% per month on the unpaid tax (18% per annum). Where export documentation is fabricated or input VAT has been recovered on a false export claim, the conduct qualifies as VAT evasion under Article 72 of Law 67/2016, carrying imprisonment and fines of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000 in addition to full clawback of credited input VAT; the Income Tax Law adds an additional charge of up to 200% of the evaded tax on falsified export declarations, and the Central Bank of Egypt may suspend the authorized bank's export-processing privileges for foreign-exchange rule breaches.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains Article VIII current-account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Agreement; the EGP was fully floated on 6 March 2024 under the CBE exchange-rate unification decision, removing former conversion queues and documentary rationing. Dividend and profit remittance by a Limited Liability Company is legally guaranteed and non-discretionary, but operationally conditioned on (i) audited financial statements prepared under Egyptian GAAP or IFRS, (ii) a tax clearance certificate issued by the Egyptian Tax Authority, and (iii) availability of EGP funds at a CBE-licensed remitting bank; no separate CBE approval is required for current-account profit transfers below the statutory reporting threshold. Capital-account outflows, including shareholder loan principal and equity liquidation proceeds, require proof that the underlying inward transaction was registered with the CBE or the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) at entry to evidence legal repatriability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The mandatory export-proceeds surrender regime was abolished by the CBE exchange-rate unification circular of 6 March 2024; no fixed conversion ratio or statutory surrender window currently applies to export receipts. Exporters must nevertheless credit proceeds to an exporter's foreign-currency account at a licensed Egyptian bank within the settlement tenor stated in the underlying commercial terms, normally 30 to 60 days from the bill of lading, via SWIFT transfer, and must support the receipt within five business days with the Nafeza/ACID customs export declaration and commercial invoice so that the bank can file the CBE export-proceeds report. Failure to credit proceeds within the agreed letter-of-credit or Incoterms period triggers CBE irregular-settlement reporting and may disqualify the entity from access to import financing facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary authority and foreign exchange regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), exercising exchange controls through its General Department of Foreign Exchange and the Banking Supervision AML/CFT Unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs regulated in the UK, EU, or Singapore are not recognised by the CBE as settlement banks in Egypt; using an unlicensed EMI to hold the LLC's operating liquidity would breach the Banking Law deposit-taking monopoly and the payment-fintech licensing regime of Law No. 5 of 2022 unless the EMI operates through an Egyptian-registered subsidiary holding a CBE licence. Commercial viability is LOW as a primary clearing rail: acceptable only as an ancillary payment-card or virtual-IBAN receiving tool, while all EPC contract settlement, government bond escrow flows, and CBE balance-of-payments-visible liquidity must reside at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections must be wired by SWIFT MT103 from the parent to the LLC's account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, or by MT202 for interbank reallocation, quoting the GAFI registration number and the bank customer-information file reference; the receiving bank credits the foreign-currency sub-account or converts the principal to EGP at the prevailing interbank market rate. The bank retains the inward SWIFT reference, issues the CBE investment-transfer form as proof of conversion, and reports values above USD 100,000 or EGP equivalent to the CBE through its electronic Balance-of-Payments reporting interface to establish the repatriation file. Government bond escrow receipts funded by the Egyptian state counterparty under the EPC concession are settled directly on the statutory escrow bank ledger, typically at NBE or Banque Misr, and are confirmed to the LLC and any senior lender through a bank-to-bank SWIFT advice.Outbound supplier settlements are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the Egyptian bank under a back-to-back documentary facility, or under a commercial letter of credit governed by UCP600 and confirmed through a foreign correspondent using the MT700/MT798 messaging chain; the bank requires proforma invoices, the ACID/Nafeza customs import declaration, and for plant and equipment the relevant import-financing notices before releasing hard currency. Dividend and profit repatriation to foreign shareholders follows the same MT103 rail after delivery of the tax clearance certificate and audited financial statements, while share-capital repatriation additionally requires the historical CBE registration evidence of the original inward injection and proof of full settlement of Egyptian taxes. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% unified corporate income tax on net taxable income of juridical persons under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended by Law No. 26 of 2020.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders under Article 111 of Law No. 91 of 2005, applied after CIT at the distributing LLC level. Resident corporate shareholders that own at least 25% for two years qualify for the domestic participation exemption. Non-resident shareholders are not entitled to the domestic exemption unless a treaty grants a lower rate.

Submission and full settlement within the four months following the fiscal year-end under Article 46 of Law No. 91 of 2005. For a 31 December balance-sheet date, the deadline is 30 April. A reasoned 60-day extension may be requested from the competent ETA directorate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 of aggregate related-party transactions within a fiscal year triggers the mandatory TP annexure and Local File preparation. An MNE group with consolidated turnover above EGP 200,000,000 must also prepare a Master File. The ETA may lower the thresholds on a risk-based selection for rail-infrastructure contractors.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies the OECD-BEPS substance-over-form doctrine through Article 36 GAAR of Law No. 91 of 2005 and the MLI Principal Purpose Test. A non-resident controlling the Egyptian EPC LLC must demonstrate real business presence, qualified engineers, site-supervision capacity, equipment ownership, insurable risk, and strategic decision-making in the treaty state. Egypt does not maintain a codified ESR return akin to the UAE/GCC regimes, but GAFI and the ETA require substantive premises, payroll, and bank activity when issuing tax cards, treaty clearances, or Investment Law incentives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent EPC Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Criminal imprisonment of 3 months to 5 years and a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 50,000 under Article 134 of Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended. The ETA issues a full re-assessment for the evaded tax, plus the 2%-per-month deferment surcharge and CBE+2% interest. The court may order publication of the conviction at the taxpayer's expense.

Director personal liability: Directors, signatories, and the resident representative are treated as principals and are jointly and severally liable for the evaded CIT and civil damages. The public prosecutor may impose a travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts, and, under Egyptian Penal Code veil-piercing rules, attach personal assets where the LLC is an alter ego of the accused.
Shell Company / DTA Treaty Abuse / GAAR

Statutory sanction: ETA application of Article 36 GAAR and the MLI Principal Purpose Test: recharacterization of all flows as taxable dividends or royalties, denial of treaty benefits, additional CIT at 22.5%, surcharge, and interest. GAFI may strike off the commercial registration, and the entity is debarred from public infrastructure tenders for up to 3 years. Administrative fines up to EGP 500,000 apply for non-disclosure of real beneficial owners.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face criminal prosecution for false declarations before GAFI and the ETA. Directors are civilly liable for the full revenue adjustment and legal costs. A travel ban and curatorship over the company bank account are imposed where the court concludes the structure exists solely for tax avoidance.
Anti-Money Laundering / Unlicensed Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Law No. 80 of 2002, as amended by Law No. 75 of 2023: imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to EGP 5,000,000. The court may order confiscation of the serviced funds, cancellation of the LLC's commercial registration, blacklisting from the Egyptian financial market, and loss of VAT/CIT registration privileges for 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer are personally subject to imprisonment and travel bans. Personal assets traceable to the laundering offense are subject to preventive seizure. Directors cannot transfer, encumber, or alienate property listed in the EMLCU freeze order until final acquittal.
Transfer Pricing Failure / PE Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA makes an upward adjustment to the arm's-length net profit and recharacterizes the excess as a deemed distribution subject to 10% WHT. The resulting additional tax attracts a 2%-per-month surcharge and CBE+2% interest. Defective documentation allows the ETA to substitute its own comparability benchmark and impose a fixed penalty of up to EGP 200,000.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and expatriate site director are personally liable under Article 71 of Law No. 91 of 2005 for the unpaid tax balance. Courts may issue a travel ban against the general manager during the audit. Civil damages for the State's lost tax revenue lie jointly against the foreign parent's appointing directors, and repeated failure triggers criminal sanction under Article 134.
