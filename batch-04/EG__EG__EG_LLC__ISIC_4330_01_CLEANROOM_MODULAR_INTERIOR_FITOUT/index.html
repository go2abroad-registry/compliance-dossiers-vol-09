Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum or maximum paid-up capital is imposed on LLCs under Law 159/1981; the founders fix the capital in the Articles of Association and divide it into equal quota shares. GAFI OSS practice requires prior deposit of the cash quotas (typically at least 50%, or 100% if so stated in the Articles) into a provisional escrow account at a licensed Egyptian bank; the bank's deposit certificate is filed with GAFI and released upon issuance of the incorporation certificate. Non-cash quotas must be fully valued in kind by a sworn certified auditor before filing.

Funding & unlock path: Egyptian Civil Defense Certificate (Iftitah) required for the company headquarters, storage yard, and each project mobilization site, verifying fire suppression systems and exit compliance. Structural and load inspection certificate must be issued by a licensed structural engineering office confirming that the header, partition anchors, and suspended ceiling grid support the required cleanroom loads. Ministry of Manpower occupational safety and health clearance under Labor Law 12 of 2003 is mandatory before any workforce deployment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt; central digital portal https://www.eta.gov.eg and dedicated electronic invoicing/clearance platform https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned LLC must first complete incorporation at the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Egyptian Companies Registry, then open a tax dossier at the ETA head-office revenue branch by submitting the duly notarised articles of association, commercial registration extract, board resolutions and powers of attorney certifying the legal representative, valid passports/IDs of directors, and proof of the registered Egyptian address and local bank account. The ETA will issue the unified nine-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) and the Tax Card. VAT registration is integrated within the same registration dossier once annual taxable turnover reaches or is expected to exceed EGP 500,000; a non-resident contractor without an Egyptian permanent establishment may nonetheless obtain a limited TIN for VAT, withholding and refund purposes, subject to appointing a resident tax representative and filing an ETA-registration form with supporting constitutional documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election for LLCs; an LLC is mandatorily a taxable juridical person under the corporate income tax (Law 91 of 2005, standard CIT rate 22.5%). Available statutory elections are limited to: (i) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 statutory threshold; (ii) the VAT margin scheme on eligible second-hand goods; (iii) an election in the annual return as to the formula for crediting foreign income taxes under Article 55 of Law 91 of 2005, with any excess credit carried forward four years; (iv) a 'company of persons' (general or limited partnership) is tax-transparent only when every partner is a natural person, while inclusion of a corporate partner makes entity-level taxation compulsory; and (v) a licensed free-zone enterprise may, with joint ETA/GAFI approval, elect to exit the free-zone gross-revenue tax (5% export-orientated, 10% general services, 15% domestic sales) and enter the standard CIT regime at the applicable transition valuation date.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies in Egypt; mandatory registration above this threshold, voluntary registration below it, and mandatory registration without threshold for importers and for non-resident suppliers making supplies through an Egyptian agent or local intermediary.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to tangible goods supplied to a non-resident buyer where title passes abroad and the goods physically leave Egyptian customs territory within 90 days of invoice date. Services and construction/fit-out works physically performed on immovable property located in Egypt do not qualify for export zero-rating even where the customer is a non-resident; cleanroom modular fit-out contracting executed in Egypt will therefore normally attract the 14% domestic VAT. Zero-rating remains available for services supplied to and wholly consumed outside Egypt by a non-resident recipient, and for goods/services delivered to licensed free-zone or Suez Canal Economic Zone establishments treated as outside the Egyptian VAT territory, subject to explicit ETA pre-approval of each zero-rated transaction together with customs and foreign-currency evidence.

  • Customs export declaration (Bayan Shaka / Single Administrative Document) stamped and certified by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or international airway bill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Egyptian customs territory within the statutory 90-day window
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice from a licensed Egyptian bank confirming receipt of the export proceeds in freely convertible foreign currency
  • Original sales invoice, underlying export contract, and the ETA prior approval decision recording the zero-rate application

All VAT-registered taxpayers must connect to the ETA electronic invoicing platform using an approved ERP/API integration. Each invoice is transmitted in real time as an ETA-compliant JSON file, validated against the ETA's schema, assigned a unique UUID and clearance response; the printed invoice must carry the ETA-issued QR code. The same real-time clearance mechanism applies to e-receipts for B2C supplies. An uncleared invoice is legally invalid for input-VAT deduction and exposes the issuer to per-invoice penalties of up to EGP 50,000, suspension of refund processing, and administrative blockage of the taxpayer's VAT clearance profile.

ETA audit of export zero-rating and refund claims carries a five-year lookback period, extended to ten years in cases of alleged fraud. A rejected or inflated zero-rating claim is retroactively clawed back with late-payment interest at the Central Bank of Egypt discount rate plus two percentage points per month of delay and an additional penalty up to 100% of the evaded tax. Criminal exposure under Law 67 of 2016 includes imprisonment and fines in the range of EGP 20,000 to EGP 50,000, in addition to loss of refund standing, temporary de-registration for e-invoicing, attachment of bank accounts, and publication of the enforcement decision by the ETA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime for the Egyptian pound (EGP). Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, and branch profits) are freely convertible subject to standard supporting documentation and applicable bank verification. Capital-account transactions, including inbound direct equity injections and shareholder loans, require registration with the CBE under the official investment/licensing framework to secure future repatriation safeguards. Profit remittances are permitted for locally registered LLCs after filing annual audited financial statements, paying applicable corporate tax (22.5% standard, 22.5% for most contracting entities), and obtaining a tax-clearance certificate; no additional currency conversion levy applies. Non-resident capital gains and dividend distributions remain repatriable through the banking system provided that source funds were lawfully admitted and evidenced. Practical hurdles include periodic FX liquidity tightness, manual documentary scrutiny at the receiving bank, and the need for prior CBE regulatory clearance on large-dollar capital transfers. A cleanroom fit-out contractor engaged on cross-border EPC and LC-backed trade will find routine current-account payments relatively smooth, but capital-account operations warrant pre-clearance and a qualified local compliance advisor.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the current liberalised settlement framework, Egypt has no mandatory percentage surrender of export proceeds into EGP; exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in a foreign-currency account at an Egyptian licensed bank. Statutory requirement: export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt and credited to the exporter's bank account within 180 calendar days from the bill-of-lading / air-waybill date, unless the CBE-approved bank provides an extension for justified transit, installation, or sovereign-offset projects. Exporters must deposit into either a regular foreign-currency account or an export-proceeds escrow sub-account; conversion to EGP is voluntary at the prevailing market rate. All export documentation (sales contract, invoice, airway bill, customs declaration, and bank instruction) must be submitted to the handling bank within the statutory timeline; failure to comply subjects the exporter to fines under Egypt's exchange-control penal code, and the handling bank is obligated to report overdue collections to the CBE. For cleanroom modular interior fit-out contractors exporting prefabricated wall panels, HEPA filter components, and modular cleanroom modules, the export proceeds should be settled via sight drafts or LC collections and credited through the CBE-authorised correspondent network.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Egyptian Exchange Control Authority and Monetary Authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and digital banks operating without a CBE licence are not permitted to provide deposit-taking, payment-account, or cross-border settlement services to Egyptian resident entities. The CBE's Payment Systems Law (Law No. 18 of 2019) mandates that any entity issuing electronic money, maintaining transaction accounts, or providing payment services must hold a valid CBE licence and be established in Egypt. Offshore EMIs may be used only as auxiliary receiving gateways for inbound remittances if the ultimate clearing passes through an Egyptian licensed bank; however, direct corporate treasury reliance on such vehicles is not viable for tax, LC, or export-proceeds compliance. A pragmatic strategy is to open a primary account with a Tier-1 Egyptian bank and use that bank's digital business-banking portal for cross-border wire initiation; any fintech partner must be CBE-licensed or expressly acting as an agent of a CBE-licensed bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident UBO structure with offshore intermediate holding entities

Root cause: Non-resident UBO structure with offshore intermediate holding entities

Defense evidence: Provide a full organisational chart showing each entity from ultimate beneficial owner to Egyptian LLC; certified copies of constitutional documents; beneficial-ownership declaration; and notarised board resolutions from each entity in the chain. Obtain a UAE or Cayman/Aden-based independent audit confirmatory letter, as applicable, and ensure all intermediate entities are in good standing with current licenses. Attach a senior-management sworn affidavit explaining the commercial rationale for the offshore structure in relation to cleanroom project financing and asset protection.
Source-of-funds and source-of-wealth inquiry for capital injection

Root cause: Source-of-funds and source-of-wealth inquiry for capital injection

Defense evidence: Submit audited group financial statements for the past three fiscal years; bank statements of the funding shareholder for at least six months; copies of executed investment agreements and the Egyptian LLC's share-purchase or equity-subscription documents; evidence of the underlying cleanroom fit-out contract or signed LOI; and a detailed capital deployment plan showing the exact use of the injected funds for imported HEPA filters, steel partitions, clean-air HVAC equipment, and project payroll. If funds originate from a trade consortium, provide the consortium agreement and project-specific escrow confirmation.
Exposure to sanctioned jurisdictions in the cleanroom supply chain (e.g., dual-use equipment or controlled-technical items)

Root cause: Exposure to sanctioned jurisdictions in the cleanroom supply chain (e.g., dual-use equipment or controlled-technical items)

Defense evidence: Prepare a complete trade-compliance dossier listing all equipment HS codes, country-of-origin, manufacturer certifications, and end-user certificates for the cleanroom build-out; obtain the final end-user and end-use statement under the Egyptian Ministry of Trade directives; secure a legal opinion from Egyptian counsel confirming the supplies are not subject to Egyptian or OFAC-administered sanctions unless an applicable general licence authorises the transaction; and provide factory-specific compliance certificates from equipment vendors.
Dependency on related-party material procurement from a parent company

Root cause: Dependency on related-party material procurement from a parent company

Defense evidence: Provide an advance pricing agreement or transfer pricing study showing arm's-length mark-ups on fabricated cleanroom walls and prefabricated modular components; prepare intercompany procurement contracts with payment-milestone schedules matching the LC or bank-transfer terms; and submit evidence of the ultimate use of the materials under the Egyptian custom-clearance and tax registration.
LC or bank-payment splitting to circumvent documentary requirements

Root cause: LC or bank-payment splitting to circumvent documentary requirements

Defense evidence: Maintain a central treasury register mapping every inbound and outbound wire to the underlying commercial contract and LC reference; ensure no single payment is split into sub-threshold amounts intentionally; provide monthly tax and customs filings showing the consistency of declared values; and implement an independent compliance audit protocol under Egyptian AML Law No. 80 of 2002 to prevent red-flag indicators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a foreign shareholder injecting equity or sub-ordinated shareholder-loan capital into the Egyptian LLC, the compliant route is a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's foreign bank into the Egyptian LLC's CBE-registered special foreign-currency escrow account, using a purpose-code designation under the CBE Balance-of-Payments classification — typically 'direct investment in Egyptian LLCs (equity share)' or 'private sector borrowing/credit for project finance'. The incoming wire must reference the CBE registration number of the investment, the Egyptian Commercial Registry (tajaree) number, and the tax identification number. The Egyptian receiving bank will require a copy of the signed share-subscription agreement or the facility agreement, a board resolution of the foreign shareholder approving the transfer, and a CBE Form (investment registration form) filed with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI). The funds can then be converted to EGP at the prevailing market rate or retained in foreign currency in a current account. To ensure future repatriation rights, the foreign investor should obtain an Investment Approval Certificate from GAFI and a CBE allotment confirmation letter; any delayed registration after arrival risks forcing the capital injection into an escrow account with restricted disbursement.Scheduled disbursements for imported cleanroom construction materials, HVAC equipment, and subcontractor retainages are best executed through established documentary trade rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 in favour of the foreign supplier for open-account progress payments, subject to presentation of pro-forma invoice, customs declaration, contract compliance, and periodic CBE submission of the Commercial Register invoice; (b) irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC at sight) opened by the Egyptian LLC's local bank for equipment purchases exceeding a defined threshold, with documents including commercial invoice, marine or airway consignment note, clean-on-board certificate, packing list, certificate of origin, and technical test certificates; and (c) for local foreign-owned whisper-suppliers, MT103 direct wires with unique purpose-code 'imports of capital goods under investment' and the requirement that the foreign supplier credits the Egyptian seller's foreign-currency account. All outbound disbursements must be accompanied by a valid e-invoice from the Egyptian Tax Authority under the E-invoice system; failure to link the wire to the e-invoice number triggers automatic documentation withholding at the bank. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% on taxable corporate income for non-banking Limited Liability Companies under Law 91 of 2005 as amended by Law 206 of 2020; banks and branches of foreign banks are subject to 32.5% and the Suez Canal Authority to 40%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by Egyptian companies to non-resident shareholders under Article 56 of Law 91 of 2005; the rate applies to actual distributions approved by the general assembly and to deemed distributions arising from excessive payments to shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form No. 5 is due within four months from the end of the fiscal year (Article 47 of Law 91 of 2005); one additional month is granted where statutorily audited financial statements are mandatory, i.e., an effective maximum deadline of five months from year-end. The fiscal year is normally the calendar year unless a different year-end is approved by the ETA.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian TP Guidelines (Premier Decree 2610 of 2021) impose the following thresholds: (i) Transfer Pricing Disclosure Schedule is mandatory for all taxpayers with related-party transactions irrespective of amount; (ii) a Local File must be prepared and submitted upon ETA request when the aggregate related-party transactions exceed EGP 10 million per transaction category (goods, services, intangibles, financial), or when the taxpayer has transactions with a related party resident in a jurisdiction with a preferential tax regime regardless of value; (iii) the Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EGP 200 million; (iv) the Country-by-Country Report notification is due when the group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EGP 200 million or the parent jurisdiction applies the EUR 750 million equivalency.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. Nevertheless, an economic substance requirement is enforced indirectly through: (i) the general anti-avoidance provision (GAAR) in Article 29 of Law 91 of 2005, which empowers the ETA to disregard transactions that do not reflect genuine commercial activity; (ii) beneficial ownership tests embedded in DTAs; (iii) CFC rules that attribute undistributed income of low-tax foreign entities to Egyptian shareholders; and (iv) the legal requirement that an Egyptian LLC possess real premises, adequate staff, and operational capacity in Egypt to claim treaty benefits and obtain a valid tax card. A cleanroom fit-out contractor operating as a hollow invoice-carrier without site management, engineering staff, inventory, or construction equipment may have its tax benefits denied, its contracts recharacterized as a service fee arrangement with a non-resident principal, and the full Egyptian revenue taxed under deemed PE rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Invoicing and Fabricated Costs

Statutory sanction: Fine equal to 100% of the tax evaded plus the tax itself; additional penalty of 25% for failure to file; ETA re-assessment with a 5-year look-back period; interests at 2% per month on the underpaid tax; criminal proceeding before the Economic Court; the company's tax card is suspended and bank accounts may be frozen; exposure to payment of a further 10% distribution tax if disguised distributions to foreign shareholders are detected.

Director personal liability: Managers and board members of the Egyptian LLC face joint civil liability with the company for the unpaid tax, penalties, and surcharges; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries imprisonment of not less than 3 years and not more than 7 years for the signatories of the false returns; the court may impose a travel ban during investigation and execution, and personal assets of the directors may be attached in restitution of the differential tax.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the Egyptian LLC as a dependent agent PE of the foreign contractor; taxation of the full construction margin in Egypt at 22.5% plus a 10% branch distribution tax; denial of DTAs for the entire revenue stream; penalty up to 100% of the tax understated; mandatory CFC inclusion for the foreign parent; deregistration from the commercial registry and tax card revocation; fines ranging from EGP 50,000 to EGP 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed declarations attesting to the company's activity and employees become personally liable for the social insurance and tax debts of the entity; fraudulent representation of substance to tax authorities triggers criminal liability for false statement; period bans on serving as directors in Egypt and seizure of personal residences and rental proceeds from the concealed true contractor.
Disguised Profit Distribution and Improper Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of all such payments as deemed dividends subject to 10% WHT, or as royalties subject to 20% WHT; additional 20% tax on the disallowed expenses of the Egyptian company; late payment surcharge of 2% per month; monetary penalty equal to 100% of the evaded WHT; inability to recover VAT on fake service invoices; blacklisting of the auditor who certified the artificial transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the payment of fake fees may be held jointly liable for the withholding tax which the company failed to remit; travel ban and asset freeze under the Enforcement Law against the managing director; criminal conviction for complicity in tax evasion if the payments were known to be disguised distributions; personal restitution equal to the amount of corporate funds siphoned off to non-residents.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Trade-Based Financial Crimes

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EGP 1,000,000 per violation of the AML Law; freezing of bank accounts and funds tied to the suspected transaction; confiscation of the proceeds of the alleged crime; disqualification from public procurement and SCZONE benefits; negative regulatory reporting to the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU); possible cancellation of the company's establishment license.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly execute or approve trade-based money laundering structures face imprisonment of up to 5 years; personal liability for restitution of laundered amounts; prohibition from holding directorships in any Egyptian joint-stock or LLC; travel ban imposed by the public prosecutor; extradition exposure where the funds cross Egyptian borders.
Non-Compliance with Withholding and Advance Payment Obligations

Statutory sanction: Late remittance penalties of EGP 10,000 per month plus 2% of the WHT due; disallowance of the unremitted WHT as a deductible expense; loss of VAT credit on underlying costs; rejection of the annual Form No. 5 until the WHT arrears are settled; sustained non-payment may lead to compulsory collection by administrative attachment against the company's movable assets and bank balances.

Director personal liability: Under the Egyptian Tax Procedures Law, the manager responsible for material withholding duties is jointly liable for the unremitted amounts, personal bank accounts may be garnished, and an administrative director disqualification order may be issued for repeated failure to settle statutory obligations.
