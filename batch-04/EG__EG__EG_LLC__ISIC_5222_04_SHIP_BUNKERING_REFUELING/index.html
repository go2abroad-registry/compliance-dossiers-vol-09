Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), the unified national tax agency (merging the former Income Tax, Sales Tax/VAT and Stamp Tax authorities); official e-portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing/e-receipt platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg with ETA REST API clearance endpoints for real-time fiscal stamping.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC registration procedure: (1) incorporate via the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) one-stop shop to obtain commercial register certificate and the statutory marine bunkering activity license; (2) file Tax Registration Application Form with ETA's central or local registration department within 30 days after commercial registration, attaching the certified memorandum of association, commercial register extract, GAFI license, board resolution, power of attorney and foreign parent details; (3) obtain a single unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) that simultaneously covers Corporate Income Tax, VAT, withholding and stamp tax accounts; (4) on the same TIN, complete mandatory VAT registration where annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000, or voluntary VAT registration where annual supplies exceed EGP 150,000 under VAT Law 67 of 2016 and its Executive Regulations; (5) onboard to ETA's e-invoicing/e-receipt API using an ETA-issued digital certificate, test environment validation, and production clearance credentials before the first taxable bunker supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no US-style check-the-box regime; legal form governs classification, and an LLC is a resident juridical person subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income. Statutory elections available under Law 91 of 2005 include: depreciation and capital-allowance method elections; SME simplified substantive tax regime election for turnover thresholds under Egypt's 2023 unified tax package; Investment Law 72 of 2017 tax-incentive elections for Suez Canal Economic Zone and eligible export-oriented activities; deemed-expense ratio elections for limited-cost logistics and export service providers; and group consolidation is not permitted. Hybrid mismatch exposure is addressed through MLI-aligned substance-over-form rules, controlled-foreign-company provisions, and transfer-pricing documentation rather than elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies triggering mandatory VAT registration; voluntary registration available where annual taxable value exceeds EGP 150,000 (VAT Law 67 of 2016 and Executive Regulations; zero-rated export supplies may be excluded from the general threshold in prescribed circumstances).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Article 30 of VAT Law 67 of 2016 applies to exported goods that physically depart Egypt and to services rendered to non-residents for direct use in international trade or transport. Marine bunker fuel and lubricants delivered to a vessel bound on a foreign voyage qualify as zero-rated export sales where the supply originates from bonded or customs-approved storage, title passes to a non-resident ship owner or operator, the vessel clears Egyptian territorial waters on an international route, and the export declaration is validated by Egyptian Customs. The bunkering service component may also be zero-rated where the recipient is a non-resident carrier and the benefit is consumed outside Egypt. Documentation must be retained for at least five years; excess input VAT refund claims must comply with ETA procedural deadlines and reconciliation with e-invoice clearance data.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document) released via Egypt's NAFIZA customs single window and stamped by Egyptian Customs for the specific bonded bunker lot
  • Bunker Delivery Note / quantity tally certified by the receiving vessel's master or chief engineer with port authority stamp
  • Bill of Lading, cargo manifest, or port clearance certificate evidencing the vessel's departure to a foreign destination on an international voyage
  • Sales contract and commercial invoice in freely convertible currency identifying the non-resident buyer and the vessel's IMO number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank confirming receipt of export proceeds attributable to the matched bunker supply shipment

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing: all B2B tax invoices must be transmitted via ETA REST API in standard JSON/XML schema and digitally cleared or stamped by ETA before issuance to the buyer; uncleared invoices are fiscally invalid and deny input VAT recovery. B2C e-receipts, applicable to retail bunkering and ancillary marine-store sales, must be posted to ETA immediately after the point of sale with an ETA-generated fiscal QR code. Marine bunkering operators must integrate the ETA digital-signing engine with ERP, terminal-weighbridge, bunker-delivery and metering systems, retain ETA receipt tokens and QR stamps in the statutory archive, and align their invoice clearing flow with customs export declarations under NAFIZA.

Egyptian Tax Authority compliance audits cross-match export declarations under NAFIZA, cleared e-invoice XML, port logs, bunker delivery tallies, and bank inward remittance records. Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating triggers: retrospective VAT clawback at the 14% standard rate on the contested supplies, disallowance of related input VAT credits on bunker fuel purchases, late-payment surcharges of not less than 2% per month of the assessed tax, administrative fines ranging from EGP 20,000 to EGP 1,000,000 under Article 75 of VAT Law 67 of 2016, and criminal imprisonment of up to five years for tax-evasion where fraudulent intent is established; responsible directors and managers may be held jointly liable for assessed arrears.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP is partially convertible. Current-account trade and shipping transactions, including marine fuel invoices issued to foreign-flagged vessels, are generally settled through CBE-authorised banks upon presentation of supporting documentation. Capital-account operations, including inbound equity contributions, shareholder loans, dividend remittances, and liquidation transfers, require an authorised Egyptian bank, CBE registration, and underlying corporate approval. Profit remittances are permitted only after audited financial statements, tax clearance, source-of-funds verification, and a valid inward-transfer certificate. USD-denominated bunkering revenue may be retained onshore in foreign-currency accounts, but conversion into EGP must take place at the prevailing authorised-bank market rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Marine fuel export proceeds must be repatriated to Egypt through an authorised bank within 90 days of the bunker delivery receipt or bill of lading date, unless a documentary credit or ship-owner settlement schedule expressly approved by the bank provides another maturity. No fixed statutory conversion ratio is currently imposed under Egypt's liberalised exchange control regime; the exporter may retain up to 100% of USD proceeds in an onshore CBE-authorised foreign-currency account. Under temporary macro-prudential circulars, the CBE may require first-offer of export USD to the authorised bank, but conversion is at market rate and not by a fixed surrender percentage. Every export consignment must be supported by the bunker supply contract, the original BDN, the Egyptian customs manifest where applicable, the commercial invoice, and the bank's foreign-currency inflow certificate.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Foreign Exchange Control Department and Banking Supervision & AML Sector

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury custody and legally insufficient for capital escrow. Egyptian regulations require all in-country corporate capital, export proceeds, and operating collections to pass through a CBE-authorised bank. An offshore digital EMI is not recognised for Egyptian tax clearance, cannot issue an Egyptian IBAN, and cannot receive export-proceeds accreditation. The only feasible use is a supplementary collection rail for third-country bunkering payments, immediately forwarded by SWIFT MT103 to the Egyptian operating account; the full documentary trail must still be submitted to the Egyptian bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% (standard corporate income tax rate for commercial enterprises under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended by Law No. 23 of 2023; banks and similar financial institutions are subject to a 27.0% rate for the 2023–2024 period; the reduced rate applies to tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2023).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10.0% (final Egyptian withholding tax on dividends distributed by an Egyptian-resident LLC to a non-resident shareholder, per Article 58 of Law No. 91 of 2005; no additional Egyptian 'secondary' tax is levied on the repatriation; distributions to Egyptian-resident corporate shareholders may be exempt under the participation regime when the participation threshold and holding period are satisfied).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form No. 10 is due within 4 months after the fiscal year-end (for a 31 December year-end: 30 April of the following year). Quarterly advance tax installments are due on the last day of the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th months of the tax year. The return must be signed by the legal representative (LLC manager) and the licensed tax/certified accountant.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory when the Egyptian taxpayer's aggregate related-party transactions reach EGP 10,000,000 per tax year (per ETA Decree No. 537 of 2019 and the Egyptian Transfer Pricing Guidelines); Master File: mandatory when the MNE group's worldwide consolidated revenue reaches EGP 200,000,000; Country-by-Country Report: mandatory for a constituent entity of a MNE group whose consolidated group revenue is at or above EGP 18,000,000,000. The annual Transfer Pricing Declaration (Form No. 63) is obligatory in every return regardless of values.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations as a separate statute, but substance-over-form is implemented through Article 44 of Law No. 91 of 2005 (the General Anti-Avoidance Rule), the 'place of effective management' test for tax residence, beneficial-ownership clauses in DTAs and the MLI Principal Purpose Test. An LLC that cannot demonstrate physical premises, licensed marine-bunkering operations, qualified personnel, tank/berth capacity and DTA-consistent decision-making may be recharacterized as a conduit or sham; free-zone exemption and treaty relief are thereby revoked retroactively with full tax, surcharges and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion: Under-declaration of Marine-Fuel Revenues

Statutory sanction: Article 133 of Law No. 91 of 2005: imprisonment for not less than 6 months and not more than 5 years and a fine of EGP 10,000–50,000, together with confiscation of the evaded assets, payment of the full evaded tax plus the 2% monthly late-payment surcharge, and publication of the conviction; the ETA additionally imposes the investment-incentive claw-back for the year of the offense.

Director personal liability: The LLC managers, financial officer and signatories are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax when the veil is pierced for fraud; the ETA applies civil asset freezes, travel bans and the blocking of personal bank accounts; criminal sentences disqualify directors from managing a company under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation: Margin Stripping and Sham Intercompany Invoicing

Statutory sanction: The ETA reallocates the profit to the Egyptian taxable base and assesses the 25.0% tax plus the monthly 2% delay surcharge; a mandatory fine of EGP 10,000–50,000 applies to the false Transfer Pricing Declaration; undocumented head-office charges are totally disallowed; the reallocation may extend back under the 5-year assessment window, with the full limitation period extended in fraud cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign Form No. 63 with false related-party values commit a false-statement offense carrying personal criminal liability; the ETA can seek civil restitution from directors and the tax agent; a travel ban and asset freeze are routinely issued when the amended assessment exceeds the LLC's ability to pay.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance in Marine Bunkering

Statutory sanction: Application of Article 44 GAAR: the shell LLC is disregarded, the foreign principal is deemed to carry on a PE in Egypt, the free-zone/SCZone exemption is revoked retroactively, corporate tax is assessed on the imputed Egyptian-source profits with the 2% monthly surcharge, and the ETA cancels the LLC's tax card and VAT registration.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and incorporators are jointly liable for the tax shortfall and for false statements in the registration file; courts have ordered the personal confiscation of nominee assets and imposed travel bans; the Commercial Registry may strike the LLC while shareholders are barred from new registration.
Anti-Money Laundering: Trade-Based Money Laundering in Cash and LC Bunkering

Statutory sanction: Law No. 80 of 2016: imprisonment of not less than 5 years and up to 15 years (with hard labor where aggravating circumstances exist) and a fine of EGP 100,000–1,000,000, or an amount equivalent to one-quarter of the laundered funds where higher; confiscation of the fuel cargoes, bank accounts and barges; the Suez Canal Authority and the Petroleum Authority jointly revoke the bunkering license; the ETA blocks the entity's e-invoicing and e-return accounts.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners, AML compliance officers and signatories face personal criminal prosecution without limited-liability shielding; their personal assets are frozen pending investigation; the FRA instructs banks to blacklist the directors; and arrest warrants or travel bans are enforced against the controlling directors.
Fiscal Registration and E-Filing Default: VAT, E-Invoicing and Advance Installments

Statutory sanction: Lump-sum late-filing penalties of EGP 5,000–20,000 per return, plus monthly 2% corporate-tax surcharge and a parallel VAT late-filing fine of up to EGP 50,000 with 1% per-month delay interest; the ETA posts the LLC on the public default list, suspends customs release of bunker imports and blocks the electronic tax card, making port clearance for the bunkering business commercially impossible.

Director personal liability: The LLC manager and the appointed tax agent are jointly liable for penalties and for the additional tax assessed by the ETA's administrative estimation; non-payment triggers personal seizure of fees, director asset seizures, and travel bans issued by competent tax courts.
