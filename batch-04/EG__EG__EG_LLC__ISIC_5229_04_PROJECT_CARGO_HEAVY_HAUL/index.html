Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance - official revenue administration; registration and filing via ETA electronic tax platform at https://www.eta.gov.eg (integrated e-invoice, e-receipt and e-filing portal).

E-government filing portal

The EG_LLC is incorporated in Egypt and must obtain its statutory Tax Card (TIN) from the Egyptian Tax Authority after incorporation. Procedure: (1) register the entity with GAFI and obtain the Commercial Registry certificate; (2) submit ETA Tax Card Registration (Form 11) online or at the competent tax office, attaching Commercial Registry certificate, articles of association, lease/evidence of business premises, identity documents of legal representatives, and initial bank account details; (3) ETA validates the application and issues the Tax Card bearing the TIN. (4) Separately file VAT registration with ETA under Law 67/2016 once annual taxable supplies reach or are expected to exceed EGP 500,000, attaching the Tax Card, activity description, and estimated turnover; ETA verifies and issues the VAT registration certificate. Foreign ownership does not change or bypass these domestic registration steps.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An Egyptian-registered LLC is a resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income under Law 91 of 2005; pass-through treatment is unavailable for LLC shareholders.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a check-the-box classification and has no domestic hybrid mismatch election. Elective mechanisms are limited to: (a) consolidated corporate income tax filing for resident companies with a 95% or greater direct/indirect holding relationship; and (b) simplified or cash-basis VAT accounting for taxpayers within ETA-affirmed small turnover thresholds. Foreign-owned LLCs cannot elect fiscal transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies of goods and services; mandatory registration applies once turnover reaches or is anticipated to reach that threshold. Non-resident suppliers carrying on taxable activities through a fixed establishment in Egypt or otherwise required to collect Egyptian VAT must register under the same threshold; supplies by foreign suppliers without an Egyptian fixed establishment are generally subject to reverse-charge by the resident recipient.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside Egypt are zero-rated under VAT Law 67/2016 where the applicant is a VAT registered exporter, the Egyptian Customs Export Declaration proves departure from Egyptian customs territory, the commercial invoice reflects arm's length terms, and the foreign currency proceeds are received in Egypt through a licensed bank within 180 days of export. Zero rating extends to directly connected project cargo and heavy haul logistics services where the service is performed in relation to export cargo or performed outside Egypt for a non-resident customer. Unused input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is eligible for refund under ETA export-refund instructions.

  • Egyptian Customs Export Declaration (ACK form and customs statistical summary)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / transport contract evidencing departure from Egyptian ports, airports or border crossings
  • Inward remittance certificate / foreign currency bank credit advice establishing export proceeds credited to an Egyptian bank account
  • Commercial export invoice together with the foreign purchase order or export contract
  • ETA e-invoice and VAT return schedule identifying the exported supplies as zero-rated

ETA mandates e-invoicing and e-receipt transmission under Ministerial Decrees 188 of 2020 and 623 of 2021. Taxpayers must issue invoices in JSON/XML format, apply digital signatures and QR encodings, and transmit them in near real-time to ETA's e-tax platform via official APIs. ETA performs fiscal clearance and validation by returning a unique invoice ID before the invoice may lawfully be used by the customer. e-Receipts are similarly cleared for retail B2C transactions. Export supplies are integrated into the same e-invoice pipeline; late, altered or unauthorized transmission attracts per-document fines and suspension of e-tax credentials.

ETA cross-validates zero-rated claims through customs export data, e-invoice transmission records and banking foreign-currency credit instruments. Failure to satisfy the export conditions, including non-repatriation of proceeds within 180 days or submission of deficient documentation, causes the supply to be retrospectively re-rated at 14% VAT; any input VAT refund already granted is clawed back and assessed together with monthly late-payment interest under the VAT Law. Fraudulent or forged export documentation constitutes VAT evasion and is subject to criminal penalties including imprisonment of up to five years and fines between EGP 10,000 and EGP 50,000, in addition to full tax and penalty readjustment; responsible officers of the LLC may also be made jointly liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a liberalized managed-float regime following the March 2024 exchange-rate unification; the Egyptian Pound (EGP) is effectively fully convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividend remittances, loan repayments) and for documented capital-account transactions, subject to CBE submitted forms. Foreign shareholders may repatriate dividends and sale proceeds in the same currency brought in, provided the underlying investment is registered with GAFI and the CBE and tax clearances (Egyptian Tax Authority Form 11 for dividend remittance) are obtained. No separate 'foreign investment approval' is required for LLC formation since non-resident ownership is fully allowed, but each inward and outward remittance requires purpose-code declaration and a CBE-compliant bank transaction slip. Capital-account outflows (loan repayments, intercompany loans) face higher evidentiary scrutiny and may be subordinated to tax registration certificates, audited financial statements, and CBE prior approval for EUR-denominated supplier payments exceeding 50,000 EUR or equivalent (Notification requirement for commercial registration of import operations is handled via the bank). Profit repatriation hurdles remain procedural rather than quantitative: the entity must evidence that (i) the dividend was declared from distributable reserves audited by a CPA licensed in Egypt, (ii) corporate income tax, stamp tax, and capital gains tax liabilities have been settled or tax clearance obtained, and (iii) the CBE Form for Outward Remittance—Profit Repatriation is countersigned by the authorized dealer bank. For out-of-gauge project-cargo logistics, freight receivables from international clients are treated as current-account export proceeds and enjoy free convertibility; no residual controls, waiting periods, or qouta approvals apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export and service-trade proceeds must be repatriated within 180 days from the date of shipment for goods, or from the contractual invoice due date for services, and credited to an account maintained at an authorized dealer bank in Egypt. The CBE's current framework (as harmonized after Circular No. 204/2022 and subsequent 2023-2024 relaxations) does not impose a mandatory EGP conversion ratio on non-commodity exporters; proceeds may be retained in eligible foreign-currency accounts (Retained FX Account / Export Proceeds Sub-Account), but the bank must receive a 'Proceeds Utilization Instruction' within 14 days of credit. Generically, for goods exporters the CBE retains the power to reinstate a partial conversion requirement (historically up to 50%) under exceptional liquidity stress, whereas for freight/logistics service exporters that power is exercised only through written CBE directive and has not been activated. Exporters of OOG heavy-haul services (transport, handling, crane and lashing operations, customs representation) must submit the Service Export Declaration in electronic form through the authorized bank with a copy of the signed service agreement, invoice, bank transaction advice, and proof of physical completion (CBM, bill of lading, project milestone certificate) to evidence the 180-day repatriation timeline. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window exposes the LLC to a fine of up to EGP 50,000 under Exchange Control Regulations and to audit adjustments by the Egyptian Tax Authority; mitigated by applying in writing to CBE for a one-time extension prior to the deadline.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 regarding the Central Bank and Banking System, together with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for FDI registration and the Ministry of Investment. All foreign exchange operations must be conducted through CBE-licensed authorized banks, and the CBE maintains supervisory authority over balance of payments reporting, inward/outward remittance coding, and hedging eligibility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low—legally non-viable as a core treasury rail. Under the Central Bank of Egypt's Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and its prior licensing circulars, any payment service provider, electronic wallet operator, or cross-border EMI acting as an account-holding institution for an Egyptian-domiciled corporate must obtain a CBE license and maintain a local establishment. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, etc.) cannot hold KYC accounts for Egyptian LLCs nor issue account/routing details for sovereign-required FX repatriation. They may be used only as auxiliary receivable collection channels for small milestone deposits under USD 5,000 from overseas counterparties, but the funds must then be swept into the Egyptian banking account via MT103; direct settlement of project-cargo invoices through offshore EMIs is prohibited and would breach the service-export repatriation window. The LLC must therefore maintain its principal transaction accounts with CBE-licensed banks; any use of unlicensed EMIs is an AML red flag.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder injection of USD/EUR equity into the EG_LLC must route through a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank via SWIFT MT103 with a correctly declared purpose code—typically 'Investment in Share Capital of an Egyptian Company' (CBE Inward Investment Purpose Indicator / Sub-Purpose 3101-Share Capital). The remittance narrative must reference the LLC's commercial registration number and, if the capital is to be treated as foreign investment under the Investment Law, the GAFI project/service invitation number obtained before transfer. On credit, the receiving bank issues a 'Certificate of Inward Transfer' (FIAC—Foreign Investment Admission Certificate) or 'Capital Registration Certificate', which the LLC must submit to GAFI to secure Investment Law protections (free transfer of profits, capital repatriation). For supplies of capital equipment by a non-resident shareholder (in-kind contribution in the form of heavy-haul trailers, cranes, or specialized bolsters), the route diverges: a notarized Contribution Agreement plus a licensed valuation report is required, and the physical import is cleared through Egyptian Customs with the bank issuing a supplementary transfer certificate to evidence the value of in-kind paid-in capital. Operational funding below the paid-in capital threshold may be injected as shareholder loans only after the capital account is open, and must be routed with purpose code 'Intercompany Borrowing/Foreign Loan' and be reported to the CBE through the bank to avoid recharacterization as capital. All inbound wires from a non-resident shareholder beneficially owning over 25% must be accompanied by a sworn source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership declaration to pass the bank's USD-compliance check.Outbound settlement rails for project-cargo operations: (i) SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for milestone disbursements to overseas vendors—heavy-lift vessel charter, container liner, trucking subcontractors, customs brokers, and port authorities—executed against a completed FX Transaction Request Form, CBE service-import purpose code, underlying invoice, contract, and the service-import declaration; funds must be remitted in the invoice currency up to the contractual limit, and any deviation above 15% triggers enhanced compliance review. (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT710) for procurement of capital heavy-haul equipment and outsourced turnkey transport operations where payment is contingent on presentation of a Clean Air or Ocean Bill of Lading together with the OOG survey and packing list; Egyptian banks issue CRC (Cash Against Documents) LCs on 100% cash cover for an unlevered LLC, or on foreign-credit lines for the parent-guaranteed structure. (iii) For intra-Egypt cash outflows, the local clearing rails are the CBE's RTGS system for EGP payments above EGP 10,000 and the Real-Time Gross Settlement via the Egyptian Banks Company for all banking payments; subcontractor reimbursements over EGP 50,000 require a tax invoice cleared against the registered VAT number. (iv) For cross-border supplier payments in USD, banks must see pro-forma invoice, registration with the Egyptian Import Registry (for goods), and, for services, a delivery confirmation (signed provider report) before release. All EGP-denominated payments to freight corporations and government-linked logistics entities must be made through the local clearing house and cannot be settled offshore.Onshore deliverable currency forwards with a CBE-licensed bank covering USD/EGP (up to twelve-month tenors), supported by underlying project-cargo revenue or cost exposure evidenced by a signed freight contract; forwards are documentary hedges and cannot be rolled or closed without the bank's confirmation.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP) between the LLC and an authorized dealer bank, executed only for matching the notional of a contracted capital-expenditure cycle; the bank-mandated margin must be posted in the same currency of exposure.Buying FX call/put options on USD/EGP for hedging of firm fuel, charter, and terminal cost escalation; writing options is prohibited for non-financial Egyptian corporates.Currency swaps on the 'deliverable EGP' basis via two separate onshore spot/forward transactions—reportable to the CBE through the FX declaration system—are permissible when the LLC has both an EGP cost base (local port services, customs, stevedoring, EGP crew wages) and USD receipts.Export/import insurance-based covers (e.g., warehouse-receipt hedges) are acceptable where they embed a CBE-approved exchange-rate cap, but must be registered as financial guarantees.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low—legally non-viable as a core treasury rail. Under the Central Bank of Egypt's Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019 and its prior licensing circulars, any payment service provider, electronic wallet operator, or cross-border EMI acting as an account-holding institution for an Egyptian-domiciled corporate must obtain a CBE license and maintain a local establishment. Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, etc.) cannot hold KYC accounts for Egyptian LLCs nor issue account/routing details for sovereign-required FX repatriation. They may be used only as auxiliary receivable collection channels for small milestone deposits under USD 5,000 from overseas counterparties, but the funds must then be swept into the Egyptian banking account via MT103; direct settlement of project-cargo invoices through offshore EMIs is prohibited and would breach the service-export repatriation window. The LLC must therefore maintain its principal transaction accounts with CBE-licensed banks; any use of unlicensed EMIs is an AML red flag.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% standard corporate income tax under Law 91 of 2005, Article 49, as amended by Law 26 of 2020. Exception: Suez Canal and oil exploration/production companies remain subject to 40.55% on worldwide profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross dividend distributions by the Egyptian LLC to all shareholders, whether resident or non-resident (Law 91/2005 Article 56, as amended by Law 96 of 2015). The distributing company must deduct the withholding, remit it to the ETA within 30 days of distribution, and report it on the periodic withholding tax return (Form 41).
