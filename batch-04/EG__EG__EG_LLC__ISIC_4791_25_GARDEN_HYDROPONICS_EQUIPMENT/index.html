Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to LLCs under Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 as amended; the founders determine the subscribed capital, which must be fully subscribed and fully paid at formation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) within the Ministry of Finance; electronic tax-services and e-filing portal https://www.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC first obtains a 10-digit Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the ETA registration service by submitting the certified incorporation certificate, memorandum of association, commercial registry extract, tax card request form, board/manager authorization, identity documents, and proof of registered address; for VAT/e-invoicing purposes the same TIN is used, after the taxpayer is added to the ETA VAT registry and the e-invoicing/e-receipt platform. Nonresident vendors with no Egyptian establishment but with taxable Egyptian supply must complete the nonresident VAT/e-invoice registration mechanism and obtain an ETA-issued taxpayer identifier before issuing cleared e-invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; an Egyptian LLC is a separate resident taxpayer under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, taxed on worldwide income, while foreign shareholders have no pass-through tax treatment and are subject to withholding on distributions if any.

Available election forms

None; Egyptian law does not provide a check-the-box election, fiscal unity/group election, or domestic hybrid mismatch election. A single-member LLC is not eligible for disregarded-entity treatment and cannot elect to be taxed as a partnership; treaty-based hybrid mismatch positions are limited by domestic substance rules and the adoption of the BEPS minimum standard.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual value of taxable supplies/services is the mandatory VAT registration threshold (voluntary registration is allowed below this); foreign e-commerce sellers making taxable remote supplies to Egyptian buyers may become obliged to register as non-resident VAT payers once the same turnover threshold is reached.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for goods physically exported from Egypt and for certain international/offshore services supplied to nonresidents. The exporter must be a registered VAT person, issue an ETA-cleared tax invoice, supply proof that ownership passed to the nonresident customer, and evidence customs exit of the goods from Egyptian territory; export proceeds must be received through the banking system in freely convertible foreign currency and, when required, repatriated within the Central Bank of Egypt deadline (180-day standard compliance horizon).

  • Customs Export Declaration (certified or electronic through Egyptian Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air, for typical cross-border e-commerce fulfilment)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the Egyptian seller
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice/tax invoice with valid QR code and UUID
  • Foreign-trade contract, purchase order and Incoterms evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit notification proving FX proceeds were received in Egypt
  • Certificate of origin and any export licence/permit applicable to the goods

Egypt has a mandatory central e-invoicing and e-receipt system administered by ETA. Registered businesses must clear each electronic invoice/receipt through the ETA integration platform in real time, using official XML/JSON schemas, submit via secure API with digital certificate authentication, and incorporate the ETA-returned unique invoice code and cryptographic QR stamp on the customer-facing document; non-compliance suspends the invoice validity and input VAT deduction rights.

ETA examination focuses on congruency among the e-invoice, customs declaration, transport documents, and bank FX statements. False or unsupported export zero-rating or refund claims are re-assessed at the standard 14% VAT, the corresponding input VAT is clawed back, late-return/delay surcharges are applied, the taxpayer may be suspended from the e-invoicing/refund systems, and criminal penalties (including imprisonment and fines) may be imposed for fraudulent invoices/export documentation; the tax authority's reopening/claw-back powers generally cover the statutory inspection years under Egyptian tax law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP operates under managed-float status and is convertible for current-transaction purposes under an IMF Article VIII orientation; however, Egypt is not a fully capital-account liberalised jurisdiction. Commercial banks receive current-account transfers with standard documentary support, but capital transactions—foreign equity contribution into an Egyptian LLC, shareholder loans, treasury derivatives, profit repatriation and liquidation proceeds—must be channelled through a CBE-authorised dealer bank and are subject to administrative controls, beneficial-ownership verification, tax clearance and statistical reporting. Profit remittance hurdles for a non-resident shareholder arise at execution points: audited local financial statements and tax clearance must exist, distributable reserves must satisfy the Company Law solvency test, and the remitting bank will require a signed board resolution, dividend declaration and a Form C remittance application before actual transfer. Since 2016 there is no legal dual exchange rate; conversion occurs at the interbank or bank-offered rate. The CBE may, during exigencies, activate temporary prioritisation rules or directed order for conversion, but as of Q1 2025 it has not imposed a general surrendered-conversion ratio on export proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE export-proceeds regulations implementing the Exchange Control Law, an Egyptian LLC exporter must repatriate to Egypt 100% of the value of goods shipped through an authorised bank within five (5) months from the date of the bill of lading or transport document; late receipt requires prior CBE approval or creates a default under the authorised bank's export documentation submission. The statutory conversion ratio to EGP is not fixed at 100%: the exporter may retain the full proceeds in a foreign-currency account (FCA) or convert them to EGP at the prevailing market/interbank rate. For the hydroponics e-commerce exporter, proceeds collected by Stripe or PayPal are treated as export receipts only when settled from the PSP settlement wallet to an Egyptian bank account in the exporter's name; such receipts must be identified in the bank's monthly foreign-currency report and attached to the export customs forms to avoid re-invoicing and transfer-pricing challenges.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole monetary, foreign-exchange, banking-licensing and payment-systems regulator for the Arab Republic of Egypt, acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 promulgating the Central Bank and Banking System Law, as amended, together with the Exchange Control Law regime, the Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002, as amended, and the Payment Systems and Electronic Money Law No. 18 of 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a standalone primary treasury rail; Medium as a complementary payment sub-acquirer. CBE Law No. 18 of 2019 requires any entity providing electronic-money services or cross-border payments in Egypt to hold a CBE e-money institution licence or operate through a licensed bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut and Payoneer cannot open corporate EGP bank accounts or hold clearing/settlement accounts directly with the CBE; for an Egyptian LLC they may act only as an unregulated facilitator or route through an authorised PSP. The recommended treasury infrastructure is to use Stripe and PayPal as front-end e-commerce PSPs, settle into a CBE-licensed bank account via local commercial acquirers, and keep all FX conversion with the Egyptian authorised dealer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders' cash equity injections must be transferred only by SWIFT to the LLC's capital suspense or operational account at a CBE-authorised dealer bank using MT103, or MT202 for bank-to-bank credit inside the same network. The remittance advice must state the ultimate remitter, the Egyptian beneficiary TIN, and the purpose narrative 'Capital subscription to EG LLC under the Egyptian Investment Law'; the bank will record the transaction in the balance-of-payments statistical register and issue an inward invoice or contract form. The applicable BPM6 purpose code is DI3, direct-investment equity other than reinvested earnings. The funds may be held in the original foreign currency or sold for EGP at the reference or interbank rate, but cannot be settled out of the country: every wire must be cleared via the CBE RTGS and each bank will require a foreign-currency account instruction and the company's current commercial registration.For purchase of hydroponics equipment and spare parts, the LLC should use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for low-value suppliers, documentary commercial import letters of credit through an Egyptian opening bank for high-value equipment with MT700 and MT710 issuance and advice, and MT202 cover payments for bank-side reimbursement. Import settlements must be registered with the CBE-authorised bank on the import documentary form and supported by proforma invoices, customs declarations, insurance certificates and GOEIC import registration, as applicable; payment in EGP is permissible for domestic suppliers.
