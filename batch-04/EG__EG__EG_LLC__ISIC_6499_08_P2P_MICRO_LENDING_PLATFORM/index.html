Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An Egyptian LLC is generally taxed as a separate legal entity and is subject to Egyptian corporate income tax on its taxable income under the Income Tax Law. Egypt does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box election for an Egyptian LLC. There is no general election allowing an Egyptian LLC to be treated as a transparent partnership solely by filing a tax form. Tax treatment follows the legal form and applicable statutory rules. Hybrid mismatch elections are not generally available. Special economic-zone, free-zone, investment-law, or sector incentive regimes require separate eligibility and approval and do not constitute a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500000 annual taxable turnover, subject to the statutory rules for compulsory registration, taxable supplies, related persons, and regulated activities. Voluntary registration may be available. Interest and other qualifying financial services may be VAT-exempt, while separately charged technology, platform, underwriting, servicing, brokerage, administration, subscription, or success fees may constitute taxable services and must be analyzed separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are generally zero-rated where the goods are exported outside Egypt and the export is evidenced by customs records and other prescribed documentation. Services supplied to a non-resident may qualify for zero-rating only where the statutory conditions are satisfied, including non-resident customer status, use or benefit of the service outside Egypt, absence of prohibited Egyptian consumption, and acceptable evidence of payment and export of the service. A platform's lending interest, qualifying financial return, or other exempt financial supply is not converted into a zero-rated export merely because the customer or lender is non-resident. Separately identifiable platform, software, underwriting, servicing, or intermediation fees must be tested independently. Input VAT refunds or credits require valid tax invoices, customs evidence, accounting records, proof of payment where required, and reconciliation to VAT returns. Zero-rating is unavailable where the transaction is actually consumed in Egypt, the customer is not genuinely non-resident, documentation is incomplete, or the supply is VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration or electronic customs export record
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for exported goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport document
  • Evidence of customs exit and shipment outside Egypt
  • Foreign-currency bank receipt, inward remittance evidence, or other approved payment evidence for qualifying exported services
  • Customer contract, purchase order, service agreement, and evidence identifying the non-resident customer
  • VAT return reconciliation linking the export to the zero-rated turnover
  • Certificate of origin or other trade document where required by the destination or customs regime

Taxpayers within the applicable e-invoicing phases must issue electronic invoices through the ETA e-invoice platform for covered business-to-business and government transactions. The invoice generally requires the supplier and customer tax identifiers, invoice data, taxable value, VAT amount, classification codes, and an ETA-assigned unique identification number. Covered invoices must be digitally signed using an approved electronic signature and submitted through the prescribed API or portal workflow for ETA validation and clearance before use in the applicable cases. The recipient must accept or reject invoices through the system. Businesses covered by the e-receipt phases must issue electronic receipts for covered business-to-consumer transactions through the ETA e-receipt system, normally using approved point-of-sale or API integration. Failure to enroll, issue, transmit, digitally sign, or retain required electronic documents may result in penalties, denial of input VAT credit, suspension of system access, and assessment of unpaid tax.

The ETA may deny zero-rating, disallow input VAT recovery, reassess VAT at the applicable domestic rate, and recover the resulting tax, interest, and statutory additional amounts where export evidence, customer status, service-use evidence, customs exit, or payment documentation is deficient. Fraudulent invoices, fictitious exports, concealed taxable supplies, false customs records, or deliberate VAT evasion may trigger administrative penalties, criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment exposure under applicable tax and customs laws, and joint liability for responsible managers or participants. Incorrect VAT returns and failure to issue required invoices may generate separate penalties. Refunds obtained without entitlement may be clawed back with applicable interest and penalties. The 180-day foreign-exchange evidence or repatriation period must be applied only where required by the relevant export, banking, customs, or VAT rules; it is not a universal substitute for transaction-specific statutory documentation. The taxpayer should retain customs, banking, contractual, accounting, and electronic-invoice records for the applicable statutory retention period and be able to produce them during an ETA audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EGP operates under a unified managed-float regime following the March 2024 liberalization and the IMF Extended Fund Facility. Current-account transactions (trade, services, royalties, interest) are freely convertible through authorized dealer banks subject to underlying documentation and automatic CBE reporting. Capital-account transactions require prior registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 to secure CBE-guaranteed repatriation rights. Profit remittance for non-resident shareholders is permitted once the Egyptian Tax Authority issues a tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements are filed, dividend withholding tax (10% statutory or reduced treaty rate) is settled, and the settling bank receives a foreign-currency purchase declaration. The practical hurdle is documentation and bank-level FX liquidity, not legal inconvertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The mandatory EGP surrender ratio for export proceeds was abolished after the 2024 float. Exporters must route 100% of proceeds through an Egyptian authorized dealer bank within 30 days from customs clearance (extendable to 180 days with CBE approval), retain foreign currency in registered Export Proceeds Accounts, and may convert freely; undocumented parallel holding of proceeds triggers CBE sanctions, account freeze, and loss of trade finance access. For this P2P platform there is no commodity export business; the analogous regime is cross-border financial-inflow declaration: all inbound foreign lender subscriptions and share capital must be routed through the local banking system and registered with CBE to establish the legal basis for later outbound repayments and dividend remittances.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020; the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) co-regulates non-bank P2P micro-lending and crowdfunding under Law No. 5 of 2022 amending the Capital Market Law No. 95 of 1992.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-nil as a primary banking layer. Offshore digital EMIs (EU/UK licensed) hold no CBE banking license, cannot access CBE RTGS, the Egypt Instant Payment Network (IPN/InstaPay), or the Egyptian ACH, and cannot lawfully hold EGP client funds. Their only workable role is as a secondary FX conversion and virtual-IBAN aggregation corridor routed to an onshore correspondent bank. A viable arrangement requires contracting with a licensed Egyptian bank under a Banking-as-a-Service platform or entering the CBE Regulatory Sandbox to graduate to a digital bank license.

The foreign shareholder wires funds by SWIFT MT103 to the LLC's FCY or EGP capital account at an authorized dealer bank, referencing the shareholder capital-subscription resolution and GAFI registration number; the bank records the inflow with CBE, converts to EGP at the interbank weighted-average rate where the operating account is EGP-denominated, and requires a stated-purpose declaration (capital subscription, shareholder loan, or crowdfunding participant funds).
