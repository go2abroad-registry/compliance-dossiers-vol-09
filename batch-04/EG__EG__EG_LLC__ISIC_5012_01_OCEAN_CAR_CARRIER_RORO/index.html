Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; electronic portal at www.eta.gov.eg, including the exclusive e-invoicing and e-receipts integrated system (EIS).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entity must first obtain a Commercial Registration certificate from the Egyptian General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or Commercial Registry, appoint a tax representative, and file the combined Tax Card application (Form 113/110) with the ETA. After issuance of the Tax Card, registration for VAT and e-invoicing is mandatory; all forms, notarized POAs and signed originals must be filed with the ETA's taxpayer services window.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident entity must first obtain a Commercial Registration certificate from the Egyptian General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or Commercial Registry, appoint a tax representative, and file the combined Tax Card application (Form 113/110) with the ETA. After issuance of the Tax Card, registration for VAT and e-invoicing is mandatory; all forms, notarized POAs and signed originals must be filed with the ETA's taxpayer services window.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None applicable. Egypt does not implement check-the-box elections, partnership pass-through elections, or hybrid mismatch elections. A foreign-owned LLC is subject to corporate income tax at the entity level under Law 91/2005; any branch-vs-subsidiary classification is a structural choice, not a tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies of goods and services; the ETA has discretion to prescribe lower thresholds for certain activities or e-commerce sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exports of goods and services supplied to non-residents, provided that (i) the goods are physically delivered outside Egypt, (ii) services are considered rendered wholly in the export market, (iii) payment is received in freely convertible foreign currency, and (iv) the full foreign-currency proceeds are remitted to and credited in an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the export date.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts for all VAT-registered entities through the ETA's digital platform. Every invoice must be transmitted in real time to the ETA fiscal clearance system, validated with a unique invoice code (UUID) and QR stamp, and reported via ETA-certified billing API integrations. Non-compliant invoices are treated as invalid and do not support input VAT recovery.

False declaration or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers mandatory retrospective VAT assessment at 14%, plus additional tax of 100% of the evaded VAT, fines from EGP 20,000 up to EGP 1,000,000 per invoice or transaction, clawback of any refunded input tax with statutory interest, criminal prosecution under Articles 65-70 of VAT Law 67/2016, and potential imprisonment. The ETA additionally applies automatic audit flags for export refunds if foreign-currency proceeds are not received within the 180-day deadline or are repatriated via non-banking channels.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt currently operates a unified, market-determined exchange-rate regime following the March 2024 monetary reform. Current-account convertibility is effectively granted for bona fide trade in goods and services, provided settlement is routed through CBE-licensed authorized dealers. Capital-account transactions are convertible but remain administratively controlled: inward equity contributions require registration and declaration through the authorized-dealer bank, while outward dividends and profit remittances require the Egyptian Tax Authority to issue a tax-clearance certificate confirming the settlement of corporate tax and withholding tax, plus audited financial statements and documented evidence of the underlying Egyptian-source earnings. The practical residual hurdles are documentary rather than quantitative: UBO declaration under Law No. 80 of 2002 (AML/CFT), source-of-funds verification, alignment of remittance narratives with CBE purpose codes, and maintenance of entity substance (registered office, books, and tax card) in Egypt.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE statutory export-receipts regime, 100% of proceeds from Egyptian export operations, including international sea-transport services rendered by a Ro-Ro carrier, must be collected through the Egyptian banking system and credited to the exporter's foreign-currency account within 30 days of the date of invoicing or performance (for goods, the statutory clock runs from the date of customs release). There is currently no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio under the liberalized flexible-rate framework; proceeds may be retained in a CBE-licensed foreign-currency account or converted into EGP at the interbank rate at the exporter's discretion. The exporter must hold a valid corporation tax card, register each operation with its authorized-dealer bank, and reconcile amounts against customs or voyage documentation. Failure to repatriate within the statutory period, absent a documented extension (normally obtainable up to 90 days for genuine open-account or documentary-credit trade terms), triggers CBE sanctions including denial of new export financing, blocking of subsequent customs clearances, and penalty assessments against both the exporter and the benefiting bank.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), which under Law No. 194 of 2020 also serves as the Foreign Exchange Administration, the AML/CFT supervisory authority for banks, and the sole issuer of banking licenses and authorized-dealer designations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury infrastructure. Offshore fintech EMIs and non-bank digital wallets are not permitted to hold Egyptian export-proceeds collection accounts, obtain domestic EGP clearing membership, or act as the settlement bank for trade transactions. Under Law No. 194 of 2020 and the National Payments Council Law No. 18 of 2019, deposit-taking and operating payment accounts in Egypt require a CBE license, and the export-repatriation framework mandates that receivables be credited to an account at a CBE-licensed bank located in Egypt. Licensed local PSPs (e.g., Fawry, e-finance, PaySky) may be engaged only for intra-Egypt disbursements or agency collections; an offshore EMI can be used at most as a sub-disbursement rail for crew transit or petty expenses, provided the underlying funded account remains the EG LLC account at the Egyptian bank and each payment is identifiable in the CBE audit trail with the company's tax card and commercial-registration references.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) from the corporate account of the actual shareholder, never from unrelated third parties. The remittance instruction must be classified under the CBE balance-of-payments purpose category for direct investment - equity capital injection, and the payment narrative must reference the EG LLC commercial-registration number, corporation tax-card number, and GAFI company-file number. The authorized-dealer Egyptian bank verifies the remitter's name against the shareholder register and CBE sanctions lists, collects the board resolution and share-subscription documents, completes the inward-transfer statistical declaration required by the CBE, and credits the USD proceeds to the capital escrow account pending release. Treasury conversion into EGP, when required for Egyptian operational costs, is executed at the interbank spot rate under a bank-issued FX deal ticket with full CBE transaction reporting. Mandatory verification also includes confirmation that the remitted amount corresponds to the registered/authorized capital, UBO screening, and retention of the complete remittance file for five years under CBE AML record-keeping rules.The principal clearing rail for foreign-currency outflows is the SWIFT MT103 initiated from the EG LLC USD operational account at the CBE-licensed bank, with each instruction justified by an underlying contract, invoice, or vessel document and labeled with the applicable CBE statistical purpose (charter hire under sea-freight service; bunkering and stores; canal transit fees; P&I insurance premiums; port disbursements). Suez Canal transit dues are paid in USD through the appointed shipping agent's collection account or directly under the Suez Canal Authority's invoice based on the vessel's GRT and transit declaration. High-value capital movements, such as the acquisition of a Ro-Ro vessel or major dry-dock repairs abroad, are effected through a documentary commercial letter of credit issued under UCP 600 or a standby-LC-backed payment milestone structure, protecting both payer and payee where title and delivery risks exist. Every outbound transaction must observe the dual-signatory treasury matrix, the CBE origin-of-funds attestation, and the bank's post-transaction monitoring protocols, and disbursement to any non-reserved third party is blocked unless that party has been pre-registered in the bank's counterparty due-diligence system.Deliverable plain-vanilla USD/EGP forward contracts transacted with a CBE-licensed bank, tenor up to 12 months, permitted strictly against genuine underlying exposures from freight invoices, Suez Canal dues, bunker purchases, port disbursements, and EGP payroll or tax obligations.FX swaps between USD and EGP (and between major convertible currencies) executed with the corporate bank for cash-flow leveling around the operating cycle, with CBE derivatives reporting.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (with principal exchange) used to hedge USD-denominated vessel-financing debt against EGP-denominated operating liquidity, subject to documented bank credit approval and the CBE derivatives-risk framework.Purchased, non-written vanilla currency options in USD/EGP and USD/EUR offered by tier-1 Egyptian banks, with upfront premium funded from the treasury account.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not recognized as an onshore-permissible instrument; offshore NDF cover, if any, must be strictly evidenced as a hedge of a firm underlying exposure and cannot substitute for the deliverable local-market hedge, so treasury policy should rely exclusively on deliverable forwards, swaps, and purchased options for Egypt-regulated flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 10,000,000 aggregate annual controlled transactions: at or above this threshold, the Egyptian LLC must prepare and maintain a full Local File and Master File and submit the Transfer Pricing Disclosure Form. Below the threshold, simplified related-party disclosure is still required on the annual CIT return; the ETA may also demand contemporaneous evidence where the transactions are prima facie non-arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but applies a robust substance-over-form doctrine through the Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, judicial precedent, and anti-treaty-abuse principles. A RoRo shipping LLC claiming free-zone CIT exemption or DTA benefits must possess and document genuine economic substance: actual board meetings and managerial decisions in Egypt, Egyptian crew or payroll functions, vessel registration and maritime operations records, Egyptian bank accounts receiving freight payments, and independent storage of charter-party documents. Letter-box LLCs interposed solely to obtain a reduced CIT or treaty rate are disregarded or disqualified.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Discretionary re-assessment of the full taxable base; penalty of 2% per month on additional tax, capped at 8%; statutory interest from the original due date; criminal fine and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Income Tax Law and Penal Code; seizure of vessels, bank balances, and maritime receivables.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors or actual beneficiaries created a sham structure to evade tax. Directors become jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, civil restitution, fines, asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment; personal bank accounts may be attached where tax was diverted through them.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of free-zone or SCZone tax status; retroactive claw-back of exempt CIT for the prior five years; denial of treaty benefits; penalties and interest; reclassification of all profits as a taxable distribution or deemed dividend; revocation of the maritime license by the Ministry of Transport.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed substance declarations knowing them to be false are personally liable for clawed-back taxes and fines. Courts may order disqualification, seizure of personal assets, and a travel ban where the LLC was the alter ego of the shareholder.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: ETA arm's-length adjustment to the Egyptian profit; additional CIT at 22.5%; penalty of 2% per month on the adjustment, capped at 8%; disallowance of inflated expenses; interest on overdue tax; and where the Local File is unavailable, adverse inference with the burden of proof shifted to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed artificial intercompany contracts, approved excessive charter rates without a benchmark, or concealed the Local File may be held liable for the tax shortfall under gross-negligence and fraud doctrines. The ETA can pierce the LLC veil to recover the disguised profit from the foreign parent's Egyptian assets or from the directors' personal estates.
Withholding Tax and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC is made collection agent for the unpaid WHT; such liability is not extinguished by the distribution. The payer is assessed for the 10% withholding, plus a 2% monthly penalty capped at 8%, statutory interest, and, for deliberate non-withholding, criminal prosecution and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly paid the dividend without withholding are personally liable for the un-withheld amount, civil restitution, and fines. Where the distribution left the LLC insolvent, directors can be stripped of limited-liability protection and subject to personal seizure orders and travel bans.
AML / Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Freezing orders and confiscation of the vessel, cargo, and freight proceeds; administrative fines up to EGP 5,000,000; criminal imprisonment for senior officers; cancellation of the ocean carrier license; listing on Egypt's financial crime registry with consequent loss of tax clearance certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as officers under Egyptian AML and Penal Law and bear personal criminal liability for failure to report suspicious maritime trade transactions or for participating in a sanctions evasion scheme. Civil recovery, travel bans, personal asset seizure, and imprisonment are available against directors where the corporate form is used to facilitate money laundering.
