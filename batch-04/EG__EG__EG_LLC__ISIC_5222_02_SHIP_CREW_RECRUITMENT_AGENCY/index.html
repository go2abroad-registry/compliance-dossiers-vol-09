Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted current-account convertibility obligations under IMF Article VIII, and the March 2024 CBE decision unified the EGP exchange rate, removed managed black-market intervention at commercial banks, and effectively moved the currency to a liberalized managed-float regime. Current-account transactions—crew recruitment fees, seafarer wage remittances, ship-management service payments, and subagent commissions—may be exchanged and remitted through authorized banks at market-determined rates, subject to full transaction documentation, source-of-funds verification, tax compliance clearance and bank-specific AML/KYC release procedures. Capital-account transactions remain subject to structural documentary controls: inbound foreign direct investment for share capital subscription must be routed through an authorized Egyptian bank, registered against the LLC's GAFI file and commercial register, and evidenced by a CBE-compliant inward transfer or investment certificate for future capital and dividend repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily practical and procedural: the paying bank will require audited Egyptian financial statements, Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax clearance or withholding-tax receipts, shareholders' meeting minutes approving the dividend, the original inward investment certificate matching the inbound capital transfer, a board resolution confirming distributable reserves, and completion of sanctions/source-of-funds screening. Banks may also queue large USD/EGP outbound transfers during liquidity tightness, and may condition remittance on confirmation that corporate tax, payroll taxes, social insurance and customs obligations have been settled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE application instructions, a maritime crew recruitment and ship-crew management agency exporting services must cause the full invoice value to be received into its Egyptian bank account through authorized banking channels within the prescribed settlement period; for crewing and seafarer payroll services the CBE-applied rule is generally repatriation within 90 days from invoice date or completion of the underlying crewing service, while goods-related maritime disbursements are subject to the six-month export-proceeds window. There is currently no mandatory fixed-percentage conversion of export proceeds into EGP after the 2024 float; the agency must nevertheless demonstrate that received foreign currency is either sold to the Egyptian banking system to settle domestic EGP obligations or retained in a CBE-approved foreign-currency account supported by a documented treasury rationale. The exporting LLC must submit to its relationship bank the underlying crew-management agreements, monthly payroll consolidations, CBE export documentation/return filings, and matching SWIFT MT103 records. The CBE may restrict future FX purchase allocations, impose reporting penalties, or require retrospective conversion if export receipts are unreported, delayed, under-invoiced, or not reconciled with commercial substance.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) acting as the monetary, foreign-exchange and banking regulator under the Central Banking and Financial System Law No. 194 of 2020, Banking Law No. 88 of 2003, the 2024 unified floating exchange-rate policy decrees, and CBE-issued prudential regulations on current-account and capital-account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for primary custodial treasury usage. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex and Mena-based open-banking platforms are not licensed by the CBE to operate deposit-taking or settlement accounts for Egyptian corporate entities and cannot satisfy any capital-control, export-receipt or profit-repatriation obligation. They may be used only as a secondary convenience for immaterial seafarer training, courier or travel-card expenses, provided no operating revenue is diverted to them and all final beneficiary cash flows are consolidated back into the CBE-licensed Egyptian bank account. Any direct use of an unlicensed EMI for operating revenue would violate Law No. 194 of 2020, expose the LLC to AML penalties, and trigger automatic downgrade of the banking relationship risk rating.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Undisclosed indirect shareholder or nominee-ownership structure masking the true beneficial owner of a foreign-controlled crew agency.

Defense evidence: Notarized/legalized beneficial-ownership declaration; full GAFI soft and hard registry extracts; chronological commercial-registry chain; unredacted passport/ID and certified residency proof for each direct and ultimate owner; source-of-wealth report prepared by a licensed Egyptian AML advisor; and two bank references covering the prior 24 months of the shareholder's banking history.
High-value inbound capital injection from an offshore holding company during the pre-revenue stage, lacking commercial substance.

Root cause: High-value inbound capital injection from an offshore holding company during the pre-revenue stage, lacking commercial substance.

Defense evidence: Shareholders' meeting minutes approving the capital increase or subscription; bank financing letter; audited or notarially certified parent-company financial statements; provenance-of-funds chain (dividend resolution, asset-sale contract, brokerage statement, trust deed); CBE purpose-code mapping to 'FDI in Egypt – share capital subscription'; detailed business plan; 13-week cash-flow forecast for the ship-crew recruitment book; and a signed undertaking that no funds originate from crypto, cash, unlicensed EMI wallets or opaque trusts.
Payroll disbursements to crews whose nationalities, passport specimens or vessel flag registries trigger OFAC, UN, EU or CBE sanctions-list hits.

Root cause: Payroll disbursements to crews whose nationalities, passport specimens or vessel flag registries trigger OFAC, UN, EU or CBE sanctions-list hits.

Defense evidence: Positive-name screening report against CBE-UN-OFAC-EU consolidated lists; seafarer discharge book and travel-document copies for each payee; STCW certificates; full payroll run with gross-to-net earnings breakdown and no third-party designee for the seafarer's salary; vessel IMO number and vessel commercial registry extract; shipowner and ship-manager KYC files; documented screening decision log with 'no match' conclusions; and director attestation that the agency does not serve sanctioned vessels or crew.

Root cause: Transaction-structuring red flags: frequent round-dollar amounts, multiple salary remittances below local reporting thresholds, or split payments to multiple seafarers on the same vessel within the same settlement window.

Defense evidence: Board-approved treasury policy setting per-transaction, per-day and per-quarter limits; consolidated payroll register with crew ID, bank account, employment contract reference and vessel assignment; signed ship-management agreement with full crewing schedule; one-file-per-vessel SWIFT MT103 payroll evidence showing no cash payments, no netting, and no receivable financing; audited annual financial statements; and VAT and social-insurance declaration receipts matching payroll volumes.
PEP involvement or government-linked vessel ownership, procurement officials or maritime labour leaders presenting a layered source of funds.

Root cause: PEP involvement or government-linked vessel ownership, procurement officials or maritime labour leaders presenting a layered source of funds.

Defense evidence: Completed PEP and ownership declaration refreshed annually; source-of-funds and source-of-wealth profile for PEP-related balances; list of government-owned or state-linked flag vessels with official registry extracts; internal AML risk decision memorandum confirming no account operation is executed for the PEP personally; negative adverse-media screen reports; and sanctions-palette screening records for all political offices held.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign shareholder capital must arrive as a clean SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own bank through an authorized Egyptian bank. The wire must identify the Egyptian LLC, the IBC/beneficiary account number, and a CBE-compatible purpose code for 'FDI – equity participation in Egyptian LLC'; it must not be sent through unlicensed intermediaries, virtual account agents, or global EMI wallets. The receiving bank will screen the sender, verify the GAFI registry entry and shareholders' resolution, release the AML/KYC hold, execute the foreign-currency-to-EGP conversion at the day's unified market rate, and issue an official inward remittance/investment certificate. This certificate and the related conversion ticket are the threshold evidence for future dividend and capital repatriation and must be retained for the full CBE record-keeping period (generally five years).For crew wages, ship-management fees, subagent commissions and welfare-fund payments, the LLC will clear through (i) SWIFT MT103 payment orders for single-beneficiary seafarer salaries and low- or medium-value subagent remittances, (ii) SWIFT MT103/MT202 paired transfers for high-volume consolidated payroll runs, and (iii) confirmed irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit only where a vessel owner requires documentary protection for a cumulative disbursement package; ordinary crew payroll does not require Letters of Credit. Domestic EGP disbursements to Egyptian suppliers, social-insurance authorities, the ETA and GAFI must be settled through the CBE real-time gross settlement system, and each foreign-currency disbursement must be supported by the underlying contract, commercial invoice, payroll schedule and withholding-tax certificate. Crew payroll in USD/EUR/GBP should be structured as mirrored individual MT103 messages from the multi-currency account rather than aggregated transfers, to preserve AML-transparent audit trails and avoid triggering fragmentation suspicious-activity alerts.Onshore deliverable USD/EGP forward contracts written by CBE-licensed bank market desks, available for tenors generally up to 12 months, requiring verified underlying receivables such as crew-fee invoices and manning contracts, plus a board-approved corporate hedging mandate.FX swap agreements (USD/EGP, EUR/EGP, GBP/EGP) for intra-month liquidity bridging between scheduled crew payroll outflows and incoming invoice collections, executable with the relationship bank's FX market desk.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for longer-term USD or EUR liabilities arising from CBE-registered parent-company loans or vessel-payroll funding structures, subject to CBE notification/approval thresholds and bank counterparty credit lines.CBE-approved commercial-bank FX options (calls or puts on EGP) where warranted for concentrated bid or revenue exposure from a single shipowner; products are reviewed on a case-by-case basis and are not standard open-market instruments.CBE-compliant Islamic hedging structures, including wa'ad-based unilateral promises converted into spot transactions, for sharia-sensitive shareholders and counterparties, provided the bank's Shariah committee approves the underlying exposure and the CBE product registration is in place.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on profit distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to its shareholders, including non-resident investors, under Article 55 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return due within four months after the end of the taxable fiscal year (e.g., 30 April for a 31 December year-end); quarterly advance payments due by the last day of the 4th, 6th, 9th, and 12th months after the start of the taxable period; final balance payable with the annual return.

Deadline: · Frequency:

PAYE and WHT remitted to the ETA by the 15th day of the month following the payment month; quarterly WHT summary return due by the last day of the month immediately following the quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the aggregate annual value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds EGP 8 million; Master File mandatory where aggregate controlled transactions exceed EGP 20 million or the multinational group's consolidated global revenue exceeds EGP 1 billion; the Transfer Pricing Disclosure Form is mandatory for all taxpayers with related-party transactions regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate an explicit Economic Substance Regulation framework comparable to Gulf Cooperation Council states; however, the general anti-avoidance rules in Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, the substance-over-form doctrine, and beneficial-ownership requirements mandate that a crew-manning agency maintain local office premises, operational staff, board decision-making, and real commercial risk; a shell subsidiary engaged solely in intercompany invoicing of manning fees will be disregarded, with profit reallocation and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of 3 to 5 years and a fine of EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000; full assessment of evaded tax plus an additional penalty of up to 100% of the avoided amount and a 1% monthly monetary surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns or directed concealed revenue are jointly and severally liable; civil restitution of the tax deficit, criminal imprisonment, disqualification from board service, and a ministerial travel ban where unpaid tax exceeds EGP 500,000.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the shell structure under GAAR, reallocation of the entire profit to the Egyptian shareholder or PE, disallowance of all intercompany deductions, and a 200% penalty on unreported income.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing for abuse of legal personality; directors personally liable for the shortfall, subject to travel bans, asset attachment, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent use of the corporate vehicle.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EGP 50,000 up to EGP 10,000,000 for documentation non-compliance; forced arm's-length adjustment with a 30% penalty on the adjusted amount; 1% monthly deferred-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: The signatory director is exposed to fines and imprisonment for missing contemporaneous TP records; tax advisors who certified incomplete files face professional liability and license sanctions.
Payroll & WHT Misclassification of Seafarers

Statutory sanction: 1% monthly surcharge on unremitted payroll WHT; fixed penalties per under-declared seafarer for social insurance; disallowance of the salary expense; criminal prosecution for repeated non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unremitted employee WHT and social contributions; travel ban and asset attachment arise when payroll liabilities exceed EGP 100,000; confiscation of withheld sums is treated as embezzlement.
AML & Cross-Border Payment Violations

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80 of 2002: fines of up to EGP 10 million and imprisonment of 2 to 7 years; account freezing; revocation of the crewing agency license by the Ministry of Transport.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability and imprisonment apply to beneficial owners and directors who ignore AML obligations; license revocation dismantles the corporate structure and exposes directors to civil restitution claims from seafarers and principals.
