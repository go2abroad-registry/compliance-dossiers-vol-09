Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), an autonomous body under Law 91 of 2005 as amended and Law 67 of 2016, operating under the Egyptian Ministry of Finance; official integrated e-filing and taxpayer services portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must complete its establishment with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) and obtain GAFI incorporation certificate and commercial registry extract. Following this, the LLC files an application with the competent ETA office to be placed on the taxpayers' register, resulting in issuance of the statutory Tax Card (بطاقة ضريبية) and a unified taxpayer identification number (TIN/VAT ID) under the Egyptian Tax Authority. The procurement procedure comprises: (1) submission of the tax registration form accompanied by certified memorandum of association, commercial registry extract, GAFI investment certificate, valid lease/deed for Egyptian premises, board resolution designating the local tax representative, and certified passports of non-resident shareholders; (2) simultaneous registration for corporate income tax under Law 91/2005 and value added tax under Law 67/2016 as a taxable person; (3) activation of the ETA e-services account and credential issuance for electronic filing; (4) procurement of an accredited electronic signature certificate and activation of the mandatory e-invoicing integration profile with the ETA. Upon completion the entity holds a permanent Tax Card that must be renewed periodically with annual turnover statements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian domestic law does not provide check-the-box or classified surplus elections. An Egyptian LLC is per se a resident juridical person taxed as a separate taxable entity on worldwide income at the statutory corporate rate. No hybrid mismatch or branch transparency elections are available under Egyptian tax law; an LLC can only select the ordinary realized net-profit basis, or, if qualifying under ETA's simplified small-business conventions, request an examinable presumptive profit determination. Election choices are codified in Law 91/2005 and implementing regulations only.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration is required where annual taxable supplies of goods and services exceed EGP 500,000; persons engaged in import activities are obliged to register regardless of turnover. The EG_LLC engaged in cross-border commodity trade in milk powder must register mandatorily without threshold reliance because its operations involve import and export of tangible goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate treatment applies to exports of goods that physically leave the customs territory of Egypt and to specified international/offshore services consumed outside Egypt. For the milk powder trading LLC, eligibility requires a genuine sale of traded dairy goods to a foreign non-resident counterpart, physical shipment of the actual goods from officially registered Egyptian customs territory (or bonded/free-zone facilities with re-export status), execution of the statutory customs export declaration, and externalization of export proceeds in convertible currency as record evidence of trade substance. The zero rate is claimed at the point of invoicing; the ETA grants credit and refund of input VAT attributable to exported goods when the substantiation bundle is intact.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed and cleared through the Automated Customs System / NAFEZA Single Window with the six-digit HS code and total declared export value
  • Original or certified Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) confirming the milk powder cargo departed Egypt to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) from the closing Egyptian bank demonstrating receipt of the export proceeds in fully convertible foreign currency
  • Sales invoices/contracts and packing list identifying the foreign buyer of the skimmed/whole milk powder
  • Certificate of Origin (where required under the Egypt-EU or Egyptian-African trade regimes), GAFI or Ministry of Trade export authorization, phytosanitary/food safety documentation and any customs broker declarations
  • Matching zero-rated electronic invoice accepted and cleared in the ETA e-invoicing platform against the export declaration and bank transfer record

Egypt enforces a mandatory real-time electronic invoicing and clearance regime. All VAT registrants must issue B2B tax invoices in the ETA's prescribed JSON/XML format and submit each invoice electronically to the ETA centralized e-invoicing platform (API integration or government web portal) for real-time technical validation and fiscal clearance before the invoice becomes legally effective. The ETA returns a globally unique identifier (UUID) with tax authority digital fingerprint/stamp; acceptance decisively conditions the buyer's input VAT deductibility. Offline or paper invoicing to registered persons is prohibited. The complementary e-receipt system is mandatory for B2C retail transactions. Non-integrated sellers face automatic rejection of invoice clearance, disallowance of input VAT credits, and administrative penalties; late integration incurs daily delay additions.

The ETA Tax Audit and Customs Authority jointly verify each zero-rated export against live customs filings, e-invoice, and Central Bank of Egypt foreign currency receipt records. Substantiation failure, cancellation of the export declaration, re-importation, failure to repatriate proceeds within the 180-day deadline, or any false declaration automatically recharacterizes the transaction as a taxable domestic supply; the ETA assesses VAT retroactively at 14%, together with statutory delay addition from the deemed due date and fines of between EGP 10,000 and EGP 50,000 per invoice, plus confiscation of wrongfully obtained refunds. Deliberate document forgery or fake outward remittance to obtain refund constitutes tax fraud and is subject to criminal prosecution of the legal representative, imprisonment, and joint and several liability. The ETA further retains set-off rights for refund claims against unrelated tax arrears of the entity or its related parties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) is partially convertible. Current account transactions — including trade-related payments, service payments, and remittances — are generally permitted through authorized banks upon submission of underlying documentation (invoices, contracts, customs declarations). Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment inflows, profit repatriation, and loan proceeds, are subject to CBE registration, investment approval via the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) / the Egyptian Ministry of Investment, and compliance with the CBE's formalities under the Foreign Exchange (FX) Manual. Profit remittance requires tax clearance certificates from the Egyptian Tax Authority, audited financial statements, proof of capital repatriation registration, and a no-objection letter from the relevant bank. Dividend and branch remittance protocols mandate a 5% withholding tax on distributed profits unless reduced by a double-tax treaty. The EGP is not freely convertible in offshore markets; a parallel-market premium may exist, so all authorized conversions must be executed at the official CBE daily fixing rate via a licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations applicable to Egyptian exporters, all export proceeds must be received through an Egyptian authorized bank within 90 days from the date of shipment (bill of lading/airway bill date) unless a written extension is obtained from the CBE. A mandatory surrender ratio of 100% applies: upon receipt, the export proceeds must be converted to EGP at the official CBE exchange rate and credited to the exporter's EGP operational account. An exporter may retain a foreign-currency balance only in a CBE-approved foreign-currency retention account, with the retention ceiling and utilization governed by the prevailing CBE circulars and subject to bank-level compliance reviews. For dairy commodity exports (whole milk powder, skimmed milk powder, lactose, whey), the required supporting packet for surrender includes the export credit contract, bill of lading, commercial invoice, customs forward declaration, and the bank's export registration form.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the national monetary authority and regulator of all banking, FX, and payments activity in Egypt, operating under the Egyptian Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Exchange Control Law No. 97 of 1976.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary clearing and settlement rail. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) and non-bank digital payment providers cannot directly hold EGP, are not licensed by the CBE under the Fintech Law No. 5 of 2022, and cannot process statutory export-proceeds surrender or import settlement through the Egyptian banking system. EMIs may only be used as an ancillary channel for collecting overseas receivables in certain low-restriction jurisdictions, provided funds are subsequently routed to an authorized Egyptian bank account through a SWIFT correspondent in compliance with CBE AML/KYC rules. Any arrangement involving an unlicensed EMI that creates a direct EGP-denominated liability to Egyptian counterparties would be unlawful.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All FDI equity capital injections for the Egyptian LLC must be routed via SWIFT in the original currency using the MT103 or MT202 COV message format and must reference the CBE-registered investment file number, the GAFI capital-account approval, and the designated purpose code for equity participation. The remitting shareholder must include the LLC's bank account IBAN (in the bank-approved format) and a beneficiary bank name notifying the underlying corporate relationship. Acceptable purpose codes under CBE and common correspondent-bank standards include code '301' (equity capital) or the CBE-specific 'Uberis' correspondence code for capital account inflows. To ensure successful clearing, the LLC must furnish to the receiving bank: a notarized board resolution resolving to open the escrow/operational account, a copy of the incorporation certificate and file number, shareholder identification (passport/CID), a capital-contribution schedule, proof of legitimate source of funds, and, if the contribution is above the USD 50,000 statutory threshold, a GAFI investment certificate and a tax registration certificate for the LLC. Funds must be first credited to the Capital Escrow Account; subsequent transfers from escrow to the operational account require a supporting capitalization certificate and CBE authorization where applicable.Outbound trade settlements (payment to overseas milk-powder suppliers, freight lines, quality testing providers, and cooperative aggregators) must be executed through the Egyptian banking system using SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments or commercial documentary letters of credit (LCs) where a payment undertaking is required. For TT/Wire settlements, the disbursement instruction must include the invoice number and date, a copy of the wholesaled contract, the certificate of origin, non-GMO/lactose specifications, and a bank confirmation of customs approval for the underlying shipment. For LC-backed purchase transactions, a documentary LC should be issued to the foreign beneficiary's bank via SWIFT MT700, incorporating Incoterms 2020 (usually CIP or FOB), a piggyback quality certificate requirement (ISO, USDC, or ESI-certified milk-powder), and a 5-business-day tolerance clause for quarantine inspection. Pursuant to CBE's trade-finance framework, a bank may disburse EGP or USD only after receiving the customs declaration (bill of entry form 'C19' and, if necessary, the release certificate from the Egyptian National Food Safety Authority), followed by a matching payment order within the permitted trade-financing period (generally 180 days from shipment date, extendable for agricultural commodities). To reduce FX conversion leakage, a same-day USD/EGP transitory clearing via CBE's banking settlement system is required if the disbursement is to a local EGP beneficiary against a foreign-currency LC.CBE-authorized outright/covered forward contracts with an Egyptian licensed bank, with a maximum tenor of 12 months and requiring a genuine underlying export/import exposure (dairy purchase contract, LC, or invoice) to qualify for hedge accounting treatment.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs or 'deliverable' equivalents approved onshore) on EGP/USD executed only through a CBE-licensed dealer or through recognized offshore NDF brokers where the shoreline bank documentation permits such settlement; each NDF must be tied to firm trade cash flows and must not exceed a 1.2x overhedge ratio.Cross-currency swaps (USD/EGP) executed with a licensed Egyptian bank or a CBE-approved global counterparty, collateralized with treasury bills, cash margin, or bank guarantees, and confined to the hedge of long-term milk-powder supply contracts and capital-account liabilities; all periodic settlements must be recorded with the hedge file for CBE inspection.FX collar/option strategies sold by Egyptian licensed banks as extended-horizon hedging solutions where initial premium and strike framework comply with CBE's prohibition on speculative derivative transactions; each option trade must be continuously supported by the underlying commodity physical-flow exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8 million total related party transactions in a tax period (local file), or EGP 80 million gross revenue with cross-border related party transactions exceeding EGP 2 million; master file required when Egypt-headquartered group turnover exceeds EGP 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not enact a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but general anti-abuse rules under Law 91/2005 and the binding DTA substance principles deny treaty benefits where the LLC lacks substantive business. For a dairy commodity trading captive, the ETA and Egyptian courts require: local office, active bank accounts, genuine staff implementing purchase/sale contracts, physical or documentary handling of milk powder, and entrepreneurial risk. A shell LLC whose daily decisions are made by the foreign parent will be recharacterized as a PE or null under substance-over-form.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of triple the tax evaded (impostable on the entity), plus monthly late penalty of 2% of unpaid tax. Under Law 91/2005 Article 133, conviction carries imprisonment for evasion when the amount exceeds EGP 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, financial officers, and the authorized accountant may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties if they signed a knowingly false return. ETA can block bank accounts, attach assets, obtain a travel ban under Egypt's executive enforcement law, and seek disqualification from board membership.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA may deny the deductible cost of purchases and services, reallocate profit under Article 52 of Law 91/2005, levy 22.5% corporate tax on the deemed Egyptian-source profit, impose withholding taxes on deemed distributions, and apply a 30% penalty on the net under-reported tax.

Director personal liability: The Egyptian-nominated director is exposed to criminal liability for 'facilitating a foreign company's tax escape'. Courts can pierce the LLC veil and hold the foreign ultimate shareholder liable if the limited liability is used as a sham. Travel bans and seizure of personal bank accounts are common in ETA penalty enforcement.
Money Laundering and Trade-Based Illicit Flows

Statutory sanction: Violation of Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002 and CBE regulations: fines from EGP 50,000 to EGP 5,000,000, confiscation of the underlying assets, imprisonment up to 7 years for company officers in severe cases, and escalation to the prosecutor's financial corruption department.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as compliance officers (even if honorary) face personal criminal punishment, including mandatory imprisonment for AML violations. Directors can be banned from traveling abroad while investigations are pending, and their personal assets used to satisfy the entity's AML fines under joint liability.
