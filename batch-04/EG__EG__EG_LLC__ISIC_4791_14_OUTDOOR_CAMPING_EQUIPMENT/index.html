Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Companies Registration and One-Stop Shop Directorate, in coordination with the Commercial Registry Directorate, Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade.

Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 as amended by Law No. 4 of 2009 and Law No. 17 of 2019, Articles 1-16 (Limited Liability Companies); read together with Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, Articles 2 and 4, and its Executive Regulations (Prime Ministerial Decree No. 2310 of 2018) establishing the GAFI One-Stop Shop.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation Contract (Meethaq Al-Taassiss / Aqd Al-Tasees) executed before a licensed Notary Public or through GAFI electronic notarization services, filed via the GAFI One-Stop Shop portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for LLCs under Law 159 of 1981 as amended; however, the capital stated in the incorporation contract must be fully subscribed and deposited into a dedicated Egyptian bank account in the company's name, evidenced by a bank capital deposit certificate issued not more than five days prior to filing with GAFI.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire Safety Certificate (Al-Himaya Al-Madaniya) required for any warehouse or office premises exceeding 100 square meters, conditioned on a successful structural load inspection and fire prevention inspection by the local Civil Defense Directorate; issuance of the commercial premises license is also conditioned on obtaining workplace occupational health and safety clearance from the competent governorate inspection office under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for LLCs under Law 159 of 1981 as amended; however, the capital stated in the incorporation contract must be fully subscribed and deposited into a dedicated Egyptian bank account in the company's name, evidenced by a bank capital deposit certificate issued not more than five days prior to filing with GAFI.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Official portal: www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign and non-resident entities must register for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and obtain a Tax Card through the ETA online portal by submitting certified constitutional documents, a local tax representative mandate (if applicable), proof of Egyptian business activity, and relevant licenses. E-commerce suppliers may use the simplified registration procedure introduced for non-resident direct sellers under Law No. 91 of 2005 and VAT Law No. 67 of 2016.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign and non-resident entities must register for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and obtain a Tax Card through the ETA online portal by submitting certified constitutional documents, a local tax representative mandate (if applicable), proof of Egyptian business activity, and relevant licenses. E-commerce suppliers may use the simplified registration procedure introduced for non-resident direct sellers under Law No. 91 of 2005 and VAT Law No. 67 of 2016.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide pass-through check-the-box elections. An Egyptian LLC is discretionarily taxable at the corporate level. Available mechanisms include: VAT group registration election (per ETA Decision No. 114 of 2020), consolidated corporate tax filing election for resident corporations (subject to ETA approval and minimum ownership thresholds), and OECD-aligned hybrid mismatch limitation elections. No branch-level imputation election is permitted for LLCs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory registration threshold); non-resident suppliers supplying digital or remote services to Egyptian consumers must register regardless of turnover if sales exceed EGP 500,000 annually

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically delivered outside Egypt are zero-rated. Services rendered to non-residents and used wholly outside Egypt are also zero-rated when supported by proper evidence. Zero-rating requires proof of physical export, receipt of foreign currency through Egyptian banking channels, and ETA pre-approval for recurring export exemption in limited cases.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Arabic: شهادة استلام الحصيلة)
  • Commercial sales invoice and the underlying export contract
  • Bank transfer receipt evidencing foreign currency proceeds received in Egypt

ETA e-invoicing is mandatory for VAT taxpayers in staged phases. All B2B and B2C transactions handled through approved channels must be transmitted in near-real-time to the ETA portal/API, validated, and returned with a UUID and QR code. E-receipt (POS) integration is mandatory for merchant transactions. Failure to obtain ETA clearance invalidates the invoice for VAT recovery.

The ETA and Customs Authority may retroactively claw back zero-rated output VAT if export evidence is deficient or proceeds are not fully repatriated within the 180-day window. Penalties include late-payment interest of 2% per month, administrative fines up to EGP 50,000 per fiscal violation, and criminal fines of up to 100% of tax evaded for fraudulent zero-rating, customs declaration forgery, or deliberate under-invoicing. Executive officers may be subject to personal liability and imprisonment for serious evidential fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-floating, partially convertible currency regime for the Egyptian Pound (EGP). Current account transactions, including trade-related payments and service fees, are generally permitted through licensed banks. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and dividend repatriation, require CBE registration/approval and supporting documentation. Profit remittance is permissible on presentation of audited financial statements, corporate tax clearance, and evidence that the capital was introduced through licensed banks and registered with CBE/Ministry of Investment. Practical constraints include periodic USD liquidity tightness, EGP devaluation risk, and enhanced bank-level documentation checks, which can delay remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be received into an account of the Egyptian exporter at a licensed Egyptian bank and fully repatriated to Egypt within 90 days from the date of shipment. There is no mandatory statutory conversion of retained export proceeds into EGP, but the exporter must evidence the corresponding customs export declarations and CBE settlement forms. Any delay in repatriation without a CBE-approved waiver subjects the exporter to penalties, suspension of export facilities, and enhanced AML/KYC review.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) acting as the national foreign exchange administrator under Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE Foreign Exchange Regulations issued pursuant to the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 88 of 2003, as amended.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — Offshore digital EMI and payment/e-money institutions are not CBE-licensed and cannot lawfully provide local EGP clearing, hold Egyptian corporate accounts, or access CBE settlement systems. They may be used only as ancillary multi-currency wallets outside Egypt for collections in third countries; they are not viable as the primary treasury or clearing rail for an Egyptian LLC. Their use increases wire-trail opacity and may trigger enhanced KYC/AML scrutiny at local banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's bank account to the LLC's account at a licensed Egyptian bank using the CBE-designated purpose code for foreign direct investment or shareholder loan. The receiving bank must obtain a copy of the investment registration, board resolution, source-of-funds confirmation, and recorded CBE inward remittance form. The LLC must ensure the full wire narrative identifies the entity name, the registered investment certificate, and the underlying purpose; the bank then credits the proceeds and files with CBE's international transactions monitoring system. For capital amounts above CBE thresholds, pre-approval or registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) is recommended.Outbound trade settlements for inventory and logistics are cleared through SWIFT MT103/MT202 payments and, for larger or documentary-risk transactions, through irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit. The paying bank requires a compliant import payment application, underlying commercial contract, invoice, packing list, transport document, and customs declaration. The LLC must route all foreign payments through its licensed Egyptian bank; the bank may require document-linked FX approval and will debit the corresponding EGP or foreign-currency account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared when annual related-party transactions exceed EGP 8 million; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual turnover exceeding EGP 100 million or when a single related-party transaction exceeds EGP 20 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the general anti-avoidance rule in Article 113 of Law 91/2005 and the Tax Fraud Law (Law 30 of 2020) authorize the ETA to disregard arrangements lacking commercial substance. For an internet retailer, material substance indicators include a physical Egyptian management office, inventory warehousing, purchase and sales contracts executed in Egypt, an operating Egyptian bank account, and autonomous pricing/risk control over the digital sales channel.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to 100% of the evaded tax, an administrative fine up to EGP 100,000, and monthly postponement surcharges; criminal conviction for intentional evasion can yield imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or knew of the evasion are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts; courts may issue travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, and impose prison sentences under Article 137 of Law 91/2005.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ETA may nullify the tax registration, disallow treaty benefits, recharacterise income, and impose back-tax assessments plus 100% penalty on any unpaid tax; registration may also be revoked under anti-shell directives.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable in restitution for the full tax and penalties; where false declarations were submitted, imprisonment from one to five years is possible, and the responsible shareholder may be personally stripped of limited liability protection.
AML / Financial Crime Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines under Egypt AML Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended, ranging up to EGP 100,000 per violation, alongside suspension of the tax card and administrative delisting from export support programs; the ETA may also impose a 4% transaction-based anti-money laundering levy.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face imprisonment between one and ten years for laundering proceeds generated through such payment structures; assets and bank accounts can be seized; travel restricted until full restitution is made.
Transfer Pricing and False Documentation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable income, plus 100% tax evasion penalty if the mispricing was willful; refusal to produce documentation triggers an administrative penalty of up to 1% of the value of undocumented transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate Form 16 certificates or certified TP disclosures may be personally assessed for the resulting tax deficiency; if fraud is established, they are jointly liable for restitution and subject to imprisonment up to five years.
