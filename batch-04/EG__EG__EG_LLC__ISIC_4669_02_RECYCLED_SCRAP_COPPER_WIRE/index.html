Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) of the Ministry of Finance; official unified tax services and electronic invoicing portals at https://www.eta.gov.eg and https://einvoice.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must: (1) incorporate through the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) one-stop shop and obtain commercial registration; (2) apply to the ETA for a Tax Card, which generates the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN), submitting the incorporation contract, notarized power of attorney, commercial register extract, lease/activity certificate, and manager appointment deed; (3) register for VAT under Law No. 67 of 2016 once taxable annual turnover exceeds EGP 500,000, or earlier on a voluntary basis, obtaining a separate VAT code; (4) activate the ETA e-invoicing account by obtaining an approved electronic signature and completing API client onboarding from the ETA sandbox to the production gateway. A non-resident seller without an onshore entity must appoint a tax representative, register a permanent establishment, and obtain a TIN before issuing Egyptian invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; an Egyptian LLC is a separate legal taxpayer subject to corporate income tax at the standard headliner rate of 22.5%; resident-managed LLCs are assessed on worldwide income, while non-resident owners and PEs are assessed on Egyptian-source income only. The LLC has no fiscal transparency or pass-through status, and purely exporting activities remain corporate-taxable unless specific Suez Canal Economic Zone or new-investment incentives apply.

Available election forms

Egyptian law does not provide a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election for LLCs. Available statutory elections include: voluntary VAT registration under Law 67/2016 before reaching the mandatory EGP 500,000 threshold; the simplified/presumptive tax regime for micro and small enterprises under Law 152 of 2020, subject to turnover limits; and the choice of eligible free-zone investment incentives under Egypt's Investment Law, where approved by GAFI, which may alter effective direct tax exposure but not LLC entity-level character.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies; mandatory VAT registration applies to any person whose taxable turnover exceeds this threshold, with voluntary registration permitted below it; registration is also mandatory for taxable importers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate applies to goods physically exported from Egypt, provided the seller is VAT-registered, the goods are dispatched under a valid customs export declaration (Bayan), title and risk pass to a non-resident buyer, the export is completed within the permitted period, and the value is received in convertible foreign currency through an authorized Egyptian bank. Related input VAT may be refunded on substantiation. Services rendered to a non-resident and consumed outside Egypt may also be zero-rated when properly evidenced. Any applicable state restrictions or permits for recycled scrap exports must be complied with in parallel to tax treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan) and Egypt Single Administrative Document
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or credited foreign-currency bank advice
  • Signed commercial invoice and packing list
  • Port or terminal release documents
  • Statutory scrap export license or GOEIC compliance certificate, if applicable

All VAT-registered taxpayers are phased into mandatory ETA e-invoicing. The issuer must transmit invoices to the ETA API gateway in JSON/XML in real time, signed with the taxpayer's approved electronic signature; the ETA performs schema, duplication, and fiscal-validation checks and returns a unique invoice identifier, a digital stamp, and a QR code. B2C cashier receipts are separately covered by the e-receipts mandate. Invoices not cleared or not integrated are treated as fiscally unissued, with consequent denial of input VAT deduction and fixed penalties.

The ETA cross-matches e-invoices, customs export declarations, shipping records, and bank FX receipts; a zero-rating claim is retrospectively denied if the documentary bundle is incomplete, the goods are not proved to have left Egypt, or export proceeds are not repatriated through the banking system within 180 days from shipment (absent an approved extension), at which point VAT at 14% becomes payable with delay penalties. Deliberate false export claims constitute tax evasion under Law 67/2016 and the Unified Tax Procedures Law, carrying monthly delay fines, additional penalties of up to 100-200% of the evaded tax, criminal sanctions including imprisonment, and a five-year or longer reassessment window in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound operates under a managed-float regime with current account convertibility for valid trade transactions routed through authorized banks. Capital account transactions, including direct equity inflows, foreign financing, and profit repatriation, remain subject to CBE authorization, documentary verification, and tax clearance. Profit remittance requires audited financial statements, a valid tax card, and confirmation that all Egyptian tax liabilities have been settled. Temporary FX liquidity narrowing can result in discretionary allocation or queuing for outward remittances; therefore trade flows must use documented underlying merchandise and supported payment purposes to obtain priority.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters must repatriate full export proceeds to a CBE-licensed bank within 180 days from the Bill of Lading date, unless a shorter period is prescribed by commodity-specific CBE circulars. The proceeds must be deposited in the exporter's foreign-currency account at an authorized Egyptian bank; conversion to Egyptian pounds is optional unless the CBE imposes a temporary full or partial surrender requirement during acute currency shortages. Failure to meet the repatriation timeline triggers fines, suspension of export financing facilities, and denial of subsequent usance LC facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore electronic money institution (EMI) licenses are not recognized by the CBE. A foreign fintech cannot provide domestic Egyptian IBANs or clear trade settlements without obtaining a local payments license and entering a sponsor-bank arrangement with an Egyptian licensed bank. For documentary trade, a CBE-authorized commercial bank conduit is legally mandatory; offshore virtual IBANs would be rejected for customs and CBE FX registration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign investment capital must be wired via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 into the LLC's EGP-denominated or USD-denominated account at an Egyptian licensed bank. The remittance instruction must use a valid CBE purpose code such as 1000 for foreign direct investment in the non-financial sector or 6311 for payment for exported industrial goods. The recipient must hold a GAFI investor certificate and submit a copy of the remittance confirmation with the underlying share subscription agreement. The receiving bank will file a CBE foreign-trade declaration, and funds are credited only after clearance of sanctions and regulatory filters. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% of taxable profits for Egyptian resident companies and Egyptian permanent establishments of non-resident enterprises.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends distributed by an Egyptian resident company are generally subject to a 10% dividend withholding or dividend-distribution tax. The rate may be reduced to 5% for qualifying dividends from listed shares where the statutory ownership and holding-period conditions are satisfied. Treaty rates may further reduce the Egyptian rate, subject to treaty entitlement, residence certification, beneficial ownership, and procedural compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within four months after the end of the financial year. For a calendar-year company ending on 31 December, the ordinary deadline is 30 April of the following year. A company using an approved non-calendar financial year follows the corresponding four-month rule.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The withholding agent must deduct tax at the time required by law, remit it within the statutory period, and submit the required declaration and certificates. The exact deadline depends on the payment category and current ETA procedures.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian transfer-pricing rules generally require local-file and master-file documentation where related-party transactions exceed EGP 8,000,000 for the relevant transaction category or otherwise fall within the applicable documentation requirements. A country-by-country report generally applies to an ultimate parent or surrogate parent in the relevant jurisdiction where consolidated group revenue reaches the statutory threshold, commonly EGP 3,000,000,000 or its prescribed equivalent under current Egyptian rules. Thresholds, filing formats, and submission timing must be checked against current ETA instructions for the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not generally operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance remains relevant under the arm's-length principle, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty anti-abuse principles, the general anti-avoidance framework, permanent-establishment rules, and the ETA's power to challenge sham or artificial arrangements. A copper-scrap trading LLC should maintain genuine Egyptian management, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises, inventory controls, commercial decision-making, banking activity, risk assumption, and documentary evidence proportionate to its functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, additional tax, interest or statutory late-payment charges, administrative fines, and criminal prosecution. Tax-evasion offenses may carry imprisonment and fines that can be linked to the evaded tax, with enhanced consequences for aggravated or repeated conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, authorized signatories, accountants, and other responsible individuals may face personal criminal liability where they directed, approved, assisted, or knowingly tolerated the conduct. Civil recovery, attachment of assets, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may arise under the applicable criminal and tax procedures.
VAT and invoice fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT assessments, denial of input credits, repayment of improperly refunded tax, additional tax, administrative fines, seizure or suspension measures where available, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent invoicing or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and signatories may be prosecuted personally for intentional VAT fraud or fraudulent documentation and may face civil recovery, asset measures, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Customs and smuggling violations

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, confiscation, fines, forfeiture of goods or conveyances, suspension of customs privileges, and criminal prosecution under Egyptian customs and anti-smuggling laws.

Director personal liability: Directors, customs brokers, logistics personnel, and beneficial owners may incur personal criminal or civil liability where they participated in, instructed, or knowingly facilitated the violation. Asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply.
Unlicensed scrap dealing and environmental non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, license cancellation, confiscation, environmental remediation orders, fines, compensation claims, and criminal penalties under applicable commercial, environmental, waste, and licensing legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may be personally liable for intentional or negligent environmental and licensing breaches, including remediation costs, civil damages, administrative restrictions, and imprisonment where a criminal offense is established.
Anti-money laundering and proceeds-of-crime exposure

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting failures, account freezing, transaction blocking, confiscation, fines, and criminal prosecution under Egyptian anti-money-laundering and proceeds-of-crime legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, beneficial owners, and employees may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, concealment, facilitation, or failure to report where legally required. Asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary or secondary tax adjustments, denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessments, penalties for inaccurate returns or missing documentation, and late-payment charges.

Director personal liability: Directors and signatories may face personal consequences where transfer-pricing manipulation forms part of deliberate tax evasion, fraud, falsification, or concealment. Ordinary bona fide pricing disputes ordinarily create company-level tax liability rather than automatic veil piercing.
Treaty abuse and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding at domestic rates, recovery of underwithheld tax, additional tax, penalties, interest, and potential fraud or tax-evasion prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly submit false treaty documents or approve abusive arrangements may face personal civil and criminal liability, including asset measures, travel restrictions, and imprisonment.
Corporate records, beneficial ownership, and AML-KYC failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, registration suspension, refusal of filings, enhanced tax audits, transaction restrictions, and criminal sanctions where the conduct involves fraud, money laundering, or concealment of ownership.

Director personal liability: Managers and authorized representatives may be personally liable for false filings and intentional concealment. Civil restitution, asset attachment, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply depending on the offense.
Profit repatriation and foreign-exchange control risk

Statutory sanction: Invalid distribution claims, withholding-tax reassessment, banking and foreign-exchange compliance measures, fines, recovery of funds, and potential criminal exposure for fraud, money laundering, or unauthorized financial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions or knowingly mischaracterized transfers may face shareholder claims, creditor claims, tax liability, civil restitution, asset restrictions, travel bans, and imprisonment where criminal conduct is proven.
Bribery and public-sector corruption

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, confiscation of benefits, contract cancellation, debarment, and corporate or administrative sanctions under Egyptian anti-corruption and penal laws.

Director personal liability: Individuals who offer, authorize, arrange, or conceal bribes may face personal imprisonment, fines, confiscation, travel restrictions, and civil recovery. Corporate liability may arise through acts of representatives and responsible management.
