Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Egyptian Limited Liability Company has no statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement; the capital determined in the Articles of Association must be fully subscribed. Where cash capital is contributed prior to incorporation, it must be deposited in a temporary bank account opened with a licensed Egyptian bank and held as an escrow/deposit block until the company receives its Commercial Register certificate and the blocked funds are released to the company treasury.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Civil Defense Authority and an occupancy clearance from the local municipal licensing office is required for the registered office and warehouse. The workplace must undergo a structural load inspection only if heavy shelving is installed, and occupational health declarations under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 must be posted and delivered to the competent labor office.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance - official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned LLC must register with the Egyptian Tax Authority at the competent tax office having jurisdiction over its Egyptian head office or place of effective management. Required filings include the incorporation contract, commercial registry extract (including foreign ownership details), board resolution appointing a tax representative if non-resident, power of attorney for the representative, lease or ownership evidence of the Egyptian premises, and valid IDs of managers and shareholders. The ETA issues a unified tax card and separate VAT registration number. Application must be made before commencing taxable activity, and a tax file number is obtained after document review, on-site inspection where necessary, and issuance of the compliance card.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or free-standing entity classification election exists under Egyptian domestic tax law. Egyptian LLCs are automatically treated as taxable persons at entity level under Law No. 91 of 2005. No domestic hybrid mismatch election is available; foreign transparent entities may be recharacterized by reference to the applicable double tax treaty and the ordinary principles of the Egyptian Income Tax Law. The LLC may elect to register for VAT within the statutory period upon exceeding the registration threshold, but substance and legal form govern classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible goods, including acetate eyeglass frames, are eligible for zero-rating when title has passed to a foreign buyer, the goods physically leave Egypt to a destination outside Egypt through customs procedures, payment is received in convertible foreign currency through banking channels, and the exporter retains complete documentation proving the export transaction. Services physically rendered outside Egypt or directly related to exported goods may also be zero-rated under the VAT Law No. 67 of 2016 where the recipient is outside Egypt and the benefit is consumed abroad. The exporter must be a registered VAT person and must file the export lists and supporting schedules electronically.

Egypt mandates phased real-time ETA e-invoicing under the electronic invoice and electronic receipt regime. Invoices must be generated in the ETA-compliant XML/JSON format, submitted through the ETA integration APIs or approved service-provider portal, validated and cleared in close-to-real-time before issuance to the buyer, and carry a unique ETA approval code and embedded QR barcode. Intra-Community-style clearance, digital stamping, and rejection handling are required under Ministerial Decree No. 491 of 2022 and subsequent amendments. All tax invoices and credit/debit notes must be digitally signed and transmitted; non-compliant invoices are treated as non-deductible and may block customs and tax clearances.

The ETA and Customs Authority may conduct joint audits of export zero-rating, reviewing bank receipts, customs manifests, freight documents, purchase records, and internal costing. Circumvention, fictitious exports, overstatement of export values, forged remittance certificates, or failure to repatriate proceeds within the prescribed 180-day deadline result in reversal of zero-rating, immediate assessment of VAT at 14% plus late-payment and delay penalties, monetary fines up to the amount of tax evaded, suspension of the taxpayer's e-invoicing capability, and potential criminal prosecution with imprisonment under Law No. 67 of 2016. Retroactive clawback may extend to prior tax periods where documentary fraud is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the March 2024 unification, the Egyptian Pound is formally market-determined under a flexible exchange-rate regime supported by the IMF Extended Fund Facility. Current-account transactions (goods trade, freight, insurance, commissions, and profit remittance against verified audited earnings) are freely convertible for licensed corporate clients subject to full banking documentation. Capital-account transactions remain regulated: foreign-direct-investment equity injections, intercompany loan servicing, liquidation proceeds, and portfolio repatriation require a CBE-licensed bank channel, GAFI registration documentation, and proof of original entry of funds; portfolio inflows additionally use the CBE Repatriation Mechanism. For the optical-frames wholesale LLC, dividend repatriation from genuine audited export earnings is lawful, but procedural hurdles include un-matched import/export customs declarations, unsettled withholding tax (10% domestic rate, often reduced to 5% under Egypt's double-taxation agreements), and the bank's demand for a complete source-of-wealth file for the foreign shareholder. There is no legal incompatibility with profit remittance; operational friction is documentary, not convertibility-based.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must repatriate the full value of export proceeds into an Egyptian-licensed bank account within 90 calendar days from the shipment date (bill of lading or airway bill date, as applicable). No mandatory fixed-ratio surrender or compulsory conversion to EGP is currently imposed under the market-determined regime; proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account with an Egyptian bank or converted to EGP at the interbank market rate. Exporters must maintain the evidential chain comprising the customs export declaration, beneficiary commercial invoice, bill of lading, and incoming SWIFT MT103. Failure to meet the 90-day window exposes the entity to CBE monetary penalties, bank negative-list classification, and suspension of trade-finance and FX facilities.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), exercising exclusive foreign-exchange regulation through the Foreign Exchange Administration under the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020 (CBBL), as amended, and CBE Board of Directors' circulars.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: non-resident shareholder issues an outgoing SWIFT MT103 wire to the EG_LLC designated account at an Egyptian Tier-1 bank, currency USD or EUR, with field 70 explicitly stating the purpose: 'EQUITY INJECTION FOR SHARE CAPITAL - LLCOWNER - [Company Commercial Registry number]'. Egyptian banks do not use IBAN; the beneficiary field must carry the exact local account number plus the bank's SWIFT/BIC. The receiving bank will classify the inflow under the CBE Balance-of-Payments code 'Direct Investment - Equity', issue a foreign-currency inflow certificate or capital-fund certificate, and record the investment in the company's file. The company must file the capital increase with GAFI and, where applicable, update its commercial registry paid-in capital; any EGP conversion is executed at the prevailing interbank rate. The inflow certificate and GAFI stamp become the essential documents for future dividend and liquidation repatriation.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfer for routine consignments and low-risk established suppliers. For major acetate and stock-frame supply contracts, an irrevocable commercial documentary letter of credit (SWIFT MT700, at-sight, usually with a confirming bank requested) is the recommended rail, with documentary collection (MT400/MT202) permissible for recurring low-risk suppliers. Each outflow requires a valid importer registration with GOEIC, an active tax card, the underlying pro-forma or commercial invoice, and the customs bill of entry after clearing; the Egyptian bank converts EGP at market rate and debits the USD account. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: fine equal to 100% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors personally liable for restitution, fines and possible imprisonment
Failure to Maintain Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment to deemed profit, penalties up to 100% of tax benefit, possible deregistration

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability; possible travel bans and civil claims
Non-Compliance with Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty EGP 50,000 to EGP 500,000 plus interest on underpaid tax

Director personal liability: Personal liability for penalties and potential criminal referral
Improper Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Additional tax equal to difference plus 2% monthly interest and possible criminal sanctions

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for additional tax, interest and potential imprisonment

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors face civil restitution, fines and imprisonment
