Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed flexible exchange-rate regime for the EGP, most recently affirmed in December 2023 and deepened in March 2024 under the IMF Extended Fund Facility. Current-account transactions, including cross-border subscription receipts for physical goods and digital services, are broadly convertible through CBE-authorised banks without case-by-case licence, subject to presentation of export invoice, customs declaration, or service contract and bank-side E-Form registration. The capital account remains subject to documentary and registration controls: inbound equity injections require GAFI investment registration and a bank-registered capital account; outbound profit remittance is guaranteed for foreign investors under Article 3 of the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 but requires audited financial statements, board/EGM distribution resolution, ETA tax clearance certificate, and availability of FX liquidity through the interbank market, which in practice can cause timing delay during periods of hard-currency scarcity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE instructions require that 100% of export proceeds derived from subscription-box merchandise be repatriated to Egypt through an authorised bank and credited to the exporter's EGP or FX account within 30 days from the date of customs export declaration; for cross-border subscription services, the repatriation window is 180 days from invoice date. The mandatory surrender ratio, reintroduced at 50% for exporters during the 2022-2023 FX-stabilisation period, was rescinded under the December 2023 flexible-exchange-rate policy; proceeds may be retained in a domiciled FX account for approved supplier payments or converted to EGP at the interbank rate. Each export shipment must be supported by a CBE-linked export e-invoice and the proceeds must reconcile to the bank-registered export documentation, with non-compliance potentially triggering suspension of exporter registration and penalty under CBE circular enforcement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), exercising statutory powers under the Central Banking and Monetary System Law No. 194 of 2020, including the Foreign Exchange Administration, Banking Supervision, and Licensing functions; AML/CFT supervision rests on Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended by Law No. 106 of 2021.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury accumulator; MEDIUM only as a sponsored payment overlay. Foreign electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, and Payoneer hold no CBE banking or payment-service-provider licence and are not permitted to maintain EGP deposit accounts or issue e-money independently under CBE payment-system regulations issued pursuant to Law No. 194 of 2020. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be remitted via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the designated Capital Escrow Account at the primary Egyptian bank, with field 50K identifying EG_LLC and field 59 matching the exact CBE-registered account name. The bank will not release funds until the GAFI investment file (post-formation registration amendment) is obtained, updated commercial registry extract is filed, board resolution authorising the account mandate is approved, and the CBE E-Form/Sirena transaction code covering direct investment equity capital is recorded. Once matched, funds are immediately available for working capital. Shareholder loans are distinct and require a subordinated facility agreement, CBE code for external shareholder financing, arm's-length interest terms, and GAFI disclosure; principal repayment is treated as a capital-account remittance requiring the same tax-clearance certification and bank-side E-Form approval.Supplier and logistics disbursements flow through four clearing rails: (i) Commercial Import Letter of Credit issued by the Egyptian bank via SWIFT MT700/MT710 to the supplier's bank under UCP 600, used for inventory procurement with 30-120 day terms; (ii) documentary collections in D/P or D/A form using MT740-series messages for mid-sized recurring suppliers; (iii) open-account SWIFT MT103 remittances for pre-approved content, packaging, and software vendors, each matched to a purchase order, vendor pro-forma invoice, CBE import/export E-Form, and customs declaration when goods cross the border; and (iv) intra-group treasury sweeps to the offshore parent only after ETA tax clearance and CBE capital-remittance filing. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final dividend withholding tax imposed on profit distributions to shareholders under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended; a double tax agreement (DTA) may reduce the rate to between 0% and 10% depending on the residence jurisdiction, holding percentage and satisfaction of beneficial-ownership and substance requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

CIT return and final self-assessment settlement are due within 4 months after fiscal year-end; for a 31 December FYE, the deadline is 30 April; quarterly provisional CIT instalments are due within one month after each calendar quarter.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Identification and Registration Legal entity name, ETA tax registration number, commercial registration number, e-invoicing certificate code, fiscal year start and end dates, and ISIC 4791 retail e-commerce activity classification. A non-resident LLC with an Egyptian permanent establishment (PE) obtains an Egyptian TIN of its own and files Form No. 4; a non-resident seller with no Egyptian PE or nexus must not file a CIT return and is instead subject only to Egyptian withholding obligations of the Egyptian counter-party. Late filing attracts an administrative fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 20,000, plus a statutory monthly delay surcharge on unpaid tax; non-filing enables ETA estimated assessment at the full 22.5% rate with surcharge; continued non-payment triggers enforced collection against bank accounts and merchant settlement balances, inventory attachment, suspension of the e-invoice certificate and a travel ban on the company's legal representative.
Box 1a - Revenue Schedule Gross subscription-box sales settled through Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net, reduced by refunds, chargebacks, discounts and cancelled orders; foreign-currency revenue converted at the Central Bank of Egypt exchange rate on the realisation date. Revenue recognising a maintained Egyptian warehouse, local fulfilment centre or substantial return-handling operation will constitute PE-triggering physical presence; revenue from pure cross-border import into Egypt without local fixed place is generally treated as offshore income of the non-resident. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian Pound (EGP): a Local File is mandatory when the aggregate value of related-party transactions during the tax year reaches EGP 15 million; a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above EGP 15 billion; Country-by-Country reporting and notification obligations attach at the same EGP 15 billion group-revenue threshold, approximating the OECD EUR 750 million standard.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not administer a separate GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, substance is enforced through free-zone and SCZone licensing conditions, beneficial-ownership clauses in Egyptian DTAs, and the general anti-abuse doctrine of the ETA. A subscription-box service operated through a mailbox or booking-company presence without real Egyptian staff, physical inventory, fulfilment controls and commercial decision-making will be disregarded as a sham: the free-zone or SCZone exemption will be revoked retroactively, treaty relief will be denied, and the profits will be re-characterised as fully taxable Egyptian-source income at 22.5% with surcharges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Assessment of all concealed tax at 22.5% plus statutory monthly delay surcharges; a fine equal to the amount of evaded tax; imprisonment of up to 5 years for corporate officers; publication of the persons concerned in the ETA tax-evader register; suspension of the e-invoicing certificate and tax card; forced execution against bank accounts, merchant settlement balances, inventory and receivables.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil is applied where managers sign or authorise false returns: directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax and surcharges, face travel bans and asset-freeze orders, and may be personally imprisoned for tax-evasion offences under the income-tax law and the Egyptian Criminal Procedure Code.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA re-determination of the taxable profit at arm's length with additional CIT assessed at 22.5% on the adjustment, plus delay interest; administrative fines for failure to respond to ETA documentation and information requests; in egregious cases of deliberate profit shifting, a penalty multiplier of up to twice the additional tax and criminal referral for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors acting as statutory representatives become personally liable when they divert assets or strip the LLC during audit; the ETA may attach personal assets, restrict exit from Egypt through travel-ban measures, and prosecute directors individually for signing intercompany agreements that artificially depress Egyptian taxable profit.
Money Laundering and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 15 years and fines of up to EGP 5,000,000 for money-laundering offences; confiscation and forfeiture of the subscription inventory, bank balances, merchant accounts and related proceeds; blacklisting with the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU); revocation of the commercial registration and tax card.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and managers are prosecuted in their personal capacity, not merely the corporate entity; civil restitution of laundered amounts; travel bans, freezing orders and disqualification from holding directorships in Egyptian companies; evidence routed through foreign or offshore accounts is subject to confiscation through mutual legal assistance.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance and Illegal Profit Extraction

Statutory sanction: ETA recovery of the full unwithheld WHT from the Egyptian LLC as withholding agent, plus monthly delay surcharge and fines up to the amount of the undeclared tax; cancellation of deductions taken for disguised fees; re-characterisation of shareholder loan write-offs as deemed dividends taxable at 22.5% CIT plus 10% WHT; interest and penalties for every foreign payment lacking proper withholding documentation.

Director personal liability: The law treats directors as personally liable where the LLC, as statutory withholding agent, fails to deduct or remit WHT; deliberate non-compliance leads to joint and several civil restitution, travel bans, seizure of personal assets and criminal conviction for infringement of the withholding provisions, notwithstanding the limited-liability shield of the LLC.
