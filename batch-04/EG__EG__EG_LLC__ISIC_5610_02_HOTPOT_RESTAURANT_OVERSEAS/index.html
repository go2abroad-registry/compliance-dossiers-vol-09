Governing statute

Egyptian Companies Law No. 159 of 1981, as amended by Law No. 7 of 1998 and Law No. 2 of 2018; Executive Regulations issued by the Ministry of Investment; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 (for the foreign-owned LLC) and its Executive Regulations; relevant provisions of the Civil Code on corporate contracts.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association (Aqd al-Taasis) and Articles of Association (Nizam al-Taasis), signed by all founding members, certified by a licensed Arabic notary, and filed with the GAFI Companies Registry

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Law No. 159 of 1981 for a restaurant LLC; however foreign capital must be deposited in a blocked Egyptian-pound bank account in the company's name at a licensed Egyptian bank, and an initial paid-in capital sufficient for the first six months of operations is required to satisfy the GAFI registration officer. Each subscriber must contribute at least one share; 50% of the subscribed capital is generally payable at incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Prevention Certificate from the Civil Defense Authority of the governorate (covering fire extinguishers, emergency exits, gas isolation valves for hotpot burners, and evacuation plan) prior to opening; Structural Load Inspection Certificate from the governorate's Engineering Administration confirming the premises can support kitchen equipment and heavy dining fixtures; Occupational Safety and Health clearance from the Ministry of Manpower (QSAS standard) for worker protection, including kitchen electrical grounding, gas line testing, and a workplace health clearance for staff food handling per FSA requirements

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) under the Ministry of Finance; statutory web portal: eta.gov.eg, including the unified electronic tax services portal 'Ingez (إنجز)' to which the e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance APIs are connected.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned EG LLC must first incorporate through the General Authority for Investment and Foreign Trade (GAFI) and obtain the commercial register extract. It then applies, through the ETA Ingez portal, for a single unified Tax Number (TIN, formerly the tax card 'بطاقة ضريبية') covering income tax, VAT, stamp duty and payroll taxes, submitting the GAFI investment certificate or commercial registration, constitutional documents, board resolution appointing Egyptian authorized signatories, accounting office mandate and bank account details; a licensed Egyptian tax agent must be appointed under notarized power of attorney for non-resident shareholders. VAT registration is completed in the same unified file; the ETA issues the tax card and VAT certificate before taxable supply commences. The condiment production activity additionally requires food-safety registration with the National Food Safety Authority (NFSA), whose license code is disclosed to ETA for the VAT file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not recognize US-style check-the-box elections; entity classification follows the Companies Law and Law No. 91 of 2005, so the LLC is a resident juridical person taxed at entity level on worldwide income with a unilateral foreign tax credit for overseas hotpot branch profits. Available elections: (1) simplified presumptive-turnover tax for small taxpayers (annual turnover within the ministerial ceiling, generally EGP 2 million); (2) free-zone/investment-zone export regime (including SCZone free-zone treatment for export-oriented condiment production); (3) cash-basis election for qualifying micro-businesses; (4) thin-capitalization, hybrid-mismatch and GAAR-responsive safe-harbour elections, including group financing coordination. No pass-through election is available to shareholders.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration when the taxable turnover of goods exceeds EGP 500,000 or taxable services (including restaurant/dining and managing services) exceed EGP 250,000 in the preceding 12 months; a condiment exporter and overseas-restaurant supply chain should register voluntarily even below the threshold to secure refundable input VAT. One unified Tax Number covers the entire VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Law No. 67 of 2016 and its Executive Regulations, 0% VAT applies to (a) tangible goods (condiment products, sauces, packaged hotpot meal kits) actually exported and customs-cleared out of Egypt, and (b) services (dining-management, franchising and offshore catering services) provided to non-resident recipients and wholly consumed outside Egypt. The exporter must be VAT-registered, issue zero-rated e-invoices with the export code, physically move the goods out of Egypt within the statutory export period, and prove receipt of the export proceeds in convertible foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system. Directly related support services and international transportation of the exported condiments are equally zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document lodged through the Nafeza/ACID customs platform, with HS codes and weights)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or international road carriage (CMR) document evidencing actual departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank confirming credit of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • ETA 0% export VAT invoice, packing list, sales contract, GAFI export records and NFSA lot-release certificates for the food consignments

ETA has implemented mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt phases for VAT-registered taxpayers; the hotpot restaurant's ERP and POS systems must integrate with ETA REST/JSON APIs, submit invoice and B2C receipt objects for near-real-time clearance, obtain ETA digital signatures and QR stamps, and follow the offline contingency guidance. The POS dining channel must use ETA-approved e-receipt software. Non-integration or issuance of unstamped invoices is penalized by ETA (fixed monetary fines per document, suspension of tax cards and customs processing) and may block VAT refunds.

ETA audits export dossiers end-to-end, reconciling the customs exit confirmation, transport documents, proceeds entry and e-invoice codes, and may trace goods to the overseas outlet. If actual exportation or repatriation is not proven, the 0% rate is reversed, VAT is assessed at 14% on the full invoice value with monthly delay additions and default penalties, refunds are clawed back with interest and prior periods may be reopened for up to five years; deliberate misdescriptions attract customs penalties, Criminal Code and VAT evasion fines (including imprisonment upon conviction), and the Central Bank of Egypt may impose exchange-control penalties for unrepatriated proceeds and the LLC may lose export/SCZone facilities.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The EGP is convertible on the current account for trade and service payments through CBE-authorized banks, subject to documentary verification (contracts, invoices, tax card, and bank trade forms). The capital account is only partially open: foreign equity must enter through GAFI-registered channels exclusively via the banking system, with an inward transfer certificate; dividend and liquidation proceeds of an Egyptian LLC are freely repatriable after tax clearance under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, Art. 4, plus CBE evidence of source and profit computation. Egyptian residents making outward direct investment, including the overseas hotpot branch network, require CBE approval or registration within the bank's FX budget; any settlement outside the authorized banking system is criminally sanctioned.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the March 2024 unified managed float, there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; however, all export and service proceeds must be repatriated through an authorized bank within the CBE-declared deadlines (generally 90 days from shipment or customs release for goods and 60 days from invoice or completion for services), with the transaction reported under the NAFEZA/ACI framework for goods. For the overseas restaurant chain, management fees, royalties, and branch remittances must enter Egypt through a licensed bank and be recorded in foreign-currency accounts; late or offshore-only receipts may trigger CBE fines, denial of import facilities, and exchange-control enforcement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE / Al-Bank Al-Markazi Al-Masri), operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), together with the CBE consolidated foreign-exchange rules issued in 2024 and the CBE Rule Book for Banks, Part 3 (Foreign Exchange Operations).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Egyptian fintech/EMI licensees (Paymob, Telda, MNT-Halan) operate only as payment and acquiring services and cannot clear EGP cross-border or extend FX lines. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) are legally unviable as primary treasury rails because Egyptian exchange-control law requires all resident B2B flows to pass through CBE-authorized banks; offshore EGP settlement is unavailable and such routing creates unreported FX exposure. At most, foreign EMIs may serve the non-Egyptian operating entities of the overseas restaurant chain for foreign-currency collections, never the Egyptian LLC's EGP treasury.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore investor remits in USD, EUR, or GBP via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code '701 – direct investment' to the LLC's foreign-currency account at an authorized Egyptian bank. The bank issues an inward transfer certificate and reports the deal to the CBE foreign-exchange reporting system; if the remittance constitutes a capital increase, GAFI registration and updated Articles of Association must precede conversion into EGP. Supporting file: investment registration certificate, board resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and UBO declarations. Shareholder loans and convertible instruments must be separately documented, transfer-priced, and reported to CBE; cash equity injections without banking record are non-compliant.Clearing-rail hierarchy: (1) SWIFT MT103/gpi TT for operating payments and service fees below the bank's documentary threshold after submission of invoice, contract, tax card, and Form 4 where goods are involved; (2) irrevocable, confirmed commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT720) for high-value kitchen and capital imports, released against NAFEZA ACI registration and customs clearance; (3) CBE-approved FX transfers for payments exceeding USD 1,000,000 or extending beyond 12 months; (4) domestic EGP settlement via CBE RTGS and Meeza rails for local suppliers and merchant reimbursements. All outbound flows require end-use documentation, bank-side screening, and five-year record retention.Onshore outright FX forward (USD/EGP) with a licensed Egyptian bank against genuine trade or repatriation exposure; tenor up to 6 months, margined at 10-20%, and prohibited for speculative dealing.FX swap (USD/EGP and cross-currency swap) for cash-flow matching of import payables against local EGP dining revenue; requires board authorization and trade-ticket support.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on EGP are prohibited onshore for Egyptian residents; only non-resident counterparties may access the offshore EGP NDF market, and the Egyptian LLC cannot enter such contracts.Currency options and structured FX hedges are not standardized in Egypt and are executed only under bank-specific ISDA/CSA documentation with CBE approval; forward and swap cover is the intended maximum sophistication for a restaurant treasury.
