Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-float exchange-rate regime with the EGP anchored against a U.S. dollar basket. Current-account transactions are fully convertible through licensed banks upon production of supporting commercial documentation; capital-account transactions, including inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, and profit remittances, require CBE authorization or must be effected through a CBE-authorized licensed bank under the applicable purpose-code framework. Profit and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted but are subject to documentary hurdles: audited financial statements, Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax clearance, CBE Form No. 16 profit-repatriation declaration, and confirmation that the underlying profits were produced from onshore operations and have been fully disclosed in prior CBE residence filings. There are no statutory surtaxes or sequential conversion queues for genuine commercial remittances, but in periods of FX liquidity tension, banks apply an internal prioritization order favouring trade-related payments, which increases the tactical importance of maintaining a committed FX facility at a Tier-1 bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE Circular No. 847 of 2020, as amended by CBE Circular No. 8 of 2021 and subsequent November 2022 directives on FX liquidity, all Egyptian exporters, including IoT hardware retailers selling cross-border, must repatriate 100% of export proceeds to an onshore foreign-currency account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank within 90 days from the date of shipment (bill-of-lading date). The exporter is then required to convert 100% of the repatriated proceeds to Egyptian pounds through the banking system unless the CBE has granted an exceptional retention waiver. Compliance is evidenced through submission of the customs export manifest, the CBE Form No. 304 export-settlement declaration, and the underlying purchase order/FX invoice. Failure to comply triggers bank-level suspension of further outward remittance facilities and referral to the CBE's Exchange Control enforcement unit.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Foreign Exchange Operations Department and the Exchange Control Administration, acting pursuant to Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and CBE Foreign Exchange Regulation No. 847 of 2020, as amended

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

LOW FEASIBILITY as a primary treasury rail. Offshore electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Payoneer, Mercury, or Airwallex are neither licensed nor recognized by the CBE as eligible payment-service providers for Egyptian-resident corporate entities under Law No. 18 of 2019 (Payment Systems Law) and its executive regulations. An Egyptian LLC may only use such EMIs as auxiliary pay-in conduits for foreign e-commerce consumer card collections, provided that all funds are ultimately swept into an onshore CBE-licensed bank account within the 90-day export repatriation window and are accompanied by full merchant-level reconciliation. Establishing the EMI account in the name of the Egyptian LLC requires the EMI to comply with Egyptian residency rules, which virtually all offshore EMIs refuse to do; consequently, the company must not design its liquidity architecture around an offshore EMI balance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections, whether equity or shareholder loans, must be routed solely through a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer quoting CBE purpose code 300 (direct investment in the capital of an Egyptian company) or code 302 (shareholder loan). The funds must be sourced from the shareholder's own bank account, not from a third-party aggregator, and must be accompanied by the notarised board resolution approving the injection, the company's incorporation contract (تيرخ), the ETA tax card, and a CBE Form No. 29 declaration filed by the bank's compliance unit within one business day of receipt. Proceeds for a capital increase must first be placed in a dedicated Capital Escrow Account and may only be utilized after the CBE issues its no-objection confirmation and the company completes the registration of the capital increase with the Commercial Registry. The bank will conduct a source-of-funds verification against the UBO's bank statements and may require a period of 5–10 business days for full settling of the remittance before crediting it to the operational account.Outbound trade settlements for IoT hardware imports are cleared via two primary rails: (i) direct SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for invoices below the bank's low-value threshold (typically USD 50,000 per invoice), supported by a supplier proforma invoice, a CBE-issued import registration certificate (IRC), and preliminary customs declaration form; and (ii) irrevocable sight commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) for larger amounts, opened through the corporate bank against a proforma and a generated LC, with payment released against a clean on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin. Importers must appear on the CBE's registered importer list and all outward remittances must be covered by the company's CBE-approved annual FX budget as recorded in the bank's forward cover system. For fast-moving consumer IoT goods, the MT103 rail is preferred for speed, while the LC rail is reserved for high-value or recurring supplier relationships where the supplier requires payment assurance and the entity receives a 30–60 day usance.Onshore EGP/USD commercial forwards (FX forward contracts against verified trade documents, tenor up to 180 days, must be collateralized by a genuine import/export invoice)Offshore EGP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed by the parent entity outside Egypt to hedge EGP depreciation risk on repatriated dividends or shareholder distributionsOnshore currency swaps with a CBE-licensed bank for restructuring existing FX-denominated liabilities into EGP-denominated facilities, subject to CBE counterparty approval
