Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Official Portal: www.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign legal entities engaging in taxable activities in Egypt must register for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT ID through the ETA's unified registration process. The foreign entity appoints a tax agent/fiscal representative, submits notarized and legalized certificate of incorporation, passport copies of authorized signatories, evidence of a local bank account or principal place of business, and a power of attorney. Registration is lodged online via the ETA portal or at the affiliated tax office; a registration certificate is issued within 30 business days. For non-residents, registration is required before any invoice issuance in Egypt or once an Egyptian withholding obligation arises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – Egyptian limited liability companies are taxable persons subject to corporate income tax on worldwide income unless a treaty limitation applies; no pass-through taxation for LLCs except for certain exempt legal forms not applicable here.

Available election forms

Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. An LLC may not elect to be taxed as a pass-through entity. The Tax Procedure Law No. 206 of 2020 and Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005 do not provide hybrid classification elections for domestic entities; however, hybrid mismatch rules under the tax treaty network and Egyptian CFC rules may recharacterize certain payments. Foreign entities may apply for advance pricing agreements or tax rulings, but not for entity tax status changes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for resident businesses; for non-resident suppliers, registration is mandatory without turnover threshold when providing taxable electronic services to Egyptian consumers (e-services subject to reverse-charge or simplified registration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside Egypt are zero-rated. Services supplied to non-residents and consumed outside Egypt, including software/SaaS licences where beneficial use is outside the country, qualify for zero rating if the service provider is a VAT registered resident and retains evidence of overseas consumption. EMR SaaS provided to foreign clinics may qualify as a zero-rated export service when payment is received in convertible currency and the recipient is a non-resident business.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (or electronic delivery proof for digital services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice confirming FX inflow)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Signed contract / license agreement for services
  • Certificate of Origin (where applicable)

Egypt has mandated electronic invoicing and e-receipts through ETA-integrated protocols. All B2B taxpayers above the relevant thresholds must issue invoices via the ETA e-invoicing system (XML/JSON via API, with fiscal coding and QR stamping). Real-time clearance is required for e-receipts in B2C transactions. Non-compliance results in fines, blocked tax credits, and suspension of VAT refunds.

The ETA may audit all supporting export documents. Fraudulent claims for zero-rating trigger: (i) revocation of the export exemption, (ii) retroactive VAT collection at 14% plus late payment charges, (iii) an administrative fine up to EGP 1 million or twice the tax evaded for reckless negligence, and (iv) criminal liability under Tax Procedure Law with imprisonment or criminal fine (composite penalties). The limitation period for VAT assessment in fraud cases is 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed-float legal-tender regime for the Egyptian pound (EGP) with current-account convertibility in principle under Law No. 194/2020; capital-account transactions, including equity capital contributions, profit repatriation, and cross-border loan repayment, require underlying documentation, CBE approval, and use of an authorized dealing bank. For an EMR/HealthTech SaaS exporter, subscription and implementation fees constitute current-account service revenues and are generally remittable after tax and customs verification; however, profit/dividend remittance triggers a 5% withholding tax (or treaty-reduced rate), Corporate Tax Clearance Certificate, and CBE form documentary validation. No blanket convertibility guarantee exists for non-residents; formal clearance from the General Department of Foreign Exchange at CBE is recommended for principal repayment and dividend transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for services and goods must be repatriated through CBE-licensed banks within 30 days from the date of invoice/transaction date as per CBE regulations; 100% of receipts are required to be recorded with the authorized bank and have to be converted into EGP at the official market rate to cover local expenses unless a retention account or intra-group netting permission is granted by CBE. The exporter must submit the export invoice, customs/service declaration, bank credit advice, and proof of performance to support settlement; failure to comply may block customs clearance and trigger fines under Egyptian export-import regulations.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) and the Egyptian Foreign Exchange Committee, operating under Law No. 194 of 2020 concerning the Central Bank and the Banking System; the CBE exercises monopoly authority over exchange-rate policy, foreign-exchange licensing, and capital-account administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore digital banks/EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) are not regulated by CBE and cannot open EGP accounts, issue Egyptian IBANs, or settle local payroll/tax obligations. Their viability is limited to receiving USD/EUR subscription revenue from overseas medical clients and disbursing funds to Egyptian bank accounts via wire transfer; all onshore EGP liquidity and CBE compliance must flow through a licensed Egyptian commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Cross-border equity capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the parent company account in the originating jurisdiction to the beneficiary account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, using CBE purpose code for equity participation and the bank's inward remittance form. The bank will require the underlying incorporation certificate, Commercial Registry number, tax card, and board resolution to approve the credit as capital. The amount will be automatically registered to the LLC's capital account and reported to CBE for the balance-of-payments statistical survey. The remitter should include the LLC's Common Identification Number / Tax National Number in the wire message to prevent blocking.For service purchases and software subcontractor payments, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 with full mandatory remittance info and CBE Form 7 for outbound service/import settlement; for physical equipment, a commercial documentary credit (Documentary Letter of Credit in USD/EUR, subject to UCP600) is required when the supplier requires shipment-backed liquidity. Payment schedules must be underpinned by authentic invoices, contract, and customs/import declarations; the bank may request a Corporate Tax Clearance Certificate before remitting dividends or foreign loans, while cross-border disbursements of intra-group royalties require a tax residency certificate and double-taxation treaty declaration.Onshore plain-vanilla FX forwards with a CBE-licensed bank, covered by a real current-account exposure (invoice/LC) and settled by gross delivery of USD against EGP.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps only where the base obligation is a CBE-approved onshore loan and the swap is executed through an authorized dealing bank with exchange-rate-risk management filing.Offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts with non-resident financial institutions are permitted only to hedge non-resident minority-owner cash-flow exposure and cannot be used to speculate or circumvent CBE conversion rules.FX swap / spot concurrent with export proceeds conversion is allowed but must be booked on the same value date and reported to CBE as a current-account conversion.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on cash or in-kind profit distributions made by an Egyptian LLC to any shareholder, including non-residents, per Article 57 of Law No. 91/2005 as amended. The LLC is obliged to withhold, report, and remit the tax within the statutory monthly declaration windows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions by the EG LLC exceeds EGP 8,000,000. Master File: required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EGP 2,000,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: required where group consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 15,000,000,000. A TP disclosure form and confirmation of related-party transactions must accompany Form 19 regardless of value thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no formal UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but applies a statutory General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) and a substance-over-form doctrine under Law No. 206/2020 and ETA practice. The ETA disregards arrangements lacking commercial rationale, employees, premises, or actual risk-bearing functions, and may recharacterize the transaction, deny treaty benefits, and impose the 22.5% CIT debit on deemed Egyptian-source income. A paper 'IP-holding' LLC or a foreign affiliate hosting the EMR platform without local staff and board minutes will be treated as a sham for transfer-pricing and withholding purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (CIT and Withholding Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 132–137 of Law No. 91/2005, imprisonment of not less than two years and a fine equal to the tax evaded up to double that amount, or either penalty; mandatory re-assessment with 2% monthly late-payment interest, administrative seizure of bank accounts, and annulment of tax payment certificates needed for Egyptian licensing.

Director personal liability: Signing directors, the general manager, and the licensed tax accountant are personally prosecutable; conviction triggers imprisonment, travel bans, asset freezes, and joint and several civil restitution of the evaded tax. Argentine/Egyptian courts' veil-piercing doctrine exposes shareholders if the LLC is a sham vehicle.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ETA/GAFI strike-off or commercial registry de-registration under the Investment and Companies Laws; retroactive denial of treaty benefits; recharacterization of service fees as taxable Egyptian-source income; penalty of up to 100% of the tax difference and revocation of the free-zone license.

Director personal liability: Managers become personally liable for the LLC's unpaid CIT, VAT, and WHT arrears under Article 80 of Law No. 91/2005; travel bans and disqualification from board memberships are imposed in GAFI enforcement proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fixed fine of up to EGP 200,000 for documentation violations; the ETA may apply best-judgment income estimation, impose a tax-difference penalty of up to 50% of the attributed tax, and add the 2% monthly surcharge from the assessment date.

Director personal liability: The CFO/finance director who certifies an inaccurate TP disclosure risks personal fines and imprisonment for false statements made to the ETA; travel bans regularly issue where TP adjustments exceed EGP 1,000,000 while the company is in collection stage.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80/2002 as amended by Law No. 112/2021, money laundering violations carry imprisonment of 7–15 years and fines of EGP 1,000,000–5,000,000; entity-level fines, freezing orders, and confiscation of funds in payment accounts are routinely ordered by the Economic Courts.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally indictable for culpable ignorance of AML controls; the management team may face travel bans, asset freezes, and personal criminal fines even where the company is ultimately acquitted of predicate offenses.
