Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Companies Registration Department / One-Stop Shop, in coordination with the Commercial Registry Office of the Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade.

Governing statute

Law No. 159 of 1981 (Companies Law) as amended, particularly Articles 118-133 governing Limited Liability Companies; Executive Regulations issued by Ministerial Decree No. 506 of 1982; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations (Prime Ministerial Decree No. 2310 of 2017); Commercial Registration Law No. 34 of 1976.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association (Company Contract) submitted through the GAFI online portal or One-Stop Shop; final Certificate of Incorporation and Commercial Registration Certificate. State filing fees are assessed in Egyptian Pounds by GAFI; no separate official USD tariff is published.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an Egyptian LLC under the Companies Law; the subscribers fix the capital in the Memorandum. The full subscribed capital must be evidenced by a bank deposit certificate or a notarized in-kind valuation report before the incorporation certificate is issued. No mandatory third-party escrow account is required under current GAFI procedure, but the funds must be held in the company's temporary pre-incorporation account pending issuance of the certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense fire-safety certificate for the office and warehouse, structural load inspection of racking and floors, emergency-exit and extinguisher compliance, municipal occupancy clearance, and Ministry of Manpower occupational safety and health clearance for any warehouse employees.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) - official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance portal: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of the LLC with the Commercial Registry (Companies Law 159/1981 or Investment Law 72/2017), the foreign shareholder(s) must (1) execute a notarized contract of association and obtain a commercial registration extract; (2) appoint a local tax representative if no physical office exists; (3) file the unified tax registration application (Form No. 1) with the competent local tax office (Mamouriya) or through the ETA electronic portal, submitting the commercial registration certificate, notarized board resolution and power of attorney for non-resident directors, passport copies, registered office lease, and bank reference letters; (4) obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card; (5) register for VAT under Law 67/2016 once annual taxable supplies exceed the statutory thresholds; and (6) activate e-invoicing credentials (ETA digital certificate, API/ERP integration, and authorized invoicing users) on invoicing.eta.gov.eg before first B2B invoice issuance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — After incorporation of the LLC with the Commercial Registry (Companies Law 159/1981 or Investment Law 72/2017), the foreign shareholder(s) must (1) execute a notarized contract of association and obtain a commercial registration extract; (2) appoint a local tax representative if no physical office exists; (3) file the unified tax registration application (Form No. 1) with the competent local tax office (Mamouriya) or through the ETA electronic portal, submitting the commercial registration certificate, notarized board resolution and power of attorney for non-resident directors, passport copies, registered office lease, and bank reference letters; (4) obtain the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card; (5) register for VAT under Law 67/2016 once annual taxable supplies exceed the statutory thresholds; and (6) activate e-invoicing credentials (ETA digital certificate, API/ERP integration, and authorized invoicing users) on invoicing.eta.gov.eg before first B2B invoice issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists in Egypt; an LLC is mandatorily taxed at entity level under Law 91/2005 at 22.5% corporate income tax on worldwide income while resident. Available statutory mechanisms: (a) simplified presumptive turnover-based tax at 0.5% - 1.5% - 2% under MSME Law 152/2020 if turnover and headcount thresholds are met; (b) VAT export refund election on quarterly or annual cycles; (c) foreign tax credit or tax-sparing election for foreign branch and dividend income; (d) group consolidation and hybrid mismatch elections are not available under Egyptian law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods; EGP 150,000 annual taxable turnover for supplies of services. Wholesale trading (ISIC 4669) is registrable once the EGP 500,000 goods threshold is exceeded. Imports of petrochemical polymers into Egypt additionally trigger customs VAT at 14% and applicable customs duties at importation, irrespective of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of polymers and other tangible goods qualifies for 0% VAT when the goods are shipped outside Egypt and the exporter holds a validated customs export declaration. Services rendered to non-residents may be zero-rated if performed and consumed entirely outside Egypt. The exporter must be VAT-registered, issue an e-invoice clearly marked as export at 0%, and evidence actual physical departure of goods from Egypt within the applicable statutory time frame.

  • Customs Export Declaration validated by the Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / road consignment note naming the Egyptian exporter as consignor
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank certificate confirming receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency)
  • Fully executed export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • VAT e-invoice marked export and export packing list
  • Copy of ETA export registration or exporter tax card as applicable

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing: VAT-registered taxpayers must issue XML-based electronic invoices using ETA-approved software or ERP and transmit them to the ETA national e-invoicing platform (invoicing.eta.gov.eg) in real time (within 24 hours of issuance) for validation, assignment of a fiscal UUID, and QR-code stamping before delivery to the counterparty; e-receipts are being phased in for B2C transactions. Invoices not cleared through the ETA platform are non-compliant and input VAT credit is denied. ETA provides API integration and a taxpayer portal; violations attract penalties under Law 67/2016.

Zero-rated export refunds are reviewed by the ETA refund department and are subject to documentary desk audits, customs-data matching, FX remittance verification, and on-site inspection. False or forged export documentation (fake bills of lading, circular exports, unrepatriated FX) triggers: (i) full clawback of the refunded VAT plus an additional penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded; (ii) daily late-payment penalties of 2% per month on any attributable unpaid VAT; (iii) criminal prosecution of the legal representative under Articles 76-79 of Law 67/2016, including imprisonment and fines; (iv) revocation of e-invoicing privileges and suspension from the exporter registry; and (v) potential application of the general anti-abuse rule with redetermination of tax on a look-through basis.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a liberalized current account payment regime for trade-related transactions, subject to documentary verification by authorized banks and compliance with CBE circulars. Capital account movements, including foreign direct investment equity injections and intercompany loans, are permissible after the investor obtains a GAFI (General Authority for Investment) certificate and registers with the CBE; statutory approval is generally automatic for standard trade and FDI flows but may be subject to periodic liquidity pressure in USD. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted for foreign shareholders after statutory audit of financial statements, payment of Egyptian corporate income tax, and submission of the bank's Profit Repatriation Form with the required supporting file; EGP is not freely convertible offshore, and all FX conversions must flow through CBE-authorized dealing banks. No material non-tariff hurdles exist for pilot imports of polyethylene/polypropylene granules, but the bank must verify the commercial invoice, customs declaration, and underlying purchase contract to comply with anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorist financing screening expectations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CBE requires export proceeds from petrochemical and polymer transactions to be repatriated no later than 120 days from the bill of lading date. The full balance of the export invoice must be received into the exporter's foreign currency account maintained with an authorized bank in Egypt. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to Egyptian Pounds; however, the exporter must offer the export proceeds to the authorized bank at the prevailing market exchange rate if it wishes to sell such proceeds. The exporter may lawfully retain the export proceeds in a foreign currency account, provided that the source is supported by customs export entries, commercial invoices, and bank receipt statements. A failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline triggers CBE sanctions, ban of the exporter from receiving trade finance facilities, and potential inclusion on the CBE blocked list, unless a force majeure waiver is approved.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) – Foreign Exchange Regulatory Unit and Banking Supervision Sector

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for fully offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs). The Central Bank of Egypt requires any entity providing payment services or e-wallet/fiat custody in EGP or foreign currency to obtain a CBE license as a 'Payment Service Provider' or operate through a licensed bank. Offshore EMIs such as UK-registered and European-licensed Virtual Account Providers cannot be used as the primary operating bank because they do not satisfy CBE's onshore bank regulatory obligation for corporate treasury, cannot issue Egyptian LCs, and cannot serve as the authorized dealer for a capital contribution or profit repatriation. Offshore EMIs may be used only as an auxiliary collection channel for international card payments from low-risk customers, provided that funds are subsequently swept into Egyptian bank-controlled settlement accounts within 24 hours. The corporate treasury legal opinion should categorically forbid the use of any unlicensed EMI as the principal settlement rail for petrochemical trade.

For a petrochemical trading LLC, the bank will additionally require recent audited financial statements of the corporate parent and, in some cases, a reference letter from the parent company's relationship bank.

Defense evidence: Submit a certified board resolution of the parent entity authorizing the injection, the share subscription agreement, a bank statement showing the source of the funds in the parent account, the SWIFT MT103 used for the transfer, tax clearance certificates, and a notarized declaration confirming that the funds originate from lawful commercial activity. If the funds originate from an offshore intermediate holding company in a non-FATF-compliant jurisdiction, the bank will require an independent legal opinion validating the capital ownership chain.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Egyptian LLC must be routed through the CBE-authorised dealing bank of the tier-one commercial bank relationship. The transfer must be made in US dollars or Euros using the SWIFT MT103 format with a proper purpose code: code '601' is applied to direct equity contributions by non-resident shareholders for the purchase of shares in an Egyptian company; code '201' is applied to pre-export and prepayment for imports; and code '901' for intercompany loans. The credit entry into the Egyptian bank must be matched within 30 days with the supporting documentation: GAFI certificate of incorporation, the LLC's commercial register extract, a board resolution of the foreign parent company, the shareholder's bank account statement, and the signed articles of association. The receiving bank must file a CBE monthly foreign currency position report and, if the total injection exceeds USD 500,000 equivalent, a special CBE capital-account notification must be lodged. The bank will verify the economic substance of the shareholder and confirm that the injection is not a disguised loan or monetary transfer subject to Central Bank licensing. The funds will be credited to the LLC's foreign currency account and may be converted to EGP only through the interbank system at the prevailing Exchange Rate for Commercial Transactions (ERCT).The primary foreign trade disbursement rails for the petrochemical polymer distributor are irrevocable documentary letters of credit (L/C) and SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers. For the letter-of-credit rail, the Egyptian importer uses a CBE-authorised bank to issue an irrevocable L/C (MT700) in favor of the global producer, with the polymer granules classified as raw material and eligible for trade payment. The L/C will require the seller to present a full set of clean-on-board bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, shipper's certificate, and a chemical or polymer quality certificate. For sight payment, the L/C is typically drawn at sight via SWIFT MT700/MT710. For recurrent supply under open account, the Egyptian importer may use MT103 for settlement of goods already verified through customs, provided that the corresponding underlying invoice, customs entry 'Bayan', import license, and exchange control form are submitted to the dealing bank. All outbound transactions must be made against real trade documents to satisfy CBE's anti-money-laundering circulars; any advance payment above 50% of the invoice value requires a specific exception from the CBE if the payment is made before shipment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 8,000,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions in a fiscal year; above this threshold, a contemporaneous transfer pricing Local File and the ETA transfer pricing disclosure declaration with the annual return are mandatory. A Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting obligations apply to multinational groups meeting higher consolidated revenue thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not currently operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the manner of UAE. However, the ETA applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and anti-avoidance doctrines under Income Tax Law 91/2005 and relevant DTAs. A petrochemical trading LLC lacking real staff, physical functions, inventory risk, or decision-making substance risks recharacterisation as a conduit, denial of treaty benefits, and attribution of profits to the ultimate foreign principal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to the proper taxable income, collection of the 22.5% tax undercharged, monthly 2% late-payment surcharge, and a criminal penalty equal to a substantial percentage of the evaded tax. Imprisonment and confiscation may also be ordered by the Economic Courts.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved false returns may be personally liable for the unpaid tax, subjected to asset freezes and travel bans, disqualified from corporate management, and sentenced to imprisonment of up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length result, additional 22.5% corporate tax, monthly delay surcharges of 2%, administrative fines for failure to produce required documentation, and possible denial of corresponding adjustments for double tax relief.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for the adjusted tax where they knowingly signed inaccurate transfer pricing declarations. In serious cases, travel bans and criminal proceedings may follow under tax evasion provisions.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Piercing of the corporate veil; reallocation of income to the real beneficial owner; denial of treaty relief; cancellation of free-zone/investment incentives; and potential deregistration or liquidation of the Egyptian entity by the ETA and GAFI.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution for diverted profits, disqualification from serving as directors in Egypt, travel restrictions, and possible criminal liability for fraudulent use of legal personality.
AML and Trade Finance Violations

Statutory sanction: Enforcement under Egypt's Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80/2002 and Central Bank of Egypt AML directives: monetary fines, confiscation of funds, suspension of banking and trade facilities, revocation of licenses, and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and signatories can be personally fined, imprisoned for up to 7 years, placed on travel bans, and subjected to asset freezes for failing to file suspicious transaction reports or knowingly participating in laundering trade proceeds.
