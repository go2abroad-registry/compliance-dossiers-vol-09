Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for Egyptian LLCs under Law 194/2020; the partners must fix a capital amount adequate for the corporate object and fully subscribe it at formation. In-kind contributions (e.g., software source code and intangibles) must be fully paid at incorporation and certified by a licensed valuer, whereas cash contributions may be paid in installments within five years with any unpaid balance recorded as a shareholder receivable. A bank deposit certificate (escrow-style capital account) must evidence the cash portion paid at formation for GAFI validation.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Administration fire-safety certificate and egress approval are required for the leased office; structural load inspection applies only where the Company constructs building alterations, which is not anticipated for a stand-alone SaaS operation. Occupational workplace clearance from the Ministry of Manpower labor inspection directorate is required to verify electrical safety, first-aid provisions, working-space adequacy, and employee health for computer-based labor.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — unified tax and e-services portal at https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipts portal at https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; foreign investment incorporation and company formation handled by the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) incorporate through GAFI and obtain the Commercial Registry extract and investment file; (2) file ETA Form 1 (for legal persons) applying for the unified Tax Card/TIN, attaching the commercial registry extract, articles of association, board resolution, notarized power of attorney to the local manager, passport copies of directors, office lease, and bank account certificate — issuance generally takes 10-30 business days; (3) register for VAT under Law 67/2016 once the statutory threshold is exceeded or immediately upon commencing zero-rated export services, and obtain the VAT registration certificate; (4) enroll in the ETA e-invoicing platform and procure ETA-approved digital signing certificates for authorized signatories; (5) non-resident electronic-service suppliers without an Egyptian permanent establishment must obtain a simplified foreign-service-provider VAT registration with a dedicated TIN directly through the ETA electronic portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) incorporate through GAFI and obtain the Commercial Registry extract and investment file; (2) file ETA Form 1 (for legal persons) applying for the unified Tax Card/TIN, attaching the commercial registry extract, articles of association, board resolution, notarized power of attorney to the local manager, passport copies of directors, office lease, and bank account certificate — issuance generally takes 10-30 business days; (3) register for VAT under Law 67/2016 once the statutory threshold is exceeded or immediately upon commencing zero-rated export services, and obtain the VAT registration certificate; (4) enroll in the ETA e-invoicing platform and procure ETA-approved digital signing certificates for authorized signatories; (5) non-resident electronic-service suppliers without an Egyptian permanent establishment must obtain a simplified foreign-service-provider VAT registration with a dedicated TIN directly through the ETA electronic portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid-mismatch election is available under Egyptian law; an LLC is an autonomous taxable person for corporate income tax (standard rate 22.5% under Law 30/2023) and for VAT. The only materially relevant election is the optional unified/simplified tax regime for small businesses with annual turnover not exceeding EGP 20 million, electable on the annual return, replacing accounting-based profit tax with a gross-receipts percentage (statutory band of 0.4%-1.5% by activity type). No fiscal unity, consolidation, or elective foreign-tax-credit pooling exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration when annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 (Law 67/2016, Art. 15); voluntary registration available for annual turnover above EGP 50,000; non-resident suppliers of electronic services must register under the foreign-service-provider scheme with no Egyptian turnover threshold once they supply Egyptian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available under Art. 32 of Law 67/2016 for services supplied to a non-resident recipient for use and enjoyment wholly outside Egypt. For cybersecurity SaaS the exporter must substantiate: (i) the customer is a non-resident without an Egyptian establishment; (ii) the cloud platform is accessed and consumed outside Egypt (supported by IP geolocation and usage logs); (iii) invoices are issued in convertible foreign currency and the proceeds are actually credited to an Egyptian bank account; and (iv) the zero-rated supply is transmitted through the ETA e-invoicing system with the statutory export/zero-rating code. Input VAT apportioned to zero-rated exports is creditable, and excess credits are refundable through the ETA electronic refund procedure after documentary review.

  • Customs Export Declaration (physical goods only; not applicable to pure SaaS — for services: signed SaaS subscription agreement, statement of work, and ETA-accepted service-export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (physical goods only; for SaaS: certificate of incorporation or tax-residence certificate of the non-resident customer, plus remote delivery and usage logs proving consumption outside Egypt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by the Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of the foreign-currency consideration on the LLC's account

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance applies to all VAT registrants: e-invoices must be transmitted to the ETA e-invoicing platform via REST API/ERP integration using ETA-defined JSON schemas, or via the ETA web portal for smaller taxpayers; every invoice is validated in near real time and returned with a UUID and QR code (digital stamp) that together constitute the legal invoice; input VAT deduction, refund claims, and export zero-rating are conditional on cleared, error-free e-invoices; B2C sales additionally require QR-coded e-receipts through the separate e-receipt platform; non-compliance blocks deduction and exposes the taxpayer to statutory fines.

The ETA subjects export zero-rating claims to heightened documentary audit, verifying the foreign beneficiary's existence, the economic substance of the SaaS delivery, and the matching of foreign-currency inflows to specific e-invoices. Deficient documentation triggers reclassification to the domestic 14% VAT rate, denial of input-credit refunds, assessment of VAT, delay fines at 2% per month (capped at 100% of the tax), and retrospective clawback of refunds within the statutory limitation period. Fraudulent or forged export and refund claims additionally attract an administrative penalty of not less than 30% and up to 100% of the tax evaded under Art. 75 of Law 67/2016, plus imprisonment of up to 5 years for tax evasion, with joint and several liability imposed on the LLC's managers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the exchange-rate unification of March 2024 under the IMF-supported reform programme, the EGP operates as a managed float and the parallel-market premium has been eliminated. Current-account convertibility is liberally permitted: SaaS and cybersecurity service export receipts may be credited in USD/EUR, and payments for imported cloud infrastructure and overseas software tools may be disbursed through licensed banks on documentary support. Capital-account convertibility for FDI is administratively open: equity injection, profit remittance, and liquidation proceeds are transferable after GAFI registration, CBE inflow reconciliation, audited financial statements, and a valid tax-clearance certificate are produced. The residual hurdles are documentary and institutional: banks apply strict source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership controls, and large remittances may be operationally deferred while CBE exchange-control codes are reconciled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All service and software export proceeds must be repatriated through an Egyptian licensed bank within the contractual credit period benchmarked by banks at 90–180 days from invoice date; extensions require bank-level exchange-control approval. The post-March-2024 unified-rate regime has ended the blanket five-business-day conversion quotas previously applied during the 2022–2023 liquidity squeeze (50%, later raised); the LLC may now retain USD export proceeds in its USD account and convert into EGP at the interbank rate as operational needs require. Mandatory full surrender remains limited to state-designated strategic commodity exporters (petroleum, gold, fertilizers) and is inapplicable to an ISIC 6201 software exporter. Every inflow must nevertheless be reported under the CBE BPM6 purpose-code reconciliation framework and matched to the LLC's export registration file.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — the sole regulator for banking, foreign-exchange administration, and AML/CFT supervision, acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 (as amended by Law No. 88 of 2022) and the CBE Unified Banking Guidance. Exchange-control functions are executed through CBE-licensed Egyptian banks, with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) responsible for inward capital registration and the Egyptian Tax Authority for tax-clearance verification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory local file and master file documentation is required where (i) the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds EGP 15 million in a tax year, or (ii) total annual turnover of the Egyptian entity exceeds EGP 20 million; country-by-country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue equal to or above EUR 750 million. The documentation must be submitted within 30 days of a formal ETA request, with a contemporaneous TP master file maintained annually.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the Gulf jurisdictions. However, the Egyptian Tax Authority enforces a substance-over-form doctrine under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 91 of Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, and may disregard, recharacterise or reallocate transactions that lack commercial rationale or economic substance. In particular, entities claiming treaty benefits, participation exemption or controlled foreign company reliefs must demonstrate real operational substance (personnel, management, physical or tangible infrastructure, risk control and principal purpose of profit-making).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Suppressed or Falsified Revenue

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for a term of up to 5 years and/or a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000; unpaid tax remains due with monthly interest surcharge of 1.5%; in egregious cases the tax authority may impose an additional penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors, signatories and actual managers who prepared or signed false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest and penal surcharges. Criminal conviction triggers a travel ban, disqualification from board service and asset freezes against personal bank accounts.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Statutory and Cross-Border WHT

Statutory sanction: The Egyptian LLC is held as statutory withholding agent and assessed for the unremitted tax; monetary penalty of up to 25% of the withheld amount and monthly late interest of 1.5% apply; deliberate non-withholding may escalate to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: The managing director, finance director and tax compliance officer may be made personally responsible for the gross WHT deficiency and penalties; ETA imposes personal travel restrictions and may block bank accounts of the responsible directors while restitution is ongoing.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch – Abusive Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: ETA may compute a transfer pricing adjustment to the taxable base; an additional tax of up to 40% of the additionally assessed tax is imposed, plus monthly interest of 1.5% on the deficiency; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation is penalised separately by fines up to between EGP 20,000 and EGP 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or executed the non-arm's-length cross-border agreements are jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalty amounts; the ETA may issue domestic enforcement warnings, travel bans and, in deliberate or fraudulent cases, refer the directors to criminal court for aiding tax evasion.
Shell Company / GAAR / DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule resulting in recharacterisation of the transaction, denial of treaty benefits, reinstatement of the whole taxable profit in the hands of the Egyptian entity, and penalty assessment up to 100% of the additional taxes; commercial licence revocation may follow under the Investment Law. Egypt also reserves anti-conduit and beneficial ownership clauses under its DTAs.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who structured or maintained the artificial arrangement may be held jointly and severally liable for the entire avoided tax plus penalties; they face travel bans, restriction on initiating insolvency proceedings, disqualification as directors, and imprisonment where their manipulation involved forged residency certificates or fabricated service contracts.
