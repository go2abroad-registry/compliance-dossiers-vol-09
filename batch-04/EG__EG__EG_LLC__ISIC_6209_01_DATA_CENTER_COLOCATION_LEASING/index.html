Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the Egyptian LLC; the Articles fix the capital, which must be fully subscribed and deposited into a temporary bank escrow account in the company name. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — الهيئة المصرية للضرائب; unified e-services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt platform: https://invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

Foreign owners may hold 100% of EG LLC equity (GAFI single-window incorporation; no minimum paid-in capital). SOP: (1) Incorporate the LLC via the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) or the Commercial Registry and obtain the Commercial Registration extract; (2) file the unified tax-registration application (Form No. 1 under Unified Tax Procedures Law 154/2023) with the competent ETA tax office (Mamouria), attaching the notarized incorporation contract, commercial registration, board/manager resolution, registered office lease, manager passports, and paid-capital evidence; (3) obtain the single Unified Tax Number (UTN) on the digital tax card, covering corporate income tax, VAT, stamp tax, salary/payroll and withholding obligations; (4) file the VAT registration application within 30 days of exceeding EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover, or earlier upon the first issue of zero-rated export invoices if input VAT recovery is sought — the ETA then issues the VAT registration certificate; (5) complete e-invoicing onboarding on the ETA platform: procure an ETA-approved digital-signature certificate, register the ERP system, pass API conformance testing in the ETA sandbox, and obtain the issuance code for the unique invoice validation/QR; (6) open a domestic corporate bank account and, if applicable, a Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)-licensed foreign-currency account to receive export proceeds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a separate juridical person subject to corporate income tax under Law 91/2005 (20% on taxable profits up to EGP 10 million; 22.5% on the excess); no pass-through or disregarded-entity treatment exists; distributions are subject to 10% dividend withholding tax; a non-resident shareholder is not taxed in Egypt on undistributed profits; the LLC income is taxed on a worldwide net-profit basis unless a treaty or statutory exemption applies.

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box entity-classification election; tax identity is fixed by the corporate-law form. Available statutory elections: (1) voluntary VAT registration from EGP 150,000 annual turnover (before the EGP 500,000 mandatory threshold); (2) presumption regime for small taxpayers under Law 26/2020 — eligible for turnover up to EGP 20 million, replacing net-income CIT with a reduced gross-revenue percentage (bracket-capped) and forfeiting input VAT recovery; the election is binding for three years; (3) Investment Law 72/2017 incentives — investment-cost deductions of 30%–80% for eligible activities, and/or establishment inside the Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) free-zone regime where a corporate tax holiday of up to 5 years and duty/VAT exemptions may be granted by contract; (4) export support/refund election under Ministerial Decree 771/2018 for verified exporters of services, allowing faster refund clearance; (5) advance pricing agreements and advance tax rulings obtainable from the ETA for transfer-pricing and VAT characterization certainty; (6) fiscal unity/group consolidation and hybrid-mismatch elections are not available — DTT and permanent-establishment characterization apply non-electively.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration: EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies (including zero-rated export supplies for threshold-counting); voluntary registration from EGP 150,000; non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services to Egyptian consumers must register irrespective of turnover; all registered taxpayers are subject to the e-invoicing mandate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 15 of VAT Law 67/2016 and the Executive Regulations, exported services are zero-rated where: (i) the recipient is a non-resident not having a permanent establishment in Egypt; (ii) the service is wholly or principally used and economically exploited outside Egypt; (iii) consideration is payable in free foreign currency and is received in Egypt; (iv) the supplier keeps a complete export file (contract, zero-rated commercial invoice, e-invoice with QR, and bank remittance evidence). For data-center colocation and bare-metal hosting, the ETA will test the place of effective use: the physical location of racks and servers in Egypt creates a rebuttable field-audit risk that the supply is locally consumed; the LLC must document, through the contract, data-flow maps, and operational evidence, that the non-resident customer's processing, storage, and management activity is carried out for offshore needs and that no Egyptian domestic traffic or Egyptian-resident data is served.

  • Customs Export Declaration (only for re-export of tangible server/IT equipment; for pure colocation services a NIL goods declaration with the export-service schedule in the VAT return is used)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (where physical equipment moves across Egyptian customs; N/A for pure colocation service transactions)
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate from the receiving Egyptian bank evidencing full foreign-currency settlement of the export invoice
  • Commercial invoice in foreign currency endorsed 'Zero-Rated Export Services — Article 15, Law 67/2016'
  • Executed colocation / rack-space / bare-metal hosting agreement with the non-resident client, expressly allocating offshore beneficial use and excluding Egyptian-market service
  • ETA-validated e-invoice (unique QR code) and the corresponding export schedule in the periodic VAT return
  • Non-resident client tax-residency certificate or signed no-Egypt-permanent-establishment declaration

Mandatory real-time central clearance: every B2B e-invoice and B2C e-receipt must be transmitted to the ETA e-invoicing service via REST API in the prescribed JSON/XML schema, digitally signed with the taxpayer's ETA-approved certificate, validated against ETA fiscal registers, and returned with a unique allocation code and QR before legal issuance to the customer. ERP-to-ETA API integration and conformance testing are compulsory; the mandate covers all VAT-registered taxpayers since 2023. Non-compliance fines range from EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000 per violation under Law 17/2022, in addition to suspension of the e-invoicing code and the VAT certificate.

Assessment window: five years from the statutory filing date under Unified Tax Procedures Law 154/2023 (six or more years where no return was filed). On rejection of export zero-rating, the ETA will: (1) assess VAT at 14% on gross export proceeds; (2) add the monthly delay fine of 2% per month (month-fraction prorated) under VAT Law Article 51; (3) impose the fixed return-inaccuracy fine of EGP 300–5,000 per return; (4) apply e-invoice/e-receipt violation fines of EGP 20,000–100,000 per invoice; and (5) in fraud cases, pursue criminal evasion under Article 66 (imprisonment and a fine of EGP 5,000–50,000), claw back previously refunded VAT with statutory interest, revoke the e-invoicing code, suspend the VAT certificate and all export refund rights, and refer the file to the Tax Prosecution and the Economic Courts. Input VAT credits related to the disallowed zero-rated supplies are likewise reversed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-float but broadly liberalised current-account convertibility: trade, services, and profit/dividend remittances are convertible through CBE-authorised banks subject to documentary review, tax-clearance validation, and CBE remittance declarations. Capital-account operations (share subscriptions, capital repatriation, liquidation proceeds) remain routed through authorised dealers with CBE-designated purpose codes and compliance declarations; possible interbank FX-liquidity queues can temporarily delay execution. For the EG LLC data-centre colocation business, outbound profit remittance is permitted on presentation of audited financial statements, corporate tax filings, a valid Tax Clearance Certificate, and the applicable withholding-tax receipt on dividends under domestic law and the relevant double-tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion/surrender ratio is currently enforced: since the 2016 floatation and the 2022–2023 CBE reform, exporters retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in Egyptian-bank FX retention accounts. Statutory repatriation obligation: proceeds from exports of goods must be credited through an Egyptian authorised bank within 90 days from the shipment date; services proceeds (including monthly infrastructure and colocation billing to offshore tenants) must be collected through the Egyptian banking system within the contractual invoicing period, with invoice and purpose-code documentation retained. Any routing of export or service receipts outside the Egyptian banking system constitutes a CBE/ECRA compliance breach and may trigger escalated AML review, suspension of outbound clearances, and Monetary Penalties under Banking Law No. 194 of 2020.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — Banking Law No. 194 of 2020, Foreign Exchange Law No. 38 of 1994, and CBE circulars implementing the 2022–2023 exchange-rate liberalisation and interbank FX-market reforms

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs (FCA/EU-licensed) lack the CBE banking licence required to maintain EGP clearing accounts or settle through Egypt's interbank systems; using an unlicensed EMI for treasury flows risks Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 penalties, rejected SWIFT messages, and blocked inbound remittances. An offshore EMI may only function as a non-clearing collection or invoicing front-end; the operative clearing rail must terminate at a CBE-authorised bank account held in the legal name of EG LLC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection is processed as an inbound SWIFT MT103 credit to the CBE-authorised Egyptian account of EG LLC with the applicable CBE foreign-investment/capital-subscription purpose code embedded in the narrative. The receiving bank lodges the statutory inward-remittance declaration and verifies the transaction against the group board resolution, updated share-capital bylaws, Commercial Registry capital-update filing, and shareholder identification pack. Funds must be held in the designated capital/escrow account until the paid-in capital is recorded in the Commercial Registry; cash import, third-party pass-through credits, and offshore-EMI intermediaries are not recognised.Operational and trade payments are settled via SWIFT MT103 customer credits or MT202 interbank cover, supported by invoices, contracts, customs declarations for equipment imports, and tax-withholding certificates; Documentary Letters of Credit (MT700/MT710 series) are the preferred rail for capital-equipment purchases from confirmed offshore suppliers. Each outbound instruction must carry the statutory CBE purpose code and satisfy the bank's transfer-pricing and tax-clearance screening; EGP-to-FX conversion is matched at the prevailing interbank rate on the value date. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual revenue of EGP 20 million triggers mandatory related-party disclosure; Local File is required when revenue is at least EGP 20 million AND related-party transaction value is at least EGP 5 million; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EGP 200 million.

Economic substance scope: Article 44 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) of Law 91/2005 empowers the ETA to disregard transactions lacking commercial substance; Investment Law 72/2017 incentive eligibility requires a tangible Egyptian presence, local staff, real infrastructure, and contractual revenue generation; a shell LLC without servers, metering records, or technical personnel in Egypt is exposed to full recharacterization of foreign colocation revenue as Egypt-source taxable income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for 2 to 5 years and a fine of up to double the amount of evaded tax (Articles 133–134, Law 91/2005); additional monthly late-payment surcharge of 2% on the unpaid balance.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and board members are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax, penalties, and surcharges; the ETA may impose travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, and seize personal assets.
Late Filing / Non-Filing of Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Fixed fine between EGP 30,000 and EGP 200,000; monthly surcharge of 2% of the unpaid tax up to 60%; denial of the annual tax clearance certificate required for banking and government transactions.

Director personal liability: Under Article 147 of Law 91/2005, legal representatives are personally liable for the company's unpaid CIT and related surcharges where the omission results from willful neglect.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ETA adjustment of taxable profits plus a penalty up to 50% of the incremental assessed tax; mandatory TP audit for repeated non-disclosure and publication of the taxpayer's name on the ETA blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors face secondary assessment for the TP adjustment where the LLC is demonstrably undercapitalized or where documentation proves deliberate falsification of intercompany rates.
Money Laundering / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80/2002, imprisonment for 3 to 7 years and fines from EGP 1 million to EGP 10 million; criminal referral to the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU); confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil is pierced where the LLC operates as a pure shell; directors become subject to criminal extradition, travel bans, personal asset confiscation, and disqualification from holding board positions in Egypt.
