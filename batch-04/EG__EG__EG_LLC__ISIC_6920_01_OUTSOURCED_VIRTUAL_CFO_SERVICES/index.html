Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a service LLC under Law No. 159 of 1981; the declared capital must be adequate for the virtual CFO activity and is commonly set at not less than EGP 50,000. At notarization, at least 25% of the cash share capital must be deposited in a provisional escrow bank account at a licensed Egyptian bank, and the bank-issued capital deposit certificate must be submitted to GAFI. The escrowed funds are released only upon issuance of commercial registration.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense fire safety clearance requires certification of fire extinguishers, an alarm and evacuation plan, and the fire-load classification of the office floor. A structural load inspection certificate must confirm the open-plan workstation occupancy load. The Ministry of Manpower issues the workplace occupational safety and health clearance covering ergonomic and electrical safety. All clearances must be attached to the GAFI operating license application file.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), headquartered in Cairo; official self-service gateways are the Egypt Tax e-Services Portal (https://www.incometax.gov.eg) and the Tax E-Invoicing / E-Receipt Platforms (https://einvoicing.invoicing.gov.eg).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must first complete commercial registration (Commercial Register) and investor file creation at the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) one-stop shop. Thereafter the Egyptian Tax Authority issues a unified Tax Card (بطاقة ضريبية) and TIN through the e-Services Portal. Required file documents include the Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Association (Mourid), board resolution appointing a statutory tax representative, proof of registered office in Egypt or a service address for a pure virtual operation, notarized power of attorney (consularized if executed abroad), and bank confirmation of the Egyptian collection account. Non-resident service suppliers without an Egyptian establishment may obtain simplified VAT TIN registration directly with the ETA non-resident desk for e-invoicing compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egyptian Tax Law No. 91/2005 and VAT Law No. 67/2016 do not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through election. An LLC is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax on its Egyptian taxable income. Available statutory elections include: (i) formation of a consolidated Egyptian tax group between a parent entity and subsidiaries in which it holds at least 95% shareholding, upon joint application; (ii) election of the simplified approximate VAT / small taxpayer regime where annual turnover falls below the statutory bands; and (iii) election to offset measurable input VAT credits against VAT liabilities or to claim a cash refund where credits exceed the output tax in any tax period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies where annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is permitted from EGP 250,000. Non-resident suppliers of digitally supplied or remote consultancy services to non-registered Egyptian recipients are required to register and self-assess VAT through the ETA simplified non-resident procedure once a digital nexus is established, irrespective of turnover thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% rate applies to exported goods physically leaving Egypt and to exported services supplied to a non-resident recipient who has no permanent establishment in Egypt, provided the service is wholly consumed offshore. Fractional CFO advisory and multicurrency ledger bookkeeping will qualify as zero-rated offshore services where the engagement contract is executed with a foreign corporate client, services are performed outside Egypt's economic territory, consideration is received in foreign convertible currency through an authorized payment rail, and the invoice is serialized on the ETA e-invoicing portal as an export transaction. Any service component benefiting an Egyptian establishment or connected to domestic clients remains taxable at 14%.

  • Customs export declaration (ACID) with Egyptian Customs Authority approval and export receipt codes
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or international CMR evidencing Egyptian point of export and offshore destination
  • ETA-stamped zero-rated tax invoice containing the valid UUID, hash, and QR code
  • Signed commercial services contract with the non-resident client
  • Inward remittance certificate issued by an Egyptian bank or foreign-exchange warrant from the Central Bank of Egypt evidencing collection of export consideration in hard currency
  • Accounting ledger reconciliation confirming full recognition of the offshore revenue against the payment rail settlement

Egypt enforces a mandatory pre-clearance fiscal e-invoicing and e-receipt regime administered by the ETA. Invoices must be generated in XML/JSON through an ETA-approved API integration and transmitted for real-time validation before issuance; each compliant invoice receives a unique UUID, cryptographic hash, and a digital QR stamp. All taxpayers above the ETA inclusion thresholds must integrate their ERP, billing, or cloud-based hosted billing engine with the ETA platform. For the Virtual CFO consultancy, the LLC's billing module (including Stripe and Wise invoicing rails) must therefore emit ETA-stamped zero-rated or standard-rated invoices. Non-integration or issuance of unstamped invoices attracts a statutory fine of at least EGP 50,000 per offense, and the buyer cannot deduct the input VAT in its ledger.

The ETA may audit zero-rated export substantiation within a five-year assessment window, with an unlimited period in fraud cases. A claim missing any element of the documentation bundle is clawed back in full: the 14% VAT is re-assessed from the invoice date, together with delay interest at 2% per month or part thereof on the unpaid tax. Deliberate fabrication of export evidence exposes the LLC to criminal tax evasion sanctions of imprisonment of at least three years and/or a fine of EGP 5,000-250,000, plus a civil penalty equal to 100% of the evaded VAT, bank account freeze, and asset attachment. Central Bank of Egypt currency-control violations additionally suspend outward remittance entitlements, revoke the Annual Account number, and disqualify the taxpayer from future VAT refund offsets until the irregularity is cured.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime following the 2016 liberalization reflected in CBE Circular No. 1 of 2017, as amended. Current-account transactions such as service fees, profit remittances, and dividends are generally convertible through licensed banks upon presentation of valid underlying documentation. Capital-account inflows and outflows require CBE registration and are assessed against systemic foreign-currency liquidity. Profit remittance by a wholly foreign-owned Egyptian LLC is permissible but requires filed audited financial statements, a valid tax clearance certificate, payment of dividend withholding tax at the applicable rate, and CBE Form A documentation; foreign-currency scarcity can cause timing delays during periodic FX shocks and CBE rationing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE rules, export proceeds must be received into an Egyptian-bank foreign-currency account within 90 days from the date of customs shipment (extendable to 180 days with prior CBE approval), supported by the customs export declaration and bank receipt voucher. No mandatory percentage conversion or surrender to the central bank has applied since the 2016 exchange-rate float; exporters may freely retain proceeds in foreign currency in onshore accounts and sell to the banking system at negotiated commercial rates.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the unified monetary authority and foreign exchange regulator under Law No. 194 of 2020, with primary rulemaking power over banking operations, FX transfers, capital account transactions, and cross-border liquidity requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Moderate only as a collection front-end. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe can serve as payment-intake channels for invoicing overseas clients, but CBE exchange-control rules require all FX service receipts to be returned to a bank account at a licensed Egyptian bank. The compliant blueprint is EMI collection followed by periodic SWIFT MT103 settlement into the LLC's Egyptian escrow or operational account, never a pure offshore treasury halt.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions in a tax year for the Local File; Master File generally required for groups with worldwide consolidated revenues exceeding EGP 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but general anti-avoidance rules under Law 91/2005 require genuine economic activity, beneficial ownership, and substance to maintain domicile and treaty benefits. A shell company lacking employees, premises, bank activity, or independent management may be re-characterized and stripped of tax treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting / Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 100%–200% of the evaded tax, imprisonment up to five years, and forced collection through attachment of bank accounts and movable assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly file false returns are jointly and severally personally liable for the tax; travel bans may be issued, and under Egyptian criminal tax provisions offenders risk imprisonment in addition to civil restitution.
Shell company / Artificial arrangement / Lack of substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the entity, denial of treaty relief, imposition of 22.5% corporate tax on deemed Egyptian-source income, plus additional penalties of up to 100% of the tax due; possible striking-off from the commercial registry.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who orchestrate the shell may be held personally liable for corporate tax debts; courts may lift the corporate veil and impose travel bans and imprisonment for deliberate abuse.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to EGP 200,000 per violation under Egypt's AML Law (Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended), suspension or revocation of the operating license, and confiscation of linked funds.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution for knowingly enabling money-laundering or circumventing AML controls; sanctions include imprisonment up to two years, personal fines, asset freezes, and travel bans pending judicial proceedings.
