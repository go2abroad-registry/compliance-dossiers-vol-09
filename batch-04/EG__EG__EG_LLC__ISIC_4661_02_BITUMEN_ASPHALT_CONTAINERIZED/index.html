Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA, مصلحة الضرائب المصرية), Ministry of Finance; unified taxpayer digital portal https://www.eta.gov.eg (e-filing, e-invoice, e-receipt); customs and foreign-trade single window operated with the General Organization for Export and Import Control (GOEIC) at https://www.nafeza.gov.eg.

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must (i) incorporate before the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) one-stop shop, obtain the Commercial Registration extract, and publish the statutory notice; (ii) apply to ETA for the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card within 30 days of commencing activity, submitting the LLC contractual documents, commercial registration certificate, power of attorney, authorized officer identity documents, registered address/lease, and board resolution; (iii) obtain a separate VAT registration code once annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 (or voluntarily above EGP 150,000); (iv) register in the NAFEZA single window and the GOEIC exporter/importer register for foreign-trade and customs codes. A non-resident exporter without an Egyptian establishment is not required to obtain an Egyptian TIN solely for zero-rated containerized export sales, but importers/buyers in Egypt must be VAT-registered to clear goods through customs.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – an Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident juridical entity taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the standard 22.5% corporate income tax rate (Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 26/2020 and consolidated for income/stamp/real-estate taxes under Unified Tax Law 67/2023). No pass-through, tax-transparent, or check-the-box status is recognized under Egyptian law; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Egypt-source income, with final withholding on dividends. A pure wholesale trading LLC situated outside a licensed free zone or Suez Canal Economic Zone project is assessed under the ordinary resident company regime; zone-located logistics projects may elect special investment-law incentives. — A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must (i) incorporate before the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) one-stop shop, obtain the Commercial Registration extract, and publish the statutory notice; (ii) apply to ETA for the unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card within 30 days of commencing activity, submitting the LLC contractual documents, commercial registration certificate, power of attorney, authorized officer identity documents, registered address/lease, and board resolution; (iii) obtain a separate VAT registration code once annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000 (or voluntarily above EGP 150,000); (iv) register in the NAFEZA single window and the GOEIC exporter/importer register for foreign-trade and customs codes. A non-resident exporter without an Egyptian establishment is not required to obtain an Egyptian TIN solely for zero-rated containerized export sales, but importers/buyers in Egypt must be VAT-registered to clear goods through customs.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation – an Egyptian LLC is a tax-resident juridical entity taxed at entity level on worldwide income at the standard 22.5% corporate income tax rate (Law 91/2005 as amended by Law 26/2020 and consolidated for income/stamp/real-estate taxes under Unified Tax Law 67/2023). No pass-through, tax-transparent, or check-the-box status is recognized under Egyptian law; non-resident shareholders are taxed only on Egypt-source income, with final withholding on dividends. A pure wholesale trading LLC situated outside a licensed free zone or Suez Canal Economic Zone project is assessed under the ordinary resident company regime; zone-located logistics projects may elect special investment-law incentives.

        
Available election forms
Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box or entity-classification election; tax classification follows legal form and place of incorporation/effective management. Available statutory and administrative elections include: (i) selection of a non-calendar fiscal year approved by ETA; (ii) voluntary VAT registration where annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 150,000 but remain below the EGP 500,000 mandatory threshold; (iii) election between investment-law incentives and the ordinary tax regime for free-zone/SCZone-licensed projects; (iv) election of monthly refund set-off against output VAT rather than cash refunds. Hybrid mismatch consequences follow the OECD BEPS-aligned provisions in the unified tax law without discretionary entity-level elections.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
14%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EGP 500,000 per annum of taxable supplies (mandatory registration within 30 days); voluntary registration is available where annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 150,000. Non-resident suppliers have no general de minimis for B2C supplies and imported B2B services are subject to reverse-charge/withholding VAT collected by the Egyptian buyer.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods physically exported from Egypt, including containerized drummed/bagged penetration-grade bitumen, qualify for the 0% VAT rate where: (i) title passes to the non-resident buyer; (ii) the goods are removed from Egypt under customs control within the statutory export period (generally 90 days from the export tax invoice date under the VAT Law and its executive regulations); (iii) the exporter retains a complete documentary file proving customs exit, foreign payment, and repatriation of proceeds. Services directly related to the export of goods, including ocean freight, cargo handling, port services, and offshore storage, together with services supplied to non-resident recipients and consumed wholly outside Egypt, are also zero-rated. Domestic sales, sales to Egyptian intermediaries for resale within Egypt, and any quantity lost or diverted in local circulation remain taxable at 14%.

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipts apply to all VAT-registered taxpayers: tax invoices must be issued exclusively through ETA-approved channels, either the taxpayer web portal or an ERP/API integration submitting XML/JSON documents with the taxpayer's digital signature and embedded QR code (E-Invoice/E-Receipt system, phased rollout completed by 2023). The ETA validates each document in near real time and issues a submission/approval reference; invoices failing validation are treated as not issued and attract administrative fines. For exports, the e-invoice must be matched with the NAFEZA/ACID customs declaration and the cargo release record.

      
The zero-rating is provisional until the complete export and FX file is substantiated. If any required customs, logistics, or repatriation document is missing, inconsistent, or late, ETA reclassifies the supply as a domestic taxable transaction, assesses VAT at 14%, imposes monthly delay fines and interest in accordance with the VAT Law and Unified Tax Law 67/2023 execution rules, and retroactively reverses any refund or input-tax offset (clawback) associated with the disallowed export. Deliberate fabrication of export declarations, circular export-import schemes, over-invoicing for foreign-currency leakage, or failure to repatriate proceeds within the CBE-prescribed period attract criminal evasion penalties, including imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines of up to EGP 10,000,000, customs penalties under Law 207/2020, Central Bank of Egypt foreign-exchange sanctions, and suspension or removal of the taxpayer's GOEIC/NAFEZA exporter registration.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Egypt maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with comprehensive capital controls enforced by the Central Bank of Egypt. Current account transactions, including trade settlements and approved profit remittances, are generally convertible upon submission of supporting documentation. Capital account transactions, such as equity injections and intercompany loans, require prior CBE approval and are subject to quantitative limits and documentation verification. Profit remittance hurdles include mandatory tax clearance, compliance with anti-avoidance provisions, and potential delays for amounts exceeding specified thresholds.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
All export receipts denominated in foreign currency must be surrendered to an authorized Egyptian bank within six months of collection. Surrender is mandatory at 100% of the realized foreign exchange value, converted to Egyptian Pounds at the prevailing official CBE rate. Non-compliance triggers administrative penalties, potential license suspension, and restrictions on future export financing facilities.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EGP 15,000,000 annual related-party transaction value per counterparty triggers mandatory Local File; Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EGP 1,000,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, Egyptian tax authorities apply substance-over-form, GAAR, and beneficial ownership principles; the bitumen trading LLC must maintain physical office/warehouse presence, staff, logistics records, and board decisions in Egypt to secure tax residency and treaty access.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal fine equal to 50%-200% of the evaded tax, imprisonment for up to 5 years, asset seizure, and mandatory re-assessment under Law No. 91 of 2005 Articles 133-138.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax; travel bans, passport restrictions, disqualification from management, and criminal imprisonment apply to those filing false returns.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, disregard of separate legal personality, application of the Egyptian GAAR, a 50% deficiency penalty on the additional tax, and possible cancellation of tax registration.

        
Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing: shareholders and directors may be personally assessed for the whole corporate tax debt, subjected to travel bans, and criminally prosecuted for fraud if the shell arrangement is considered a sham.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Misstatement
        
Statutory sanction: ETA re-characterizes the profit at the arm's length level, imposes a TP adjustment penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax, plus a 2% per month late-payment surcharge on the adjusted tax due.

        
Director personal liability: Chief financial officer and tax directors can be held personally liable; civil restitution for lost tax, director disqualification, and criminal liability for knowingly submitting incorrect TP documentation.

      

      
      

        Money Laundering / AML
        
Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80 of 2002 and its amendments, imprisonment up to 15 years, a fine equal to at least twice the value of the laundered funds, freezing of bank accounts and cargo, and confiscation of the bitumen inventory.

        
Director personal liability: Directors involved in or negligently facilitating money laundering are subject to personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, international extradition, and imprisonment; corporate banking privileges are suspended.

      

      
      

        Withholding Tax Avoidance
        
Statutory sanction: Monthly 2% delay penalty up to 50% of the unpaid WHT, fixed deficiency fines of EGP 1,000-5,000, enforced collection via bank attachment, and criminal prosecution for collection evasion.

        
Director personal liability: Directors remain personally liable for the withheld tax not remitted, with joint liability for restitution; travel bans and attachment of personal assets are common enforcement measures.
