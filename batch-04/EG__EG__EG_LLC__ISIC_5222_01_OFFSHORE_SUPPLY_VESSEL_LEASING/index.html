Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — central tax administration operating the unified e-Tax portal (eta.gov.eg) and the ETA e-Invoicing/e-Receipt clearance platforms

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC must apply for a unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and tax card through the ETA e-Tax portal using the unified tax registration workflow. Required filings include the certificate of incorporation and memorandum/Articles of Association, Commercial Registry extract, corporate board resolution, notarized legalized power of attorney in favor of the local fiscal representative, and identification documents of directors/shareholders. A foreign entity without a fixed Egyptian establishment that supplies taxable services into Egypt must separately appoint an Egyptian fiscal representative and register for VAT under Law No. 67/2016; the ETA issues a single TIN that integrates income tax, VAT, and stamp tax registration records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed at entity level on worldwide income under Income Tax Law No. 91/2005; shareholders are not pass-through participants; no US-style check-the-box regime exists

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available. Permitted elections and administrative mechanisms include: (i) adopting an ETA-approved non-calendar fiscal year; (ii) filing consolidated corporate tax returns for a qualifying Egyptian group subject to prior ETA approval (Law No. 91/2005); (iii) tax depreciation method and rate elections; and (iv) documentary transfer-pricing compliance elections. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are not recognized under domestic Egyptian tax law, and BEPS-aligned general anti-abuse rules prevail.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration applies once the annual value of taxable supplies exceeds EGP 500,000; voluntary registration is available below the threshold; non-resident suppliers with Egyptian VAT nexus must register through an Egyptian fiscal representative

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) tangible goods physically exported from Egypt within the statutory timeframe and evidenced by ETA and Customs-approved export declarations, and (ii) services supplied to non-resident persons and wholly or effectively consumed outside Egypt, including international transport and maritime services ancillary thereto. Offshore platform vessel leasing/chartering to non-resident operators qualifies when the lease is with a non-resident charterer, the vessel operates in international waters beyond Egyptian territorial waters or supports offshore installations outside the Egyptian VAT territory, and consideration is paid in foreign currency through the Egyptian banking system. The taxpayer must issue the export invoice through the ETA e-Invoice platform with the statutory export code, present the underlying charter contract, and submit customs and banking evidence; input VAT connected to zero-rated export sales is refundable after ETA verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document lodged through the NAFEZA single window) duly stamped by Egyptian Customs and ETA
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing exportation of goods and/or vessel dispatch to non-Egyptian offshore points
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an authorized Egyptian bank confirming receipt of foreign currency from the non-resident charterer
  • ETA-cleared XML e-Invoice carrying the zero-rated export code and digital fiscal stamp
  • Time charter, voyage charter, or bareboat lease contract and offshore platform supply service order identifying the non-resident customer and the delivery/service location

The ETA mandates real-time electronic invoicing (e-Invoice) and electronic receipt (e-Receipt) reporting. Taxable persons must submit structured XML or JSON invoices to the ETA platform via approved APIs or web portal, digitally sign/stamp invoices with an ETA-accredited e-signature, and obtain automatic validation and fiscal clearance before use. B2C transactions must use machine-readable e-Receipts. Non-compliance triggers disconnection of e-signature clearance, refusal of invoice ratification, and statutory penalties under Law No. 67/2016 and the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 188/2020.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to automated ETA cross-checks among the e-Invoice/e-Receipt clearing system, Egyptian Customs NAFEZA records, and Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) foreign-exchange repatriation records. Failure to substantiate physical export or offshore consumption, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day CBE deadline, exposes the taxpayer to reassessment of VAT at the standard rate of 14%, late-payment interest and additional tax, administrative fines, criminal monetary penalties and imprisonment for deliberate fraud, suspension from e-Invoice clearance, and retrospective clawback of refunds and zero-rating for the applicable statutory limitation period (up to five years), with joint liability for the local fiscal representative where applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-determined managed float for the Egyptian Pound (EGP) and holds IMF Article VIII status, under which current-account international payments, including offshore charter hire and vessel-leasing fees payable to an Egyptian resident exporter, are fully convertible through CBE-licensed banks without prior central-bank approval. Capital-account inflows by non-resident shareholders are freely admissible at entry under the Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, but the right to repatriate capital and dividends is compliance-based: the foreign investor must register the inward remittance with the receiving Egyptian bank under the CBE capital-registration mechanism and, at the time of outward remittance, must present audited financial statements, tax clearance, and the bank-issued capital-registration certificate. Following the March 2024 comprehensive FX liberalization, dividend and capital repatriation orders are executable by banks at the prevailing interbank spot rate without prior CBE approval. Residual hurdles are limited to correspondent-bank source-of-funds due diligence and mandatory balance-of-payments reporting by the bank. For this LLC, US-dollar charter income earned from non-resident charterers constitutes an export of services under ISIC 5222 and is freely receivable into an Egyptian foreign-currency retention account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE regulations issued pursuant to Law No. 194 of 2020, export service proceeds, including offshore supply-vessel charter and leasing hire, must be repatriated to Egypt and credited to a CBE-licensed bank account within 180 days from the date the charter service is performed (30 days for physical goods export shipments). The full hard-currency receipt must enter the exporter's Egyptian foreign-currency retention account; the compulsory surrender-conversion ratios in force before March 2024 have been repealed, and exporters may currently retain 100% of receipts in foreign currency for working capital, import settlement, loan servicing, and lawful dividend distribution, provided the bank is notified and the underlying charter contract, hire invoices, and collection evidence are maintained. Non-repatriation, retention of proceeds in an unauthorized offshore account, or false export declarations constitute exchange-control violations under Law No. 194 of 2020, expose the account to freeze, and trigger mandatory disclosure to the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit (EFCU) under AML Law No. 80 of 2002, as amended by Law No. 4 of 2023.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole monetary authority and foreign-exchange administration - exercising powers conferred by the Central Bank and Banking System Law No. 194 of 2020, its Executive Regulations, and the CBE Unified Foreign Exchange Rules binding on all banks and licensed payment service providers in Egypt.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail; MEDIUM only as an auxiliary collection-and-payment conduit subject to strict legal constraints. Under Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE payment-system regulations, only CBE-licensed banks and registered payment service providers may hold or settle EGP balances or access the domestic RTGS (EGNCH) and InstaPay instant-payment rails. Offshore digital EMIs, such as UK FCA-licensed or EU-licensed electronic-money institutions, cannot open EGP IBANs and have no direct access to the Egyptian interbank system. They may lawfully act as multi-currency collection agents vis-a-vis foreign charterers, but every USD/EUR collection must be promptly swept by SWIFT MT103 into the LLC's Egyptian foreign-currency retention account to satisfy the 180-day export-receipts repatriation window. The Egyptian account bank must remain the ultimate settlement institution, and the EMI's transaction data must be fully reconcilable for CBE balance-of-payments statistics and EFCU reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the non-resident shareholder initiates a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for very large values) through its home-country bank and a recognized correspondent such as JPMorgan Chase, Citibank, or Bank of New York Mellon, addressed to the receiving Egyptian bank and designating the LLC's foreign-currency account; the payment narrative must read 'Share capital contribution under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017' and reference the LLC's Commercial Registry number and CBE capital-registration file. (2) The Egyptian bank validates the inbound FDI purpose code 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' (or 'Intercompany Loan' for shareholder loans) and performs source-of-funds verification. (3) The LLC submits the board resolution, subscription agreement, shareholder incorporation documents, and UBO declaration. (4) The bank credits the foreign-currency account under a 'capital under registration' status and thereafter issues the CBE capital-registration certificate, which is the mandatory instrument for any future dividend, interest, or capital repatriation. (5) The LLC separately registers the foreign investment with GAFI to secure statutory investment protection. Under Law No. 194 of 2020 and CBE payment-system regulations, only CBE-licensed banks and registered payment service providers may hold or settle EGP balances or access the domestic RTGS (EGNCH) and InstaPay instant-payment rails. Offshore digital EMIs, such as UK FCA-licensed or EU-licensed electronic-money institutions, cannot open EGP IBANs and have no direct access to the Egyptian interbank system. They may lawfully act as multi-currency collection agents vis-a-vis foreign charterers, but every USD/EUR collection must be promptly swept by SWIFT MT103 into the LLC's Egyptian foreign-currency retention account to satisfy the 180-day export-receipts repatriation window. The Egyptian account bank must remain the ultimate settlement institution, and the EMI's transaction data must be fully reconcilable for CBE balance-of-payments statistics and EFCU reporting.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% (base CIT under Law 91/2005; 25% marginal rate applies to net taxable income exceeding EGP 10,000,000; entities registered in an approved Special Economic Zone, including SCZONE, may elect a 15% flat rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory final withholding tax on distributions by Egyptian LLCs; the company as withholding agent must remit within 15 days of payment. Treaty-reduced rates commonly 5%–10% subject to holding period, TRC, and beneficial ownership review.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual revenue above EGP 8,000,000 triggers mandatory TP disclosure. Full Local File required where entity revenue exceeds EGP 26,000,000 or related-party transactions exceed EGP 3,000,000 (services) or EGP 6,000,000 (goods). Master File required where consolidated group revenue exceeds EGP 500,000,000. CbCR applies to groups with consolidated revenue above the EUR 750,000,000 equivalent at the filing date exchange rate.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but substantive presence is enforced through (i) GAAR in Article 33 of Law 91/2005, (ii) treaty beneficial-ownership clauses, (iii) the real-seat doctrine and management-and-control test of the Egyptian Commercial Code, and (iv) the MLI Principal Purpose Test for treaty access. The vessel-leasing LLC must demonstrate local contractual authority, physically staffed Egyptian operations, commercial acumen in charter negotiations, vessel registry interests, and audited financials to avoid sham-vehicle recharacterization and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporation Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 150, Law 91/2005: imprisonment of 3–5 years; fine equal to 100%–300% of evaded tax; confiscation of vessel equipment; blacklisting from public tenders; loss of tax clearance certificate.

Director personal liability: Signing directors may be prosecuted as accomplices; personal criminal conviction, travel bans, freezing of personal Egyptian and maritime escrow assets, and restitution of evaded tax with surcharges.
Shell Company and Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization; nullity of treaty benefits; back-taxes assessed with 2% monthly surcharge (cap 25%); evaded-tax fine of up to 20%–40% of recharacterized adjustments; annulment of the investment incentive certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for the disregarded entity's tax debt; administrative travel bans; criminal prosecution where misleading statements were submitted to the ETA International Tax Unit.
AML / CFT Violation

Statutory sanction: Law 80/2002 as amended by Law 194/2020: imprisonment 3–7 years and fine EGP 100,000–1,000,000; aggravated or organizational offending up to 15 years' imprisonment; administrative fines and permanent revocation of maritime licenses by E-FIU and the General Authority for Maritime Safety.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability for obstructing E-FIU disclosure; asset freezes ordered by the Money Laundering Combating Unit; D&O insurance indemnification rendered void by willful misconduct.
Persistent Late Filing and Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: 2% monthly late-payment surcharge capped at 25%; fixed infraction fines EGP 500–5,000; ETA deemed-assessment power; forcible bank garnishment or vessel attachment; refusal of the good tax record certificate; blacklisting from charterer qualification.

Director personal liability: Directors who prefer other creditors over the ETA may face personal recourse for unpaid corporate tax; travel bans applicable where tax arrears exceed EGP 1,000,000; administrative disqualification from maritime licensing boards.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment with a 20% penalty on the adjustment amount; rejection of the taxpayer's own margin data; administrative fines for missing Local File or CbCR from EGP 100,000 up to EGP 1,000,000; criminal penalties if the TP documentation is forged.

Director personal liability: De facto directors signing false benchmarking or margin certificates face joint and several liability for the additional tax and surcharges; willful misrepresentation attracts criminal prosecution and travel restrictions.
Maritime Licensing and Flag Violation

Statutory sanction: Vessel arrest and detention; suspension or cancellation of the offshore charter license; fine up to double the unlawful charter hire; suspension of the offshore operations permit under Law 1/1996 (Egyptian Maritime Law) and Law 12/1998.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal prosecution for willful maritime safety breaches; admiralty court may impose travel bans and freeze Egyptian assets; director license revocation; liability overrides any corporate indemnity.
