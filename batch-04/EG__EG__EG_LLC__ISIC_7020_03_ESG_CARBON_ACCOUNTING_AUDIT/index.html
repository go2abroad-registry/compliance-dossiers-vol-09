Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) – Investor Services Center (One-Stop Shop), Companies Department, Ministry of Investment and Foreign Trade, Arab Republic of Egypt.

Governing statute

Law No. 159 of 1981 concerning Joint Stock Companies, Partnerships Limited by Shares, and Limited Liability Companies, as amended by Law No. 138/2019 – Articles 1-12 (formation and registration of LLCs) and investment-related amendments under Law No. 72/2017; implementing executive regulations as issued through GAFI.

Formation / constitutional filings

Notarized Contract of Incorporation (Incorporation Agreement) being the LLC Memorandum and Articles of Association combined; submitted electronically through GAFI One-Stop Shop and registered in the Commercial Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Law 159/1981 as amended in 2019. The founders freely determine the capital, which must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited into a temporary escrow bank account in the LLC's name at an Egyptian bank approved by GAFI, evidenced by a bank deposit certificate, and the funds are released upon issuance of the Commercial Registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire Safety Certificate (certificate of fire suitability) from the General Administration of Civil Protection for the registered office; structural load inspection certificate for any archive-heavy storage room; occupational workplace clearance includes workplace hazard assessment, first-aid inventory, and incident reporting under Ministerial Decree 211/2003; no high-risk chemical or explosive clearance is required for ESG-carbon advisory operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an LLC under Law 159/1981 as amended in 2019. The founders freely determine the capital, which must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited into a temporary escrow bank account in the LLC's name at an Egyptian bank approved by GAFI, evidenced by a bank deposit certificate, and the funds are released upon issuance of the Commercial Registration certificate.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
At the first meeting of the directors held immediately after incorporation, the board resolved: (1) the company is confirmed as validly incorporated under Law No. 159 of 1981 as amended and registered with GAFI; (2) the subscribed share capital stated in the Notarized Contract of Incorporation is hereby declared fully subscribed and paid; (3) the registered office is adopted at [address], Egypt; (4) the undersigned persons are appointed as directors and authorized signatories with the signing powers approved by the members; (5) the appointed directors are delegated to complete commercial registry, tax, social insurance, publication, and bank account opening formalities; and (6) the Chairman may execute all ancillary documents to give effect to these resolutions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, through the ETA Taxpayer Portal, VAT registration systems, and the Egyptian e-Invoicing Portal.

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian LLC must complete incorporation and commercial registration, obtain the tax card and unified tax registration number from the competent ETA tax office or electronic portal, and submit the constitutional documents, commercial register, registered address evidence, identification documents of authorized representatives, lease or ownership evidence, bank details where requested, and business activity information. VAT registration is required where the entity conducts taxable supplies and reaches the statutory registration threshold, or where voluntary registration is accepted by the ETA. A foreign entity without an Egyptian legal entity must generally register through its Egyptian permanent establishment, branch, local tax representative, or other legally recognized taxable presence where it conducts business in Egypt. The registration file should identify corporate income tax obligations, VAT obligations, payroll withholding obligations, and e-invoicing obligations. The ETA may conduct an address or activity verification before issuing or activating the tax registration. Taxpayers must maintain current registration data and notify the ETA of material changes, cessation, branch additions, or changes in authorized signatories.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not generally provide a United States-style check-the-box election for an Egyptian LLC, nor a general elective pass-through regime for ordinary LLCs. The LLC is normally subject to Egyptian corporate income tax at entity level, with distributions potentially subject to separate withholding or dividend tax rules. VAT registration may be mandatory upon exceeding the statutory threshold and may be available voluntarily below the threshold subject to ETA acceptance and compliance. Special tax treatment may arise from qualifying free-zone, investment-zone, or other incentive regimes, but those regimes require separate statutory eligibility and do not constitute a general entity-classification election. No broad Egyptian hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of taxable supplies during the relevant statutory measurement period, generally measured by annual turnover. Voluntary registration may be available below the threshold subject to ETA procedures and approval. Certain goods and services may be exempt, zero-rated, or subject to special rates or schedules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods may qualify for zero-rated export treatment where they are exported outside Egypt under the VAT Law and customs procedures, with proof that the goods physically left Egypt and the export transaction is properly reported. Services supplied to a nonresident may qualify for zero-rating where the statutory conditions are satisfied, including that the recipient is outside Egypt and the service is used or enjoyed outside Egypt; services directly connected with Egyptian real estate, goods located in Egypt, or activities performed in Egypt may fail the offshore-service test. Carbon accounting, ESG advisory, audit, and offset-brokerage services should be analyzed by deliverable, place of performance, customer status, economic use, and the location of the underlying project or asset. A zero-rated invoice must state the applicable VAT treatment, and the taxpayer must retain contracts, customer and residency evidence, work product, correspondence, payment records, and evidence that the service benefit was outside Egypt. Input VAT refunds generally require a valid VAT registration, compliant tax invoices or customs documents, proof of payment and business use, accurate VAT returns, and reconciliation of the claim to accounting records and e-invoice data.

  • Customs export declaration or equivalent electronically accepted export record
  • Commercial invoice identifying the Egyptian exporter, foreign customer, goods, value, currency, and zero-rated treatment
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, airway bill, or other carrier evidence where applicable
  • Evidence of customs release and physical exit from Egypt
  • Foreign customer contract, purchase order, and proof of nonresident status
  • Bank receipt, inward remittance evidence, or other permitted proof of export proceeds where required by the bank, customs, or foreign-exchange rules
  • VAT return and accounting reconciliation linking the export transaction to the relevant invoice, customs record, and payment
  • For offshore services: executed service agreement, deliverables, timesheets or work papers, customer location evidence, proof of offshore use, and payment records; customs documents do not ordinarily apply unless goods are also exported

Entities within the mandatory scope must issue electronic tax invoices for business-to-business transactions through the ETA e-Invoicing Portal. Implementation requires taxpayer onboarding, approved electronic signature or seal, structured invoice data, integration through an approved API or compliant ERP/accounting solution, and generation of the ETA-issued unique invoice identifier and digital validation or approval status. Invoices must be submitted to the ETA in accordance with the applicable technical timing and data requirements; cancellation, rejection, credit notes, and amendments must follow the portal workflow. Electronic receipts apply to covered business-to-consumer transactions under the ETA e-Receipt system. Export transactions and transactions with nonresident customers should be separately coded and supported by evidence of export or offshore use. Failure to issue, report, retain, or correctly authenticate electronic documents can result in VAT assessments, administrative penalties, denial of input-tax credits, and enforcement action.

The ETA may deny or reverse zero-rating where export, nonresident-customer, offshore-use, customs, payment, or invoice evidence is incomplete or inconsistent. Denied treatment can result in assessment of the applicable VAT, late-payment charges, administrative penalties, interest, denial or recovery of input VAT refunds, and retrospective tax liabilities for the audited periods. False invoices, fictitious exports, concealed taxable supplies, fraudulent refund claims, or intentional evasion may trigger tax-evasion proceedings, monetary fines, civil recovery, and potential criminal sanctions under the VAT Law and related tax legislation. Export proceeds and foreign-exchange documentation may also be reviewed by customs, banks, and competent authorities. The 180-day period should be treated as a transaction-control deadline for repatriation evidence where applicable to the relevant export, banking, customs, or foreign-exchange rules; it is not a universal substitute for the separate VAT statutory conditions, and transaction-specific Central Bank of Egypt, banking, and customs requirements must be confirmed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified, market-determined exchange-rate regime for the EGP since March 6, 2024, replacing the prior managed band. Current-account transactions (cross-border service fees, dividends, profit repatriation) are convertible through licensed banks upon presentation of underlying contracts and, where thresholds apply, an Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax-clearance certificate. Capital-account transactions (equity injection, shareholder loans, liquidation proceeds) remain canalized through licensed Egyptian banks, require Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and GAFI registration formalities, and are subject to the CBE investor currency-transfer declaration. The free-transfer guarantee of the Investment Law applies only after tax compliance and CBE documentary controls; the LLC's accumulated EGP balances cannot be re-exported without underlying commercial documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the post-March 2024 unified-rate circulars, the mandatory surrender and conversion ratio for cross-border service-export proceeds is effectively zero percent; the LLC may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in a CBE-licensed foreign-currency account. Statutory repatriation still applies: proceeds from cross-border carbon-audit services must be received into an Egyptian bank account within 180 days from the date of invoice (CBE directives during the 2022-2023 dollar-discipline phase compressed certain commodity export windows to 60-90 days, but non-oil professional services stand at the 180-day outer ceiling). Receipts must be credited through SWIFT and cannot be held offshore indefinitely without CBE approval; a service-export register with underlying contracts and signed audit reports must be maintained for the bank's repatriation due-diligence audit.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - البنك المركزي المصري; exchange-control and investor-inflow coordination with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low direct viability. Egypt does not recognize foreign e-money institution (EMI) licenses for holding EGP or for accessing the CBE's domestic clearing rails (IEPS/IPN). Offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut-type payment gateways) may be used only as ancillary receivables collectors in USD/EUR from non-resident carbon-audit clients, subject to standard OFAC/EU sanctions screening by their partner correspondent banks; such EMIs cannot lawfully settle with Egyptian tax authorities or issue EGP-denominated receipts. The durable treasury architecture must route 100% of Egyptian-invoiced contract value to the onshore CBE-licensed account to satisfy repatriation windows; sustained EMI intermediation above USD 50,000 per month should be avoided for the Egyptian-resident entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital is wired by SWIFT MT103 in USD or EUR to the LLC's foreign-currency account at the onboarding bank, referencing 'equity contribution - Investment Law No. 72 of 2017' and including the GAFI registration number. The receiving bank files the CBE investor currency-transfer declaration within five business days and mirrors the deposit into the capital-escrow ledger for issuance of the certificate of deposit required at the notary (El-Shaher El-Aqary) stage. Ongoing operational inflows from exported carbon-audit services are received by SWIFT MT103 referencing the invoice number and 'ESG advisory services - ISIC 7020'; EGP conversion takes place at the onshore interbank rate. Payment rails are restricted to wire transfer; no card-acceptance infrastructure is necessary at this treasury stage.Cross-border operational settlements (international verifier license fees, subscription software, travel, external expert fees) are executed by SWIFT MT103 from the LLC's foreign-currency account, supported by invoice, contract, and ETA clearance where requested. EGP funding for such wires is converted through the bank's dealing desk against a foreign-currency purchase contract; the bank must retain underlying commercial documents to preserve the CBE current-account standard.
