Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – official registration portal: www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing platform: portal.invoicing.eta.gov.eg

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must appoint a local fiscal representative, present incorporation documents, the parent company certificate of incorporation (apostilled), board resolution, POA, and signed EI/LI application to the Tax Account Department of the Taxpayer Services Sector. The ETA issues a unified Tax Identification Number (‘Tax Card’ / ‘Tax ID’) and VAT registration certificate. Registration is required before commencing taxable activities; the process typically takes 5–15 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; resident LLC is taxed on worldwide income; non-resident LLC is taxed on Egypt-source income only; no territorial exclusion for resident companies. — A foreign entity must appoint a local fiscal representative, present incorporation documents, the parent company certificate of incorporation (apostilled), board resolution, POA, and signed EI/LI application to the Tax Account Department of the Taxpayer Services Sector. The ETA issues a unified Tax Identification Number (‘Tax Card’ / ‘Tax ID’) and VAT registration certificate. Registration is required before commencing taxable activities; the process typically takes 5–15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; resident LLC is taxed on worldwide income; non-resident LLC is taxed on Egypt-source income only; no territorial exclusion for resident companies.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Egyptian law. Tax consolidation is not permitted; each entity stands alone. A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC may elect to be treated as a real estate investment company (REIC) only if it meets specific real estate objects, not applicable here. No other elective entity classification mechanisms are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 per annum of taxable turnover (goods or services).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export applies to physical goods that leave Egypt, and to services rendered to non-resident recipients where the benefit of the service is enjoyed entirely outside Egypt (offshore services), provided the supplier retains documentary proof of export from the Egyptian customs authority and receipt of foreign currency proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (certified copy from the Egyptian Customs Authority)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (or CMR for land transport) evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank confirming foreign currency proceeds credited to the exporter’s account

Egypt mandates mandatory e-invoicing under Law No. 67 of 2016 and the e-invoicing resolution. Taxable persons must transmit documents to the ETA’s e-invoice portal in real time via API (JSON format); the ETA validates and cryptographically stamps the invoice before issuance. E-receipts are also required for business-to-consumer sales. Non-compliance with e-invoicing results in rejection of deduction, fines, and escalation to mandatory reverse-charge imposition.

Export zero-rating is heavily audited. Failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the 180-day deadline or missing documentation triggers additional assessment of the 14% VAT, plus delayed tax penalty of 2% per month (or 3% for value-added tax), and a tax evasion fine of up to EGP 10 million and/or imprisonment when fraud is shown. Retrospective clawback applies for the full VAT amount plus interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a unified and flexible exchange-rate regime re-established in March 2024. EGP is freely convertible for current-account transactions including software export receipts and profit remittances, subject to documentary verification by an authorized bank. Capital-account inflows are admitted only through the CBE's foreign capital registration mechanism; the inward remittance must be tied to the EG_LLC's capital file and declared in the CBE balance-of-payments return. Profit and dividend remittance require audited financial statements, an Egyptian Tax Authority tax clearance certificate, the relevant CBE purpose code, and execution at the official interbank rate. Practical hurdles include an audit trail of underlying contracts, limited onshore FX depth for large one-off remittances, and bank concentration limits for forward cover.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts from software and IT services must be credited to an Egyptian licensed bank within 90 days of invoice or shipment date, in line with CBE trade-related transaction regulations; a five-month extension may apply for certain categories with prior CBE approval. The historic 100% mandatory surrender ratio has been abolished under the March 2024 liberalization. Exporters may retain and use foreign-currency receipts in CBE-licensed accounts. The bank must validate the underlying transaction through the commercial registry and e-invoice system, and the exporter must submit a monthly balance-of-payments declaration identifying realization date and currency composition.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) / البنك المركزي المصري - the sole monetary authority regulating banking, foreign exchange, payment systems and AML/CTF compliance in the Arab Republic of Egypt.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low as a primary or sole treasury habitat for EG_LLC. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer cannot hold EGP, cannot settle through the CBE RTGS or Instant Payment Network, and cannot issue Egyptian IBANs; they therefore fail the statutory liquidity and audit requirements of the CBE. Locally licensed fintechs such as Fawry, Paymob, or Geidea may be used as payment-orchestration rails for payroll disbursement and domestic collections, but they must remain nested beneath a full-service CBE-licensed bank account for statutory custody, FX, and capital movements. The recommended treasury topology is to maintain the licensed bank account as the system of record and use EMIs only as niche sub-collectors for cross-border SEPA or SWIFT collections in EUR or USD, with immediate sweeping into the Egyptian account.

Equity proceeds must be wire-transferred via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's foreign bank to the EG_LLC's CBE-licensed beneficiary bank, identifying the beneficiary company and its Egyptian IBAN in the beneficiary field. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File trigger: aggregate controlled transactions exceeding EGP 8,000,000 per tax year; Master File trigger: MNE group consolidated revenue above EGP 1,000,000,000. Simplified TP disclosure is always required in Form 19.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no standalone ESR regime; substance is enforced through the place-of-effective-management test (Article 3, Law 91/2005), beneficial-ownership conditions for treaty relief, and GAAR introduced under Law 25/2020. Payroll-data consistency checks by the ETA will recharacterize artificial or low-substance routing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Payroll Reporting

Statutory sanction: Article 131 (Law 91/2005): imprisonment 3-5 years and/or fine EGP 20,000-50,000; recovery of evaded tax with Article 127 delay interest; seizure of bank accounts and cancellation of e-invoice registration.

Director personal liability: Directors prosecuted jointly with the LLC; court-ordered travel bans, asset freezes, restitution orders; disqualification from GAFI and Commercial Registry positions.
Shell Company / Artificial Substantive Failure

Statutory sanction: GAAR and TP recharacterization (Law 25/2020; Article 35 bis, Law 91/2005): upward TP adjustment, disallowed deductions, additional tax at 22.5% plus penal interest; denial of treaty benefits for non-beneficial owners.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable where they knowingly structured sham arrangements; referral to the Public Prosecutor; corporate veil pierced on grounds of fraud.
AML / Counter-Financing-of-Terrorism Failure

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment 3-7 years and/or fine EGP 100,000-1,000,000; legal-person fine up to EGP 10,000,000; freezing of payment-rails license and FCI authorization; enhanced de-risking by correspondent banks.

Director personal liability: Board members and AML compliance officer jointly and severally liable; travel bans and criminal seizure during investigations by the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit.
Cross-Border Profit Stripping and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of payments, denial of deductibility, WHT at 20% plus late interest; penal assessment under Law 91/2005 Article 130; mutual agreement procedure not available once penal assessment is final.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax agents exposed to civil restitution and criminal complicity for signing false TP documentation; Mutual Legal Assistance requests follow the money trail.
