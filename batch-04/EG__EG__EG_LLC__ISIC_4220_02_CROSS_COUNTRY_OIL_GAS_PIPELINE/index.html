Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) — Arab Republic of Egypt, Ministry of Finance; official portal: www.eta.gov.eg; electronic invoicing and e-receipt platform: https://einvoicing.invoicing.eta.gov.eg (ETA Invoicing Portal with production REST API endpoints); customs single-window: www.nafiza.gov.eg (NAFIZA).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Egyptian LLC is incorporated either through the Egyptian Commercial Registry (MOCR) for onshore companies or through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) one-stop-shop where investment incentives apply. Following the issuance of the Commercial Registration certificate, the LLC must open a corporate tax file at the competent ETA tax office within the statutory window and obtain the unified Tax Card number, which serves as both the corporate TIN and the VAT identification number. VAT registration is executed on the statutory Form No. 1, accompanied by the commercial registration certificate, lease/deed evidencing business premises, board/shareholder resolutions, authorization deeds, and the contract portfolio. For a non-resident EPC contractor with an Egyptian VAT nexus but no Egyptian permanent establishment, the ETA requires a local fiscal representative/tax agent to complete the registration; the processing time from incorporation to tax registration is generally 15 to 30 working days. After registration, the taxpayer must complete NAFIZA customs registration and establish an export/import file at an Egyptian authorized bank for trade settlement and FX repatriation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A standard Egyptian LLC is mandatorily taxed at the entity level as a separate corporate taxpayer under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005, as amended; profits are subject to the standard Egyptian corporate income tax rate (currently 22.5%) without any domestic pass-through classification being available for LLCs. Distributions to foreign shareholders may attract withholding taxes under applicable law and double tax treaties. The LLC is also a VAT taxable person required to register under Law No. 67 of 2016 when its Egyptian taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold.

Available election forms

Not Available — Egypt does not operate a US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through regime for LLCs; an Egyptian LLC is irrevocably taxed at the entity level under the domestic corporate income tax. No hybrid-mismatch or entity-classification elections are provided under Egyptian domestic law; however, dual-residence issues and permanent establishment determinations are governed by the applicable double taxation agreements and Egypt's tie-breaker rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover of goods and services — mandatory VAT registration threshold for persons whose taxable supplies exceed this amount in a 12-month period; voluntary registration is available below the threshold. A foreign non-resident supplier of cross-border pipeline construction services into Egypt becomes registrable once its Egyptian taxable supplies exceed the same EGP 500,000 threshold, subject to the Egyptian place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Egyptian VAT applies to (i) goods physically exported outside Egypt, substantiated by a valid customs export declaration and proof of physical exit from Egyptian customs territory; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident person where the place of supply is determined to be outside Egypt under the VAT place-of-supply rules and the service is actually consumed abroad. For the ISIC 4220 cross-country gas pipeline EPC scope, the following allocations govern eligibility: welding, trenching, and construction services physically performed inside Egypt on Egyptian land or connected with immovable property situated in Egypt are subject to the standard 14% VAT; by contrast, the supply of equipment, materials, and prefabricated line pipes that are exported from Egypt, and any offshore services physically rendered outside Egyptian territory to the foreign principal on pipeline segments located abroad, may qualify for 0% export treatment. The EPC contract must clearly sever the onshore Egyptian portion from the offshore/export portion, and the exporter must present the foreign principal contract, works-done certificates for offshore segments, customs export declarations, transport evidence, and the ETA-cleared zero-rate e-invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (accepted through the NAFIZA single window), including the transport/transit declaration for pipeline goods physically crossing the Egyptian border
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or equivalent international transport evidence, proving physical departure from Egyptian customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian authorized bank evidencing receipt of the export consideration in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice together with the signed EPC contract, purchase order, and offshore works completion certificates identifying the non-resident principal
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice bearing the zero-rated VAT code, invoice UUID, and QR code
  • Letter of Credit (Sovereign_Project_Wire) or SWIFT/TT transfer documentation matching the invoiced export value and the foreign project payment milestone

Mandatory nationwide e-invoicing is in effect: all ETA-registered taxpayers phased into the program must issue invoices exclusively through the ETA e-invoicing platform in the ISO 20022 UBL (XML) cross-industry format and submit every invoice in real time through the official REST API; the ETA performs synchronous fiscal clearance and returns a unique invoice UUID and a machine-readable QR code that must appear on the invoice presented to the buyer or to customs for exports. The parallel e-receipt system (FATOORA) applies to business-to-consumer transactions. Any invoice not cleared by the platform is deemed non-compliant and cannot support input VAT deduction, zero-rating, or refund claims. For exported goods, the zero-rated e-invoice must be matched in the ETA environment against the NAFIZA customs export declaration before refund can be processed; digital stamping of movement and transit documents is additionally required for cross-country pipeline assets transiting Egyptian territory.

The ETA, the Egyptian Customs Authority, and the Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) jointly audit export zero-rating claims against the NAFIZA customs records, the cleared e-invoice data, bank statements, and the inward remittance history. Where export proceeds are not received and repatriated through the Egyptian banking system within the CBE deadline (maximum 180 days from export unless a valid extension is approved), the zero-rating is retroactively reversed and the 14% VAT becomes immediately assessed from the date of the invoice, together with the statutory late-payment surcharge (2% per month) and interest. Fraudulent zero-rating, fictitious exports, materially false customs declarations, or schemes engineered to avoid VAT or to divert export proceeds abroad are sanctioned under VAT Law No. 67/2016 and CBE Law No. 194/2020 as tax evasion and foreign-exchange violations, respectively: the penalties include a fine of up to three times the tax evaded, additional fines of up to EGP 300,000, and imprisonment of up to five years for the responsible directors; the ETA may also blacklist the entity, suspend its e-invoicing clearance access, claw back any refunded input VAT with surcharges, and the customs authority may seize the goods and impose customs penalties of up to double the applicable duties under the Customs Law No. 207 of 2020 when documentation has been falsified.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a market-driven exchange rate regime supervised by CBE, with current-account transactions broadly convertible through authorized dealer banks upon supporting documentation. Capital-account flows, including equity injections, intra-group loans, intercompany balances, and disposal proceeds, require CBE-compliant registration, documentary verification, and tax clearance. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is legally permissible after satisfaction of the Egyptian Tax Authority certificate, audited financial statements, GAFI direct-investment registration, and board/E.G.M. resolution; the conversion and outward transfer are executed through the local licensed bank within the limits imposed by the entity's historical remittance capacity and the bank's CBE external-debt reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE foreign-exchange instructions, export proceeds must be repatriated to an Egyptian authorized bank within 180 days from shipment or service completion. The exporter must file the relevant export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading/customs manifest, and final invoice through the banking channel; the bank credits the proceeds to the exporter's foreign-currency account. There is no fixed statutory percentage of forced conversion in the current flexible-exchange regime; the exporter may hold the proceeds in USD/EUR or convert into EGP at the prevailing interbank buying rate, while the CBE retains legal authority to impose temporary surrender or conversion ratios under exceptional balance-of-payments pressure.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore EMIs. CBE allows only licensed banks, authorized exchange bureaus, and regulated payment service providers to undertake cross-border treasury operations for Egyptian corporate entities. Offshore EMI digital-IBAN platforms operating under EU or UK regulatory licenses are not authorized dealers under Egyptian Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and cannot legally be used as the primary settlement or escrow rail for the Egyptian LLC. Such platforms may serve only a non-Egyptian holding or offshore SPV, and even then must not receive or disburse EGP or clear Egyptian export proceeds without a CBE correspondent arrangement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder shall initiate a SWIFT MT103 credit from its treasury account to the Egyptian LLC's capital account at the selected Tier-1 Egyptian bank. The wire transfer must include the GAFI direct-investment registration number, the LLC tax identification number, and the payment reference 'DIRECT INVESTMENT / EQUITY / EG_LLC / ISIC 4220'. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for offshore EMIs. CBE allows only licensed banks, authorized exchange bureaus, and regulated payment service providers to undertake cross-border treasury operations for Egyptian corporate entities. Offshore EMI digital-IBAN platforms operating under EU or UK regulatory licenses are not authorized dealers under Egyptian Banking Law No. 194 of 2020 and cannot legally be used as the primary settlement or escrow rail for the Egyptian LLC. Such platforms may serve only a non-Egyptian holding or offshore SPV, and even then must not receive or disburse EGP or clear Egyptian export proceeds without a CBE correspondent arrangement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5% under Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended by Law No. 8 of 2015; no separate municipal surtax for an Egypt LLC engaged in commercial or industrial pipeline EPC activities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on Egyptian-source dividend distributions paid to non-resident shareholders, according to Law No. 91 of 2005 as amended; distributions to resident corporate shareholders may qualify for parent-subsidiary exemption, while non-resident shareholders may seek treaty reduction.

The annual return must be filed within four months after the end of the LLC's fiscal year; a calendar-year taxpayer must file no later than 30 April. Non-resident contractors with an Egyptian permanent establishment (PE) follow the same deadline based on the PE's audited Egyptian branch accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian Transfer Pricing Guidelines (Ministerial Decree No. 106 of 2019) require: (i) a Local File where annual turnover exceeds EGP 20 million or controlled transactions exceed EGP 10 million; (ii) a Master File where global group consolidated turnover exceeds EGP 2 billion; (iii) a transfer pricing disclosure schedule attached to Form No. 8 by taxpayers with controlled transactions above the ETA filing threshold.

Economic substance scope: Egypt is not an ESR jurisdiction; however, the ETA applies the substance-over-form rule and statutory anti-abuse articles (Articles 79-81 of Law No. 91 of 2005) to disregard transactions lacking commercial rationale. A pipeline EPC contractor using an Egyptian LLC must therefore maintain physical staff, project equipment, banking, decision-making functions, and project management in Egypt; absence of these facts can lead to PE reattribution, denial of treaty relief, and recharacterization of the LLC as a sham entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Articles 133-136 of Law No. 91 of 2005 impose a fine equal to not less than 100% and up to 300% of the evaded tax, imprisonment for up to five years, mandatory tax attachment proceedings, and disqualification from Egyptian state tenders.

Director personal liability: Directors and the de facto manager who sign or cause false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency and penalties; Egyptian courts may pierce the veil, issue travel bans, freeze personal assets, and impose custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Sham Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the LLC under Articles 79-81 of Law No. 91 of 2005; reallocation of the full pipeline profit to the Egyptian PE; application of 2% per-month delay surcharges; denial of DTA benefits; and potential cancellation of the investment license.

Director personal liability: Directors may be treated as the taxable person for the reattributed profits, with personal liability for the full assessed tax, asset freezes, and travel restrictions until the liability is discharged; corporate veil piercing is regularly applied in sham-PE cases.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment to the Egyptian taxable profit, a 50% penalty on the additional profit, monthly delay surcharges, and administrative fines up to EGP 500,000 for failure to file the transfer pricing disclosure under Ministerial Decree No. 106 of 2019.

Director personal liability: Directors and chief financial officers who approve or fail to document related-party transactions may be personally assessed for the penalty portion, debarred from acting as tax representatives, and exposed to ETA collection enforcement.
AML / Financial Criminality / Foreign Corrupt Practices

Statutory sanction: Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended and its executive regulations impose imprisonment for three to seven years, fines up to EGP 1,000,000, confiscation of funds and project proceeds, inclusion on the Central Bank of Egypt blacklist, and mandatory reporting to the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU).

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners, chief executive officers, and compliance officers who authorize, conceal, or fail to report suspicious transactions are personally subject to criminal prosecution, travel bans, passport seizure, personal asset confiscation, and prohibition from managing Egyptian companies.
