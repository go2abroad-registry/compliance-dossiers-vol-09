Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance — official e-services and e-filing portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing API host: https://einvoice.invoicing.eta.gov.eg; export customs clearance is supervised by the Egypt Customs Authority under the same Ministry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned LLC obtains its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the GAFI one-stop-shop: (1) notarized Articles of Association; (2) GAFI incorporation certificate; (3) commercial registry entry; (4) ETA unified registration application filed electronically within 30 days of incorporation, followed by ETA verification of the registered seat, local bank account, and the appointed tax representative; (5) issuance of the electronic tax card containing the unified TIN. A non-resident company that establishes a permanent establishment in Egypt must complete the same registration within 30 days of commencing its Egyptian activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person taxed under Income Tax Law 91/2005 on worldwide profits at 22.5% CIT; downstream petroleum wholesale/brokerage profits remain in the standard CIT regime unless the entity is incorporated inside a GAFI/SCZONE free-zone facility, in which case a 1%–3% annual fee on turnover replaces CIT and VAT (special zone treatment). Dividends remitted to a foreign shareholder expose the LLC to 10% withholding (treaty-reduced), and no pass-through recognition is available.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box and no domestic hybrid-mismatch election exists in Egypt. Available elections: (i) free-zone status under Investment Law 72/2017 — annual fee of 1% (warehousing/wholesale) or 3% (services) of turnover in lieu of CIT/VAT; (ii) consolidated tax filing for an Egyptian parent holding at least 95% of Egyptian subsidiaries (Law 91/2005, Art. 53 bis, with ministerial approval); (iii) investment-incentive deduction of 30%–50% of qualifying capital expenditures listed under Investment Law 72/2017 and its regional implementation maps; (iv) optional application of the 40.55% petroleum tax rate under production-sharing contract frameworks (E&P only). Five-year loss carryforward is automatic.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies (mandatory threshold); registration filing is due within 60 days of the end of the month in which cumulative taxable supplies cross the threshold; imports of any value are subject to VAT at customs without a registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of crude oil and refined petroleum products (ISIC 4661) are zero-rated under Art. 14 of Law 67/2016 when: the exporter holds a valid ETA tax card and an EGPC/General Authority of Petroleum export authorization naming the goods and quantity; the goods physically leave Egypt within 180 days of the invoice date; the bill of lading names an Egyptian port of loading and a foreign discharge port; the export proceeds are received in foreign currency through an Egyptian licensed bank; and the corresponding export customs declaration is accepted and closed by Egypt Customs. Offshore brokerage services for marine fuel supplied to non-resident principals are zero-rated as exported services when the contract, negotiation, and benefit of the service are located abroad and the commission is remitted from outside Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (final closed 'exit' copy) issued by Egypt Customs Authority
  • Original Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit advice showing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds into the exporter's Egyptian bank account
  • Commercial invoice and underlying export contract (purchase order / sale confirmation)
  • EGPC / General Authority of Petroleum export authorization for the specific petroleum product and quantity
  • Accredited marine surveyor's quality-and-quantity certificate plus terminal loading certificate

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing (and e-receipts for B2C sales) for VAT registrants: the taxpayer must connect its local billing environment to the ETA's e-invoicing API, issue invoices in the ETA JSON/XML schema, sign each invoice with the ETA-certified digital certificate, and transmit the invoice to the ETA clearance engine within 24 hours of issuance; only ETA-cleared invoices (bearing a unique invoice ID, QR code, and digital signature) are admissible for input-VAT deduction and export zero-rating substantiation. No physical stamping or paper pre-certification remains.

ETA export audits may reopen files for five years from the filing date; if any mandatory document is absent, forged, or the 180-day repatriation deadline is missed, the sale is re-characterised as a domestic supply: 14% VAT is assessed retroactively with a 2% monthly delay fine and an evasion penalty of up to 50% of the under-assessed tax (Law 191/2022), and deliberate document falsification carries imprisonment up to 5 years. The exporter is also suspended from the ETA zero-rated exporter list and referred to the Central Bank of Egypt and EGPC for license revocation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the March 2024 exchange-rate unification and the CBE-led liberalization under the IMF Extended Fund Facility arrangement, the EGP functions as a bank-mediated managed float. Current-account convertibility — import settlement, freight, insurance, marine-fuel procurement and commissions — is operational through licensed banks subject to presentation of underlying contracts, invoices, shipping documents and E-Invoice/E-Receipt tax records; bona fide trade payments do not require central quantitative pre-approval. Capital-account convertibility remains restricted: foreign shareholders must register the investment with GAFI and effect the transfer through an Egyptian bank, while dividend repatriation and post-liquidation proceeds require audited financial statements, Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) clearance, Social Insurance clearance, and CBE-compliant banking codes evidencing the original inward investment. Practical hurdles for a fuel-wholesale trading LLC are: (i) banks enforce stricter documentary review and purpose-code verification for energy-trading flows; (ii) during USD-liquidity cycles (as experienced in 2022-2024), FX queues on dividend remittances may arise; and (iii) profit distribution is permitted in EGP equivalent unless the bank is satisfied with the USD earning evidence — treasury planning should therefore maintain a 2-4 week USD settlement buffer and parallel EGP liquidity lines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the unified post-2024 regime, no mandatory conversion ratio into EGP applies — exporters may retain export receipts in foreign-currency accounts at an Egyptian bank. The statutory repatriation rule (CBE implementing instructions in force as of 2025) requires 100% of export proceeds to be credited to the exporter's Egyptian bank account through banking channels within 60 days of the bill-of-lading or airway-bill date; for bulk energy commodities, marine fuels and refined petroleum products, an extension to 180 days is obtainable through the handling bank where the letter-of-credit tenor, long-dated incoterms or demurrage schedules justify it. External collection through offshore accounts, offset, barter, or trade-for-debt arrangements is generally prohibited absent an express CBE Governor exemption; non-compliance exposes the exporter to suspension of export-document processing, loss of foreign-exchange access, and AML penalties.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) — البنك المركزي المصري, constituted under Law No. 88 of 2003 (Central Bank, Banking Sector and Monetary System Law, as amended). The CBE is the sole regulator of foreign-exchange operations, banking licensing, capital movements and payment-system rails inside Egypt; the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) registers foreign investment and certifies capital inflows under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, while the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit (EMLCU) operates as the national financial-intelligence unit for AML/CFT screening of cross-border wires.

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Legal constraints: CBE Law No. 88 of 2003, the CBE e-money licensing decree of 2020 and the Central Bank prohibition on unlicensed digital-payment intermediation require all EGP and export-import settlement rails to pass through a licensed Egyptian bank; offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex, Dukascopy-type) cannot lawfully hold trade proceeds for the Egyptian LLC, serve as the FX disbursement node, or evidence customs/tax-relevant payment flows. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) the foreign shareholder registers the equity investment with GAFI and obtains an investment certificate under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017; (2) the Egyptian LLC opens a Capital Escrow Account at the licensed bank and files the GAFI certificate, commercial-registry extract, tax card and E-Invoice activation proof; (3) the shareholder remits capital by SWIFT MT103/MT202 exclusively from its own bank account with the purpose code 'Direct investment — equity participation in Egyptian LLC', referencing the LLC name, GAFI registration number and escrow account number; (4) the receiving bank verifies payer-name match against the GAFI certificate and issues an inward-investment deposit certificate; (5) following full Commercial Registry activation and capital verification, the escrow balance is released into the operational account for energy-contract disbursements.
