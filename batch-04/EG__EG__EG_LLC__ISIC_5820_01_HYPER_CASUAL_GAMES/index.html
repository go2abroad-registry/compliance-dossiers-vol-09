Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for a Limited Liability Company under Egyptian law; the capital is determined in the Articles of Association. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of software and digital game publishing services qualify for 0% VAT when services are supplied for use outside Egypt, the payment is received in foreign convertible currency, and the services are not connected with Egyptian territorial consumption. The exporter must be able to evidence that the recipient is a non-resident and that the actual consumption occurs abroad

  • Customs Export Declaration (for physical goods; not applicable for pure digital services)
  • Signed service agreement or contract with foreign customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) evidencing receipt in foreign exchange
  • Record of platform payout statements (e.g., AdMob/AppLovin) showing source and deduction of withholding or commissions
  • Apostilled or notarized proof of non-resident recipient status

Mandatory e-invoicing for all VAT-registered taxpayers transacting with B2B/B2G partners; invoices must be transmitted in real time to the ETA e-invoice portal with API integration (JSON/XML), cryptographic digital stamping, and fiscal clearance before use. E-receipts are mandatory for consumer sales in phased rollout

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the 6 March 2024 flexible exchange-rate reform anchored by the IMF EFF programme, the Egyptian Pound is a unified, market-determined currency with full legal convertibility for current-account transactions: ad-network proceeds (Google AdMob, AppLovin), software royalties and services receipts may be credited and repatriated in USD, EUR or EGP through the authorized banking system without prior CBE licence, at the interbank rate. Residual capital-account gates remain: (i) foreign shareholder loans should be documented on arm's-length terms and supported by executed facility agreements; (ii) dividend remittances require an Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) tax-clearance certificate confirming settlement of the 10% statutory withholding, or a treaty-reduced rate, plus a bank-of-exchange FX purpose form; (iii) CBE circulars restrict physical foreign-currency cash deposit and withdrawal channels but do not constrain SWIFT-clearing corporate transfers; (iv) the 2022-23 import L/C-only restrictions were abolished, leaving no quantitative limits on current-account outward payments. The net assessment is unrestricted convertibility for a low-risk digital-services exporter, subject only to documentary and tax-clearance compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CBE no longer imposes any statutory surrender percentage or mandatory conversion ratio on export receipts. Pursuant to the 2023 ministerial measures and the March 2024 unified-FX regime, the Egyptian exporter may retain one hundred percent of foreign-currency proceeds in its USD/EUR corporate accounts and convert at the interbank rate when EGP funds are required. Repatriation is nevertheless obligatory: all export and ad-network proceeds must be credited through an Egyptian licensed bank of exchange within the CBE benchmark collection window - approximately 120 days from the service-rendition date for digital and software exports, with longer windows for goods exports subject to CBE-endorsed extensions - and must be supported by the registered export form, platform settlement statements and bank-of-exchange documentation. Delinquent repatriation triggers adverse CBE reporting, enhanced AML/CFT scrutiny and potential suspension of subsequent inward-clearing approvals.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - the sole sovereign monetary, banking-licensing and foreign-exchange control authority in the Arab Republic of Egypt, with powers derived from Law No. 88 of 2003 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) as amended and recodified by Law No. 194 of 2020 (Foreign Exchange Regulation). The General Authority for Investment (GAFI) governs investment registration and foreign-capital entry, while the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) supervises non-bank financial institutions. Every cross-border treasury transaction of the Egyptian resident limited liability company must be executed through a CBE-authorized bank of exchange.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% gross-basis withholding tax on dividends distributed by an Egyptian LLC to non-resident shareholders (Law 91/2005, Art. 58); the rate applies to cash and deemed distributions and is reduced under applicable double tax agreements, generally to 5-15% depending on ownership levels.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Understatement of Digital Advertising Revenue

Statutory sanction: A fine equal to the full amount of the evaded tax and no less than EGP 10,000 and no more than EGP 50,000, plus imprisonment for a term of up to 5 years (Law 91/2005, Art. 133); additionally, the ETA imposes a 2%-monthly delay surcharge on the unpaid tax and may compound penalty interest through judicial attachment.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general manager who sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax under the Egyptian Tax Collection Law; the ETA may apply for precautionary asset freezes, obtain a travel ban against each responsible director, and criminal conviction disqualifies the individual from managing any Egyptian legal entity.
Shell Company / Sham Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: ETA disregards the LLC under Article 143 GAAR, reattributes the profits to the foreign principal, denies all treaty reductions, and assesses 22.5% CIT on the re-attributed income plus 20% WHT on deemed dividends and royalties, with the 2%-per-month delay surcharge; additional fines range from EGP 20,000 to EGP 100,000 depending on the financial benefit obtained.

Director personal liability: Directors executing sham service and licensing agreements may be pierced; the court can impose personal civil restitution for the re-attributed tax, order the release of bank guarantees, impose imprisonment where false declarations about substance are proven, and freeze personal assets pending final tax litigation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's-Length Mispricing

Statutory sanction: ETA upward adjustment under the approved OECD methodologies (CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM, Profit Split); additional charge of the full CIT deficiency at 22.5%, plus a 2%-monthly delay surcharge; separate administrative fine up to EGP 20,000 for missing documentation; if the mispricing is shown to be intentional, the evasion penalties and imprisonment provisions of Art. 133 apply.

Director personal liability: The TP adjustment binds the LLC, but directors who knowingly certified the absence of controlled transactions in Form 21 may be personally liable for the differential where corporate assets are insufficient; ETA enforcement includes personal bank attachment, travel bans on the financial officer, and in flagrant cases criminal referral for false attestation.
Money Laundering and Unlicensed Payment Intermediation

Statutory sanction: Confiscation of the laundered funds; fine of up to EGP 1,000,000 or double the value of the funds, whichever is greater; imprisonment from 5 to 10 years for persons convicted of money laundering; additionally, ETA blocks the foreign payout chain without reopening an Egyptian bank account, and the tax authority retroactively assesses 22.5% CIT plus 20% repatriation WHT on the cleansed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable solely on the basis of negligent oversight of payment flows even without personal benefit; a conviction cancels banking mandates, imposes a travel ban, disqualifies the director from future Egyptian financial institution roles, and creates a statutory lien over personal assets to settle the tax arrears.
