E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first complete commercial registration with the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) or the Ministry of Trade and Industry, submit a notarised and legalised power of attorney to the Egyptian Tax Authority, provide the parent entity's certificate of incorporation and passport copies of directors, and file the prescribed tax registration forms (Form No. 1 for tax card / VAT registration as applicable). The ETA issues a unified tax identification number (TIN) and a VAT registration certificate once the application and supporting documents are reviewed and approved.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Egyptian tax law. An Egyptian-registered LLC is automatically treated as a taxable juridical person subject to Egyptian corporate income tax; no elective pass-through classification is available. Hybrid mismatch rules apply under the Egyptian Income Tax Law and are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable turnover from goods and services, excluding capital assets, subject to mandatory VAT registration

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of exported goods that physically leave Egypt within the prescribed customs timelines and supplies of certain services rendered to non-resident recipients may be zero-rated for Egyptian VAT purposes, provided demonstrable documentary evidence exists that the goods or services are genuinely exported or used outside Egypt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Cargo Declaration / Export Form) duly stamped by Egyptian Customs Authority
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical departure from Egypt
  • Inward Foreign Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and contract of sale, and supporting warehouse/export delivery records

Egypt has implemented a mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance system administered by the Egyptian Tax Authority. Registered taxpayers must issue invoices and receipts through the ETA's approved electronic reporting platform, transmit real-time XML data via API integration, and obtain a unique electronic invoice/receipt verification code (UUID) before tax clearance. Phased compliance applies by turnover band; penalties apply to non-compliant entities.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to enhanced ETA audit verification. Failure to satisfy documentary requirements or to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline reverses the zero rating and re-exposes the taxpayer to standard-rate VAT plus late payment interest. Fraudulent zero-rating claims attract administrative penalties of up to 30% of the tax due, criminal fines ranging from EGP 10,000 to EGP 100,000, and potential imprisonment for tax evasion under Egyptian tax law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Since the November 2016 float and the March 2024 flexible-exchange-rate reform (backed by the IMF Extended Fund Facility), the Egyptian Pound (EGP) is a managed-float currency with full current-account convertibility. Dividend and profit remittances are treated as current-account payments and require no prior CBE approval provided the bank holds (i) audited financial statements, (ii) an annual tax-clearance certificate, (iii) proof of statutory reserve appropriations, and (iv) the shareholder distribution resolution. Capital-account operations (loan repayments, capital repatriation, liquidation proceeds) remain subject to CBE registration and underlying GAFI approval checks. Practical hurdles include periodic interbank FX liquidity tightness, the mandatory Arabic translation and consular legalization of Chinese corporate documents, UBO documentation under CBE Circulars, and bank-internal priority queues for outbound transfer requests.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egypt abolished mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratios in the 2024 liberalization; the current surrender ratio is 0%, and exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign-currency accounts at authorized banks. Statutory repatriation timeline: 100% of export proceeds must be credited to an Egyptian bank account within 90 days from the vessel/airway/land shipment date, with extension up to 180 days obtainable upon bank application to the CBE for substantiated delays. The exporter must present the customs export declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and financial settlement evidence to the bank within the compliance period.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 as the sole monetary, banking and foreign-exchange control authority; General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) administers inward-investment registration and capital verification; Egyptian Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) oversees non-bank financial activities. All resident banking and FX operations must be conducted through CBE-authorized banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Foreign digital EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer and similar) cannot lawfully open EGP accounts, issue local IBANs, or settle through Egyptian clearing for a resident LLC without CBE authorization under Law No. 194 of 2020 and the Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019. EMIs remain tactically useful only for collecting USD/EUR franchise fees or paying Chinese suppliers outside Egypt; all onshore treasury must be anchored to a licensed Egyptian bank. The central legal blocker is the CBE's exclusive licensing of payment services and the prohibition on non-bank access to the Egyptian Banking Settlement System (EBSS).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the total aggregate value of controlled transactions of the Egyptian LLC exceeds EGP 10 million per annum; Master File is required for any multinational group having consolidated revenues of at least EGP 2 billion. For transactions below EGP 10 million, a simplified contemporaneous TP self-assessment form is required.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply the GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the ETA may apply the general anti-abuse rule under Articles 14 and 32 of Law 91/2005 and the BEPS-aligned beneficial-ownership test to deny treaty benefits or re-characterize a shell LLC. A restaurant LLC with actual staff, local sourcing, a physical dining/seating area, and autonomous managerial decisions in Egypt will generally satisfy substance requirements. The Chinese franchise parent’s rights to IP are not sufficient to create Egyptian economic substance, and royalty deductions require that the LLC itself performs value-added functions with real employees and premises.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Reporting / Cash Suppression

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the evaded tax plus a penalty equal to 100%–200% of the evaded amount, a fine of EGP 20,000–100,000, and imprisonment for 3–7 years under Article 50 of Law 91/2005 and Article 75 of the Tax Procedure Law, in addition to withdrawal of the tax card and the commercial license.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the fraudulent return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. The ETA may obtain a court order freezing their personal assets, impose a travel ban on the legal representative, and, upon conviction, disqualify them from board membership and trigger extradition for repeat offenders.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The ETA recharacterizes the transactions and makes an upward income adjustment equal to the overpaid fees, plus an additional tax penalty of 20%–30% of the resulting incremental tax and an administrative fine of up to EGP 2,000,000 for missing documentation, while penalties for under-declaration may be duplicated when intent is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve inflated intercompany payments and fail to document them are personally liable for the tax shortfall and accrued surcharges; the tax administration can issue a debarment order preventing departure from Egypt until the debt is secured and, in fraud cases, the directors face criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The ETA may invoke Article 32 regarding the substance-over-form rule, disregard the legal arrangement, treat the Chinese parent as having a permanent establishment in Egypt, and tax the entire gross restaurant margin at 22.5%, plus late-payment surcharges and a fine of up to EGP 1,000,000 for filing a tax return that is grossly inconsistent with the actual activity.

Director personal liability: Where the shell structure exists for tax evasion, the corporate veil is pierced under the Egyptian Civil Code, making directors personally liable for all corporate tax arrears. The competent Court of First Instance may extend travel bans and seize personal real estate of the directors, and the tax evasion provision can result in imprisonment from 3 to 5 years.
AML / Counter-Terrorism Financing and Cash-Handling Lapses

Statutory sanction: Under Law No. 80 of 2002 (as amended by Law 36/2024), the Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Unit may impose fines ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 1,000,000 and imprisonment of 6 months to 3 years for non-reporting; the LLC’s bank accounts and free-floating assets may be administratively frozen for up to 60 days by order of the General Prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for AML compliance are personally exposed to criminal liability if they knowingly or negligently omit filing the Suspicious Transaction Report; in practice, banking activity is suspended and a travel ban is issued; conviction leads to disqualification from being a director of any Egyptian company for a period of up to 5 years.
