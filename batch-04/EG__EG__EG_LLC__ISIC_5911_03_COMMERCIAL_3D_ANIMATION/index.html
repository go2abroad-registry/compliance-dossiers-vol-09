Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Egyptian LLC law generally imposes no fixed statutory minimum capital for an ordinary LLC. The partners determine the capital in the incorporation contract. Capital quotas must be fully subscribed and paid as stated in the contract; GAFI may require evidence of payment or a bank certificate where applicable. Additional sector-specific or free-zone capital rules may apply if the company elects a special regime.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain premises fire-safety approval or Civil Protection clearance where required by the local authority, landlord, building use, or occupancy classification. Maintain compliant extinguishers, alarms, emergency exits, evacuation plans, electrical safety, and employee training. Structural load inspection is required where heavy servers, generators, storage, or specialized studio installations are introduced. Comply with Egyptian occupational health and safety requirements, including workplace risk assessments, safe electrical installations, emergency procedures, and employee health and safety records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance – unified electronic services portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; customs single window (NAFIZA): https://www.nafiza.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

For an Egyptian LLC with non-resident shareholders: (1) legalize the certificate of incorporation, memorandum of association and board resolution (apostille/consular legalization) and complete GAFI commercial registration; (2) within 30 days of commercial registration, apply to the competent ETA office for the unified Tax Identification Number / Tax Card under Law 30/2023 (Unified Tax Procedures Law); (3) register the same TIN for Corporate Income Tax (Law 91/2005), payroll tax and stamp tax; (4) VAT registration is mandatory once annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000; an export-oriented CGI/VFX entity should voluntarily register early to secure zero-rating and input VAT refund rights; (5) obtain an ETA-issued digital signature certificate and onboard to the e-invoicing sandbox. A local tax representative with power of attorney is required for non-resident principals. Estimated completion: 10–30 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt has no check-the-box or pass-through regime; an LLC is per se a resident corporation taxed at 22.5% on worldwide income. Available statutory elections: (i) non-calendar fiscal-year election at registration with ETA approval; (ii) voluntary VAT registration below the EGP 500,000 threshold; (iii) small business presumptive-tax election for annual turnover up to EGP 10 million under Law 152/2020, generally unattractive for exporters relying on VAT credits and zero-rating; (iv) branch PE attribution/separate-accounting elections with ETA transfer-pricing review; (v) foreign tax credit election for foreign income taxes. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under OECD-aligned amendments (Law 33/2023) are mandatory and non-elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable turnover (mandatory registration); voluntary registration permitted below the threshold; non-resident suppliers of electronic/digital services to Egyptian B2B recipients are subject to reverse-charge assessment even absent local registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies under Article 22 of Law 67/2016 to exported goods and to services supplied to a non-resident recipient for consideration where the services are performed in Egypt but delivered to and economically consumed abroad. Commercial CGI/3D animation/VFX services qualify when: (a) the contractual counterparty is a non-Egyptian client; (b) the digital output is delivered and exploited outside Egypt; and (c) the consideration is received through an authorized Egyptian bank. The zero-rated export invoice must be issued through the ETA e-invoicing system; attributable input VAT remains creditable and refundable.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document lodged via NAFIZA (mandatory for physical goods; not applicable to pure digital deliverables)
  • Signed export/service agreement with the non-resident buyer covering jurisdiction, delivery and exploitation terms
  • ETA-cleared zero-rated e-invoice bearing the ETA invoice ID and export code
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (goods) or digital delivery log / service acceptance certificate (CGI/VFX deliverables)
  • Foreign Currency Inward Remittance Certificate from an authorized Egyptian bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Mandatory ETA e-invoicing for all VAT-registered taxpayers, phased by turnover and now generally binding. Each B2B invoice must be transmitted in real time to the ETA platform in JSON/XML format, digitally signed with the ETA-issued certificate (ECDSA-SHA256 per ETA Data Model), validated and stamped with a unique ETA invoice ID; API integration and the ETA web portal are the permitted issuance channels. B2C sales require ETA e-receipts with QR digital stamping. Invoices not cleared by ETA are null for input VAT deduction; late or non-submission triggers per-invoice fines and suspension of VAT refunds.

ETA audit window is 5 years generally, 6 years for concealed or incorrect returns. Failed zero-rating substantiation reclassifies the supply as domestic taxable at 14%, with a deficiency assessment, monthly delay charge (2% per month under Law 67/2016, harmonized by Law 30/2023), denial of input VAT credits and clawback of any VAT refund/exemption. Fraudulent claims – fictitious exports, forged remittance certificates, circular FX arrangements – attract criminal penalties: imprisonment up to 5 years, fines of EGP 10,000 to EGP 1,000,000 (Art. 73 Law 67/2016), possible doubling of the tax, publication of the conviction, cancellation of the tax card and debarment from export incentive programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current account convertibility is broadly permitted for trade in services, including commercial CGI, VFX, and 3D animation exports, provided the transaction passes through a CBE-authorized bank with complete underlying contracts, invoices, tax card, and CBE Balance-of-Payments reporting codes. The Egyptian pound is not freely convertible offshore, and no unrestricted non-resident EGP market exists outside Egypt. Capital account inflows, such as non-resident equity subscriptions, require a capital account and GAFI-compliant documentation. Profit and dividend remittance is legally guaranteed under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, but operational hurdles include audited financial statements proving distributable reserves, Egyptian Tax Authority tax clearance, CBE BoP classification, and bank-level USD/EGP liquidity availability. There is no published quantitative profit-remittance ceiling, but the treasury must anticipate administrative FX rationing during periods of reserve pressure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

CBE regulations require that service export proceeds, including animation/VFX production receivables, be repatriated through an authorized Egyptian bank within six months from the completion certificate or invoice due date, whichever is later, unless a CBE extension is obtained. The exporter must submit an export-monitoring declaration, underlying service contract, and delivery evidence. The mandatory conversion ratio for non-oil service export proceeds is effectively 100%: the proceeds must either be sold to the bank for EGP at the prevailing interbank rate or be credited to an Egyptian foreign-currency operating account under CBE reporting if the authorized bank accepts such treatment. This policy ratio is subject to CBE directive changes; monthly monitoring of CBE notices is required.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Banking and Central Bank System), assisted by the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) for investment registration, capital injection approval, and profit remittance verification; AML/CFT supervision is exercised by the Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit under Law No. 80 of 2002.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore EMIs such as Wise Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex are not licensed by the CBE to provide Egyptian account settlement, foreign exchange conversion, or Balance-of-Payments reporting. Their viability as the primary treasury rail is LOW. They may be used only as ancillary collection or payout channels if the underlying funds are transmitted onward through a CBE-authorized Bank into Egypt via SWIFT. Regulatory constraints include the inability to complete mandatory export-proceeds surrender, statutory tax clearance, e-invoice matching, and GAFI capital monitoring solely on an EMI platform.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All non-resident share capital must be wired directly to the LLC's capital subscription account at a CBE-authorized bank using SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for interbank transactions). The MT103 field 70 must clearly state the purpose narration: 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION [LLC_NAME] [COMMERCIAL_REGISTER_NUMBER] [UBO_NAME]'. The receiving bank maps the transaction to the CBE Balance-of-Payments code for direct investment equity and issues the inward remittance certificate. The LLC must hold the funds intact until GAFI registration and commercial-register updates are completed. Any use of unlicensed crypto, cash brokers, or third-party non-bank exchangers is prohibited and will trigger AML rejection.International payments for subcontracting, software royalties, rendering farm leases, licensing fees, and dividends should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV wires at a CBE-licensed bank. For physical equipment procurement, such as high-performance GPU hardware and storage infrastructure, the standard clearing rail is a commercial documentary letter of credit at sight, supported by proforma invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, and any required CBE/import registration. Egyptian pound legs for domestic operating expenses should be settled through the CBE RTGS/ACH system after authorized-bank conversion from USD. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Egyptian Transfer Pricing Guidelines under Law No. 91 of 2005 and the Executive Regulations require the Local File and Master File where controlled transactions with related parties exceed EGP 10,000,000 per fiscal year, or where the value of loans from related parties exceeds EGP 10,000,000. The CbC reporting threshold applies where the consolidated group revenue exceeds EGP 13,300,000,000. A simplified TP disclosure form is annexed to the tax return when the aggregate controlled transaction threshold exceeds EGP 1,000,000. For the standalone commercial 3D animation LLC, mandatory transfer pricing documentation is triggered if the studio transacts with a related party in excess of the local threshold, including using a foreign parent's software, rendering farm services, or establishing the studio to serve as a low-tax profit booking vehicle.

Economic substance scope: Egypt adopts substance-over-form principles requiring the audited financial statements and tax affairs to reflect actual contractual arrangements and business activity. The Egyptian Tax Authority applies economic substance principles pursuant to OECD BEPS Action 5 and the Inclusive Framework recommendations; a hollow entity with no genuine staff, premises, board decisions or management functions in Egypt faces recharacterization of its income as taxable in the shareholder's jurisdiction and possible denial of treaty benefits. Domestic law also includes beneficial ownership and anti-avoidance rules in the Income Tax Law and the Unified Taxation Law, requiring the animation studio to have substantive operational activity in Egypt.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for a term not exceeding five years and a fine equal to twice to five times the amount of tax evaded, at the discretion of the court under Law No. 91 of 2005 and the Unified Taxation Law. Tax assessments may be made with retrospective effect for up to six fiscal years, with interest and late-payment surcharges, and the tax authority may freeze asset accounts and impose payment guarantees upon referral to the economic court.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general manager who sign or knowingly approve false tax returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, surcharges and fines. Criminal liability extends to personal imprisonment for up to five years and travel bans may be issued during prosecution. Confiscation of assets and disqualification from serving as board members in Egypt may also result.
Shell Company / Artificial Deficit

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the scheme as void for tax purposes, disallowance of all intra-group charges and synthetic deductions, taxation at the full 22.5% rate on the undistributed revenues, and withholding tax at 20% on all outbound payments and fees. Additional penalties of up to 100% of the disallowed amounts may be applied, and the tax registration may be cancelled. Court rulings may invalidate intercompany contracts as sham transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who are in active management while the LLC is artificially deprived of profits are exposed to civil restitution, joint and several liability for the full tax, criminal fraud charges, and personal travel bans. Where the offshore related party is the sole shareholder, the corporate veil may be pierced and the foreign parent's assets in Egypt may be attached to settle the assessment.
Anti-Money Laundering Violation

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment from 3 to 7 years and a fine of EGP 1,000,000 to EGP 5,000,000 per occurrence for money laundering, with an additional fine of 50% of the laundered funds. The studio and its bank accounts can be forfeited to the state and all licences revoked. The Central Bank of Egypt may also impose administrative penalties reaching EGP 10,000,000 and revoke the institution's payment service provider status.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, compliance officers and any shadow manager are criminally prosecuted regardless of corporate shield protections; imprisonment terms are personal and can be compounded by confiscation of personal bank accounts, real estate and vehicles, as well as a statutory travel ban until the resolution of the criminal case.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Imposition of a 25% assessment adjustment penalty on the incremental taxable profit, plus an additional 50% of the adjustment where the taxpayer failed to present any documentation within the statutory time limit. The Egyptian Tax Authority may impose a penalty of EGP 100,000 where the TP disclosure form is not submitted, and the tax assessment is reopened for a full audit of transactions for up to six years.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify incorrect intercompany prices and valuations may be liable to compensate the LLC for the full amount of the tax adjustment and penalties, and civil claims are enforceable against their personal estates. In flagrant cases, criminal fraud sanctions may be applied together with a travel ban pursuant to the tax collection law.
Improper Profit Repatriation Structuring

Statutory sanction: The disguised amounts are recharacterized as taxable profits of the LLC; a 20% final withholding tax is applied to any recharacterized dividend or royalty, together with a monthly late-payment surcharge. Criminal sanctions for tax evasion apply where the recharacterization involves knowingly false documents, leading to fines of up to five times the tax evaded and confiscation of the funds transferred.

Director personal liability: The directors, financial controller and foreign ultimate beneficial owner can be jointly and severally liable for the full tax and penalties. Enforcement may extend to non-resident shareholders through procedures under the investment protection treaty network and judicial assistance arrangements, including seizure of assets held by the shareholder in other jurisdictions.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Failure to withhold and pay results in the tax authority recovering the unpaid withholding tax directly from the Egyptian LLC, and additionally imposing a penalty of up to 100% of the withheld amount under the Unified Tax Procedures Law. The payment can be denied as an expense deduction, increasing the overall corporate income tax base, and criminal tax evasion proceedings can be initiated against the responsible manager.

Director personal liability: Directors who are personally involved in authorizing non-compliant wire transfers to foreign vendors assume statutory personal liability for the unpaid withholding tax, penalties and interest. The Egyptian Tax Authority may impose travel bans, asset freezes and enforcement liens to extract payment, and the director may face imprisonment for willful failure to deduct tax at source.
