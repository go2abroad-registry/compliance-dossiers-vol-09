Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), Corporate Registration Department, One-Stop Shop, Cairo, in coordination with the Commercial Registry and the Egyptian Tax Authority co-located at the GAFI integrated services window.

Governing statute

Foundation of Egyptian joint persons and LLCs is primarily governed by Companies Law No. 159 of 1981 (the law applicable to limited liability companies) and Investment Law No. 72 of 2017, which introduced the simplified Founding Declaration route before GAFI; relevant ancillary provisions are drawn from Commercial Registry Law No. 34 of 1976, Capital Market Law No. 95 of 1992 and its subsequent amendments, Law No. 10 of 2009 creating the Financial Regulatory Authority, and Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 80 of 2002. Note: FRA licensing practice restricts retail dealing licenses in securities to Egyptian joint-stock companies; if the formation vehicle remains an LLC, the organizers should convert the entity to a JSC under Law No. 159 of 1981 or hold the license through a licensed JSC subsidiary.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 Founding Declaration (Iqrar Al-Ta'sis) filed through GAFI together with the notarized Articles of Association (Aqd Al-Sharika); GAFI issues the Certificate of Incorporation and the unified commercial registration extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 102 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for ordinary LLC incorporation; nevertheless, founding capital must be subscribed and evidenced by a bank escrow certificate at a licensed Egyptian bank. For ISIC 6611 retail securities brokerage, the FRA license requires minimum fully paid-up capital of EGP 10,000,000 (approximately USD 210,000) held in escrow or as a security deposit, with ongoing net-capital and client-asset segregation requirements under Capital Market Law regulations.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Directorate fire safety certificate and periodic inspection under Law No. 112 of 1996; structural load inspection certificate under Building Law No. 119 of 2008 covering the trading floor, server vault, and equipment weights; occupancy clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 and Executive Regulation Decree No. 173 of 2003 including evacuation plans, fire suppression equipment, first aid, and server-room fire control measures.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA) – Ministry of Finance; unified digital portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing and e-receipts clearance portal: https://einvoice.invoicing.eta.gov.eg; SCZONE one-stop-shop for licensed zone projects.

E-government filing portal

For EG_LLC (foreign-owned Egyptian Limited Liability Company engaged in regulated securities brokerage): (1) incorporate before the Commercial Registry and obtain the Commercial Registration Certificate plus the FRA (Financial Regulatory Authority) brokerage licence; (2) submit to the competent ETA taxpayer service office the registry certificate, notarised memorandum of association, board resolution appointing the resident legal representative, certified power of attorney, registered-office lease, and beneficial-owner identification to obtain the unified Tax Card (TIN) under Law 91/2005; (3) file the VAT registration declaration with the ETA within 30 days of exceeding the EGP 500,000 threshold, appointing a local VAT representative; (4) register the company ERP and authorised signatory digital certificates on the ETA e-invoicing platform; the ETA issues a single electronic TIN linked to the tax card, VAT account, e-invoicing profile and customs exporter code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election is recognised; an Egyptian LLC is statutorily an entity-level taxpayer at the 22.5% corporate income tax rate, with branch/withholding and transfer-pricing rules applied separately. Available elections include: (i) consolidated group taxation for related Egyptian companies (restricted approval by the ETA/Minister of Finance); (ii) free-zone project exemption election under Investment Law 83/2002 and SCZONE regime where the licensed activity is physically performed in an approved zone; (iii) export-income incentive deduction elections; (iv) simplified transfer-pricing documentation election for controlled transactions below the statutory threshold. No hybrid-mismatch check-the-box elections exist; Law 177/2020 anti-abuse provisions disallow interest/dividend deductibility in arrangements exhibiting hybrid mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 in annual taxable supplies (mandatory registration for resident persons); voluntary registration is permitted once annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 100,000; for non-resident suppliers without a local establishment, registration is due on the first taxable supply unless an Egyptian reverse-charge-bearer performs the accounting role.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rating: physical export and customs exit of goods from Egypt on the customs declaration. Services zero-rating: services rendered by EG_LLC to non-resident counterparties for activities performed and consumed wholly outside Egypt — offshore securities brokerage, agency and custody services to foreign clients qualify where the contract is signed with a non-resident, the execution/beneficial use occurs outside Egypt, and the consideration is received in convertible foreign currency via the Egyptian banking system. The zero rate applies to the commission/fee only and does not extend to local arranging, data or corporate services supplied to Egyptian residents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) with ETA/approved customs gateway validation, evidencing goods exit (only for any physical instrument or tangible component of the service package)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (carrier's receipt) proving actual export out of Egypt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by an Egyptian licensed bank evidencing full credit of the export/commission proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Signed brokerage service contract and non-residency affidavit of the offshore client (with country-of-residence evidence to support consumption outside Egypt)
  • VAT-compliant e-invoice with ETA clearance reference and the detailed commission/trade-ticket allocation

Mandatory e-invoicing for all registered and ETA-designated taxpayers (B2B/B2G), phased since 2020, and mandatory e-receipts for B2C retail flows: every transaction must be transmitted to the ETA system in structured JSON/XML, signed using the taxpayer's certified digital certificate (HSM), cleared through the ETA invoice clearance house (near-real-time validation with automatic rejection of non-compliant items), and stamped with a validated QR code before the invoice is delivered to the client; integration is via REST API certified by the ETA. An invoice lacking the ETA clearance stamp is legally ineffective for input-tax deduction and triggers automated penalties; brokerage fee credit notes and cross-border service invoices must likewise pass through the same clearance pipeline.

The ETA triangulates e-invoice declarations, customs export data, banking remittance certificates and the client's non-residency documentation. Where zero-rating is claimed without proof of actual export/offshore consumption or of timely inward remittance, the ETA claws back the VAT at the 14% standard rate (or the applicable special rate for goods) on the entire invoice value, adds delay tax of 2% per month on the unpaid tax, and imposes statutory evasion penalties ranging from 100% to 200% of the evaded tax; wilful fraudulent documentation exposes the legal representative, tax representative, signatories and — where chain fraud is proven — the receiving bank to joint and several liability, cancellation of the tax card, blacklisting from e-invoicing, criminal fines and imprisonment under Law 67/2016 (as amended) and the Unified Tax Procedures provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound (EGP) operates under a liberalized managed float regime. Current account payments, including dividend distributions and service fees, are generally remittable through CBE-authorized banks upon presentation of documentary evidence comprising audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates and, for regulated financial entities, an FRA no-objection. Capital account transactions, including shareholder equity injections and offshore loan repayments, require bank-level verification against the CBE External Transactions Classification and may attract enhanced due diligence; approval friction is moderate for a licensed broker. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore administrative rather than structural, contingent on proof of tax settlement, compliance with CBE anti-money-laundering checks and FRA notification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Egyptian exporters must repatriate export proceeds through an Egyptian bank within 90 days of shipment, extendable to 270 days with CBE approval, and may retain the full proceeds in a foreign currency account or convert them at the interbank rate; mandatory surrender ratios were abolished in 2024 and no forced conversion percentage is currently imposed, although the CBE reserves the right to re-impose calibrated surrender requirements for balance-of-payments stability. For this non-exporting securities broker the regime applies only indirectly: all cross-border receipts, including client subscription funds and shareholder capital, must be routed through CBE-licensed banks and recorded under the applicable external transactions code.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) in conjunction with the Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) for capital market intermediation activities

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Regulatory viability of offshore digital EMIs is Low. Egypt does not recognize foreign e-money institution authorizations; only CBE-licensed payment service providers and FRA-licensed fintech entities may hold client moneys for brokerage flows. Offshore EMIs such as Wise or Payoneer may be used strictly as ancillary collection or disbursement channels routed onward to a licensed Egyptian bank, never as the primary segregated client money custodian. Domestic payment acceptance is required through licensed processors including Fawry, EFin and Masary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting corporate bank to the company's EGP or USD account at a CBE-licensed bank, referencing the CBE Economic Classification of External Transactions code for equity participation in a limited liability company together with the shareholding percentage; client money references are strictly prohibited. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EGP 5,000,000 annual aggregate related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File / Master File preparation; Country-by-Country Report filing is required where consolidated group revenue exceeds EGP 8,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Egypt applies general anti-avoidance and treaty-access substantive tests rather than a standalone ESR regime. A securities-brokerage LLC must maintain a licensed office, employed investment advisers, real business records, boards, and key management in Egypt; shell vehicles without economic activities will be denied treaty benefits and can have tax registration revoked. The Egyptian Financial Regulatory Authority independently requires a physical presence, compliance officers, and capital adequacy so as to avoid shell-company abuse.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for 2–5 years and a fine of up to EGP 100,000, in addition to an additional tax of 100% of the evaded tax and a monthly 3% late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors and founding partners who sign false returns can be jointly liable for the tax debt and may face travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment under Law 91/2005.
Transfer pricing / GAAR abuse

Statutory sanction: An upward transfer-pricing adjustment by the ETA, a late-payment surcharge, an administrative fine of up to 30% of the adjustment, and potential criminal penalties for presenting fraudulent transfer-pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Board members who directed artificial profit shifting face personal assessment, freezing orders on overseas assets, and piercing of the corporate veil where funds are extracted as undisclosed distributions.
Shell company / money laundering in securities brokerage

Statutory sanction: Under Egyptian Anti-Money Laundering Law No. 8/2002 and the Financial Regulatory Authority licensing regime: revocation of the securities brokerage licence, confiscation of proceeds, fines up to EGP 1,000,000, and imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to personal punishment, including travel bans and restitution to defrauded clients; the corporate veil will be pierced where shell vehicles are used for fraudulent transaction routing.
Withholding tax and dividend repatriation failure

Statutory sanction: The ETA will collect the unwithheld tax from the Egyptian LLC together with a monthly surcharge of 3% and an additional penalty of 30%–100% of the unremitted amount.

Director personal liability: Officers who approve distributions without withholding are personally liable for the cumulative tax, plus possible criminal punishment for intentional non-compliance; travel restrictions can be imposed pending settlement.
