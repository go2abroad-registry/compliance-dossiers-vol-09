Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for the Egyptian Limited Liability Company. Cash contributions must be paid into a licensed Egyptian bank account held in the company's name and blocked pending issuance of the incorporation certificate. In-kind contributions must be valued by a sworn accountant and described in the Articles of Association.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification must be obtained from the General Administration for Civil Defense for the registered office and any auxiliary warehouse, including inspection of fire-extinguishing apparatus, emergency exits, and electrical load. Structural load clearance is verified through the building owner's civil-defense building card and the office tenancy contract. Occupational health and safety clearance for the workplace is issued by the Ministry of Manpower, after acknowledgment of the employer's OSH duties under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for the Egyptian Limited Liability Company. Cash contributions must be paid into a licensed Egyptian bank account held in the company's name and blocked pending issuance of the incorporation certificate. In-kind contributions must be valued by a sworn accountant and described in the Articles of Association.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Cairo; national portal: www.eta.gov.eg; e-filing portal: etax.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing clearance platform: invoicing.eta.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors obtain a unified statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Steps: (1) Incorporate the LLC in the Commercial Register and obtain the ACID/company file; (2) File Tax Registration Form (Form No. 1) with the competent ETA district office via etax.eta.gov.eg or in person, attaching certified Arabic translations of the commercial registration extract, articles of association, board resolution and power of attorney appointing the resident manager, lease contract, bank certificate, and directors' ID copies; (3) ETA issues the unified TIN within 5-15 working days, covering CIT, VAT, and payroll simultaneously. Non-residents without an Egyptian establishment must appoint a resident tax representative/fiscal agent; VAT registration is embedded in the same TIN application and becomes obligatory when taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. An Egyptian LLC is a resident juridical person mandatorily taxed at entity level on Egyptian-source business profits and on foreign permanent establishment profits (with a foreign tax credit) per Income Tax Law No. 30/2023. No pass-through classification is available; non-resident platform merchants are taxed solely on Egyptian-source income via withholding and have no flow-through access to the LLC's VAT position.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections exist under Tax Procedures Law 206/2020 or Income Tax Law 30/2023; entity classification follows the Commercial Register and is not elective. Permitted options: (i) group tax consolidation for closely held Egyptian groups with prior ETA approval; (ii) export-profit incentive granting a 25% reduction of CIT due on documented export proceeds (Law 30/2023, Executive Regulations 418/2023); (iii) deemed-profit simplified taxation for small taxpayers with annual turnover not exceeding EGP 20 million; (iv) bilateral advance pricing agreements for cross-border marketplace commissions and intercompany services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 annual taxable supplies of goods/services made by a person with an Egyptian establishment (mandatory). Exporters may voluntarily register at EGP 150,000 annual turnover. E-invoicing obligations attach to every VAT-registered taxpayer regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 16 of VAT Law 67/2016, zero rating applies to (i) goods physically exported from Egypt within 90 days of invoice date, evidenced by the customs export declaration; and (ii) services supplied to a non-resident beneficiary with no Egyptian establishment where the benefit is wholly consumed offshore, including B2B marketplace facilitation and cross-border platform commission income. Conditions: valid VAT registration at supply date, direct foreign-currency remittance of export proceeds, retention of full supporting documentation for five years, and verification of the offshore counterparty's non-resident status via tax residence certificate.

  • Customs export declaration (NAFEZA single-window submission with ACID number and endorsed exit summary)
  • Bill of lading / airway bill / combined transport document evidencing physical departure from Egyptian customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate in CBE Form 20/40 (or SWIFT interbank confirmation) showing foreign-currency credit of export proceeds
  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice, commercial invoice and sales contract with incoterms and place-of-supply details
  • Tax residence certificate of the offshore B2B buyer or platform merchant and service description demonstrating offshore consumption

Mandatory e-invoicing (B2B) and e-receipts (B2C) integrated with ETA's fiscal clearance system. Taxpayers must connect approved ERP/billing software to ETA APIs (XML schema, digital signatures, HMAC), transmit each invoice in real time for clearance before issuance, and receive a unique UUID, serial number, and QR-code digital stamp. Invoices not cleared are legally invalid, input VAT is disallowed, and non-integration penalties reach EGP 50,000 per violation with suspension of VAT deduction rights until compliance.

ETA reviews zero-rating claims under the general 5-year assessment window (6 years for fraud). Failure to export, incomplete documentation, or non-repatriation within the 180-day CBE deadline converts the supply to standard-rated: 14% VAT is recovered on the gross invoice retroactively plus monthly delayed-payment interest capped at 100% of the tax due. Deliberate fraud (forged customs declarations, circular remittances, shell buyers) triggers the greater of EGP 50,000 or 200% of evaded VAT as penalty, imprisonment up to 5 years, suspension of VAT certificate and customs privileges, publication of the taxpayer's name, and full clawback of all input VAT refunds previously granted on such exports.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Following the March 2024 exchange-rate unification anchored by the IMF Extended Fund Facility, the Egyptian pound (EGP) is market-determined and current-account transactions, including imports and dividend/profit remittances of a non-resident-owned LLC, are de facto convertible through CBE-licensed banks. Capital-account transactions remain controlled and require a documented underlying transaction, GAFI registration for foreign direct investment, a valid CBE FX purpose code, and execution through a licensed domestic bank. Residual hurdles are documentary, not availability-based: source-of-funds evidence, tax settlement of withholding obligations, and bank-level AML clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the CBE export-settlement circular framework, export proceeds must be repatriated through a CBE-licensed bank within 30 days of shipment for general non-essential goods and within 60 days for essential-goods schedules. In the post-float regime there is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio: export receipts may be held in a foreign-currency account with a licensed bank, and any conversion is executed at the prevailing market rate. Each receipt must be matched against the export customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and CBE statistical purpose code; persistent failure to repatriate triggers a regulatory query and potential administrative enforcement.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), the sole monetary, banking, and foreign-exchange regulator acting under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law) and the Foreign Exchange Administration framework delegated to its Banking Supervision and Foreign Operations Directorates.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. A resident Egyptian LLC may not lawfully use an unlicensed offshore EMI as its primary treasury spine: CBE Law No. 194 of 2020 and foreign-exchange regulations require all export proceeds and inward FDI capital to be settled through a bank licensed in Egypt. An offshore digital EMI is viable only as an ancillary disbursement wallet for low-value supplier payments, and the underlying aggregator agreements must be disclosed to the appointed Egyptian bank to avoid FX-rule violations. Alibaba Pay may be used only as a merchant collection aggregator feeding a reconciled settlement to the licensed Egyptian bank; it cannot replace the statutory clearing channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders execute a SWIFT MT103 wire from the remitting bank to the LLC's capital account at a CBE-licensed Egyptian bank, with narrative 'FDI Equity Contribution' and the applicable CBE statistical purpose code for direct investment in Egypt. The receiving bank performs KYC/AML verification, checks the CBE FX transaction-reason register, credits the foreign-currency capital account, and files the inflow as inward direct investment. The LLC then registers the injection with GAFI and the tax administration within the statutory filing window, attaching the bank receipt, the board resolution, and the share-allotment ledger.Primary clearing rails: (1) Telegraphic-transfer bank payment via SWIFT MT103 against supplier pro-forma or commercial invoices; (2) documentary commercial letters of credit opened through the Egyptian issuing bank using SWIFT MT700/MT707 and settled via MT202 bank-to-bank transmissions; (3) domestic EGP legs executed through the CBE RTGS and Instant Payment Network rails; (4) all FX conversion processed by the licensed dealing desk on the basis of the underlying import or trade-document package.Onshore deliverable outright FX forwards, typically up to 12 months, entered through a CBE-licensed bank against documented trade receivables or payablesFX swaps between EGP and USD/EUR/GBP to hedge flow mismatches between settlement cyclesCross-currency swaps matched to foreign-currency loans or intercompany debt with documented underlying exposureOffshore nondeliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for a resident Egyptian corporate without a CBE-licensed intermediary; onshore entities should rely exclusively on licensed bank hedging contracts
