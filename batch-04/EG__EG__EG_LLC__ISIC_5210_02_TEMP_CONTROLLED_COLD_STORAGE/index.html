Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI) - Companies Registration Department and Investor One-Stop Shop, Ministry of Investment and Foreign Trade; supporting notarization by the Egyptian Notary Public Authority (Al-Shahr Al-Akari).

Governing statute

Companies Law No. 4 of 2023 (Law Promulgating the Companies Law), effective 1 January 2024, Articles 1-4, 57-76 on Limited Liability Companies, read with its Executive Regulations; Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and its Executive Regulations; Commercial Registration Law No. 34 of 1976; Civil Code No. 131 of 1948; and the Egyptians Tax Authority unified tax identification procedures.

Formation / constitutional filings

Company Establishment Contract and Memorandum of Articles of Association (Aqd El-Ta'assiss wal-Nizam El-Asasi), executed before the notary public and filed electronically through the GAFI investor portal, culminating in the Certificate of Incorporation and Commercial Registration Certificate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 3 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to LLCs under Law No. 4 of 2023; the founders freely fix the capital in the memorandum. The capital must be fully subscribed, and at least 25% of the cash portion must be paid up before incorporation and deposited into a temporary escrow account ('under-formation account') opened in the company's name at a licensed Egyptian bank. The bank deposit certificate is annexed to the GAFI filing, and the escrow funds are released only upon presentation of the incorporation certificate. In-kind contributions must be fully paid at incorporation and assessed by a licensed expert or the statutory valuation procedure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance; official taxpayer portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing, e-receipt, and digital fiscal clearance systems are operated by ETA under the national Tax Digitization Program; physical export clearance is administered by the Egypt Customs Authority (ECA), Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Egyptian LLC: (1) obtain commercial registration through the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and the Egyptian Commercial Register; (2) file a unified tax registration application at the competent ETA tax office using the statutory TIN form, attaching the articles of association, commercial register extract, and executed powers of the local manager(s); (3) receive a single unified Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Tax Card covering Income Tax, VAT, and stamp duties; (4) open a corporate bank account in Egypt and complete e-invoicing/e-receipt enrollment; (5) for export activities, obtain an exporter code at the ECA and reference the ETA TIN in all customs submissions. Non-resident entities without an Egyptian permanent establishment are not issued a local TIN but remain taxable on Egyptian-source income and are subject to VAT at importation through the customs system. Registration by an operating LLC must be completed within 30 days of commencing licensed activity and updated upon any change of address, activity, or management.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification election exists under Egyptian law; an Egyptian LLC is automatically taxed as a separate resident company under Income Tax Law No. 91 of 2005. Available limited elections: (i) the simplified/presumptive small and medium enterprise tax regime under Law No. 152 of 2020 for qualifying small and micro projects, subject to ETA eligibility and the revenue ceilings set out in the Executive Regulations; (ii) consolidated tax-group filing under Article 56 of Law No. 91 of 2005 where an Egyptian parent directly holds at least 90 percent of an Egyptian subsidiary's shares and both entities share the same fiscal year, subject to prior ETA approval; and (iii) simplified cash accounting for micro taxpayers where expressly permitted under Law No. 152 of 2020. These elections do not create pass-through or disregarded-entity treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 of annual taxable supplies mandates VAT registration; EGP 200,000 of annual turnover permits voluntary registration; registration may also be required for importers and businesses using imported goods/services even where the domestic supply threshold is not exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate is available for: (a) exported goods physically leaving Egypt to a foreign destination without substantial local transformation, evidenced by the customs export declaration; (b) services directly connected with export handling, cold storage, transport, and freight of the same goods where the benefit is received by a non-resident; and (c) other service exports under the place-of-supply rules in the VAT Executive Regulations, where the recipient is established outside Egypt and economic use occurs abroad. Eligibility requires invoicing in foreign currency, actual receipt of the foreign-currency consideration, and cross-matching with the customs declaration, the e-invoice, and the inward remittance record. Cold-storage operators must trace each zero-rated consignment to its export declaration and Cargo Tracking Number; intercompany exports to a foreign affiliate are accepted only at arm's length with full documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) electronically cleared and stamped by the Egypt Customs Authority (ECA)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill or multimodal transport document evidencing removal of goods to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or foreign-currency bank receipt issued by a Central Bank of Egypt-licensed bank evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds
  • ETA-cleared e-invoice bearing the Unique Invoice Identifier and QR code linked to the corresponding export declaration and customs manifest
  • Certificate of Origin and destination-country health or phytosanitary permits for perishable pharmaceutical, food, and temperature-controlled products, as applicable
  • Cold-chain temperature log and warehouse storage records demonstrating the goods were held and dispatched in the declared refrigerated condition

All registered taxpayers must issue electronic invoices through ETA-approved channels (webform, REST API, or ERP connector) in the ETA XML-SIG schema; each invoice is transmitted in near-real time to the ETA and passed through centralized validation, with the ETA returning a Unique Invoice Identifier, cryptographic hash, and QR code only after clearance. Only cleared e-invoices are admissible for input-VAT deduction. Business-to-consumer sales are additionally governed by the e-receipt (Iqbal) system requiring per-sale fiscal-code clearance. Cold-storage and logistics operators must integrate warehouse billing systems with ETA APIs, apply sequential fiscal numbering, and retain ETA clearance codes for the statutory five-year record-keeping period.

ETA's Post-Clearance Audit and Automated Risk Model units, together with ECA and the Central Bank of Egypt, cross-match export declarations, cleared e-invoices, freight records, and foreign-exchange receipts. If goods do not physically leave Egypt, or export proceeds are not received and repatriated within the statutory 180-day window, the 0% status is clawed back and VAT at 14% becomes immediately due together with the monthly late-payment charge and administrative fines for non-compliant e-invoicing. Fraudulent export documentation or misdeclaration triggers a punitive addition generally tied to the tax evaded, up to twice the undercharged amount, and criminal liability for responsible officers under Law No. 67 of 2016 and Customs Law No. 207 of 2020, including possible imprisonment, revocation of the exporter code, and joint and several liability of directors and shareholders for duties, taxes, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt operates a liberalized, bank-anchored foreign exchange regime in which the Egyptian Pound (EGP) is freely convertible for current account transactions and for approved capital account operations. Capital controls are limited to prior registration of foreign investment through GAFI, strict source-of-funds verification by domestic licensed banks, and documentary embedding of each cross-border flow in the CBE reporting grid. Profit remittances are processed only after distraint of evidenced tax clearance, audited financial statements, and the submitting bank's confirmation of the relevant purpose code; non-resident capital return requires liquidation form submission to the CBE through the customer's Egyptian bank. Since the 2024 unification of the official and market exchange rates, there are no statutory surrender or involuntary conversion ratios, but every hard-currency receipt must flow through the regulated banking channel and be reported on the bank's prudential return.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under CBE directives applicable to the target company, 100% of export proceeds from cold storage services or re-exported pharmaceutical goods must be repatriated into the Egyptian banking system within 6 months from the date of shipment or service performance. The exporter retains full discretion as to whether such proceeds are kept in an Egyptian foreign-currency account or converted into EGP at the prevailing interbank rate; there is no mandatory conversion ratio. The 6-month repatriation obligation is monitored by the collection bank, and failure to repatriate may subject the exporter to CBE penalties and to denial of access to official import-financing facilities. Goods exports additionally require customs-compliant invoice matching under the National Single Window, while service export commissions are admitted on the basis of the underlying service contract and the Ministry of Finance's online services registry.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE), acting in coordination with the Egyptian Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA), the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), and the Ministry of Finance, pursuant to Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank, Banking and Foreign Exchange Law).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital banks and electronic money institutions (EMIs) cannot independently open Egyptian foreign-currency accounts without a CBE license, and Law No. 194 of 2020 restricts the execution of foreign exchange transactions to CBE-licensed banks and authorized payment service providers. Non-bank EMIs domiciled in the EU or MENA hubs may operate only through a local correspondent bank relationship, which largely eliminates their treasury efficiency advantage for this cold-storage project. A CBE-licensed Egyptian fintech such as a digital payment platform may provide collection and disbursement rails, but cross-border clearing and FX conversion must still be settled through a tier-1 banking counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital, shareholder advances, and long-term project funding must be wired exclusively through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages into the LLC's EGP-designated or foreign-currency account at a licensed Egyptian bank. Each transfer requires a precise CBE purpose code (asset subscription or FDI capital contribution code), and the receiving bank must file the CBE FDI declaration with the repo of the transaction; the wire is credited only after the bank verifies the underlying GAFI certificate and the match between the remitter's name, the share ledger, and the board resolution. For the target cold-storage facility, capital injections should be structured as registered equity rather than loans to avoid circularity penalties under the CBE's external debt reporting regime. Each receivable is routed through the interbank EGP counterparty to be cleared at the official exchange rate, and the resulting EGP balances are fungible across the operational working capital account.Trade settlements utilize SWIFT MT103 for direct service fees, MT700 documentary letters of credit for imports of refrigeration equipment or pharmaceutical inventory, and the Egyptian Central Securitization and Settlement Co. (EGCS) clearing for EGP-denominated payments. Local payments are settled via the Egyptian Payment Network (EGN) and the CBE Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system, while cross-border supplier payments require invoice matching, customs declarations, and the submitting bank's percentage check against the LLC's approved export generation.
