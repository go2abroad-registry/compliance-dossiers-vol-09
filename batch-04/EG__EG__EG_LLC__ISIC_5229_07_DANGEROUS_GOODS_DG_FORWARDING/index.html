Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Egyptian pound is subject to CBE-supervised managed floating. Current-account payments, including trade settlements and freight charges, are generally processed if the underlying commercial documentation is complete and the transaction is routed via an authorized bank. Capital-account inflows such as foreign direct investment or shareholder loans must be registered with the Central Bank of Egypt (sometimes via a qualified bank) to secure future outbound remittance rights. Dividend and profit remittances require a tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, and evidence of lawful receipt of foreign exchange; liquidity shortages and bank-level compliance screening may delay execution, but no express prior CBE approval is required for routine profit remittances if funds are held in a foreign-currency account and all taxes are paid.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from services and goods (including logistics and forwarding services) must be received into a CBE-authorized bank in Egypt. The standard CBE directive requires the foreign currency to be credited to the exporter's account within 30 days of the shipment/export date; there is no fixed statutory percentage mandatory conversion to Egyptian pounds under the current managed-float regime, and exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency. However, the CBE may issue temporary circulars imposing conversion or repatriation obligations if the local foreign-exchange position depletes; the account-holding bank has the right to demand supporting bills of lading, postal/airway bills, customs declarations, and the DG forwarding licence to release funds.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore EMIs are not regulated by the CBE and cannot be used as primary corporate bank accounts for Egyptian LLCs; their viability is Low because the CBE's licensing regime and payment infrastructure are not aligned with offshore EMI license models. Some licensed Egyptian fintech payment service providers may offer API-driven treasury views or faster local settlement channels, but they are not a substitute for a resident bank relationship. For a DG forwarding company, the CBE and AML authorities expect a fully regulated banking channel and an identifiable clearing path, so offshore fintechs are best used as treasury management overlays rather than primary custody rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a foreign shareholder, the contribution must enter Egypt via an authorized bank as a foreign direct investment transfer; the bank will assign a transaction purpose code (typically 'Investment Contribution'/'Capital Injection' under the BOP classification) and verify the foreign origin with the sending bank. The LLC must be registered with the General Authority for Investment (GAFI) and maintain a CBE-approved bank account. If the funds are to be used for initial DG forwarding working capital, the required documents are the investment file, share register, board resolution, and bank-specific BOP reporting forms. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months following the end of the fiscal year (Article 33, Law No. 91 of 2005). For a calendar-year entity the filing deadline is 30 April; the full self-assessed tax balance is payable at filing after quarterly advance instalments.

Instalments of 25% of estimated annual tax due within the last 15 days of each quarter of the fiscal year; the balance is due with the annual Form No. 43.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate controlled transactions in a tax year exceed EGP 8,000,000; Master File and Country-by-Country Report apply to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EGP 2,000,000,000; a Simplified Transfer Pricing Disclosure (Schedule T-1) must accompany every Form No. 43 regardless of transaction value, and the documentation must be contemporaneous.

Economic substance scope: Egypt has no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the UAE/CCAT regime; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 30, Law No. 91 of 2005), the beneficial-ownership provisions of DTAs, and the Investment Law require an Egyptian DG forwarding LLC to maintain physical premises, operational employees, DG-specific licenses, active management decisions in Egypt, and genuine business risk. A pass-through shell or offshore-managed entity will be re-characterized, denied treaty benefits, and stripped of SCZone tax incentives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to five years and a fine of EGP 5,000–50,000 per offense; additional penalty equal to 100–200% of the evaded tax; forced reassessment with a 25% surcharge; suspension of the DG forwarding license and deregistration from the ETA.

Director personal liability: Directors, general managers, and board members are jointly and severally liable for the evaded taxes and fraudulent distributions; the ETA enforcement committees may impose travel bans, freeze personal bank accounts, and place names on airport watch lists for arrears above EGP 200,000; conviction carries direct imprisonment without suspension.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: GAAR re-characterization and non-recognition of the entity for tax purposes; denial of treaty benefits; up to a 150% uplift on deemed underpaid tax; revocation of Investment Law incentives; criminal referral for operating a fictitious company.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil results in personal liability for corporate tax, customs duties, and penalties; confiscation of personal assets; administrative travel bans; imprisonment up to five years for maintaining a sham entity to evade tax or customs.
Anti-Money Laundering (High-Risk DG Sector)

Statutory sanction: Under AML Law No. 80 of 2002 as amended by Law No. 115 of 2019: imprisonment of 7–15 years and fines of EGP 100,000–1,000,000; compulsory seizure and confiscation of cargo, vehicles, bank deposits, and shares; freezing orders within 48 hours; exclusion from the Egyptian Financial Regulatory Authority and ETA e-invoicing platforms.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability attaches personally to directors and beneficial owners; travel bans, real-time asset freezing, extradition where a bilateral treaty exists, imprisonment from seven years, placement on terror/AML blacklists, and prohibition from serving as a director of any Egyptian regulated company.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalties of EGP 20,000–200,000 for documentation failure; primary transfer pricing adjustment plus an accuracy penalty of 25–50% of the adjustment; monthly interest of 2% on the revised liability; the ETA may substitute a deemed assessment where records are withheld.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate declarations are subject to criminal referral for false statements; joint and several liability applies when the intercompany transaction was executed for the personal benefit of an officer or controlling shareholder; travel bans are initiated for aggregate tax debts after a transfer pricing reassessment.
Dividend Stripping / Concealed Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of disguised payments as dividends subject to the 10% final WHT, plus the 20% WHT on the hidden fee, and penalties of up to 200% of the unreported WHT; repatriation is blocked by the ETA and the Central Bank until all taxes are settled; the DG cargo dispatch license is suspended.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes remittance knowing the withholding tax was unpaid bears personal liability for the withheld amounts; travel bans and seizure of personal bank accounts may be ordered; conviction for violation of foreign exchange and tax laws carries imprisonment of up to three years.
