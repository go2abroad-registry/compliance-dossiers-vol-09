Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for LLC under Law 159/1981; the capital stated in the Memorandum must be fully subscribed and the paid-up portion deposited in an Egyptian bank account in the company's name before incorporation, and the bank certificate must be attached to the GAFI filing.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory Civil Defense fire-safety clearance for the office premises, including portable fire extinguishers, illuminated exit signs, smoke detection where required, and an electrical load certificate. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance, Arab Republic of Egypt. Official portal: https://www.eta.gov.eg; e-invoicing/e-receipt clearance platform: https://einvoicing.eta.gov.eg; e-filing/e-services platform: https://dgrtax.gov.eg.

E-government filing portal

An Egyptian LLC obtains a unified Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) from the ETA within 30 days of issuance of the Commercial Registration (CR) certificate. Procedure: (1) Execute a notarized LLC Articles of Association (AOA) with a certified capital structure; obtain CR and Chamber of Commerce card from the General Authority for Investment (GAFI, for foreign-owned LLCs); (2) Appoint an Egypt-licensed tax representative or statutory auditor for all ETA filings if management is offshore; file the corporate Power of Attorney and board resolution with the ETA; (3) Submit the electronic registration application via the ETA e-registration module with: CR certificate, AOA, ISIC 5813 activity classification statement, IDs and tax domicile declarations of shareholders/managers, and an Egyptian bank account certificate; (4) Upon approval, obtain the unified TIN (per Income Tax Law No. 91/2005 Article 36) and the separate VAT registration certificate (TIN-VA) under VAT Law No. 67/2016; (5) Enroll in the mandatory e-invoicing system on the ETA platform and obtain the digital XML-signature certificates/QR token before issuing invoices. A non-resident entity with no Egyptian establishment that supplies digital content (including the digital magazine portal) to Egyptian consumers may instead apply for simplified direct VAT registration on the ETA Foreign Digital Services portal; this registration does not create a permanent establishment nor triggers CIT filing obligations, and applies solely to the 14% VAT on remote B2C digital services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Egypt does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or pass-through treatment for LLCs; the EG LLC is mandatorily a resident juridical person taxed on worldwide income at a 22.5% CIT rate. Available elections and reliefs: (a) VAT group registration election under Article 17 of Law 67/2016 allowing related Egyptian entities to be treated as one VAT person (ETA pre-approval required); (b) Election to conduct business inside a Qualified Economic Zone (e.g., Suez Canal Economic Zone/SCZone) under Investment Law No. 72/2017, which permits customs and indirect-tax suspension regimes for zone-inbound machinery and inputs (the digital magazine portal however is outside zone benefits unless the adjudication officer confirms the zone-boundary exemption); (c) Export income investment incentive election: up to 50% reduction of taxable export profits under Investment Law 72/2017, phased out in observance of WTO subsidy disciplines; (d) Election to use the ETA pro-rata input VAT recovery ratio (export zero-rated arm vs. domestic 14% taxable arm); (e) Election to apply deferred CIT installments for startup/subscale companies under ETA guidelines. No hybrid mismatch or entity-classification elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EGP 500,000 mandatory annual taxable turnover threshold (Article 14, VAT Law No. 67/2016); voluntary VAT registration is permitted once taxable turnover reaches EGP 50,000 in any 12-month look-back. Non-resident digital-content suppliers (including foreign magazine portals) are compulsorily registrable on the ETA non-resident digital services module without turnover threshold when making B2C supplies to Egyptian consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods and exported services are zero-rated at 0% under Article 21(1) and (3) of VAT Law No. 67/2016. For the digital magazine portal (ISIC 5813), the following cumulative tests must be satisfied: (a) supply is made to a non-resident subscriber, advertiser or B2B customer whose usual place of residence or business is outside Egypt (verified by subscriber BIN/payment-account country, IP-address geolocation and contractual domicile); (b) for digital subscriptions, the digital content is made available and downloaded/streamed by the non-resident customer outside Egypt; no Egyptian server-intellectual-rights test is applied; (c) for sponsored media placements, the advertising campaign is published/broadcast outside Egypt to non-resident audiences, or paid by the Egyptian entity only if the advertisement relates to an export transaction; where the sponsor documents an EG domestic audience or EG contractual counterparty, the supply is taxable at 14%; (d) consideration is settled in freely convertible foreign currency and is repatriated through an Egyptian bank account; (e) VAT invoices are issued through the ETA e-invoicing platform with zero-rated tax code and are linked to the underlying export evidence. Input VAT attributable to the zero-rated export arm is creditable and refundable through e-filing, subject to a six-month statutory refund claim window and ETA document verification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (C31) / NAFEZA Egyptian Single Window export receipt containing the shipment/transaction metadata and official customs approval
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) issued in the exporter's name as shipper and the non-resident counterparty as consignee, carrying carrier stamp and date
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IBC) or bank transfer advice from the Egyptian bank evidencing hard-currency receipt matching the invoice number and amount
  • Signed commercial invoice and underlying subscription/placement contract with the non-resident customer or advertiser
  • ETA-validated zero-rated e-invoice with UUID, QR code and the zero-rating tax code assigned by the e-invoicing platform
  • Place-of-supply evidence bundle for digital magazine services (payment-card BIN country, subscriber registered address declaration, IP-address geolocation logs and platform usage logs)
  • Freely convertible FX settlement proof when the counterparty is located in a listed non-cooperative target jurisdiction (additional ETA documentation may be requested)

Mandatory for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Each B2B invoice and B2C e-receipt must be generated through ETA-approved invoicing software and transmitted in near real-time (intra-day, at latest within 24 hours) to the ETA e-invoicing API in XML or JSON with a compliant XML digital signature. The ETA performs real-time schema validation, deduplication, tax-code cross-check and encodes a Unique Identifier (UUID) plus a QR code / barcode that must be printed or embedded on the issued document. Phased enforcement became fully mandatory for all VAT registrants during fiscal year 2023 and is strictly audited. Non-submission or rejection of an invoice by the ETA is legally equal to non-issuance of the invoice, leading to fines from EGP 1,000 up to EGP 50,000 per rejected document plus suspension of the taxpayer's e-invoice account.

The ETA conducts segmented audits of export-ledgers, cross-referencing NAFEZA customs declarations, inbound remittance slips and subscriber/IP geolocation logs. Civil clawback: any restored, unaudited or disproved zero-rated export supply is re-assessed at the 14% standard VAT rate (or at the 20% additional tax under special cases for digital B2C supplies if applicable) plus default interest at 2% per month (24% annualized) from the original refund date. Administrative fines under Law 67/2016 apply from EGP 1,000 to EGP 50,000 per missing, forged or late supporting document. Criminal exposure under Articles 41-43 (as amended) applies where the exporter deliberately uses forged shipping documents, round-tripped invoices or fabricated subscriber remittances to obtain a VAT refund: imprisonment from 3 to 5 years and fines up to double the evaded tax or the amount of the fraudulently obtained refund. Additionally, if export proceeds are not received within the CBE prescription period (default 180 days from shipment/invoice), the zero-rating is retrospectively rescinded, the VAT becomes due in full, and Central Bank regulations impose an administrative penalty of 1% per month on the unrepatriated amount until settlement or exporter proof of force majeure is accepted by the CBE.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF, so current account transactions are generally freely convertible and supported by licensed banks. Capital account transactions remain subject to CBE licensing and registration requirements. Profit remittances by non-resident shareholders of an Egyptian LLC are permissible upon submission of audited financial statements, a tax compliance certificate confirming payment of all Egyptian profits tax and withholding taxes, and a duly completed CBE-approved bank transfer request; practical delays and secondary FX documentation may arise during periods of external sector stress, but no formal blanket prohibition exists for a correctly lodged profit remittance flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For both merchandise and service exporters, including digital publication and advertising revenue exports derived through platforms such as Stripe and AdSense, CBE instructions require full repatriation of proceeds through a bank authorized in Egypt within 90 days from the date the income is accrued. A mandatory conversion ratio of at least 50% of the repatriated foreign currency amount to Egyptian pounds is currently applied at the time of the transaction; the remaining balance may be retained in a foreign currency account of the LLC at an Egyptian licensed bank. The CBE retains authority to raise or lower the mandatory conversion ratio by circular, and export proceeds must first be credited to an Egyptian bank account before any disbursement to foreign suppliers is made.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) under Law No. 194 of 2020 promulgating the Central Bank and Banking System Law, together with its Executive Regulations and Foreign Exchange Law No. 38 of 1994, as amended

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Moderate. Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., virtual multi-currency account services domiciled in the UK/EU) are not licensed by the CBE and cannot receive export proceeds directly in satisfaction of Egyptian surrender and repatriation requirements as the sole clearing route. Such EMIs can be used only as an ancillary collection mechanism if: (1) the primary settlement of export proceeds is ultimately routed to and fully disclosed to an Egyptian licensed bank, and (2) no residual funds remain in the EMI beyond that required for ongoing operational settlement. Purely offshore EMI wallets holding retained EGP-linked balances or converting EGP without CBE authorization would breach CBE foreign currency control rules and may trigger AML scrutiny.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for an Egyptian LLC's equity injection require the foreign shareholder to remit via SWIFT with purpose code corresponding to CBE's 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital in Egyptian Companies' (typically code 13S1 or equivalent as detailed in the current CBE coding manual). The receiving Egyptian bank must first verify the LLC is duly registered in the CBE's database through the Commercial Registry. The injection is then credited to the LLC's capital escrow account and must be released to the LLC's operational account only upon presentation of the recommendation letter of the General Authority for Investment (GAFI), the issued share premium statement, and a signed copy of the shareholder resolution. Regulatory approval under Article 80 of Law 194/2020 may be required if the remittance amount exceeds the published consultation threshold. Full beneficial ownership information and sworn declaration as to the non-criminally derived nature of the funds must accompany the wire.Clearing rails for trade settlements from an Egyptian LLC to foreign vendors take the form of SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for unescrowed payments and commercial letters of credit (LC) for high-value or recurring acquisition of digital content and licensing arrangements. Each outbound payment must be supported by the USD/EGP purpose code '5I15 - Services - Advertising' or '5I12 - Royalties and Broadcasting' as appropriate, and is routed via a bank-approved RAIS system where the bank conducts post-shipment and post-performance verification. Recurrent licensing or content licensing payments should be documented by an LC-favored frame agreement to establish commercial reality.
