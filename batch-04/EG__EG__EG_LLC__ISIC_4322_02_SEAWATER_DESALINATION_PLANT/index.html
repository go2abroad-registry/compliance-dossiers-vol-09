Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (GAFI), One-Stop Shop (OSS), in coordination with the Commercial Registry of the Ministry of Supply and Internal Trade.

Governing statute

Law No. 159 of 1981 (Egyptian Companies Law), as amended by Law No. 5 of 2015, particularly the LLC provisions (Arts. 1-117), read with Investment Law No. 72 of 2017 and Presidential Decree No. 2310 of 2017 (Executive Regulations); commercial registration governed by Law No. 34 of 1976.

Formation / constitutional filings

Certificate of Incorporation application accompanied by the notarized Articles of Association (Aqd Ta'sis) and GAFI's standard incorporation form.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for LLCs under Law 159/1981; the Articles freely fix the share capital, but 100% of the subscribed nominal capital must be fully paid at formation into a temporary blocked account at a licensed Egyptian bank, released after GAFI issues the Certificate of Incorporation; up to 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Directorate (Ministry of Interior) fire-safety certificate under Law No. 441 of 1959 covering hydrant network, fire-fighting pumps, gas detection, and evacuation plans; structural-load and building-license approvals under Law No. 106 of 1976; Ministry of Manpower occupational safety and health clearance under Labor Law No. 12 of 2003 including establishment registration, OHS committee formation, and pre-operational site inspection.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for LLCs under Law 159/1981; the Articles freely fix the share capital, but 100% of the subscribed nominal capital must be fully paid at formation into a temporary blocked account at a licensed Egyptian bank, released after GAFI issues the Certificate of Incorporation; up to 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Egyptian Tax Authority (ETA), Ministry of Finance. Official e-services portals: ETA Tax Portal (https://www.eta.gov.eg), e-Invoice/e-Receipt platform (https://www.invoice.mis.gov.eg), and unified tax filing portal (https://www.tax.gov.eg).

E-government filing portal

For a desalination EPC LLC: (1) incorporate the LLC through the General Authority for Investment & Free Zones (GAFI) or the local Commercial Registry and obtain the Commercial Registration number; (2) apply to the competent ETA branch for the Tax Card (Egyptian Tax Identification Number), submitting incorporation certificate, Memorandum of Association, lease/office agreement, and board resolution; (3) register for VAT and obtain a separate VAT certificate prior to first taxable supply or import; (4) if acting through an Egyptian branch, register the branch as a Permanent Establishment of the non-resident entity for Corporate Income Tax; (5) obtain ETA electronic invoice transmitter credentials and integrate the ERP with the ETA e-invoicing API.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: an Egyptian LLC is an opaque corporate taxpayer subject to Corporate Income Tax under Law No. 91 of 2005. Residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident LLCs and branches are taxed only on Egypt-source income (territorial limitation). No pass-through election is available to the LLC.

Available election forms

Egypt does not apply a US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. The LLC is mandatorily taxed at entity level. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) group tax consolidation for wholly-owned resident companies meeting the Law 91/2005 conditions; (ii) election among fiscal depreciation methods within statutory ceilings; (iii) voluntary VAT registration at EGP 100,000 annual turnover; and (iv) election to apply transfer pricing comparable methods and advanced pricing arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

14%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration for a supplier of taxable goods or services whose annual taxable supplies exceed EGP 500,000. Voluntary registration is permitted from EGP 100,000. Importers of goods into Egypt must register regardless of turnover; non-resident suppliers may be subject to recipient accounting / reverse charge obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT-registered exporter may apply 0% VAT where: (i) goods are physically exported from Egypt to a non-resident buyer within the statutory period and the Customs Export Declaration shows foreign destination; (ii) services are supplied to a non-resident person and are effectively used or consumed outside Egypt, including offshore seawater desalination engineering services; and (iii) export proceeds are received in freely convertible foreign currency. Domestic desalination construction works in Egypt remain taxable at 14%; only the exported portion qualifies for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bayan Shika) processed through the Al-Nafeza single window
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill evidencing goods left Egypt
  • Commercial invoice and underlying EPC/desalination contract
  • Certificate of origin (if required by buyer or for preferential tariff claims)
  • ETA VAT export invoice with the 0% rate code
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange statement confirming receipt of export proceeds
  • Export manifest and shipping instructions issued by the vessel/operator

Egypt mandates real-time e-invoicing and e-receipt clearance through the ETA central system. Registered taxpayers must transmit certified B2B invoices via the ETA API in the approved JSON/XML schema; each invoice receives a unique compliance code and QR signature after ETA validation. B2C retail transactions must use the e-receipt system. The same fiscal data must be integrated with ERP or middleware using ETA digital signature credentials. Failure to clear invoices in real time may block transactions and attract penalties.

ETA cross-matches export zero-rating claims with Customs export declarations, e-invoice data, and Central Bank of Egypt foreign-exchange remittance records. If documentation is incomplete, the export is recharacterized as a taxable domestic supply and VAT is assessed at 14%, together with late-payment interest and administrative fines. Knowingly issuing a false zero-rated invoice or submitting forged export documents is a VAT fraud offense punishable by a penal fine of not less than EGP 50,000, potential imprisonment, and an additional penalty of at least 100% of the tax evaded. Retained refunds are clawed back with legal surcharge; the statute of limitation for audit is generally five years, extended in cases of fraud or concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Egypt maintains a managed-floating exchange-rate regime. Current-account transactions (imports of goods/services, interest, and repatriation of duly audited profits) are convertible through the interbank market upon presentation of genuine underlying documents, subject to US-dollar liquidity allocation during stressed periods. Capital-account flows are legal but require CBE registration and, where applicable, GAFI (General Authority for Investment) clearance under Foreign Investment Law No. 72 of 2017. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily documentary: audited financial statements, valid tax clearance certification, board resolution authorizing distribution, and CBE purpose-code verification (code 8810 for profit remittances); banks may impose queueing during scarcity episodes. The desalination EPC company should contract for settlement in USD to reduce EGP conversion risk and maintain full traceability of each inflow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All proceeds from export/sale of goods or services must be credited to an account in an Egypt-licensed bank within the tenor of the documentary credit or sale contract, and in any event no later than 180 days from issuance of the bill of lading or service-completion certificate, with extension only by CBE approval. No mandatory conversion to EGP is presently in force after the November-2016 float and the 2024 liberalization; 100% of proceeds must nonetheless enter the Egyptian banking system (100% surrender into a domestic foreign-currency account), after which the exporter may retain the currency or convert at interbank rates. Netting, offshore retention, or settlement through a non-Egyptian clearing hub is prohibited for third-party export receivables.

Central bank supervision: Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) - Al-Bank Al-Markazi Al-Masry, the sole supervisory and regulatory authority for banking and the exchange market under Law No. 194 of 2020 (Central Bank and Banking System Law), exercising licensing, commercial-bank supervision, AML/CFT rulemaking, and balance-of-payments statistics reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Offshore digital EMIs and multi-currency wallets (for example Revolut Business, Wise Business, Payoneer) are not licensed or passported into Egypt and cannot serve as the primary treasury or settlement rail for the Egyptian LLC. Legal constraints arise from the CBE's exclusive licensing of banks under Law No. 194 of 2020, the Payment Systems Law No. 18 of 2019, and CBE AML/CFT rules that require customer funds and FX conversion to be conducted within licensed Egyptian institutions. Foreign EMI wallets may be used only as an ancillary collection bridge for incidental pre-export or international R&D recoveries; such funds must be repatriated onward to a CBE-licensed bank file. Relying on any offshore EMI as an EPC payment trunk will create adverse AML/KYC inference and will not be accepted for the project escrow or customs-driven settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire-transfer protocol: inbound equity capital must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 7210 ('Direct investment in the capital of an Egyptian company'), routed from the foreign parent's home account through its correspondent to the designated CBE-licensed account bank, and credited to the Capital Escrow Account. The receiving bank issues a certified inward-transfer certificate (I.N. Form) for CBE balance-of-payments registration, and must retain evidence linking the wire to the LLC: the notarized capital contribution resolution, the LLC's commercial-registry extract, its tax card, and audited financials. Where funding is a shareholder loan, purpose code 7220 ('Intercompany lending') applies and requires the signed loan agreement, board approval, and amortization schedule. Funds must derive directly from the shareholder's own account; third-party pass-through is rejected unless it represents a licensed pre-payment of the desalination project. USD-to-EGP conversion for local expenditures must occur in the interbank market, evidenced by the bank's conversion ticket.Operational payments (consulting fees, insurance, head-office recharges below CBE threshold) are cleared by SWIFT MT103 (serial or cover) via the bank's correspondent network. High-value capital procurement for the desalination plant - membranes, pressure vessels, high-pressure pumps, thermal evaporators, switchgear and SCADA equipment - must be settled through irrevocable commercial letters of credit (MT700) opened by the Egyptian bank at sight or up to 180-day usance, with presentation of full shipping sets (commercial invoice, bill of lading, inspection certificate, packing list) through the advising/confirming bank. USD clearing is settled via CHIPS/Fedwire through New York correspondents; EUR-denominated options are settled over TARGET2 where suppliers are European. Domestic large EGP disbursements are executed through the Egyptian interbank RTGS. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

22.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on the net distributable profit paid to shareholders, applicable equally to residents and non-residents under Law 91/2005. Distributions from Free Zone companies may be exempt if the Free Zone certificate and the source of the distributed profits are entirely non-Egyptian.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within four months of the fiscal year-end; the fiscal year must match the legal accounting period. The Egyptian Tax Authority may grant limited extensions for consolidated groups only.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 30 days after the end of each fiscal quarter (31 March, 30 June, 30 September, 31 December). The final annual return reconciled in Form No. 2 fully substitutes and settles all four advances.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation are required where total controlled transactions with related parties exceed EGP 15 million in a single fiscal year. The Master File is mandatory where the ultimate parent's consolidated group turnover is EGP 200 million or more. The TP disclosure form (Form 2 – Transfer Pricing Declaration) must be filed with the annual return whenever related-party transactions exceed EGP 10 million.

Economic substance scope: Egypt does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute in the style of Gulf jurisdictions; however, under Investment Law and the General Anti-Abuse Rule of Article 30 of Law 91/2005, a company claiming tax incentives or treaty relief must maintain genuine decision-making management, qualified employees, operational premises, accounting records, and active bank accounts within Egypt. A desalination LLC used as a dormant shell while a foreign parent negotiates and performs the EPC contract within Egypt will cause the foreign parent to be treated as having a permanent establishment and the shell to be disregarded for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Under-reporting of construction revenue)

Statutory sanction: A fine of at least 20% of the tax evaded and, in aggravated circumstances, fines up to triple the evaded tax, plus the monthly 2% delayed-payment surcharge capped at 25%. Criminal conviction under Law 91/2005 Article 133 carries imprisonment from six months to five years and joint liability of all executive directors who knowingly signed or directed the fraudulent return.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally responsible for the full amount of under-declared tax, the fine and criminal penalties. Egyptian courts regularly impose asset attachment, travel bans, and up to five years' imprisonment on directors who approved false revenue recognition or instructed concealment of project receipts.
Shell Company and Artificial Entity Abuse

Statutory sanction: The artificial entity is disregarded, all revenue is re-characterized as attributable to a permanent establishment of the foreign principal, and the full 22.5% corporate tax is imposed on the relevant EPC profit, together with default interest and administrative penalties of up to 25% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: The managing director who authorized the shell arrangement and signed the intercompany agreements is exposed to recovery of the full attributed tax under a veil-piercing theory. The court may order confiscation of personal assets, restrict exit through travel bans, and impose a period of imprisonment if the scheme is deemed a fraudulent misrepresentation to the Egyptian Tax Authority.
AML / Misdeclared Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Immediate freezing of the LLC's bank accounts and revocation of the tax card; additional monetary penalties up to EGP 150,000 and criminal fines up to five times the laundered sum. The Egyptian Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Combating Unit automatically receives the annual tax return and any unusual transaction report, initiating administrative and criminal procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors face individual criminal prosecution if they authorize cross-border withdrawals or escrow disbursements without adequate know-your-counterparty and beneficial-ownership due diligence. Conviction may lead to imprisonment for up to two years, a personal fine, and an order to restore all laundered funds to the State, effectively removing the limitation of liability otherwise enjoyed by LLC shareholders.
